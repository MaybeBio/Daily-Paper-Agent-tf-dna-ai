## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript presents a potentially useful computational framework for integrating heterogeneous genomic data to infer dynamic regulatory networks. The core idea of using DNA-binding data to inform sparsity in a time-varying regression model is interesting and could be of value to the genomics community. However, the manuscript is currently incomplete in its presentation, with key methodological details and results (figures, tables) missing. The claims of biological relevance are based on literature interpretation of inferred edges, but the statistical and computational validation is insufficiently described. The comparison to a static correlation method is superficial and does not establish clear superiority. The manuscript would benefit from a more rigorous evaluation of the method's performance, including simulations or benchmarks against established tools.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Computational biologists and bioinformaticians developing network inference methods, particularly those integrating multi-omic data. Researchers studying gene regulatory networks in development, especially neurodevelopment, may also find the application to cortical development interesting. The method's computational efficiency could appeal to those working with large-scale single-cell datasets.
- **Major strengths**
  - The conceptual advance of integrating DNA-binding data to guide regulatory network inference is timely and addresses a known limitation of expression-only co-expression networks.
  - The proposed method is computationally efficient, making it scalable to genome-wide applications.
  - The application to a relevant biological system (human cortical development) with interpretation of known regulators demonstrates potential utility.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **R1-M1** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes) The manuscript lacks the full mathematical specification of the model. Equations (1)-(4) are referenced but not fully presented, making it impossible to assess the statistical properties of the model, the penalisation scheme, or the optimisation algorithm. This is a fundamental issue that prevents evaluation of the method's validity and novelty.
  - **R1-M2** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes) The results section is entirely dependent on figures that are not provided. Without Figures 1-7, the claims of inferred trajectories, network structures, and comparisons cannot be evaluated. The text descriptions are insufficient to assess the quality or correctness of the results.
  - **R1-M3** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes) The pseudo-time inference method is described only in general terms (t-SNE, PAM, minimum spanning tree). The specific implementation, parameter choices, and validation of the inferred trajectory are not detailed. This is a critical step that heavily influences downstream network inference, and its lack of detail undermines reproducibility.
  - **R1-M4** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No) The comparison with the thresholded correlation matrix method is weak. The choice of threshold (|ρ| ≥ 0.4) appears arbitrary, and the comparison is only made for one target gene (SATB2). A more systematic benchmark, including quantitative metrics of network accuracy (e.g., against known regulatory interactions) or comparison with other state-of-the-art methods, is needed to support the claim of superiority.
  - **R1-M5** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No) The biological interpretation of the inferred networks relies on literature knowledge, but the statistical significance of the inferred edges is not addressed. The manuscript does not discuss how to distinguish robust regulatory interactions from noise or how to assess the confidence of individual edges. This limits the practical utility of the method.
- **Minor Comments**
  - **R1-m1** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Clarity) The abstract and background sections are well-written and accessible, but the methods section is dense and would benefit from a more structured presentation, perhaps with a step-by-step algorithm box.
  - **R1-m2** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Reproducibility) The manuscript does not state whether the code is publicly available. Providing code and a demonstration on a small simulated dataset would greatly enhance reproducibility and usability.
  - **R1-m3** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Data availability) The DNase-seq data accession numbers are provided, but the specific processing pipeline for these data (e.g., alignment, peak calling) is not described. This is important for reproducing the binding probability estimates.
  - **R1-m4** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Presentation) The discussion of the method's limitations is brief. A more thorough discussion of potential biases (e.g., from the choice of DNA-binding data, the pseudo-time inference) and the generalizability of the approach would strengthen the manuscript.
  - **R1-m5** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Terminology) The term "genomic network" is used, but the model infers regulatory influences based on expression and binding data. Clarifying the distinction between co-expression, regulatory, and "genomic" networks would help avoid confusion.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established**
  - R1-M1 (missing model specification)
  - R1-M2 (missing figures and results)
  - R1-M3 (insufficient pseudo-time method details)
  - R1-M4 (inadequate comparison)
  - R1-M5 (lack of statistical significance assessment)
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**
  - **Originality:** The idea of using DNA-binding data to modulate the sparsity penalty in a time-varying regression model is a novel contribution. However, the lack of full methodological detail prevents a complete assessment of its novelty relative to existing integrative approaches.
  - **Scientific importance:** If the method is robust and validated, it could provide a valuable tool for genome-wide regulatory network inference, which is a significant challenge in genomics. The application to neurodevelopment is of high importance.
  - **Interdisciplinary readership:** The work sits at the intersection of statistics, computer science, and developmental biology. The presentation is currently too technical for a broad biological audience and too incomplete for a statistical audience. The potential for broad readership is present but not yet realized.
  - **Technical soundness:** Cannot be assessed due to missing equations and figures. The described approach is plausible, but the lack of validation and benchmarking is a major concern.
  - **Readability for nonspecialists:** The abstract and background are clear, but the methods and results are not accessible to nonspecialists in their current form. The manuscript would need significant revision to be understandable to a broad scientific audience.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The manuscript has a promising core idea, but the missing methodological details and results make it impossible to evaluate the validity of the claims. The authors should be encouraged to resubmit a complete version with full equations, figures, and a more rigorous validation.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that the method "confirms genomic regulatory patterns which are recognised as being fundamental to neuronal development" is unsupported without the figures and a statistical analysis of the significance of these findings.
- The claim that the method "compares very favourably" with the correlation-based approach is unsupported due to the arbitrary threshold choice and lack of quantitative comparison.
- The claim of computational efficiency is plausible but not quantified with runtime or scalability benchmarks.
- The generalizability of the method to other biological systems is asserted but not demonstrated.
