## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript addresses a potentially interesting question about the conformational response of ACE2 to spike protein binding and the possibility of allosteric modulation by small molecules. However, the supplied material is limited to an abstract, which prevents a rigorous evaluation of the methodology, validation, and robustness of the claims. The core conclusion that ACE2 undergoes persistent structural changes upon S1 binding is plausible but not established from the evidence provided here. The additional claim regarding ligand-induced allosteric changes and classification of compounds is intriguing but currently unsupported by any quantitative detail.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Structural biologists, computational biophysicists, and researchers studying viral entry mechanisms, particularly those focused on SARS-CoV-2 host recognition. The potential for identifying small molecules that modulate ACE2 conformation could interest drug discovery groups targeting host-pathogen interactions.
- **Major strengths** The use of independent training and testing datasets, including long trajectories from an external source (D. E. Shaw Research), is a commendable approach to avoid overfitting. The application of a machine learning method (LDA) to classify conformational ensembles is a modern and potentially powerful analytical tool. The focus on long-range allosteric effects is a novel angle beyond static structural comparisons.
- **Major Concerns**  
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1  
  - **Severity** Major  
  - **Blocking** Yes  
  - **Axis** Technical soundness  
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim "persistent differences in ACE2 structure upon binding" based on all-atom molecular dynamics simulations.  
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided  
  - **Concern** The abstract states that differences are "persistent" but provides no quantitative metrics, such as effect sizes, confidence intervals, or statistical significance. No information is given on the number of simulations, their lengths, convergence criteria, or how "persistence" was defined. Without these details, the claim of persistent structural differences cannot be assessed.  
  - **Why it matters** A central claim of the paper hinges on the reliability and reproducibility of the simulation results. If the simulations are not converged or the differences are within thermal noise, the entire premise collapses.  
  - **Resolution test** Provide a detailed methods section with simulation parameters, convergence analysis, and statistical tests comparing apo and bound ensembles. Show that the LDA separation is robust across independent replicates and that the differences exceed noise thresholds.  
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2  
  - **Severity** Major  
  - **Blocking** Yes  
  - **Axis** Technical soundness  
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that LDA classification is "validated using independent training and testing datasets".  
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided  
  - **Concern** No details are given on the size of the training and testing sets, the split criteria, the classification accuracy, or the choice of features for LDA. The abstract mentions "long trajectories generated by D. E. Shaw Research" but does not specify how these were used in validation. Without these numbers, the validation claim is unverifiable.  
  - **Why it matters** Machine learning results are highly sensitive to feature selection and dataset composition. If the validation is weak or the datasets are not truly independent, the classification may be overfitted.  
  - **Resolution test** Report the number of trajectories in each set, the classification accuracy, confusion matrices, and the specific LDA features. Demonstrate that the classifier generalizes to unseen data.  
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3  
  - **Severity** Major  
  - **Blocking** Yes  
  - **Axis** Scientific importance  
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that projecting ligand-bound ACE2 structures onto the LDA vector allows prediction of "apo-like" vs "complex-like" compounds and pinpoints "long-range ligand-induced allosteric changes".  
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided  
  - **Concern** The abstract provides no information on the 78 compounds, their chemical diversity, binding sites, or how the projections were interpreted. The claim of allosteric changes is made without any structural or dynamical evidence, such as residue-level contact maps or correlation analyses.

  - **Resolution test** Provide a list of compounds with their binding modes, show the distribution of projections, and include residue-level analyses that demonstrate allosteric communication pathways.
