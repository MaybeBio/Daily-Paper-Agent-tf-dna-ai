## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, results, discussion, methods) from bioRxiv preprint
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content and technical soundness as presented in the provided text; no supplementary figures or data files were available for inspection
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors use microsecond-scale all-atom molecular dynamics simulations and a machine learning post-processing approach (multilayer perceptrons with layerwise relevance propagation) to compare the interaction of the bacterial DNA glycosylase MutM with DNA containing either a single 8-oxoguanine lesion or a tandem lesion consisting of 8-oxoguanine adjacent to an abasic site. They report that the tandem lesion perturbs the canonical interaction network, induces local DNA structural changes, and results in a more rigid MutM:DNA complex, which they propose contributes to reduced repair efficiency.
- **Visible evidence base** Main text figures referenced (Figures 1-5), Tables 1, and supplementary figures S1-S7 are cited but not provided; simulation parameters and analysis protocols are described in Methods
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Supplementary figures and tables, simulation trajectory data, MLP training details (hyperparameters, validation metrics), force field parameter files, and convergence assessments were not available for review

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript addresses a biologically and radiochemically relevant question, namely the molecular basis for the reduced repair of tandem DNA lesions by MutM. The combination of long-timescale MD simulations with a machine learning-based interaction analysis is timely and potentially useful. However, the evidence presented is largely descriptive and based on two 1-microsecond trajectories per system, which raises concerns about statistical robustness and sampling convergence. The central claim that the tandem lesion reduces repair efficiency is inferred from structural and dynamical observations rather than directly demonstrated. The manuscript would benefit from additional quantitative analyses, error estimates, and a clearer link between the observed molecular changes and functional outcomes.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in DNA damage and repair, radiation biology, and computational biophysics would find this work relevant. The study addresses a gap in understanding how clustered DNA lesions are recognized by repair enzymes, which is important for models of radiation-induced cellular damage and cancer risk. The methodological combination of MD and machine learning may also interest computational biologists developing analysis tools for biomolecular simulations.
- **Major strengths** The study tackles a biologically important and under-explored problem, namely the structural basis for the resistance of tandem lesions to base excision repair. The use of microsecond-scale simulations is ambitious and appropriate for capturing the relevant conformational dynamics. The machine learning approach provides a systematic, unbiased way to identify residues important for protein-DNA interactions, complementing visual inspection. The comparison between singly and tandem damaged systems is well designed, with the only difference being the presence of the abasic site.
- **Major Concerns** 
  - R1-M1: The statistical robustness of the simulation results is not established. Only two replicas per system were run, and no block averaging, bootstrapping, or other error analysis is reported. The reported distances and percentages (e.g., 91.8% stacking occupancy, 42% of simulation time for a short R264-OG16 distance) are presented without confidence intervals, making it impossible to assess whether the observed differences between systems are significant.
  - R1-M2: The central claim that the tandem lesion reduces repair efficiency is not directly tested. The authors observe structural and dynamical changes, but no free energy calculations, binding affinity estimates, or reaction coordinate analyses are provided to link these changes to impaired catalysis or recognition. The statement in the Discussion that rigidity "could explain the difficulty of MutM to process such multiple damaged sites" is speculative.
  - R1-M3: The machine learning analysis is described at a high level, but key details are missing. The choice of a 10 Å cutoff for labeling bound versus non-bound states is not justified. No information is provided on training/validation splits, model accuracy, or how the importance scores were calibrated. The threshold of 0.04 for "singling out" residues appears arbitrary and is not derived from any statistical test.
  - R1-M4: The functional significance of the observed changes is unclear. The authors report that the tandem lesion increases the rigidity of the complex and reduces the involvement of catalytic N-terminal residues, but they do not quantify the magnitude of these effects or relate them to known kinetic or thermodynamic data on MutM-mediated repair of tandem lesions.
- **Minor Comments**
  - R1-m1: The abstract states that the work "advocates for the use of such high throughput numerical simulations," but the computational cost of two 1-microsecond simulations is substantial and not obviously "high throughput." The phrasing should be revised to reflect the actual scope.
  - R1-m2: The manuscript would benefit from a more detailed description of the MLP architecture and training procedure, including the number of training epochs, learning rate, and any regularization used. This information is essential for reproducibility.
  - R1-m3: The authors mention that the beta F-alpha 10 loop (residues 217-237) was reconstructed using Modeller, but no validation of this modeled region is provided. Given that this loop is implicated in DNA recognition, its structural accuracy is important.
  - R1-m4: The text states that "the numbering of MutM residues refers to the crystallographic structure (PDB ID 3GO8)," but it is unclear whether the numbering corresponds to the full-length protein or a construct. This should be clarified.
  - R1-m5: The authors report that the distance between R264 and OG16 is below 4 Å for 42% of the simulation time in the tandem-damaged complex versus 11% in the singly-damaged system. However, the biological relevance of this specific interaction is not discussed in the context of known MutM mechanisms.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** 
  - Lack of error analysis and statistical confidence intervals for all reported quantitative observables
  - No demonstration of simulation convergence (e.g., time evolution of key observables, replica consistency)
  - No direct link between observed structural changes and functional outcomes (e.g., no free energy perturbation or umbrella sampling along the extrusion pathway)
  - Insufficient details on MLP training and validation to assess the reliability of importance scores
  - No comparison with experimental data on MutM binding or repair of tandem lesions to contextualize the findings
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - Originality: The application of machine learning to analyze MD trajectories of a DNA repair enzyme in the context of tandem lesions is novel, though the underlying MD approach is standard. The specific biological question has not been addressed computationally at this level of detail before.
  - Scientific importance: The problem of tandem lesion repair is relevant to radiation biology and cancer therapy, but the study does not provide definitive mechanistic insights that would change current understanding. The findings are suggestive rather than conclusive.
  - Interdisciplinary readership: The work bridges computational chemistry, structural biology, and DNA repair, and could appeal to a broad audience if the conclusions were more firmly established. In its current form, the descriptive nature of the results may limit broader impact.
  - Technical soundness: The simulation protocol is generally sound, but the lack of statistical rigor and convergence analysis undermines confidence in the conclusions. The ML analysis is not sufficiently validated.
  - Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is clearly written and the figures are well described, but the technical details of the ML approach may be challenging for readers without a computational background. The abstract and introduction are accessible.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The manuscript presents an interesting hypothesis and a promising methodological approach, but the evidence is insufficient to support the central claim that the tandem lesion impairs MutM-mediated repair. Additional analyses, including error estimates, convergence checks, and a more direct assessment of functional consequences, are required before the case can be made.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that the tandem lesion "significantly perturb[s] the protein/nucleic acid interaction pattern" is supported by the reported distance and occupancy changes, but the significance of these changes is not statistically established.
- The statement that the tandem lesion results in a "more rigid" complex is inferred from reduced fluctuations and increased interaction occupancies, but no quantitative measure of rigidity (e.g., root-mean-square fluctuations, elastic network analysis) is provided.
- The suggestion that the observed changes "might also contribute to the lower repair efficiency" is speculative and not directly tested.
- The claim that the MLP analysis "allows to disentangle a complex interaction pathway" is overstated given the lack of validation of the importance scores.
- The assertion that the work demonstrates the "predictive power" of the combined MD/ML approach is not supported by any prospective or retrospective validation against experimental data.
