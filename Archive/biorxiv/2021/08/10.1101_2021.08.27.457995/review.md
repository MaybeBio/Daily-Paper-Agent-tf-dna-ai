## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, background, results, discussion, methods, declarations)
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, methodological soundness, validity of claims, and presentation quality as presented in the supplied text. No supplementary figures or tables were available for inspection.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors introduce JAMS (Joint Accessibility-Methylation-Sequence) models, a computational framework that jointly models ChIP-seq pull-down and control read counts as a function of DNA sequence, chromatin accessibility, regional methylation, and base-resolution intra-motif CpG methylation. They apply JAMS to CTCF as a benchmark, demonstrate cross-cell-type transferability and differential binding prediction, and then apply the framework to 2209 ChIP-seq experiments covering 604 TFs, yielding high-confidence models for 260 TFs. They report that 45% of TFs are inhibited by intra-motif methylation in vivo, while only 16 TFs (6%) preferentially bind methylated sites, including 11 novel methyl-binding TFs mostly from the C2H2-ZF family. They further report a systematic shift toward methylation avoidance in vivo compared to in vitro, potentially due to competition with MBD proteins.
- **Visible evidence base** Main text figures referenced (Figs. 1-5), supplementary figures and tables referenced but not provided, methods section, and abstract. No raw data, code output, or validation metrics beyond those described in text were available.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Supplementary Figures 1-7, Supplementary Tables 1-5, the GitHub repository contents, and the Zenodo data deposit were not provided. These are essential for evaluating model fitting details, cross-validation performance, the full list of TF classifications, and the precision/recall calculations against SELEX data.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a timely and potentially valuable computational framework for deconvolving the in vivo effects of CpG methylation on TF binding from confounding factors. The core idea of jointly modeling background and pull-down signals within a generalized linear model framework is sound and addresses a real gap in the field. The benchmark on CTCF is convincing, and the large-scale application to 260 TFs represents a substantial resource. However, the manuscript as supplied has several weaknesses that prevent full endorsement. The most significant concerns are: (1) the lack of a clear, formal statement of the statistical model and its assumptions, (2) the absence of key validation details, particularly regarding the negative binomial dispersion parameter and the handling of confounding between methylation and accessibility, (3) the reliance on a single motif alignment strategy that may bias the sequence features learned, and (4) the interpretation of the in vivo vs. in vitro comparison, which is based on a limited overlap of TFs and may be confounded by differences in experimental platforms. The writing is generally clear, but the results section would benefit from more explicit quantification of effect sizes and uncertainties. Overall, the work is promising and likely of interest to the transcription factor and epigenomics communities, but the current evidence base is insufficient to fully establish all claims.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying transcription factor binding mechanisms, particularly those interested in the interplay between DNA methylation and gene regulation. The large compendium of TF methylation preferences will be a useful resource for the community. Computational biologists developing methods for integrating ChIP-seq, WGBS, and chromatin accessibility data will also find the JAMS framework of interest. The findings on KRAB-ZF proteins and transposable elements may attract researchers studying genome evolution and silencing.
- **Major strengths** The manuscript addresses a significant and technically challenging problem: isolating the base-resolution effect of CpG methylation on TF binding in vivo from confounding variables. The JAMS framework is conceptually well-designed, jointly modeling background and specific signals, which is a clear improvement over simpler approaches. The benchmark on CTCF is appropriate and the cross-cell-type transferability test is a strong validation step. The large-scale application to 260 TFs provides a valuable community resource. The comparison with in vitro SELEX data is a thoughtful approach to benchmarking, and the observation about the shift toward methylation avoidance in vivo is biologically interesting and potentially important.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that JAMS models "quantitatively explain the TF binding strength" and that the model can "deconvolve the specific effect of methylation from the confounding effect of other variables."
  - **Evidence pointer** Methods section, "Implementation of JAMS"; Figure 1E
  - **Concern** The statistical model is described only in general terms. The exact form of the linear predictor, the link function, the treatment of the dispersion parameter, and the handling of overdispersion are not fully specified. The authors state they use "negative binomial with a log-link function" and fit with `glm.nb`, but the details of how the predictor matrix X is constructed, particularly the encoding of methylation as a percentage and the binning of accessibility, are not described with sufficient precision to be reproduced. More critically, the model assumes that the effects of sequence, accessibility, and methylation are additive on the log scale. This is a strong assumption that is not tested or discussed. For example, the effect of methylation might depend on the local sequence context or on the level of accessibility. The claim of "deconvolving" is therefore only as strong as this additivity assumption. The manuscript does not provide diagnostics to assess whether this assumption holds.
  - **Why it matters** If the model is misspecified, the estimated methylation coefficients could be biased, leading to incorrect classification of TFs as methyl-plus or methyl-minus. The central claims of the paper rest on the validity of these coefficients. Without a more rigorous presentation of the model and its assumptions, and without diagnostics to support the additivity assumption, the reliability of the reported TF classifications is not established.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should provide a complete mathematical specification of the model, including all terms in the linear predictor. They should also provide diagnostics, such as residual plots or a comparison of model predictions against held-out data stratified by different levels of the confounding variables, to demonstrate that the additivity assumption is reasonable. A sensitivity analysis, perhaps comparing the results to a model with interaction terms, would also strengthen the case.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that JAMS models have "high precision for inferring intra-motif methylation effects" and that the comparison with SELEX data shows "~82% precision" for methyl-minus and "~80% precision" for methyl-plus TFs.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results, "Systematic inference of the in vivo methyl-binding preferences of 260 TFs using JAMS"; Figure 4D
  - **Concern** The precision calculations are based on a comparison of JAMS classifications with SELEX data for only 76 TFs. The manuscript does not report the recall (sensitivity) of the JAMS approach, i.e., what fraction of SELEX-defined methyl-plus or methyl-minus TFs are correctly identified by JAMS. The text states that only 5 out of 20 SELEX-based methyl-plus TFs are identified as methyl-plus or mixed-effect by JAMS, which implies a low recall for this category. The authors interpret this as a biological difference between in vivo and in vitro, but an alternative explanation is that JAMS has low statistical power to detect positive methylation effects, particularly for TFs with weaker binding or lower occupancy. The manuscript does not provide an analysis to distinguish these possibilities. Furthermore, the criteria for selecting "high-confidence" models (correlation > 0.2, etc.) are arbitrary and may introduce bias. The precision estimates are therefore conditional on these thresholds and may not generalize.
  - **Why it matters** The central biological conclusion that most in vitro methyl-plus TFs appear methylation-agnostic in vivo depends critically on the sensitivity of the JAMS method. If JAMS simply cannot detect positive methylation effects, then the conclusion is an artifact of the method, not a biological finding. The manuscript needs to address this alternative explanation directly.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should report recall as well as precision for the JAMS-SELEX comparison. They should also perform a power analysis, perhaps using simulations, to determine the effect sizes that JAMS can reliably detect given typical ChIP-seq data depth and the number of peaks used. If the power to detect positive effects is low, this should be acknowledged as a major limitation, and the biological interpretation should be tempered accordingly.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Scientific importance
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that "45% of TFs are inhibited by intra-motif methylation in vivo" and that this represents a "substantial expansion of the repertoire of in vivo methyl-binding TFs."
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; Results, "Systematic inference of the in vivo methyl-binding preferences of 260 TFs using JAMS"; Figure 4B
  - **Concern** The classification of TFs into methyl-minus, methyl-plus, mixed-effect, and no-effect is based on the statistical significance of individual methylation coefficients in the JAMS model. The manuscript does not report the distribution of effect sizes, only the counts of TFs in each category. It is unclear how many TFs are classified as methyl-minus based on a single significant position versus multiple positions, and what the magnitude of the effects is. The biological significance of a statistically significant but small effect is questionable. The manuscript would be strengthened by reporting the distribution of effect sizes and by providing examples of specific TFs with their inferred methylation sensitivity profiles.
  - **Why it matters** The headline claim of "45% of TFs inhibited" is a key result. Without a sense of the magnitude of these effects, it is difficult to assess their biological relevance. A TF with a 5% reduction in binding upon methylation is very different from one with a 50% reduction. The current presentation does not allow the reader to make this distinction.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should provide a supplementary figure showing the distribution of the maximum absolute methylation effect size for each TF, perhaps separated by classification category. They should also provide a table listing the top methyl-plus and methyl-minus TFs with their effect sizes and the specific positions involved. This would allow readers to assess the biological importance of the findings.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M4
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that "JAMS models can predict differential TF binding across cell types, including differential TF binding events that are driven by changes in the methylation of the putative binding sites."
  - **Evidence pointer** Results, "Differential TF binding across cell lines can be explained using JAMS models"; Figure 3C-E
  - **Concern** The analysis of differential binding between GM12878 and HeLa-S3 cells is presented as a validation of the model's ability to predict cell-type-specific binding. However, the correlation between predicted and observed log-fold changes is modest (r=0.40 for all peaks, r=0.14 for non-differentially accessible peaks). While the authors note that the correlation increases for highly accessible peaks, the overall predictive power appears limited. The claim that JAMS can "predict" differential binding driven by methylation is based on a correlation that, while statistically significant, explains only a small fraction of the variance. The manuscript does not provide a comparison against a null model (e.g., a model using only sequence and accessibility, without methylation) to demonstrate that the inclusion of methylation significantly improves predictive performance for these differential binding events.
  - **Why it matters** The ability to predict differential binding is presented as evidence for the model's validity. If the model's predictive power is low, or if the improvement over a model without methylation is marginal, this weakens the claim that JAMS captures the specific effect of methylation on binding.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should compare the predictive performance of the full JAMS model against a reduced model that excludes methylation variables, using the same cross-validation or held-out data. They should report the improvement in correlation or other metrics (e.g., AUC for classifying differentially bound peaks) attributable to the methylation variables. This would provide a direct test of the specific contribution of methylation to predicting differential binding.
- **Minor Comments**
  - **Concern ID** R1-m1
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Readability for nonspecialists
  - **Affected element** Abstract and Introduction
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; Background section
  - **Issue** The abstract and introduction use terms like "methyl-minus," "methyl-plus," and "methylation-agnostic" without defining them clearly for a general reader. The distinction between "intra-motif methylation" and "regional methylation" is also introduced without a clear explanation of why this distinction is important.
  - **Required correction** Define these terms explicitly at first use. For example, "We classify a TF as 'methyl-minus' if its binding is inhibited by methylation of CpG sites within its binding motif, and 'methyl-plus' if its binding is enhanced." A brief explanation of why intra-motif versus regional methylation matters (e.g., direct steric interference versus indirect effects on chromatin state) would also improve accessibility.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m2
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Affected element** Methods, "Formatting and preprocessing of data for JAMS"
  - **Evidence pointer** Methods section
  - **Issue** The authors state that peaks overlapping repeat regions were removed, but the rationale is only briefly mentioned ("homology of repeat elements can confound the modeling"). The potential impact of this filtering on the results is not discussed. For example, if a TF predominantly binds within repeat elements, its methylation preferences would be systematically missed.
  - **Required correction** Provide a more detailed justification for this filtering step and discuss its potential limitations. If possible, perform a sensitivity analysis by running the pipeline without this filter on a subset of TFs to assess the impact.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m3
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Scientific importance
  - **Affected element** Discussion
  - **Evidence pointer** Discussion section
  - **Issue** The discussion of the biological implications of the shift toward methylation avoidance in vivo is interesting but somewhat speculative. The authors suggest competition with MBD proteins as a potential explanation, but do not discuss alternative mechanisms, such as the possibility that methylation affects TF binding indirectly through changes in nucleosome positioning or histone modifications.
  - **Required correction** Briefly acknowledge alternative mechanisms and, if possible, cite relevant literature. This would provide a more balanced and comprehensive discussion.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m4
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Readability for nonspecialists
  - **Affected element** Figure 2A
  - **Evidence pointer** Results, "JAMS models reveal the contribution of CpG methylation to TF binding"
  - **Issue** The "dot plot logos" are introduced as a visualization tool, but the description of how to interpret them is insufficient. A reader unfamiliar with this type of plot may not understand what the dots represent or how to compare them across positions.
  - **Required correction** Provide a clear legend or a more detailed description in the text explaining the dot plot logo format, including what the size and color of the dots represent.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** The primary technical failing is the lack of a complete and precise specification of the JAMS statistical model, including the exact form of the predictor matrix, the treatment of the dispersion parameter, and the assumptions of additivity. Without this, the model is not fully reproducible, and the validity of the estimated coefficients cannot be assessed. A second technical failing is the absence of a power analysis or sensitivity analysis to determine the reliability of the TF classifications, particularly for detecting positive methylation effects. A third failing is the lack of a direct comparison against a reduced model (without methylation) in the differential binding analysis to demonstrate the specific contribution of methylation.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**
  - **Originality** The work is original in its aim to systematically characterize in vivo methylation preferences of TFs at base resolution while controlling for confounding factors. The JAMS framework is a novel contribution, although it builds on existing statistical approaches (GLMs, negative binomial regression).
  - **Scientific importance** The question of how DNA methylation affects TF binding in vivo is of fundamental importance to gene regulation. The large-scale resource generated here has the potential to be widely used. However, the importance of the specific findings (e.g., the proportion of methyl-minus vs. methyl-plus TFs) is tempered by the methodological concerns raised above.
  - **Interdisciplinary readership** The topic is relevant to molecular biologists, epigenomics researchers, and computational biologists. The methods are likely to be of interest to bioinformaticians. The presentation is generally accessible, though some parts of the methods are dense.
  - **Technical soundness** The core idea is sound, but the manuscript as presented does not provide sufficient detail to fully assess the technical soundness of the implementation. The lack of model specification, diagnostics, and power analysis are significant gaps.
  - **Readability for nonspecialists** The abstract and introduction are clear, but the results section becomes technical. The use of undefined jargon (e.g., "methyl-minus") and the insufficient explanation of the dot plot logos detract from readability for a broader audience.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The manuscript addresses an important problem and presents a potentially valuable resource. However, the current evidence base is insufficient to fully establish the central claims. The authors must provide a complete model specification, validation diagnostics, and a power analysis to address the concerns raised above. The differential binding analysis should also be strengthened by comparing against a reduced model. If these issues are resolved, the work would be a significant contribution to the field.
