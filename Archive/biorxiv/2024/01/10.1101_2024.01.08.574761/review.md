## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, results, discussion, methods) as provided
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, experimental logic, data interpretation, and claims as presented in the provided text; no external data or supplementary figures were available for review
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors propose that Claspin undergoes a phosphorylation-regulated conformational transition between a disordered "closed" state, in which the N-terminal DNA-binding region is masked by an acidic patch (AP)-dependent intramolecular interaction with the C-terminal segment, and a structured "open" state that binds DNA and replication factors. They further propose that this transition is functionally important for DNA replication initiation, with the closed form inhibiting MCM helicase activity and the open form relieving this inhibition.
- **Visible evidence base** Results sections describing DNA-binding assays (Fig. 1A-C), helicase assays (Fig. 1D), pull-down assays (Fig. 2B), trypsin digestion (Fig. 2C), gel-shift assays with truncated polypeptides (Fig. 3B-D), PLA assays (Fig. 4B-C), kinase assays (Fig. 5A-C), and cell-based checkpoint assays (Fig. 7A-B); methods section; discussion
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Supplementary figures (Fig. 1A, 2A, 2B, 3) were referenced but not provided; raw gel images, quantification details, statistical analyses, and experimental replicates were not described; PLA signal quantification methodology was not detailed; structural prediction details for Fig. 8 and Supplementary Fig. 3 were not provided

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript addresses an important and timely question regarding how phosphorylation regulates the structure and function of the intrinsically disordered protein Claspin. The authors present a compelling model in which phosphorylation controls a disordered-to-structured conformational transition that modulates DNA binding, protein interactions, and MCM helicase inhibition. The combination of biochemical assays, PLA-based proximity detection, and cell-based functional assays is commendable. However, several technical concerns and gaps in quantitative analysis limit the strength of the conclusions. The claim that phosphorylation induces a global conformational change from disordered to structured states is inferred primarily from indirect assays (trypsin sensitivity, native gel mobility) and would benefit from direct biophysical validation. The functional significance of the proposed conformational switch for replication initiation remains correlative rather than causal. Overall, the work is potentially significant but requires additional experimental support and clarification before the model can be considered established.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying DNA replication initiation and fork progression, particularly those interested in the regulation of replisome components. The work will also appeal to investigators studying intrinsically disordered proteins and how phosphorylation modulates their conformational ensembles and interactions. The proposed model of a phosphorylation-regulated disordered-to-structured switch may have broader relevance for understanding how disordered proteins function in dynamic cellular processes.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses a fundamental question about how phosphorylation regulates the function of an intrinsically disordered protein. The combination of in vitro biochemical assays with cell-based PLA experiments provides complementary evidence for the proposed intramolecular interaction. The identification of the AP segment as a critical mediator of intramolecular interactions and helicase inhibition is a novel finding. The use of phospho-mimetic and phospho-null mutants in cell-based checkpoint assays links the biochemical observations to functional outcomes.
- **Major Concerns** 
  - R1-M1: The central claim that phosphorylation induces a global disordered-to-structured conformational transition is not directly demonstrated. The evidence relies on indirect assays (trypsin sensitivity, native gel mobility) that could be influenced by factors other than global conformational change, such as local structural stabilization or altered electrostatic interactions.
  - R1-M2: The functional significance of the proposed conformational switch for DNA replication initiation is not causally established. The helicase inhibition data are generated with purified MCM hexamer rather than the CMG complex, and the relevance of this inhibition to initiation in cells is inferred rather than demonstrated.
  - R1-M3: The relationship between phosphorylation state and the proposed open/closed conformations is not clearly defined. The authors show that dephosphorylation enhances DNA binding and that phospho-mimetic mutations impair checkpoint function, but the specific phosphorylation sites responsible for these effects are not identified.
  - R1-M4: The PLA experiments demonstrate proximity between N- and C-terminal tags but do not distinguish between intramolecular and intermolecular interactions. The authors claim intramolecular interaction, but the experimental design does not exclude the possibility of dimerization or aggregation.
  - R1-M5: The quantitative basis for several key conclusions is not provided. DNA-binding affinities, helicase inhibition kinetics, and PLA signal quantification are not presented with statistical analysis.
- **Minor Comments**
  - R1-m1: The authors state that "the wild-type Claspin inhibits the helicase activity of MCM in an AP-dependent manner" but do not provide quantitative data on the extent of inhibition or the concentration dependence.
  - R1-m2: The statement that "ΔAP and dephosphorylated Claspin exhibit resistance to trypsin digestion compared to wild-type" would benefit from quantification of digestion kinetics rather than qualitative band intensity descriptions.
  - R1-m3: The kinase assay results (Fig. 5) are described qualitatively; quantification of phosphorylation levels and statistical comparison between conditions would strengthen the conclusions.
  - R1-m4: The authors mention that "the N-terminal segment #2 of Claspin (1-350aa) is predicted to be structured only at two segments" but do not provide the prediction method or confidence scores.
  - R1-m5: The discussion of the "closed conformation" inhibiting MCM helicase would benefit from a more explicit model of how the intramolecular interaction sterically or allosterically affects helicase activity.
  - R1-m6: The authors should clarify whether the "degron-mutant" and "double-tagged full-length" constructs used in Fig. 1A are properly controlled for expression levels and post-translational modifications.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 (conformational transition not directly demonstrated), R1-M2 (functional significance not causally established), R1-M4 (intramolecular vs. intermolecular interaction not distinguished), R1-M5 (quantitative data lacking)
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - Originality: The proposed phosphorylation-regulated disordered-to-structured conformational switch for Claspin is a novel concept that extends current understanding of how IDP function is regulated. The identification of AP as a key regulatory element is original.
  - Scientific importance: The work addresses a fundamental question in DNA replication regulation and provides a potential mechanism for how Claspin coordinates helicase activity with replisome assembly. The broader implications for IDP regulation by phosphorylation are significant.
  - Interdisciplinary readership: The work will appeal to DNA replication specialists, biochemists studying protein-DNA interactions, and investigators interested in IDP biology. The conceptual framework may attract readers from structural biology and cell cycle research.
  - Technical soundness: The experimental approaches are generally appropriate, but several key conclusions rely on indirect measurements. The lack of direct biophysical characterization of the proposed conformational change and the absence of quantitative analyses weaken the technical foundation.
  - Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is generally well-written and logically organized. However, the discussion of conformational transitions and IDP behavior may be challenging for readers without background in this area. The model figure (Fig. 8) would benefit from clearer annotation.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The proposed model is interesting and potentially important, but the current evidence does not fully establish the conformational transition mechanism or its functional significance. Direct biophysical characterization of the open and closed states, identification of key phosphorylation sites, and quantitative functional assays would substantially strengthen the case.

## Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Conformational transition claim
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that phosphorylation induces a conformational transition from a disordered "closed" state to a structured "open" state, and that this transition is central to Claspin function.
- **Evidence pointer** Results sections "Phosphorylation reduces intra- and inter-molecular interaction by conformational change" and "Potential regulation of DNA binding by interaction between N-terminal and C-terminal domains"; Fig. 2C (trypsin digestion); Fig. 3B-D (native gel mobility)
- **Concern** The evidence for a global disordered-to-structured conformational transition is indirect. Trypsin sensitivity and native gel mobility can be affected by local structural changes, phosphorylation-induced charge alterations, or differential protein stability without a global disorder-to-order transition. The authors do not provide direct biophysical evidence (e.g., circular dichroism, NMR, SAXS, or single-molecule FRET) to support the claim of a large-scale conformational change. The prediction of structured domains in the ST/AA mutant (Supplementary Fig. 3) is computational and not experimentally validated.
- **Why it matters** The central model of the paper rests on the idea that Claspin switches between disordered and structured conformations. If the observed differences in trypsin sensitivity and gel mobility reflect more local or electrostatic effects rather than a global conformational transition, the proposed mechanism would need substantial revision.
- **Resolution test** Provide direct biophysical characterization of wild-type, dephosphorylated, and phospho-mimetic Claspin (e.g., SEC-MALS, SAXS, or NMR) demonstrating differences in global conformation and compactness. Alternatively, use hydrogen-deuterium exchange or limited proteolysis coupled with mass spectrometry to map regions that become structured or protected upon dephosphorylation.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Functional significance
- **Claim pointer** The authors propose that the conformational transition from closed to open state triggers DNA replication initiation by relieving MCM helicase inhibition.
- **Evidence pointer** Results section "Claspin inhibits DNA helicase activity of the Mcm2∼7 hexamer"; Fig. 1D; Discussion section "Cell cycle-dependent phosphorylation may trigger conformational change of Claspin"
- **Concern** The helicase inhibition experiments use the Mcm2∼7 hexamer rather than the CMG complex, which is the functional helicase at the replication fork. The relevance of Mcm2∼7 inhibition to CMG activity is unclear. Furthermore, the connection between the biochemical observations and replication initiation in cells is correlative. The authors show that phospho-mimetic mutants are defective in checkpoint activation, but this does not directly demonstrate that the conformational switch controls initiation.
- **Why it matters** The proposed functional model has significant implications for understanding replication initiation. If the helicase inhibition is not relevant to CMG function or if the conformational switch does not directly regulate initiation, the physiological significance of the findings would be diminished.
- **Resolution test** Test the effect of Claspin and its mutants on CMG helicase activity in vitro. Use cell-based assays to directly measure replication initiation efficiency (e.g., DNA combing or origin firing assays) in cells expressing wild-type versus phospho-mimetic Claspin. Rescue experiments with mutants that separate DNA binding from helicase inhibition would help establish causality.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** No
- **Axis** Phosphorylation site identification
- **Claim pointer** The authors state that phosphorylation inhibits intramolecular interactions and DNA binding, and that dephosphorylation activates these functions, but specific phosphorylation sites responsible for these effects are not identified.
- **Evidence pointer** Results sections "DNA-binding activity of Claspin is inhibited by phosphorylation" and "Phosphorylation reduces intra- and inter-molecular interaction by conformational change"; Fig. 1B-C; Fig. 2B
- **Concern** The manuscript does not identify which phosphorylation sites on Claspin mediate the observed effects. The ST/A and ST/D mutations cover the N-terminal 350 amino acids, but the specific residues that are phosphorylated in cells and that regulate the conformational transition are not mapped. Without this information, it is difficult to assess the physiological relevance of the proposed mechanism or to design experiments to test it in vivo.
- **Why it matters** Identification of specific phosphorylation sites is essential for understanding how Claspin function is regulated by kinases and phosphatases. It would also enable phospho-specific antibodies and site-directed mutants for functional studies in cells.
- **Resolution test** Perform mass spectrometry-based phosphoproteomics on Claspin isolated from cells under different conditions (e.g., untreated, Calyculin A-treated, or after kinase inhibition). Validate candidate sites by site-directed mutagenesis and test their effects on DNA binding, intramolecular interactions, and helicase inhibition.

- **Concern ID** R1-M4
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Intramolecular vs. intermolecular interaction
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that the N-terminal and C-terminal segments of Claspin interact intramolecularly, and that this interaction is dependent on the AP segment.
- **Evidence pointer** Results section "Claspin AP is essential for its intramolecular interaction as measured by PLA"; Fig. 4B-C
- **Concern** The PLA assay detects proximity between two tags but cannot distinguish between intramolecular interactions within the same molecule and intermolecular interactions between two Claspin molecules. Given that Claspin is a large protein with multiple interaction domains, the possibility of dimerization or oligomerization cannot be excluded. The pull-down assays (Fig. 2B) use separate N-terminal and C-terminal polypeptides, which would detect intermolecular interactions between these fragments, not intramolecular interactions within full-length Claspin.
- **Why it matters** The proposed model of a closed conformation depends on an intramolecular interaction between N- and C-terminal segments. If the observed interactions are intermolecular, the model would need to be revised to account for Claspin oligomerization.
- **Resolution test** Perform single-molecule experiments (e.g., single-molecule FRET or optical tweezers) on full-length Claspin to directly demonstrate intramolecular interactions. Alternatively, use cross-linking coupled with mass spectrometry on full-length Claspin to identify intramolecular contacts. Analytical ultracentrifugation or SEC-MALS can determine the oligomeric state of Claspin under different phosphorylation conditions.

- **Concern ID** R1-M5
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** No
- **Axis** Quantitative analysis
- **Claim pointer** The authors make several quantitative claims about DNA binding, helicase inhibition, and phosphorylation effects without providing statistical analysis or quantitative measurements.
- **Evidence pointer** Fig. 1A-D; Fig. 3B-D; Fig. 5A-C; Fig. 7A-B
- **Concern** The manuscript lacks quantitative measurements of DNA binding affinity (e.g., Kd values), helicase inhibition kinetics, and phosphorylation stoichiometry. The gel-shift and helicase assays are presented as representative images without quantification or statistical analysis. The PLA data are shown as representative images without quantitative analysis of signal intensity or statistical comparison between conditions.
- **Why it matters** Quantitative data are essential for assessing the magnitude of the observed effects and for comparing the behavior of different mutants. Without statistical analysis, it is difficult to determine whether the observed differences are significant and reproducible.
- **Resolution test** Provide quantification of at least three independent experiments for key assays, with appropriate statistical tests. For DNA binding, determine apparent Kd values. For helicase assays, quantify the fraction of unwound substrate. For PLA, quantify the number of signals per cell and perform statistical comparison between conditions.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that Claspin undergoes a global disordered-to-structured conformational transition upon dephosphorylation is not directly supported by the data. Indirect evidence from trypsin sensitivity and native gel mobility is suggestive but not conclusive.
- The claim that the closed conformation of Claspin inhibits MCM helicase activity in a manner relevant to replication initiation in cells is not established. The experiments use Mcm2∼7 hexamer rather than the CMG complex, and no direct link to initiation is demonstrated.
- The claim that the N-C interaction masks N-terminal phosphorylation sites and exposes C-terminal phosphorylation sites is based on qualitative kinase assays and requires quantitative validation.
- The functional significance of the proposed conformational switch for checkpoint activation is correlative. The ST/D mutant shows defective checkpoint activation, but the mechanism linking the conformational change to Chk1 phosphorylation is not explored.
- The PLA data are interpreted as evidence for intramolecular interaction, but the experimental design does not exclude intermolecular interactions or protein aggregation.
- The prediction of structured domains in the ST/AA mutant (Supplementary Fig. 3) is computational and not experimentally validated.
