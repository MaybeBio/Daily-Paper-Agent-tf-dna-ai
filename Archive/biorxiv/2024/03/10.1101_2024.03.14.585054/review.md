## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, conclusion) without figures, tables, or supplementary materials
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific validity, methodological soundness, claims versus evidence, and clarity of the reported work
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors propose StrucTFactor, a deep learning method that uses 3D secondary structural information (derived from AlphaFold structures via DSSP) to predict whether a protein is a transcription factor. They claim it is the first such method, and that it significantly outperforms existing sequence-based methods (DeepTFactor, DeepReg) across 12 datasets, with improvements up to 17% in MCC on the most reliable dataset.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract, main text sections 1-7, references to figures (Figures 1-5), tables (Tables 1-3), and supplementary items (Figures S1-S3, Tables S1-S5) that were not provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** All figures, tables, and supplementary materials; exact numerical results per dataset; hyperparameter details for DeepReg; details on the integrated gradients analysis; the full DSSP-to-SSG mapping table

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript addresses a relevant problem in computational biology, namely improving transcription factor prediction by incorporating structural information. The idea of using 3D secondary structure from predicted models is timely given the availability of AlphaFold structures. However, the evidence as presented in the text alone is insufficient to fully evaluate the claims. Key results are referenced in figures and tables that were not provided, and several methodological details are missing. The statistical testing is described but the actual outcomes are not reported in the text. The interpretability analysis is interesting but its presentation is incomplete. The manuscript is clearly written and the motivation is sound, but the core evidence base is not accessible from the supplied material.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Computational biologists working on protein function prediction, transcription factor annotation, and deep learning applications in genomics. Researchers interested in leveraging predicted 3D structures for downstream functional inference would also find this relevant. The work could be of interest to those developing methods for genome annotation and regulatory genomics.
- **Major strengths** The problem is well motivated and the use of predicted 3D structures is a sensible and timely direction. The authors are careful to control for architecture differences by keeping the model similar to DeepTFactor. The evaluation across multiple datasets addressing different bias scenarios is thorough in design. The interpretability analysis using integrated gradients is a valuable addition that goes beyond simple performance comparison.
- **Major Concerns** 
  - R1-M1: The central claim of significant outperformance is not verifiable from the text alone. All numerical results are in figures and tables that were not provided.
  - R1-M2: The comparison with DeepReg may be confounded by hyperparameter choices. The authors state they use recommended values, but no details are given for DeepReg's architecture or training.
  - R1-M3: The interpretability analysis is described but the actual findings are not reported in the text. The claim that StrucTFactor focuses more on DBD regions is not supported by any numbers or statistical test in the visible text.
  - R1-M4: The choice of DSSP-derived secondary structure as the structural feature is not justified in depth. The authors do not discuss why secondary structure, rather than tertiary structure or other structural descriptors, would be most informative for TF prediction.
- **Minor Comments** 
  - R1-m1: The abstract states "p-value < 0.001" for the main comparison, but the text later reports p < 0.005 for the Wilcoxon test in Section 3.2. This inconsistency should be clarified.
  - R1-m2: The description of the DSSP-to-SSG mapping in Table 1 is referenced but the table is not provided. The mapping itself should be described in the text.
  - R1-m3: The authors mention that TFpredict was outperformed by DeepTFactor, but they do not include TFpredict in their comparison. A brief justification for excluding it beyond "not recent" would strengthen the rationale.
  - R1-m4: The conclusion states that StrucTFactor "could also be used to identify DBDs of a novel TF," but this is speculative and not directly tested. This should be clearly labeled as a potential application rather than a demonstrated capability.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** The absence of all figures, tables, and supplementary materials means the core quantitative evidence cannot be assessed. The statistical test results are mentioned but not reported. The interpretability analysis lacks any reported quantitative outcome. These are not technical failings in the work itself but in the completeness of the provided material for review.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - Originality: The use of 3D secondary structure for TF prediction appears novel. However, the incremental advance over sequence-based methods needs to be clearly demonstrated. The originality is moderate.
  - Scientific importance: Improving TF prediction is important for genome annotation and regulatory biology. The potential to identify novel TFs without known DBDs is a meaningful contribution. The importance is moderate to high.
  - Interdisciplinary readership: The work sits at the intersection of structural biology, machine learning, and genomics. It could appeal to a broad readership if the methods and results are presented accessibly. The current text is reasonably accessible but assumes familiarity with deep learning and protein structure concepts.
  - Technical soundness: The experimental design is thoughtful, with attention to data leakage and class imbalance. However, the lack of visible results prevents a full assessment of technical soundness. The choice of DSSP features is reasonable but not deeply justified.
  - Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is clearly written and the logic is easy to follow. However, some terms (e.g., DSSP, one-hot encoding, integrated gradients) are used without brief explanations that would help nonspecialists.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The core idea is promising and the experimental design is careful, but the evidence base must be fully visible and the statistical claims must be substantiated with reported numbers. The interpretability analysis needs to be reported with actual outcomes. If these are addressed, the work could be a solid contribution.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that StrucTFactor is the first method to use 3D secondary structural information for TF prediction is not verifiable from the text alone, as no comprehensive literature search is presented.
- The claim of significant outperformance (p < 0.001) is not supported by any reported numbers in the visible text.
- The claim that StrucTFactor captures DBD regions better than DeepTFactor is not supported by any reported quantitative outcome in the visible text.
- The statement that DeepReg's performance is "close to random" for some datasets is not supported by reported values.
- The claim that the use of 3D secondary structure "potentially explains StrucTFactor's superiority" is speculative and not directly tested.
- The suggestion that StrucTFactor could be used to identify DBDs of novel TFs is not demonstrated and should be framed as a hypothesis.
