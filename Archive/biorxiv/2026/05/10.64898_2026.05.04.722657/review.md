## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript addresses a genuine gap in the literature by determining the molecular structures of two widely used commercial nucleic acid dyes, RiboGreen and OliGreen, whose structures were previously unknown. The combination of NMR spectroscopy and high-resolution mass spectrometry is appropriate for this task, and the confirmation of the previously proposed PicoGreen structure adds internal validation. The photophysical comparisons and binding affinity measurements are useful additions. However, the manuscript's central claims regarding the DNA binding mode of RG rest on a limited set of experimental observations (NMR on a single 10-mer duplex) and simulation data that cannot be fully evaluated from the provided text. The claim that the binding mode is "predominantly" electrostatic and π-π stacking, and the proposal of a "dynamic encapsulation" mode, require stronger experimental support. The manuscript is technically sound in its structural determination aspects but is weaker in the functional and mechanistic sections. The writing is generally clear, though some sections would benefit from additional detail for nonspecialist readers.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in nucleic acid biochemistry, biophysics, and analytical chemistry who use fluorescent dyes for nucleic acid detection, quantification, and imaging. The structural information will be of direct value to those developing assays or interpreting fluorescence data with these commercial dyes. The binding mode analysis may interest computational chemists and biophysicists studying dye-nucleic acid interactions. The NMR chemical shift reference data will be useful for spectroscopists working with unsymmetric cyanine dyes.
- **Major strengths** 1. The de novo structural determination of RG and OG fills a clear and important knowledge gap for widely used commercial reagents. 2. The combination of NMR and ultra-high resolution MS with MS/MS fragmentation provides a robust approach for structural elucidation. 3. The confirmation of the previously proposed PG structure provides internal validation of the methodology. 4. The complete NMR chemical shift assignments expand the reference data set for this dye class. 5. The photophysical comparison across four nucleic acid substrate types is systematic and informative.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness of binding mode claims
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims that NMR spectroscopy and long-timescale MD simulations show that RG interacts with dsDNA predominantly by two binding modes: electrostatic interactions with the phosphodiester backbone and π-π stacking with accessible nucleobases.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results and Discussion, "DNA Binding Mode of RG and OG"; Figure 5; Figure 6; Materials and Methods, "NMR Spectroscopy" and "Molecular Dynamics Simulations"
  - **Concern** The NMR evidence for the binding mode consists of observations of new imino proton resonances upon addition of RG to a 10-bp duplex, with the authors noting the appearance of resonances in chemical shift ranges characteristic of terminal AT and GC base pairs. This is interpreted as indicating multiple binding sites and a complex binding mode. However, no intermolecular NOEs between the dye and DNA are reported, which would be the definitive NMR evidence for a specific binding mode. The chemical shift changes alone are insufficient to distinguish between the proposed binding modes (electrostatic, π-π stacking, terminal capping) and other possibilities such as minor groove binding or partial intercalation. The MD simulations are described as showing "dynamic" sampling of multiple binding modes, but the provided text does not include quantitative details such as free energy differences between binding modes, residence times, or convergence criteria beyond a description of random walk behavior. The claim that the binding is "predominantly" electrostatic and π-π stacking is not quantitatively supported by the data presented.
  - **Why it matters** The binding mode of RG to nucleic acids is a central claim of the manuscript, highlighted in the abstract and conclusions. If the evidence is insufficient to distinguish between competing binding models, the mechanistic framework proposed by the authors is not established. This affects the broader significance of the work, as the authors use this to propose a general model for how unsymmetric cyanine dyes interact with double-stranded nucleic acids.
  - **Resolution test** Provide intermolecular NOE restraints between RG and the DNA duplex to define the binding site(s) experimentally. Alternatively, provide quantitative analysis of the MD simulations, including free energy decomposition by interaction type (electrostatic vs. π-π vs. van der Waals), per-residue contact maps, and a clear statement of the statistical uncertainty in the free energy profile. If the NMR data cannot support a specific binding mode, the claims should be revised to reflect the level of evidence actually available.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness of structural determination
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims de novo determination of the molecular structures of RG and OG based on NMR spectroscopy and ultra-high resolution mass spectrometry.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results and Discussion, "Molecular Structures of RG, OG, and PG"; Figures S1-S24; Materials and Methods, "NMR Spectroscopy" and "Mass Spectrometry"
  - **Concern** The structural determination relies on NMR and MS data presented in supplementary figures that are not provided in the manuscript text. The text states that "established approaches" were used, citing references 37-39, but the specific logic of the structural elucidation is not described. For example, it is not stated whether the structures were determined purely from MS/MS fragmentation patterns, from NMR chemical shifts and coupling patterns, or from a combination. The manuscript does not describe how the regiochemistry of the substituents was established, nor how the connectivity of the unsymmetric cyanine scaffold was determined. The reported m/z values are consistent with the proposed molecular formulas, but MS alone cannot distinguish all possible isomeric structures. The NMR assignments are stated to be complete, but no spectra or assignment tables are shown in the main text.
  - **Why it matters** The structural determination is the primary contribution of the manuscript. If the evidence for the structures is not transparently presented and the logic of the determination is not clear, the central claim cannot be independently evaluated. This is particularly important for commercial dyes where the structures have been proprietary or unknown, and where errors in structural assignment would propagate to all downstream interpretations.
  - **Resolution test** Include the key NMR spectra (or a representative subset) and the MS/MS spectra in the main text or provide a detailed description of the structural elucidation logic in the Results section. Clearly state which spectral features (e.g., specific NOEs, coupling constants, HMBC correlations, fragmentation patterns) were decisive in assigning each structural element. If the structures are unambiguous from the data, this should be demonstrable from the presented evidence.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Scientific importance and interpretation
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims that the photophysical data demonstrate that all three dyes "behave as strongly fluorogenic probes whose signal increases markedly upon binding nucleic acids, with the greatest enhancement observed for double-stranded substrates" and that the differences in marketed applications do not reflect simple, exclusive selectivity for a single nucleic acid class.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results and Discussion, "Photophysical properties of RG, OG, and PG"; Figure 4; Figure S28; Table S2
  - **Concern** The photophysical characterization is presented as a relative comparison of fluorescence intensities across substrate types, but the manuscript does not report quantum yields of the bound states or absolute fluorescence enhancements. The statement that the dyes "perform similarly" is based on a qualitative comparison of fluorescence intensities at a single dye dilution (200-fold from stock). The concentration of the dye stock is not disclosed by the manufacturer, and the authors do not report the actual dye concentrations used in the fluorescence measurements. This limits the quantitative value of the comparison. The claim that the dyes show similar preferences for nucleic acid types is interesting, but the lack of absolute measurements (quantum yields, binding stoichiometry) weakens the interpretation.
  - **Why it matters** The photophysical comparison is used to support the conclusion that the marketed differences between RG, OG, and PG do not reflect fundamental differences in nucleic acid selectivity. If the measurements are not quantitative, this conclusion is not firmly established. The finding that all three dyes behave similarly is potentially important for users of these dyes, but the evidence should be robust.
  - **Resolution test** Report quantum yields of the bound states for each dye on each substrate, or at minimum report the dye concentrations used and the absolute fluorescence intensities. If quantum yield measurements are not feasible, the claims should be tempered to reflect the qualitative nature of the comparison.

  - **Issue** The connection between the MD simulation observations and the previously published differential scanning fluorimetry (DSF) data on RNA is made in one sentence. The relevance of the DSF data to the DNA binding mode is not fully explained. The authors state that the DSF profiles of RNA in the presence of RG show increased fluorescence before the melting temperature, which they interpret as evidence for binding to partially denatured regions. This is an interesting hypothesis, but the link between the DSF data and the MD simulations is not developed.
  - **Required correction** Expand the discussion of how the DSF data support the proposed binding model, or clarify that the DSF data are suggestive but not conclusive evidence for the "dynamic encapsulation" mode.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that RG interacts with dsDNA "predominantly" by electrostatic and π-π stacking interactions is not quantitatively supported by the presented data.
- The proposal of a "dynamic encapsulation" binding mode, described as "to our knowledge previously unknown," is based solely on MD simulations and is not experimentally validated.
- The statement that the dyes "perform similarly" across nucleic acid types is based on qualitative fluorescence intensity comparisons without reported dye concentrations or quantum yields.
- The claim that the DSF data on RNA support the binding model derived from MD simulations on DNA is speculative and not directly supported by the data presented.
- The structural assignments of RG and OG are stated to be complete, but the evidence (spectra, assignment tables) is not provided in the manuscript text and cannot be independently evaluated.
