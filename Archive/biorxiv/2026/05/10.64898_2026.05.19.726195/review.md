## Review setup

- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, results, discussion, methods, and supplementary declarations) as provided in the submission.
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific and technical evaluation of the methodology, claims, and evidence as presented. No assessment of code functionality, reproducibility of the GitHub repository, or verification of database contents beyond what is described.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors introduce GISTnet-MD, a spatiotemporal graph neural network with group-aware contrastive learning, designed to extract replica-invariant dynamic signatures from molecular dynamics trajectories. They claim the method generalizes across independent replicas, outperforms static and end-to-end baselines, and identifies residue-level determinants of function in three test systems: T4-Lysozyme, PDZ3, and the A2A adenosine receptor.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract, Introduction, Results (architecture, convergence analysis, three application case studies), Discussion, Methods (architecture, training protocol, xAI pipeline, MD simulation details), and references to figures and tables (Fig. 1-6, Table 1-2, Extended Data Fig. 2-8, Supplementary Notes 1-4).
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Figure panels and actual data values are not visible in the text-only submission. Extended Data figures, Supplementary Notes, and Table 2 contents are referenced but not provided. Quantitative performance metrics (accuracy values, confidence intervals, statistical tests) are described qualitatively but not numerically in the text. The ablation study results are summarized but individual data points are not shown.

## Reviewer

- **Overall assessment** The manuscript presents a technically sophisticated and well-motivated approach to a genuine problem in computational biophysics: extracting generalizable dynamic signatures from MD ensembles. The architectural choices, particularly the group-aware contrastive learning and the leave-one-replica-out validation, are thoughtful and directly address known pitfalls in MD-based machine learning. The three application systems are well chosen to demonstrate different regimes of dynamic perturbation. However, the evidence as presented in the text is largely qualitative, with key quantitative results relegated to figures and supplementary materials that were not available for review. The masking experiments, while clever, raise questions about circularity and the interpretation of attribution scores that are not fully addressed. The claim of generalizability across unseen data is supported by the validation scheme, but the absence of comparisons to non-deep-learning baselines (e.g., classical mutual information or LDA-based approaches) weakens the case for superiority over existing methods.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Computational biophysicists and structural biologists working on allostery, conformational ensembles, and MD analysis will find the method directly relevant. Researchers developing explainable AI for scientific applications, particularly in chemistry and biology, will be interested in the IG-based attribution pipeline and the masking strategy. The GPCR community, specifically those studying A2A receptor activation, may find the activation signature analysis useful. Methodologists in machine learning for dynamical systems may also be interested in the group-aware contrastive learning formulation.
- **Major strengths** The problem is well defined and important. The group-aware contrastive learning design is a principled solution to replica overfitting, a known and underappreciated issue in MD-ML. The LORO-CV and LOGO-CV validation schemes are rigorous and appropriate for the stated goal of generalization. The masking experiments in PDZ3 and A2A are a creative way to probe what the model actually learns. The choice of three systems with distinct dynamic signatures (localized cavity, distributed allostery, GPCR activation) demonstrates breadth. The discussion of data scaling as a convergence metric is a useful practical contribution.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **R1-M1** (Major, Blocking: Yes): The central quantitative claims are not verifiable from the provided text. Accuracy values, confidence intervals, and statistical significance are described qualitatively ("drops significantly", "reaching a stable plateau", "retained discrete accuracy of approximately 70%") but no numerical data are present in the manuscript text. The figures and tables that would contain these data are referenced but not included in the submission. Without these, the core claim that GISTnet-MD generalizes across replicas and outperforms baselines cannot be assessed.
  - **R1-M2** (Major, Blocking: No): The masking experiments, while creative, raise a concern about circularity. In the PDZ3 case, the model is trained on data where the binding site is removed from the input graph, yet the authors interpret the residual classification accuracy as evidence of "distributed allosteric coupling." An alternative explanation is that the model is exploiting subtle artifacts of the simulation setup (e.g., periodic boundary conditions, solvent distribution, or minor differences in the starting coordinates that persist through the trajectory). The authors do not address this possibility or provide control experiments (e.g., training on trajectories with randomized labels, or on simulations with identical starting coordinates but different random seeds).
  - **R1-M3** (Major, Blocking: No): The comparison to existing methods is incomplete. The authors state that traditional methods (RMSD, RMSF, PCA) fail to separate the ensembles, but they do not compare GISTnet-MD to more sophisticated classical approaches such as mutual information networks, dynamical network analysis, or LDA-based methods. The claim that GISTnet-MD "overcomes" limitations of existing approaches requires a direct comparison to the best available alternatives, not just to simple geometric metrics.
  - **R1-M4** (Major, Blocking: No): The A2A analysis is described in a way that conflates two different comparisons. The agonist-versus-antagonist task starts from different crystal structures, which introduces a large geometric bias that the model could trivially exploit. The agonist-versus-active-apo task is more informative but is described as detecting "early deactivation-associated dynamics" without a clear definition of what "early" means in this context or how the model's temporal resolution relates to the physical timescales of receptor deactivation.
- **Minor Comments**
  - **R1-m1** (Minor, Axis: Clarity): The Methods section contains a duplicated sentence describing the tensor reshaping for the temporal convolution. This should be consolidated.
  - **R1-m2** (Minor, Axis: Reproducibility): The hyperparameter analysis is referenced as Supplementary Note 4, but the specific hyperparameter values used in the final model (beyond hidden dimension D=128) are not stated in the main text. Key parameters such as learning rate, batch size, number of training epochs, and the margin m in the contrastive loss should be stated in the main text or Methods.
  - **R1-m3** (Minor, Axis: Statistical rigor): The IG attribution scores are averaged across cross-validation folds, but no measure of variance or stability across folds is reported. Given that the authors emphasize the importance of replica independence, reporting the standard deviation or confidence intervals of attribution scores across folds would strengthen the claims.
  - **R1-m4** (Minor, Axis: Terminology): The term "binding-ready" is used for T4-Lysozyme, but the L99A mutant is not necessarily "binding-ready" in a functional sense; it has a pre-formed cavity. Consider using "cavity-containing" or "pre-organized" to avoid teleological language.
  - **R1-m5** (Minor, Axis: Completeness): The authors state that the A2A analysis was excluded from the convergence analysis due to limited agonist-bound simulations, but they do not state how many simulations were available. This number should be reported for transparency.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** The absence of quantitative results in the text (R1-M1) is the primary technical failing. The lack of control experiments for the masking procedure (R1-M2) and the absence of comparisons to state-of-the-art classical methods (R1-M3) are secondary but important gaps. The A2A analysis conflates geometric and dynamic differences (R1-M4), which undermines the interpretation of the results for that system.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: The combination of group-aware contrastive learning with spatiotemporal GNNs for MD analysis is novel and addresses a real methodological gap. The masking strategy for probing allosteric networks is also original. Scientific importance: The problem of extracting generalizable dynamic signatures from MD is of broad importance in computational biology and drug discovery. If the claims hold, the method could become a standard tool. Interdisciplinary readership: The work bridges machine learning, biophysics, and structural biology, and is written in a way that is largely accessible to all three communities, though some ML terminology may be challenging for experimentalists. Technical soundness: The architecture and training protocol are well motivated and the validation scheme is appropriate, but the lack of quantitative evidence in the text prevents a full assessment. Readability for nonspecialists: The Introduction and Discussion are clear and well written. The Methods section is dense but appropriate for a specialist audience. Overall, the manuscript has the potential to meet Nature-level standards, but the evidence as presented is insufficient to establish the case.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that GISTnet-MD "generalizes across unseen data" is supported by the validation scheme but not by quantitative evidence in the text.
- The claim that the model "recovers" the gating mechanism in T4-Lysozyme is plausible but relies on the interpretation of IG scores, which are not validated against any ground truth beyond literature agreement.
- The claim that the PDZ3 masked model identifies "distributed allosteric coupling" is not supported by control experiments ruling out artifact-based classification.
- The claim that the A2A analysis isolates "ligand-independent activation signature" is confounded by the use of different starting structures for agonist and antagonist simulations.
- The convergence analysis (Fig. 3) is described qualitatively; the specific accuracy values and variance across folds are not provided.
- The statement that "standard analytical methods" fail to separate the ensembles is based on the authors' analysis, but the specific metrics and thresholds used to reach this conclusion are not reported.
