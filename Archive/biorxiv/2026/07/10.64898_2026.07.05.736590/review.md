## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, results, discussion, methods, supplementary figure/table legends as referenced)
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific validity, technical soundness, and significance of the claims as presented in the provided manuscript text. No independent experimental verification performed.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report the synthesis of circular protein-DNA chimeras of ubiquitin at NMR scale, and use NMR spectroscopy to show that tethering single-stranded DNA to two surface sites shifts the conformational equilibrium of ubiquitin's C-terminal β5 strand toward a retracted state. They propose that local protein-DNA interactions, rather than spring-like mechanical force, dominate this effect.
- **Visible evidence base** NMR chemical shift assignments (BMRB 53195, 53189, 53191), HSQC spectra, 15N-edited NOESY data, MICS secondary structure propensity and order parameter calculations, gel electrophoresis data, SEC chromatograms (referenced as Supplementary Figures S1-S10, Tables S1-S2)
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full spectra figures (only described in text), quantitative chemical shift perturbation values, NOE peak intensity ratios, MICS output tables, detailed synthesis characterization data (e.g., mass spectrometry of conjugates), and the actual Supplementary Figures and Tables are not provided in the submitted text. This limits independent verification of several quantitative claims.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript presents a technically demanding and potentially valuable methodological advance: the production of NMR-scale circular protein-DNA chimeras. The authors demonstrate that this is feasible and that the resulting constructs are tractable for detailed NMR study. The key scientific claim, that ssDNA tethering shifts the β5 strand equilibrium toward a retracted state, is supported by multiple independent NMR observables (chemical shifts, NOEs, secondary structure propensities). However, the manuscript falls short of establishing the mechanistic interpretation that local protein-DNA interactions dominate over spring-like forces, as the control experiments are incomplete and the quantitative analysis is not fully presented. The work is likely of interest to the protein biophysics and NMR communities, but the significance for a broad readership is not yet clearly established.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in protein biophysics, NMR spectroscopy, and single-molecule mechanics. The methodological advance (NMR-scale circular chimeras) will be of direct interest to groups working on DNA springs or other mechanical perturbation approaches. The specific finding regarding ubiquitin's β5 strand equilibrium may interest those studying mitophagy and PINK1 signaling, though the connection to biology is currently indirect.
- **Major strengths** 1) The synthesis and purification of NMR-scale circular protein-DNA chimeras is a significant technical achievement, addressing a clear gap in the field. 2) The use of multiple complementary NMR observables (chemical shifts, NOEs, MICS-derived parameters) to probe conformational changes is rigorous. 3) The authors are appropriately cautious in some interpretations, acknowledging the coexistence of conformers rather than a full transition. 4) The control experiment with two separate short ssDNA strands is a thoughtful design to test the spring hypothesis.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency for mechanistic claim
  - **Claim pointer** "These results provide a necessary benchmark for future studies using DNA springs to probe the functional dynamics of proteins" and "local interactions, possibly complemented by the non-trivial persistence length of ssDNA, can substantially influence the outcomes of using DNA springs"
  - **Evidence pointer** Results, Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) spectroscopy; Discussion
  - **Concern** The central mechanistic conclusion, that local protein-DNA interactions rather than spring-like forces dominate the observed effect, is not adequately supported. The control experiment with two separate short ssDNA strands (ssDNA(s)-Ub) is mentioned only in passing (Figure S9) and its NMR data are not described in the main text. To distinguish local interactions from spring mechanics, the authors need to present a quantitative comparison of the conformational effects of the circular chimera versus the two-strand control, ideally with the same total DNA length and attachment chemistry. Without this, the claim that "attachment of a single strand of DNA suffices to alter this conformational equilibrium" could be explained by simple steric or electrostatic effects of any DNA tether, regardless of circularity.
  - **Why it matters** The stated purpose of the manuscript is to establish a benchmark for using DNA springs to probe protein mechanics. If the observed effects are dominated by local interactions rather than mechanical force, the interpretation of all future studies using this system will be affected. The current evidence does not allow readers to distinguish these possibilities.
  - **Resolution test** Provide a quantitative comparison of chemical shift perturbations, MICS-derived parameters, and NOE patterns between the circular ssDNA-Ub chimera and the two-strand control (ssDNA(s)-Ub), with statistical analysis. If the two constructs produce similar effects, the spring hypothesis is weakened. If they differ substantially, the role of circularity and spring action can be assessed. Additionally, a construct with a longer or stiffer DNA linker would help separate local from mechanical effects.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Quantitative evidence for conformational shift
  - **Claim pointer** "We found that attachment of ssDNA, at positions 9 and 63, strongly affected the central β-sheet of ubiquitin and shifted the C-terminal β5 strand's conformational equilibrium towards a retracted state"
  - **Evidence pointer** Results, Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) spectroscopy; Figure 6; Figure 7; Table S2
  - **Concern** The claim that the β5 strand equilibrium is shifted toward the retracted state is based on qualitative observations of chemical shift changes, increased β-strand propensity in residues 71-75, and a limited set of NOEs. However, no quantitative population analysis is presented. The authors state "we lack quantitative data to determine the relative populations of these states," which is a significant limitation. The NOE data (Figure 7, Table S2) are described as showing "patterns consistent with both" conformers, but the actual peak intensities and distances are not provided in the text. Without quantitative population estimates or at least a clear demonstration that the retracted state population is substantially increased relative to apo-Ub, the central claim remains suggestive rather than established.
  - **Why it matters** The biological significance of the finding depends on the magnitude of the population shift. A shift from <1% to a few percent would be mechanistically interesting but biologically negligible. The manuscript does not provide the data needed to assess this.
  - **Resolution test** Provide quantitative population analysis using, for example, CPMG relaxation dispersion or CEST experiments to directly measure the population of the retracted state in apo-Ub versus ssDNA-Ub. Alternatively, provide a quantitative analysis of the NOE data with distance restraints and structural modeling to estimate the relative populations. At minimum, provide the chemical shift perturbation values in tabular form with significance thresholds.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Completeness of methodological validation
  - **Claim pointer** "we describe the NMR-scale synthesis of circular chimeras of single-and double-stranded DNA with ubiquitin" and "The resulting samples contained circular U-[13C,15N] protein-DNA chimeras at a quantity (500 µL of 150-300 µM) and quality (>95% pure; Fig 3B) sufficient for NMR studies"
  - **Evidence pointer** Results, Protein Biochemistry; Figure 3; Figure 4; Methods
  - **Concern** The manuscript claims successful synthesis of both ssDNA-Ub and dsDNA-Ub chimeras, but the characterization data for the dsDNA construct are limited. The text states that complementary strand addition was confirmed by 1D 1H NMR of imino protons (Figure S6), but no data are shown for the stoichiometry of the hybridized complex, the melting temperature, or the stability of the dsDNA-Ub construct under NMR conditions. Additionally, the yield and purity data are only presented for the ssDNA-Ub construct. For a methods-focused manuscript, this level of characterization is insufficient.
  - **Why it matters** The dsDNA-Ub construct is a key part of the study, and its proper formation and stability are essential for interpreting the NMR data. If the dsDNA construct is partially dissociated or improperly formed, the conclusions drawn from its comparison to ssDNA-Ub would be compromised.
  - **Resolution test** Provide additional characterization of the dsDNA-Ub construct, including native gel electrophoresis, size exclusion chromatography with multi-angle light scattering, or analytical ultracentrifugation to confirm the 1:1 stoichiometry of the complex. Include melting curves or temperature-dependent imino proton spectra to demonstrate stability under the NMR experimental conditions.

  - **Concern ID** R1-m1
  - **Severity** Minor

  - **Affected element** Figure 6 and associated text
  - **Evidence pointer** Results, Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) spectroscopy
  - **Issue** The text states "The 1H N and 15N chemical shift differences we observed between these sets established that ssDNA binding affected sites throughout the protein" but does not provide a quantitative threshold for what constitutes a significant chemical shift perturbation.   - **Concern ID** R1-m2
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Completeness of methods
  - **Affected element** MICS calculations
  - **Evidence pointer** Methods, Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) spectroscopy
  - **Issue** The MICS calculations are described in a single sentence without details on the input data, parameters, or validation. Given that MICS-derived secondary structure propensities and order parameters are used to support the central claim, the methods should be described in sufficient detail for reproduction.
  - **Required correction** Expand the methods section to include the specific MICS version, input chemical shifts used, any reference corrections, and the criteria for interpreting the output. Consider including a validation against the apo-Ub structure.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m3
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Interpretation of NOE data
  - **Affected element** Figure 7 and Table S2
  - **Evidence pointer** Results, Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) spectroscopy
  - **Issue** The text states that some observed NOEs "did not have distances under ~5 Å in either structure" and the authors "favor the former interpretation" (conformational dynamics). However, spin diffusion at 125 ms mixing time is a well-known artifact in NOE experiments and could also explain these observations. The authors should address this possibility more directly.
  - **Required correction** Discuss the potential contribution of spin diffusion to the observed NOEs and, if possible, include a control experiment with a shorter mixing time or a quantitative analysis of NOE buildup rates to distinguish genuine long-range contacts from spin diffusion artifacts.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m4
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Biological relevance
  - **Affected element** Introduction and Discussion
  - **Evidence pointer** Introduction; Discussion
  - **Issue** The connection to mitophagy and PINK1 signaling is mentioned in the Introduction but not developed in the Discussion. The authors do not discuss whether the population shift they observe (if confirmed) would be sufficient to affect PINK1 phosphorylation or downstream signaling.
  - **Required correction** Add a brief discussion of the potential biological implications of the observed population shift, including estimates of whether the magnitude of the effect would be relevant for PINK1 phosphorylation kinetics.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** 1) The quantitative comparison between the circular chimera and the two-strand control is missing, which is essential to support the local-interaction hypothesis. 2) The population of the retracted state is not quantified, leaving the central claim at a qualitative level. 3) The dsDNA-Ub construct is insufficiently characterized to ensure its integrity under experimental conditions. 4) The MICS methodology is under-described, preventing assessment of the reliability of the secondary structure and dynamics predictions.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: The work is original in its methodological approach, combining circular protein-DNA chimera synthesis with NMR-scale analysis. This combination has not been previously demonstrated. Scientific importance: The importance is moderate. The work establishes a technical benchmark but does not yet provide a major biological or mechanistic insight. The finding that ssDNA tethering affects ubiquitin's conformational equilibrium is interesting but the mechanism and magnitude are not fully resolved. Interdisciplinary readership: The work will appeal primarily to biophysicists and NMR spectroscopists. The connection to cell biology (mitophagy) is too underdeveloped to attract a broader audience. Technical soundness: The synthesis and NMR methods appear sound, but the quantitative analysis is incomplete, and the control experiments are insufficient to support the mechanistic claims. Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is clearly written but assumes significant background in NMR and protein biophysics. The significance statement helps, but the technical detail in the Results section may be challenging for nonspecialists.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The methodological advance is real and valuable, and the NMR data are suggestive of an interesting effect. However, the central mechanistic claim (local interactions dominate over spring forces) is not established from the provided evidence, and the magnitude of the conformational shift is not quantified. These issues must be addressed before the case is fully made.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that "local interactions, possibly complemented by the non-trivial persistence length of ssDNA, can substantially influence the outcomes of using DNA springs" is not supported by the presented control experiments, as the two-strand control data are not quantitatively compared.
- The claim that the β5 strand equilibrium is "shifted towards a retracted state" is supported only qualitatively; no population estimates are provided.
- The statement that "attachment of a single strand of DNA suffices to alter this conformational equilibrium" is not quantitatively established, as the magnitude of the effect is not reported.
- The assertion that the observed NOEs "strongly suggest that both conformers exist in the sample" is weakened by the lack of quantitative analysis and the potential for spin diffusion artifacts.
- The claim that the synthesis approach "does not scale well" for the alternative strategy (attributed to prior work) is presented without direct comparison data in this manuscript.
