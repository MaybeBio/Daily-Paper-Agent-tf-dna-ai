## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript addresses a timely and important question in computational biology: understanding when and why multimodal protein foundation models succeed or fail in distinguishing allosteric from orthosteric binding sites. The proposed framework is well-structured and the central claim, that dataset properties dominate over architectural choices, is potentially significant. However, the manuscript as provided is incomplete, with key results and figures missing. The conclusions are stated with high confidence, but the visible evidence base is insufficient to fully evaluate the strength of the claims. The work is likely of interest to the computational biology and drug discovery communities, but the current presentation does not allow for a full assessment of its validity.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in computational biology, structural bioinformatics, and drug discovery, particularly those working on binding site prediction, protein function annotation, and the application of foundation models to biological problems. The framework for diagnosing model performance across different data regimes is broadly applicable and could inform the design of future benchmarks and model selection strategies.
- **Major strengths**
    1.  The central question is well-posed and practically relevant.
    2.  The experimental design is thoughtful, separating encoder-level (pretraining) from embedding-level (downstream) contributions.
    3.  The use of variance decomposition to attribute performance to different factors is a rigorous and commendable approach.
    4.  The identification of distinct "separability regimes" provides a useful conceptual framework.
    5.  The interpretability analysis attempts to connect model performance to underlying biological properties.
- **Major Concerns**
    - **R1-M1** (Blocking): The core quantitative results are not visible in the provided text. The manuscript repeatedly references figures and tables (e.g., Figure 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7; Table 3) that contain the actual data supporting the central claims. Without these, the claims of "63.7% of explainable variance" and the existence of distinct regimes cannot be verified. The narrative is clear, but the evidence is absent from the provided material.
    - **R1-M2** (Blocking): The manuscript does not provide a clear definition of the "OneProt" model's architecture or training procedure, referring instead to a previous study. While this is acceptable for a methods section, the claims about the influence of "architectural complexity" are difficult to evaluate without a clear description of what constitutes the different levels of complexity being compared. The text mentions "encoder architectures that differ in the modalities incorporated during pretraining" but does not detail the specific differences in model capacity or design.
    - **R1-M3** (Blocking): The interpretability analysis is described in qualitative terms. The text states that "prediction success reflects the underlying biological organization of each regime" and provides examples (e.g., Rossmann fold in PPI-Site, Type III sites in KinSite), but the specific structural or functional features that were identified as important are not presented. The link between the model's decisions and the biological features is asserted, not demonstrated with data.
- **Minor Comments**
    - **R1-m1** (Axis: Clarity): The abstract and introduction are well-written and accessible. However, the methods section is dense and would benefit from a more explicit statement of the hypothesis being tested at each stage of the framework.
    - **R1-m2** (Axis: Reproducibility): The data availability statement is exemplary. However, the manuscript would be strengthened by a more detailed description of the hyperparameter search for the MLP classifier, as this is a common source of variability in such studies.
    - **R1-m3** (Axis: Presentation): The use of "ρ" for class imbalance ratio is not defined in the main text. It is used in the methods section but its definition should be stated clearly at first use.
    - **R1-m4** (Axis: Completeness): The discussion of the "diminishing returns" of architectural complexity is interesting but could be more nuanced. The text states that "architectural complexity provides diminishing returns once sufficient biological signal is present," but it is unclear if this is a statement about the specific models tested or a general principle.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established**
    - The primary technical failing is the lack of visible data. The central claims are entirely dependent on the results shown in the missing figures and tables. The manuscript cannot be evaluated for technical soundness without this information.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**
    - **Originality**: The framework is a novel contribution. While individual components (ablation studies, variance decomposition) are standard, their combination into a diagnostic framework for multimodal foundation models is original.
    - **Scientific importance**: The question of when and why multimodal models work is of high importance to the field. The claim that dataset properties are the primary driver of performance is a significant one that could influence future research directions.
    - **Interdisciplinary readership**: The topic is of interest to a broad audience in computational biology, structural biology, and drug discovery. The writing is generally accessible, though the methods section is technical.
    - **Technical soundness**: Cannot be fully assessed from the provided material. The experimental design is sound, but the results are not visible.
    - **Readability for nonspecialists**: The abstract and introduction are clear. The results and discussion are more technical but still largely understandable. The manuscript is well-written.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The manuscript presents a compelling narrative and a well-designed framework, but the core results are not visible in the provided text. A full review is contingent on access to the complete manuscript, including all figures, tables, and supporting information. If the data support the claims, this could be a valuable contribution. However, as presented, the evidence is insufficient to support the strong conclusions drawn.
