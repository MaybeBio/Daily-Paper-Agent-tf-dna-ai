## Proteome-scale inference of transcription factor binding motifs from predicted protein-DNA structures


## Abstract

Transcription factors (TFs) control development and cell identity by binding sequence-specific DNA motifs, yet the binding specificities of most TFs remain unknown because experimental binding assays do not scale. In zebrafish, only one-third of TFs have assigned motifs, nearly all inferred by homology from human or mouse experimental data. Here we present a proteome-scale computational screen that infers DNA-binding specificity from predicted protein-DNA structures. We use AlphaFold3 to model zebrafish TFs with candidate motif sequences derived from accessible chromatin, then use NA-MPNN to infer the DNA motifs from the most confident predictions. Across 210 TFs with experimentally characterized orthologs, predicted motifs match the known motif for 44%. Propagated through sequence clusters, these predictions form ZebraMotif, a motif database for all 2,543 zebrafish TFs. ZebraMotif covers 2.8 times as many TFs as CisBP's zebrafish collection and links accessible regulatory elements to candidate upstream regulators genome-wide. Because the approach requires only a proteome and a set of candidate motifs, it is applicable to any species lacking experimental binding data.
