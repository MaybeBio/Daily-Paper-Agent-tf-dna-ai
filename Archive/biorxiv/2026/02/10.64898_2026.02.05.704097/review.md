The identification of a second DNA-binding site and the demonstration of DNA-stimulated ATPase activity are mechanistically interesting. The combination of structural, biochemical, and in vivo approaches is commendable. The demonstration that ATP hydrolysis is essential for SMCHD1 function in a mouse model provides physiological relevance.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Technical soundness  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim to present the first high-resolution structure of the wild-type human SMCHD1 ATPase using cryo-EM.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract states that a high-resolution cryo-EM structure was obtained, but provides no information on resolution, model quality, or validation metrics. Without these details, the structural claim cannot be evaluated.  
- **Why it matters** A high-resolution structure is a central claim of the work. If the resolution or model quality is insufficient, the structural interpretations regarding DNA binding and ATPase regulation would be compromised.  
- **Resolution test** Provide the cryo-EM resolution, map-to-model FSC curves, and validation statistics in the full manuscript.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Technical soundness  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that DNA binding at the BAH-IGL-1 domains stimulates the ATPase activity of full-length SMCHD1 in vitro.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract reports stimulation of ATPase activity but does not provide quantitative data, such as fold stimulation, concentration dependence, or comparison to the hinge-domain DNA-binding site. The specificity of the stimulation for the BAH-IGL-1 site versus the previously identified hinge domain is unclear.  
- **Why it matters** The claim of DNA-stimulated ATPase activity is a key mechanistic finding. Without quantitative data and appropriate controls, it is not possible to assess whether the effect is specific, robust, or physiologically relevant.  
- **Resolution test** Provide ATPase activity measurements with DNA substrates, mutant controls lacking the BAH-IGL-1 site, and dose-response data in the full manuscript.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Technical soundness  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim to demonstrate in a mouse model that ATP hydrolysis is essential for SMCHD1 function in vivo.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract does not describe the nature of the mouse model, the specific mutation used to ablate ATP hydrolysis, or the phenotypic readout. It is unclear whether the mutation affects ATP binding, hydrolysis, or both, and whether the phenotype is specific to SMCHD1 function.  
- **Why it matters** The in vivo claim is essential for establishing physiological relevance. Without details on the model and the specificity of the ATPase mutation, the conclusion that ATP hydrolysis is essential for SMCHD1 function cannot be verified.  
- **Resolution test** Provide details of the mouse model, the mutation strategy, and the phenotypic assays in the full manuscript.

- **Concern ID** R1-M4  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Scientific importance  
- **Claim pointer** The authors conclude that bidentate DNA binding and DNA-stimulated ATP hydrolysis are central features of SMCHD1 function.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract does not establish that the two DNA-binding sites function together in a coordinated manner. The term bidentate implies simultaneous engagement, but no evidence is presented for cooperative or simultaneous binding.  
- **Why it matters** The central conclusion of the work rests on the bidentate model. If the two sites do not function cooperatively, the proposed mechanism would need to be revised.  
- **Resolution test** Provide binding assays demonstrating simultaneous or cooperative DNA binding by the two sites in the full manuscript.
