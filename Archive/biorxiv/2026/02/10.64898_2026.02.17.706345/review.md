## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text including abstract, introduction, results, methods, and supplementary figure legends
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, methodological rigor, data interpretation, and claims as presented in the provided manuscript
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors investigate how charge, hydrophobicity, and cooperativity influence the target search dynamics of the pluripotency transcription factors SOX2 and ESRRB in mouse embryonic stem cells. They generate hydrophobic and negatively charged variants of these TFs, combine single-molecule tracking with ChIP-seq, and report that increased hydrophobicity impairs specific binding while promoting confined intranuclear interactions, that additional negative charges decrease sampling of weak binding sites, and that SOX2 facilitates ESRRB target search by restricting its diffusion and promoting stable DNA binding.
- **Visible evidence base** Results sections on variant characterization, single-molecule tracking (diffusion fractions, coefficients, anisotropy), binding kinetics (survival time distributions, GRID analysis), ChIP-seq occupancy comparisons, and cooperative binding analyses; methods sections for cell culture, plasmid construction, lentiviral production, imaging, and data analysis
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Supplementary figures and tables referenced throughout (Figures S1-S10, Tables S1-S7) are not provided; specific statistical test details and effect sizes are not fully reported; raw data and analysis code are not available; details on the Alphafold 3 modelling parameters and validation are incomplete

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript addresses a timely and important question in transcription factor biology: how intrinsic biophysical properties and cooperative interactions shape target search in the nucleus. The combination of engineered TF variants, single-molecule imaging, and genomic analyses is conceptually strong and the findings are potentially significant. However, several technical and interpretive concerns need to be addressed before the conclusions are fully supported. The claim that hydrophobicity destabilizes long binding events while negative charges increase transient interaction residence times is interesting but requires more rigorous statistical support and consideration of alternative explanations. The cooperative relationship between SOX2 and ESRRB is a highlight, but the causal direction and mechanism remain incompletely resolved.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in transcription factor dynamics, single-molecule biophysics, chromatin biology, and stem cell biology would find these results valuable. The work speaks to fundamental mechanisms of target search and binding specificity, with implications for understanding how TF properties and cooperativity shape gene regulatory networks. The methodological approach of perturbing biophysical properties while preserving native sequences could be of broad interest to the biophysics community.
- **Major Strengths** The combination of engineered variants with single-molecule imaging and genomics is innovative and well-suited to the question. The use of GRID analysis to resolve binding states is a sophisticated approach. The finding that SOX2 and ESRRB exhibit distinct search strategies and that SOX2 plays a dominant cooperative role is novel and mechanistically interesting. The authors are careful to note limitations, such as the inability to express the ESRRBneg variant.
- **Major Concerns** 
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that increased hydrophobicity impairs specific binding while promoting confined intranuclear interactions, and that this is due to the hydrophobic properties themselves.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "TF hydrophobicity promotes slow and constrained diffusion"; Figures 2C-F, S4B-C
  - **Concern** The hydrophobic variants are generated by adding 18 hydrophobic amino acids to the termini. This is a substantial perturbation that could affect protein folding, stability, or interactions with chaperones or other proteins, independent of the intended hydrophobicity effect. The Alphafold 3 modelling is used to argue that folding and DNA binding are unaltered, but this is a computational prediction and not experimentally validated. The authors do not provide evidence that the added hydrophobic stretch does not create new protein-protein interaction surfaces or cause aggregation. The observed increase in bound fraction and confinement could be due to non-specific hydrophobic interactions with chromatin or other nuclear components rather than a specific effect on search dynamics.
  - **Why it matters** The central conclusion that hydrophobicity modulates search behavior depends on the assumption that the only effect of the added residues is to increase hydrophobicity. If the variants have altered folding, stability, or novel interactions, the interpretation is compromised.
  - **Resolution test** Provide experimental validation of variant folding and stability, such as circular dichroism, thermal stability assays, or limited proteolysis. Alternatively, use a more conservative perturbation, such as point mutations that increase hydrophobicity at specific positions, and show consistent results. Demonstrate that the added hydrophobic stretch does not cause aggregation or novel protein interactions, for example by size-exclusion chromatography or co-immunoprecipitation.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that SOX2 loss decreases ESRRB search efficiency and genome occupancy, and that this indicates SOX2 facilitates ESRRB target search.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "SOX2 loss decreases ESRRB search efficiency and genome occupancy"; Figures 5A-G, S7-S9
  - **Concern** The experiments in the 2TS22C cell line involve SOX2 depletion for 26 hours. This is a long perturbation that could have indirect effects on the pluripotency network, chromatin state, or cell cycle, which could in turn affect ESRRB dynamics. The authors note that ESRRB levels are only 12% lower, but this does not rule out broader transcriptional or epigenetic changes. The claim that SOX2 "reduces ESRRB search time by restricting its nuclear diffusion" implies a direct physical mechanism, but the data are correlative. The increased diffusion speed and reduced bound fraction of ESRRB upon SOX2 loss could be due to changes in chromatin accessibility or nuclear architecture rather than a direct loss of SOX2-ESRRB interaction.
  - **Why it matters** The cooperative relationship between SOX2 and ESRRB is a key finding of the paper. If the observed effects are indirect, the mechanistic conclusion is weakened.
  - **Resolution test** Perform time-course experiments to assess how quickly ESRRB dynamics change after SOX2 depletion. Use acute depletion systems, such as auxin-inducible degron, to minimize indirect effects. Measure chromatin accessibility or histone modifications at ESRRB target sites upon SOX2 depletion to distinguish direct from indirect effects. Perform rescue experiments by re-expressing SOX2 or a SOX2 mutant that cannot interact with ESRRB.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Scientific importance
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that their findings highlight how TF biophysical features and cooperativity jointly shape the efficiency and specificity of target search.
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; Discussion
  - **Concern** The study uses only two TFs, SOX2 and ESRRB, and the variants are engineered with artificial hydrophobic or charged stretches. The physiological relevance of these perturbations is unclear. The authors note that ESRRBwt is already more hydrophobic than SOX2, but the range of hydrophobicity explored may not reflect natural variation. The claim of joint shaping of search efficiency and specificity is broad, but the evidence is limited to a specific context.
  - **Why it matters** The generalizability of the findings to other TFs and to natural regulatory mechanisms is important for the significance of the work.
  - **Resolution test** Discuss how the range of hydrophobicity and charge explored compares to natural variation across TF families. If possible, include additional TFs or natural variants to test generalizability. Acknowledge the limitations of the artificial perturbation approach more explicitly.

- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 and R1-M2 are blocking concerns. The lack of experimental validation for the variant properties and the potential for indirect effects in the SOX2 depletion experiments undermine the central claims. The manuscript cannot be considered to have established its case until these are addressed.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - **Originality** The approach of modulating biophysical properties with short peptide additions is creative, and the combination of SMT with genomics in this context is not routine. The finding of asymmetric cooperativity between SOX2 and ESRRB is novel. However, the conceptual framework of facilitated diffusion and the role of IDRs is well-established, and the incremental advance over prior work needs to be more clearly articulated.
  - **Scientific importance** The question of how TFs find their targets is fundamental and of broad interest. The potential implications for understanding pluripotency networks and TF cooperativity are significant. However, the limited scope (two TFs, artificial variants) tempers the immediate importance.
  - **Interdisciplinary readership** The work bridges biophysics, genomics, and stem cell biology, and would appeal to researchers in all three fields. The methods are sophisticated but the presentation is generally accessible.
  - **Technical soundness** The SMT and GRID analyses appear well-executed, but the lack of validation for the variant properties and the potential for indirect effects in the depletion experiments are significant technical weaknesses. The statistical reporting is incomplete.
  - **Readability for nonspecialists** The manuscript is generally well-written, but the methods and some results sections are dense. The abstract is clear and accessible. The figures are likely to be informative, but the missing legends are a problem.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The core question is important and the approach is promising, but the blocking concerns regarding variant validation and indirect effects must be addressed. The manuscript would be strengthened by additional experiments to validate the variant properties and to distinguish direct from indirect effects of SOX2 depletion.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that increased hydrophobicity impairs specific binding while promoting confined intranuclear interactions is not fully supported without experimental validation of the variant properties.
- The claim that SOX2 reduces ESRRB search time by restricting its nuclear diffusion and facilitating stable DNA binding is correlative and does not establish a direct mechanism.
- The claim that negative charges decrease sampling of weak binding sites is based on a single variant (SOX2neg) and lacks mechanistic detail.
- The claim that ESRRBhydro is redirected to SOX2-bound regions due to impaired search is plausible but not definitively proven; alternative explanations such as altered chromatin interactions are not excluded.
- The generalizability of the findings to other TFs and natural regulatory mechanisms is asserted but not demonstrated.
