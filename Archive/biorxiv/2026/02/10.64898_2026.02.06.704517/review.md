## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, results, discussion, methods, data availability)
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, methodological soundness, internal consistency, and support of claims by presented evidence. No assessment of editorial fit or publication venue.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors propose that the non-motile kinesin KIF7 repurposes conserved kinesin regulatory principles, specifically autoinhibition via intramolecular occlusion of the microtubule-binding interface by the negatively charged neck-coil, relief of autoinhibition upon GLI2 binding, and ATPase-dependent release from microtubules, to support its scaffolding function in Hedgehog signaling.
- **Visible evidence base** HDX-MS data (differential, kinetic, non-EX2 analysis), AlphaFold3 structural models, microtubule-stimulated ATPase assays, TIRF microscopy-based microtubule binding assays, cell-based localization experiments in NIH3T3 and HeLa cells
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** No supplementary data files were provided for inspection. No raw HDX-MS peptide-level data, no replicate-level statistics, no full gel images or representative raw microscopy images, no statistical details for cellular quantifications, no AlphaFold confidence metrics beyond referenced extended data figures, no negative controls for the TIRF binding assay, no validation of the K100A mutant beyond ATPase activity.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript addresses a genuinely interesting question: how a kinesin family member has been repurposed for a non-motile signaling function. The combination of HDX-MS with AlphaFold modeling is appropriate and potentially powerful. However, the evidence as presented does not fully establish the central claims. The HDX-MS data are described qualitatively, with no statistical framework, no replicate-level presentation, and no clear criteria for what constitutes a significant difference. The AlphaFold models are used as structural evidence but are not experimentally validated. The cellular experiments rely on overexpression and lack key controls. The mechanistic model proposed, while plausible, is not uniquely supported by the data presented. Several claims go beyond what the evidence can support, particularly regarding the temporal ordering of events during activation and the allosteric propagation pathway.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying kinesin regulation and motor protein biology, particularly those interested in how motor proteins evolve non-motile functions. The Hedgehog signaling community will be interested in the mechanistic understanding of KIF7 function at the cilium. The methodological combination of HDX-MS with AlphaFold may interest structural biologists studying dynamic protein complexes. The broader cell biology community interested in cilium biology and signal transduction will also find relevance.
- **Major strengths** The question is well-framed and biologically significant. The combination of HDX-MS with AlphaFold modeling is a modern and appropriate approach for studying dynamic conformational states. The use of multiple complementary assays (HDX-MS, ATPase, TIRF, cell-based) is commendable. The comparative analysis with the KIF27 chimera provides a useful control for understanding neck-coil specificity. The rigor mutant approach to probe ATPase function is well-conceived. The writing is clear and the figures are logically organized.
- **Major Concerns** 
  - R1-M1: The HDX-MS data are presented without statistical analysis or clear significance criteria. The manuscript reports differences in deuterium uptake as evidence for conformational changes, but no confidence intervals, replicate-level data, or statistical tests are shown. The reader cannot assess whether the reported differences are robust or within experimental noise.
  - R1-M2: The AlphaFold3 models are treated as structural evidence for the autoinhibited state, but no experimental validation is provided. The claim that the motor domain "folds back onto the neck-coil" is based entirely on computational prediction. The authors themselves note that the isolated domains do not bind in trans, which raises questions about the strength and specificity of this interaction.
  - R1-M3: The mechanistic model proposes a specific sequence of events during activation, but the data are largely correlative and static. The HDX-MS experiments measure equilibrium states, not transitions. The claim that GLI2 binding "displaces" the motor domain implies a temporal ordering that is not directly demonstrated.
  - R1-M4: The cellular experiments rely on overexpression of KIF7 constructs and lack key controls. The quantification of cilium tip localization is described only as percentages with no statistical analysis, no cell counts, and no demonstration that the observed effects are specific to the KIF7-GLI interaction rather than general microtubule sequestration.
  - R1-M5: The ATPase experiments show a three-fold increase in microtubule-stimulated ATPase activity upon GLI2 binding, but the functional significance of this modest change is not established. The connection between this in vitro observation and the cellular phenotype is speculative.
- **Minor Comments** 
  - R1-m1: The abstract states that KIF7 "concentrates GLI transcription factors at the cilium tip" but the manuscript does not directly demonstrate this concentration function; it only shows that KIF7 binds GLI2.
  - R1-m2: The introduction claims that KIF7 has "an intact ATPase pocket" but the functional relevance of ATP binding versus hydrolysis is not distinguished in the experiments.
  - R1-m3: The methods describe experiments performed in duplicate for HDX-MS, which is minimal for this technique. The field typically expects at least triplicate measurements with technical replicates.
  - R1-m4: The comparison between KIF7-NC isolated and KIF7-NC-DM is complicated by the different digestion profiles and coverage. The authors acknowledge this but the interpretation of differences in deuterium incorporation between these constructs should be more cautious.
  - R1-m5: The manuscript would benefit from a clearer statement of what is novel relative to the prior work cited in reference 3, which already established GLI2 binding to the neck-coil and enhanced microtubule binding.
  - R1-m6: The Discussion section introduces the concept of "electrostatic complementarity" as a mechanism for autoinhibition, but this is not directly tested in the experiments.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 (statistical treatment of HDX-MS), R1-M2 (validation of AlphaFold models), R1-M4 (cellular quantification and controls), and the lack of any experimental test of the proposed autoinhibitory interaction between the motor domain and neck-coil.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - Originality: The question of how a kinesin is repurposed for non-motile signaling function is original and interesting. The specific mechanism proposed, involving the neck-coil as an autoinhibitory element, is a novel hypothesis. However, the conceptual framework of autoinhibition and cargo-induced activation is well-established in the kinesin field, and the manuscript does not fully distinguish its findings from prior work on KIF7.
  - Scientific importance: The work has the potential to be important for understanding both kinesin biology and Hedgehog signaling. If the proposed mechanism is correct, it would represent a significant advance. However, the importance is contingent on the evidence being strengthened.
  - Interdisciplinary readership: The topic bridges motor protein biology, signal transduction, and cilia biology, which would appeal to a broad readership. The methodological approach combining HDX-MS with AlphaFold is of general interest.
  - Technical soundness: The HDX-MS methodology appears sound in principle but the presentation lacks statistical rigor. The AlphaFold modeling is used appropriately as a predictive tool but is over-interpreted as structural evidence. The cellular experiments lack quantitative rigor.
  - Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is generally well-written and accessible. The figures are logically organized. However, the HDX-MS results section assumes familiarity with the technique and its interpretation.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The core question is interesting and the approach is appropriate, but the evidence as presented does not fully establish the mechanistic claims. The manuscript would be substantially strengthened by statistical treatment of HDX-MS data, experimental validation of the proposed autoinhibitory interaction, and more rigorous cellular quantification.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that the motor domain folds back onto the neck-coil in the autoinhibited state is based solely on AlphaFold predictions and is not experimentally validated. The in trans binding experiment showing no interaction between isolated domains actually weakens this claim.
- The claim that GLI2 binding "displaces" the motor domain from the neck-coil implies a competitive mechanism that is not directly demonstrated. The data show correlated changes, not displacement.
- The claim that ATP hydrolysis enables "dynamic cycling" of KIF7 on and off microtubules is inferred from the K100A mutant phenotype but the dynamic behavior is not directly measured.
- The claim that the neck-coil is "repurposed as an autoinhibitory docking site" is presented as a conclusion but is more accurately a hypothesis consistent with the data.
- The statement that KIF7 "concentrates GLI transcription factors at the cilium tip" is presented as established fact in the abstract but is not directly demonstrated in this manuscript.
- The claim that the KIF7-NL helix forms a "helical bundle" upon activation is based on AlphaFold predictions and HDX changes, but the structural interpretation is speculative.
- The functional significance of the three-fold increase in ATPase activity upon GLI2 binding is not established; the manuscript does not demonstrate that this change is necessary or sufficient for the observed cellular effects.
