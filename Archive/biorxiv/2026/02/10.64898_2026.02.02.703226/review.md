### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that "the specific interaction with phosphorylated M18BP1 induces conformational changes in the condensin II complex, relieving an autoinhibitory conformation and thus allowing stable DNA binding and DNA compaction."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; no structural or biochemical data provided  
- **Concern** The abstract states that conformational changes relieve autoinhibition, but no cryo-EM maps, structural comparisons, or binding assays are described. The term "autoinhibitory conformation" implies a specific structural state that must be demonstrated, not assumed.  
- **Why it matters** The central mechanistic claim rests on this conformational transition. Without direct structural evidence, the proposed model remains speculative.  
- **Resolution test** Provide cryo-EM structures of condensin II in the absence and presence of phosphorylated M18BP1, with clear differences in conformation. Include quantitative binding data showing that phosphorylation enhances M18BP1 association and that this correlates with DNA binding.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that "multimeric species that withstand the forces applied by optical tweezers" highlight the contribution of condensin II protein–protein interactions to mechanical reinforcement.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; no single-molecule force data or multimer characterization provided  
- **Concern** The observation of force-resistant multimeric species is presented as a key finding, but no force magnitudes, rupture events, or controls are described. It is unclear whether these species are specific to condensin II or arise from nonspecific aggregation.  
- **Why it matters** The claim that multimerization contributes to chromosome mechanical integrity is a major conceptual extension. Without rigorous single-molecule data, this claim cannot be evaluated.  
- **Resolution test** Provide force-extension curves with statistical analysis, show that multimer formation is dependent on condensin II concentration and ATP, and include controls with mutant or truncated condensin II lacking interaction domains.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Reproducibility  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract implies a causal link between M18BP1-mediated activation and DNA compaction, but the experimental system is not described.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; no methods or experimental conditions provided  
- **Concern** The abstract does not specify whether the experiments were performed with purified components, in cell extracts, or in cells. The relevance of the findings to mitotic chromosomes in vivo is unclear.  
- **Why it matters** The physiological significance of the proposed mechanism depends on the experimental context. In vitro observations may not recapitulate the complexity of mitotic chromosome assembly.  
- **Resolution test** Clearly state the experimental system in the full manuscript. If in vitro, include validation in a cellular or extract-based system to confirm the relevance of the proposed mechanism.
