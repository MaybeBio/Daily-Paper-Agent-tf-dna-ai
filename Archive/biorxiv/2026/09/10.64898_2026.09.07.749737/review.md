## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence presented in the abstract
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors present PRIS, a unified deep-learning framework for high-throughput screening of protein-binding RNAs. PRIS comprises two components: PRIScore for structure selection and PRISeq for nucleotide preference inference. The framework is claimed to outperform existing methods in docking success rate, binding preference prediction, and virtual screening speed and enrichment.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no figures, tables, methods, or supplementary materials provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, all figures and tables, method details, benchmark definitions, dataset descriptions, code, and trained models

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract describes a potentially impactful framework for RNA-protein interaction prediction and screening. The reported performance metrics are impressive, particularly the screening speed and enrichment factors. However, the abstract alone provides insufficient detail to evaluate the technical soundness, reproducibility, or generalizability of the claims. Several key methodological aspects and benchmark comparisons are not adequately described.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in computational structural biology, RNA biology, drug discovery, and aptamer engineering would be interested. The framework promises to accelerate the discovery of RNA aptamers and therapeutic RNAs by combining structure prediction with sequence screening, addressing a bottleneck in the field.
- **Major strengths** 1. The unified framework integrating structure selection and binding preference inference is a conceptually novel approach. 2. The reported screening speed (129,248 hairpins in 11.95 seconds) is orders of magnitude faster than typical physics-based methods. 3. The framework demonstrates applicability to multiple protein targets (MS2, NELF-E, GFP), suggesting potential generalizability.
- **Major Concerns**
    - **Concern ID** R1-M1
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** Yes
    - **Axis** Technical soundness
    - **Claim pointer** "PRIScore improves the selection of native-like protein-RNA predictions generated by AlphaFold3, achieving a top-1 success rate of 81.91% on a docking benchmark, compared to 79.26% for AlphaFold3."
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided
    - **Concern** The reported improvement over AlphaFold3 is marginal (2.65 percentage points). The abstract does not specify the size, composition, or diversity of the docking benchmark, nor the statistical significance of this difference. Without confidence intervals or error bars, it is unclear whether this improvement is meaningful or within the noise of the evaluation.
    - **Why it matters** A core claim of the framework is that PRIScore improves structure selection. If the improvement is not statistically robust, the entire pipeline's advantage over AlphaFold3 alone is questionable.
    - **Resolution test** Provide the full docking benchmark details (number of complexes, protein and RNA diversity, difficulty distribution), report confidence intervals or statistical tests for the success rate comparison, and show per-complex performance distributions.

    - **Concern ID** R1-M2
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** Yes
    - **Axis** Technical soundness
    - **Claim pointer** "On a PWM benchmark, PRISeq achieved a mean absolute error (MAE) of 0.75, outperforming FoldX, Rosetta-based scoring functions, and NA-MPNN."
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided
    - **Concern** The abstract does not define the PWM benchmark, the number of targets, or the specific metrics for the baselines. "Outperforming" is vague without reporting the MAE values of the compared methods. Additionally, the MAE scale (0.75 relative to what?) is unclear.
    - **Why it matters** Without a clear benchmark definition and explicit comparison values, the claim of superior performance cannot be assessed or reproduced.
    - **Resolution test** Describe the PWM benchmark (number of proteins, RNA length, data source), report MAE values for all baselines, and clarify the MAE unit (e.g., probability scale 0-1 or log-odds).

    - **Concern ID** R1-M3
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** Yes
    - **Axis** Reproducibility
    - **Claim pointer** "In virtual screening against MS2 protein, PRISeq screens 129,248 RNA hairpins within 11.95 seconds, achieving the highest EF 0.5% of 14.40, approximately double the best baseline."
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided
    - **Concern** The abstract does not specify the hardware used for timing, the definition of the "best baseline," or the composition of the RNA library. The enrichment factor (EF 0.5%) is reported without confidence intervals or comparison to random expectation. The screening speed claim is hardware-dependent and may not be reproducible.
    - **Why it matters** Speed and enrichment are key selling points for high-throughput screening. Without hardware details and statistical rigor, these claims are not actionable.
    - **Resolution test** Report hardware specifications (GPU/CPU model, memory), define the baseline methods and their EF values, provide confidence intervals for EF, and describe the RNA library (sequence diversity, length, structure constraints).

    - **Concern ID** R1-M4
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** No
    - **Axis** Generalizability
    - **Claim pointer** "PRIS also effectively enriches active aptamers against NELF-E and GFP while preserving sequence diversity."
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided
    - **Concern** The abstract does not quantify "effectively enriches" or "preserving sequence diversity." No metrics (e.g., enrichment factor, hit rate, diversity index) are provided for these targets. The claim is qualitative and cannot be evaluated.
    - **Why it matters** Generalizability to multiple targets is a strength, but without quantitative evidence, the claim remains anecdotal.
    - **Resolution test** Provide enrichment metrics (e.g., EF, AUC, hit rate) for NELF-E and GFP, and report sequence diversity metrics (e.g., Shannon entropy, pairwise identity) before and after screening.

- **Minor Comments**
    - **Concern ID** R1-m1
    - **Severity** Minor
    - **Axis** Clarity
    - **Affected element** Framework description
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract
    - **Issue** The relationship between PRIScore and PRISeq is described as "complementary," but the abstract does not clarify whether they are used sequentially or in parallel, or whether the output of PRIScore is required for PRISeq.
    - **Required correction** Clarify the workflow: are PRIScore-selected structures used as input for PRISeq, or are they independent modules?

    - **Concern ID** R1-m2
    - **Severity** Minor
    - **Axis** Readability
    - **Affected element** Method name
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract
    - **Issue** The acronym "PRIS" is not defined. The full name of the framework is not provided.
    - **Required correction** Define the acronym PRIS (e.g., Protein-RNA Interaction Screener) at first use.

    - **Concern ID** R1-m3
    - **Severity** Minor
    - **Axis** Completeness
    - **Affected element** Benchmark description
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract
    - **Issue** The abstract mentions "PWM benchmark" but does not explain what PWM stands for or how the benchmark was constructed.
    - **Required correction** Define PWM (Position Weight Matrix) and briefly describe the benchmark construction.

- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1, R1-M2, R1-M3. The core performance claims (docking success rate improvement, PWM prediction accuracy, and virtual screening speed/enrichment) are not adequately supported by the abstract alone. Without full benchmark details, statistical rigor, and hardware specifications, the case for PRIS's superiority is not established.

- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**
    - **Originality**: The unified framework combining structure selection and binding preference inference is conceptually novel. However, the individual components (graph attention networks, distance prediction) are established techniques. The originality lies in the integration, which is not fully detailed in the abstract.
    - **Scientific importance**: High. Efficient RNA aptamer discovery is a significant challenge in therapeutic development. If validated, PRIS could substantially accelerate this process.
    - **Interdisciplinary readership**: Moderate. The work bridges computational biology, structural biology, and RNA therapeutics. The abstract is accessible to a broad audience but lacks sufficient detail for domain experts to evaluate.
    - **Technical soundness**: Not assessable from the abstract. The reported metrics lack statistical context, benchmark definitions, and reproducibility details.
    - **Readability for nonspecialists**: Good. The abstract is clearly written and conveys the main claims without excessive jargon.

- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The abstract presents promising results, but the lack of methodological detail, benchmark definitions, and statistical rigor prevents a full evaluation. A revised manuscript with complete methods, benchmark descriptions, and confidence intervals would be required to assess the framework's validity. Supportive if technical concerns are resolved.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that PRIScore "improves the selection of native-like protein-RNA predictions generated by AlphaFold3" is unsupported without statistical significance testing and benchmark details.
- The claim that PRISeq "outperforming FoldX, Rosetta-based scoring functions, and NA-MPNN" is unsupported without explicit comparison metrics and benchmark definition.
- The claim of screening "129,248 RNA hairpins within 11.95 seconds" is unsupported without hardware specifications.
- The claim of "effectively enriches active aptamers against NELF-E and GFP while preserving sequence diversity" is unsupported without quantitative metrics.
