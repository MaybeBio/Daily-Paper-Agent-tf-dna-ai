## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence presented in the abstract; no full text, figures, tables, or supplementary materials were provided for review
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors present a comprehensive atlas of Arabidopsis thaliana transcription factor (TF) binding, generated by reanalyzing 1,157 public ChIP-seq and DAP/ampDAP-seq datasets under a unified processing framework. After curation, 681 experiments were retained, covering 425 TFs across 42 families and 23 structural classes. The authors report that TF binding predictability varies widely, with family identity explaining more variation than structural class; deep learning models outperform classical position weight matrices (PWMs) in predictive accuracy, though PWMs remain competitive and interpretable. They further report family-dependent TFBS positioning relative to transcription start sites, pervasive homotypic TFBS spacing preferences, and a comparison of in vivo and in vitro binding profiles that identifies shared sequence-driven binding and in vivo-enriched sites associated with non-canonical or composite features.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no figures, tables, methods, or supplementary data were provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, all figures and tables, methods section, supplementary materials, data availability details, and any statistical or computational validation details

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract describes a potentially valuable community resource and reports several biologically interesting observations about the positional and syntactic organization of TF binding in Arabidopsis. However, the abstract alone provides insufficient detail to evaluate the technical rigor of the data processing pipeline, the validity of the curation criteria, the statistical robustness of the reported patterns, or the comparative performance claims for prediction models. The claims are plausible but currently not verifiable from the supplied material.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Plant molecular biologists and gene regulation researchers would be the primary audience, as the atlas could serve as a reference resource for TF binding in Arabidopsis. Computational biologists working on TFBS prediction and regulatory genomics would also be interested in the comparative model assessment and the organizational principles reported. The resource aspect may attract a broader plant science community seeking to interpret their own ChIP-seq or DAP-seq data.
- **Major strengths** The scale of the resource is substantial, with 1,157 datasets reanalyzed and 681 high-quality experiments retained. The unified processing framework addresses a common issue of cross-dataset comparability. The inclusion of both in vivo and in vitro data allows for a comparative analysis that could distinguish intrinsic sequence-driven binding from context-dependent effects. The reported findings on TFBS positioning and homotypic spacing suggest the atlas enables discovery beyond simple motif identification.
- **Major Concerns** None identified from the supplied material, as the abstract provides insufficient detail to raise specific technical concerns. However, the absence of methodological detail means that all substantive claims are currently unverifiable.
- **Minor Comments** None identified from the supplied material, as the abstract provides insufficient detail for specific minor comments.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** The abstract does not provide information on the unified processing framework, including alignment parameters, peak calling methods, quality control metrics, or the criteria used for the stringent curation that reduced 1,157 datasets to 681. The evaluation methodology for TFBS prediction models is not described, including training and test set separation, cross-validation strategy, and performance metrics. The statistical methods used to establish family-dependent positioning and pervasive homotypic spacing are not described. The comparison of in vivo and in vitro binding profiles lacks detail on how the shared core and in vivo-enriched sites were defined and validated.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: The resource itself may be original in its scale and unified processing, but the abstract does not clearly distinguish it from existing Arabidopsis TF binding compilations. Scientific importance: Potentially high for the plant community, but the abstract does not demonstrate how the atlas enables new biological insights beyond what existing resources provide. Interdisciplinary readership: The abstract is written in a way that is accessible to plant biologists and computational biologists, but the broader interdisciplinary appeal is not evident from the abstract alone. Technical soundness: Cannot be assessed from the abstract; the unified processing framework and curation criteria are not described. Readability for nonspecialists: The abstract is generally clear, though terms such as "syntactic organization" and "homotypic TFBS" may require domain familiarity.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The abstract presents a potentially valuable resource and interesting observations, but the evidence base is insufficient to evaluate the technical soundness of the work or the validity of the reported claims. A full manuscript review would be required to assess whether the case is established.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that 681 high-quality experiments were retained after stringent curation is unverifiable without knowledge of the curation criteria.
- The claim that family identity explains substantially more variation in predictive performance than structural class is unverifiable without statistical details.
- The claim that deep learning approaches achieved the highest overall predictive accuracy is unverifiable without model evaluation details.
- The claim that TFBS positioning is strongly family-dependent and more concentrated near promoters in vivo is unverifiable without supporting data.
- The claim that preferred spacing between homotypic TFBS is pervasive across TF families is unverifiable without supporting data.
- The claim that a shared core of sequence-driven binding exists alongside in vivo-enriched sites is unverifiable without details on the comparative analysis.
