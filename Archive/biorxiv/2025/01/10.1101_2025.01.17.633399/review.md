## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, background, results, discussion, conclusions, methods, declarations)
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific validity, methodological soundness, evidence quality, and clarity of claims as presented in the provided text
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors present baal-nf, a Nextflow-based computational workflow that integrates ChIP-seq data processing, allele-specific binding (ASB) inference via BaalChIP, and motif analysis using known JASPAR and de novo NoPeak motifs. They report 298,783 ASBs across 558 TFs and 46 cell lines, of which 1,960 are classified as "high-quality" ASBs with concordant motif disruption. These high-quality ASBs are claimed to be evolutionarily conserved, enriched for trait associations and molecular QTLs, and proposed as strong candidates for causal trait-altering variants.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract, Background, Results (workflow description, FOXA1 case study, genome-scale application), Discussion, Conclusions, Methods (read QC, alignment, ASB inference, motif prediction, non-ASB derivation, evolutionary/functional analyses), Declarations
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Figures and supplementary tables referenced (Figures 1-3, S1-S15; Tables S1-S10) are not provided. No code repository contents, no detailed parameter settings, no full statistical output tables, no validation benchmarks against existing methods, no comparison with orthogonal experimental data (e.g., electrophoretic mobility shift assays, reporter assays).

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript describes a useful and potentially valuable computational resource for prioritizing regulatory variants that disrupt transcription factor binding. The workflow integrates existing tools in a reproducible framework, and the inclusion of de novo motif discovery is a sensible addition. However, the evidence presented in the text alone is insufficient to fully evaluate the technical soundness of the pipeline, the statistical rigor of the ASB calling, or the robustness of the functional enrichment analyses. Several claims are made without adequate methodological detail or quantitative support. The manuscript would benefit from clearer presentation of validation steps, error rates, and sensitivity analyses.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in regulatory genomics, population genetics, and variant interpretation would be interested. The high-quality ASB catalog could serve as a resource for prioritizing non-coding variants in disease association studies. Computational biologists developing pipelines for allele-specific analysis would also find the workflow relevant.
- **Major strengths** The workflow addresses a real gap by combining ASB detection with motif-based mechanistic interpretation. The use of de novo motif discovery is a strength, as it can capture motifs not present in curated databases. The emphasis on reproducibility through Nextflow and containerization is commendable. The large scale of the analysis (558 TFs, 46 cell lines) is impressive.
- **Major Concerns** 
  - R1-M1: The definition and validation of "high-quality" ASBs is not sufficiently rigorous. The criteria (concordant motif disruption, location within a peak) are described, but no assessment of false discovery rates, sensitivity, or specificity is provided. The claim that these are "strong candidates for causal trait-altering variants" is not supported by direct functional validation.
  - R1-M2: The evolutionary conservation analysis relies on PhastCons scores, but the text does not describe how the comparator non-ASB sets were matched beyond minor allele frequency. Confounding factors such as GC content, recombination rate, or proximity to genes are not addressed. The empirical p-values are mentioned but the methodology for their calculation is not fully described.
  - R1-M3: The enrichment analyses for trait associations and QTLs are described only qualitatively. No effect sizes, confidence intervals, or multiple testing corrections are reported. The comparison with low-quality ASBs is mentioned but not quantified in the text.
  - R1-M4: The workflow's dependence on hg19 is a limitation that is acknowledged, but the authors do not discuss the potential impact of using an older genome build on variant calling and motif mapping. The lack of a liftOver or re-alignment strategy is a practical concern for users.
  - R1-M5: The FOXA1 case study is presented as an exemplar, but the text does not provide quantitative results (e.g., number of ASBs, motif enrichment statistics, overlap with known FOXA1 binding sites). Without these details, the case study does not convincingly demonstrate the workflow's utility.
- **Minor Comments** 
  - R1-m1: The abstract states "1,960 variants as strong candidates for causally altering human traits." This overstates the evidence, as no causal validation is presented. Suggest rewording to "candidate variants for functional follow-up."
  - R1-m2: The term "affinity-concordant positions" is used but not defined clearly. Please clarify what constitutes concordance.
  - R1-m3: The description of NoPeak motif clustering is brief. Please specify the parameters used for the de Bruijn sequence approach and the rationale for the 0.7 similarity threshold.
  - R1-m4: The methods state that BaalChIP corrects for reference mapping bias, but the details of how this bias is estimated are not provided. A brief explanation would improve reproducibility.
  - R1-m5: The text mentions "OpenTargets V2G score" but does not explain what this score represents. A brief definition would help nonspecialist readers.
  - R1-m6: The comparison with ADASTRA is mentioned in the Discussion, but the overlap analysis (Figure S13) is not described in the text. Please summarize the key findings.
  - R1-m7: The availability statement mentions OSF and GitHub, but the specific version of the code and the data access procedures are not detailed. Please provide a persistent DOI for the code.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** The lack of quantitative validation metrics for ASB calling (e.g., comparison with known ASBs, replication across replicates), the absence of effect sizes and confidence intervals for enrichment analyses, and the incomplete description of the non-ASB matching procedure are the most significant technical gaps.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - Originality: Moderate. The combination of existing tools is not entirely novel, but the integration of de novo motif discovery with ASB calling at this scale is a useful contribution.
  - Scientific importance: Moderate. The resource could be valuable, but the lack of functional validation limits the strength of the claims.
  - Interdisciplinary readership: The topic is relevant to genomics, bioinformatics, and human genetics, but the presentation is somewhat technical and may not be accessible to a broad audience.
  - Technical soundness: Not fully established from the provided text. Key methodological details and validation steps are missing.
  - Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is generally clear but uses jargon without sufficient explanation. The abstract is accessible, but the methods and results sections assume familiarity with ChIP-seq and motif analysis.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The manuscript describes a potentially useful tool, but the claims of high-quality ASBs and their functional significance are not sufficiently supported by the data presented. Major revisions and additional validation are required.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that 1,960 variants are "strong candidates for causally altering human traits" is unsupported without functional validation or causal inference methods.
- The assertion that high-quality ASBs are "more evolutionarily conserved" is based on an analysis that is not fully described and may be confounded.
- The statement that baal-nf "accounts for known biases" is not substantiated with specific performance metrics or comparisons against other methods.
- The claim that de novo motif discovery "substantially expanded" the set of high-quality ASBs is not quantified in the text.
- The generalizability of the workflow to other genome builds or non-human species is not discussed, despite the hg19 limitation.
