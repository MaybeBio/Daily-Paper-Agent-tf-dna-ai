## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, results, discussion, methods) as provided
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, methodology, data interpretation, and claims as presented in the provided text
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors propose that genomic context features, beyond the mere presence of a TF binding motif, can predict whether a given LEAFY (LFY) binding site is regulatory in Arabidopsis thaliana. They integrate ChIP-seq, DAP-seq, and expression data to label 1164 regulatory and 56411 nonregulatory LFY binding sites, then train a random forest classifier using 69 genomic features. The model reportedly outperforms controls using only PWM and POcc scores, with feature importance analysis identifying LFY-related features and co-occurring TF binding sites as key predictors.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract, Introduction, Results (narrative), Discussion, Materials and Methods (narrative). No figures, tables, or supplementary materials were provided.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** All figures (Figures 1-3), all tables (Tables 1-3), all supplementary figures and tables, and the full results section with quantitative outcomes. Without these, model performance metrics, feature importance values, and data distributions cannot be independently assessed.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript addresses a timely and important question in gene regulation: whether genomic context can improve prediction of functional TF binding sites beyond motif presence. The authors have assembled a substantial dataset and applied a reasonable machine learning approach. However, the provided text lacks the quantitative details necessary to evaluate the strength of the claims. Key performance metrics, feature importance values, and validation results are referenced but not shown. The conceptual framework is sound, but the evidence base as presented is insufficient to fully assess technical soundness and the robustness of the conclusions.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Plant molecular biologists studying transcriptional regulation, particularly those working on LEAFY and flower development. Computational biologists developing predictive models for cis-regulatory element function. Researchers interested in the general question of how TF binding site context influences regulatory outcomes, as the approach could be transferable to other TFs and species.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses a well-recognized limitation of motif-based predictions by incorporating genomic context. The integration of multiple public and newly generated datasets (ChIP-seq, DAP-seq, microarray, RNA-seq) is thorough. The use of a machine learning approach with careful attention to class imbalance (using PR curves rather than ROC) shows methodological awareness. The authors explicitly discuss limitations and potential extensions to other TFs and species.
- **Major Concerns** 
  - R1-M1: The central claim of model performance superiority cannot be evaluated without the actual performance metrics. The text states the model "outperforms by far" the control, but no AUPRC values, confidence intervals, or statistical comparisons are provided in the visible text.
  - R1-M2: The labeling strategy for regulatory versus nonregulatory sites relies on the union of multiple expression datasets with different experimental conditions (mutant, overexpression, inducible). The potential for condition-specific regulatory effects to be conflated or missed is not discussed in the visible text.
  - R1-M3: The feature importance analysis is described qualitatively. The text states that LFY-related features are "most important" and that other TF clusters contribute, but no quantitative importance scores, rankings, or stability assessments are shown.
  - R1-M4: The exclusion of conservation features is presented as improving performance, which contradicts common expectations. The explanation for this counterintuitive result is not provided in the visible text.
- **Minor Comments** 
  - R1-m1: The number of features is stated as 69 in one section and 68 in another ("68 features for 57575 observations"). This inconsistency should be resolved.
  - R1-m2: The text mentions "47 TF clusters" in the methods but states "46 TF clusters" were used after excluding LFY. Clarification of the final count is needed.
  - R1-m3: The definition of "undefined" sites is clear, but the proportion of the total sites falling into this category is not stated, which would help assess potential selection bias.
  - R1-m4: The choice of 3 kb upstream and 1 kb downstream for regulatory regions is stated but not justified. Some discussion of how this window was selected would strengthen the methods.
  - R1-m5: The newly generated RNA-seq experiment is described briefly. More detail on biological replicates, sequencing depth, and quality control would be useful.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 (model performance metrics), R1-M3 (quantitative feature importance), R1-M4 (explanation for conservation feature exclusion)
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - Originality: The concept of using genomic context to predict regulatory function of TF binding sites is not entirely new, but the specific application to LEAFY with a comprehensive feature set is a valuable contribution. The approach is a logical extension of existing work rather than a paradigm shift.
  - Scientific importance: Understanding why some TF binding sites are functional while others are not is a fundamental question in gene regulation. The findings could inform future studies of cis-regulatory element function and TF cooperativity. The importance is moderate to high within the plant biology community.
  - Interdisciplinary readership: The work bridges plant developmental biology and computational biology. The machine learning approach is standard and would be accessible to computational readers, but the biological focus may limit broader interdisciplinary appeal beyond these two fields.
  - Technical soundness: Cannot be fully assessed from the provided text. The methodological choices (random forest, PR curves, permutation importance) are appropriate, but the absence of quantitative results prevents verification of the claims.
  - Readability for nonspecialists: The text is generally clear and well-structured. The introduction provides adequate background. However, some sections assume familiarity with specific genomic concepts and tools (e.g., POcc, JASPAR, bedtools) that may not be accessible to all readers.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The conceptual approach and dataset assembly are promising, but the manuscript as provided lacks the quantitative evidence needed to establish the case. The authors should provide full model performance metrics, feature importance values with uncertainty estimates, and a more detailed discussion of the counterintuitive conservation result.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that the model "outperforms by far" the control model is unsupported without quantitative performance metrics.
- The claim that "the density and quality of binding sites constitute the most important features" is unsupported without quantitative feature importance values.
- The claim that "the presence of binding sites for other transcription factors and the overall richness in TFBS were also essential" is unsupported without quantitative evidence.
- The claim that excluding conservation features "improved predictions" is unsupported and lacks explanation.
- The generalizability of the approach to other TFs is speculative and not demonstrated.
- The statement that the 1164 sites are "high-confidence regulatory LFYBS" depends on the union of multiple datasets with different conditions; the confidence level is not quantitatively justified.
