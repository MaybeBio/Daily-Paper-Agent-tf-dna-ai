## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, results, discussion, materials and methods) from bioRxiv preprint
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, methodological soundness, claims versus evidence, and presentation quality. No assessment of editorial fit or publication decision.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors propose that during natural transformation in firmicutes, a tripartite protein complex comprising the transmembrane channel ComEC and two cytoplasmic ssDNA-binding proteins ComFA and ComFC mediates import of single-stranded DNA. Using AlphaFold3 structural modelling, targeted mutagenesis, and transformation assays in Streptococcus pneumoniae, they identify key residues involved in protein-protein and protein-ssDNA interactions and validate their functional importance. They further show that the ComEC channel is conserved in the diderm Helicobacter pylori and that conserved residues are important for transformation in both organisms.
- **Visible evidence base** Full text including results sections, figure legends (referenced but figures not fully visible), supplementary figure references, materials and methods, and supplementary table references
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Actual figures and supplementary figures (only legends referenced), supplementary tables (strain lists, sequence alignments, model statistics), raw transformation efficiency data points, AlphaFold3 model confidence metrics (PAE, pLDDT plots), and model coordinates

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a compelling integration of AI-based structural prediction with targeted in vivo functional assays to address a long-standing question in bacterial transformation: how ssDNA is imported across the cytoplasmic membrane. The use of AlphaFold3 to generate testable hypotheses about a tripartite membrane-associated complex is innovative and timely, and the experimental validation strategy is generally well designed. The cumulative effects of double mutations on transformation efficiency provide strong genetic evidence for the model. However, several concerns temper enthusiasm: the structural evidence for the ssDNA path through ComEC is indirect, the functional data rely on a single assay (transformation efficiency) without direct measurement of ssDNA uptake, and the conservation claims across firmicutes are based primarily on computational models without experimental validation in other species. The manuscript would be substantially strengthened by direct biochemical or biophysical evidence for the proposed interactions and by clearer presentation of model confidence metrics.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying bacterial horizontal gene transfer, natural transformation, membrane transport mechanisms, and competence development would find this work directly relevant. The demonstration of AlphaFold3 as a hypothesis-generating tool for membrane protein complexes will also interest structural biologists and computational biologists. The conserved channel architecture across monoderm and diderm species has implications for understanding the evolution of transformation mechanisms and could inform studies in other transformable species, including those of clinical relevance.
- **Major strengths** 1) The study addresses a mechanistically important and previously intractable question using a creative combination of state-of-the-art structural prediction and classical bacterial genetics. 2) The experimental design includes appropriate controls, including single and double mutants, saturating and limiting DNA conditions, and protein stability validation. 3) The demonstration of functional conservation of key residues in both S. pneumoniae and H. pylori strengthens the generalizability of the findings. 4) The cumulative effects of combining mutations across different interaction interfaces provide strong genetic evidence for the tripartite model. 5) The authors are appropriately cautious in interpreting weak or negative results and acknowledge limitations of the structural modelling approach.
- **Major Concerns**
- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency for central claim
- **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims that ssDNA passes through a narrow channel within a ComEC monomer during transformation, and that the tripartite complex of ComEC/ComFA/ComFC is required for ssDNA internalization.
- **Evidence pointer** Results sections "ComEC is a conserved monomeric transmembrane channel..." and "A positively charged groove on Sp ComFC..."; Figures 2, 3, 4; Figure S1
- **Concern** The central claim that ssDNA traverses a channel within ComEC is supported primarily by computational modelling (AlphaFold3) and indirect genetic evidence (mutations in channel-lining residues reduce transformation). No direct biochemical evidence demonstrates that ssDNA actually passes through the ComEC channel, nor is there direct measurement of ssDNA uptake into cells. The transformation efficiency assay measures the endpoint of a multi-step process (DNA binding, uptake, integration, recombination), and reduced transformation could reflect defects in any of these steps. The authors do not measure ssDNA internalization directly, nor do they demonstrate that the mutant phenotypes are specifically due to impaired DNA transport rather than, for example, altered protein stability, mislocalization, or defective interactions with other components of the transformation machinery.
- **Why it matters** The title and abstract make a strong mechanistic claim about DNA transport through a tripartite complex. Without direct evidence for ssDNA translocation, the data are equally consistent with models where the identified residues affect complex assembly, protein stability, or coupling to downstream recombination steps. The distinction matters because the proposed mechanism has specific implications for how transformation is powered and regulated.
- **Resolution test** Provide direct evidence of ssDNA uptake, for example using radiolabeled or fluorescently labeled DNA internalization assays in wild-type versus mutant strains. Alternatively, demonstrate in vitro that purified ComEC (or the tripartite complex) can conduct ssDNA across liposomes or nanodiscs. At minimum, show that the mutant proteins are properly localized to the membrane and that the complex assembles normally in the absence of the mutated interactions.
- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Structural model validation
- **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims high-confidence structural models of the tripartite complex and the ssDNA path through ComEC, with specific residue-level predictions validated by mutagenesis.
- **Evidence pointer** Results sections on structural modelling; Figures 1, 2, 3; Figure S1, S2; Table S2
- **Concern** The AlphaFold3 models are presented as high-confidence (ipTM = 0.74, pTM = 0.82), but the confidence in the ssDNA structure and its interactions is acknowledged to be low (chain pTM for ssDNA = 0.08; ipTM for protein-ssDNA pairs = 0.27-0.45). The authors rely on "complementary electrostatic surfaces and evolutionary conservation" to support the relevance of the ssDNA interactions, but these are indirect validations. The manuscript does not report per-residue confidence metrics (pLDDT) or PAE plots for the key interface residues, nor does it discuss whether the predicted ssDNA path is consistent with known biochemical constraints (e.g., the size of the channel relative to ssDNA diameter, the presence of positively charged residues lining the path). The claim that the channel is "narrow" is not quantified, and the functional significance of specific channel residues is inferred from mutagenesis without structural validation of the mutant proteins.
- **Why it matters** The entire study hinges on the accuracy of the structural models. If the predicted interfaces or the ssDNA path are incorrect, the mutagenesis results, while interesting, would not validate the proposed mechanism. The low confidence in the ssDNA modelling is particularly concerning because the central claim is about DNA transport. Without transparent reporting of model confidence at the residue level and discussion of alternative conformations or binding modes, readers cannot assess the reliability of the structural predictions.
- **Resolution test** Provide per-residue confidence metrics (pLDDT, PAE) for the key interface residues and the ssDNA path. Show that the predicted ssDNA-binding residues are conserved across a broader phylogenetic sampling. Consider validating the model using complementary approaches such as cross-linking mass spectrometry, hydrogen-deuterium exchange, or cysteine accessibility scanning of the channel. If possible, compare the AlphaFold3 model with any experimental structures of homologous channels.
- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** No
- **Axis** Generalizability of conservation claims
- **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims that the tripartite complex and its mode of ssDNA interaction are conserved in firmicutes, and that the ComEC channel is conserved in diderm species such as H. pylori.
- **Evidence pointer** Results sections on structural conservation; Figures 3, 6; Table S1, S2; Figure S5
- **Concern** The conservation claims are based on computational models of nine firmicute species and one diderm species (H. pylori). While the structural models show similar overall architecture, the functional validation is limited to S. pneumoniae and H. pylori. The authors do not test whether the specific residues identified in S. pneumoniae are functionally important in any other firmicute species. The H. pylori data are limited to three residues in ComEC; no functional validation of ComF (the H. pylori ComFC homolog) is provided. The claim that the "hook/pocket" interaction is conserved in firmicutes is based on structural modelling alone, without experimental evidence that this interaction is functionally important in any species other than S. pneumoniae.
- **Why it matters** The title and abstract emphasize conservation across firmicutes, which implies a general mechanism. If the specific interactions are only validated in one species, the conservation claim is premature. The phylogenetic sampling is also limited; firmicutes include many diverse lineages, and the nine species chosen may not represent the full diversity of the phylum.
- **Resolution test** Test the functional importance of conserved residues in at least one additional firmicute species, ideally one that is distantly related to S. pneumoniae. Alternatively, demonstrate that the hook/pocket interaction is required for transformation in a heterologous system. Provide a more systematic phylogenetic analysis of the conservation of key residues across a larger and more diverse set of firmicute genomes.
- **Concern ID** R1-M4
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** No
- **Axis** Mechanistic interpretation of mutant phenotypes
- **Claim pointer** The manuscript interprets reduced transformation efficiency in mutants as evidence for the importance of specific protein-protein and protein-ssDNA interactions in the tripartite complex.
- **Evidence pointer** Results sections on mutagenesis; Figures 2, 4, 5, 6, 7; Figure S3, S6, S7, S8
- **Concern** The mutagenesis strategy relies on charge-reversal or charge-removal mutations (e.g., to aspartate or lysine) designed to disrupt predicted interactions. While the authors validate protein stability for some mutants, they do not directly demonstrate that the mutations specifically disrupt the intended interaction without affecting other functions of the proteins. For example, the Sp ComFC K83D mutation is interpreted as disrupting ssDNA binding, but it could also affect protein folding, oligomerization, or interactions with other partners. The authors do not perform biochemical or biophysical validation of the disrupted interactions (e.g., pull-downs, surface plasmon resonance, isothermal titration calorimetry). The cumulative effects of double mutations are interpreted as evidence for independent interactions, but could also reflect additive effects on protein stability or folding.
- **Why it matters** The mechanistic conclusions depend on the specificity of the mutations. Without direct evidence that the mutations disrupt the intended interactions, alternative explanations (e.g., global misfolding, aggregation, or pleiotropic effects) cannot be excluded. This is particularly important for membrane proteins, where mutations can have unpredictable effects on topology or stability.
- **Resolution test** Perform biochemical validation of the disrupted interactions, for example using co-immunoprecipitation, pull-down assays, or bacterial two-hybrid assays with the mutant proteins. For ssDNA-binding mutants, perform electrophoretic mobility shift assays or fluorescence anisotropy with purified proteins. For membrane-embedded interactions, consider using split-GFP or FRET-based assays to monitor complex assembly in vivo.
- **Minor Comments**
- **Concern ID** R1-m1
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Presentation and accessibility
- **Affected element** Figure 1 and general figure accessibility
- **Evidence pointer** Figure 1; Results section "Structural model of a tripartite protein complex..."
- **Issue** The manuscript relies heavily on structural models, but the figures are not available in the provided text. The description of the tripartite complex in Figure 1 would benefit from a schematic overview that clearly labels each component, the membrane, and the proposed path of ssDNA. For readers not expert in structural biology, a simplified cartoon model would greatly aid comprehension.
- **Required correction** Consider adding a schematic figure that summarizes the proposed model of ssDNA import, showing the membrane, ComEC channel, ComFA, ComFC, and the direction of ssDNA translocation. This would make the model accessible to a broader readership.
- **Concern ID** R1-m2
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Statistical reporting
- **Affected element** Transformation assay data
- **Evidence pointer** Figures 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7; Materials and Methods "S. pneumoniae strains, competence and transformation"
- **Issue** The manuscript reports transformation efficiencies as fold-decreases relative to wild-type but does not consistently report the number of biological replicates, the variability between replicates, or the statistical test used for each comparison. The methods state that an unpaired t-test was used for pairwise comparisons, but it is unclear whether data were normally distributed and whether multiple testing corrections were applied.
- **Required correction** Provide a clear description of the statistical analysis for each figure, including the number of replicates, the measure of variability (e.g., SD or SEM), and the specific statistical test used. Consider showing individual data points in all figures, as is done for the split-luciferase assay.
- **Concern ID** R1-m3
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Methodological detail
- **Affected element** AlphaFold3 modelling parameters
- **Evidence pointer** Materials and Methods "Structural analyses"
- **Issue** The methods describe using the AlphaFold3 web server but do not provide key parameters such as the number of models generated, the ranking method used, whether the ssDNA was included as a single-stranded or double-stranded molecule, and how the poly-A sequence was chosen. The authors also do not state whether the models were relaxed or whether any energy minimization was performed.
- **Required correction** Provide a more detailed description of the AlphaFold3 modelling procedure, including input sequences, any template usage, the number of predicted models, and the criteria for selecting the final model. This is essential for reproducibility.
- **Concern ID** R1-m4
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Interpretation of negative results
- **Affected element** Sp ComEC Y157E and K297E mutants
- **Evidence pointer** Figure 2F, 2G
- **Issue** The Y157E and K297E mutations show no effect under saturating conditions but significant effects under limiting conditions. The authors interpret this as evidence that these residues contribute to ssDNA interaction, but the differential effect could also reflect differences in assay sensitivity or in the rate-limiting step under different DNA concentrations.
- **Required correction** Discuss the interpretation of saturating versus limiting conditions more explicitly. Consider whether the limiting condition is more sensitive to subtle defects in DNA uptake or whether it introduces additional variables (e.g., differences in DNA binding kinetics).
- **Concern ID** R1-m5
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Literature context
- **Affected element** Discussion
- **Evidence pointer** Discussion section
- **Issue** The discussion of the mechanism of ssDNA import would benefit from more explicit comparison with recent structural and biochemical studies of related systems, particularly the recent preprint on Moorella glycerini ComEC and studies on the Bacillus subtilis transformation machinery. The authors mention these but do not critically evaluate how their model agrees or disagrees with these studies.
- **Required correction** Expand the discussion to more thoroughly compare and contrast the proposed model with existing literature, highlighting points of agreement and disagreement and suggesting experiments that could resolve discrepancies.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** 1) Direct evidence of ssDNA translocation through the ComEC channel is lacking; the current data support a role for the identified residues in transformation but do not establish the transport mechanism. 2) The AlphaFold3 models, particularly for protein-ssDNA interactions, have low confidence scores that are not fully reported or discussed. 3) The specificity of the designed mutations for disrupting the intended interactions is not experimentally validated. 4) The conservation claims across firmicutes are based on computational models without functional validation in additional species.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: The application of AlphaFold3 to model a tripartite membrane-associated DNA transport complex is novel and timely, and the study provides the first integrated model of ssDNA import during transformation in firmicutes. Scientific importance: Understanding the molecular mechanism of DNA uptake during natural transformation is of fundamental importance to bacterial evolution, horizontal gene transfer, and the spread of antibiotic resistance. The proposed tripartite complex, if validated, would represent a significant advance. Interdisciplinary readership: The work bridges structural biology, microbiology, and molecular genetics, and the use of AlphaFold3 will interest computational biologists. However, the heavy reliance on structural modelling without experimental structural validation may limit appeal to structural biologists. Technical soundness: The genetic experiments are generally well designed with appropriate controls, but the lack of direct biochemical validation of the proposed interactions and the incomplete reporting of model confidence metrics weaken the technical foundation. Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is clearly written and the logic is easy to follow, but the structural descriptions may be challenging for readers without a structural biology background. A schematic model figure would improve accessibility.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The study addresses an important question with a creative approach and provides compelling genetic evidence for the tripartite complex. However, the central claim of ssDNA transport through the ComEC channel requires direct experimental validation, and the structural models need more rigorous confidence assessment. If the authors can provide direct evidence of ssDNA translocation and validate the specificity of their mutations, this would constitute a significant contribution.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that ssDNA passes through a narrow channel within a ComEC monomer is not directly supported by experimental evidence; it is inferred from computational modelling and indirect mutagenesis data.
- The claim that the tripartite complex is conserved across firmicutes is based on computational models of nine species without functional validation in any species other than S. pneumoniae.
- The claim that the identified residues are specifically involved in protein-protein or protein-ssDNA interactions is not directly validated biochemically; alternative explanations (e.g., effects on protein stability or folding) are not excluded.
- The claim that the "hook/pocket" interaction between ComEC and ComFC is conserved in firmicutes is based solely on structural modelling and sequence analysis, without experimental evidence in other species.
- The functional importance of the Sp ComEC Y157E and K297E residues is only evident under limiting DNA conditions, and the interpretation of this differential effect is not fully justified.
