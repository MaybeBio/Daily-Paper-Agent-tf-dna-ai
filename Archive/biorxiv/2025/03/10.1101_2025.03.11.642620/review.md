## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text, including abstract, introduction, results, discussion, methods, and figure legends as provided.
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, experimental design, data analysis, interpretation, and claims as presented in the provided text. No assessment of formatting, grammar, or editorial style beyond readability for nonspecialists.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors map 3D genome organization, chromatin accessibility, and gene expression in iPSCs and neural stem cells from human, chimpanzee, gorilla, and macaque. They identify human-specific epigenetic signatures, use deep learning to dissect regulatory grammar, and report conserved TADs with species-specific enhancer-promoter interactions. They propose that synergistic enhancer activation drives species-specific gene regulation.
- **Visible evidence base** Results sections describing comparative transcriptomics, ATAC-seq, Hi-C, ChromBPNet modeling, and organoid validation. Methods sections for cell culture, NSC generation, multimodal profiling, and computational analyses. Figure references are present but figure panels themselves are not provided.

Statistical details, exact sample sizes, and quality metrics for Hi-C and ATAC-seq are referenced but not fully detailed. The MPRA data are mentioned but not described in methods. No code or model parameters for ChromBPNet are provided.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a substantial and timely dataset that will be of considerable interest to the fields of primate evolution, neurodevelopment, and 3D genome biology. The inclusion of four primate species and two matched cell types is a clear strength, as is the integration of multiple epigenomic layers. The finding that TADs are largely conserved while enhancer-promoter interactions and chromatin accessibility show species-specific changes is an important contribution. However, several key claims, particularly regarding the sufficiency of DNA sequence to explain regulatory divergence and the role of synergistic enhancer activation, are not fully supported by the evidence presented. The manuscript would benefit from clearer statistical frameworks, additional validation of the deep learning predictions, and a more nuanced discussion of the limitations of the in vitro system.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying primate evolution, comparative genomics, neural development, and 3D genome organization. The dataset will be a valuable resource for those investigating the regulatory basis of human brain evolution. The methodological approach, particularly the use of ChromBPNet and cross-species alignment, will interest computational biologists. The organoid validation and identification of candidate human-specific enhancers will appeal to those working on neurodevelopmental disorders.
- **Major strengths** 1) A comprehensive, multi-species, multi-omics dataset that is a significant resource for the community. 2) The use of matched iPSC and NSC populations across four primate species allows for the separation of species-specific from cell type-specific effects. 3) The integration of deep learning with comparative epigenomics is a sophisticated approach that goes beyond simple peak overlap analyses. 4) The validation of human-specific DARs in an orthogonal organoid system strengthens the relevance of the findings. 5) The observation that TADs are conserved while finer-scale interactions change is an important conceptual advance.
- **Major Concerns** 
  - **R1-M1** (Blocking: Yes) The claim that DNA sequence alone is insufficient to explain regulatory divergence is based on a comparison of model prediction accuracy that may be confounded by model training and evaluation procedures. The authors do not provide sufficient detail on how cross-species predictions were normalized or how the "conserved" versus "divergent" DAR sets were matched for potential confounders like GC content or sequence complexity. Without this, the conclusion that trans-acting factors are required is not robustly established.
  - **R1-M2** (Blocking: Yes) The central claim of "synergistic enhancer activation" as a key mechanism is inferred from the observation that upregulated genes interact with multiple DARs. However, no functional validation of synergy is provided. The analysis does not distinguish between additive effects of multiple enhancers and true synergy, nor does it rule out alternative explanations such as enhancer redundancy or the presence of a single dominant enhancer. The statistical framework for testing synergy is not described.
  - **R1-M3** (Blocking: No) The organoid validation, while welcome, is limited. Only a small number of DARs (n=80) were tested in the MPRA, and the overlap with human NSC DARs is modest (42.8%). The authors do not discuss potential reasons for the lack of overlap, such as differences between 2D and 3D culture systems or the specific cell types isolated. The generalizability of the findings to in vivo fetal tissue is not addressed.
  - **R1-M4** (Blocking: No) The analysis of TF motif changes is largely descriptive. While the authors identify motif gains and losses, they do not directly link these to changes in TF binding or expression. The claim that "changes in TF motifs emerge as key elements" would be strengthened by integrating TF expression data or performing binding assays.
- **Minor Comments** 
  - **R1-m1** The abstract states "unexpected global shifts in 3D genome architecture" but the results section does not clearly define what constitutes a "global shift" versus a local change. Please clarify the metric used.
  - **R1-m2** The description of the ChromBPNet model training is brief. Please provide details on hyperparameter selection, model convergence, and the number of training epochs.
  - **R1-m3** The methods for the MPRA are not described. Please include details on the library design, number of replicates, and analysis pipeline.
  - **R1-m4** The authors mention "low mappability" regions were removed but do not specify the criteria. Please provide the exact thresholds used.
  - **R1-m5** The discussion of the discrepancy with a previous study on human-specific TADs is brief. Please expand on the potential methodological differences that could explain the conflicting results.
  - **R1-m6** The figure legends are not provided, making it difficult to assess the quality of the data presentation. Please ensure all figures are self-contained and clearly labeled.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 and R1-M2 are the primary technical concerns. The lack of a clear statistical framework for testing synergy and the potential confounders in the deep learning analysis must be addressed. Additionally, the limited organoid validation and lack of MPRA methods are secondary but important issues.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - **Originality** High. The multi-species, multi-omics approach with matched cell types is novel and goes beyond previous comparative studies.
  - **Scientific importance** High. The findings have implications for understanding the regulatory basis of human brain evolution and the role of 3D genome organization in species differences.
  - **Interdisciplinary readership** Moderate to high. The topic will appeal to evolutionary biologists, neuroscientists, and genomicists, but the technical depth may limit accessibility to a broader audience.
  - **Technical soundness** Moderate. The experimental design is sound, but the analytical approaches, particularly the deep learning and synergy analyses, require more rigorous validation and clearer statistical frameworks.
  - **Readability for nonspecialists** Moderate. The abstract and introduction are accessible, but the results section is dense and assumes familiarity with epigenomic techniques. The discussion could be more accessible with clearer explanations of key concepts.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The dataset and initial findings are valuable, but the central claims regarding the role of DNA sequence and synergistic enhancer activation need to be more rigorously supported. The manuscript would be suitable for a high-impact journal after substantial revision.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that "DNA sequence alone is not sufficient to fully explain epigenome evolution" is not fully supported due to potential confounders in the model comparison.
- The claim that "synergistic enhancer activation is a key mechanism" is inferred but not functionally validated.
- The statement that "changes in TF motifs emerge as key elements" is descriptive and lacks direct evidence of causality.
- The claim of "unexpected global shifts in 3D genome architecture" is not clearly defined or quantified.
- The generalizability of the organoid findings to in vivo fetal tissue is not established.
