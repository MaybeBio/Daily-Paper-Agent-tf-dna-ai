## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, results, methods, data availability, author contributions, competing interests, acknowledgments)
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific claims, methodological soundness, evidence quality, and alignment with Nature-style criteria. No line numbers were provided, so all evidence pointers refer to section or figure names only.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report that a variational autoencoder (VAE) trained on LuxR-family DNA-binding domains can generate hybrid transcription factors with dual or partially novel promoter recognition specificities, as demonstrated by in vivo reporter assays, sort-seq, structural modeling, and MD simulations.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract, Introduction, Results sections (four subsections), Methods (partial), Data availability statement, Supplementary figure references (S1, S2, S3, S5, S6), Supplementary tables (S1, S2, S3)

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript presents a conceptually interesting application of VAE-based latent space interpolation to generate hybrid transcription factors with altered DNA-binding specificity. The core idea, that functional intermediates between homologous TFs can be generated by sampling between latent representations, is timely and relevant to synthetic biology. However, the evidence as presented is incomplete and insufficient to fully support the central claims. Several key experimental details, quantitative results, and control analyses are missing or only described qualitatively. The manuscript would benefit from a more rigorous presentation of the data, including effect sizes, statistical comparisons, and negative controls, before the claims can be considered established.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in synthetic biology, protein engineering, and computational protein design would be the primary audience. The work addresses a fundamental question about whether latent-space interpolation can generate functional hybrid proteins, which is relevant to those interested in expanding the design space of genetic circuits and in understanding sequence-function relationships in transcription factors. The sort-seq methodology and the use of AlphaFold3 for structural interpretation may also interest method developers.
- **Major strengths** 1) The research question is well-motivated and addresses a clear gap in synthetic biology, namely the limited complexity of genetic circuits due to reliance on orthogonal parts. 2) The use of a VAE to interpolate between LuxR and LasR is a novel and principled approach, distinct from recombination-based or ancestral reconstruction methods. 3) The combination of in vivo assays, sort-seq, and structural modeling provides a multi-pronged approach to characterize the generated variants. 4) The observation that some variants show dual-responsive behavior not seen in either parent is potentially significant.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
  - **Claim pointer** "a subset of functional variants exhibited dual-responsive behavior while maintaining sequence-selective DNA recognition"
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "Functional characterization of VAE-designed TFs toward lux and las promoters"; Results section "Promoter recognition profiles of VAE-designed proteins revealed by sort-seq"; Figure 2b, 3b, 3c
  - **Concern** The claim of dual-responsive behavior is based on a small number of variants (four from the initial 18 constructs, and three from the sort-seq validation). The quantitative data supporting this claim, such as the magnitude of activation relative to wild-type, the dynamic range, and the reproducibility across replicates, are not presented in the text. The sort-seq results are described qualitatively ("broad dynamic range", "dual-responsive profiles") without providing the actual activity scores, distributions, or statistical comparisons. The individual validation of only three variants (20L, 22L, 24L) is a very small sample size to support the general claim that VAE-generated hybrids frequently exhibit dual-responsiveness.
  - **Why it matters** Without quantitative data and statistical analysis, it is impossible to assess the robustness and significance of the observed dual-responsive behavior. The claim that these variants "maintain sequence-selective DNA recognition" is critical to distinguish from non-specific DNA binding, but the evidence for this is not clearly presented in the text.
  - **Resolution test** Provide quantitative activity scores for all variants tested, including the distribution of activities across the sort-seq bins, with appropriate statistical tests comparing the variants to wild-type LuxR and LasR. Show the individual dose-response curves or activation ratios for the validated variants (20L, 22L, 24L) and include negative controls (e.g., empty vector, non-specific DNA binding controls). The number of validated dual-responsive variants should be increased, or the claim should be tempered to reflect the small sample size.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Claim pointer** "we designed hybrid TFs that activate both the lux and las promoters" and "the VAE-designed variants 20L and 22L did not simply exhibit indiscriminate DNA binding"
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "Promoter recognition profiles of VAE-designed proteins revealed by deep sequencing"; Figure 4b, 4c, 4d
  - **Concern** The randomized promoter library experiment is the key evidence for sequence-selective recognition, but the description is insufficient. The number of randomized positions, the library complexity, the depth of sequencing, and the method for calculating "enrichment-based activity scores" are not described in the main text. The claim that the variants "preferentially activated a subset of sequences not strongly activated by both wild-type TF" is based on a "four-dimensional activity landscape" (Figure 4d) that is not described in sufficient detail. The statistical significance of this "partially novel" recognition profile is not established.
  - **Why it matters** The central claim of the paper is that the VAE generates proteins with hybrid and partially novel specificities, not just non-specific binders. The randomized promoter library experiment is the primary evidence for this. Without a clear description of the experimental design and quantitative analysis, the claim of sequence-selective recognition is not supported.
  - **Resolution test** Provide a detailed description of the randomized promoter library design (positions randomized, library size, coverage), the sequencing depth, and the exact formula for the activity score. Show the full enrichment plots for all variants and the statistical significance of the differences in nucleotide preferences between the VAE variants and the wild-type TFs. The "four-dimensional activity landscape" should be explained, and the claim of "partially novel" recognition should be supported by a statistical test showing that the VAE variants activate a significantly different set of promoter sequences compared to both parents.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Reproducibility
  - **Claim pointer** "we employed a VAE to generate hybrid transcription factor sequences" and "we obtained a pool of unique candidate hybrids"
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "Design of hybrid transcription factors using a variational autoencoder (VAE)"; Methods section "Model building and validation using VAE"
  - **Concern** The description of the VAE training and sequence generation is incomplete. The architecture is only briefly described, and the hyperparameter search is referred to Supplementary Methods. The criteria for selecting the "top 10 models" are not specified. The number of unique candidate hybrids obtained after CD-HIT filtering is not stated. The sampling procedure (hyperspherical region, radius R) is described, but the values of R and the number of sequences sampled per model are not given.
  - **Why it matters** For a computational design method, reproducibility is essential. The lack of specific parameters and selection criteria makes it difficult for others to replicate the work or assess the robustness of the approach.
  - **Resolution test** Provide the full VAE architecture, hyperparameter ranges, and the specific values used for the final models. State the number of unique sequences generated and the criteria for model selection. Include the values of R and the sampling density.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M4
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
  - **Claim pointer** "we reveal the molecular basis underlying the DNA-binding properties of these hybrid proteins"
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "Structural insights into promoter recognition by the transcription factors"; Figure 3d; Supplementary Figure S3
  - **Concern** The structural analysis is presented as providing a "molecular basis" for the observed properties, but the results are described only in general terms. The specific interactions that differ between the VAE variants and the wild-type proteins are not described. The MD simulation results are only mentioned as "reproducible patterns" without quantitative data (e.g., RMSF, binding free energies, hydrogen bond occupancy). The link between the structural observations and the functional data is not explicitly made.
  - **Why it matters** The claim of a "molecular basis" is a strong one that requires specific structural and dynamic evidence. Without a detailed presentation of the structural findings, this claim is not supported.
  - **Resolution test** Provide a detailed description of the key residue-DNA interactions in the VAE variants compared to the wild-type proteins, with specific examples. Include quantitative MD data (e.g., RMSD plots, hydrogen bond occupancy, interaction energies) and a clear discussion of how these structural features explain the observed promoter recognition profiles.

- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 (quantitative evidence for dual-responsiveness), R1-M2 (quantitative evidence for sequence selectivity), R1-M3 (reproducibility of the computational method)
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - Originality: The concept of using VAE latent-space interpolation to generate hybrid TFs is novel and distinct from existing approaches. This is a strength.
  - Scientific importance: The work addresses a significant bottleneck in synthetic biology, namely the limited complexity of genetic circuits. If the claims are fully supported, the approach could have broad implications. However, the current evidence is insufficient to fully establish this importance.
  - Interdisciplinary readership: The work bridges computational protein design, synthetic biology, and structural biology. The topic is of interest to a broad readership, but the presentation is currently too specialized and lacks the context needed for a general audience.
  - Technical soundness: The experimental design is reasonable, but the lack of quantitative data and statistical analysis undermines the technical soundness of the claims. The computational methods are not described in sufficient detail for reproducibility.
  - Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is written in a technical style that assumes familiarity with VAE, sort-seq, and LuxR-family biology. The abstract is clear, but the results section would be difficult for a nonspecialist to follow. The key claims are not clearly highlighted.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The core concept is promising and the experimental approach is appropriate, but the manuscript lacks the quantitative detail and statistical rigor required to support the central claims. The authors should be encouraged to resubmit after providing a more complete and rigorous presentation of their data.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that "a subset of functional variants exhibited dual-responsive behavior" is unsupported by quantitative data.
- The claim that the VAE variants "maintain sequence-selective DNA recognition" is unsupported by the presented analysis of the randomized promoter library.
- The claim that the VAE variants acquire "partially novel recognition profiles" is unsupported by statistical evidence.
- The claim that structural analysis reveals "the molecular basis" of the observed properties is unsupported by the qualitative description of the structural results.
- The claim that the VAE approach is superior to or distinct from ASR in a way that matters functionally is not directly tested, as the ASR comparison is only mentioned in the Discussion and not shown in the Results.
