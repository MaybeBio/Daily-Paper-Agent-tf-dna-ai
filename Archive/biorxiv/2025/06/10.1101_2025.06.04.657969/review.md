## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and conclusions as stated in the abstract; no methods, figures, or statistical details were provided for verification
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report that physiological concentrations of yeast condensin increase the effective viscosity and elasticity of dense λ-DNA solutions in the absence of ATP; that condensin binds double-stranded DNA via its hinge domain in addition to its head domains; that ATP fluidifies the entangled solution, possibly via loop extrusion; and that the observed rheology can be modelled by treating SMCs as transient crosslinkers in bottle-brush-like entangled polymers.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no figures, tables, methods, or supplementary materials were supplied
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, experimental protocols, rheological data, single-molecule imaging data, biochemical binding assays, model details, statistical analyses, and control experiments

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a potentially interesting set of observations linking condensin activity to the viscoelastic properties of entangled DNA. The claim that condensin acts as a transient crosslinker to modulate rheology is mechanistically plausible and could be of interest to the chromosome biophysics community. However, the abstract alone provides insufficient evidence to evaluate the robustness of the experimental findings, the quantitative magnitude of the reported effects, or the validity of the proposed model. Several claims are stated as discoveries without supporting data or statistical context. The manuscript may merit consideration after full review, but the case is not established from the supplied material.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in chromosome mechanics, SMC biology, polymer physics of DNA, and biophysics of genome organisation would be interested. The work bridges single-molecule biochemistry and bulk rheology, which could appeal to both experimental and theoretical groups studying how protein complexes alter the mechanical properties of chromatin.
- **Major strengths** The abstract addresses a relatively underexplored question, namely the rheological consequences of SMC binding on entangled DNA. The combination of biochemical, single-molecule, and modelling approaches is ambitious and potentially powerful. The observation that ATP reverses the condensin-induced stiffening is a conceptually interesting result that could link loop extrusion to macroscopic material properties.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that physiological concentrations of yeast condensin increase both effective viscosity and elasticity of dense λ-DNA solutions in the absence of ATP.
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided
  - **Concern** No quantitative data are provided to support the magnitude, concentration dependence, or statistical significance of the reported increases in viscosity and elasticity. It is unclear what "physiological concentrations" means in this context, how many replicates were performed, or whether the effects were observed across a range of DNA concentrations.
  - **Why it matters** The central rheological claim is the foundation of the paper. Without quantitative rheological data, the reader cannot assess whether the effect is robust, specific to condensin, or within the range expected for a crosslinking mechanism.
  - **Resolution test** Provide rheological measurements with error bars, concentration series, and appropriate controls (e.g., non-binding proteins, heat-inactivated condensin) to demonstrate specificity and dose dependence.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Mechanistic support
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that yeast condensin binds double-stranded DNA through its hinge domain in addition to its heads.
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided
  - **Concern** The abstract states this as a discovery but provides no experimental detail. It is unclear whether this binding was demonstrated by single-molecule imaging, biochemical pull-downs, or mutagenesis. The functional relevance of hinge binding to the rheological phenotype is also not established.
  - **Why it matters** The proposed crosslinking model depends on the existence of a second DNA-binding site. If hinge binding is not directly demonstrated with appropriate controls, the model lacks a molecular basis.
  - **Resolution test** Show binding data (e.g., fluorescence colocalisation, gel shift, or surface plasmon resonance) with a hinge mutant or truncation as a negative control, and correlate hinge-binding competence with the rheological effect.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Model validation
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that the observed rheology can be understood by modelling SMCs as transient crosslinkers in bottle-brush-like entangled polymers.
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided
  - **Concern** No model parameters, fitting procedures, or comparison between model predictions and experimental data are provided. It is not possible to assess whether the model is uniquely consistent with the data or whether alternative mechanisms (e.g., DNA compaction, changes in persistence length) could explain the results.
  - **Why it matters** The modelling claim is presented as a key outcome, but without validation the reader cannot judge whether the model adds explanatory power or is merely a narrative device.
  - **Resolution test** Provide model equations, parameter values, and a quantitative fit to the rheological data, including a discussion of alternative models and why they are excluded.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M4
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Interpretation of ATP effect
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that ATP fluidifies the entangled solution possibly by activating loop extrusion.
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided
  - **Concern** The word "possibly" indicates uncertainty, but the abstract does not describe any experiments that test the loop extrusion hypothesis. It is unclear whether ATP hydrolysis is required, whether non-hydrolysable ATP analogues were tested, or whether the fluidification could be due to a different ATP-dependent process.
  - **Why it matters** The ATP-dependent transition from stiffening to fluidification is a central and potentially novel observation. If the mechanism is not tested, the claim remains speculative and weakens the overall narrative.
  - **Resolution test** Include experiments with ATPγS or other non-hydrolysable analogues, and if possible, direct visualisation of loop extrusion under the same conditions to link the rheological change to the proposed mechanism.
