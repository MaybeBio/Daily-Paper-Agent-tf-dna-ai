## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a substantial and technically impressive contribution to the field of regulatory genomics. The model architecture, which unifies long-range context, multimodal prediction, and base-pair resolution, is a significant advance over existing approaches. The breadth of evaluation across many benchmarks and the detailed ablation studies are commendable. However, the manuscript's claims of state-of-the-art performance need to be carefully scrutinized against the actual data presented in the figures, which are not fully visible in the provided text. The novelty of the architecture, while building on prior work, appears to be a meaningful integration of existing ideas rather than a radical departure. The clinical and biological insights, particularly the TAL1 analysis, are compelling but require further validation. Overall, this is a strong paper that is likely to be of high interest to the community, but the current evidence base is insufficient to fully assess the strength of all claims.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** This work will be of great interest to computational biologists, genomicists, and researchers studying gene regulation, non-coding variation, and disease genetics. The model's ability to predict variant effects across multiple modalities from sequence alone has broad applications, including variant prioritization in rare disease diagnostics, interpretation of GWAS loci, and understanding the mechanistic basis of regulatory mutations. The detailed methods and ablation studies will also be valuable for researchers developing similar sequence-to-function models.
- **Major strengths**
    1.  **Unified architecture**: The model's ability to handle 1 Mb context, base-pair resolution, and multiple modalities in a single framework is a significant technical achievement.
    2.  **Comprehensive evaluation**: The manuscript presents a wide range of benchmarks covering many regulatory modalities and variant effect prediction tasks, comparing against strong external baselines.
    3.  **Detailed ablations**: The ablation studies (Fig. 7) provide valuable insights into the contribution of key design choices, such as sequence length, resolution, and multimodal learning.
    4.  **Biological insights**: The TAL1 case study demonstrates the model's potential for generating mechanistic hypotheses about disease-associated variants.
    5.  **Practical tools**: The provision of a hosted API and interpretation suite will facilitate broad adoption and further research.
- **Major Concerns**
    - **R1-M1**: The claim of "state-of-the-art" performance is made broadly, but the specific metrics and statistical significance of the improvements over baselines are not fully detailed in the provided text. The figures are referenced but not visible, making it impossible to verify the magnitude and consistency of the reported gains.
    - **R1-M2**: The novelty of the architecture, while integrating several known components (U-Net, transformers, pair representations), needs to be more clearly articulated. The manuscript should explicitly state what is conceptually new versus an engineering integration of existing ideas.
    - **R1-M3**: The evaluation of variant effect prediction, while extensive, relies heavily on QTL and ClinVar datasets. The manuscript should discuss potential biases in these benchmarks and the generalizability of the model to other types of variants (e.g., structural variants, tandem repeats).
    - **R1-M4**: The TAL1 analysis is presented as a key demonstration of the model's utility, but the analysis is qualitative. The manuscript would be strengthened by a more quantitative assessment, such as comparing the model's predictions against experimentally validated enhancer activities or chromatin conformation data.
    - **R1-M5**: The manuscript does not fully address the potential for confounding by sequence composition or genomic context in the variant effect predictions. A discussion of model interpretability and potential failure modes would be valuable.
- **Minor Comments**
    - **R1-m1**: The abstract is dense and could benefit from a clearer statement of the model's key innovation and its most significant result.
    - **R1-m2**: The methods section is highly technical and may be challenging for non-specialist readers. A high-level schematic of the architecture and training pipeline in the main text would be helpful.
    - **R1-m3**: The description of the "splice junction prediction approach" is novel but could be explained more clearly. A specific example of how the model predicts a novel junction would be illustrative.
    - **R1-m4**: The manuscript mentions "sequence parallelism across 8 interconnected TPUv3 devices" but does not provide details on training time, computational cost, or scalability. This information is important for assessing the practical utility of the model.
    - **R1-m5**: The discussion of limitations is brief. The authors should more thoroughly discuss the model's inability to predict certain types of regulatory effects (e.g., those dependent on 3D context beyond the input window) and its performance on non-human species.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established**
    - The primary technical failing is the lack of visible data to support the quantitative claims. The figures and tables are essential to the argument and must be provided for a complete review. Without them, the strength of the reported improvements cannot be assessed.
    - The manuscript should include a more detailed comparison of the model's performance against baselines, including statistical tests for significance where appropriate.
    - The description of the model's training and inference procedures should be complete enough for reproduction, including hyperparameters, optimization details, and data preprocessing steps.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**
    - **Originality**: The work is a significant integration of existing ideas (U-Net, transformers, pair representations) into a unified framework. While not a radical conceptual departure, the specific combination and scale of the model are novel. The splice junction prediction approach appears to be a new contribution.
    - **Scientific importance**: The model addresses a central challenge in genomics: predicting the functional consequences of non-coding variation. Its ability to do so across multiple modalities from sequence alone has high scientific importance and broad applicability.
    - **Interdisciplinary readership**: The work is primarily of interest to computational biologists and genomicists. The potential applications in disease genetics and molecular biology make it relevant to a broader audience, but the technical complexity may limit its accessibility.
    - **Technical soundness**: The technical approach appears sound, with a well-motivated architecture and comprehensive evaluation. However, the lack of visible data prevents a full assessment of the robustness of the results.
    - **Readability for nonspecialists**: The manuscript is well-written but highly technical. The abstract and introduction are accessible, but the methods and results sections will be challenging for non-specialists. A clearer summary of the key findings and their implications would improve readability.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The manuscript presents a potentially important advance, but the current evidence base is insufficient to fully establish the strength of the claims. The authors must provide the full set of figures and tables, along with more detailed statistical analysis, to support their conclusions.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim of "state-of-the-art" performance on 24 out of 26 variant effect prediction tasks is not fully supported by the visible text, as the specific metrics and comparisons are not detailed.
- The claim that AlphaGenome is "the first system to predict all three" splicing-related quantities (splice sites, splice site usage, splice junctions) alongside direct RNA-seq coverage prediction is a strong claim that requires careful verification against the literature.
- The generalizability of the model to non-human species beyond mouse is not demonstrated.
- The model's performance on predicting the effects of structural variants or variants in non-coding regions far from genes is not assessed.
- The claim that the model "accurately recapitulates the mechanisms of clinically-relevant variants near the TAL1 oncogene" is based on a qualitative analysis and requires more rigorous quantitative validation.
