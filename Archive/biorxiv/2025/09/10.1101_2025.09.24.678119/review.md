## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, results, discussion, data/code availability, author contributions, competing interests, supplementary materials list)
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content and technical soundness of the claims as presented in the provided text; no line numbers were available, so all evidence pointers refer to section or figure names
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors propose that cohesin-mediated loop extrusion generates rare but long-lived encounters between pairs of genomic loci, that these encounters constitute a distinct dynamic signature of active loop extrusion, and that a time-gated model in which only such long-lived encounters are transcriptionally productive can quantitatively explain nonlinear enhancer-promoter distance-response relationships and transcriptional changes upon cohesin or NIPBL perturbation.
- **Visible evidence base** Polymer simulations (ring polymer and Rouse models with loop extrusion), live-cell imaging of two loci separated by 150 kb in mESCs (2-s frame rate, xy projection), RAD21 depletion experiments, a 50-kb control cell line, and reanalysis of previously published transcriptional data from the Sox2 locus
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Materials and Methods, Supplementary Figures 1-5, Supplementary Tables 1-2, and all figure panels are not provided in the submitted text. The manuscript text alone does not allow verification of simulation parameters, imaging analysis pipelines, statistical tests, or the quantitative fitting procedures that underpin the central claims.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript addresses a fundamental and timely question in gene regulation: how do physical encounters between enhancers and promoters translate into transcriptional output? The authors combine polymer simulations with high-resolution live-cell imaging to identify a class of rare, long-lived encounters that arise specifically from loop extrusion, and they propose a time-gated model that quantitatively links these encounters to transcriptional nonlinearities. The conceptual framework is elegant and potentially unifying. However, the strength of the conclusions depends heavily on the simulation parameters, the imaging analysis, and the quantitative fitting to transcriptional data, none of which can be fully assessed from the provided text. The manuscript is likely to be of high interest to the chromosome dynamics and gene regulation communities, but the current evidence base is insufficient to fully establish the case.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying 3D genome organization, loop extrusion, enhancer-promoter communication, transcriptional bursting, and single-cell dynamics. The work bridges polymer physics and quantitative biology, and the proposed time-gated model offers a testable framework that could influence both experimental and theoretical studies of transcriptional regulation.
- **Major strengths** The manuscript identifies a previously uncharacterized class of encounters (rare, long-lived, loop-extrusion-mediated) and provides a clear mechanistic explanation for their origin. The combination of theory and experiment is well conceived, and the authors carefully address potential confounders such as chromatin mobility differences. The prediction that productive encounters are extremely rare (less than once per cell cycle at certain separations) is striking and testable. The model's ability to quantitatively reproduce published transcriptional data without parameter fitting for NIPBL depletion is a notable strength.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness of simulations
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that their ring polymer model with loop extrusion reproduces experimentally observed contact probabilities and dynamic properties of chromatin, and that the resulting encounter duration distributions constitute a distinct signature of loop extrusion.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "Loop extrusion dynamics generates rare but long-lived encounters between pairs of loci"; Figure 1; Supplementary Figure 1; Methods (not provided)
  - **Concern** The simulation results are central to the entire manuscript, yet the provided text does not include the Methods section or the actual simulation data. Key parameters (e.g., ring polymer size, volume fraction, extrusion velocity, cohesin density, encounter radius definitions) are stated in the text, but the sensitivity of the conclusions to these parameters cannot be assessed. In particular, the claim that the ring polymer model is necessary (rather than an open-chain model) to reproduce the observed contact probability scaling is not verifiable without the corresponding figures and fitting procedures. The authors state that the same qualitative behavior is observed in an open-chain Rouse model, which raises the question of whether the ring topology is essential or merely convenient.
  - **Why it matters** The central claim that loop extrusion produces a distinct class of long-lived encounters rests entirely on the simulation framework. If the simulation parameters are not well constrained by experimental data, or if the results are sensitive to model choices, the conclusions may not be robust.
  - **Resolution test** Provide the full Methods section, including simulation details, parameter estimation procedures, and sensitivity analyses. Show that the encounter duration distributions are robust to reasonable variations in model parameters and that the ring polymer model is justified over simpler alternatives.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Experimental validation and data analysis
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that improved live-cell microscopy allows detection of the predicted dynamic signature of loop extrusion, and that the experimental data validate the model predictions.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results sections "Defining experimental conditions allowing to observe the dynamic signature of loop extrusion" and "Improved live-cell fluorescence microscopy allows experimental validation of model predictions"; Figures 2-3; Supplementary Figures 2-3; Methods (not provided)
  - **Concern** The experimental validation depends on the ability to distinguish apparent encounters (after xy projection and with localization error) from true physical encounters. The authors acknowledge that absolute encounter durations cannot be recovered, but the claim that the dynamic signature remains detectable relies on the assumption that the error is below approximately 50% of the threshold. The provided text does not include the actual distributions, the statistical comparisons, or the control analyses that would support this claim. The 50-kb cell line is a clever control, but the equivalence of spatial and dynamic properties between the two cell lines is stated without showing the data. Furthermore, the number of trajectories and movies is reported, but the statistical framework for comparing distributions is not described.
  - **Why it matters** The experimental data are the only direct evidence that the predicted long-lived encounters exist in living cells. If the analysis is not sufficiently rigorous, or if the controls do not fully exclude alternative explanations, the experimental validation is weakened.
  - **Resolution test** Provide the full imaging analysis pipeline, including localization error estimation, tracking algorithms, and encounter detection criteria. Show the actual encounter duration distributions for all conditions, with appropriate statistical tests. Demonstrate that the 50-kb control cell line indeed matches the 150-kb cell line in spatial and dynamic properties, and that the observed differences in encounter durations are not due to residual mobility differences.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Quantitative model fitting and interpretation
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that a time-gated model, in which only encounters exceeding a minimum duration are productive, quantitatively reproduces previously published measurements of Sox2 transcriptional output as a function of enhancer-promoter distance, and predicts transcriptional changes upon cohesin or NIPHL depletion.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results sections "A model of loop-extrusion mediated transcriptional activation" and "Prediction of transcriptional effects of depleting cohesin and cofactors"; Figures 4-5; Supplementary Figures 4-5; References 10-13, 15, 19
  - **Concern** The model fitting to the Sox2 data is described qualitatively, but the fitting procedure, the parameter ranges explored, and the criteria for "best agreement" are not provided. The claim that the model predicts the transcriptional effects of NIPHL depletion "without any parameter fitting" is strong, but the comparison to the experimental data from reference 13 is not shown. The model's prediction that productive encounters are extremely rare (0.001-0.1 per hour) is striking, but the relationship between these predicted encounter frequencies and the observed transcriptional bursting dynamics (reference 19) is only discussed qualitatively. The model also assumes that all encounters are equally likely to be productive once they exceed the duration threshold, which may be an oversimplification.
  - **Why it matters** The quantitative link between encounter dynamics and transcriptional output is the key conceptual contribution of the manuscript. If the fitting is not rigorous, or if the model can fit a wide range of data without being truly predictive, the conclusions are weakened.
  - **Resolution test** Provide the full fitting procedure, including the objective function, parameter ranges, and uncertainty estimates. Show the predicted versus experimental transcriptional outputs for all conditions, including the NIPHL depletion predictions. Discuss the sensitivity of the conclusions to the model assumptions, particularly the threshold duration and the relationship between encounter frequency and transcriptional bursting.
- **Minor Comments**
  - **Concern ID** R1-m1
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Clarity of definitions
  - **Affected element** Definition of "encounter" in simulations and experiments
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "Loop extrusion dynamics generates rare but long-lived encounters between pairs of loci"; Figure 1C
  - **Issue** The operational definition of an encounter (both monomers within a sphere of radius Re centered on either monomer) is stated, but the relationship between this definition and the "apparent encounters" measured experimentally is not fully clarified. The authors mention that apparent encounters are not true physical contact times, but the mapping between the two is not explicitly discussed.
  - **Required correction** Add a paragraph or figure panel that explicitly illustrates the relationship between true encounters, apparent encounters, and the effects of projection and localization error.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m2
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Interpretation of control experiments
  - **Affected element** The 50-kb cell line control
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "Improved live-cell fluorescence microscopy allows experimental validation of model predictions"; Figure 3C-D
  - **Issue** The 50-kb cell line is used to control for differences in chromatin mobility, but the text does not specify whether the genomic context (e.g., local chromatin state, presence of CTCF sites) is otherwise matched between the 50-kb and 150-kb cell lines. If the local environment differs, the control may not be fully valid.
  - **Required correction** Clarify the genomic context of the two cell lines and discuss any potential differences that could affect the interpretation.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m3
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Generalizability of the model
  - **Affected element** The claim that the model offers a "unifying framework"
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; Discussion
  - **Issue** The model is tested against data from the Sox2 locus and the Car2 promoter, but the authors claim it offers a unifying framework for enhancer-promoter communication. It is unclear whether the model would apply to loci with different chromatin environments, different cohesin densities, or different regulatory architectures.
  - **Required correction** Add a discussion of the model's assumptions and limitations, and specify the conditions under which the model is expected to apply.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** The absence of the Methods section and all supplementary figures and tables is a major limitation. The central claims of the manuscript cannot be fully evaluated without these materials. Specifically, the simulation parameters and sensitivity analyses, the imaging analysis pipeline and error estimation, and the quantitative model fitting procedures must be provided. Additionally, the statistical framework for comparing encounter duration distributions between conditions is not described.

Scientific importance: High. The work addresses a central question in gene regulation and offers a mechanistic framework that could influence both experimental and theoretical studies. Interdisciplinary readership: High. The work bridges polymer physics, cell biology, and quantitative modeling, and should appeal to readers across these fields. Technical soundness: Cannot be fully assessed from the provided text. The simulation and imaging approaches are appropriate in principle, but the details required to evaluate their rigor are missing. Readability for nonspecialists: Good. The text is clearly written and the conceptual framework is accessible, although some sections assume familiarity with polymer physics and single-molecule imaging.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that the ring polymer model is necessary to reproduce observed contact probability scaling is not verifiable without the corresponding figures and fitting procedures.
- The claim that the dynamic signature of loop extrusion is detectable in live-cell imaging under the described conditions is not fully supported without the actual encounter duration distributions and statistical comparisons.
- The claim that the time-gated model quantitatively reproduces Sox2 transcriptional data is not verifiable without the fitting procedure and the comparison to experimental data.
- The claim that the model predicts transcriptional changes upon NIPHL depletion "without any parameter fitting" is not verifiable without the predicted versus experimental comparison.
- The claim that productive encounters are extremely rare (0.001-0.1 per hour) is a strong prediction that is not directly tested in the manuscript.
- The generalizability of the model to other loci and chromatin environments is asserted but not demonstrated.
