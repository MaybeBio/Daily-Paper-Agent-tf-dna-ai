## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, materials and methods, results, discussion, acknowledgements, footnotes) as provided
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, experimental design, data interpretation, and claims as presented in the provided text. Figures, tables, and supplementary materials are referenced but not directly examined.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors used BioID proximity labelling in *Leishmania tarentolae* to identify proteins near the VTC complex in acidocalcisomes. They report known acidocalcisomal proteins (mPPase, V-H+-ATPase, Ca2+-ATPase, ZnT, PAT2) and three novel VTC binding partners (VBP1-3), validated by colocalisation, pulldown assays, and AlphaFold3 predictions. They propose that VBPs contribute to VTC complex organisation and regulation in phosphate homeostasis.
- **Visible evidence base** Methods descriptions for culture, CRISPR-Cas9 tagging, BioID, pulldown, activity assays, microscopy, and structure prediction. Results sections describing localisation, BioID identification, colocalisation, pulldown, knockout phenotypes, and AlphaFold3 predictions. References to figures (Fig 1-9) and supplementary items (S1-S11) without direct visual inspection.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Figures and supplementary tables (S1-S11) not provided. Raw mass spectrometry data not accessible. Statistical details for PCC comparisons partially reported. AlphaFold3 prediction details (e.g., full confidence metrics, multiple sequence alignments) not fully described. Western blot images and gating strategies not shown.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript addresses a relevant gap in understanding the VTC complex composition in kinetoplastids. The combination of BioID, colocalisation, pulldown, and structural prediction is appropriate and potentially impactful. However, several technical and interpretational issues need clarification. The claim of "novel binding partners" is supported by multiple lines of evidence, but the functional significance remains speculative. The knockout experiments for VBP1 show a modest phenotype that requires stronger validation. The AlphaFold3 predictions, while useful, are not definitive proof of interaction. Overall, the work is promising but requires revisions to fully establish the case.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying acidocalcisome biology, polyphosphate metabolism, kinetoplastid parasites (*Leishmania*, *Trypanosoma*), and vacuolar transporter chaperone complexes. The identification of novel interactors may inform studies on parasite adaptation, virulence, and potential drug targets. The methodological approach (BioID in *L. tarentolae*) may also interest parasitologists using proximity labelling.
- **Major strengths** 1) Addresses a clear knowledge gap in VTC complex composition in kinetoplastids. 2) Uses a multi-method approach (BioID, microscopy, pulldown, structure prediction) to validate interactions. 3) Identifies three novel, kinetoplastid-specific proteins, which is a significant finding. 4) Provides a coherent framework for future functional studies. 5) The use of *L. tarentolae* as a non-pathogenic model is practical and relevant.
- **Major Concerns** 
  - R1-M1: The functional significance of VBPs is not experimentally demonstrated. The knockout phenotype for VBP1 is modest and based on population-level analysis without clonal selection. The claim that VBPs "may contribute to structural organisation and regulatory function" is speculative without direct functional assays (e.g., polyphosphate levels, growth under stress).
  - R1-M2: The BioID data interpretation is incomplete. The manuscript reports 1,240 unique proteins but does not provide a clear filtering strategy to distinguish specific interactors from background. The criteria for selecting VBPs (presence in all datasets) is mentioned but not rigorously justified. The lack of quantitative thresholds (e.g., enrichment ratios, statistical cutoffs) weakens the confidence in the interactor list.
  - R1-M3: The AlphaFold3 predictions are used to support interactions, but the methodology is not fully described. The ipTM scores for some complexes (e.g., LtVtc1-LtVtc4 at 0.62) are below conventional confidence thresholds. The actifpTM scores are presented as supportive, but the validation of these metrics is not established. The structural models are not experimentally validated (e.g., by mutagenesis or crosslinking).
  - R1-M4: The pulldown assays lack proper controls and quantification. The manuscript does not describe negative controls (e.g., untagged cells, non-specific baits) or show input/flowthrough fractions for all experiments. The interaction strength is not quantified, and the possibility of non-specific binding during membrane protein solubilisation is not addressed.
  - R1-M5: The colocalisation analysis relies on Pearson's correlation coefficients, but the manuscript does not provide details on image acquisition parameters, background subtraction, or the number of biological replicates. The statistical comparison (one-way ANOVA) is mentioned, but the effect sizes and confidence intervals are not reported.
- **Minor Comments** 
  - R1-m1: The abstract states "three novel VTC binding partners" but the introduction mentions "three proteins of unknown function" without naming them until later. Consider naming VBPs earlier for clarity.
  - R1-m2: The methods for BioID mention solubilisation with DDM or FC-12, but the rationale for using two detergents is not explained. Clarify whether this was to capture different membrane protein populations.
  - R1-m3: The GO analysis is described, but the results are not presented in the main text. Consider summarising key enriched categories in the results section.
  - R1-m4: The discussion mentions a recent study on TcVtc6 (reference 61) but does not provide a detailed comparison of findings. Expand on how the current results align or differ.
  - R1-m5: The manuscript uses "LtVPB2" in one instance (Discussion) but "LtVBP2" elsewhere. Standardise nomenclature.
  - R1-m6: The methods for the polyP activity assay are described, but the results are only referenced in supplementary figures. Consider including a brief summary in the main text to support the claim of functional activity.
  - R1-m7: The statistical analysis for PCC values is mentioned, but the exact test parameters (e.g., normality assumptions, post-hoc corrections) are not fully described.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** 
  - R1-M2 (BioID filtering criteria) and R1-M4 (pulldown controls) are critical. Without clear thresholds and controls, the interactor list and interaction claims are not fully substantiated.
  - R1-M1 (functional significance) is important but may be addressed by additional experiments or by tempering the claims.
  - R1-M3 (AlphaFold3 validation) requires more methodological detail and acknowledgment of limitations.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - **Originality**: The identification of novel VTC interactors in kinetoplastids is original and addresses a specific gap. The use of BioID in *L. tarentolae* is a methodological advance for this organism. However, the conceptual novelty is moderate, as the VTC complex is already known, and the findings are incremental.
  - **Scientific importance**: The results are important for understanding acidocalcisome biology and polyphosphate metabolism in parasites. The potential implications for drug targeting are relevant but not directly demonstrated. The importance is moderate to high for the parasitology community.
  - **Interdisciplinary readership**: The work will appeal to parasitologists, cell biologists, and biochemists. The methods (BioID, AlphaFold3) may attract structural biologists. However, the narrow focus on a specific parasite organelle may limit broader interdisciplinary interest.
  - **Technical soundness**: The experimental design is generally sound, but the lack of detailed controls and quantitative thresholds in BioID and pulldown assays reduces confidence. The AlphaFold3 predictions are used appropriately but require more rigorous validation. The statistical analysis is adequate but could be more detailed.
  - **Readability for nonspecialists**: The manuscript is clearly written and provides sufficient background. However, the methods sections are dense, and the significance of VBPs may not be immediately clear to nonspecialists. The abstract and author summary are accessible.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The core findings are potentially significant, but the manuscript requires revisions to strengthen the evidence for specific interactions and functional relevance. The authors should address the BioID filtering, pulldown controls, and functional assays before the case is fully established.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that VBPs "may contribute to the structural organisation and regulatory function of the VTC complex" is not directly supported by functional experiments. The knockout phenotype for VBP1 is modest and based on population-level analysis.
- The statement that "the newly identified VBPs may contribute to the structural organisation and regulatory function of the VTC complex in phosphate homeostasis" is speculative and not tested.
- The assertion that "the complex was found near several known acidocalcisomal proteins" is based on BioID data, but the specificity and quantitative significance are not fully established.
- The claim that "AlphaFold3 structural predictions support complex assembly" is based on computational models without experimental validation.
- The statement that "the model indicates two additional contact sites on LtVtc4" is based on predictions and requires experimental confirmation.
- The claim that "VBP1 affects the correct localisation of LtVtc4 to the acidocalcisome" is based on a single knockout experiment without clonal selection and with modest effect size.
- The statement that "the stoichiometry and overall arrangement remain unresolved" is appropriately cautious, but the preceding claims of interaction are not fully supported.
