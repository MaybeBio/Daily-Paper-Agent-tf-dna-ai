## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, materials and methods, results, discussion, author contributions, funding, data availability)
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, technical soundness, structural claims, evolutionary claims, and data interpretation as presented in the provided text. No supplementary files, figures, or tables were available for direct inspection.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors identify a family of eight Drosophila melanogaster C2H2 zinc finger transcription factors containing a single N-terminal C2H2 domain with a conserved 'CGEx' motif. They demonstrate homodimerization and heterodimerization of these domains using yeast two-hybrid, cross-linking, and SEC-MALS. They determine the NMR solution structure of the IMZF C2H2 domain dimer, revealing a six-stranded antiparallel β-sheet interface involving an additional N-terminal β-strand. They further show that 'CGEx'-type C2H2 domains are restricted to Diptera and that C2H2 domains with additional secondary structure elements are widespread across metazoans, most prevalent in insects.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract, Introduction, Materials and Methods, Results, Discussion, Author Contributions, Funding, Data Availability. Figures and tables are referenced but not provided. Supplementary materials are referenced but not provided.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** All figures (Figure 1-5), supplementary tables (S1-S7), supplementary figures (S1-S10), supplementary files (1-2), and the deposited NMR ensemble (PDB 9VCD, BMRB-36762) were not available for inspection. This substantially limits verification of the structural claims, the bioinformatic pipeline, and the quality of the NMR data.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a potentially interesting observation: a conserved N-terminal extension of a C2H2 zinc finger domain, termed 'CGEx', mediates dimerization in a family of Drosophila transcription factors. The combination of biophysical, structural, and bioinformatic approaches is appropriate. However, the manuscript as provided has several significant weaknesses. The NMR structure is described with limited validation details, the bioinformatic survey is described with insufficient methodological transparency, and the functional significance of the dimerization is not experimentally addressed. The claim that 'CGEx'-type domains are a distinct structural subclass specific to Diptera is supported by the bioinformatic analysis but the robustness of this conclusion cannot be fully assessed without the supplementary data. The broader claim that C2H2 domains with additional secondary structure elements are widespread and likely dimerize is speculative and based on computational predictions that are not experimentally validated. The manuscript would benefit from a more focused presentation and a clearer articulation of what is novel versus what is confirmatory.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying zinc finger protein structure and function, transcription factor dimerization mechanisms, and the evolution of protein-protein interaction domains in insects. The structural data on a novel dimerization interface for a C2H2 domain will be of interest to structural biologists. The evolutionary analysis of 'CGEx' domains in Diptera will be of interest to evolutionary and comparative genomicists. The potential role of these proteins in early Drosophila development may interest developmental biologists.
- **Major strengths** 1. The identification of a conserved 'CGEx' motif in a subset of C2H2 domains is a clear and testable observation. 2. The combination of Y2H, cross-linking, and SEC-MALS provides convergent evidence for dimerization. 3. The NMR structure provides a high-resolution view of the dimer interface, which is a valuable contribution. 4. The structure-guided mutagenesis experiments directly test the proposed interface. 5. The bioinformatic survey, while needing more detail, addresses the evolutionary distribution of this domain type.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness of structural determination
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims to have determined the solution structure of the dimeric C2H2 domain from IMZF, with a backbone RMSD of 0.52 Å for well-ordered regions.
  - **Evidence pointer** Materials and Methods: NMR-solution structure of IMZF 1-62; Results: The NMR structure of IMZF 1-62; Supplementary Tables S2A, S2B, S2C; Supplementary Figures S1, S2
  - **Concern** The description of the NMR structure determination is incomplete. The manuscript states that 1674 NOE-derived distance restraints were used, but does not provide the number of unambiguous versus ambiguous restraints, the number of dihedral angle restraints, or the number of hydrogen bond restraints (beyond stating 6 inter-subunit hydrogen bonds). The final ensemble is described as having a backbone RMSD of 0.52 Å, but no information is provided on the RMSD for all heavy atoms, the percentage of residues in allowed/disallowed Ramachandran regions (only "no residues in disallowed regions" is stated), or the number of NOE violations above a certain threshold. The methods for obtaining intermolecular NOEs are described, but the number of intermolecular NOEs is not stated. Without these validation statistics, the quality of the structure cannot be properly assessed. The statement that "residual dipolar couplings (RDCs) were not included in the structure determination, as the symmetric nature of the homodimer leads to opposing orientations of N-H bond vectors in each monomer, resulting in complete cancellation of RDCs" is technically questionable. In a symmetric homodimer, RDCs do not cancel; rather, they are identical for the two subunits. The alignment tensor is the same for both monomers, so the RDCs are the same, not opposite. This suggests a misunderstanding of RDC behavior in symmetric oligomers.
  - **Why it matters** The structural model is the central piece of evidence for the proposed dimerization mechanism. If the structure is not rigorously validated, the conclusions drawn from it are weakened. The incorrect statement about RDCs raises concerns about the authors' understanding of the methods used.
  - **Resolution test** Provide a complete table of NMR restraints and structural statistics, including the number of unambiguous and ambiguous NOEs, dihedral restraints, hydrogen bond restraints, RMSD values for backbone and heavy atoms, Ramachandran statistics, and a list of NOE violations. Clarify the statement about RDCs, or remove it if it is not relevant to the structure calculation.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Support for evolutionary claims
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims that 'CGEx'-type C2H2 domains are specific to Diptera and that putatively dimerizing C2H2 domains with additional structural elements are widespread among diverse eukaryotic taxa, with the highest prevalence in insects.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results: Single N-terminal C2H2 domain defines the new family; Results: The presence of additional secondary structure elements; Materials and Methods: Bioinformatics; Supplementary Table S3, S7; Supplementary Figure S4
  - **Concern** The bioinformatic analysis is described at a high level but lacks critical details. The initial search identified 1186 orthologs, reduced to 630 after deduplication. The manuscript then states that orthologs lacking the N-terminal 'CGEx' motif were excluded, resulting in 324 sequences from HMMSEARCH, and the final dataset consisted of 449 sequences from 55 Diptera species. The relationship between these numbers is unclear. How were the 324 HMMSEARCH sequences related to the 630 orthologs? How did the final dataset grow to 449? The criteria for defining a 'CGEx' motif are not explicitly stated. The statement that 'CGEx'-type domains are "specific to Diptera" is a strong negative claim that requires a comprehensive search across all metazoan proteomes. The manuscript states that no such domains were found in other arthropods or mammals, but the search strategy and the completeness of the databases used are not described. The claim that C2H2 domains with additional secondary structure elements are "widespread" and "most prevalent in insects" is based on a computational pipeline (AlphaFold2-Multimer predictions) that is described in only one sentence. The criteria for identifying a "putatively dimerizing" domain are not given.
  - **Why it matters** The evolutionary claims are a major part of the manuscript's significance. If the bioinformatic analysis is not rigorous and reproducible, these claims are not supported. The claim of Diptera-specificity is particularly strong and requires a thorough search to be credible.
  - **Resolution test** Provide a detailed description of the bioinformatic pipeline, including database versions, search parameters, filtering criteria, and the exact definition of the 'CGEx' motif. Clarify the relationship between the different numbers of sequences. Provide a more comprehensive search for 'CGEx' domains across a wider range of taxa. Describe the criteria used to classify a domain as "putatively dimerizing" based on AlphaFold2-Multimer predictions.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Functional significance
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript implies that the dimerization of these C2H2 domains is functionally relevant for the transcription factor activity of the identified proteins.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results: Single N-terminal C2H2 domain defines the new family; Discussion
  - **Concern** The manuscript demonstrates that these domains can dimerize in vitro and in solution, but does not provide any evidence that this dimerization is functionally important. The expression data show that the genes are expressed during early development, but no experiments link dimerization to DNA binding, transcriptional regulation, or any biological process. The discussion speculates about the role of dimerization in increasing specificity and organizing long-range interactions, but these are hypotheses, not conclusions.
  - **Why it matters** The title and framing of the manuscript suggest that dimerization is a key feature of these proteins. Without functional data, the significance of the dimerization is unclear. The manuscript would be stronger if it included at least one experiment showing a functional consequence of dimerization, such as a change in DNA binding affinity or transcriptional activity.
  - **Resolution test** Include experiments that test the functional role of dimerization, such as comparing DNA binding or transcriptional activity of wild-type and dimerization-deficient mutants.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M4
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Support for heterodimerization claims
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims that the C2H2 domains are capable of forming heterodimers, primarily within their respective clusters.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results: Dimers are the main oligomeric state; Figure 2A; Supplementary Figure S5
  - **Concern** The Y2H data for heterodimerization are described qualitatively. The manuscript states that "all family members are capable of forming heterodimers, primarily within their respective clusters," but does not provide quantitative data or a clear matrix of interactions. The strength of these interactions relative to homodimerization is not discussed. The Y2H assay is a binary readout and can be prone to false positives and negatives. The manuscript does not discuss any controls for the specificity of the interactions.
  - **Why it matters** The heterodimerization network is a potentially interesting finding, but the data as presented are not sufficient to support the claim. A more rigorous analysis would include quantitative measurements of binding affinities or at least a clear presentation of the Y2H results with appropriate controls.
  - **Resolution test** Provide a complete matrix of Y2H interactions with quantitative data (e.g., β-galactosidase activity) and appropriate controls. Consider validating a subset of interactions with an orthogonal method, such as co-immunoprecipitation or pull-down assays.

  - **Concern ID** R1-m3
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Interpretation of AlphaFold predictions
  - **Affected element** Results: The presence of additional secondary structure elements
  - **Evidence pointer** Figure 5A, 5B
  - **Issue** The manuscript states that AlphaFold2-Multimer predictions were used to assess dimerization potential, but the criteria for a "positive" prediction are not stated. The text says "structures with interchain PAE score below 5.0 and pLDDT values of residues at the predicted dimerization interface above 70 were further inspected visually." This is a reasonable threshold, but it is not clear how many predictions passed these criteria and how many were rejected.
  - **Required correction** Provide the number of predictions that passed the initial filters and the number that were confirmed by visual inspection.

  - **Issue** The manuscript mentions that C2H2 domains can mediate protein-protein interactions, but the discussion of the structural mechanisms of C2H2 domain self-association is brief. A more detailed comparison with the Ikaros family dimerization mechanism and other known zinc finger dimerization modes would strengthen the discussion.

  - **Issue** The manuscript refers to "IMZF (Immune-mediated Zinc Finger) protein" but the gene name CG18262 is also used. It would be helpful to clarify the relationship between the gene name and the protein name, and to use a consistent nomenclature throughout.

- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 (NMR structure validation and the incorrect RDC statement) and R1-M2 (bioinformatic pipeline transparency and support for evolutionary claims) are the primary technical issues. R1-M3 (lack of functional data) is a significant weakness but may be considered beyond the scope of the current manuscript. R1-M4 (heterodimerization data) needs more rigorous presentation.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** **Originality:** The identification of a novel dimerization interface in a C2H2 domain is a new observation. The 'CGEx' motif and its role in dimerization have not been previously described. This is a point in favor of the manuscript. **Scientific importance:** The finding that single C2H2 domains can mediate dimerization is potentially important for understanding the function of a large family of transcription factors. However, the functional significance is not demonstrated, which limits the current importance. **Interdisciplinary readership:** The manuscript will appeal to structural biologists, molecular biologists, and evolutionary biologists. The combination of structural and evolutionary analysis is attractive. **Technical soundness:** The technical soundness is currently questionable due to the incomplete NMR validation and the potentially incorrect statement about RDCs. The bioinformatic analysis is not described in sufficient detail to be assessed. **Readability for nonspecialists:** The manuscript is generally well-written and accessible, but the technical details of the NMR and bioinformatics sections may be challenging for nonspecialists. The abstract is clear and concise.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The core observation is interesting and the structural data, if properly validated, would be a valuable contribution. However, the manuscript in its current form does not fully support its claims due to the issues outlined above. The authors must provide a complete and rigorous description of the NMR structure determination and the bioinformatic analysis. The incorrect statement about RDCs must be corrected. The lack of functional data is a weakness but may not be a barrier to publication in a specialized journal.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that 'CGEx'-type C2H2 domains are "specific to Diptera" is not fully supported by the evidence presented, as the search strategy and database coverage are not described in sufficient detail.
- The claim that C2H2 domains with additional secondary structure elements are "widespread" and "putatively dimerizing" is based on computational predictions that are not experimentally validated and are described with insufficient methodological detail.
- The statement that RDCs cancel in a symmetric homodimer is technically incorrect and should be corrected or removed.
- The functional significance of the dimerization is not demonstrated; the discussion speculates on potential roles without experimental support.
- The heterodimerization network is described qualitatively without quantitative data or appropriate controls.
