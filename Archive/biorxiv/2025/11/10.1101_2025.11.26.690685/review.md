## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, results, discussion, materials and methods) as provided
- **Assessment boundary** Methodological soundness, statistical validity, biological claims, and reproducibility of the RegEvol framework as described
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors present RegEvol, a likelihood-based framework that integrates machine learning predictions of transcription factor binding effects (via gapped k-mer SVMs) with population-genetic models of selection to detect directional selection in regulatory sequences. The method is validated through simulations, compared favorably to a previous permutation-based approach, and applied to Drosophila melanogaster and human ChIP-seq datasets, identifying directional selection signals enriched near reproductive and immune genes in flies and in nervous and male reproductive tissues in humans.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract, Introduction, Results sections 1-6, Discussion, Materials and Methods, figure citations (Figures 1-6), supplementary figure and table citations
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Supplementary figures and tables (including detailed mathematical formalism), figure panels themselves, exact parameter values for simulations, full likelihood equations, and code implementation details are referenced but not provided in the submitted material

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript presents a conceptually appealing and potentially valuable framework that addresses a genuine gap in detecting selection in regulatory sequences by linking functional predictions to explicit population-genetic models. The core idea of using predicted phenotypic effects to parameterize fitness landscapes is well-motivated and represents a meaningful advance over purely sequence-based or permutation-based approaches. However, the current manuscript has several technical concerns that need to be addressed before the case is fully established. The simulation validation is described at a high level, and key details about the likelihood framework, parameter estimation, and model comparison procedures are deferred to supplementary materials. The biological applications, while suggestive, rely on aggregated signals and would benefit from more rigorous validation of the underlying assumptions. The claim of robustness to ascertainment bias is supported by simulation but requires more careful examination of real-data scenarios. Overall, the framework is promising and the results are interesting, but the manuscript would benefit from more transparent presentation of the statistical machinery and more thorough sensitivity analyses.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Evolutionary biologists studying regulatory sequence evolution, population geneticists interested in detecting selection on non-coding regions, computational biologists developing functional genomics-based evolutionary inference methods, and researchers studying adaptation in Drosophila and mammalian systems. The framework's flexibility to incorporate different predictive models makes it relevant to a broad community interested in linking genotype to phenotype in an evolutionary context.
- **Major strengths** 1) The conceptual advance of using machine learning-based functional predictions to parameterize explicit population-genetic fitness models is novel and well-motivated. 2) The nested model framework (neutral, stabilising, directional) provides a principled statistical approach to model comparison. 3) The aggregation strategy for low-divergence datasets addresses a practical limitation and is a useful contribution. 4) The validation against a previous permutation-based method demonstrates clear improvements in sensitivity and specificity. 5) The application to large-scale Drosophila and human datasets provides biologically interesting results.

- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: The approach of integrating ML-based functional predictions with explicit population-genetic models of selection is genuinely novel and represents a conceptual advance over existing methods. Scientific importance: The ability to detect directional selection in regulatory sequences with functional resolution addresses a significant gap in evolutionary genomics and has broad implications for understanding adaptation. Interdisciplinary readership: The work bridges machine learning, population genetics, and functional genomics, making it relevant to multiple communities, though the technical complexity may limit accessibility. ### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Technical soundness
- **Claim pointer** The likelihood framework with nested models (neutral, stabilising, directional) can reliably distinguish between these selective regimes and identify the best-fitting scenario for each peak.
- **Evidence pointer** Results section 3, Figure 3, Supplementary Figure 5
- **Concern** The manuscript reports high accuracy in identifying the correct evolutionary model in simulations, but the details of the likelihood framework, including the exact form of the fitness functions, the parameterization of the Beta distributions, and the optimization procedure, are deferred to supplementary materials. This makes it difficult to assess whether the model comparison is well-calibrated, whether the parameters are identifiable, and whether the likelihood ratio tests are appropriately specified. The claim that the method is "conservative" and controls false positives is supported by simulation results, but the mechanism by which this conservativeness is achieved is not fully explained. Specifically, the manuscript does not clarify how the likelihood ratio test threshold of 1% is calibrated, whether the test statistics follow the expected asymptotic distributions, or how the nested model structure affects the multiple testing burden.
- **Why it matters** The statistical validity of the inference framework is the core contribution of the manuscript. Without transparent presentation of the likelihood machinery and validation of the model comparison procedure, readers cannot assess whether the reported results are reliable or whether the method would perform well on other datasets. The claim of conservativeness is particularly important because it underlies the interpretation of the biological results.
- **Resolution test** Provide the full mathematical formalism of the likelihood framework in the main text or a detailed supplement, including the exact form of the fitness functions, the parameterization of the Beta distributions, the computation of fixation probabilities, and the optimization procedure. Include calibration plots showing the distribution of p-values under the null hypothesis for each model comparison, and demonstrate that the likelihood ratio test statistics follow the expected null distributions. Show parameter recovery simulations demonstrating that the model parameters are identifiable across the range of biologically plausible values.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Technical soundness
- **Claim pointer** The aggregation strategy, which sums likelihood differences across sets of regulatory elements, can increase statistical power when substitution numbers are limited, and reveals enrichment of directional signals in nervous and male reproductive tissues in human CTCF binding.
- **Evidence pointer** Results section 6, Figure 6, Materials and Methods section "Tissue-level aggregation analysis of human CTCF binding sites"
- **Concern** The aggregation strategy is a key methodological contribution, but the manuscript provides limited details on how the aggregation is performed, how the resampling procedure controls for differences in sample size, and how statistical significance is assessed. The description mentions 10,000 resamplings of 5,000 peaks per tissue, but does not clarify whether the resampling is performed with replacement, how the null distribution is generated, or whether the Wilcoxon rank-sum tests are appropriately corrected for multiple comparisons across tissues. The claim that the nervous system shows a "strong positive shift" (Cliff's delta = 0.55) while the male reproductive system shows a "smaller positive shift" (Cliff's delta = 0.23) is presented without confidence intervals or effect size variability. Additionally, the manuscript does not address potential confounding factors, such as differences in chromatin state, replication timing, or gene density across tissues, which could affect the interpretation of the aggregated signals.
- **Why it matters** The aggregation strategy is presented as a solution to the low power problem in short-branch analyses, and the human CTCF results are a key biological finding. If the aggregation methodology is not rigorously validated, the biological conclusions drawn from it may be unreliable. The lack of detail on the resampling procedure and statistical testing makes it difficult to assess whether the reported enrichment is robust.
- **Resolution test** Provide a detailed description of the aggregation procedure, including the exact resampling scheme, the construction of the null distribution, and the multiple testing correction. Show that the aggregation strategy has appropriate type I error control across a range of simulation scenarios, including varying numbers of peaks, varying substitution counts, and varying effect sizes. Provide confidence intervals for the Cliff's delta estimates and demonstrate that the tissue-level results are robust to alternative analysis choices, such as different peak filtering criteria or different resampling schemes.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Technical soundness
- **Claim pointer** The gSVM-based predictions of transcription factor binding capture functional signals beyond sequence conservation, as evidenced by correlations with ChIP-seq read coverage and the ability to identify functional binding sites.
- **Evidence pointer** Results section 1, Figure 1, Supplementary Figures 1-4
- **Concern** The manuscript claims that SVM-based metrics capture TF binding intensity more accurately than conservation-based scores, based on correlations with ChIP-seq read coverage. However, the analysis is limited to a single TF (CEBPA) in the main text, with additional TFs relegated to supplementary figures. The median Pearson correlation coefficients reported (0.31 for SVM, 0.02 for conservation scores) are modest, and the manuscript does not report the variability of these correlations across TFs or the statistical significance of the differences. More importantly, the manuscript does not address whether the SVM predictions are calibrated across different sequence contexts, whether the ASVM metric is appropriate for capturing the full range of mutational effects, or whether the choice of gSVM over other predictive models (e.g., deep learning) affects the results. The claim that the model captures "functional signals beyond sequence conservation" is supported by the correlation analysis, but the biological interpretation of this finding is not fully developed.
- **Why it matters** The quality of the genotype-to-phenotype map is fundamental to the entire framework. If the SVM predictions are noisy or biased, the downstream inference of selection will be affected. The manuscript needs to demonstrate that the SVM-based predictions are sufficiently accurate and well-calibrated across diverse sequence contexts to support the evolutionary inferences.
- **Resolution test** Provide a more comprehensive validation of the SVM predictions, including correlation analyses across multiple TFs and species, calibration plots showing the relationship between predicted and observed binding effects, and a comparison of gSVM performance to alternative predictive models. Show that the ASVM metric is robust to the choice of sequence context and that the SVM predictions are not systematically biased by GC content, repeat content, or other sequence features.

- **Concern ID** R1-M4
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Technical soundness
- **Claim pointer** The method is robust to ascertainment bias in ChIP-seq peak detection, maintaining low false positive rates across different peak quality categories.
- **Evidence pointer** Results section 4, Figure 4, Supplementary Figures 8-9
- **Concern** The manuscript addresses ascertainment bias by subsampling peaks by derived ASVM and showing that RegEvol maintains a low false positive rate. However, the analysis is limited to simulated data, and the manuscript does not address whether the method is robust to other forms of bias that may affect real ChIP-seq datasets, such as biases in peak calling, differences in antibody quality, or variation in chromatin accessibility across cell types. The claim that the method is "robust to peak strength" is supported by the simulation results, but the manuscript does not discuss the potential impact of these other biases on the biological results. Additionally, the manuscript reports that the permutation test shows an increased false positive rate with high ASVM values, but does not fully explain the mechanism by which RegEval avoids this bias.
- **Why it matters** ChIP-seq data are subject to multiple sources of bias, and the robustness of the method to these biases is critical for the reliability of the biological conclusions. The manuscript needs to demonstrate that the method is robust to realistic sources of bias in ChIP-seq data, not just the specific form of ascertainment bias tested in simulations.
- **Resolution test** Provide additional simulations that introduce realistic sources of bias, such as peak calling errors, variable signal-to-noise ratios, and differences in peak width, and demonstrate that RegEvol maintains appropriate false positive rates and power. Discuss the potential impact of these biases on the biological results and provide sensitivity analyses that vary key analysis parameters.

### Minor Comments

- **Concern ID** R1-m1
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Clarity and reproducibility
- **Affected element** Materials and Methods, "Evolutionary Model Inference via Likelihood Optimisation"
- **Evidence pointer** Materials and Methods section
- **Issue** The description of the likelihood optimization procedure is brief and lacks specific details on the optimization algorithm, convergence criteria, and handling of local optima. The manuscript states that "model parameters (a, P) were optimised using the scipy.optimize.minimize function" but does not specify the optimization method, the number of starting points, or the bounds on the parameters.
- **Required correction** Provide a more detailed description of the optimization procedure, including the specific algorithm used, the parameter bounds, the number of random starting points, and the convergence criteria. If multiple optima are possible, describe how the global optimum is identified.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that RegEvol "corrects biases that affected previous tests based on machine learning of transcription factor binding" is supported by simulation results but requires more thorough validation on real data with known ground truth.
- The claim that the aggregation strategy "reveals enrichment of directional signals in nervous and male reproductive systems" in human CTCF binding is based on a single analysis and requires replication with alternative methods or datasets.
- The claim that the method is "readily applicable to experimentally detected regulatory elements with alignable ancestral sequences" is plausible but not demonstrated with a real example outside of the two presented datasets.
- The claim that the framework is "flexible to future advances in understanding regulatory function" is speculative and not directly supported by the presented results.
- The biological interpretation of the Drosophila results, including the enrichment near reproductive and immune genes, is presented without detailed functional validation or discussion of potential confounding factors.
