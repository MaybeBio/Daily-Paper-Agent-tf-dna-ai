- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The abstract reports that the intrinsically disordered RelA transactivation domain (TAD) in the NFκB p50/RelA heterodimer is compact and partially helical in solution, increases the stoichiometry of NFκB-DNA complexes on tandem κB site DNA, enhances binding affinity for all tested κB sequences, and increases affinity for non-specific DNA by over 10-fold, thereby reducing overall specificity for κB sites. ## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents an intriguing and potentially significant finding that challenges the modular view of transcription factor domains. The claim that the RelA TAD alters DNA binding affinity and specificity is novel and could have broad implications for understanding NFκB function. However, the abstract alone provides insufficient detail to evaluate the robustness of the biophysical measurements, the experimental design, or the statistical significance of the reported effects. The central claims are plausible but not fully established from the supplied material.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in transcription regulation, intrinsically disordered proteins, NFκB signaling, and biophysical characterization of protein-DNA interactions would find these results relevant. The finding that a TAD can modulate DNA binding specificity may interest those studying allosteric regulation in multi-domain transcription factors and those interpreting in vivo chromatin binding data.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses a timely and under-explored question about the functional interdependence of transcription factor domains. The use of biophysical methods to characterize a disordered TAD in the context of a full-length heterodimer is commendable. The connection to in vivo observations of non-consensus NFκB binding under strong inflammatory signals provides biological relevance.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence quality  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that the presence of the TAD increased the stoichiometry of NFκB-DNA complexes on tandem κB site DNA by promoting binding of dimers in excess of the number of κB sites.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
- **Concern** The stoichiometry claim is presented without any quantitative data, such as the measured stoichiometric ratios, the methods used (e.g., sedimentation equilibrium, native gel electrophoresis, or fluorescence), or the range of DNA and protein concentrations tested.  
- **Why it matters** Stoichiometry changes are central to the proposed mechanism. Without experimental details, it is unclear whether the observed excess binding is specific to the TAD or an artifact of the assay conditions.  
- **Resolution test** Provide representative stoichiometry measurements with error bars, a description of the assay, and control experiments showing that the effect is TAD-dependent (e.g., using a TAD-deleted construct).

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Quantitative support  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract states that the TAD enhanced binding affinity for all κB sequences tested but increased affinity for non-specific DNA by over 10-fold, leading to decreased specificity.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
- **Concern** No binding affinities (e.g., Kd values) or specificity ratios are reported. The 10-fold increase for non-specific DNA is a key quantitative claim, but the abstract does not indicate how many sequences were tested, what constituted "non-specific" DNA, or the statistical significance of the differences.  
- **Why it matters** The specificity decrease is the main functional conclusion. Without quantitative affinity data and appropriate controls, the claim cannot be evaluated or reproduced.  
- **Resolution test** Include a table of Kd values for all DNA sequences tested, with standard deviations and p-values for comparisons between TAD-containing and TAD-lacking constructs.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Mechanistic interpretation  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract proposes a novel function of the RelA TAD in promoting binding to non-consensus DNA, linking to in vivo studies of NFκB-DNA binding under strong inflammatory signals.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
- **Concern** The connection between the in vitro biophysical observations and the cited in vivo studies is asserted but not detailed. The abstract does not specify which in vivo studies are referenced, how the non-consensus binding was defined, or whether the in vitro conditions mimic the inflammatory context.  
- **Why it matters** The biological relevance of the finding depends on this link. Without a clear description of the in vivo evidence and how it aligns with the in vitro data, the claim remains speculative.  
- **Resolution test** Provide a brief description of the relevant in vivo studies, including the cell types, stimuli, and methods used to assess NFκB-DNA binding, and discuss how the in vitro results quantitatively or qualitatively match those observations.
