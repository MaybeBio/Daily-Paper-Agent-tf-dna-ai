Fever is a typical symptom of most infectious diseases. While prolonged fever may be clinically undesirable, mild reversible fever (< 39°C, 312K) can potentiate the immune responses against pathogens. Here, using molecular dynamics, we investigated the effect of febrile temperatures (38°C to 40°C, 311K to 313K) on the immune complexes formed by the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein with two neutralizing antibodies. We found that, at mild fever temperatures (311-312K), the binding affinities of the two antibodies improve when compared to the physiological body temperature (37°C, 310K). Furthermore, only at 312K, antibodies exert distinct mechanical effects on the receptor binding domains of the spike protein that may hinder SARS-CoV-2 infectivity. Enhanced antibody binding affinity may thus be obtained using appropriate temperature conditions.
