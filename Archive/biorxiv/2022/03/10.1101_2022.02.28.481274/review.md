## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and conclusions as presented in the abstract; no evaluation of methods, figures, or statistical analyses beyond what is stated
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report a co-crystal structure of a truncated *Bacillus subtilis* Noc protein bound to NBS DNA, showing that the N-terminal CTP-binding domain (NTD) is disengaged from the DNA-binding domain (DBD) in each subunit. This disengagement is partly driven by swapping of helices 4 and 5 at the domain interface. Site-specific crosslinking data suggest the conformation exists in solution. The results are interpreted as supporting a model where NBS binding prevents re-engagement of NTD and DBD from the same subunit, thereby catalyzing CTP binding and DNA entrapment.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no figures, tables, methods, or supplementary materials provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, structural coordinates, crystallographic statistics, crosslinking experimental details, sequence constructs, and any validation data are not available. The abstract alone does not permit independent verification of structural interpretation or solution-state claims.

## Reviewer

The proposed domain disengagement and helix swapping mechanism is plausible and aligns with recent proposals in the field. However, the evidence base is limited to the abstract, and several claims, particularly those regarding solution-state conformation and mechanistic implications, cannot be fully assessed without the full manuscript. The work is likely of interest to structural biologists and researchers studying chromosome segregation and cell division, but the current evidence is insufficient to establish the case definitively.

The observation of domain disengagement and helix swapping provides a concrete conformational model for the Noc-NBS complex at a critical step. The inclusion of crosslinking data to support solution-state relevance strengthens the structural finding. The interpretation connects the structure to a broader mechanistic model, which is intellectually valuable.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Technical soundness  
- **Claim pointer** The co-crystal structure reveals disengagement between the CTP-binding domain and the NBS-binding domain of each DNA-bound Noc subunit.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; no crystallographic statistics or structural validation data provided  
- **Concern** The abstract states the structure is solved but provides no information on resolution, refinement quality, or model validation. Without these data, the reliability of the observed domain disengagement and helix swapping cannot be assessed.  
- **Why it matters** Structural claims are only as strong as the underlying crystallographic evidence. Poor resolution or model bias could lead to misinterpretation of domain arrangements.  
- **Resolution test** Provide crystallographic statistics (resolution, R-free, Ramachandran analysis) and electron density maps for the domain interface and helix swapping region in the full manuscript.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Technical soundness  
- **Claim pointer** Site-specific crosslinking data suggest that this conformation of Noc-NBS exists in solution.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; no crosslinking methods or controls described  
- **Concern** The abstract claims solution-state relevance based on crosslinking, but no details are given on the crosslinking strategy, residues targeted, or controls for specificity. Without these, the claim that the crystal conformation reflects the solution state is unsubstantiated.  
- **Why it matters** A crystal structure may capture a non-physiological conformation. The crosslinking data are essential to bridge the structural and solution states, but the abstract does not provide enough information to evaluate their validity.  
- **Resolution test** Describe the crosslinking approach, including residues modified, reaction conditions, and negative controls, and show that crosslinking efficiency is consistent with the proposed domain disengagement.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** No  
- **Axis** Scientific importance  
- **Claim pointer** The results lend support to the proposal that NBS binding catalyzes CTP binding and DNA entrapment by preventing re-engagement of NTD and DBD from the same subunit.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; no functional or biochemical data beyond the structure and crosslinking  
- **Concern** The mechanistic conclusion is extrapolated from a static structure and crosslinking data. The abstract does not present any direct evidence that the disengaged conformation is causally linked to CTP binding or DNA entrapment.  
- **Why it matters** The broader significance of the work depends on this mechanistic link. Without functional validation, the claim remains speculative.  
- **Resolution test** Provide additional experiments, such as mutagenesis of the helix-swapping interface or CTP-binding assays, to demonstrate that the disengaged conformation is required for CTP binding and downstream function.
