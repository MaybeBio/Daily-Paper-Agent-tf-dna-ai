## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence as presented in the abstract; no figures, tables, methods, or supplementary materials were provided
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors propose that aromatic character in the androgen receptor (AR) activation domain is essential for transcription factor activity via partitioning into transcriptional condensates. They then use this insight to rationally optimize a small molecule inhibitor targeting a partially folded, aromatic-rich transactivation unit. Optimized compounds are reported to show increased target affinity, inhibition of AR-dependent transcriptional programs, and antitumorigenic effects in cell and in vivo models of castration-resistant prostate cancer (CRPC). A generalizable framework for targeting activation domains of oncogenic transcription factors is claimed.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no experimental data, statistical details, compound structures, or model descriptions are available
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, all figures and tables, methods, compound synthesis and characterization data, biophysical binding data, transcriptomic datasets, in vivo study details, and any statistical analyses

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a conceptually appealing and potentially impactful strategy for targeting intrinsically disordered transcription factor activation domains, with a clear translational angle toward CRPC. However, the evidence base available for this review is limited to the abstract, which precludes verification of the central mechanistic claims, the quality of the optimization process, and the robustness of the preclinical efficacy data. The claim of a generalizable framework is ambitious and currently unsupported by the provided material. The work is likely of interest to the transcription factor biology and drug discovery communities, but the case is not established from the abstract alone.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in transcription factor biology, intrinsically disordered proteins, transcriptional condensates, and targeted cancer therapy would find the results relevant. The proposed framework for drugging activation domains could attract medicinal chemists and structural biologists working on challenging therapeutic targets. Clinicians and translational researchers focused on prostate cancer, particularly CRPC, would also be interested given the reported antitumorigenic effects.
- **Major strengths** The abstract identifies a specific molecular feature (aromatic character) as functionally important for AR activation domain activity and links it to condensate partitioning, providing a mechanistic rationale for inhibitor design. The optimization of a previously identified phenotypic hit into higher-affinity compounds with in vivo efficacy represents a logical and potentially generalizable approach. The work addresses a well-recognized challenge in drug discovery, namely targeting intrinsically disordered regions of transcription factors.
- **Major Concerns**  
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1  
  - **Severity** Major  
  - **Blocking** Yes  
  - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
  - **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that aromatic character of the AR activation domain is key for its activity by allowing partitioning into transcriptional condensates.  
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided  
  - **Concern** The mechanistic claim linking aromatic character to condensate partitioning and transcriptional activity is presented without any supporting data. No experimental evidence is described that would establish a causal relationship, such as mutagenesis studies, biophysical measurements of phase separation, or functional assays with aromatic-altered variants.  
  - **Why it matters** This claim is foundational to the rational optimization strategy. If the mechanistic link is not robustly demonstrated, the rationale for the optimization approach is weakened, and the generalizability of the framework is called into question.  
  - **Resolution test** Provide mutagenesis data showing that aromatic residue substitutions reduce condensate partitioning and transcriptional activity, with quantitative biophysical and cellular assays. Include appropriate controls and statistical analysis.

  - **Concern ID** R1-M2  
  - **Severity** Major  
  - **Blocking** Yes  
  - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
  - **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that optimized compounds had more affinity for their target, inhibited AR-dependent transcriptional programs, and had antitumorigenic effect in models of CRPC in cells and in vivo.  
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided  
  - **Concern** No quantitative data are provided for binding affinity, transcriptional inhibition, or antitumorigenic efficacy. The abstract does not specify the magnitude of improvement over the original inhibitor, the selectivity of the compounds, or the statistical significance of the in vivo results. Without these details, the strength of the optimization and the validity of the efficacy claims cannot be assessed.

  - **Resolution test** Include binding affinity values (e.g., Kd or IC50) for original and optimized compounds, dose-response curves for transcriptional inhibition, and in vivo tumor growth data with statistical comparisons to controls and to the original compound.
