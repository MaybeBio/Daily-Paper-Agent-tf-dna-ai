## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, results, discussion, methods, figure legends, table)
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific validity, technical soundness, and support of claims based solely on the provided manuscript content
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report crystal structures of NF-κB p52:p52 homodimer bound to natural G/C-centric PSel-κB DNA and variants, revealing a widened minor groove distinct from prior NF-κB-DNA complexes. They combine these structures with MD simulations, ITC, BLI, and luciferase reporter assays to propose that DNA sequence dictates intrinsic conformational preferences, and that faster binding kinetics (entropy-driven) correlate with higher transcriptional activation by p52:p52:Bcl3.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract, full introduction, full results, full discussion, full methods, Table 1 (crystallographic summary), figure legends (Figs 1-6), supplemental figure legends (S1-S7), supplemental table legend (S1)
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Actual figures and supplemental figures (images, plots, gels) are not provided; only legends are visible. Electron density maps, refinement validation (e.g., Ramachandran statistics, B-factor distributions) are not shown. MD simulation convergence metrics and force field validation details are not fully described. BLI and ITC raw data traces are not shown. Luciferase assay numerical values and statistical test details are not fully specified.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript addresses an important and timely question in gene regulation: how subtle DNA sequence variations at non-contacted positions influence transcription factor binding dynamics and downstream transcriptional outcomes. The structural finding of a widened minor groove in p52:p52-PSel-κB DNA complexes is novel and potentially significant, as it contrasts with the compressed minor groove observed in all prior NF-κB-DNA structures. The integration of structural, computational, biophysical, and cellular data is ambitious and commendable. However, the manuscript has several technical and interpretive gaps that currently prevent full support of the central claims. The correlation between binding kinetics and transcriptional activity is based on a small number of sites and lacks mechanistic depth. The role of the Bcl3 cofactor in modulating DNA binding kinetics is not fully explored. The structural basis for the widened minor groove and its functional consequences require stronger validation, particularly regarding the influence of crystal packing and the lack of electron density for the GRR region. Overall, the work is potentially important but requires substantial revision and additional evidence to fully establish the proposed model.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Structural biologists studying transcription factor-DNA recognition, biochemists and biophysicists interested in protein-DNA binding kinetics and thermodynamics, and researchers in the NF-κB field, particularly those focused on non-canonical signaling and Bcl3-dependent gene regulation. The findings may also be of interest to computational biologists studying DNA conformational dynamics and sequence-dependent deformability.
- **Major strengths** 1. The structural determination of p52:p52 in complex with G/C-centric DNA fills a gap, as prior structures used A/T-centric sites. 2. The observation of a widened minor groove is a clear and novel finding that challenges the prevailing view of compressed minor grooves in NF-κB-DNA complexes. 3. The combination of multiple biophysical techniques (ITC, BLI) with structural and computational approaches provides a multi-faceted view of the binding process. 4. The correlation between faster off-rates and higher transcriptional activity is an interesting hypothesis that could have broad implications for transcription factor biology.
- **Major Concerns** 
  - **R1-M1** (Major, Blocking: Yes): The claim that the widened minor groove is a general feature of p52:p52-G/C-centric DNA complexes is not fully supported. Only one natural G/C-centric sequence (PSel) and its variants were crystallized. The authors did not crystallize p52:p52 with other G/C-centric κB sites (e.g., Skp2) to demonstrate generality. The single example could be sequence-specific rather than a general property of G/C-centric sites.
  - **R1-M2** (Major, Blocking: Yes): The functional link between binding kinetics and transcriptional activity is correlative and based on only a few DNA sites. The manuscript does not provide a mechanistic explanation for why faster off-rates would lead to higher transcriptional activation. The authors speculate that slower off-rates allow HDAC3 recruitment, but this is not tested. The correlation could be coincidental or driven by other factors (e.g., differences in Bcl3 recruitment, chromatin context).
  - **R1-M3** (Major, Blocking: No): The MD simulations are used to argue that free DNAs exist in distinct preferred conformations and that p52 induces minimal conformational changes on the natural PSel-κB DNA. However, the simulations were started from the bound conformations, which could bias the results. The manuscript does not report simulations starting from canonical B-form DNA to test whether the widened minor groove is intrinsically preferred by the sequence. Convergence of the simulations is not demonstrated.
  - **R1-M4** (Major, Blocking: No): The role of the GRR region in the observed structural differences is speculative. The authors note that no electron density was observed for the GRR, yet they attribute the widened minor groove and altered dimer organization to the longer p52 construct. The comparison between long (1-398) and short (1-327) p52 is confounded by the use of different DNA lengths (18 bp vs 13 bp). A more controlled experiment (e.g., same DNA length with different p52 constructs) is needed.
  - **R1-M5** (Major, Blocking: No): The ITC and BLI experiments are described, but the results are not shown in the visible text. The manuscript states that binding affinities are similar, but the thermodynamic parameters (ΔH, ΔS) are only qualitatively described. The BLI kinetic data are not shown, and the fitting model (1:1) may not be appropriate for a homodimer binding to DNA with potential cooperativity.
- **Minor Comments** 
  - **R1-m1** (Minor, Axis: Clarity): The abstract states "a nearly two-fold widened minor groove" but the comparison baseline is not clearly defined. Is this relative to B-form DNA or to other NF-κB-DNA complexes? Please clarify.
  - **R1-m2** (Minor, Axis: Technical detail): The methods for MD simulations state five replicas of 2 µs each, but the clustering and hydrogen bond analyses are not described in sufficient detail. Please specify the clustering criteria and the definition of hydrogen bonds used.
  - **R1-m3** (Minor, Axis: Statistical reporting): The luciferase assay data are described as "mean standard deviations (SD) of three independent experiments in triplicates," but the statistical test used to determine significance is not stated. Please specify the test and the p-values.
  - **R1-m4** (Minor, Axis: Figure clarity): Figure 1I compares minor groove widths, but the exact sequences and positions are not clearly labeled. Please ensure all DNA sequences are shown with position numbering consistent with the text.
  - **R1-m5** (Minor, Axis: Completeness): The manuscript mentions "Supplemental Fig. S2E" listing prior NF-κB-DNA structures, but the list is not exhaustive. Please ensure all relevant structures are included and that the sequence alignment is clear.
  - **R1-m6** (Minor, Axis: Interpretation): The discussion of "entropy-driven" binding for the natural PSel-κB DNA is interesting, but the authors do not discuss the potential role of water release or ion redistribution in driving entropic changes. Please consider these factors.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** 1. Lack of structural data for a second G/C-centric κB site to support generality (R1-M1). 2. Lack of a mechanistic test for the proposed kinetic model of transcriptional regulation (R1-M2). 3. MD simulation setup and convergence not adequately described or validated (R1-M3). 4. Confounded comparison between long and short p52 constructs (R1-M4). 5. Missing raw data for ITC, BLI, and luciferase assays (R1-M5).
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - **Originality**: High. The widened minor groove in a G/C-centric κB DNA complex is a novel observation that challenges existing paradigms. The integration of kinetics with transcriptional output is also a fresh perspective.
  - **Scientific importance**: Moderate to high. If the kinetic model is correct, it could have broad implications for understanding how transcription factors discriminate between DNA sites. However, the current evidence is correlative and needs stronger support.
  - **Interdisciplinary readership**: Moderate. The work will appeal to structural biologists, biophysicists, and NF-κB researchers. The broader transcription community may find the kinetic model interesting, but the current presentation is somewhat specialized.
  - **Technical soundness**: Moderate. The structural work appears sound, but the MD simulations and biophysical assays have gaps in validation and reporting. The lack of raw data reduces confidence.
  - **Readability for nonspecialists**: Moderate. The manuscript is generally well-written, but the discussion of thermodynamics and kinetics may be challenging for readers without a biophysics background. The abstract is clear and accessible.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The core structural finding is novel and potentially important, but the functional claims require additional evidence. The authors should address the concerns regarding generality, mechanistic testing, and technical validation before the manuscript can be considered for publication in a high-impact journal.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that the widened minor groove is a general feature of p52:p52-G/C-centric DNA complexes is unsupported, as only one natural G/C-centric sequence was crystallized.
- The claim that faster binding kinetics (entropy-driven) correlate with higher transcriptional activity is correlative and lacks mechanistic validation.
- The claim that the GRR region plays a role in the observed structural differences is speculative, as no electron density was observed for this region.
- The MD simulation results are used to support the idea that free DNAs have distinct preferred conformations, but the simulations were started from bound conformations, which may bias the results.
- The statement that "p52:p52 homodimer induces the least amount of conformational changes on the more transcriptionally active natural PSel-κB DNA" is based on a comparison of only a few sequences and may not be generalizable.
