## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, data availability, author contributions, figure attribution, funding, supplementary data list)
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific claims, methodological soundness, validation quality, software utility, and presentation. Code functionality was not independently executed or verified.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors present TF-COMB, a Python-based tool using market basket analysis to identify co-occurring transcription factor binding sites and investigate binding grammar (distance, orientation) from diverse genomic data sources including ChIP-seq, ATAC-seq footprints, and motif positions. They claim accurate identification of known co-occurring TFs, discovery of positional preferences, and network-level insights into TF complexes.
- **Visible evidence base** Main text sections 1-4, abstract, figure legends referenced in text, supplementary table descriptions. No actual figures, tables, or supplementary data files were provided for inspection.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** All figures and supplementary tables are referenced but not included. Code repository and documentation are linked but were not reviewed. Statistical methods for Z-score calculation, cosine score thresholds, and null model construction are described only at a high level.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript describes a potentially useful software tool for investigating TF co-occurrence and binding grammar. The conceptual framing is clear and the application domains are relevant. However, the evidence presented in the text alone is insufficient to fully evaluate the tool's performance, validation rigor, or biological claims. Several key methodological details are missing, and the validation strategy relies heavily on literature concordance without quantitative benchmarks against existing tools. The manuscript would benefit from clearer presentation of statistical methods and more rigorous comparative analysis.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying transcriptional regulation, enhancer biology, and gene regulatory networks would find this tool relevant. Computational biologists developing or using TF binding analysis methods would be the primary audience. The tool's flexibility in handling multiple data types (ChIP-seq, ATAC-seq, motifs) and its network analysis capabilities could appeal to a broad segment of the regulatory genomics community.
- **Major strengths** The tool addresses a genuine gap in available software for TF co-occurrence analysis. The flexible input design and inclusion of binding grammar parameters (distance, orientation) are thoughtful features. The application to multiple data types and the network analysis module extend beyond typical pair-wise co-occurrence tools. The authors demonstrate awareness of limitations in existing approaches and attempt to address them.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims TF-COMB calculates a Z-score of significance using a null model of random co-occurrences generated by shuffling TF labels.
  - **Evidence pointer** Section 2, Approach of TF-COMB; location not provided
  - **Concern** The description of the null model and Z-score calculation is insufficiently detailed. The text states TF labels are shuffled, but does not specify how many permutations are performed, how the null distribution is constructed, whether the shuffling preserves genomic clustering properties, or how multiple testing is controlled across the large number of TF pairs tested.
  - **Why it matters** The Z-score is a primary filter for identifying significant co-occurrences. If the null model does not appropriately account for the non-random genomic distribution of TF binding sites, the significance estimates could be systematically biased, leading to false positives or false negatives. This directly affects the validity of all downstream biological claims.
  - **Resolution test** Provide a complete statistical description including permutation count, null distribution construction, multiple testing correction method, and validation of null model calibration (e.g., QQ plots or empirical false positive rates).
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Validation rigor
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims TF-COMB has the best predictive ability compared to MCOT and SpaMo based on ROC curve analysis using known PPIs from BioGRID.
  - **Evidence pointer** Section 4.1, Results; Supplementary Figure 1a; location not provided
  - **Concern** The ROC analysis is described in a single sentence without methodological detail. It is unclear what constitutes a "positive" prediction, how the ROC curve is generated from ranked scores, whether the comparison is fair across tools with different output formats, and whether the BioGRID PPI set is an appropriate gold standard for TF co-occurrence. The choice of PPI as ground truth is questionable since co-occurrence does not require physical interaction.
  - **Why it matters** This is the primary quantitative validation of the tool's core functionality. Without clear methodology and appropriate benchmarking, the claim of superior predictive ability is not established. The choice of gold standard could bias results in favor of tools that detect physically interacting TFs.
  - **Resolution test** Provide detailed ROC methodology, justify the gold standard choice, include precision-recall curves as a complementary metric, and report confidence intervals or statistical tests for AUC differences.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Reproducibility
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims TF-COMB is a flexible tool that can handle ChIP-seq peaks, motif positions, footprints, and other input data.
  - **Evidence pointer** Section 3.1, Availability and implementation; location not provided
  - **Concern** The methods section does not describe the input data formats, preprocessing steps, or parameter choices used in the analyses presented. For example, how are ChIP-seq peaks reduced to 1bp regions, what motif score thresholds are applied, how are overlapping peaks handled, and what are the default parameters for the sliding window approach?
  - **Why it matters** Reproducibility is a core requirement for computational tools. Without clear input specifications and parameter documentation, other researchers cannot apply the tool to their own data or reproduce the presented results. This limits the tool's utility and the manuscript's scientific value.
  - **Resolution test** Provide complete input format specifications, preprocessing pipelines, parameter defaults and ranges, and a step-by-step example workflow with expected outputs.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M4
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Biological interpretation
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims that preferred binding distances and orientations between TFs are biologically significant, and that network analysis identifies TF complexes and hubs.
  - **Evidence pointer** Sections 4.4-4.6, Results; location not provided
  - **Concern** The biological significance of the distance and orientation preferences is asserted but not rigorously demonstrated. The manuscript does not provide statistical comparison against appropriate null expectations (e.g., randomized motif placements), functional validation of predicted complexes, or experimental follow-up. The network analysis identifies clusters but the biological interpretation of these clusters beyond literature concordance is limited.
  - **Why it matters** The biological claims extend beyond tool description into scientific discovery. Without rigorous statistical and functional validation, these claims remain speculative. The distinction between tool utility and biological insight needs to be clearer.
  - **Resolution test** Provide statistical significance testing for distance/orientation preferences against appropriate null models, and include functional validation or orthogonal evidence for at least a subset of predicted complexes and hubs.

- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 (statistical methodology for Z-score), R1-M2 (ROC validation methodology), R1-M3 (input specifications and reproducibility)
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: The tool combines market basket analysis with TF co-occurrence in a flexible framework, which is a novel application. However, the individual components (co-occurrence analysis, distance/orientation analysis, network analysis) are not new. Scientific importance: The tool addresses a real need in regulatory genomics, but the biological insights presented are largely confirmatory of known biology. Interdisciplinary readership: The tool would interest computational biologists and experimentalists studying gene regulation, but the manuscript is written primarily for a bioinformatics audience. Technical soundness: The core approach is reasonable, but the statistical validation is insufficiently described and the benchmarking is limited. Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is generally clear but assumes familiarity with TF biology and genomic data types. The figures and supplementary materials are essential for full comprehension but were not available for review.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The tool appears to address a genuine need and the conceptual framework is sound. However, the current manuscript does not provide sufficient methodological detail or validation evidence to fully establish the tool's reliability or the biological claims. Major revisions addressing the statistical methodology, validation rigor, and reproducibility would be required before the case is fully established.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that TF-COMB has the best predictive ability among compared tools is not fully supported without detailed ROC methodology and fair benchmarking.
- The biological significance of preferred binding distances and orientations is asserted without rigorous statistical testing against null models.
- The identification of TF complexes and hubs from network analysis is presented as biologically meaningful without functional validation.
- The claim that TF-COMB can accurately identify known co-occurring TFs is based on literature concordance, which is a weak validation standard.
- The statement that "the majority of TF binding sites are located in open chromatin outside of promoter regions" is presented without quantitative support in the text.
- The claim that pioneer factors show less association with open chromatin is based on a limited set of examples and lacks systematic analysis.
- The interpretation of the H3K9ac/H3K79me2-depleted cluster as related to pioneer factors or lineage specification is speculative.
- The manuscript states that TF-COMB outperforms other tools in runtime, but no runtime data or benchmark conditions are provided.
- The claim that TF-COMB can handle "a variety of data sources" is not demonstrated with concrete examples beyond ChIP-seq and ATAC-seq.
