### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Mechanistic claim  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that 4-sulfonic calixarenes inhibit AIM2 by binding competitively to the DNA binding site.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text, location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract states that molecular modelling and biochemistry revealed potent inhibitors that work by competitive binding to the DNA binding site, but no data are shown to support this mechanism. Competitive binding requires demonstration of direct interaction with the AIM2 DNA binding domain, displacement of DNA in a concentration-dependent manner, and ideally a co-crystal structure or mutagenesis data.  
- **Why it matters** The mechanistic claim is central to the paper's novelty and to the proposed broad utility. If the binding mode is not competitive or is not specific to the DNA binding site, the entire premise of the inhibitor design is weakened.  
- **Resolution test** Provide binding assay data (e.g., surface plasmon resonance, isothermal titration calorimetry, or fluorescence polarization) showing direct binding to AIM2 and competition with DNA. Include structural or mutagenesis evidence to confirm the binding site.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Potency and selectivity  
- **Claim pointer** The authors describe the compounds as potent inhibitors of AIM2 and less potent inhibitors of cGAS and TLR9.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text, location not provided  
- **Concern** No potency values (e.g., IC50 or Kd) are provided for any of the sensors. The term "potent" is undefined, and the relative potency across AIM2, cGAS, and TLR9 is not quantified. Without these data, the selectivity profile and the claim of broad utility cannot be assessed.  
- **Why it matters** Potency and selectivity are fundamental to any drug discovery claim. If the compounds are only weakly active or if they inhibit multiple sensors with similar potency, the therapeutic window and the specificity of the proposed mechanism are questionable.  
- **Resolution test** Provide dose-response curves and IC50 values for AIM2, cGAS, and TLR9 in relevant cellular or biochemical assays. Include selectivity data against other inflammasome sensors or DNA-binding proteins.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Translational relevance  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that 4-sulfonic calixarenes inhibited AIM2-dependent post-stroke T cell death, providing proof of concept for treating post-stroke immunosuppression.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text, location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract mentions a post-stroke model but does not specify whether this is in vivo, ex vivo, or in vitro. No details on the model, the dosing regimen, the route of administration, or the magnitude of the effect are given. The link between AIM2 inhibition and T cell death is asserted but not demonstrated with data.  
- **Why it matters** The translational claim is a key selling point of the paper. Without clear experimental evidence, the relevance to human disease and the potential for clinical development cannot be evaluated.  
- **Resolution test** Provide detailed methods for the stroke model, including species, induction method, timing of treatment, and outcome measures. Show data demonstrating that the effect is AIM2-dependent (e.g., using AIM2 knockout or knockdown controls) and quantify the effect size.

- **Concern ID** R1-M4  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Suramin repurposing claim  
- **Claim pointer** The authors propose that suramin, due to structural similarities, is an excellent inhibitor of DNA-dependent inflammation and could be rapidly repurposed.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text, location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract states that suramin is an excellent inhibitor but provides no data on its potency, mechanism, or efficacy in any model. The structural similarity to 4-sulfonic calixarenes is mentioned but not shown. The claim of rapid repurposing is speculative without evidence of efficacy in relevant disease models.  
- **Why it matters** Repurposing suramin could have immediate clinical implications, but such a claim requires robust preclinical data. If the evidence is weak, the claim could be misleading and overstate the translational readiness.  
- **Resolution test** Provide dose-response data for suramin against AIM2, cGAS, and TLR9. Include in vivo or ex vivo efficacy data in a DNA-driven inflammation model. Show structural comparison or modelling data to support the similarity claim.
