## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and conclusions as presented in the abstract, without access to full text, figures, or supplementary data
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report that RNA Polymerase II (Pol II) initiation at small nuclear RNA (snRNA) gene promoters is mediated by the snRNA-activating protein complex (SNAPc), which enables DNA opening and transcription initiation independent of TFIIE and TFIIH in vitro. Cryo-EM structures of SNAPc-containing Pol II preinitiation complexes (PICs) on U1 and U5 snRNA promoters reveal that the SNAPc core binds two turns of DNA and recognizes the proximal sequence element (PSE), while two SNAPc extensions, wing-1 and wing-2, bind TFIIA and TFIIB, respectively. Comparison of closed and open promoter complexes is used to elucidate TFIIH-independent DNA opening.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no figures, tables, methods, or supplementary materials provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, cryo-EM density maps, model validation statistics, biochemical assay details, sequence alignments, and structural coordinates are not available. These are essential for assessing the robustness of structural interpretations and functional claims.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a potentially significant advance in understanding Pol II initiation at non-coding snRNA promoters, a process that has been structurally uncharacterized. The proposed mechanism, in which SNAPc substitutes for TFIIE and TFIIH functions, is conceptually interesting and could have broad implications for transcriptional regulation. However, the evidence base is limited to the abstract, and several key claims, particularly regarding the mechanistic details of DNA opening and the functional independence from TFIIE and TFIIH, cannot be fully evaluated without access to the underlying data. The structural resolution and biochemical validation are not described, which limits confidence in the conclusions.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Structural biologists studying transcription initiation complexes, biochemists investigating RNA polymerase mechanisms, and researchers focused on non-coding RNA gene regulation would find these results relevant. The work could also interest those studying the evolution of transcriptional machineries and the functional diversity of Pol II initiation mechanisms.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses a clear gap in the field, as structural information on Pol II initiation at snRNA promoters has been lacking. The identification of SNAPc as a functional substitute for TFIIE and TFIIH is a novel and testable hypothesis. The use of two different snRNA promoters (U1 and U5) adds potential generality to the findings. The proposed structural model, with specific roles for SNAPc wings in recruiting TFIIA and TFIIB, provides a coherent framework for understanding promoter specificity.
- **Major Concerns**  
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1  
    **Severity** Major  
    **Blocking** Yes  
    **Axis** Technical soundness  
    **Claim pointer** The claim that SNAPc enables DNA opening and transcription initiation independent of TFIIE and TFIIH in vitro.  
    **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
    **Concern** The abstract states that SNAPc enables DNA opening and transcription initiation independent of TFIIE and TFIIH, but no experimental details are provided. It is unclear whether this conclusion is based on direct biochemical assays (e.g., in vitro transcription with purified components) or inferred from structural comparisons. The absence of quantitative data, such as transcription efficiency or DNA opening rates, prevents assessment of the robustness of this claim.  
    **Why it matters** This is a central claim that distinguishes the proposed mechanism from canonical Pol II initiation. If the evidence is weak or indirect, the mechanistic conclusion may be overstated.  
    **Resolution test** Provide detailed in vitro transcription assays with purified Pol II, SNAPc, TBP, TFIIA, TFIIB, and DNA templates, showing that initiation occurs without TFIIE and TFIIH, and include appropriate controls and quantitative comparisons.  
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2  
    **Severity** Major  
    **Blocking** Yes  
    **Axis** Technical soundness  
    **Claim pointer** The cryo-EM structures of SNAPc-containing PICs on U1 and U5 snRNA promoters are used to support the proposed mechanism.  
    **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
    **Concern** The abstract does not report the resolution of the cryo-EM structures, the number of particles, or the validation metrics (e.g., Fourier shell correlation). Without these details, the reliability of the structural models, particularly for the SNAPc wings and their interactions with TFIIA and TFIIB, cannot be assessed.  
    **Why it matters** Structural claims are only as strong as the underlying maps and models. If the resolution is insufficient to resolve specific interactions, the proposed binding interfaces may be speculative.  
    **Resolution test** Include cryo-EM data collection and refinement statistics, local resolution estimates, and model validation (e.g., MolProbity scores, map-to-model cross-correlation) in the full manuscript.  
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3  
    **Severity** Major  
    **Blocking** No  
    **Axis** Originality  
    **Claim pointer** The comparison of closed and open promoter complexes elucidates TFIIH-independent DNA opening.  
    **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
    **Concern** The abstract claims that comparing closed and open complexes provides mechanistic insight into DNA opening, but it does not specify how the open complex was generated or stabilized. It is unclear whether the open state is a functional intermediate or a structural artifact.  
    **Why it matters** The mechanism of TFIIH-independent DNA opening is a key novelty. If the open complex is not well-defined or functionally validated, the proposed mechanism may be premature.  
    **Resolution test** Describe the conditions used to capture the open complex, provide evidence that it represents a bona fide initiation intermediate (e.g., by cross-linking or biochemical assays), and discuss alternative interpretations.  
  - **Concern ID** R1-M4  
    **Severity** Major  
    **Blocking** No  
    **Axis** Interdisciplinary readership  
    **Claim pointer** The abstract states that SNAPc recognizes the PSE located upstream of the TATA box-binding protein TBP.  
    **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
    **Concern** The abstract does not explain how SNAPc binding to the PSE is coordinated with TBP binding to the TATA box, nor does it address potential steric or allosteric effects. The spatial relationship between SNAPc and TBP is not described.  
    **Why it matters** Understanding the cooperative or competitive binding of SNAPc and TBP is essential for a complete model of promoter recognition. Without this, the model is incomplete.  
    **Resolution test** Provide structural or biochemical data showing the relative positions of SNAPc and TBP on the promoter and discuss how their binding is coordinated.
