## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and conclusions as stated in the abstract; no methods, figures, tables, or supplementary material were provided
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report the crystal structure of *Vibrio cholerae* (6-4) photolyase at 2.5 Å resolution, showing binding of FAD and DMRL cofactors, a putative third [4Fe-4S] cluster, a stabilizing hydrogen bond involving Asp106, and a proposed DNA binding region (residues 478-484) with Arg483 forming a potential salt bridge. They further claim that DNA lesion binding requires conformational change relative to the *Drosophila melanogaster* ortholog, with plasticity in the 23rd helix.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no structural coordinates, electron density maps, crystallographic statistics, or comparative analysis details were provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, methods, crystallographic data and refinement statistics, electron density figures, sequence alignments, structural superposition figures, and any functional or biophysical validation data

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a potentially interesting structural study of a bacterial (6-4) photolyase with three cofactors, which would be of value to the DNA repair and photobiology communities. However, the claims as stated cannot be adequately evaluated from the abstract alone. Several assertions, particularly the presence of a [4Fe-4S] cluster and the functional relevance of the proposed DNA binding region, require supporting evidence that is not visible in the supplied material. The work appears promising but the case is not established from the provided evidence.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in DNA repair mechanisms, photobiology, structural biology of flavoproteins and iron-sulfur cluster proteins, and those studying *Vibrio cholerae* biology. The finding of a third cofactor in a (6-4) photolyase would be of broad interest if rigorously validated, as it challenges the canonical two-cofactor paradigm for this enzyme family.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses a clear gap in structural knowledge for bacterial (6-4) photolyases. The reported 2.5 Å resolution is appropriate for detailed cofactor and active site analysis. The identification of a potential third cofactor and a specific DNA binding region provides testable hypotheses for follow-up work.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Claim pointer** The structure contains a third cofactor with distinct electron clouds corresponding to a [4Fe-4S] cluster
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided
  - **Concern** The claim of a [4Fe-4S] cluster is based solely on electron density features. No spectroscopic data (e.g., EPR, Mössbauer, or anomalous scattering) or biochemical validation is mentioned. Iron-sulfur clusters can be misassigned from density alone, especially at 2.5 Å resolution, and adventitious metal ions or buffer components can produce similar features.
  - **Why it matters** If the third cofactor assignment is incorrect, the central novelty of the paper is undermined. The presence of a [4Fe-4S] cluster in a (6-4) photolyase would be a significant departure from known family members and requires rigorous proof.
  - **Resolution test** Provide anomalous difference maps at the iron edge, metal content analysis (e.g., ICP-MS), and/or spectroscopic confirmation. If the cluster is present, show that it is stoichiometric and not a crystallization artifact.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Claim pointer** The region between residues 478-484 may bind the lesioned DNA, with Arg483 potentially forming a salt bridge with DNA
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided
  - **Concern** The proposed DNA binding region is inferred from the apo structure without any DNA-bound complex or mutagenesis data. The claim that Arg483 forms a salt bridge with DNA is speculative and not supported by direct experimental evidence in the abstract.
  - **Why it matters** The functional relevance of the proposed binding region is a key conclusion. Without validation, the claim remains a hypothesis that cannot be distinguished from other possible binding modes.
  - **Resolution test** Provide mutagenesis data (e.g., alanine scanning of residues 478-484), DNA binding assays, or a co-crystal structure with a lesion-containing DNA substrate.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Claim pointer** The DNA lesion could not bind to the Vc (6-4) PL in a similar fashion to the Dm (6-4) PL without a significant conformational change, and the 23rd helix has remarkable plasticity
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided
  - **Concern** The comparative analysis with the *Drosophila* ortholog is described without presenting the structural superposition, RMSD values, or the specific conformational differences. The claim of helix plasticity is not supported by any quantitative or visual evidence in the abstract.
  - **Why it matters** This comparative claim is central to the proposed mechanism of DNA binding. Without the underlying data, the conclusion cannot be assessed for rigor or accuracy.
  - **Resolution test** Provide the structural alignment figures, quantitative measures of conformational differences (e.g., per-residue displacement), and ideally molecular dynamics or B-factor analysis to support the plasticity claim.

  - **Required correction** Clarify the hydrogen bonding network involving Asp106, water, and DMRL with a clear description or schematic.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 (third cofactor validation), R1-M2 (DNA binding region validation), R1-M3 (comparative conformational analysis support)
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**  
  *Originality*: The potential finding of a third cofactor in a (6-4) photolyase is original if validated, but the abstract alone does not establish this.  
  *Scientific importance*: The work addresses a relevant gap in understanding bacterial (6-4) photolyase structure and cofactor composition, which could have implications for DNA repair mechanisms. However, the importance is contingent on the validity of the third cofactor claim.  
  *Interdisciplinary readership*: The topic would appeal to structural biologists, photobiologists, and microbiologists, but the abstract is written in a way that assumes familiarity with photolyase nomenclature and cofactor chemistry.  
  *Technical soundness*: The crystallographic resolution is adequate, but the key claims lack supporting evidence in the abstract. The absence of validation for the [4Fe-4S] cluster and DNA binding region is a significant concern.  
  *Readability for nonspecialists*: The abstract is concise but uses field-specific terms without sufficient context. The "23 rd helix" notation is confusing.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The study has potential merit, but the abstract alone does not provide sufficient validation for the central claims. A full review of the manuscript with methods and figures would be required to assess whether the technical concerns can be resolved.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The presence of a [4Fe-4S] cluster is unsupported without spectroscopic or anomalous scattering data.
- The proposed DNA binding region and Arg483 salt bridge are speculative without mutagenesis or binding assays.
- The comparative claim regarding conformational differences with the *Drosophila* ortholog is unsupported without structural alignment data.
- The functional significance of Asp106 in stabilizing DMRL is plausible but not experimentally validated.
