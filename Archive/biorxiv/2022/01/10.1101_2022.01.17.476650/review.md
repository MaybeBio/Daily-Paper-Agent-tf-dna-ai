### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** The claim that KGlu promotes compaction of SSB-coated ssDNA beyond KCl, linking compaction to NNN cooperativity, is presented without supporting data.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract states that KGlu promotes compaction of a single polymeric SSB-coated ssDNA beyond what occurs in KCl, but no experimental data, conditions, or quantitative measures are shown. The link between compaction and NNN cooperativity is asserted without direct evidence.  
- **Why it matters** This is a central observation that connects the two phenomena under study. Without visible data, the reader cannot assess whether the compaction effect is robust, specific, or quantitatively meaningful.  
- **Resolution test** Provide the compaction data (e.g., hydrodynamic or single-molecule measurements) with appropriate controls, statistical analysis, and a direct comparison between KGlu and KCl conditions.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** The claim that LLPS is promoted by increasing [KGlu] in the physiological range and eliminated by KCl or IDL deletion is stated without quantitative phase diagrams or titration data.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract reports that LLPS is promoted by KGlu and inhibited by KCl and IDL deletion, but no phase boundaries, protein concentrations, salt concentrations, or imaging data are provided. The physiological relevance of the KGlu range is not quantified.  
- **Why it matters** LLPS is a concentration- and condition-dependent phenomenon. Without phase diagrams or systematic titrations, the claim that KGlu promotes LLPS in a physiologically relevant manner cannot be evaluated.  
- **Resolution test** Provide LLPS phase diagrams (e.g., turbidity or microscopy-based) as a function of protein and salt concentrations, with and without IDL, and clearly mark the physiological KGlu range.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Mechanistic support  
- **Claim pointer** The deduction that KGlu and KCl exert opposing effects through interactions with amide groups is presented as a conclusion from model compound studies, but no data are shown.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided  
- **Concern** The authors state that quantitative determinations of KGlu and KCl interactions with protein model compounds lead to the conclusion that KGlu interacts unfavorably and KCl favorably with amide groups. No model compounds, measurement methods, or thermodynamic quantities are provided.  
- **Why it matters** This is the mechanistic core of the paper. Without the underlying data, the proposed amide-based mechanism is unsupported and cannot be distinguished from alternative explanations (e.g., ion-specific effects on water structure or electrostatic screening).  
- **Resolution test** Present the model compound interaction data (e.g., osmometry or solubility measurements) with derived interaction parameters, and show how these parameters quantitatively predict the observed effects on LLPS and cooperativity.

- **Concern ID** R1-M4  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** No  
- **Axis** Generalizability  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract implies that the amide-based mechanism is general for salt regulation of LLPS, but no evidence from other proteins or systems is provided.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided  
- **Concern** The final sentence states that these results highlight the importance of salt interactions in regulating protein LLPS propensity. This generalization is reasonable but not supported by data from other proteins or conditions.  
- **Why it matters** Overgeneralization from a single protein system could mislead the field if the mechanism is specific to SSB or its IDL composition.  
- **Resolution test** Either temper the claim to be specific to SSB or provide comparative data from at least one additional protein system with different IDL compositions.
