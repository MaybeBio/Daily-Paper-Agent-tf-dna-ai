## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, conclusion, and supplementary references) as provided in the preprint.
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, methodological soundness, validity of claims, and presentation quality as inferable from the supplied text. No code, data files, or supplementary tables were available for direct inspection.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors present PTFSpot, a hybrid Transformer-DenseNet deep-learning system that co-learns on transcription factor (TF) structures and their binding regions (TFBRs) to deliver a universal TF-DNA interaction model for plants. They claim >30% performance lead over existing tools, >90% accuracy on unseen species and TF families, and the ability to annotate TFBRs across any plant genome without species- or TF-specific models.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract, introduction, methods sections (data retrieval, motif anchoring, dataset construction, transformer implementation, DenseNet architecture, performance metrics), results and discussion sections (benchmarking against nine tools, cross-species validation, case study on MYB88), and conclusion. References to Supplementary Tables 1–6 and Supplementary Figures 1–3 are made but their contents are not provided.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Supplementary tables and figures (including detailed benchmarking results, dataset compositions, hyperparameter settings, and structural comparison data), the actual model code, trained model weights, and the full dataset files. Without these, several quantitative claims cannot be independently verified.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript addresses a genuine and important gap in plant genomics: the lack of universal, cross-species TFBR prediction tools. The core idea of co-learning TF structure and binding region variability is conceptually appealing and potentially transformative. However, the current text provides insufficient methodological detail and incomplete evidence to fully substantiate the strong claims of "impeccable universality" and "revolutionary" performance. Several critical aspects, such as negative dataset construction, hyperparameter optimization details, and the exact nature of the structural input to the DenseNet, are only superficially described. The benchmarking comparisons, while extensive in scope, lack sufficient statistical rigor and transparency in the presented text. The manuscript is promising but requires substantial revision and additional evidence before the claims can be considered established.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Plant molecular biologists and regulatory genomics researchers would be the primary audience. The tool promises to enable TFBR annotation in non-model plant species without costly experiments, which is highly relevant for crop improvement and evolutionary studies. Computational biologists working on deep learning for genomics, particularly those focused on transfer learning and cross-species prediction, would also find the approach of interest. The demonstration of cross-species variability in TF structure and binding preferences has broader implications for understanding gene regulatory evolution.
- **Major strengths**
  - Addresses a critical, under-served problem in plant genomics with a novel conceptual framework (co-learning TF structure and binding regions).
  - The emphasis on moving beyond motif-centric models to include flanking context and structural information is well-justified by cited literature.
  - The use of a challenging negative dataset design (motifs from bound regions located in unbound regions) is a thoughtful approach to reduce false positives.
  - The cross-species validation strategy, including testing on species and TF families never seen during training, is appropriate for demonstrating universality.
  - The case study on MYB88 and PIN7/zmPIN1c provides a concrete, biologically relevant illustration of the tool's potential utility.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **R1-M1** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes, Axis: Technical soundness, Claim pointer: The claim that PTFSpot achieves >90% accuracy on unseen species and TF families, and >30% lead over existing tools, is central to the paper. Evidence pointer: Results and discussion sections, specifically "PTFSpot transformer models consistently surpassed all other compared TFBR finding tools" and "Deep co-learning on TF sequence, structure, and corresponding DNA binding regions brings impeccably accurate universal model of TF-DNA interaction spots"; location not provided. Concern: The text reports accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, MCC, and F1 scores, but does not provide confidence intervals, statistical significance tests for the performance differences (except one p-value for the ablation), or details on the variance across the 10-fold runs beyond a mention of "minimal dispersion." The comparison with other tools on Dataset "F" is limited to only six TFs, which is a very small sample for claiming universal superiority. The p-value of 1.46e-05 for the wheat comparison is reported, but the effect size and the number of test instances are not given. Why it matters: Without proper statistical treatment, the strong quantitative claims cannot be distinguished from results obtained by chance or favorable data selection. The small number of TFs in the cross-species benchmark undermines the generality of the conclusion. Resolution test: Provide confidence intervals for all reported metrics, perform and report statistical significance tests (e.g., McNemar's test or bootstrapping) for comparisons with other tools, and expand the cross-species benchmark to a larger and more diverse set of TFs and species. Report the number of test instances for each metric.)
  - **R1-M2** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes, Axis: Technical soundness, Claim pointer: The claim that the model learns "co-variability between TF structure and binding regions" to achieve universality. Evidence pointer: Methods sections "Structural and molecular dynamics studies" and "Transformer-DenseNet system for cross-species identification of the binding regions"; location not provided. Concern: The description of how the 3D structural information is integrated into the DenseNet is vague. The text mentions "atom-wise coordinates" and a "300×24×3" input dimension, but it is unclear how these coordinates are derived from AlphaFold2 models, how they are normalized, how the model handles variable-length protein sequences, and how the structural features are fused with the sequence-based transformer output. The ablation study (removing the structural part) shows a drop in accuracy, but the exact nature of the structural input and its processing is not sufficiently specified for reproducibility. Why it matters: The core novelty of the approach hinges on the effective co-learning of structure and sequence. If the structural input is not clearly defined, the method cannot be replicated or properly evaluated by others. Resolution test: Provide a detailed, step-by-step description of the structural feature extraction, normalization, and integration into the DenseNet architecture. Include a diagram or pseudocode. Clarify how the model handles TFs of different lengths and whether the structural information is used at training time only or also at inference time for unseen TFs.)
  - **R1-M3** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes, Axis: Technical soundness, Claim pointer: The claim that the negative dataset design creates a "highly confusing realistic negative dataset" that downplays the importance of motifs. Evidence pointer: Methods section "Dataset Creation"; location not provided. Concern: The description of negative dataset construction is incomplete. The text states that "regions reflected in the peak data were removed, leaving only unrepresented regions for selection" and that these regions were scanned for prime motifs. However, it is not specified how the unbound regions were defined (e.g., what genomic context, what distance from bound regions, how many negative instances per positive instance), how the 1:1 pooling was performed across TFs with different numbers of peaks, and whether the negative instances were matched for GC content or other confounding factors. The choice of negative set is critical for training a discriminative model, and an improperly constructed negative set can lead to inflated performance metrics. Why it matters: A biased or unrealistic negative set would compromise the validity of all reported performance metrics and the comparison with other tools. Resolution test: Provide a detailed protocol for negative set construction, including the genomic regions used, the criteria for "unbound," the matching strategy (if any), and the exact number of negative instances per TF. Discuss potential biases and how they were mitigated.)
  - **R1-M4** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No, Axis: Scientific importance, Claim pointer: The claim that existing tools are "hardly of any use" for plants and that their use has generated "misleading results." Evidence pointer: Introduction; location not provided. Concern: This is a strong and sweeping dismissal of existing tools. While the authors provide some justification (e.g., variability across plant species), the statement is overly broad and not supported by a systematic comparison or literature review in the text. Some existing tools may perform adequately for certain TFs or species, and the claim of "misleading results" is a serious accusation that requires more nuanced support. Why it matters: Overstating the limitations of prior work can undermine the credibility of the manuscript and may not accurately reflect the state of the field. Resolution test: Soften the language and provide a more balanced assessment of existing tools, acknowledging their contributions and specifying the precise conditions under which they fail. Cite specific studies that demonstrate the failures in plant systems.)
  - **R1-M5** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No, Axis: Technical soundness, Claim pointer: The claim that the model can work "even for any novel and never reported before transcription factors and genomes with same level of accuracy." Evidence pointer: Conclusion; location not provided. Concern: This is an extremely strong claim that goes beyond the evidence presented. The model was tested on 21 unseen TF families and two unseen species (maize and rice), which is commendable, but this does not guarantee "same level of accuracy" for all possible plant TFs and genomes. The structural information from AlphaFold2 is predicted, not experimentally determined, and its accuracy may vary for novel TFs. Why it matters: Overclaiming the scope of applicability can mislead users and lead to inappropriate use of the tool. Resolution test: Qualify the claim to reflect the tested scope and discuss potential limitations for highly divergent TFs or genomes. Suggest how users should interpret results for novel TFs.)
- **Minor Comments**
  - **R1-m1** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Readability for nonspecialists, Affected element: Abstract, Evidence pointer: Abstract; location not provided. Issue: The abstract uses terms like "co-learning," "co-variability," and "impeccable universality" which are not standard and may be unclear to a broad audience. Required correction: Define these terms more precisely or use more standard language, e.g., "jointly learning from TF structure and binding sequence" and "high accuracy across diverse species.")
  - **R1-m2** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Technical soundness, Affected element: Methods, Evidence pointer: "Word representations and tokenization for sequence data"; location not provided. Issue: The text mentions "469 tokenized words" but the calculation is not shown. With 16 2-mers, 1,024 5-mers, and 16,384 7-mers, the total vocabulary would be much larger. It is unclear how the vocabulary was reduced to 469. Required correction: Explain the vocabulary selection process, e.g., frequency filtering or selection of informative k-mers.)
  - **R1-m3** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Technical soundness, Affected element: Methods, Evidence pointer: "Implementation of the Transformers"; location not provided. Issue: The number of transformer layers, hidden dimensions, and other hyperparameters are not specified in the main text. The authors refer to the supplementary for "comprehensive implementation and optimization details," but these are not available for review. Required correction: Provide at least the key hyperparameters in the main text or ensure the supplementary is complete and accessible.)
  - **R1-m4** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Interdisciplinary readership, Affected element: Results, Evidence pointer: "Deep co-learning on TF sequence, structure, and corresponding DNA binding regions brings impeccably accurate universal model of TF-DNA interaction spots"; location not provided. Issue: The biological validation of the MYB88 case study is brief. The authors mention gene expression correlation but do not provide the correlation plot or the statistical significance of the correlation beyond the coefficient. Required correction: Include the correlation plot or a table with the data points, and report the p-value for the correlation.)
  - **R1-m5** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Readability for nonspecialists, Affected element: Introduction, Evidence pointer: Introduction; location not provided. Issue: The introduction could benefit from a brief explanation of what TFBRs are and why their prediction is important for readers outside the immediate field. Required correction: Add one or two sentences providing context for the general reader.)
  - **R1-m6** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Technical soundness, Affected element: Methods, Evidence pointer: "Performance Assessment"; location not provided. Issue: The authors state that "10 times independent random training and testing trials" were used to detect overfitting, but the results of these trials are not reported in the main text. Required correction: Report the mean and standard deviation of the performance metrics across the 10 trials, or refer to a supplementary table that contains this information.)
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**
  - Originality: The concept of co-learning TF structure and binding region variability is novel and represents a significant departure from existing motif-based or species-specific approaches. This is a strong point.
  - Scientific importance: The problem addressed is of high importance to plant biology and genomics. A truly universal TFBR predictor would be a valuable resource. However, the importance is somewhat diminished by the lack of complete evidence in the current text.
  - Interdisciplinary readership: The work bridges deep learning, structural biology, and plant genomics. The potential impact is broad, but the presentation is currently too specialized and lacks sufficient context for a general scientific audience.
  - Technical soundness: The overall approach is plausible, but the methodological details are insufficiently described to fully assess technical soundness. Key aspects like structural feature integration and negative dataset construction need more detail. The statistical treatment of results is inadequate.
  - Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is written in a style that is accessible to computational biologists but may be challenging for experimental plant biologists or general readers. The abstract and introduction could be more accessible.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The core idea is promising and the preliminary results are encouraging, but the manuscript in its current form does not provide sufficient evidence to fully establish the claims of universality and superior performance. The authors must address the major concerns regarding methodological transparency, statistical rigor, and the scope of validation before the claims can be considered established.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim of "impeccable universality" and "complete freedom from TF and species specific models' limitations" is not fully supported by the evidence presented, as the cross-species validation was limited to two species and a small number of TFs.
- The claim that existing tools are "hardly of any use" for plants is an overstatement not backed by a systematic comparison in the text.
- The claim that the model can work for "any novel and never reported before transcription factors and genomes with same level of accuracy" is not supported by the data.
- The specific performance numbers (e.g., >90% accuracy, >30% lead) are reported without confidence intervals or statistical significance tests, making them difficult to evaluate.
- The effectiveness of the "co-learning" of structure and sequence is suggested by the ablation study, but the mechanism and the exact contribution of structural information are not clearly demonstrated.
- The biological relevance of the MYB88 case study is suggested but not fully validated with statistical evidence.
