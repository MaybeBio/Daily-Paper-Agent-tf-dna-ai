## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text including abstract, introduction, materials and methods, results, discussion, data availability, and supplementary figure references.
- **Assessment boundary** Assessment is limited to the scientific content, methodology, and claims presented in the provided manuscript text. Supplementary figures and tables are referenced but not directly examined.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors present COBIND, a non-negative matrix factorization (NMF) based computational pipeline for de novo discovery of transcription factor (TF) co-binding patterns in genomic regions flanking known TF binding sites (TFBSs). Applied to 8,293 TFBS datasets from UniBind covering 404 TFs across seven species, COBIND reportedly recovers known co-binding events (e.g., POU5F1 with SOX2/SOX17) and identifies novel ones, with evidence from motif similarity, protein-protein interaction data, evolutionary conservation, DNase I hypersensitivity footprints, and single-molecule footprinting supporting functional relevance.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Supplementary figures and tables (Additional file 1 and 2) are referenced but not provided. Detailed parameter settings for the RSAT matrix-clustering tool are only partially described. The full list of 898 predicted co-binding patterns and per-dataset results are not included in the main text. Code and data availability are stated but not verified.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript describes a novel computational approach to a biologically important problem, with a comprehensive application across multiple species and integration of several orthogonal validation strategies. The core idea of applying NMF to one-hot encoded flanking sequences is methodologically sound and represents a useful contribution to the field. However, several technical aspects require clarification and additional validation before the claims can be fully established. The reliance on synthetic data for parameter selection, the interpretability of NMF components, the statistical framework for conservation and footprinting analyses, and the generalizability of the approach across diverse TF families need further scrutiny. The manuscript is well-written and the figures are referenced appropriately, but the absence of supplementary materials limits a complete evaluation.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in transcriptional regulation, computational biology, and genomics would be the primary audience. Specifically, those studying TF cooperativity, cis-regulatory grammar, and the evolution of gene regulatory elements would find the resource valuable. The method's ability to discover de novo co-binding patterns without prior motif knowledge could also interest bioinformaticians developing motif discovery tools. The multi-species scope and the public availability of predictions through a dedicated website enhance its utility for the broader gene regulation community.
- **Major strengths** The manuscript addresses a significant gap in the field by providing a de novo approach to discover TF co-binding patterns, moving beyond methods that rely on known motif collections. The application to a large, multi-species dataset (8,293 datasets, 404 TFs, 7 species) demonstrates scalability and generalizability. The integration of multiple validation layers, including evolutionary conservation, chromatin accessibility, and single-molecule footprinting, provides converging evidence for the functional relevance of the predictions. The recovery of well-established co-binding events (POU5F1 with SOX2/SOX17) serves as a strong proof-of-concept. The method's flexibility, demonstrated by its application to intron-exon boundaries, suggests broader utility beyond TF co-binding.
- **Major Concerns** 
  - **R1-M1** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes, Axis: Technical soundness) The parameter selection for the number of NMF components (k) is based on synthetic data with implanted motifs, but the manuscript does not clearly describe how the optimal k range [3, 6] was determined from these experiments. The choice of k critically affects the resolution and interpretability of the discovered patterns. The authors state that they ran COBIND with k in [2, 17] on synthetic data and assessed specificity and sensitivity, but the specific criteria for selecting [3, 6] are not detailed. This is a critical methodological gap because the number of components directly determines the number of potential co-binding patterns extracted.
  - **R1-M2** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes, Axis: Technical soundness) The manuscript does not provide a clear statistical framework for determining the significance of the discovered co-binding patterns. While the Gini coefficient filter is described, the manuscript does not explain how the authors distinguish between genuine co-binding patterns and noise or artifacts arising from the NMF decomposition. The comparison with random sequences for Gini threshold selection is mentioned, but the details of this comparison and the false discovery rate calculation are not fully described.
  - **R1-M3** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes, Axis: Technical soundness) The inference of co-binding TFs relies on motif similarity (Tomtom p-value < 0.05) and PPI data (STRING score >= 500). However, the manuscript does not address the potential for false positives in this inference. The threshold of p-value < 0.05 for motif similarity is permissive, especially when comparing against a large database of 5,272 motifs, and multiple testing correction is not mentioned. Similarly, the STRING score threshold of 500 is described as indicating physical interaction, but the manuscript does not discuss the precision and recall of this threshold for the specific TFs analyzed.
  - **R1-M4** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No, Axis: Scientific importance) The evolutionary conservation analysis, while a valuable validation, is presented in a somewhat simplified manner. The manuscript states that co-bound regions are more conserved than regions without co-binding, but the analysis does not account for potential confounding factors such as the genomic context (e.g., promoter vs. enhancer), the baseline conservation of regions bound by the anchor TF, or the potential for the co-binding motif to be part of a larger, more conserved regulatory module. The comparison of conservation between co-bound and non-co-bound regions should be more rigorously controlled.
  - **R1-M5** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No, Axis: Technical soundness) The single-molecule footprinting (SMF) analysis is a strong validation, but the manuscript does not provide sufficient detail on how the SMF data were processed and how the occupancy states were assigned. The classification of molecules into five states (Accessible, Nucleosome, Anchor, Co-binding, Anchor + Co-binding) is described, but the criteria for these classifications and the statistical comparison between co-bound and non-co-bound regions are not fully detailed.
- **Minor Comments** 
  - **R1-m1** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Readability for nonspecialists) The abstract and introduction could benefit from a brief explanation of what NMF is and why it is well-suited for this problem, as readers unfamiliar with matrix factorization may find the methodology opaque.
  - **R1-m2** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Technical soundness) The manuscript mentions that COBIND was applied to intron-exon boundaries as a test case, but this result is only briefly mentioned in the Discussion. A more detailed description of this application, including the results, would strengthen the claim of generalizability.
  - **R1-m3** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Technical soundness) The description of the motif clustering step (Step 6) is brief. The manuscript states that the RSAT matrix-clustering tool is used, but the specific parameters and the criteria for defining a cluster are not provided in the main text. This information is essential for reproducibility.
  - **R1-m4** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Scientific importance) The manuscript reports that 84% of TFs share a co-binding motif with another TF from the same structural family. This is an interesting observation, but the analysis could be strengthened by comparing this proportion to what would be expected by chance, given the number of TFs and motifs analyzed.
  - **R1-m5** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Readability for nonspecialists) The heatmap visualization (Figure 1, Step 7) is described as illustrating co-binding events, but the manuscript does not explain how to interpret the color intensity and the inserted numbers. A brief explanation in the figure legend or text would be helpful.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** The lack of detail on the selection of k, the statistical significance of discovered patterns, and the potential for false positives in TF inference are the most critical technical issues. The manuscript must provide a clearer description of these aspects to establish the reliability of the method. Additionally, the absence of supplementary materials prevents a full evaluation of the results.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - Originality: The application of NMF to discover TF co-binding patterns is novel and represents a methodological advance over existing tools that rely on known motif collections. The idea of decomposing one-hot encoded sequences to reveal fixed-spacing patterns is creative and well-motivated.
  - Scientific importance: TF cooperativity is a fundamental aspect of gene regulation, and a de novo method that can systematically identify co-binding patterns across species has the potential to significantly advance the field. The large-scale application and public availability of results enhance the importance.
  - Interdisciplinary readership: The topic is of interest to molecular biologists, computational biologists, and genomicists. The manuscript is generally accessible, though some methodological details may be challenging for non-specialists.
  - Technical soundness: The core methodology is sound, but several technical aspects require clarification and additional validation, as detailed in the Major Concerns. The lack of detail on parameter selection and statistical significance is a significant weakness.
  - Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is well-written and logically structured. The use of case studies (SOX2/SOX17, TEAD1, HY5) helps illustrate the method's utility. However, the methodology section could be more accessible.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The manuscript presents a promising and potentially valuable method, but the current level of detail on key methodological aspects is insufficient to fully establish the reliability of the claims. A revised manuscript that addresses the concerns regarding parameter selection, statistical significance, and potential false positives would be a strong candidate for publication.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that COBIND can "automatically" identify co-binding patterns is not fully supported, as the method requires user-defined parameters (e.g., flanking region length, k range, Gini coefficient threshold) that require careful tuning.
- The claim that 84% of TFs share a co-binding motif with another TF from the same structural family is presented without a statistical comparison to a null expectation, making it difficult to assess its significance.
- The claim that the co-binding patterns are "likely functionally relevant" based on evolutionary conservation is supported, but the analysis does not fully control for potential confounding factors.
- The claim that SMF data confirm co-binding events "likely occurring on the same DNA molecules" is supported for the analyzed cases, but the generalizability of this finding to all predicted co-binding patterns is not established.
- The claim that COBIND is a "powerful alternative" to other motif discovery tools is not fully supported by the benchmarking, which only compares against a limited set of tools and does not assess performance on a diverse range of biological scenarios.
