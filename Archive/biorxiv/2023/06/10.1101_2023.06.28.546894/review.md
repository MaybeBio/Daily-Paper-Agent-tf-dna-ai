## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a substantial and potentially valuable dataset that maps combinatorial TF binding in the developing mouse basal ganglia and links these patterns to enhancer activity, chromatin state, and evolutionary conservation. The integration of twelve TF ChIP-seq datasets with chromatin conformation and functional enhancer assays is a significant technical undertaking, and the observation that a small subset of broadly-bound enhancers shows extreme conservation and complex regulatory interactions is of considerable interest. However, the manuscript as presented has several weaknesses that temper enthusiasm. The clustering approach and the interpretation of "broadly-bound" elements require more rigorous statistical justification. The enhancer assay validation, while commendable in scale, lacks clear quantitative reporting of reproducibility and specificity. The causal relationship between combinatorial TF binding and enhancer function is inferred rather than directly tested. The manuscript would benefit from clearer presentation of the computational methods and more explicit statistical frameworks for the key claims.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Developmental neuroscientists studying forebrain patterning and GABAergic neuron specification would find the TF-cRE maps and enhancer resources directly useful. Researchers interested in the evolution of cis-regulatory elements and the relationship between TF binding density, conservation, and enhancer function would be engaged by the "broadly-bound enhancer" concept. Computational biologists working on integrative analysis of ChIP-seq, chromatin conformation, and enhancer prediction would value the methodological framework. The enhancer resource (74 newly-defined elements) would be of practical use to the gene therapy and cell reprogramming communities targeting interneuron populations.
- **Major strengths** 1) The scale and integration of the dataset: twelve TFs spanning multiple TF families profiled in the same tissue at the same developmental stage is a valuable community resource. 2) The combination of biochemical (ChIP-seq), conformational (PLAC-seq), and functional (transgenic assays) data provides a multi-layered view of regulatory wiring. 3) The identification of a small set of "broadly-bound" enhancers with extreme conservation and complex interaction patterns is a conceptually interesting finding that could inform models of enhancer evolution and function. 4) The validation of predicted enhancers in vivo with a substantial number of new elements is a strong contribution.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **R1-M1** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes, Axis: Statistical rigor / clustering methodology, Claim pointer: "We leveraged unbiased K-means clustering implemented in DeepTools... This approach yielded 7 clusters of promoter-proximal pREs and 18 clusters for distal pREs", Evidence pointer: Figure 2, Methods section "Computational and Statistical Analysis", location not provided, Concern: The clustering approach is described as "unbiased" but the number of clusters was determined by visual inspection ("increased by one until no further patterns were visually captured by the authors"). This is a subjective criterion that could substantially affect the resulting cluster definitions and all downstream interpretations. The manuscript does not report cluster stability, silhouette scores, or any quantitative metric for cluster quality. The decision to further sub-cluster one large distal cluster is also not justified with statistical criteria. Why it matters: The central claim of the paper, that distinct combinatorial TF binding modules exist with different functional properties, rests entirely on the validity of these clusters. If the clustering is unstable or the number of clusters is arbitrary, the biological conclusions about "broadly-bound" versus "specifically-bound" enhancers may not be robust. Resolution test: Provide quantitative cluster validation (e.g., silhouette analysis, bootstrap stability, gap statistic) and report the variance explained by different cluster numbers. Justify the final cluster count with a reproducible criterion rather than visual inspection.
  - **R1-M2** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes, Axis: Causal inference / functional validation, Claim pointer: "Combinatorial TF binding reveals cREs with distinct neurodevelopmental roles, cell-type specificity, and regulatory function" and "Enhancer activity in developing mouse telencephalon is predicted by combinatorial TF binding", Evidence pointer: Figures 3 and 4, location not provided, Concern: The manuscript claims that combinatorial TF binding "encodes" and "predicts" enhancer activity, but the evidence presented is correlative. The transgenic enhancer assays test individual elements in isolation, which does not directly test whether the specific combination of TFs bound at a given element is necessary or sufficient for its activity. The authors do not perform TF perturbation experiments (e.g., knockout, knockdown, or dominant-negative approaches) to demonstrate that the identified TF combinations are functionally required for the observed enhancer activity. The claim that broadly-bound enhancers "drive" GABAergic neurogenesis is an inference from association, not a demonstrated causal relationship. Why it matters: The title and framing of the manuscript make a strong causal claim ("encodes cis-regulatory wiring"). Without functional perturbation data, the manuscript demonstrates correlation between TF binding patterns and enhancer activity, but does not establish that the combinatorial binding itself is the mechanism encoding the regulatory wiring. This distinction is critical for the paper's significance. Resolution test: Include at least one perturbation experiment (e.g., mutation of TF binding sites in a representative enhancer, or TF knockdown followed by enhancer activity assessment) to demonstrate that the identified TF combinations are functionally required. Alternatively, substantially soften the causal language throughout the manuscript.
  - **R1-M3** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No, Axis: Quantitative reporting of enhancer assays, Claim pointer: "we identified 84 novel TF-bound pRE loci and tested them using the same transgenic enhancer assay as used in VISTA discovery" and "Enhancer activities of newly-tested pREs with broader BG TF binding were more likely to exhibit subthalamic specificity", Evidence pointer: Figure 4, Supplementary Figure S4, location not provided, Concern: The manuscript reports that 74 of 84 tested enhancers showed reproducible activity, but the criteria for "reproducible" are not clearly defined. The text states that "only LacZ activity patterns that were observed in more than 30% of embryos resulting from independent transgene integration events of the same construct were considered reproducible," but the number of embryos per construct, the total number of independent integration events, and the statistical framework for this threshold are not reported. The classification of enhancer activity into "subthalamic," "pallial," etc. appears to be based on qualitative visual assessment without inter-rater reliability metrics or a formal annotation scheme. Why it matters: The enhancer assay data is a key validation of the computational predictions. Without clear quantitative reporting of the assay criteria, reproducibility, and annotation reliability, the reader cannot assess the robustness of the claim that combinatorial TF binding predicts enhancer activity. This is particularly important given that the authors use this data to argue for the functional relevance of their TF-cRE modules. Resolution test: Report the number of embryos per construct, the distribution of activity patterns across integration events, and provide a formal annotation scheme with inter-rater reliability statistics. Consider providing a quantitative scoring system for enhancer activity rather than binary active/inactive calls.
  - **R1-M4** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No, Axis: Evolutionary conservation analysis, Claim pointer: "Broadly-bound enhancers are characterized by TAATTA homeobox motif composition and deep evolutionary conservation" and "1_D represented an extreme in both binding patterns and sequence composition", Evidence pointer: Figure 5, location not provided, Concern: The conservation analysis compares cluster 1_D to other clusters and to random genomic regions, but the statistical framework for these comparisons is not fully described. The manuscript reports that 1_D has the highest conservation scores, but does not report whether this difference is statistically significant after controlling for genomic features such as GC content, repeat density, or distance to genes. The PhyloP and PhastCons scores are averaged across elements, but the distribution of scores within clusters is not shown. The claim that TAATTA motif density drives conservation is inferred from correlation, but alternative explanations (e.g., that conserved elements are simply more likely to be bound by any TF) are not excluded. Why it matters: The evolutionary conservation analysis is used to support the claim that broadly-bound enhancers represent a special, functionally important class of elements. If the conservation differences are not robust to confounders or are not statistically significant, this claim is weakened. Resolution test: Provide a formal statistical comparison of conservation scores between clusters, controlling for relevant genomic covariates. Show the distribution of conservation scores within each cluster, not just the mean. Consider a regression framework that tests whether TF binding density predicts conservation after accounting for other genomic features.
  - **R1-M5** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No, Axis: Generalizability of TF representation, Claim pointer: "To elucidate the combinatorial TF-cRE interactions driving mouse basal ganglia development, we integrated ChIP-seq for twelve TFs", Evidence pointer: Figure 1, Methods, location not provided, Concern: The twelve TFs profiled represent a curated set, but the manuscript does not justify why these particular twelve were chosen or how representative they are of the full TF repertoire active in the developing basal ganglia. The authors note that SP9 binding appears largely indirect, and that six TFs share a similar TAATTA motif, which may limit the apparent diversity of combinatorial patterns. The manuscript does not discuss how the inclusion of additional TFs (e.g., other homeobox families, basic helix-loop-helix factors, or repressors) might alter the identified modules. Why it matters: The claim that the identified TF-cRE modules represent the "regulatory wiring" of the basal ganglia depends on the comprehensiveness of the TF sampling. If important TFs are missing, the combinatorial patterns identified may be incomplete or biased. Resolution test: Provide a rationale for TF selection based on expression data, known functional roles, or prior ChIP-seq availability. Discuss the potential impact of missing TFs on the identified modules, or perform a sensitivity analysis with a subset of TFs to assess robustness.

- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 (clustering methodology lacks statistical justification), R1-M2 (causal claims not supported by perturbation experiments), R1-M3 (enhancer assay reporting lacks quantitative detail)
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: The manuscript offers a novel integrative dataset and a conceptually interesting framework for thinking about combinatorial TF binding in a developing brain region. The "broadly-bound enhancer" concept, while related to prior work on TF "hotspots" and "super-enhancers," is applied here in a developmental context with evolutionary analysis, which adds originality. Scientific importance: The dataset and enhancer resources are likely to be valuable to the field, and the findings about the relationship between TF binding density, conservation, and enhancer function have implications beyond the basal ganglia. However, the lack of causal validation limits the immediate importance of the conceptual claims. Interdisciplinary readership: The work bridges developmental neuroscience, genomics, and evolutionary biology, and would appeal to readers across these fields. Technical soundness: The ChIP-seq and PLAC-seq data generation appears technically sound, but the analytical framework (clustering, statistical comparisons) lacks rigor in several places, as detailed above. Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is generally well-written and the main concepts are accessible, but the results section is dense and would benefit from clearer signposting of the main findings. The abstract is effective but could be more precise about the distinction between correlation and causation.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The dataset and resources are valuable, and the conceptual framework is interesting, but the manuscript currently overstates the causal relationship between combinatorial TF binding and enhancer function, and the clustering methodology requires more rigorous justification. If the authors can address the statistical concerns and either provide perturbation data or temper the causal language, the manuscript would be a strong contribution.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that combinatorial TF binding "encodes" cis-regulatory wiring (title and abstract) is not supported by the data presented, which is correlative.
- The claim that "broadly-bound enhancers" represent a distinct class with "exceptional" evolutionary conservation is based on comparisons that are not fully statistically controlled.
- The claim that specific TF-cRE modules have "complementary roles driving GABAergic neurogenesis and suppressing other developmental fates" is inferred from pathway enrichment and chromatin state, not directly demonstrated.
- The statement that "six of the TFs recognize variations of a highly similar 'TAATTA' motif" is based on motif analysis, but the manuscript does not demonstrate that these TFs actually bind these motifs in vivo at the identified elements.
- The claim that "SP9 primarily bound cREs via indirect mechanisms" is based on motif absence, which is a weak inference.
- The manuscript states that "74 newly-defined enhancers" were identified, but the criteria for "newly-defined" and the full list of these elements are not provided in the text.
- The statement that "nearly all" complex interaction ensembles had overlap with at least one TF-bound pRE is vague and not quantified.
