## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript addresses a relevant and timely problem: binding site prediction on predicted protein structures, which is increasingly important given the widespread use of AlphaFold2. The use of ESM-IF1 embeddings is a reasonable and potentially novel approach. However, the manuscript as provided has several critical gaps. The most significant concern is the lack of actual data tables and figures, making it impossible to evaluate the strength of the reported results. The description of the dataset construction, particularly the HAP set, raises questions about representativeness and potential biases. The evaluation methodology, especially the use of MD simulations to simulate low-accuracy structures, is creative but may not fully replicate the error characteristics of real predicted structures for novel proteins. The ensembling results are promising but the mechanism and generalizability need more rigorous exploration. The writing is generally clear, but some methodological details are underspecified.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Computational biologists and bioinformaticians working on protein function prediction, structure-based drug discovery, and those developing or using tools for analyzing AlphaFold2-predicted structures. The work is also relevant to researchers interested in applying protein language model embeddings to structural bioinformatics tasks beyond their original training objectives.
- **Major strengths** 1. The problem addressed is highly relevant and timely. 2. The use of ESM-IF1 embeddings is a novel and interesting approach for this task. 3. The authors explicitly address the important issue of evaluating methods on structures with realistic accuracy levels for novel proteins, rather than only on highly accurate AF2 structures. 4. The exploration of ensembling across multiple structures is a practical and useful contribution. 5. The writing is generally clear and well-structured.
- **Major Concerns** 
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Evidence quality
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims that IF-SitePred is "competitive with state-of-the-art methods" and "outperforms other methods" in various settings, supported by quantitative results in Tables 2 and 3 and Figures 3-5.
  - **Evidence pointer** Tables 2, 3; Figures 3, 4, 5 (location not provided in the text)
  - **Concern** The actual data tables and figures are not provided in the manuscript text. All quantitative claims regarding performance comparison, statistical significance, and effect sizes cannot be assessed. The reader cannot verify the magnitude of the reported differences, the variance, or whether the differences are meaningful.
  - **Why it matters** The central claims of the paper are quantitative. Without the data, the claims are unverifiable and the scientific contribution cannot be evaluated.
  - **Resolution test** Provide the actual data tables and figures with clear labels, error bars, and statistical test results. The data must be sufficient to support the specific claims made in the text.

  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Methodology validity
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript states that the HAP set was designed to include proteins with "no more than 25% sequence identity to the training sets of IF-SitePred, FPocket and DeepPocket" and that the HAP-small set has "no more than 25% sequence identity to the training data used for comparator method P2Rank."
  - **Evidence pointer** Methods, Datasets section
  - **Concern** The description of the dataset construction is incomplete. It is unclear how sequence identity was calculated (e.g., global vs. local alignment, threshold for coverage). More critically, the statement about the HAP set's relationship to the training sets of IF-SitePred, FPocket, and DeepPocket is confusing. IF-SitePred is trained on Binding MOAD, and the authors state they removed proteins with >25% identity to the test set from the training set. However, the text also says the HAP set has no more than 25% identity to the training sets of IF-SitePred, FPocket, and DeepPocket. This implies the HAP set was filtered against the training sets of the comparators, which is unusual and could introduce bias. The exact filtering procedure and its implications for fair comparison are not clear.
  - **Why it matters** Dataset construction is fundamental to the validity of the benchmark. If the HAP set is not representative of typical use cases, or if the filtering introduces bias, the comparative results may not be generalizable.
  - **Resolution test** Provide a detailed, step-by-step description of the dataset construction, including the exact sequence identity calculation method, the databases used for filtering, and a clear explanation of how the HAP and HAP-small sets relate to the training sets of all methods compared.

  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Methodology validity
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript states that "we used molecular dynamics simulations of predicted structures to create lower accuracy protein models (up to 4Å RMSD) and evaluate how binding site prediction success varies with structural accuracy."
  - **Evidence pointer** Methods, Molecular dynamics simulations section; Results, Figure 3
  - **Concern** The MD simulation protocol is described as heating proteins from 298K to 548K and simulating for 20ns at each 10K interval. This is an unusual protocol for generating structures with specific RMSD values. The rationale for this specific protocol is not given. More importantly, the structural distortions generated by high-temperature MD may not be representative of the types of errors found in real predicted structures for novel proteins, which often involve domain misorientations or errors in loop regions. The authors do not validate that the MD-generated structures are a realistic proxy for low-accuracy predictions.
  - **Why it matters** The claim that IF-SitePred is more robust on low-accuracy structures is based entirely on this MD-based proxy. If the proxy is not realistic, the conclusion may not hold for real-world use cases.
  - **Resolution test** Justify the choice of MD protocol. Provide evidence that the error characteristics of the MD-generated structures (e.g., types of distortions, local vs. global errors) are similar to those of real low-accuracy predicted structures. Alternatively, validate the findings on a set of real predicted structures with known low accuracy.

  - **Concern ID** R1-M4
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript describes the ensembling method as combining predictions from multiple structures, where "only residues that were predicted as ligand-binding in at least 3 of the 10 structures were given a final prediction as ligand-binding."
  - **Evidence pointer** Methods, Combining predictions section; Results, Figure 5
  - **Concern** The threshold of 3 out of 10 structures is described as "arbitrarily selected." This is a critical parameter for the ensembling method, and its choice likely has a significant impact on the results. The authors do not explore the sensitivity of their results to this threshold. Furthermore, the comparison of ensembling benefits across methods (IF-SitePred vs. P2Rank vs. DeepPocket) may be confounded if the optimal threshold differs between methods.
  - **Why it matters** The claim that IF-SitePred benefits more from ensembling than other methods may be an artifact of the chosen threshold. Without a sensitivity analysis, the robustness of this conclusion is unclear.
  - **Resolution test** Perform a sensitivity analysis on the ensembling threshold (e.g., 1 to 10 out of 10) and show that the relative performance of the methods is consistent across a range of thresholds. Alternatively, provide a principled method for selecting the threshold.

  - **Concern ID** R1-M5
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Scientific importance
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript states that "IF-SitePred outperforms other methods if ensembles of predicted protein structures are generated."
  - **Evidence pointer** Results, Figure 5
  - **Concern** The practical scenario where a user would have access to an ensemble of predicted structures for a novel protein is not clearly articulated. While MD simulations can generate such ensembles, they are computationally expensive. The authors mention other potential sources of ensembles (e.g., multiple prediction tools), but do not test these. The practical utility of the ensembling approach is therefore not fully established.
  - **Why it matters** The claimed advantage of IF-SitePred in the ensembling scenario may not translate to a practically useful setting if generating multiple structures is not feasible for most users.
  - **Resolution test** Discuss the practical scenarios where ensembles of predicted structures would be available. If possible, test the ensembling approach using ensembles generated by different prediction tools or by sampling from a single tool's output.

- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 (missing data), R1-M2 (dataset construction details), R1-M3 (validity of MD proxy for low-accuracy structures). These are blocking because without the actual data, the core quantitative claims cannot be evaluated, and without a clear and unbiased dataset description, the comparative results are not interpretable. The validity of the MD-based evaluation is central to the claim of robustness on low-accuracy structures.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - Originality: The use of ESM-IF1 embeddings for binding site prediction is a novel application, though the underlying concept of using language model embeddings for protein property prediction is not entirely new. The specific combination of embeddings with point cloud clustering appears original.
  - Scientific importance: The problem of binding site prediction on predicted structures is important and timely. The finding that a method is more robust on low-accuracy structures would be of significant interest. However, the importance is contingent on the validity of the evaluation.
  - Interdisciplinary readership: The work is primarily of interest to computational biologists and bioinformaticians. The potential applications in drug discovery may attract a broader readership, but the technical nature of the manuscript may limit this.
  - Technical soundness: The technical approach is reasonable, but the evaluation methodology has significant gaps as detailed in the major concerns. The lack of data prevents a full assessment.
  - Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is generally well-written and the concepts are explained clearly. However, some methodological details are dense and may be challenging for nonspecialists. The abstract is accessible.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The core quantitative claims cannot be verified without the actual data tables and figures. The methodology has potential, but the dataset construction and the validity of the MD-based evaluation need to be clarified and justified. The manuscript could become a valuable contribution if these issues are addressed.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that IF-SitePred is "competitive with state-of-the-art methods" is unsupported without the actual data in Tables 2 and 3.
- The claim that IF-SitePred "outperforms other methods" on low-accuracy structures is unsupported without the data in Figure 3 and without validation that the MD-generated structures are a realistic proxy.
- The claim that ensembling improves prediction "significantly" is unsupported without the data in Figures 4 and 5 and without a sensitivity analysis of the ensembling threshold.
- The claim that the HAP set is representative of novel protein targets is not fully supported, as the filtering criteria and their implications are not fully described.
- The claim that ESM-IF1 embeddings are robust to structural errors is a reasonable hypothesis but is not directly tested, as the authors do not compare against a version of their method that uses side chain information.
