### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** "The BRCA1-BARD1 complex preferentially interacts and modifies di-nucleosomes over mono-nucleosomes"  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract states a preference for di-nucleosomes over mono-nucleosomes but provides no quantitative data, such as binding affinities, catalytic rates, or fold differences. It is unclear whether this preference is modest or dramatic, and whether it is observed across all substrate combinations tested.  
- **Why it matters** This is the central observation on which the bridging model rests. Without quantitative comparison and appropriate controls (for example, nucleosome concentration, salt conditions, and linker DNA length), the physiological relevance of the preference cannot be evaluated.  
- **Resolution test** Provide binding and activity data with error bars and statistical tests for at least three independent experiments, comparing mono- and di-nucleosome substrates under matched conditions. State the magnitude of the preference and demonstrate that it is not an artifact of substrate preparation.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Mechanistic support  
- **Claim pointer** "Bridging is aided by multivalent cross-nucleosome interactions that enhance BRCA1-BARD1 E3 ubiquitin ligase catalytic activity"  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract asserts that multivalent cross-nucleosome interactions enhance catalytic activity, but no data are shown to distinguish between enhanced activity due to increased local concentration (avidity) versus a true allosteric effect on the enzyme. The distinction is mechanistically important.  
- **Why it matters** If the enhancement is purely avidity-based, the conclusion that bridging "enhances" activity is trivial. If an allosteric mechanism is proposed, structural or mutational evidence is required. The current claim conflates these possibilities.  
- **Resolution test** Include experiments with mutant BRCA1-BARD1 that disrupt cross-nucleosome interactions without affecting the active site, and show whether activity enhancement is lost. Alternatively, provide structural data showing conformational changes upon di-nucleosome binding.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Claim consistency  
- **Claim pointer** "Multivalent interactions across nucleosomes explains how BRCA1-BARD1 can recognize chromatin that retains partial di-methylation at H4 Lys-20 (H4K20me2), a parental histone mark that blocks BRCA1-BARD1 interaction with nucleosomes, to promote its enzymatic and DNA repair activities"  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided  
- **Concern** The logic of this claim is unclear. If H4K20me2 blocks BRCA1-BARD1 interaction with nucleosomes, it is not obvious how multivalent interactions across nucleosomes overcome this block. The abstract does not specify whether the H4K20me2 mark is present on one or both nucleosomes in the di-nucleosome substrate, nor how partial methylation is defined or quantified.  
- **Why it matters** This is the most biologically significant claim, as it directly addresses how BRCA1-BARD1 functions in the presence of an inhibitory mark. If the mechanism is not clearly articulated and experimentally supported, the conclusion is not established.  
- **Resolution test** Provide experiments with defined nucleosome substrates containing H4K20me2 on one or both nucleosomes, and show binding and activity data. Clarify the stoichiometry of the mark and demonstrate that bridging occurs specifically when at least one nucleosome lacks the inhibitory mark.

- **Concern ID** R1-M4  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** No  
- **Axis** Technical validation  
- **Claim pointer** "Using high speed-AFM to provide real-time visualization of BRCA1-BARD1 complex recognising chromatin, we show a highly dynamic complex that bridges two nucleosomes and associates with the DNA linker region"  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided  
- **Concern** High-speed AFM can introduce artifacts due to surface tethering and tip-sample interactions. The abstract does not describe how the authors controlled for these effects, nor how many independent imaging sessions and molecules were analyzed.  
- **Why it matters** The imaging data are used to support the dynamic bridging model. If the observed bridging is influenced by the experimental setup, the biological interpretation may be compromised.  
- **Resolution test** Provide details on sample preparation, surface chemistry, imaging conditions, and statistical analysis of molecular populations. Include control experiments with a non-bridging mutant or with nucleosomes alone.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that BRCA1-BARD1 "preferentially interacts and modifies di-nucleosomes over mono-nucleosomes" is unsupported without quantitative data.
- The claim that multivalent cross-nucleosome interactions "enhance" catalytic activity is unsupported without distinguishing avidity from allosteric effects.
- The claim that multivalent interactions explain tolerance of H4K20me2 is mechanistically under-specified and unsupported.
- The claim of "highly dynamic complex" behavior based on high-speed AFM is not assessable without imaging controls and statistics.
- The overall model of nucleosome bridging as a mechanism for integrating chromatin signals is plausible but not established from the abstract alone.
