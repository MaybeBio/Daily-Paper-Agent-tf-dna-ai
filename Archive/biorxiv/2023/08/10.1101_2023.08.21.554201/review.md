## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, results, discussion, methods, supplementary figure legends) as provided in the submission.
- **Assessment boundary** The scientific claims, methodology, and evidence presented in the manuscript. No external validation of the computational tools or databases was performed beyond what is described in the text.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors use in silico homology modeling and docking to predict that the extracellular matrix proteoglycan lumican binds a 22-mer CpG DNA (ODN_2395) via leucine-rich repeat (LRR) motifs 1-11, primarily through hydrogen bonding, salt bridges, and hydrophobic interactions. They compare this predicted interaction with a modeled TLR9-CpG ODN_2395 complex and suggest that lumican's binding may sequester CpG DNA, thereby modulating TLR9-mediated inflammation.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract, Introduction, Results (Sections 2.1-2.5), Discussion, Materials and Methods (Sections 4.1-4.6), and references to Supplementary Figures S1-S5 and Tables 1-4 (tables not provided in the submitted text).
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** The actual figures (Fig. 1-5), all tables (Table 1-4), and the supplementary figures (Fig. S1-S5) are not included in the provided text. The HDOCK docking scores, confidence metrics, and detailed interaction data are referenced but not shown. The AlphaFold model of lumican is not provided for independent assessment.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript addresses a potentially interesting question regarding the molecular basis of lumican-CpG DNA interactions and their possible role in modulating TLR9 signaling. However, the current evidence is entirely computational and descriptive, with no experimental validation. The absence of key data (figures, tables, docking scores) prevents a full evaluation of the technical soundness of the modeling. The claim that lumican's LRR motifs mimic TLR9 in CpG DNA binding is plausible but not convincingly established from the provided evidence. The manuscript would benefit from a more rigorous presentation of the docking methodology, validation of the models, and a clearer discussion of the limitations of the in silico approach.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in the fields of extracellular matrix biology, innate immunity, and proteoglycan function. Specifically, those studying TLR9 signaling, CpG DNA recognition, and the immunomodulatory roles of small leucine-rich proteoglycans (SLRPs) like lumican. The potential implication for modulating inflammation and autoimmunity could also attract interest from translational researchers.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses a novel question, as the molecular details of lumican-CpG DNA interactions have not been previously characterized. The use of a clinically relevant CpG ODN (ODN_2395) and the comparison with TLR9 provide a useful framework. The identification of specific LRR motifs and residue types involved in binding offers testable hypotheses for future experimental work.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness of the docking methodology
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that the docking analysis identified specific LRR motifs and residues in lumican that interact with CpG ODN_2395, and that the predicted interactions are biologically meaningful.
  - **Evidence pointer** Section 2.2, 2.3, 2.4; Tables 1-4 (not provided); Figures 2-4 (not provided)
  - **Concern** The manuscript does not provide the docking scores, confidence metrics, or the criteria used to select the top model from the 100 generated by HDOCK. The authors state that only the top model with the highest confidence and lowest docking score was considered, but these values are not reported. Without this information, the quality and reliability of the predicted complexes cannot be assessed. Furthermore, the authors do not discuss whether the docking results were validated by any independent method, such as re-docking or comparison with known protein-DNA interaction patterns.
  - **Why it matters** The core of the manuscript's claims rests on the accuracy of the docking predictions. If the docking scores are poor or the models are not reliable, the entire set of conclusions about specific interacting residues and binding modes is unsupported. The lack of this data prevents the reader from evaluating the technical soundness of the study.
  - **Resolution test** The authors must provide the docking scores, confidence scores, and a clear description of the model selection process. They should also include a validation step, such as comparing the predicted TLR9-CpG ODN_2395 interaction with the known crystal structure of TLR9 with CpG DNA (if applicable) or performing a cross-docking analysis.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Evidence for the biological relevance of the predicted interaction
  - **Claim pointer** The authors suggest that the predicted lumican-CpG DNA interaction, with a smaller buried surface area (1803 A²) compared to TLR9 (2094 A²), implies a potentially lower binding strength, and that this interaction is relevant for sequestering CpG DNA in endosomes.
  - **Evidence pointer** Section 2.2, 2.3, 2.5; Discussion
  - **Concern** The manuscript relies entirely on computational predictions to infer binding strength and biological function. No experimental data, such as surface plasmon resonance, isothermal titration calorimetry, or cellular assays, are provided to support the claim that lumican binds CpG DNA with the predicted affinity or that this interaction occurs in a cellular context. The authors mention their prior experimental work (reference 5) but do not connect it to the specific predictions made here. The claim that the smaller BSA implies lower binding strength is speculative and not directly supported by the data presented.
  - **Why it matters** The manuscript's central hypothesis is that lumican modulates TLR9 signaling by binding and sequestering CpG DNA. Without experimental validation of the predicted interaction, the biological relevance of the modeling remains unproven. The study is presented as providing "molecular insights," but these insights are not grounded in experimental evidence.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should either provide experimental data supporting the predicted interaction (e.g., binding assays with wild-type and mutant lumican) or clearly state that the study is purely predictive and that experimental validation is required. The claim about binding strength should be tempered or supported with additional computational analyses (e.g., free energy calculations).
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Model quality and structural validation
  - **Claim pointer** The authors use the AlphaFold-predicted structure of lumican and the crystal structure of TLR9 (PDB: 3WPF) for docking, and they report a high pLDDT score for the lumican model.
  - **Evidence pointer** Section 2.1, 4.1
  - **Concern** While the pLDDT score for the lumican model is reported as high (>90%) for the analyzed range, the authors do not provide a Ramachandran plot, clash score, or other structural validation metrics for the final docked complexes. The quality of the docked models, including any steric clashes or unfavorable geometries, is not discussed. Additionally, the authors do not specify which residues of the lumican model were used for docking (e.g., whether the N-terminal and C-terminal regions were included) and how the model was prepared (e.g., protonation states, energy minimization).
  - **Why it matters** The reliability of the predicted interactions depends on the quality of the starting structures and the docked complexes. Without proper validation, the specific residue-level interactions reported may be artifacts of the modeling process.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should provide structural validation metrics for the docked complexes, such as Ramachandran analysis, clash scores, and a description of the model preparation steps. They should also discuss the potential impact of using a predicted structure (AlphaFold) on the docking results.

- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** The lack of docking scores and confidence metrics (R1-M1) and the absence of any experimental validation (R1-M2) are the primary technical failings. - **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - **Originality** The question of how lumican interacts with CpG DNA at a molecular level is novel, as previous work focused on functional outcomes. The use of computational modeling to address this is a reasonable first step.
  - **Scientific importance** The potential role of lumican in modulating TLR9 signaling and inflammation is of interest, but the current study does not provide direct evidence for this. The importance is therefore moderate and contingent on future experimental validation.
  - **Interdisciplinary readership** The topic bridges extracellular matrix biology and innate immunology, which could appeal to a broad audience. However, the purely computational nature of the study may limit its immediate appeal to experimentalists.
  - **Technical soundness** The technical soundness cannot be fully assessed due to missing data (docking scores, model validation). The methodology is described but not sufficiently detailed to be reproducible or to evaluate the quality of the results.
  - **Readability for nonspecialists** The manuscript is generally well-written and accessible, but the results section is dense with specific residue numbers and interaction types that may be challenging for nonspecialists. The figures (not provided) would likely help clarify the spatial arrangements.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The manuscript presents a plausible computational model, but the lack of key data and experimental validation prevents the claims from being supported. The authors should be encouraged to provide the missing data and to temper their conclusions or add experimental evidence.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that lumican binds CpG ODN_2395 with a "potentially lower binding strength" than TLR9 based solely on BSA comparison is unsupported. BSA is not a direct measure of binding affinity.
- The claim that the identified "hot spot" residues in lumican are "biochemically somewhat similar" to TLR9 residues is weakly supported, as the comparison is based on a low overall sequence identity (22%) and a structural alignment with a high RMSD (6.4 Å).
- The prediction that collagen binding will "likely interfere" with lumican-CpG DNA binding is speculative and not tested in this study.
- The statement that the study "provides molecular insights" that "may lead to molecular targets" is an overstatement given the purely computational nature of the work and the lack of experimental validation.
