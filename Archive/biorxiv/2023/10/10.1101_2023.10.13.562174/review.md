## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript addresses a relevant topic in transcription factor DNA recognition using computational approaches, but the current text is severely compromised by vague, imprecise, and at times nonsensical language that obscures the scientific content. The results section lacks quantitative detail, and the methods are described at a level insufficient for reproducibility. The scientific claims, while potentially interesting, cannot be evaluated from the provided material.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Computational biologists and structural biologists studying transcription factor DNA recognition dynamics might find the comparative analysis of two TF families of interest, provided the results are properly validated and clearly presented. Researchers working on Rex-family regulators or C2H2 zinc finger proteins could benefit from dynamic insights into binding mechanisms.
- **Major strengths** The selection of two distinct TF families for comparative dynamic analysis is a reasonable approach. The use of established tools (Gromacs, Amber force field, TIP3P water model, AlphaFold) is appropriate. The focus on multiple dynamic properties (RMSD, RMSF, SASA) provides a multifaceted view of TF DNA binding behavior.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that DNA association stabilizes the TF structure is unsupported without access to the RMSD data and statistical analysis.
- The claim that DNA association initiates double helix opening is unsupported without structural evidence or base-pair distance analysis.
- The claim that ZFH1 and ZFH2 exhibit distinct dynamic properties is unsupported without comparative quantitative data.
- The claim that the findings reveal "molecular mechanisms of the recognition and regulation" is not substantiated by the presented results.
- The description of the simulation methodology is incoherent and cannot be evaluated for correctness.
- The modeling and docking results for ZFH1 and ZFH2 are not validated or described in sufficient detail.
- The statement that the two TFs "were belonged to the same transcription families with slightly differences particularly in the DNA binding regions" is vague and not supported by sequence or structural comparison.

## Assessment against Nature-style criteria
- **Originality** The topic of TF DNA binding dynamics is not new, and the manuscript does not clearly articulate a novel hypothesis or approach. The comparative analysis of two TF families could be of interest, but the current presentation does not establish originality.
- **Scientific importance** The question of how TFs recognize and bind DNA is important, but the manuscript does not demonstrate that the findings provide significant new insights beyond existing knowledge. The lack of mechanistic detail and quantitative rigor limits the potential impact.
- **Interdisciplinary readership** The topic could appeal to structural biologists, computational biologists, and molecular biologists, but the unclear writing and lack of accessible explanations would likely deter nonspecialists.
- **Technical soundness** The technical soundness cannot be assessed due to the incoherent methods description and missing data. The reported values have large uncertainties that are not addressed.
- **Readability for nonspecialists** The manuscript is not readable even for specialists due to pervasive grammatical errors, vague terminology, and unclear logical flow. The abstract and introduction do not provide a clear entry point for readers.
