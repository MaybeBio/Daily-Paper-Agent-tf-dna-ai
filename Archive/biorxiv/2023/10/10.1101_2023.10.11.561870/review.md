However, the claims are broad and largely qualitative, and the absence of methodological detail and quantitative evidence prevents a rigorous evaluation of the central conclusions. The work may interest researchers in regulatory genomics and disease genetics, but the current evidence base is insufficient to establish the stated findings.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in regulatory genomics, population genetics, and disease variant interpretation would be interested, as the work addresses how non-coding variants impact gene regulation through coordinated chromatin elements. The focus on cell type-specificity and disease relevance, including chronic lymphocytic leukaemia, may attract translational researchers and those studying immune cell biology.

The proposed integration of epigenomic variation, chromatin modularity, and genotype information is conceptually appealing. The emphasis on cell type-specificity and practical guidelines for CM mapping could provide useful resources for the community.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Technical soundness
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim to "comprehensively evaluate and streamline existing CM mapping tools" and present "guidelines for optimal utilization."
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract provides no details on which tools were evaluated, what criteria were used for comparison, or how the guidelines were derived. There is no mention of benchmarking datasets, performance metrics, or statistical validation.
- **Why it matters** Without this information, the claim of a comprehensive evaluation and optimal guidelines cannot be assessed. The value of the proposed recommendations depends entirely on the rigor of the underlying comparison.
- **Resolution test** Provide a description of the tools evaluated, the benchmarking approach, and quantitative results showing superiority or equivalence of the recommended practices.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Technical soundness
- **Claim pointer** The authors state that "many non-coding disease-associated variants affect the activity of CMs in a cell type-specific manner by affecting the binding of cell type-specific transcription factors."
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract does not specify how variant effects on CM activity were measured, how cell type-specificity was established, or how transcription factor binding disruption was determined. No effect sizes, significance thresholds, or validation approaches are mentioned.
- **Why it matters** This is a central claim with broad implications for disease variant interpretation. Without evidence of robust statistical association and functional validation, the claim remains speculative.
- **Resolution test** Include details on the variant-CM association analysis, statistical controls, and experimental or computational validation of transcription factor binding effects.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Reproducibility
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim to demonstrate "cell type-specificity of CRE interactions in CMs and their relevance for gene expression."
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract does not describe the cell types analyzed, the number of individuals, the epigenomic assays used, or how cell type-specificity was quantified. No information is given on data access or code availability.
- **Why it matters** Reproducibility is a core requirement for studies of this nature. The absence of any methodological or data availability information prevents independent verification and limits the utility of the proposed guidelines.
- **Resolution test** Provide a detailed methods section, including data sources, sample sizes, analysis pipelines, and a clear statement on data and code availability.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim of a "comprehensive evaluation" of CM mapping tools is unsupported without details on the tools and benchmarks.
- The claim that "many" non-coding variants affect CM activity is unsupported without quantitative estimates of the number and proportion.
- The claim of cell type-specificity of CRE interactions is unsupported without specification of the cell types and statistical evidence.
- The relevance of CMs to gene expression is asserted but not demonstrated with any correlation or causality measures.
