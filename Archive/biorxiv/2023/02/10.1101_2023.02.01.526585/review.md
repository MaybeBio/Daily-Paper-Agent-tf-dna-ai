## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, conclusion) as provided
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific and technical evaluation of the manuscript's claims, methodology, and results as presented; no assessment of code functionality or reproducibility beyond what is described
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors present PLANET, a graph neural network model that predicts protein-ligand binding affinity using only the 3D structure of the binding pocket and the 2D chemical structure of the ligand. The model is trained via a multi-objective process incorporating binding affinity, protein-ligand contact map, and intra-ligand distance matrix prediction tasks. The authors claim scoring power comparable to other deep learning models on CASF-2016, superior screening power on DUD-E and LIT-PCBA compared to other deep learning models, and computational efficiency approximately two orders of magnitude faster than Glide SP.

## Reviewer

- **Overall assessment** The manuscript addresses an important problem in computational drug discovery, namely the development of efficient and accurate protein-ligand binding affinity prediction methods that do not require 3D complex structures as input. The multi-objective training strategy is a reasonable and potentially valuable contribution, and the reported screening power results on DUD-E and LIT-PCBA are of interest. However, the manuscript has several significant technical weaknesses that need to be addressed. The comparison to other methods in Table 1 is incomplete without error bars or statistical significance testing. The claim that PLANET's scoring power is "underrated" due to not using 3D complex structures is speculative and not directly tested. The ablation study design is reasonable but the interpretation is somewhat limited. The computational efficiency comparison to Glide is interesting but the comparison is not entirely fair given that Glide performs exhaustive conformational sampling. The manuscript would benefit from a more rigorous statistical analysis of the results and a clearer discussion of the limitations of the approach.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Computational chemists and structural biologists working on structure-based drug design would be interested in this work. The model's ability to predict binding affinity without requiring 3D complex structures could be valuable for virtual screening applications where complex structures are not readily available. Researchers developing deep learning methods for molecular property prediction would also be interested in the multi-objective training approach and the use of auxiliary tasks to improve model generalization. The comparison with Glide SP on LIT-PCBA is relevant to practitioners who need to choose between docking-based and machine learning-based screening approaches.
- **Major strengths** 1) The problem addressed is practically important, and the model design that avoids the need for 3D complex structures is a meaningful contribution. 2) The multi-objective training strategy with auxiliary tasks for contact map and intra-ligand distance prediction is well-motivated and provides some interpretability. 3) The evaluation on multiple benchmarks (CASF-2016, DUD-E, LIT-PCBA) is comprehensive. 4) The ablation studies help to isolate the contributions of different training components. 5) The computational efficiency advantage over Glide is clearly demonstrated and practically relevant.
- **Major Concerns** 
  - R1-M1: The comparison of scoring power in Table 1 lacks statistical rigor. The authors report a single Pearson correlation coefficient and RMSE for PLANET on CASF-2016 without error bars, confidence intervals, or statistical significance tests against other methods. Given that the differences between methods are small (e.g., R_p values around 0.80), it is unclear whether the observed differences are meaningful.
  - R1-M2: The claim that PLANET's scoring power is "underrated" because it does not use 3D complex structures is not directly tested. The authors compare against methods that use crystal complex structures, but they do not provide a direct comparison where PLANET is given the same information (i.e., 3D complex structures) to isolate the effect of input representation.
  - R1-M3: The screening power evaluation on DUD-E and LIT-PCBA is presented without a clear description of how the binding pocket was defined for each target. The manuscript states that the binding pocket is defined by the known ligand, but the details of how this was done for targets with multiple known ligands or for the decoy molecules are not provided.
  - R1-M4: The computational efficiency comparison between PLANET and Glide is not entirely fair. Glide performs exhaustive conformational sampling, which is a different task than what PLANET does. A more appropriate comparison would be against other deep learning methods that also avoid docking, or the authors should clearly acknowledge this difference in the comparison.
  - R1-M5: The manuscript does not provide sufficient details on the hyperparameter selection process, the sensitivity of the results to these hyperparameters, or the training convergence. This limits the reproducibility of the work.
- **Minor Comments** 
  - R1-m1: The abstract states that PLANET "exhibited a scoring power comparable to other deep learning models" but the manuscript later claims it is "at least close to the best of these." These statements should be consistent.
  - R1-m2: The description of the decoy selection process is somewhat brief. The manuscript states that decoys were selected based on similar physicochemical properties, but the specific criteria and the number of decoys per binder are not clearly stated.
  - R1-m3: The manuscript mentions that the scale of the three objectives was close to 10.0 at the beginning of training, but it is unclear how this was determined and whether the weights were adjusted during training.
  - R1-m4: The UMAP analysis in Figure 6 is mentioned but not discussed in detail. The authors should explain what the reader should take away from this visualization.
  - R1-m5: The manuscript states that "PLANET achieves its computational efficiency by skipping the time-consuming molecular docking process and replaces it by prediction of the protein-ligand contact map." This is somewhat misleading, as the contact map prediction is an auxiliary task, not a replacement for docking.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 (statistical rigor of comparisons), R1-M2 (direct test of the "underrated" claim), R1-M3 (binding pocket definition details), R1-M4 (fairness of computational comparison), R1-M5 (hyperparameter and training details)
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - Originality: The multi-objective training approach with auxiliary tasks for contact map and intra-ligand distance prediction is a reasonable contribution, but similar ideas have been explored in other deep learning works. The specific combination of inputs (3D pocket + 2D ligand) is somewhat novel but not groundbreaking.
  - Scientific importance: The problem of efficient binding affinity prediction is important for drug discovery. The potential for fast virtual screening without docking is practically valuable. However, the demonstrated performance gains over existing methods are modest, and the limitations of the approach are not fully addressed.
  - Interdisciplinary readership: The work is primarily of interest to computational chemists and structural biologists. The methods and results are presented in a way that is accessible to these communities, but the broader interdisciplinary appeal is limited.
  - Technical soundness: The methodology is generally sound, but the lack of statistical rigor in the comparisons and the incomplete details on hyperparameters and training procedures are concerns. The ablation study is well-designed but the interpretation could be more thorough.
  - Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is generally well-written and the concepts are explained clearly. However, some sections, particularly the model architecture description, may be challenging for readers without a background in graph neural networks.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The core idea is promising and the results are of interest, but the manuscript needs to address the statistical rigor of the comparisons, provide more details on the experimental setup, and clarify the claims about the model's advantages.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that PLANET's scoring power is "underrated" by the CASF-2016 test is speculative and not directly supported by the data presented.
- The statement that PLANET "has some 'understanding' of protein-ligand interactions" based on the wider range of predicted affinities is an overinterpretation of the results.
- The claim that the model is "more robust and more efficient" than methods using 3D complex structures is not fully supported, as the robustness is not directly tested and the efficiency comparison is not entirely fair.
- The assertion that the decoy selection process "greatly expanded" the chemical space coverage is not quantitatively supported in the visible text.
- The claim that the multi-objective training strategy is the primary reason for the improved screening power is not fully isolated from other factors, such as the architecture choices or the training data augmentation.
