Perfluorooctanoic acid (PFOA) and perfluorooctanesulfonic acid (PFOS) are widely detected in the environment and wild animals, thus posing a threat to wildlife and public health; however, knowledge about their immunotoxicity and the underlying mechanism remains limited. In the present study, male black-spotted frogs (Rana nigromaculata) were exposed to environmentally relevant concentrations (0, 1, and 10 mug/L) of PFOA or PFOS for 21 days; subsequently, biochemical analysis, molecular docking, and gene expression determination were conducted. The results indicated that exposure to 10 mug/L PFOA decreased the serum levels of immunoglobulin A. PFOS exposure significantly increased the hepatic levels of interleukin-1beta, interleukin-6, tumor necrosis factor-alpha, interferon-gamma, and nitric oxide; but PFOA significantly increased the levels of only tumor necrosis factor-alpha. Furthermore, PFOA and PFOS exposure significantly decreased the activity of inducible nitric oxide synthase and total nitric oxide synthase. IBRv2 analysis indicated that PFOA and PFOS had a similar effect on these immune indicators, but PFOS was more toxic than PFOA. Molecular docking revealed that PFOA and PFOS can bind to nuclear factor-kappaB (NF-kappaB) by forming stable hydrogen bonds. PFOA and PFOS exposure upregulated the gene expression of NF-kappaB and its downstream genes. Significant correlations between the expression of genes involved in the NF-kappaB pathway and immune-related indicators suggests that PFOA- and PFOS-induced immunotoxicity was associated with the activation of NF-kappaB. Our findings provide novel insights into the potential role of NF-kappaB in immunotoxicity induced by PFOA and PFOS in frogs.
