# Identification of probable inhibitors for the DNA polymerase of the Monkeypox virus through the virtual screening approach


## Abstract
  Given the paucity of antiviral treatments for monkeypox disease, caused by the Monkeypox virus (MPXV), there is a pressing need for the development/identification of new drugs to treat the infection. MPXV possesses a linear dsDNA genome that is replicated by a DNA replication complex of which DNA polymerase (DPol) forms an important component. Owing to the importance of DPol in the viral life cycle, identifying/designing small molecules abolishing its function could yield new antivirals. In this study, we first used the AlphaFold artificial intelligence program to model the 3D structure of the MPXV DPol; like the fold of DPol from other organisms, the MPXV DPol structure has the characteristic exonuclease, thumb, palm, and fingers sub-domains arrangement. Subsequently, we have identified several inhibitors through virtual screening of ZINC and antiviral libraries. Molecules with phenyl scaffold along with alanine-based and tetrazole-based molecules showed the best docking score of −8 to −10 kcal/mol. These molecules bind in the palm and fingers sub-domains interface region, which partially overlaps with the DNA binding path. The delineation of DPol/inhibitor interactions showed that majorly active site residues ASP549, ASP753, TYR550, ASN551, SER552, and ASN665 interact with the inhibitors. These compounds exhibit good Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism and Excretion properties.


## Introduction
  Monkeypox virus (MPXV), the causative agent of monkeypox disease, was isolated and identified from cynomolgus monkeys maintained in the laboratory [1]. MPXV belongs to the clade Orthopoxviruses (family Poxviridae), which contains notable viruses such as Cowpox (CPXV), Variola (VARV) [causative agent of smallpox] and Vaccinia (VACV) [2], [3], [4]. Monkeypox infection was earlier reported in the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) in 1970, where the fatality rate ranged from 3.6 % to 10.6 % between various strains [5]. MPXV infection is not new; however, it is spreading worldwide at an increasing rate causing diseases in various countries [6], [7], [8]; therefore, the current and upcoming population pool are considered completely immune naïve. Thus, these people are predisposed to any accidental or deliberate infection from exposure to virulent MPXV disease or any other Orthopoxviruses and this predisposition poses a severe threat to public health [9]. The disease manifestation of MPXV infection is similar to smallpox, but with a more localized pustular rash distribution and significantly lower casualties [10], [11]. PCR, tissue culture, electron microscopy, and immunohistochemistry, such as enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) are used to diagnose MPXV infection [12].

  All the members of Poxviridae family have a linear, double-stranded DNA genome [13]. MPXV, VACV and VARV have a genome size of approximately 197 kb [14], 190 kb [15] and 186 kb [16], respectively. Members of the Poxviridae family possess the E9L gene-encoded DNA polymerase (DPol) which is a major component of the DNA replication complex and has been extensively studied [17], [18], [19]. Mainly due to their critical roles in viral replication, DPols and RNA polymerases are major targets for developing antiviral interventions [20], [21], [22], [23].

  The world health organization's (WHO) declaration, “monkeypox outbreak represents a public health emergency of international concern,” [6], [24] necessitates to ramp up efforts towards finding solutions to treat monkeypox infection. In this context, one approach beyond the vaccines to limit the virus spread would be developing small molecules targeting the function of key viral enzymes. Over the years, the availability of many small molecule databases and enhanced automation involving advanced tools and networks in target-based drug discovery areas has accelerated the efforts for identifying new drug candidates. For example, many of these kinds of studies led to the discovery of a large number of lead molecules [25], [26], [27], [28], [29], [30]. Although the WHO has recommended the current smallpox drugs Tecovirimat (TPOXX or ST-246), Brincidofovir (Tembexa), and Cidofovir to treat monkeypox [6], [10], new molecules must be discovered due to the lack of a specific antiviral treatment for monkeypox. In this study, we sought to identify new small molecule inhibitors targeting the MPXV DPol (encoded by OPG071) employing a structure-based approach. As the experimental 3D structure of MPXV DPol is unavailable, we first built a model by the AlphaFold Artificial intelligence (AI) program [31]. In comparison to homolog DPols, the modeled structure of MPXV DPol consists of palm, thumb, fingers, exonuclease, and N-terminal domains. These domains are organized in a way that shares an architectural feature as a disk with a hole in the center. Additionally, using high-throughput virtual screening, we have identified several small molecule inhibitors against the active site of MPXV DPol. These proposed compounds would help in the development of novel therapeutics against MPXV for the treatment of monkeypox. This study aimed to identify potential inhibitors for the MPXV DPol that could provide a base for developing effective antivirals for monkeypox infection.


## Materials and methods

### Model construction, sequence alignment and figure generation
  In order to construct a 3D model structure of MPXV DPol, its amino acid sequence (Gene ID- OPG071; Protein ID- QNP12927.1) was obtained in the FASTA format from the NCBI database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) [32]. Subsequently, this sequence was provided to the AlphaFold AI program [31] for structure generation. The stereochemical quality and accuracy of the AlphaFold predicted model was evaluated using Ramachandran map calculations computed with the PROCHECK [33] program. Additionally, the BLASTp server (https://blast.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Blast.cgi?PAGE=Proteins) [34] was used for structural homologs prediction. Sequence alignment and its analysis were carried out using Clustal Omega [35] and ESpript [36]. The DPol/DNA interactions were visualized using the protein-ligand interaction profiler tool [37].


### Receptor preparation and grid generation
  An important and essential step for docking/virtual screening is the receptor preparation. The preparation of the receptor includes assigning bond orders and adding missing hydrogen atoms, followed by optimizing orientations of hydrogen-bonded groups and minimizing the structure using optimized potentials for liquid simulations (OPLS-3) force field [38]. This process was performed using the Protein preparation wizard module [39] in Schrödinger [40]. Afterward, the receptor grid box was generated using the receptor grid generation wizard in the Glide grid module (Maestro version 12.8.117) in Schrödinger. The receptor grid box includes active site residues ASP549, ASP753, TYR550, ASN551, SER552, and ASN665.


### Compounds library screening
  To identify MPXV DPol specific inhibitors, we screened two compound libraries: drug-like molecules (available at ZINC database [41]) and the antiviral annotated library (ChemDiv, available at https://www.chemdiv.com/catalog/sets/Antiviral annotated-library/). After the protein preparation and grid generation, antiviral annotated compounds and drug-like compounds were used for High Throughput Virtual Screening (HTVS) against the target protein using Schrödinger's Maestro module [40]. This module utilizes the structure data file (SDF/MOL File) of compounds and glide grid file [42] of the receptor as the input and performs with default parameters employing the Glide program of Schrödinger. HTVS and Standard Precision (SP) docking steps utilize the self-same scoring function, whereas Extra Precision (XP) uses a different scoring function. XP helps to answer how good a given pose of a given molecule is compared to HTVS and SP via reducing the intermediate conformations and thoroughness of the torsional refinement and sampling [41]. All figures were generated using PyMol software (PyMOL Molecular Graphics System Version 2.3.5, Schrödinger, LLC) [43] and the Maestro software visualization tool [40].


### Prediction of binding free energies and Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism and Excretion (ADME) properties
  Prime Molecular Mechanics Generalized Born Surface Area (MM-GBSA) [44] post-docking application of Schrödinger software was used to analyze the binding free energy of identified ligand molecules to provide the correct ranking based on their MM-GBSA score. The docking score and binding energy/affinity are given in kcal/mol. Lipinski's rule of five (RoF 5) is a rule of thumb and ADME that helps in distinguishing between drug-like and non-drug molecules. The QikProp module, Schrödinger [45], [46], was used to analyze the druggable properties of the compounds.


## Results and discussions

### Prediction of the 3D structure of MPXV DPol and illustrating its structural features
  Our search to find an experimental structure of the MPXV DPol in the protein data bank showed no structure availability; hence we built its model structure using the AlphaFold AI program [31]. The stereochemistry of the AlphaFold predicted model (Fig. 1A) was verified by the PROCHECK [33] validation server, and the Ramachandran plot showed 93.3 % of the residues in the most favorable region. No residue was found in the disallowed region (Fig. S1). The AlphaFold model structure of MPXV DPol contains five major domains: the palm, the thumb, the fingers, the exonuclease, and the N-terminus, similar to as described earlier for other DPols [47], [48]. The domains share an architectural feature as a disk with a hole in the center (Fig. 1A). The N-terminal domain is comprised of mainly residues 1–157 and 497–523; exonuclease domain: 158–353 and 435–496; palm domain: 524–618 and 676–829; Insert II: 354–434; thumb domain: 830–1006, and fingers domain: 619–675 (Fig. 1A, B, C, D, E). The palm domain contains the polymerase activity. The fingers domain interacts with the template strand and plays a major role in nucleotide recognition and binding. The thumb is involved in DNA substrate binding. The exonuclease domain provides binding platform for the primer in the editing mode [47], [48], [49]. The N-terminal domain (colored in green) links the palm domain (in blue) and exonuclease domain (Fig. 1A, B). The loop of the N-terminal domain interacts with the loop of the palm and exonuclease domain and thus is playing a role in polymerase stability and fidelity (Fig. 1A, B) [50], [51]. The exonuclease domain (in red) lies on the opposite side of the polymerase active site of the palm domain (Fig. 1B) having a 3′-5′ proofreading activity. In the process of proofreading activity, the β-hairpin motif which is a part of the exonuclease domain plays an important role in the partitioning of the DNA between the polymerization and proofreading sites [52], [53], [54]. This large β-hairpin motif (in purple) consisting of residues 300–326 has important glycine residues, as shown in Fig. 1C. The insert II domain (in cyan) lies within the essential exonuclease domain and consists of an alpha-helix and a little 6-stranded β-barrel structure (Fig. 1A, B). The palm domain is highly conserved in all the DPols. This domain (in blue, Fig. 1A, B) has two conserved motifs, “DXXSLYP” (found in loop region; residues 549–555, in yellow and green) and “DTDS” [51], [54], [55], [56] (found in the loop-helix region; residues 751–754, in yellow and green). These motifs contain two highly conserved Aspartic acids (in green), as shown in Fig. 1E, that are crucial for catalyzing the nucleotide transfer reaction via serving as ligands for metal ions A and B. Thus, the palm domain shows catalytic core polymerase activity. The fingers domain (in yellow, Fig. 1A, B) is composed of two long alpha-helices and is located in the palm domain having a conserved dNTP binding motif “KXXXNSXYG” [51], [54], [55], [56] as shown in Fig. 1D (found in the helix region; residue 661–669, conserved residues are shown in pink and rest in yellow). ### Structural comparison
  In order to compare structural similarities and differences, we superimposed the model structure of MPXV DPol with the available crystal structure of DPol of VACV (PDB ID: 5N2E [59]); and observed a significant amount of structural similarity with a root mean square deviation (RMSD) value of 0.353 Å (Fig. S2 A). However, the β-hairpin of the exonuclease domain and the region of thumb and palm interface of MPXV DPol (Fig. S2 A) are structurally ordered than that of the similar regions of the VACV DPol. Additionally, the conserved sequence palm motif-1 DXXSLYP and motif-2 DTDS and a fingers motif KXXXNSXYG known for DPol of VACV, Human herpesvirus, Homo sapiens, and Saccharomyces cerevisiae are present in MPXV DPol sequence. Multiple sequence alignment of the DPols encoded by these organisms show that motif 1 and motif 2 contain the catalytic Aspartic acid residues in the active site in the palm domain (Fig. S2 B).


### Delineating the DNA binding path and mapping DPol/DNA interactions
  The genome of MPXV forms concatemers, which then become the template for replication. This happens because the genome contains a multitude of cis-acting replication-sequence. The single most important event of initiation of replication is the assembly of the replication initiation complex at the origin of replication, consisting of protein, namely DPol, helicase-primase, uracil DNA glycosylase, processivity factor, protein kinase, single-stranded DNA-binding protein, DNA ligase, and FEN1 protein [13], [60]. Of these proteins, DPol polymerizes the replicating chain of DNA. Hence, in order to understand the DPol/DNA complex, we generated a model of the MPXV DPol initiation complex using the Human delta DPol structure (PDB ID: 6S1M, chain A [61]) as the template. Notably, the superimposition of both MPXV DPol and DNA/Thymidine-5′-Triphosphate (DNA/TTP)-bound DPol structure of Human yielded an RMSD value 3.80 Å (599 residues align) shown in Fig. 2A. Notably, this high value of RMSD is largely due to low sequence identity (23.95 %) between these two DPols. This model enabled us to delineate the probable DNA binding path of MPXV DPol (Fig. 2B). The DNA interacts with the palm, thumb, fingers, and N-terminal domains of DPol. ### Identification of inhibitors against DPol through chemical library screening
  Prior studies suggest that the polymerase protein has been extensively studied [13], [39], [59] and is the most targeted protein in the poxvirus family, which includes the VACV [18], [19], [20]. Since MPXV belong to this family, it can also be targeted to find novel inhibitors for DPol. In the computer-aided drug screening by molecular docking study, we used antiviral annotated compounds and drug-like compounds from the ZINC database against MPXV DPol. All ligand compounds were docked in the predicted active site. Subsequently, the potential molecules with the highest estimated binding affinity against the target protein were selected for further analysis. The Maestro software visualization of the docked pose of every ligand showed the binding pocket of protein where ligands are bound. ### Delineating the enzyme/inhibitors interactions
  To gain insights into the enzyme/inhibitor interactions, we carried out a detailed analysis of the results of the compound screening. The DNA binding site residues GLU790, ARG634, ASP549, GLU792, LYS340, and ASP753 of DPol were involved in forming salt bridge interactions with most of the top screened compounds from the antiviral annotated library (Table 3 and Supplementary Table S1). The detailed view of the molecular interactions between DPol and antiviral annotated compounds showed that the compound ID 1040 (docking score − 8.4 kcal/mol) was involved in forming salt bridge interaction with ARG497 (Fig. 6A); compound ID 1425 (docking score − 7.5 kcal/mol) with GLU790 (Fig. 6B). The compound ID 6436 (docking score − 7.4 kcal/mol) (Fig. 6C), compound ID 89 (docking score − 7.1 kcal/mol) (Fig. 6D), and compound ID 9857 (docking score -7.1 kcal/mol) (Fig. 6E) were found to interact with DPol via a salt bridge with residue ARG634.

  Careful investigation of the docking complexes of DPol with most of the top screened compounds from the ZINC database revealed that residues ASN551, SER552, TYR554 and ASN665 formed hydrogen bonds while residues LYS804 and LYS661 formed salt bridge interactions. On the other hand, residues LYS308, LYS340, ASP753, GLU792, GLU790, ARG634, ASP549, and LYS638 were involved in both hydrogen bond and salt bridge interactions as shown in Table 4 and Supplementary Table S2. Exploration of interactions between enzyme/inhibitors of ZINC compound showed that ZINC000003860313 (docking score − 11.5 kcal/mol) formed both hydrogen bond and salt bridge interactions with LYS308, LYS340 and hydrogen bond with LYS804 (Fig. 7A). ZINC000025628231 (docking score − 10.4 kcal/mol) is involved in hydrogen bond interactions with LYS638, ARG634; salt bridge interactions with ASN551, SER552; and ASP753 is involved in both hydrogen bond and salt bridge interactions (Fig. 7B). In the interactions view of ZINC000034743445 (docking score − 9.5 kcal/mol), residue ASP753 formed both hydrogen bond and salt bridge interactions; residues ASP549, ASP751 formed salt bridge interaction and TYR554 formed hydrogen bond interaction (Fig. 7C). Similarly, ZINC000005648679 (docking score − 9.1 kcal/mol) revealed hydrogen bond interactions found with ARG634, ASN665, SER552, LEU553 and a salt bridge interaction with GLU792, GLU790 (Fig. 7D). ZINC000239038527 (docking score − 9.0 kcal/mol) was found to interact with DPol with hydrogen bonding via residues ASN551, SER552, LEU553; salt bridge interactions via LYS638; both interactions via ARG634, ASP753 (Fig. 7E).

  To comprehend the enzyme/inhibitor interactions, we superimposed the docked structures of the identified hit compounds with substrate dNTPs, FDA-approved anti-smallpox drugs, and the crystal structure of TTP-bound human DPol. These molecules sit in the vicinity of the template DNA in the active pocket of MPXV DPol, similar to TTP (Fig. S3 and Fig. S6A). In the substrate, dGTP, dCTP, and dTTP interact with ASP753 in motif-1 and TYR554 in motif-2 of the palm domain, while dATP interacts with motif-2 catalytic residue (ASP753) as shown in Fig. 4, Fig. S6B, and Table 1. Moreover, the binding sites of each dNTP are comparable, with specific residues interacting with the exonuclease domain, as observed in the crystal structure of TTP-bound human DPol. Besides this, these are comparable with Brincidofovir, Cidofovir, and Tecovirimat, which target similar catalytic residue ASP753 of motif-2 of the palm domain; Brincidofovir also targets another similar catalytic residue ASP549 in the motif-1 (Fig. 5, Fig. S6C, and Table 2). In addition, the docked structure of antiviral annotated compound ID 1040 interacts directly with DNA in the vicinity of the N-terminal domain of ARG497 (Fig. 6 and Table 3). Other antiviral compounds, similar to dNTPs, surround the template DNA (Fig. S3 and Fig. S6A). Similar to dNTPs and antiviral annotated compounds, the ZINC compound resides near the template DNA in the active pocket, as shown in Fig. S3, Fig. S4, and S6D). There was a striking resemblance between the interactions of these compounds and the dNTPs-active site interactions that target the catalytic ASP residue in either motif-1 or motif-2 of the palm domain (Fig. S2).

  The results of the current study suggest that antiviral annotated ID 1040 (PubChem ID 4410488) with a docking score of −8.4 kcal/mol and binding energy of −58.32 kcal/mol from the antiviral annotated library and the two compounds ZINC000025628231 and ZINC000239038527 with docking score of −10.4 kcal/mol, and −9.0 kcal/mol; and the binding score of −48.65 kcal/mol, and −52.88 kcal/mol respectively from the ZINC database have prospects to become potent anti-MPXV compounds. In summary, the compound ID 1040, ZINC000025628231, and ZINC000239038527 can bind to MPXV DPol, with binding energies comparable to dNTPs and known drugs, hence contradicting the function of the DPol leading to viral eradication (graphical abstract).

## Conclusions and future perspective
  In the pursuit of deriving the structural insights into the MPXP DPol and utilizing these results to design/ identify inhibitors against it has led to significant outcomes. Firstly, the analysis of the 3D structure pinpointed structural differences between the DPols of MPXV and VACV. In particular, the β-hairpin of the exonuclease domain and the region of thumb and palm interface of MPXV DPol (Fig. S2 A) are structurally ordered than that of the similar regions of the VACV DPol. However, both these regions are far apart from the inhibitor binding sites. Further, the 3D structure enabled tracing the probable DNA binding path, which importantly aided in mapping the interactions between the DPol/DNA. This information was critical for choosing the grid box (contains the active site residues ASP549, ASP753, TYR550, ASN551, SER552 and ASN665) size for virtual screening of compound libraries.

  Importantly, the ZINC database compound ZINC000025628231 shows a high docking score (−10.4 kcal/mol) followed by ZINC000239038527 (−9.0 kcal/mol) and antiviral annotated compound 1040 (− 8.4 kcal/mol). Major interactions between the DPol and compounds pertaining to these docking scores are mainly hydrogen bond, salt bridge, and Pi-Pi types. These three molecules can be potential anti-MPXV drug candidates. The results of our study form a basis to carry out further experimental studies which could yield anti-monkeypox lead molecules.
