## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, methodology, data interpretation, and conclusions as presented in the manuscript
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors identify specific amino acid changes that transform the ancestral KRAB-A domain of human PRDM9 into a canonical modern KRAB-A domain capable of transcriptional repression and TRIM28 binding, guided by the KRAB domain of ZNF10, and propose structural and evolutionary implications based on AlphaFold2 modeling.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract, Introduction, Results (Sections 2.1–2.5), Discussion, Methods (Section 4), Conclusions, Acknowledgments
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Supplementary figures and tables (e.g., Figure S1, S2, Table S1, S2, PDB files) referenced but not provided; raw data for luciferase assays and immunoprecipitation quantifications not included; full sequence alignments and HMM details not fully described.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript presents a systematic and well-structured investigation into the evolutionary transition from ancestral to modern KRAB domains, using a combination of sequence analysis, mutagenesis, functional assays, and structural modeling. The identification of specific amino acid changes (MLE insertion, F9, L20, V27) that confer canonical function to PRDM9's aKRAB-A is a valuable contribution. However, several technical and interpretive concerns need to be addressed to fully establish the claims, particularly regarding the generalizability of the findings, the quantitative rigor of the functional assays, and the interpretation of AlphaFold2 models.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in the fields of transcriptional regulation, evolutionary biology, and structural biology would find this work relevant. Specifically, those studying KRAB zinc finger proteins, TRIM28-mediated repression, and the evolution of protein domains would benefit from the identified amino acid determinants and the proposed evolutionary trajectory. The findings may also interest scientists working on genome engineering tools that utilize KRAB domains.
- **Major strengths** The study combines evolutionary sequence analysis with experimental validation, providing a clear and testable model for the functional evolution of KRAB domains. The use of ZNF10 as a blueprint and the systematic mutagenesis approach are logical and well-executed. The integration of AlphaFold2 modeling adds a structural dimension that strengthens the interpretation. The manuscript is clearly written and the figures are informative.
- **Major Concerns** 
  - **R1-M1** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes) The claim that the identified amino acid changes are sufficient to transform aKRAB-A into a canonical mKRAB-A is based on a limited set of mutants and a single functional context. The authors do not test whether these changes are sufficient in the absence of the ZNF10-B domain, nor do they explore whether the same changes would work in other aKRAB-A contexts. The generalizability of the findings is therefore not established.
  - **R1-M2** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes) The quantitative data for the luciferase reporter assays and immunoprecipitation experiments are not fully presented. The manuscript reports fold-repression values and co-immunoprecipitation results, but without statistical analysis, error bars, or raw data, the robustness of the functional differences between mutants cannot be assessed.
  - **R1-M3** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No) The interpretation of AlphaFold2 models as evidence for a "protein-protein interaction code" is speculative. The models are not experimentally validated, and the authors do not provide sufficient evidence that the predicted structural differences correlate with functional outcomes beyond the tested mutants. The claim that side-chain orientations reveal a code for graded TRIM28 binding is not directly supported by the data.
- **Minor Comments** 
  - **R1-m1** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Clarity) The manuscript would benefit from a clearer description of the criteria used to define "ancestral" versus "modern" KRAB domains, particularly in the context of the sequence logo analysis and HMM classification.
  - **R1-m2** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Methodology) The authors should provide more details on the construction of the HMMs, including the alignment parameters and the threshold for inclusion of sequences, to allow reproducibility.
  - **R1-m3** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Presentation) The figures are generally clear, but the labels in Figure 3 and Figure 7 could be more explicit to help readers follow the mutant nomenclature and structural comparisons.
  - **R1-m4** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Interpretation) The discussion of the evolutionary implications would be strengthened by a more explicit comparison with previously proposed models of KRAB evolution, and by acknowledging the limitations of inferring evolutionary trajectories from a limited set of extant sequences.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** The lack of quantitative data and statistical analysis for the functional assays (R1-M2) is a critical technical shortcoming. Additionally, the absence of experimental validation for the AlphaFold2 models (R1-M3) limits the strength of the structural claims. The sufficiency of the identified changes without the ZNF10-B domain (R1-M1) is a key technical gap.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - **Originality** The work is original in its systematic identification of specific amino acid changes that confer canonical function to an ancestral KRAB domain. This goes beyond previous descriptive studies and provides a mechanistic hypothesis.
  - **Scientific importance** The findings are important for understanding the evolution of a major transcriptional repression system and may have implications for the design of KRAB-based tools. However, the limited generalizability and lack of quantitative rigor reduce the immediate impact.
  - **Interdisciplinary readership** The topic is of interest to evolutionary biologists, molecular biologists, and structural biologists. The use of AlphaFold2 may also attract computational biologists. The manuscript is accessible to a broad scientific audience.
  - **Technical soundness** The experimental design is logical, but the lack of quantitative data and statistical analysis is a significant weakness. The AlphaFold2 modeling is used appropriately but without experimental validation.
  - **Readability for nonspecialists** The manuscript is well-written and the concepts are explained clearly. The figures aid understanding, though some labels could be improved.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The core idea is promising and the experimental approach is sound, but the manuscript requires additional quantitative data, statistical analysis, and a more cautious interpretation of the structural models before the claims can be fully established.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that the identified amino acid changes are sufficient to transform aKRAB-A into a canonical mKRAB-A is not fully supported, as the experiments were only performed in the context of the ZNF10-B domain and with a limited set of mutants.
- The assertion that AlphaFold2 models reveal a "protein-protein interaction code" is speculative and not directly validated by experimental data.
- The statement that the transition from aKRAB to mKRAB occurred during the evolution of the common ancestors of coelacanth and tetrapods is inferred from sequence analysis and not directly tested.
- The claim that the identified changes "emulate transitions of KRAB during evolution" is an interpretation that goes beyond the direct evidence presented.
