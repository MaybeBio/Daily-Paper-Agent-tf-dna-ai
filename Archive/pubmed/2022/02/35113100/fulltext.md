Ku70 protein and topoisomerase IIalpha (Topo IIalpha) are promising targets of anticancer drugs, which play critical roles in DNA repair and replication processes. Three platinum(II) complexes, [PtCl(NH(3))(2)(9-(pyridin-2-ylmethyl)-9H-carbazole)]NO(3) (OPPC), [PtCl(NH(3))(2)(9-(pyridin-3-ylmethyl)-9H-carbazole)]NO(3) (MPPC), and [PtCl(NH(3))(2)(9-(pyridin-4-ylmethyl)-9H-carbazole)]NO(3) (PPPC), were designed as inhibitors of Ku70 and Topo IIalpha. Their antitumor activity and inhibitory efficacy on Ku70 and Topo IIalpha were investigated on cellular and molecular levels. OPPC exhibited high antiproliferative activity against various cancer cell lines, with acute toxicity to mice being lower than that of cisplatin. Moreover, OPPC could enter cancer cells effectively and cause DNA damage, which was evidenced by the enhanced expression of gamma-H2AX, Chk1/2 phosphorylation, p53 and cell cycle arrest. OPPC also downregulated the DNA damage repair protein Ku70 and inhibited the formation of Ku70 foci-the central points or loci of Ku70, which would suppress DNA repair and induce a nonhomologous end joining response in cancer cells. More importantly, these complexes showed inhibition towards Topo IIalpha; in particular, OPPC was more effective than MPPC and PPPC. In the Topo IIalpha knockdown cells, Ku70 and Topo IIalpha were directly associated with the DNA damage and apoptotic response. The molecular docking provided detailed structural insights into the interactions of the complexes with Topo IIalpha. This study demonstrates that the cytotoxicity of these complexes is associated with the DNA damage and repair pathways mediated by Ku70 and Topo IIalpha; OPPC is an effective inhibitor of Ku70 and Topo IIalpha and restrains cancer cells via a mechanism utterly distinct from that of cisplatin.
