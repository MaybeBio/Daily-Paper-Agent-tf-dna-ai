# Mechanism of Astragali Radix for the treatment of osteoarthritis: A study based on network pharmacology and molecular docking


## Abstract
  Osteoarthritis (OA) is a degenerative joint disease caused by many factors. Astragali Radix (Huangqi), a traditional Chinese medicine (TCM), is widely used to treat OA. Although it can inhibit the progression of OA, its pharmacological mechanism is unclear.

  In this study, we used a network pharmacological approach to determine the mechanism by which Huangqi inhibits the progression of OA. We obtained the active ingredients of Huangqi from the Traditional Chinese Systems Pharmacology database and identified potential targets of these ingredients. Next, we identified the OA-related targets by using the GeneCards and Online Mendelian Inheritance in Man databases. Then, a protein–protein interaction (PPI) network was established based on the overlapping genes between the Huangqi targets and the OA targets, and the interactions were analyzed. Subsequently, the Metascape database was used to perform the Gene Ontology biological functions and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes pathways enrichment analysis. Furthermore, selected active ingredients and corresponding targets were investigated through molecular docking.

  In total, 20 active ingredients and 206 related targets were identified. The results of Gene Ontology enrichment analysis showed that the intersection targets were mainly involved in immune inflammation, proliferation, and apoptosis. The Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes pathway analysis revealed that Huangqi might exert antiosteoarthritis effect mainly through the PI3K–Akt signaling pathway, apoptosis, the mitogen-activated protein kinases signaling pathway, and the p53 signaling pathway. Moreover, the molecular docking results indicated that quercetin and kaempferol exhibited the good binding capacity to transcription factor JUN, tumor necrosis factor, and protein kinase B.

  In summary, we investigated the therapeutic effects of Huangqi from a systemic perspective. These key targets and pathways provide promising directions for future studies to reveal the exact regulating mechanism of Huangqi against OA.


## 1. Introduction
  Osteoarthritis (OA) is a degenerative joint disease that adversely affects the quality of life of the middle-aged and elderly. The typical clinical symptoms are articular cartilage degeneration, synovitis, narrowing of joint space, joint pain, swelling, limitation of movement, and deformities.[1–3] About 300 million people worldwide suffer from OA, which is the leading cause of disability among the elderly.[4,5] Therefore, early diagnosis and treatment can slow down the progression of OA.[6]Additionally, finding a safer and more effective drug for OA treatment is also extremely important.

  Recent evidence shows that traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) plays an important role in the treatment of OA. Several Chinese herbs have been found to possess beneficial effects on OA treatment, including chondroprotective, anti-inflammatory, and antioxidant properties.[7] Many studies have shown that various TCM treatments, including oral administration and external use of TCM, can effectively cure OA with only mild side effects.[8,9] In the future, TCM will be widely used in clinical practice for the treatment of OA.

  Astragali Radix (Huangqi), a commonly used botanical medicine, has been widely used to treat various diseases for a long time in China. Huangqi exhibits various biological activities, such as anticancer, antivirus, antiasthma activities, radiation protection, and auxiliary treatment of autoimmune diseases.[10] Some researchers [11] treated chondrocytes in patients and OA mouse models with astragaloside, which is an active ingredient of Huangqi. The results showed that astragaloside could inhibit the production of inflammatory factors induced by interleukin (IL)-1β in a dose-dependent manner in vitro and mitigate cartilage degeneration in the OA mouse model in vivo. Although Huangqi has a therapeutic effect on OA, the exact therapeutic mechanism is not clear. As the therapeutic effect of Huangqi is regulated in many ways, elucidating the mechanism of action of Huangqi through scientific experiments commonly used in western medicine is challenging.

  Network pharmacology has been reported to provide a new approach to clarifying the complex relationship between the active components of Chinese herbs and therapeutic targets of diseases. In this study, we used network pharmacology as a tool to identify the active ingredients, potential targets, and related therapeutic approaches of Huangqi for treating OA. The results of our study may provide guidance for future studies on the application of Huangqi for the treatment of OA. The protocol of our experiment is shown in Figure 1.


## 2. Materials and Methods

### 2.1. Screening of active ingredients and target genes
  The Traditional Chinese Medicine Systems Pharmacology (TCMSP) Database (https://tcmsp-e.com/) was used to identify the ingredients of Huangqi.[12] Pharmacokinetic parameters refer to the absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion of exogenous chemicals and reflect the dynamic changes of drugs in animals or humans. The oral bioavailability (OB) indicates the percentage of oral drugs reaching systemic circulation, which is an important pharmacokinetic parameter in drug screening. Drug likeness (DL) is a qualitative indicator of the “drug-like” degree of a compound and is used to remove chemically inappropriate compounds. The ingredients selected met both OB of ≥30% and DL of ≥0.18 were considered to be active ingredients. The target genes of all active ingredients of Huangqi were obtained from the DrugBank database.


### 2.2. Collection of OA-related targets
  Based on the GeneCards (https://www.genecards.org/)[13] and the Online Mendelian Inheritance in Man (https://omim.org/)[14] databases, we searched the key-word “osteoarthritis” to obtain the OA-related targets. We merged the results of these 2 databases, and all targets of OA were obtained after removing duplicate targets. Furthermore, the intersection targets of Huangqi and OA were identified and visualized with a Venn diagram.


### 2.3. Protein–protein interaction network construction and key gene screening
  The intersection targets of Huangqi and OA were imported into the Search Tool for the Retrieval of Interacting Genes/Proteins online database (http://string-db.org/)[15] to construct the protein–protein interaction (PPI) network, with the species limited to “Homo sapiens” and a confidence score of >0.9. Furthermore, the Cytoscape V3.7.2 plugin Network Analyzer (http://cytoscape.org) was used to analyze the network topology data.[16] The key targets were finally obtained based on degree values.


### 2.4. Construction of ingredient-target network
  To determine possible interaction between active ingredients and targets, the ingredient-target network was constructed according to the degree value of the active ingredients. The color and node shape of the active ingredients in the network varies according to the degree value. Those nodes with a greater number of connected ingredients or targets were represented with a larger size, indicating their significance in the overall network.


### 2.5. Enrichment analysis of Gene Ontology and KEGG
  The intersection targets of Huangqi and OA were submitted to the Metascape online database (https://metascape.org)[17] for Gene Ontology (GO) functional enrichment analysis and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis. In the GO enrichment analysis, the top 10 items of biological process, cellular component, and molecular function were visualized and displayed with a bubble chart. The top 20 KEGG pathways ranked by P value were also visualized and displayed with a bubble chart.


### 2.6. Molecular docking
  The 2D structures of the pharmaceutical bioactive ingredients were obtained from the PubChem (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov)[18] database and then converted into the mol2 format using the OpenBabel software.[19] The protein crystal structures of transcription factor JUN (JUN), tumor necrosis factor (TNF), and protein kinase B (AKT1) were obtained from PDB database (https://www.rcsb.org).[20] After removing the water molecules and ligands using the PyMoL software,[21] the protein receptors were obtained. Then, the files were uploaded to the SwissDock website (http://www.swissdock.ch)[22] for molecular docking.


## 3. Results

### 3.1. Screening of the active ingredients of Huangqi and OA-related targets
  In total, 20 active ingredients were obtained from the TCMSP database, which matched the screening criteria of OB ≥30% and DL ≥0.18 (Table 1). Next, we identified 206 targets corresponding to the active ingredients in Huangqi from the TCMSP database. Furthermore, 3211 and 95 OA-related targets were identified through the GeneCards and Online Mendelian Inheritance in Man databases, respectively. In total, 3239 OA-related targets were obtained after removing duplicates. After matching the targets of Huangqi with those of OA, 136 intersection targets were identified and shown in a Venn diagram (Fig. 2).


### 3.2. Construction of the PPI network and screening of key genes
  We submitted 136 intersection targets to the Search Tool for the Retrieval of Interacting Genes/Proteins online database for PPI network construction. The PPI network consisted of 120 protein nodes and 523 interaction edges after hiding disconnected nodes in the network (Fig. 3A). The target genes were ranked according to degree values. The top 10 genes based on degree values are shown in Figure 3B.


### 3.3. Ingredient-target network construction and analysis
  In the active ingredient-target network, the red node represented the active ingredients of Huangqi, and the green node represented the intersection targets (Fig. 4). Three kinds of active ingredients without corresponding targets were removed, and the disconnected nodes in the PPI network were hidden. Together, 11 active ingredients were found to interact with the intersection targets. The active ingredients with a degree value >10 were quercetin (degree = 71) and kaempferol (degree = 24), indicating their key roles in Huangqi for OA treatment.


### 3.4. Results of the GO functional enrichment analysis and the KEGG signal pathway analysis
  To further elucidate the mechanism of drug treatment systematically, the enrichment analysis was performed with Metascape. The top 10 GO items were selected based on counts of hit genes and P values (Fig. 5A). For biological functions, the targets were mainly enriched in cell proliferation, apoptosis, immune response, and transcriptional regulation.

  Subsequently, we conducted KEGG pathway enrichment of the 136 intersection targets to determine the mechanism by which Huangqi exerts therapeutic effects on OA. In total, 163 pathways were identified based on log P value. The intersection targets were mainly associated with cancer, the AGE-RAGE signaling pathway in diabetic complications, the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, apoptosis, the mitogen-activated protein kinases (MAPK) signaling pathway, the p53 signaling pathway, and the estrogen signaling pathway. The top 20 KEGG pathways with the lowest log P value were visualized with a bubble chart (Fig. 5B). As a result, our data of functional enrichment analysis suggested that Huangqi might affect OA through multiple targets and pathways.


### 3.5. Molecular docking results of the main active ingredients of Huangqi associated with OA
  Generally, when the binding energy is <0, the lower binding energy between the ligand and the receptor indicates a greater possibility of interaction between them. When the binding energy is <–5.0 kcal/mol, it has a good affinity. The results of molecular docking showed that the molecular docking affinity of the main active ingredients of Huangqi to the key targets of OA was <–5.0 kcal/mol, suggesting the active ingredients of Huangqi can bind with high affinity to the key targets of OA (Table 2). ## 4. Discussion
  OA is a chronic, metabolic joint disease, characterized by degenerative changes of the joint, including the loss of articular cartilage, synovial inflammation, and secondary hyperosteogeny.[23] There is still no effective treatment for OA. Current mainstream treatments for OA are partially successful and unsatisfactory for the reason that they only temporarily relieve the symptoms of the disease.[24] Although a few drugs are administered for relieving OA pain, including cyclooxygenase-2 inhibitors, acetaminophen, and nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs. Unfortunately, no medication with established structural-amendment efficacy in OA is currently available. Several studies have shown that these drugs might also cause many side effects, such as skin rashes, gastrointestinal discomfort, bleeding and perforation associated with gastrointestinal ulcers, and acute hepatorenal failure.[25] Therefore, it is important to find innovative and effective ways to treat OA. Accumulating evidence has shown that TCM has beneficial effects on the prevention and treatment of OA, which provide new ideas and methods for the development of Chinese medicines.

  Several studies have found that Huangqi might have anti-inflammatory effects on OA. Despite these findings, the molecular mechanism by which Huangqi prevents OA remains largely unknown. In this study, we used network pharmacology as a tool to determine the mechanism by which Huangqi exerts therapeutic effects on OA. Based on absorption, distribution, metabolism, and elimination system evaluation, 20 active ingredients of Huangqi were identified from the TCMSP database. Further, the ingredient-target network analysis showed that quercetin had the highest number of target connections (degree = 71), followed by kaempferol (degree = 24). Our results were consistent with those of previous studies, which indicated that quercetin and kaempferol might play a leading role in the treatment of OA with Huangqi. Quercetin can attenuates oxidative stress-induced apoptosis via SIRT1/AMPK-mediated inhibition of ER stress in rat chondrocytes and prevent the progression of OA in rats.[26] Quercetin was also reported to alleviate the progression of OA in OA rat model by reducing inflammation, inhibiting chondrocyte apoptosis, and regulating the polarization of synovial macrophages to M2 macrophages.[27] Previous study has shown that kaempferol can protect cartilage-derived ATDC5 cells from inflammatory damage triggered by lipopolysaccharides by downregulating miR-146a, showing strong anti-inflammatory and therapeutic effects.[28] Additionally, kaempferol can significantly reduce the expression of proinflammatory mediators in IL-1β-induced rat chondrocytes by inhibiting nuclear factor-κB (NF-κB) pathway.[29]

  In the PPI network, 10 key targets were identified by performing a topological analysis. The targets included JUN, AKT1, TNF, MAPK1, tumor suppressor p53, transcription factor p65, MAPK14, interleukin (IL)-6, MAPK8, and myc proto-oncogene protein. Tumor necrosis factor alpha (TNF-α) is 1 of the 19 ligands within the tumor necrosis factor superfamily.[30] TNF-α is a key inflammatory cytokine involved in the pathophysiological processes that occur in OA. It increases the production of inducible nitric oxide synthase, cyclooxygenase-2, IL-6, IL-8, monocyte chemoattractant protein 1, regulated upon activation normal T cell expressed and secreted factor, as well as the levels of prostaglandin E2 and nitric oxide in chondrocytes and cartilage explants.[31] TNF-α exacerbates catabolic processes in OA and probably modulates the inflammatory response in joint tissue. Activator protein-1, a heterodimeric transcription factor comprising proteins belonging to the c-Jun and c-Fos families, plays an important roles in many inflammatory processes and autoimmune diseases.[32] The inflammatory process in patients with OA involves activation of the JNK-activator protein-1 signaling pathway. This pathway mediates the activation and transcription of c-Jun, which is required for IL-1 induction of matrix metalloproteinase-13.[33] Therefore, the inhibition of c-Jun is also a potential therapeutic target for the prevention and treatment of OA joints. The NF-κB is an important molecule that controls normal development and the pathological destruction of cartilage.[34] Rela (p65) is a key subunit mediating NF-κB signaling pathway, which participates in chondrogenic differentiation, cell survival, and catabolic enzyme production.[35] Rela is involved in the regulation of chondrocyte differentiation during skeletal development via glycogen synthase kinase-3α and glycogen synthase kinase-3β.[36] Therefore, Rela might be extensively involved in anabolic and catabolic processes of cartilage during skeletal growth, articular cartilage homeostasis, and OA development. All the targets mentioned above may contribute to the development of OA and are potential targets for the treatment of OA with Huangqi.

  &&Through the GO functional enrichment analysis of intersection targets, we found that the potential targets of Huangqi in the treatment of OA were closely associated with the biological processes such as proliferation and repair of bone and chondrocytes, apoptosis, and the development of inflammatory mechanism. Chondrocyte apoptosis may be an important target for regulating cartilage degeneration for the reason that the loss of chondrocyte vitality is a key marker of human OA.[37] Some studies have reported the protective effects of certain drugs on chondrocyte apoptosis. For example, Takahashi et al[38] found that intra-articular injection of hyaluronic acid reduced the severity of OA and the number of apoptotic chondrocytes in a rabbit ACLT model. Inflammation also plays a central role in the pathogenesis of OA. Recent studies on human patients and animal models have shown that the whole synovial joint, including articular cartilage, subchondral bone, synovial tissue, ligament, and meniscus, are involved in the formation of inflammatory network.[39] Therefore, targeting the inflammatory pathway may be a promising strategy for the treatment of OA.

  The results of KEGG pathway analysis revealed that the targets of Huangqi might contribute to the prevention and treatment of OA by influencing the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, apoptosis, the MAPK signaling pathway, and the p53 signaling pathway. Chondrocyte apoptosis plays an important role in cartilage degeneration in OA. Excessive chondrocyte apoptosis and reduced proliferation frequently accompany cartilage degradation.[40] The PI3K/AKT pathway is an important and complex signaling pathway, which is required to maintain homeostasis in many cellular processes, including the cell cycle, cell survival, inflammation, metabolism, and apoptosis.[41,42] Therefore, the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway is a vital regulator of chondrocyte survival and apoptosis.[43] Various growth factors and bioactive compounds, such as IGF-1, PDGF-bb,[44] tormentic acid,[45] can prevent OA by activating the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway to reduce chondrocyte apoptosis. As mentioned earlier, NF-κB is the main regulator of OA-related inflammatory mediators and is mainly activated by IkB kinase-mediated IkBa degradation and RelA phosphorylation.[46] Active PI3K/AKT can also participate in NF-κB p65 phosphorylation and nuclear translocation and increase the production of inflammatory mediators.[43] MAPK signaling is important in eukaryotic cells as it adjusts and controls the structure and function of the cell. When chondrocytes are stimulated, the MAPK signal pathway is the most important signal transduction system to mediate cartilage injury in OA.[47] Various inflammatory factors specifically bind to the receptors on the cell membrane, activate the MAPK signal transduction pathway in cells, and cause different reactions, such as an increase in the expression of matrix metalloproteinases, chondrocyte apoptosis, and cartilage destruction.[48] Inhibiting the p38-MAPK signaling pathway can inhibit the apoptosis and expression of proinflammatory cytokines in human OA chondrocytes.[49] Moreover, nitric oxide increases the expression of p53 by phosphorylating p38 MAPK and p53, further upregulating BAX, which is a proapoptotic gene.[50] Chondrocytes that express p53 show similar morphological characteristics as osteoarthritic cartilage cells and exhibit apoptosis. However, the downregulation of p53 prevents chondrocytes from undergoing apoptosis or senescence.

  The network pharmacology analysis was performed to speculate on the significance of Huangqi. From the results of molecular docking, we found that quercetin and kaempferol had a significant interaction with JUN, TNF, and AKT1. Our results demonstrated that quercetin and kaempferol play an important role in the treatment of OA. However, considering the presence of quercetin and kaempferol in many herbs, these active ingredients are not unique to Huangqi. Thus, other herbs containing these 2 ingredients might have the same therapeutic effect on OA.

  Last but not least, there are still some limitations as this study relies mainly on web-based databases. The screened active ingredients of Huangqi lacked analytical chemistry identification. In addition, further in vivo and in vitro experiments are needed to validate the effect of Huangqi and its active components.


## 5. Conclusion
  To summarize, we used the method of network pharmacology to comprehensively analyze the mechanism by which Huangqi exerts a therapeutic effect on OA. The effect of Huangqi on OA treatment was through multiple pathways, targets, and active ingredients. Among them, the most important active ingredients, targets, and regulatory pathways were quercetin, JUN, and pathways associated with cancer, respectively. Through molecular docking, we found that quercetin and kaempferol had a good affinity for the key targets of OA. Therefore, we hypothesized that Huangqi directly or indirectly regulates the above-mentioned potential targets and pathways to treat OA. Our study provides information for future research on OA. However, the results of this study need to be experimentally verified.
