- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors present a supervised machine learning method, Wimtrap, that integrates multiple genomic features (gene position, sequence conservation, chromatin state, and CRE footprints) to predict transcription factor binding sites and target genes in plants. They report that chromatin features are crucial for accuracy, evaluate model transferability across TFs, organs, and species, and validate the method by inferring target genes of key TFs in Arabidopsis. The tool is provided as an R package with a Shiny interface and pre-built models for four plant species.

- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, all figures and tables, supplementary data, model performance statistics, benchmark comparisons, and software implementation details

However, the abstract provides no quantitative evidence of performance, no comparison to existing methods, and no details on validation rigor. The core claims of improved accuracy and transferability cannot be assessed from the supplied material. The work may be of interest to plant molecular biologists and bioinformaticians, but the case is not established from the abstract alone.

The explicit focus on plant species and organ/condition specificity addresses a gap in existing tools. The provision of a user-friendly R package with pre-built models enhances practical utility and reproducibility.
