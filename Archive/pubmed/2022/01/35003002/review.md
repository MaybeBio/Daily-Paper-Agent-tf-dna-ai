## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text including abstract, introduction, results, discussion, materials and methods, and supplementary figure/table references.
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, experimental design, data interpretation, and claims as presented in the manuscript. No assessment of editorial fit or journal policy.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The manuscript reports biochemical and biophysical characterization of the MocR/GabR-type regulator EnuR from Ruegeria pomeroyi DSS-3, demonstrating that its aminotransferase domain mediates dimerization, that pyridoxal-5′-phosphate is covalently attached to Lys302, that the ectoine and hydroxyectoine hydrolysis products α-ADABA and hydroxy-α-ADABA bind EnuR with low micromolar affinity, and that EnuR binds a 48 bp DNA fragment containing its predicted operator sites with a Kd of approximately 2 μM.
- **Visible evidence base** Results sections on protein purification, size exclusion chromatography, mass spectrometry, microscale thermophoresis binding assays, structural modelling, docking experiments, phylogenomic analysis, and targeted metabolomics. Figures 1–8 and Supplementary Figures 1–3 and Supplementary Tables 1–6 are referenced.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Raw mass spectrometry spectra, full MST binding curves and fitting statistics, detailed docking scoring data, coordinates for the EnuR model, and uncropped gel images were not provided. The manuscript states that supplementary material is available online, but it was not included in the supplied text.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript presents a solid biochemical characterization of the EnuR regulator, with particular strength in the demonstration of covalent PLP attachment by mass spectrometry and the quantitative binding data for the two inducers. The work extends prior studies on this regulator family and provides useful insight into the inducer specificity of EnuR. However, several claims regarding the regulatory model and the structural interpretation of docking results are not fully supported by the data presented. The DNA binding affinity measurement, while novel, is based on a single fragment and lacks mutational validation of the predicted operator sites. The metabolomics data are semi-quantitative and the interpretation of γ-ADABA detection requires more careful consideration. Overall, the work is technically sound in its core binding assays but would benefit from additional validation of the structural model and a more cautious interpretation of the in vivo relevance of the measured affinities.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying bacterial transcriptional regulation, particularly the MocR/GabR subfamily of GntR-type regulators, will find the biochemical characterization of EnuR valuable. The work also has relevance for those interested in the catabolism of compatible solutes in marine bacteria and the ecological role of ectoines as nutrient sources. The demonstration that hydroxy-α-ADABA serves as a high-affinity inducer for EnuR provides a clear link between the initial steps of hydroxyectoine catabolism and transcriptional regulation, which will be of interest to microbiologists studying osmoprotectant metabolism.
- **Major strengths** The mass spectrometry data unambiguously demonstrate covalent PLP attachment to Lys302, resolving a question that had previously only been addressed indirectly. The MST binding assays provide quantitative affinity data for both α-ADABA and hydroxy-α-ADABA, showing a clear preference for the latter. The phylogenomic analysis is comprehensive and places the EnuR regulator in a broad ecological context. The DNA binding data, while limited, represent the first quantitative measurement of EnuR-operator interaction for this system.
- **Major Concerns** 
  - R1-M1: The structural model and docking results are presented as providing "a view into the cofactor and inducer binding site" but the validation of the model is limited to an RMSD value against the GabR template. No cross-validation, molecular dynamics, or experimental mutagenesis is provided to support the predicted binding poses. The claim that the docking explains the affinity difference between α-ADABA and hydroxy-α-ADABA is speculative given the lack of quantitative scoring analysis.
  - R1-M2: The DNA binding data are based on a single 48 bp fragment containing two predicted operators. The manuscript does not demonstrate that both sites are required for binding, nor does it test whether mutations in the predicted operator sequences abolish binding. The Kd of 1.9 μM is substantially higher than that reported for the related S. meliloti system, and the authors do not adequately discuss this discrepancy.
  - R1-M3: The metabolomics data are presented as evidence for the presence of inducers in cells, but the quantification is semi-quantitative and the authors do not account for potential losses during extraction or matrix effects. The detection of γ-ADABA in cells grown on ectoine is surprising given that R. pomeroyi cannot synthesize ectoines, and the authors do not provide a satisfactory explanation for this observation.
  - R1-M4: The regulatory model presented in the Discussion, while plausible, is not directly tested in this manuscript. The claim that the measured affinities support a specific model of inducer-mediated derepression requires experimental validation through in vivo reporter assays or transcription analysis.
- **Minor Comments** 
  - R1-m1: The manuscript uses both "Ectoine Nutrient Utilization Regulator" and "EnuR" throughout; the full name should be defined at first use and then the abbreviation used consistently.
  - R1-m2: The size exclusion chromatography data are presented as elution volumes but the calibration of the column with molecular weight standards is not described in the methods.
  - R1-m3: The authors state that the ATD can fold independently from the DNA-binding domain, but no biophysical evidence (e.g., circular dichroism, thermal stability) is provided to support this claim beyond the observation that the purified ATD is soluble and dimeric.
  - R1-m4: The docking figure (Figure 6) would benefit from a clearer representation of the protein surface and the position of the PLP cofactor relative to the bound ligands.
  - R1-m5: The manuscript would be strengthened by a direct comparison of the EnuR binding affinities with those reported for other MocR/GabR regulators to place the measured values in context.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** The structural interpretation relies on an unvalidated homology model; the DNA binding data lack mutational controls; the metabolomics quantification is not rigorously controlled; and the regulatory model is not tested experimentally.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - Originality: Moderate. The work provides new biochemical data on a specific regulator but does not introduce a conceptually novel mechanism or paradigm. The demonstration of hydroxy-α-ADABA as a high-affinity inducer is a useful advance but is incremental.
  - Scientific importance: Moderate. The work contributes to understanding of ectoine catabolism regulation, which is relevant for marine microbiology and compatible solute ecology. However, the broader significance beyond this specific system is limited.
  - Interdisciplinary readership: Limited. The manuscript will primarily appeal to specialists in bacterial transcriptional regulation and osmoprotectant metabolism. The methods and findings are unlikely to attract readers outside these fields.
  - Technical soundness: Generally sound for the core binding assays, but the structural modelling and docking components lack adequate validation. The metabolomics data are presented with insufficient methodological detail for full assessment.
  - Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is clearly written and the figures are generally well organized. However, the introduction assumes familiarity with GntR-type regulator biology and the discussion is dense with system-specific detail.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The core biochemical findings are likely valid and of interest to the specialist community, but the manuscript would be substantially strengthened by addressing the concerns regarding model validation, DNA binding specificity, and the regulatory model.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that the docking results explain the observed affinity difference between α-ADABA and hydroxy-α-ADABA is not supported by quantitative analysis.
- The claim that the 48 bp fragment contains two functional EnuR operator sites is not experimentally validated.
- The detection of γ-ADABA in cells grown on ectoine is reported without a mechanistic explanation and may represent an artifact or an uncharacterized metabolic pathway.
- The regulatory model presented in the Discussion is plausible but is not directly supported by experimental data in this manuscript.
- The statement that the ATD "can fold independently" is inferred from the observation of a soluble dimeric domain but is not directly demonstrated.
