## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text including abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, data availability, code availability, and supplementary figure/table references.
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific validity, methodological soundness, interpretability claims, biological insights, and reproducibility of the described deep learning framework (CITRUS) for modeling somatic alteration effects on transcriptional programs.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors present CITRUS, a partially interpretable neural network with encoder-decoder architecture and self-attention, to link somatic alterations (mutations, CNVs) to transcriptional programs via inferred transcription factor (TF) activities, using TF-target gene priors derived from pan-cancer ATAC-seq data across 17 TCGA cancer types. They claim improved prediction over affinity regression, identification of cancer subtype-specific TF programs, and causal insights via in silico knockout analysis.
- **Visible evidence base** Methods section (data preprocessing, model architecture, training, AR baseline, in silico knockout), Results section (performance comparisons, attention weight analyses, TF-tumor associations, subtype identification, in silico knockout findings), Figures 1-5 referenced, Supplementary Tables 1-5 and Figures 1-8 referenced.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Actual figure images, supplementary data files (Supplementary Tables 1-5, Supplementary Figures 1-8, Supplementary Data 1), detailed hyperparameter values, exact model architecture dimensions, and code implementation details are not provided in the submitted text. These are essential for full reproducibility and for verifying several statistical claims.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript presents a novel and potentially valuable deep learning framework for integrating somatic alterations, chromatin accessibility, and gene expression to infer TF activities in a cancer context. The core idea of using a self-attention mechanism to model contextual interactions among alterations, combined with a biologically constrained decoder, is timely and relevant. However, the current evidence base is insufficient to fully validate the key claims of interpretability, biological insight, and superiority over existing methods. Several methodological details and statistical validations are either missing or only partially described, limiting the strength of the conclusions. The work is promising but requires substantial additional analysis and clarification to establish its case.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Computational biologists, cancer genomics researchers, and bioinformaticians developing interpretable machine learning models for precision oncology. The framework's ability to generate testable hypotheses about TF dysregulation in individual tumors would be of interest to experimentalists studying transcriptional regulation in cancer. The methodological approach may also appeal to researchers working on integrating multi-omics data with deep learning.
- **Major strengths** 1) The conceptual framing of linking somatic alterations to transcriptional programs through an interpretable intermediate TF layer is clear and biologically motivated. 2) The use of ATAC-seq-derived TF-target gene priors to constrain the model is a thoughtful way to incorporate chromatin context. 3) The in silico knockout approach for causal inference is a useful and practical application of the model. 4) The pan-cancer scope across 17 TCGA types provides a broad and relevant testbed.
- **Major Concerns**
    - **R1-M1** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes, Axis: Technical soundness) The description of the model architecture and training procedure is incomplete, preventing full assessment of its validity and reproducibility. Specific dimensions of the encoder, decoder, and attention layers are not given. The exact formulation of the loss function, regularization terms, and optimization details (beyond optimizer and learning rate) are missing. The claim of "partial interpretability" hinges on the specific design of the TF layer, but the mathematical relationship between the tumor embedding, the TF layer, and the final prediction is not fully specified.
    - **R1-M2** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes, Axis: Technical soundness / Statistical rigor) The statistical evaluation of the in silico knockout analysis is inadequately described. The text mentions t-tests and multiple hypothesis correction "across models," but the exact number of tests, the method of correction (Bonferroni vs. FDR), and the threshold for significance are not stated. More critically, the validation of the knockout predictions against independent experimental data (e.g., known TF perturbation effects) is not presented, which is essential to support the claim of causal inference.
    - **R1-M3** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes, Axis: Scientific importance / Originality) The claim of superiority over existing methods is not convincingly established. The comparison is only against affinity regression (AR). The authors do not compare CITRUS to other relevant methods for inferring TF activity from gene expression and regulatory data, such as VIPER, MARA, or other network-based approaches. Without such comparisons, the unique contribution and advantage of CITRUS are not clear.
    - **R1-M4** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No, Axis: Technical soundness) The biological validation of the TF-target gene matrix is weak. The matrix is derived from ATAC-seq peaks and motif scanning, but the text does not describe any quality control steps, such as comparison to experimentally validated TF binding sites (e.g., ChIP-seq) or assessment of the matrix's coverage and specificity. The potential for false positive or false negative connections in this prior matrix could significantly impact the inferred TF activities.
- **Minor Comments**
    - **R1-m1** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Readability for nonspecialists) The abstract and introduction are accessible, but the methods section becomes highly technical without sufficient context for a broader readership. A high-level schematic of the model architecture in the main text (beyond Figure 1) would be helpful.
    - **R1-m2** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Technical soundness) The choice of the top 2500 most variable genes per cancer type and the union across types is not fully justified. The sensitivity of the results to this gene selection step is not explored.
    - **R1-m3** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Technical soundness) The text states that "tanh" was chosen over "ReLU" because it "generates more biologically meaningful results," but this is subjective. A quantitative comparison of the two activation functions in terms of prediction performance and downstream biological validation is needed.
    - **R1-m4** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Scientific importance) The discussion of clinical implications is somewhat speculative. While the potential for precision oncology is mentioned, the manuscript would be strengthened by a more concrete discussion of how the model's outputs could be used in a clinical decision-making workflow.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1, R1-M2, R1-M3, R1-M4.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
    - **Originality:** The combination of self-attention for somatic alterations with a biologically constrained decoder for TF activity is a novel architectural choice. However, the individual components are not new, and the originality is in their integration. This is a moderate strength.
    - **Scientific importance:** The problem of linking genomic alterations to transcriptional consequences is of high importance in cancer biology. A robust and interpretable tool for this purpose would be valuable. However, the current evidence does not fully demonstrate that CITRUS provides a significant advance over existing approaches.
    - **Interdisciplinary readership:** The work is primarily of interest to computational and cancer biologists. The methods are too specialized for a general audience, and the biological insights, while potentially interesting, are not yet presented in a way that would appeal to a broad readership.
    - **Technical soundness:** This is the major weakness. The incomplete methodological details and insufficient statistical validation prevent a full assessment of the model's soundness. The lack of comparison to other methods further undermines the technical case.
    - **Readability for nonspecialists:** The abstract and introduction are clear, but the methods and results sections are dense and assume significant prior knowledge of deep learning and genomics. The manuscript is not easily accessible to a nonspecialist.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The core idea is promising, but the manuscript requires major revisions to provide complete methodological details, robust statistical validation, and a more comprehensive comparison to existing methods. I would be supportive if these technical concerns are resolved.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that CITRUS "yields biological insights into delineating TFs associated with somatic alterations in individual tumors" is not fully supported without validation of the in silico knockout predictions against independent experimental data.
- The claim of "significantly better performance" than AR is based on a single baseline and lacks comparison to other state-of-the-art methods.
- The claim that the self-attention mechanism captures "complex contextual interactions" is not directly validated; the analysis of attention weights is correlative and does not demonstrate causal or mechanistic understanding.
- The biological significance of the identified TF-tumor type associations (e.g., FUBP1, IRF3) is presented as consistent with literature, but the statistical strength and specificity of these associations are not fully quantified.
- The generalizability of the model to cancer types without ATAC-seq data is not discussed, limiting its practical utility.
