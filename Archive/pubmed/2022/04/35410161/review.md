## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a conceptually interesting and potentially useful approach for dissecting the binding preferences of individual DNA binding domains within multi-domain transcription factors. The application to CTCF is well-motivated, and the recovery of known motifs for ZFs 3–11 provides a strong internal validation. However, the evidence for the novel ZF 1 motif is largely indirect and correlative, and several methodological details and potential confounders are not fully addressed. The manuscript would benefit from clearer presentation of the model interpretation results and additional validation to strengthen the causal link between ZF 1 and the proposed motif.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying transcription factor biology, particularly those interested in multi-zinc finger proteins, CTCF function, and the development of computational methods for interpreting neural network models of genomic data. The approach could be of broad interest to computational biologists developing methods for motif discovery from differential binding data.
- **Major strengths**
  - The core idea of using differential binding between wild-type and mutant TFs to isolate the contribution of a specific DBD is elegant and well-motivated.
  - The successful recovery of known motifs for ZFs 3–7 and 8–11 serves as a strong positive control, demonstrating the potential of the approach.
  - The use of multiple independent lines of evidence (HT-SELEX, in silico predictions, peak strength analysis) to support the novel ZF 1 motif is commendable.
  - The manuscript is generally well-written and the methods are described in sufficient detail to be reproducible, assuming the supplementary materials are complete.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **R1-M1** (Blocking: Yes): The causal link between ZF 1 and the proposed GAGCCA motif is not established. The authors acknowledge that the motif could be explained by indirect stabilization or by ZF 3 binding. The evidence from CTCF-s comparison is indirect, and the in vitro data (HT-SELEX, EMSA) do not isolate ZF 1. The claim that this is a "putative binding preference" is appropriately cautious, but the title and abstract may overstate the finding.
  - **R1-M2** (Blocking: No): The interpretation of the neural network models is not fully described. The authors state they used DeepLIFT and TF-MoDISco, but the specific steps for converting importance scores to motifs, the selection of motifs for downstream analysis, and the handling of the "mega-motif" construction are not detailed in the main text. This limits the reader's ability to assess the robustness of the motif discovery.
  - **R1-M3** (Blocking: No): The potential for confounding by indirect effects is not fully explored. The negative set includes peaks that are stronger in the mutant, which could include peaks where the mutation leads to a gain of function or where the mutant protein binds with altered specificity. The authors do not discuss how this might affect the interpretation of the learned motifs.
  - **R1-M4** (Blocking: No): The statistical comparison of peak strengths between motif groups is presented, but the effect sizes and the biological significance of the observed differences are not discussed. The p-values are very small, but it is unclear if the differences in peak strength are meaningful in a biological context.
- **Minor Comments**
  - **R1-m1** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Clarity): The abstract states "we identified the known binding preferences of CTCF ZFs 3–11 as well as a putative GAG binding motif for ZF 1." The term "GAG binding motif" is slightly imprecise, as the full motif is GAGCCA. Please clarify.
  - **R1-m2** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Reproducibility): The manuscript mentions a "Supporting Website" but does not provide a URL. This should be included for reproducibility.
  - **R1-m3** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Clarity): In the Results section, the authors state "for every ZF including ZF 1, we found over ten thousand peaks that are significantly stronger in the wild-type than they are in the mutant." It would be helpful to state the total number of peaks considered and the proportion that were differential.
  - **R1-m4** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Interpretation): The discussion of the previous study by [52] is useful, but the authors could more explicitly state how their approach differs and why it might be more sensitive.
  - **R1-m5** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Technical detail): The description of the neural network architecture is brief. Please provide the number of parameters, the exact layer dimensions, and the training time.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established**
  - The lack of a clear demonstration that the model's learned importance scores are specifically attributable to the mutated ZF, rather than to correlated changes in the binding of other ZFs. A control experiment, such as training a model on a mutant with a known minimal effect (e.g., R339W) and showing that no novel motifs are learned, would strengthen the case.
  - The absence of a direct experimental validation of the ZF 1-GAGCCA interaction, such as an electrophoretic mobility shift assay (EMSA) with a ZF 1-only construct or a targeted mutation of the GAGCCA motif in a reporter assay.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**
  - **Originality**: High. The approach of using differential binding to train a neural network for DBD-specific motif discovery is novel and a clear advance over previous methods that rely on de novo motif discovery in peak sets.
  - **Scientific importance**: Moderate to high. The findings have implications for understanding CTCF function and the general problem of DBD-specific binding. However, the importance is somewhat tempered by the lack of definitive proof for the ZF 1 interaction.
  - **Interdisciplinary readership**: Moderate. The work will be of interest to computational biologists and molecular biologists studying transcription. The methods section is technical but accessible.
  - **Technical soundness**: The core computational approach appears sound, but the lack of detail on model interpretation and the potential for confounding effects are concerns.
  - **Readability for nonspecialists**: The abstract and introduction are clear. The results and methods sections are dense but understandable for a technical audience.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The approach is promising and the validation of known motifs is compelling. However, the evidence for the novel ZF 1 motif needs to be strengthened, and the methodological details need to be clarified before the conclusions can be fully accepted.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that the GAGCCA motif is a "putative binding preference" for ZF 1 is not fully supported by the provided evidence. The data are consistent with this interpretation, but alternative explanations (e.g., indirect effects, ZF 3 binding) are not ruled out.
- The claim that the approach "can be applied to any TF for which in vivo binding data from both the wild-type and mutated versions of the TF are available" is a reasonable extrapolation but is not demonstrated here.
- The statement that the model "should have learned sequence patterns related to the binding preferences of the mutated DBD" is an assumption that is not directly tested. The model could learn patterns related to other differences between the wild-type and mutant datasets (e.g., changes in chromatin accessibility).
