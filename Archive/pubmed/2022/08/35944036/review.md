- **Visible evidence base** Abstract, Introduction, Materials and methods, Results and discussion, Conclusion, figure and table references (figures and tables not provided)

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript presents a standard in silico pipeline for predicting deleterious nsSNPs in FOXP2, combining multiple established prediction tools with molecular docking. The work is methodologically conventional and the findings are largely confirmatory of previously reported disease associations. The core claim that R553C and L558P destabilize protein-DNA interactions is plausible but insufficiently supported by the evidence presented, as docking scores, interaction distances, and structural validation metrics are referenced but not shown. The manuscript would benefit from clearer presentation of quantitative results and a more critical discussion of the limitations of computational prediction.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying FOXP2-related speech and language disorders, computational biologists working on SNP prioritization pipelines, and clinicians interested in genetic variants associated with neurodevelopmental conditions. The results may also be of interest to those studying transcription factor DNA-binding specificity.
- **Major strengths** The study employs a comprehensive set of well-established computational tools for SNP effect prediction, which strengthens the confidence in the prioritization of deleterious variants. The inclusion of molecular docking to assess functional impact on DNA binding is a valuable addition beyond simple pathogenicity prediction. The identification of five high-confidence deleterious variants in the forkhead domain provides a focused set for future experimental validation.
- **Major Concerns** The manuscript lacks quantitative presentation of docking results, including binding scores, energy values, and interaction distances, which are essential to support the claim that specific mutations destabilize protein-DNA interactions. The structural models generated by RaptorX are not validated against known experimental structures of FOXP2, and the quality metrics are only summarized without showing the actual data. The selection of the consensus DNA sequence CAAATT as the sole docking target is not justified, and the biological relevance of this sequence for FOXP2 binding is not discussed. The manuscript does not address the potential limitations of the computational tools used, such as their training data biases and false positive rates.
- **Minor Comments** The abstract mentions five variants in the results section but the conclusion only highlights two, creating a slight inconsistency in emphasis. The introduction could benefit from a more focused discussion of previous computational studies on FOXP2 to better contextualize the novelty of this work. The methods section would be improved by specifying the exact versions and default parameters of the tools used. The statement that "further experimental investigations may need to verify" the findings is appropriate but could be strengthened by suggesting specific experimental approaches.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** The absence of docking scores and interaction distance data (referenced as Fig 3 and S3 Table but not provided) prevents independent assessment of the claim that R553C and L558P alter protein-DNA binding. The structural model quality metrics are only described qualitatively, and the actual Ramachandran plot statistics and ProSA Z-scores are not shown. The manuscript does not provide the multiple sequence alignment or conservation scores that would support the ConSurf analysis.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: The work is not conceptually novel, as similar in silico SNP prioritization studies have been published for many proteins, including FOXP2. Scientific importance: The findings are of moderate importance to the FOXP2 research community but do not fundamentally advance understanding of the protein's function or disease mechanisms. Interdisciplinary readership: The manuscript is likely to appeal primarily to computational biologists and geneticists, with limited broader appeal. Technical soundness: The methodology is generally sound but the presentation of results is incomplete, particularly regarding docking analysis. Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is written in a clear and accessible style, though some sections assume familiarity with bioinformatics tools.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved, specifically the provision of quantitative docking data and structural validation metrics. Currently not established from the provided evidence due to missing key results.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence completeness
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that R553C and L558P mutant variants destabilize protein structure by changing protein-DNA interface interactions and disruption of hydrogen bonds that may reduce the specificity and affinity of the binding
- **Evidence pointer** Results and discussion, Docking analysis section, Fig 3, S3 Table (not provided)
- **Concern** The central claim of the manuscript regarding the mechanistic impact of the identified mutations on DNA binding is based on molecular docking results that are not quantitatively presented. The manuscript references Fig 3 and S3 Table for interaction details, but these are not included in the provided material. Without docking scores, binding free energies, or interaction distance measurements, the claim that specific mutations alter binding affinity cannot be independently evaluated.
- **Why it matters** The conclusion that R553C and L558P are the most damaging variants rests on the docking analysis. If the docking results do not show significant differences between wild-type and mutant binding, the primary conclusion of the study is weakened.
- **Resolution test** Provide the docking scores (e.g., HDock confidence scores), binding free energy estimates, and a table of interaction distances for both wild-type and mutant complexes. Show that the differences between wild-type and mutant binding are statistically or energetically significant.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Technical validation
- **Claim pointer** The authors state that the structural models of the wild-type and variant FOXP2 DNA binding domain were validated using ProSA-web and Ramachandran plot analysis, with over 94% of residues in favored regions
- **Evidence pointer** Results and discussion, Structural validation section, Table 6 (not provided)
- **Concern** The structural models generated by RaptorX are not compared to the known experimental structure of the FOXP2 DNA binding domain. The quality metrics are only described in text, and the actual values are not shown. Without this information, the reliability of the models used for docking cannot be assessed.
- **Why it matters** Docking results are only as reliable as the input structures. If the models contain significant errors, the predicted interactions may be artifacts rather than biologically meaningful.
- **Resolution test** Provide the ProSA Z-scores and Ramachandran plot statistics for all models, and compare the RaptorX models to the experimental FOXP2 structure (e.g., PDB ID) using root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) calculations.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** No
- **Axis** Methodological justification
- **Claim pointer** The authors performed molecular docking of FOXP2 DNA binding domain with its most common target sequence 5'-CAAATT-3'
- **Evidence pointer** Materials and methods, Molecular docking section
- **Concern** The choice of the CAAATT sequence as the docking target is not justified. FOXP2 is known to bind a range of DNA sequences, and the selection of a single consensus motif may not capture the full range of binding interactions affected by the mutations.
- **Why it matters** If the selected DNA sequence is not representative of FOXP2's natural binding sites, the docking results may not reflect the true impact of the mutations on DNA binding in vivo.
- **Resolution test** Justify the choice of CAAATT based on published FOXP2 binding site preferences, or perform docking with multiple known FOXP2 target sequences to demonstrate that the observed effects are consistent.
