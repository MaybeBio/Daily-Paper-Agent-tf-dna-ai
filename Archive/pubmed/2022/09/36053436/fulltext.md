Glioblastoma multiforme (GBM) is the most malignant intracranial tumor with high mortality rates and invariably poor prognosis due to its limited clinical treatments. There is an urgent need to develop new therapeutic drugs for GBM treatment. As a natural prenylated chalcone compound, Isobavachalcone (IBC)'s favorable pharmacological activities have been widely revealed. However, potential inhibitory effects of IBC on GBM have not been explored. In the present study, we aimed to detect the effects of IBC on GBM and clarify its anti-GBM mechanisms for the first time. It was observed that IBC could inhibit GBM cell proliferation, migration, and invasion in vitro and prevent tumor growth without any significant drug toxicity in both subcutaneous and orthotopic GBM xenograft tumor models in vivo. Mechanistically, IBC may target NOD-like receptor family pyrin domain-containing 3 (NLRP3) transcription factor estrogen receptor alpha (ESR1 gene) by network pharmacology and molecular docking analysis. Experimentally, IBC alleviated NLRP3 inflammasome-related pyroptosis and inflammation, arrested cell cycle at G1 phase, and induced mitochondria-dependent apoptosis in GBM cells. IBC's inhibition on NLRP3 could be rescued by the NLRP3 antagonist CY-09 both in vitro and in vivo. These results indicate that IBC is a potential therapeutic drug against GBM and provide a new insight into GBM treatment.
