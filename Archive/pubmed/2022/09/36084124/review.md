## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, results, discussion, methods, supporting information references)
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific validity, methodological soundness, claims versus evidence, and alignment with Nature-style criteria based solely on the provided manuscript content
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors claim that modern MD force fields with residue-specific backbone potentials (ff99SBnmr2) combined with replica exchange and microsecond-scale MD simulations can produce highly realistic IDP ensembles for p53TAD and Pup, closely reproducing experimental Rg distributions and NMR 15N R1/R2 relaxation rates without reweighting or post hoc corrections, and that graph-theoretic analysis of these trajectories reveals dominant inter-residue contact clusters and residue-specific contact propensities relevant to IDP function.
- **Visible evidence base** Figures 1–6 (described in text), S1–S5 Figs, S1–S4 Tables, equations 1–12, experimental references [61,63,67,73]
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Actual figure images, numerical data tables, simulation parameter details (S1 Table contents), experimental relaxation data files, trajectory convergence metrics, force field parameter files, and code availability statements were not provided in the supplied material

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a technically competent application of state-of-the-art MD simulation methodology to two IDP systems, with validation against multiple experimental observables. The central claim that the ff99SBnmr2 force field with REMD-initialized long MD trajectories can reproduce both global ensemble properties and site-resolved NMR relaxation data without reweighting is potentially significant for the IDP simulation community. However, the evidence as presented has several gaps that prevent full assessment of the claims, particularly regarding trajectory convergence, statistical robustness of the relaxation calculations, and the biological interpretability of the graph-theoretic analysis. The work is likely of interest to specialists in IDP biophysics and computational chemistry, but the broader significance and novelty relative to prior force field validation studies need to be more clearly articulated.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Computational biophysicists and chemists developing and validating MD force fields for disordered proteins; NMR spectroscopists studying IDP dynamics; researchers investigating p53 regulation and Pup-mediated proteasomal degradation; and those studying the molecular determinants of liquid-liquid phase separation, given the contact propensity analysis. The demonstration that a residue-specific force field can reproduce relaxation data without corrections is directly relevant to practitioners seeking reliable simulation protocols for IDPs.
- **Major strengths** 1) The use of multiple complementary validation targets (Rg, shape parameters, R1, R2) provides a reasonably comprehensive test of the simulation protocol. 2) The comparison against polymer theory for Rg scaling adds a useful theoretical anchor. 3) The graph-theoretic approach to analyzing transient contact networks is a creative analytical contribution. 4) The authors are transparent about outlier trajectories and their potential impact on the ensemble averages. 5) The discussion of force field development context is well-grounded in the literature.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness / Statistical robustness
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that the computed R1 and R2 relaxation rates are in "close agreement" and "good agreement" with experiment, with correlation coefficients of 0.78–0.88 for R1 and 0.70–0.88 for R2.
  - **Evidence pointer** Figure 3, Results section "Validation against R1, R2 relaxation data", equations 7–12
  - **Concern** The manuscript reports correlation coefficients and RMSEs but does not provide confidence intervals, per-residue error analysis, or a statistical test comparing simulated and experimental relaxation rates. The claim of "close agreement" is based on aggregate metrics that may mask systematic deviations. The authors note that R1 is underestimated by 4–6% and R2 overestimated by 26–34%, which is a substantial systematic bias for R2. The manuscript does not discuss whether this level of systematic error is acceptable for the stated goal of "quantitative prediction" of ensemble dynamics. Additionally, the calculation of relaxation rates from MD trajectories is known to be sensitive to the length of the correlation function analysis window and the fitting procedure; the manuscript does not provide a convergence analysis of the correlation functions or the fitted parameters.
  - **Why it matters** The central claim of the paper is that the simulation protocol can quantitatively predict NMR relaxation data without corrections. If the systematic overestimation of R2 by 26–34% is not adequately explained or contextualized, the claim of "quantitative prediction" is weakened. Without statistical measures of uncertainty, readers cannot assess whether the agreement is meaningful or within expected error ranges for such calculations.
  - **Resolution test** Provide per-residue plots of experimental versus calculated R1 and R2 with error bars, a discussion of the expected accuracy of relaxation rate calculations from MD (e.g., sensitivity to force field, water model, trajectory length), and a statistical analysis (e.g., reduced chi-square, RMSD normalized by experimental uncertainty) that quantifies the goodness of fit. If the systematic bias is inherent to the force field, this should be explicitly acknowledged and discussed in the context of the claim of "quantitative prediction."
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness / Convergence and sampling
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that the 10 1-μs MD trajectories provide "statistically well-converged results" and that the ensemble properties are representative of the IDP conformational ensembles.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "Ensemble properties of radius of gyration tensor", Figure 1, Methods section "Molecular dynamics simulations"
  - **Concern** The manuscript does not provide any convergence analysis for the MD trajectories. For IDPs, which sample highly heterogeneous conformational ensembles, 1 μs per trajectory may not be sufficient to achieve convergence, especially for global properties like Rg distributions. The authors show Rg as a function of time for representative trajectories (Fig 1A, 1B) but do not show block-averaging analysis, autocorrelation times of Rg, or comparison of ensemble properties between the first and second halves of the trajectories. The selection of 10 starting structures from the REMD ensemble is described, but the statistical independence of these trajectories is not demonstrated. The outlier trajectories (#4 for p53TAD, #5 and #7 for Pup) are identified but their removal from the analysis is described as "diagnostic" without a clear protocol for how such outliers should be handled in a principled manner.
  - **Why it matters** The validity of all subsequent analyses (relaxation rates, contact propensities, graph-theoretic clusters) depends on the assumption that the MD trajectories have adequately sampled the relevant conformational space. If the trajectories are not converged, the reported ensemble properties and derived quantities may be biased and not representative of the true IDP ensembles. This is a fundamental issue that affects the interpretation of all results.
  - **Resolution test** Provide a convergence analysis, including block-averaging of Rg, autocorrelation functions of Rg and other key observables, and a comparison of ensemble properties computed from different trajectory segments. Discuss the statistical independence of the 10 trajectories and provide a rationale for the number of trajectories used. If convergence is not fully achieved, acknowledge this limitation and discuss its potential impact on the conclusions.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Scientific importance / Novelty
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that their approach "produces native conformational ensembles" and that the findings "indicate that modern MD force fields with residue-specific backbone potentials can produce highly realistic IDP ensembles."
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract, Discussion
  - **Concern** The novelty of the claim needs to be better contextualized. The authors cite prior work on force field development and validation, but the specific advance over previous studies is not clearly articulated. The use of ff99SBnmr2 was previously reported by the same group, and the validation against R1/R2 relaxation data for IDPs has been attempted with other force fields. The manuscript does not provide a direct comparison with results from other force fields or simulation protocols under identical conditions, which would strengthen the claim that the current approach is superior or uniquely capable. The statement that the protocol produces "native conformational ensembles" is strong and requires more evidence that the ensembles are not just in agreement with a limited set of observables but are truly representative of the native states.
  - **Why it matters** For a Nature-style audience, the significance of the work depends on whether it represents a clear advance over the state of the art. If the improvement over existing methods is incremental or not clearly demonstrated, the broader impact of the work is diminished.
  - **Resolution test** Add a direct comparison with simulations using other force fields (e.g., ff99SB, ff03ws, CHARMM36m) for the same proteins and experimental data. Quantify the improvement in agreement with experiment. Discuss the specific features of ff99SBnmr2 that lead to improved performance and why these features are transferable to other IDPs.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M4
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Technical soundness / Contact analysis methodology
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that graph-theoretic analysis reveals "dominant inter-residue contact clusters and characteristic amino-acid contact propensities" that provide insights into IDP function.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "Inter-residue contact propensities" and "Contact analysis by graph theory", Figures 5 and 6
  - **Concern** The contact analysis uses a single distance threshold (4 Å) for defining contacts, and the sensitivity of the results to this threshold is not discussed. The graph-theoretic clustering approach is described, but the statistical significance of the identified clusters is not assessed. For example, how do the observed cluster size distributions compare to those expected from a random coil model with the same amino acid composition? The authors note that the cluster size distributions are "strikingly similar" between the two proteins, but this similarity is not quantified or compared to a null model. The biological interpretation of the clusters (e.g., the p53TAD hydrophobic cluster A1) is plausible but speculative, and the connection to function is not directly tested.
  - **Why it matters** The contact propensity and cluster analyses are presented as a key outcome of the work, providing "new insights into their biological function." If the methodology is not robust to parameter choices or if the identified patterns are not statistically significant, the biological conclusions are not well supported.
  - **Resolution test** Perform a sensitivity analysis of the contact definition (e.g., varying the distance threshold from 3.5 to 5 Å) and show that the main conclusions are robust. Compare the observed cluster size distributions and contact propensities to those from a suitable null model (e.g., a Gaussian chain or a simulation with a non-interacting model). Provide statistical measures of significance for the identified clusters.

  - **Affected element** Methods section

  - **Issue** The Methods section lacks specific details needed for reproduction: the exact number of atoms in each simulation system, the salt concentration and ionic species used, the thermostat and barostat algorithms and coupling constants, the equilibration protocol, and the criteria for selecting the 10 starting structures from the REMD ensemble.   - **Evidence pointer** Figure 3
  - **Issue** The manuscript reports correlation coefficients and RMSEs for R1 and R2 but does not show the data in a way that allows per-residue comparison. The text mentions "close agreement" and "good agreement" but the reader cannot visually assess where the discrepancies are largest.

- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 (statistical robustness of relaxation validation) and R1-M2 (convergence and sampling) are the primary technical issues that must be addressed. Without these, the central claim of quantitative prediction without corrections is not fully established. R1-M3 (novelty) and R1-M4 (contact analysis robustness) are also important but may be addressable with additional analysis and contextualization.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - **Originality** Moderate. The application of a residue-specific force field to IDPs and validation against relaxation data is a useful contribution, but the approach builds directly on prior work by the same group and others. The graph-theoretic analysis is a novel analytical angle, but its added value over standard contact map analysis is not fully demonstrated.
  - **Scientific importance** Moderate to high for the IDP simulation community. The demonstration that a force field can reproduce multiple experimental observables without corrections is practically important. However, the broader biological significance is less clearly established, as the functional implications of the contact propensities remain speculative.
  - **Interdisciplinary readership** Limited. The work is primarily of interest to computational biophysicists and NMR spectroscopists. The biological implications for p53 regulation and LLPS are mentioned but not developed sufficiently to attract a broader cell biology or biochemistry audience.
  - **Technical soundness** The methodology is generally sound, but the lack of convergence analysis and statistical rigor in the validation against relaxation data are significant gaps. The contact analysis methodology needs sensitivity testing.
  - **Readability for nonspecialists** The manuscript is written for a specialist audience and would benefit from additional context and definitions for key concepts.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The manuscript addresses an important problem in IDP biophysics and presents a potentially valuable simulation protocol. However, the central claims of quantitative prediction and realistic ensemble generation require stronger evidence of convergence and statistical robustness. The novelty relative to prior work needs to be more clearly articulated. If these issues are addressed, the work could be a solid contribution to the field, though its broad appeal for a general Nature-style audience may be limited.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that the simulation protocol produces "native conformational ensembles" is not fully supported, as "native" implies a level of accuracy that cannot be established from agreement with a limited set of observables.
- The claim of "quantitative prediction" of relaxation data is weakened by the systematic overestimation of R2 by 26–34%, which is not adequately explained.
- The statement that the outlier trajectories are "not representative" and can be excluded for diagnostic purposes is not supported by a clear, principled criterion for outlier identification.
- The biological relevance of the identified contact clusters (e.g., for p53-MDM2 binding or LLPS) is speculative and not directly tested.
- The claim that the two IDPs have "strikingly similar" cluster size distributions is not quantified or compared to a null model.
- The assertion that the protocol "realistically captures" the dominant minima of the free energy surface is not directly testable from the presented data.
