## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, results, discussion, materials and methods, data availability, supplementary material references)
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific claims, experimental design, computational methodology, data interpretation, and conclusions as presented in the provided text. No external validation of data or code was performed.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors investigate homo- versus heterodimerization preferences among BTB domains of ZBTB transcription factors. Using a fluorescent two-hybrid (F2H) assay in mammalian cells, they report that all eight tested BTB domains can homodimerize, but only PATZ1-PATZ2 forms a stable heterodimer. Molecular dynamics simulations and MM-GBSA calculations on five homodimers and four putative heterodimers suggest that dimer stability arises from diverse mechanisms, with the PATZ1-PATZ2 heterodimer being energetically favorable. Co-expression analysis using ImmGen data supports potential physiological relevance of the PATZ1-PATZ2 interaction.
- **Visible evidence base** Results sections describing F2H assay outcomes (Figs 2, 3, S1, S2), structural comparisons (Fig 1), MD simulation and MM-GBSA data (Figs 4, 5, S5, S6, S7; Tables 2, 3), ImmGen co-expression analysis (Table 1, Figs S3, S4), and Materials and Methods descriptions.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Raw F2H microscopy images and quantification data beyond summary tables; MD simulation input files, force field parameters, and trajectory analysis scripts; MM-GBSA calculation details and error estimates; ImmGen data processing scripts and full correlation matrices; supplementary figures and tables referenced but not provided in the submitted text; details on model construction for homology models and docking poses.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript addresses a timely and important question in transcription factor biology: whether BTB domains can form heterodimers and what governs dimerization partner choice. The combination of a cellular assay, structural analysis, and computational thermodynamics is commendable and provides a framework for studying dimerization specificity across a large protein family. However, the central claim that only PATZ1-PATZ2 forms heterodimers rests on a single assay system with potential limitations, and the computational results are presented in a way that makes independent assessment difficult. The co-expression analysis is suggestive but not definitive. The manuscript would be strengthened by additional orthogonal validation, more transparent computational reporting, and a clearer articulation of the biological consequences of heterodimer formation.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying transcription factor dimerization, BTB domain structure and function, ZBTB family biology, and gene regulatory networks. The F2H assay adaptation for dimerization screening may interest cell biologists and assay developers. Computational biochemists studying protein-protein interaction energetics may find the MM-GBSA comparative analysis useful. Clinicians and geneticists interested in ZBTB-associated disorders (e.g., ICF syndrome) may also find relevance.
- **Major strengths** 1) The question is significant and the systematic pairwise screening approach is logical. 2) The combination of experimental and computational methods provides complementary perspectives. 3) The finding that only one heterodimer forms among eight BTB domains is a clear and potentially important result. 4) The analysis of diverse energetic mechanisms contributing to dimer stability is conceptually interesting. 5) The F2H assay adaptation is a practical contribution.
- **Major Concerns** 
  - **R1-M1** (Major, Blocking: Yes): The F2H assay is the sole experimental evidence for the central claim that only PATZ1-PATZ2 forms heterodimers. The assay relies on co-localization of GFP and RFP signals at a LacO locus, which is an indirect readout of protein-protein interaction. The authors do not provide orthogonal validation (e.g., co-immunoprecipitation for all negative pairs, size-exclusion chromatography, or biophysical binding assays). The co-IP confirmation is shown only for PATZ1-PATZ2 (Fig 3B). Without independent validation, the conclusion that all other pairs do not heterodimerize is not established.
  - **R1-M2** (Major, Blocking: No): The MM-GBSA calculations are presented as quantitative estimates of binding free energy, but the authors acknowledge these are "scores" correlated with experimental Kd values. However, the manuscript does not provide sufficient detail on the convergence of the calculations, the error bars on the reported values, or a comparison with experimental binding data for any of the dimers. The claim that PATZ1-PATZ2 is "22 kcal/mol lower than the average energy expected from the homodimers" is presented as meaningful, but without error estimates or experimental validation, this difference may not be significant.
  - **R1-M3** (Major, Blocking: No): The co-expression analysis using ImmGen data is used to support the physiological relevance of PATZ1-PATZ2 heterodimerization. However, the analysis is described only briefly, and the correlation coefficients and statistical thresholds are not reported in the text. The claim that PATZ1 and PATZ2 are "co-expressed in many cell types" is not quantified, and the figure referenced (Fig S4) is not provided. The logic linking co-expression to heterodimer formation is also not fully developed.
  - **R1-M4** (Major, Blocking: No): The structural models for heterodimers are generated by docking monomers from homodimer structures, but the manuscript does not describe how the docking poses were validated or whether alternative poses were considered. The MD simulations are described as 500 ns, but the equilibration criteria and the choice of the "equilibrated portion" for analysis are not clearly defined. This limits the reproducibility of the computational results.
- **Minor Comments** 
  - **R1-m1** (Minor, Axis: Clarity): The abstract states "only one heterodimer" is found, but the body of the text reports that the F2H assay identified PATZ1-PATZ2 as the only positive pair. The abstract could be more precise about the assay system used.
  - **R1-m2** (Minor, Axis: Methodology): The F2H assay quantification is described as "manual or using the JACoP plugin," but the criteria for scoring a pair as positive or negative are not explicitly defined. A clear threshold for co-localization would strengthen the reproducibility.
  - **R1-m3** (Minor, Axis: Data Presentation): Table 2 and Table 3 list energy components, but the units are not stated in the text. The authors should specify whether these are in kcal/mol and clarify the per-residue normalization.
  - **R1-m4** (Minor, Axis: Literature Context): The discussion of "forced" heterodimers (Stead & Wright, 2014) is brief. The authors could more thoroughly discuss why their F2H assay did not detect MIZ1-BCL6 heterodimerization despite the published forced heterodimer structure, as this is a key discrepancy.
  - **R1-m5** (Minor, Axis: Terminology): The term "sibling rivalry" in the title is informal. While attention-grabbing, it may not be appropriate for a scientific journal and could be reconsidered.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 (lack of orthogonal validation for negative results), R1-M2 (insufficient error analysis and validation of MM-GBSA scores), R1-M4 (incomplete description of computational model construction and analysis).
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - **Originality**: The question of BTB domain heterodimerization specificity is relatively underexplored, and the systematic pairwise screen is a novel contribution. The finding that heterodimerization is rare is original and potentially impactful.
  - **Scientific importance**: Understanding dimerization partner choice in a large transcription factor family has implications for gene regulation, disease mechanisms, and therapeutic targeting. The results could inform future studies on ZBTB function.
  - **Interdisciplinary readership**: The topic bridges structural biology, cell biology, and computational biophysics. The F2H assay and MD/MM-GBSA approaches may appeal to different audiences, but the manuscript is likely to be of primary interest to specialists in transcription factor biology and protein-protein interactions.
  - **Technical soundness**: The experimental design is reasonable, but the lack of orthogonal validation for the central claim and the incomplete reporting of computational methods reduce confidence. The MM-GBSA approach is known to have limitations, and the authors' caveat is appropriate, but more rigorous validation is needed.
  - **Readability for nonspecialists**: The manuscript is generally well-written and accessible, but some sections (e.g., MM-GBSA methodology) may be challenging for readers without a computational background. The figures and tables are referenced clearly, but the absence of supplementary material in the provided text limits full assessment.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The central claim of exclusive PATZ1-PATZ2 heterodimerization requires orthogonal experimental validation. The computational results need more transparent reporting and error analysis. If these issues are addressed, the manuscript could make a valuable contribution to the field.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that "only one heterodimer" forms among the tested pairs is not fully supported without orthogonal validation of the negative results.
- The claim that PATZ1-PATZ2 heterodimer formation is "as stable as" the corresponding homodimers is based solely on MM-GBSA scores without experimental binding data.
- The claim that co-expression of PATZ1 and PATZ2 in immune cells supports physiological relevance is suggestive but not quantified or statistically validated in the provided text.
- The statement that "all heterodimers show favorable interaction energy" is based on computational scores and should be interpreted with caution.
- The claim that the F2H assay can be used as a high-throughput screening tool is speculative and not demonstrated in this study.
