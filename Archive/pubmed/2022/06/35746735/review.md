## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text, including abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, and conclusions. Figures and supplementary materials are referenced but not provided in full.
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, methodological soundness, interpretation of results, and alignment with stated claims. Assessment is limited to the supplied text; figures, tables, and supplementary data are referenced but not directly examined.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report that solid-state NMR spectroscopy reveals substantial conformational changes in Ff phage gene V protein (gVp) upon binding to full-length viral single-stranded DNA (ssDNA). They report an average protein-DNA distance of 5.5 Å, extensive chemical shift perturbations across the protein, and torsion angle differences localized to the DNA binding loop, a broad connecting loop, and the C-terminus, suggesting that the bound structure differs significantly from the free form.
- **Visible evidence base** Text descriptions of agarose gel electrophoresis, UV spectroscopy, 2D and 3D solid-state NMR experiments (DARR, RFDR, CORD, NCA, NCO, NCACX, NCOCX, zfr-INADEQUATE), REDOR distance measurements, chemical shift assignments, CSP calculations, and TALOS-N torsion angle predictions. Figures 1–10 and supplementary figures/tables are referenced but not directly available.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full figures and figure captions, supplementary information (Figures S1–S5, Table S1), NMR spectral quality metrics (line widths, signal-to-noise), detailed REDOR fitting parameters, and the previously published free gVp chemical shift assignments. These are essential for independent verification of assignment completeness, spectral resolution, and distance measurement reliability.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a solid-state NMR study of a protein-ssDNA complex that is technically challenging and of interest to the structural virology and NMR communities. The work addresses a relevant gap, as no atomic-resolution structure of the gVp-ssDNA complex exists. The authors provide a plausible case for conformational change upon DNA binding, supported by chemical shift perturbations and torsion angle predictions. However, the evidence as presented is incomplete in several respects. The REDOR-derived distance is based on a simplified model, the assignment completeness is not fully documented, and the interpretation of CSPs as evidence for structural change relies on assumptions about the free-state reference. The manuscript would benefit from clearer presentation of spectral quality, more rigorous distance analysis, and a more cautious interpretation of the functional implications. The work is likely to be of interest to specialists in phage biology and solid-state NMR, but the broader significance is moderate given the absence of a full structure or functional validation.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying filamentous phage assembly and replication, particularly those interested in gVp function and protein-ssDNA interactions. Solid-state NMR practitioners will be interested in the methodological application to a nucleoprotein complex, including the use of REDOR for distance measurement and the assignment strategy for a protein in a large complex. Structural biologists studying conformational changes in DNA-binding proteins may also find the results relevant, though the lack of a high-resolution structure limits the impact for this broader audience.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses a biologically relevant and previously unresolved question regarding the structure of gVp in its DNA-bound state. The use of solid-state NMR on a full-length viral ssDNA complex is technically demanding and represents a methodological advance. The assignment of nearly complete backbone chemical shifts in a protein-ssDNA complex is a significant achievement. The combination of CSP analysis and torsion angle predictions provides complementary evidence for conformational change. The REDOR experiment directly probes protein-DNA contacts, which is a strength relative to indirect binding assays.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim an average distance of 5.5 Å between protein amino acid residues and the phosphate backbone of ssDNA, derived from 31P-13C REDOR experiments.
  - **Evidence pointer** Section 3.1, Figure 4
  - **Concern** The REDOR analysis is described as fitting the data to a simulation using three carbons at a distance of 5.5 Å from the phosphorus. The text states that including more than three carbons did not alter the result and fewer carbons gave a worse fit. However, the description is insufficient to assess the uniqueness of this fit. A single distance with three equivalent carbons is a highly simplified model for a protein with multiple residues in proximity to DNA. The authors do not report the number of independent data points, the quality of the fit, error estimates on the distance, or whether alternative distance distributions were tested. The claim that 5.5 Å is an "average typical distance" is not quantitatively justified.
  - **Why it matters** The 5.5 Å distance is a central quantitative result that supports the existence of close protein-DNA contacts. If the distance measurement is not robust, the conclusion that gVp directly contacts the DNA backbone is weakened. The simplified model may mask heterogeneity in the actual distances, which is relevant given the authors' own suggestion of conformational changes.
  - **Resolution test** Provide the full REDOR dephasing curve with error bars, the number of data points, the best-fit simulation with residuals, and a systematic exploration of alternative models (e.g., two distances, a distance distribution, or a Gaussian distribution of distances). Report confidence intervals for the fitted distance and justify the choice of three carbons based on the known protein structure or assignment data.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Claim pointer** The authors report assignment of 98% of N, Cα, Cβ, and CO chemical shifts and 90% of all possible 13C and 15N chemical shifts of gVp in the complex.
  - **Evidence pointer** Section 3.2, Figure 5, Figure 6, Figure S4, Table S1
  - **Concern** The assignment completeness is stated, but the manuscript does not provide sufficient evidence to evaluate the reliability of these assignments. No information is given on spectral resolution, line widths, or the number of resolved peaks versus expected resonances. The text mentions that some residues were unassigned (e.g., residues 41, 42, and V43 ambiguous with V45), but the criteria for assignment confidence are not described. The use of sparse labeling with 1,3-13C glycerol is mentioned, but the specific benefits and limitations for this system are not discussed. Without access to the actual spectra or a completeness table with per-residue confidence, the claim of near-complete assignment cannot be independently verified.
  - **Why it matters** The CSP analysis and torsion angle predictions depend entirely on the accuracy and completeness of the chemical shift assignments. If assignments are incorrect or incomplete, the derived structural conclusions are unreliable. The manuscript's central claim of conformational change rests on these data.
  - **Resolution test** Provide representative 2D and 3D spectra with assigned peaks labeled, a table of per-residue assignment confidence (e.g., unambiguous, tentative, or unassigned), and a discussion of spectral quality metrics such as line widths and signal-to-noise ratios. Deposit the assignments in a public database (already stated as BMRB 51391) and provide the chemical shift list as supplementary data for direct inspection.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Interpretation
  - **Claim pointer** The authors conclude that "the structure of gVp in complex with ssDNA differs significantly from the structure of gVp in the free form" based on CSPs and torsion angle differences.
  - **Evidence pointer** Section 3.3, Figures 8–10
  - **Concern** The interpretation of CSPs as evidence for conformational change is reasonable but not definitive. CSPs can arise from direct binding interactions (e.g., ring current effects from DNA bases) without large-scale structural rearrangements. The authors do not discuss this alternative explanation in detail. The torsion angle analysis compares predicted angles from TALOS-N to those from the X-ray structure of free gVp, but TALOS-N predictions are based on chemical shifts and may be biased by the same effects that cause CSPs. The statistical significance of the torsion angle differences is mentioned (p < 0.01) but the test used is not described. The authors also note that some residues with large CSPs (e.g., in the DNA binding loop) are expected to contact DNA, which could explain the shifts without invoking a global conformational change.
  - **Why it matters** The conclusion that the bound structure "differs significantly" from the free form is a strong claim with implications for understanding gVp function and phage assembly. If the observed changes are primarily due to local binding effects rather than global rearrangements, the interpretation would need to be revised. The distinction is important for the biological narrative.
  - **Resolution test** Provide a more nuanced discussion that separates residues likely involved in direct DNA contacts from those undergoing conformational changes. If possible, use additional experimental constraints (e.g., distance measurements between specific residues, or relaxation data) to support the claim of structural rearrangement. Describe the statistical test used for the torsion angle comparison and justify the choice of the X-ray structure as the free-state reference, given that solution NMR structures are also available.
- **Minor Comments**
  - **Concern ID** R1-m1
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Clarity
  - **Affected element** Section 2.1, sample preparation
  - **Evidence pointer** Section 2.1
  - **Issue** The description of the complexation step states that gVp and ssDNA were mixed at a mass ratio of 9 to 1, and the binding stoichiometry is reported as four nucleotides per gVp monomer. However, the text does not specify the buffer conditions, incubation time, or temperature beyond "room temperature for 20 min." The final complex is precipitated with PEG and MgCl2, but the efficiency of complex formation and the potential for free protein or free DNA in the sample are not quantified.
  - **Required correction** Provide additional details on the complexation buffer composition, the concentration of gVp and ssDNA, and any quality control steps (e.g., native gel electrophoresis or size-exclusion chromatography) to confirm that the sample is a homogeneous complex without excess free components.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m2
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Completeness
  - **Affected element** Section 3.1, UV measurements
  - **Evidence pointer** Figure S2
  - **Issue** The UV shift from 276 nm to 258 nm is cited as evidence of complexation, but the text does not provide the raw spectra or a titration curve. The shift is described as being "in accordance with the observations of Day et al.," but no quantitative comparison is made.
  - **Required correction** Show the UV spectra for free gVp, free ssDNA, and the complex, and if a titration was performed, include the data points and the derived binding stoichiometry. This would strengthen the evidence for complex formation.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m3
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Presentation
  - **Affected element** Section 3.3, Figure 10
  - **Evidence pointer** Figure 10
  - **Issue** The torsion angle difference plot is described as showing "significant (p < 0.01) differences" for residues 10–31 compared to residues 52–71, but the statistical method is not described. The text states that the mean difference for residues 10–31 is 30 degrees versus 10 degrees for residues 52–71, but the variability within each region is not reported.
  - **Required correction** Describe the statistical test used (e.g., Student's t-test, Mann-Whitney U test) and report the standard deviation or standard error for each region. Consider showing the per-residue differences with error bars to allow readers to assess the significance visually.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m4
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Interpretation
  - **Affected element** Section 4, Conclusions
  - **Evidence pointer** Section 4
  - **Issue** The conclusion states that "extensive structural alterations are required for binding to ssDNA and in order to allow cooperative binding of dimers to form the entire filamentous premature phage particle." This is speculative, as the study does not directly measure cooperative binding or the assembly process.
  - **Required correction** Soften the language to indicate that the observed changes "may" be required for cooperative binding, or cite prior studies that support this link. Avoid overstating the functional implications beyond the data.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** The REDOR distance analysis (R1-M1) and the assignment reliability (R1-M2) are the two most critical technical issues. The distance measurement is presented as a single value without adequate model testing or error analysis, and the assignment completeness is claimed without sufficient supporting evidence. Both are essential to the central conclusion of conformational change. The interpretation of CSPs (R1-M3) is a conceptual issue that needs to be addressed with additional analysis or discussion.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: The study is original in its application of solid-state NMR to a full-length protein-ssDNA complex, which has not been previously characterized at this level. The finding of conformational changes in gVp upon DNA binding is novel, though not entirely unexpected given prior biochemical data. Scientific importance: The work is relevant to understanding filamentous phage assembly and the function of a model ssDNA binding protein. However, the importance is moderate, as the study does not provide a full atomic-resolution structure of the complex, which would be needed to fully understand the conformational changes. Interdisciplinary readership: The manuscript will primarily appeal to structural biologists and NMR specialists. The phage biology context is clear, but the technical nature of the methods may limit broader readership. Technical soundness: The NMR methodology is appropriate and state-of-the-art, but the analysis as presented has gaps in the distance measurement and assignment validation that need to be addressed. Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is generally well-written and accessible, with clear explanations of the biological context. However, some NMR-specific terms and concepts may be challenging for nonspecialists, and the figures are not available for assessment.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The manuscript addresses a relevant question with an appropriate and challenging technique, and the results are potentially interesting. However, the current evidence is insufficient to fully support the central claims. The REDOR distance analysis and assignment reliability must be strengthened, and the interpretation of CSPs needs to be more nuanced. If these issues are addressed, the manuscript could make a solid contribution to the field.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim of an average 5.5 Å protein-DNA distance is not fully supported by the described REDOR analysis, as the model simplicity and lack of error analysis prevent assessment of robustness.
- The claim of 98% backbone and 90% total chemical shift assignment completeness cannot be verified without access to the spectra or a detailed assignment table with confidence levels.
- The conclusion that the bound structure "differs significantly" from the free form is supported by CSPs and torsion angle predictions, but the possibility that CSPs arise primarily from direct binding contacts rather than global conformational changes is not adequately addressed.
- The statement that structural changes "presumably" allow cooperative binding to form the phage particle is speculative and not directly tested in this study.
- The UV shift from 276 nm to 258 nm is cited as evidence of complexation, but the raw data are not shown, and the quantitative interpretation is limited.
