### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that both enantiomers bind to CT-DNA via intercalation, driven by enthalpy and stabilized by van der Waals forces or hydrogen bonds.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract; methods listed but no data provided  
- **Concern** The abstract states that intercalation is the binding mode and that enthalpy drives the interaction, but no thermodynamic parameters (e.g., Delta H, Delta S, Delta G from ITC) or spectroscopic data (e.g., hypochromism, red shift, induced CD signals) are presented to support these conclusions.  
- **Why it matters** Binding mode and thermodynamic driving force are central mechanistic claims. Without quantitative data, the reader cannot assess whether the evidence distinguishes intercalation from other modes such as groove binding or external electrostatic association.  
- **Resolution test** Provide representative UV-visible titration spectra with binding constants, ITC thermograms with fitted Delta H and Delta S values, and CD spectral changes. Show that the data are consistent with intercalation and enthalpy-driven binding.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Claim-data alignment  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that Delta-[Ru(bpy)2MBIP]2+ intercalates into DNA more deeply than Lambda-[Ru(bpy)2MBIP]2+ and exhibits better photocleavage ability.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract; photocleavage experiment and viscosity measurements mentioned but no results shown  
- **Concern** The abstract asserts a difference in intercalation depth and photocleavage efficiency between enantiomers, but no comparative data (e.g., viscosity slopes, photocleavage gel images, or quantitative cleavage yields) are provided.  
- **Why it matters** The enantioselective difference is a key finding of the study. Without direct comparative evidence, the claim of differential behavior is unsupported.  
- **Resolution test** Present viscosity measurements showing relative viscosity changes for both enantiomers, and photocleavage assays with quantified DNA cleavage percentages under identical conditions.
