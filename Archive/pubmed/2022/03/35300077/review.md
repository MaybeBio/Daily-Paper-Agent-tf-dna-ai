## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text, abstract, and supplementary material references as provided.
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, methodology, data interpretation, and claims as presented in the supplied text. No external validation or replication performed.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report the design, synthesis, and biological evaluation of novel pyridazin-3(2H)-one-based guanidine derivatives as potential DNA minor groove binders with anticancer activity. They claim that bis-guanidinium compounds 1–4 show weak DNA binding affinity and moderate antiproliferative effects in three cancer cell lines, with compound 2 showing an IC50 of 21 ± 1 μM in A2780 cells.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract, main text, Tables 1 and 2, Figures 1–4, Schemes 1–2, and supplementary material references (Figures S1–S15, Tables S1, Schemes S1–S4).
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full experimental procedures, characterization data for all compounds, raw UV-thermal denaturation curves, raw cell viability data, statistical analysis details, and complete docking output files were not provided in the supplied material.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript presents a systematic synthetic effort to generate a series of pyridazin-3(2H)-one-based guanidine derivatives and evaluates their DNA binding and antiproliferative properties. The synthetic chemistry is well described and the compound library is logically designed. However, the biological data are limited in scope and depth, and the link between DNA binding and anticancer activity is not established. The docking studies are used as a preliminary filter but are not validated experimentally. The claims of "potential DNA minor groove binders" are only weakly supported by the thermal denaturation data, which show small ΔTm values. The antiproliferative data are preliminary and lack dose-response curves for most compounds. Overall, the work is of interest to medicinal chemists but does not yet provide a compelling case for the proposed mechanism of action or therapeutic potential.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Medicinal chemists and researchers in anticancer drug discovery, particularly those focused on DNA-targeting agents and minor groove binders. The synthetic methodology for pyridazinone functionalization and guanidine introduction may also be of interest to synthetic organic chemists. The structure-activity relationships around the pyridazinone core and guanidine positioning could inform future design of related compounds.
- **Major strengths** 1. Logical compound design based on a well-established scaffold (pyridazin-3(2H)-one) and known MGB pharmacophores. 2. Comprehensive synthetic route with good yields and purities, demonstrating versatility in functionalization. 3. Use of docking studies to guide compound selection prior to synthesis. 4. Clear presentation of thermal denaturation and cell viability data for a representative set of compounds. 5. Honest discussion of limitations, including the lack of direct evidence for DNA-mediated mechanism of action.
- **Major Concerns** 
  - R1-M1: The DNA binding affinity data are weak and not convincingly supportive of the "minor groove binder" designation. ΔTm values of 1.1–1.4 °C are minimal and may not reflect specific minor groove binding. No competition assays, circular dichroism, or other biophysical methods were used to confirm groove binding. The claim that these compounds are "potential DNA minor groove binders" is therefore not well supported.
  - R1-M2: The antiproliferative data are limited to percentage inhibition at a single concentration (100 μM) for most compounds, with only compound 2 having an IC50 value reported. This is insufficient to assess the anticancer potential of the series. No selectivity data against non-cancerous cells are provided.
  - R1-M3: The correlation between DNA binding affinity and antiproliferative activity is not established. The authors acknowledge this, but the manuscript would benefit from a more direct investigation of the mechanism of action, such as cell cycle analysis, apoptosis assays, or DNA damage markers.
  - R1-M4: The docking studies are used as a preliminary screen, but the correlation between docking scores and experimental DNA binding is not quantitatively assessed. The rigid-flexible approach and the use of a single DNA model limit the generalizability of the docking results.
- **Minor Comments** 
  - R1-m1: The manuscript would benefit from a more detailed description of the UV-thermal denaturation assay conditions, including buffer composition, DNA concentration, and compound-to-DNA ratio.
  - R1-m2: The purity of compounds is stated as ≥94%, but the method of purity determination (e.g., HPLC, elemental analysis) is not specified.
  - R1-m3: The authors mention that compounds 8 and 9 were separated by reverse phase chromatography, but the yields and characterization of these individual compounds are not fully detailed.
  - R1-m4: The statement that "the presence of the diaryl core seems to correlate with the inhibition observed" is based on a small number of compounds and should be supported by a more systematic structure-activity relationship analysis.
  - R1-m5: The manuscript would benefit from a brief discussion of the physicochemical properties (e.g., logP, solubility) of the synthesized compounds, as these could influence both DNA binding and cellular uptake.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1, R1-M2, R1-M3, R1-M4.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - Originality: Moderate. The use of pyridazin-3(2H)-one as a scaffold for MGBs is novel, but the overall concept of guanidine-based DNA binders is well established.
  - Scientific importance: Moderate. The work adds to the growing library of potential MGBs but does not yet demonstrate a significant advance in potency, selectivity, or mechanistic understanding.
  - Interdisciplinary readership: Limited. The work is primarily of interest to medicinal chemists and cancer biologists; the biophysical and computational aspects are not sufficiently developed to attract a broader audience.
  - Technical soundness: Adequate for the synthetic chemistry, but the biological and biophysical evaluations are preliminary and lack depth.
  - Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is clearly written and accessible, but the significance of the findings is not compellingly conveyed to a general scientific audience.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The synthetic work is solid, but the biological and biophysical data are too preliminary to support the claims of potential DNA minor groove binding and anticancer activity. The manuscript would require substantial additional experimental work, including confirmation of DNA binding mechanism, more comprehensive antiproliferative profiling, and mechanistic studies linking DNA binding to cellular effects, before it could be considered for publication in a high-impact journal.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The designation of compounds 1–4 as "potential DNA minor groove binders" is not supported by the weak ΔTm data alone; no direct evidence of minor groove binding is provided.
- The claim that "the presence of the diaryl core seems to correlate with the inhibition observed" is based on a limited dataset and is not statistically validated.
- The statement that "the two terminal guanidinium moieties are essential for DNA binding" is inferred from a small set of compounds and may not be generalizable.
- The antiproliferative activity of compound 2 (IC50 = 21 ± 1 μM) is reported without details on the assay methodology, replicates, or statistical significance.
- The docking results are presented as supportive but are not validated against experimental binding data, and the limitations of the rigid-flexible approach are not fully discussed.
