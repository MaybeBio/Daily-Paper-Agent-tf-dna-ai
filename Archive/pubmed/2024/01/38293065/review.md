## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (preprint, bioRxiv)
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, methodology, data presentation, and claims as presented in the provided manuscript text. No external data or supplementary files were assessed beyond what is described in the text.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The manuscript reports the completion of the modERN consortium effort, presenting ChIP-seq binding profiles for 604 Drosophila melanogaster and 350 Caenorhabditis elegans transcription factors, identifying 3.6M and 0.9M binding sites respectively. The authors describe metapeak clustering, co-binding correlations, motif analyses, and use random forest models with single-cell RNA-seq data to identify transcription factors important for cell-type-specific gene expression. They present the data as a community resource.
- **Visible evidence base** Main text, tables (Table 1, Table 2), figures (Figure 1-7, Supplementary Figures 1-7), and methods sections as described in the manuscript.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Supplementary files (File 1-4), detailed figure legends, and the actual figures themselves were not provided. The full methods for the random forest model and the specific parameters for the "best" models are only partially described. The Java program for lineage visualization is mentioned but not described in detail.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript represents a substantial and valuable community resource. The scale of the data generation effort is impressive, and the uniform reanalysis of all ChIP-seq data is a significant strength. The metapeak concept is useful for aggregating binding events. However, the manuscript is largely descriptive, and the analytical validations of the key claims, particularly the random forest modeling and the biological insights derived from it, are presented with insufficient detail and statistical rigor to fully assess their validity. The claim of "tissue specific regulatory relationships" is only weakly supported by the presented analyses. The manuscript would be strengthened by a more thorough validation of the model predictions and a clearer demonstration of the biological utility of the resource beyond descriptive correlations.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in gene regulation, developmental biology, and genomics, particularly those working with Drosophila or C. elegans. The resource will be of high value to computational biologists developing models of gene regulation and to experimental biologists studying specific transcription factors or developmental processes. The data will also be useful for comparative genomics and evolutionary studies of gene regulation.
- **Major strengths**
  1. The scale and comprehensiveness of the resource: 954 TFs across two major model organisms is a landmark dataset.
  2. The uniform reanalysis of all data with a single pipeline is a major strength, ensuring comparability across experiments.
  3. The metapeak concept provides a useful and logical way to aggregate binding data and reduce complexity.
  4. The integration with single-cell expression data to infer cell-type-specific regulatory relationships is a forward-looking and potentially powerful approach.
  5. The clear presentation of the resource's availability and the user-friendly interface (website, Java program) will maximize its utility to the community.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **R1-M1** (Major, Blocking: Yes): The central claim of "tissue specific regulatory relationships" is not rigorously validated. The random forest model is described, but the manuscript lacks a clear demonstration that the model's predictions are accurate. There is no cross-validation, no comparison to a null model, and no experimental validation of any predicted regulatory relationship. The cosine similarity analysis is descriptive and the threshold of 0.2 is presented without a clear statistical justification. The manuscript needs to demonstrate that the model can accurately predict expression patterns or identify known regulatory relationships with a high degree of precision and recall.
  - **R1-M2** (Major, Blocking: No): The analysis of co-binding correlations and the definition of metapeaks, while interesting, is presented without a robust statistical framework. The manuscript does not provide a null expectation for co-binding, nor does it assess the significance of the observed correlations. The relationship between metapeak occupancy, conservation, and function is discussed, but the analyses are largely descriptive. A more formal statistical treatment is needed to support the claim that these metapeaks represent functional regulatory modules.
  - **R1-M3** (Major, Blocking: No): The manuscript does not adequately address the potential for technical artifacts to influence the results. The difference in the number of peaks per experiment between fly and worm is noted, but the potential contribution of differences in antibody quality, chromatin preparation, or sequencing depth is not thoroughly explored. The validation of the ChIP-seq data (e.g., against known binding sites or expression data) is not presented in sufficient detail.
- **Minor Comments**
  - **R1-m1** (Minor, Axis: Clarity): The description of the random forest model is incomplete. The text mentions "more than 40 different models" were tested, but the specific features, hyperparameters, and the criteria for selecting the "best" model are not fully described. A more detailed methods section is required.
  - **R1-m2** (Minor, Axis: Reproducibility): The manuscript states that the Java program is available, but the version, dependencies, and system requirements are not specified. This will hinder its immediate use by the community.
  - **R1-m3** (Minor, Axis: Presentation): The figures are referenced but not provided in the text. The legends are not available, making it difficult to fully interpret the results described in the text.
  - **R1-m4** (Minor, Axis: Statistical Rigor): The use of the cosine similarity as a measure of correlation between TF and target expression is not well justified. The manuscript should discuss the limitations of this metric and consider alternative measures.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established**
  - R1-M1: Lack of validation of the random forest model's predictions.
  - R1-M2: Lack of statistical framework for co-binding and metapeak analyses.
  - R1-M3: Insufficient discussion of potential technical artifacts.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**
  - **Originality:** The work is not conceptually novel, as it is a large-scale application of established ChIP-seq and machine learning methods. However, the scale and the integration of data across two species and with single-cell data provide a unique and original resource.
  - **Scientific importance:** The resource is of high importance to the fields of developmental biology and gene regulation. It will likely become a standard reference dataset for the community. The biological insights presented, however, are incremental and do not yet demonstrate a major conceptual advance.
  - **Interdisciplinary readership:** The data and resource will be of interest to a broad readership, including molecular biologists, developmental biologists, and computational biologists. The manuscript is written in a way that is largely accessible to these groups.
  - **Technical soundness:** The core data generation and processing appear sound. However, the analytical methods used to derive the main biological claims (random forest, co-binding) are not presented with sufficient rigor to be fully assessed. The lack of validation is a major technical shortcoming.
  - **Readability for nonspecialists:** The manuscript is well-written and the main concepts are explained clearly. However, the methods sections are dense and would be challenging for a non-specialist to follow.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The resource itself is clearly valuable and worthy of publication. However, the manuscript's central claims about "tissue specific regulatory relationships" are not currently established from the provided evidence. The authors must provide a rigorous validation of their model and a more robust statistical analysis of their co-binding and metapeak data to support these claims.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that the random forest model identifies TFs with a "prominent role in promoting target gene expression in specific cell types" is unsupported without model validation.
- The claim that "tissue specific regulatory relationships" are revealed is not supported by the descriptive analyses presented.
- The assertion that the metapeaks "sample much of the regulatory DNA in these two genomes" is a reasonable inference but is not directly tested.
- The biological significance of the co-binding clusters, beyond descriptive correlation, is not established.
- The claim that the resource "should fuel investigations into TF function during development" is a reasonable expectation but is not a scientific claim that can be tested from this manuscript.
