# Conditional Loss of CaMKK2 in Osterix-Positive Osteoprogenitors Enhances Osteoblast Function in a Sex-Divergent Manner


## Abstract
  Ca2+/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase kinase 2 (CaMKK2) is a multi-functional, serine/threonine protein kinase with predominant roles in inflammation, systemic energy metabolism, and bone remodeling. We previously reported that global ablation of CaMKK2 or its systemic pharmacological inhibition led to bone mass accrual in mice by stimulating osteoblasts and inhibiting osteoclasts. However, a direct, cell-intrinsic role for the kinase in the osteoblast lineage has not been established. Here we report that conditional deletion of CaMKK2 from osteoprogenitors, using the Osterix 1 (Osx1) - GFP::Cre (tetracycline-off) mouse line, resulted in increased trabecular bone mass due to an acute stimulation of osteoblast function in male and female mice. The acute simulation of osteoblasts and bone formation following conditional ablation of osteoprogenitor-derived CaMKK2 was sustained only in female mice. Periosteal bone formation at the cortical bone was enhanced only in male conditional knockout mice without altering cortical bone mass or strength. Prolonged deletion of CaMKK2 in early osteoblasts was accompanied by a stimulation of osteoclasts in both sexes, indicating a coupling effect. Notably, alterations in trabecular and cortical bone mass were absent in the doxycycline-removed “Cre-only” Osx1-GFP::Cre mice. Thus, the increase in osteoblast function at the trabecular and cortical bone surfaces following the conditional deletion of CaMKK2 in osteoprogenitors is indicative of a direct but sex-divergent role for the kinase in osteoblasts.


## Introduction
  Osteoporosis and the associated risk of fragility fractures are major health issues plaguing the elderly in the United States and worldwide [1–4]. An estimated 10.2 million adults aged 50 and above in the United States suffer from osteoporosis [4]. Bisphosphonates, the first-line therapy against osteoporosis, are very effective at halting further bone loss but do not stimulate new bone formation [5–7]. Moreover, their long-term use is associated with adverse effects such as osteonecrosis of the jaw and atypical femoral fractures [5, 8, 9]. Therefore, bone anabolic therapies that stimulate osteoblastic bone formation are in high clinical demand. Parathyroid hormone (PTH) and PTH-related peptide synthetic analogs, teriparatide and abaloparatide, as well as the anti-sclerostin antibody romosozumab, are osteoanabolic therapies approved by the FDA to treat patients with osteoporosis who are at high risk for fractures [10–12]. Though very effective, these anabolics have either a limited therapeutic window and/or potential side effects [10, 13–15]. Thus, it is imperative that molecular mechanisms that govern bone formation and remodeling are thoroughly studied to identify novel osteoanabolic therapeutic targets.

  Mice with a global loss of Ca2+/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase kinase 2 (CaMKK2) possess increased numbers of bone-forming osteoblasts and fewer bone-resorbing osteoclasts, resulting in a bone anabolic phenotype [16, 17]. Pharmacological inhibition of CaMKK2 protects against ovariectomy-induced bone loss, reverses age-associated bone loss, and accelerates fracture healing [17–19]. We recently reported a cell-autonomous role for CaMKK2 in osteocytes in regulating osteoclast function in a sex-specific manner [20]. In vitro studies using bone marrow cells from Camkk2−/− mice indicate a direct role for CaMKK2 in osteoblasts and osteoclasts by regulating cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP)–protein kinase A (PKA) and cAMP response element binding protein (CREB)–nuclear factor of activated T cells cytoplasmic (NFATc1) signaling mechanisms, respectively [17]. However, an in vivo, cell-intrinsic role for CaMKK2 in early osteoblasts has not been tested.

  The purpose of this study was to understand the cell-autonomous role of CaMKK2 in osteoblasts and post-natal bone formation. Bone-forming osteoblasts originate from mesenchymal progenitors through a differentiation process governed by an array of transcription factors [21]. Runx2, a Runt domain-containing transcription factor, and its downstream target Osterix (Osx) play crucial roles in embryonic and post-natal osteoblast differentiation [21–23]. Expression of Osx marks the commitment of mesenchymal progenitors to the osteoblast lineage, and Osx null mice possess a cartilaginous skeleton due to the lack of bone formation [24]. In this study, we conditionally deleted CaMKK2 in Osx-positive early osteoblast progenitors (osteoprogenitors) using the reverse tetracycline-inducible Osx1-GFP::Cre mouse line [25] to characterize its effect on postnatal bone acquisition.


## Materials and Methods

### Mice and Experimental Setup:
  Animal studies were performed with prior approval from Indiana University School of Medicine (IUSM) Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) and all experiments were performed in compliance with NIH guidelines on the use and care of laboratory and experimental animals. All animals were housed in the IUSM Laboratory Animal Resource Center (LARC, Indianapolis, IN, USA) under a 12-h light, 12-h dark cycle. Food and water were provided ad libitum. All skeletal phenotyping and in vitro experiments were performed using mice of both biological sexes, and data from male and female mice were analyzed separately.

  Osx-GFP::Cre (Sp7-tTA,tetO-EGFP/cre)1Amc) transgenic mice (Osx-GFP::Cre) [25, 26] were purchased from Jackson Laboratory (Bar Harbor, ME, USA; Stock Number 006361) and backcrossed to C57BL6/J mice for 10 generations. Deletion of CaMKK2 in osteoprogenitors and their descendants was achieved by crossing Osx-GFP::Cre mice with Camkk2flox/flox mice (C57BL6; exons 2–4 of Camkk2 flanked by loxP sites as described before [27]). Heterozygous Osx1-GFP-Cre:Camkk2 flox/WT mice were crossed to generate homozygous Osx1-GFP-Cre:Camkk2 flox/flox mice (Supp. Fig 1). Osx1-GFP-Cre:Camkk2 WT/WT were generated separately. Osx-GFP::Cre mice harbor reverse tetracycline (Tet) response element controlled GFP-Cre fusion protein under the control of Osx1 regulatory sequences [25]. In this Tet-off system, Cre-GFP fusion protein expression is suppressed in the presence of the tetracycline or its analogue doxycycline (Dox), and activated when Dox is removed [25]. Male and female Osx-GFP::Cre:Camkk2 flox/flox and Osx-GFP-Cre:Camkk2 WT/WT (Cre-only controls) mice were kept on Dox in drinking water (1 mg/mL Doxycycline hyclate in 2% (wt/vol) sucrose (both from Millipore Sigma, Burlington, MA, USA)) from conception till weaning at day 28 or week 4 post-partum, to suppress Cre-GFP expression in Osx-positive osteoprogenitors during embryogenesis and early postnatal growth. At weaning, male and female Osx-GFP-Cre:Camkk2 flox/flox were either kept on Dox-sucrose water for 4 or 12 additional weeks (w; Camkk2ctrl) or switched to 2% sucrose water for 4 or 12 w (Camkk2Ob−) (Fig. 1A). Similarly, at weaning, male and female Osx1-GFP-Cre:Camkk2 WT/WT mice were either kept on Dox for 4 or 12 additional weeks (Osx-GFP::Cre Dox+) or weaned off Dox for 4 or 12 w (Osx-GFP::Cre Dox−) (Fig. 1A). Water for all animals was changed biweekly. Skeletal phenotyping was performed on male and female mice that remained on or off Dox for 4 w post-weaning (8 w of age) and/or on or off Dox for 12 w post-weaning (16 w of age) (Fig. 1A). In vivo double-fluorochrome labeling was conducted by administering alizarin red (30 mg/kg; Millipore Sigma) twice via intraperitoneal injections at 7 and 2 days prior to euthanasia for evaluating osteoblast function.


### Micro-computed tomography (μCT) imaging and analysis:
  Left femurs excised from 8 and/or 16 w old male and female mice were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA) for 48 h at 4°C, transferred to 70% ethanol and μCT scanned using a high-resolution Skyscan1172 scanner (Bruker, Kontich, Belgium) using the following settings: 59 kV source voltage, 0.5 mm aluminum filter, 167 μA source current, 885 ms exposure time, 5.86 μm image pixel size and rotation step degree of 0.7. Collected 3D images were reconstructed using NRecon version 1.6.9.4 (Skyscan, Kontich, Belgium), with a ring artifact reduction of 5, beam hardening correction of 20%, and smoothing of 2 using a Gaussian kernel. Two-D/3D analyses were performed using CT Analyzer version 1.14.4.1+ (Skyscan). An analyzed region of interest (ROI) for the trabecular bone was defined as beginning 1 mm below the distal growth plate and extending 200 consecutive images from the distal growth plate, whereas the ROI for cortical analysis was defined as beginning approximately 2.5 mm below the final image of the trabecular region of interest and extending 50 consecutive images of the femur midshaft. Finally, CTvox version 2.3.2.0 (Skyscan) was used to generate reconstructed 3D visualization models. Trabecular bone volume per total volume (BV/TV), trabecular separation (Tb.Sp), trabecular thickness (Tb.Th), trabecular number (Tb.N), total bone area (Tt.Ar), marrow area (Ma.Ar), cortical area (Ct.Ar), and cortical thickness (Ct.Th), minimum moment of inertia (Imin) and maximum moment of inertia (IMax) were calculated using previously established and accepted guidelines [28].


### Mechanical Testing:
  Right femurs were excised from 8 and/or 16 w old male and female mice, wrapped in phosphate buffered saline (PBS) soaked gauze and stored at −20°C. On the day of testing, femurs were warmed to room temperature and their mechanical strength assessed using a three-point bending apparatus (TestResources, Shakopee, MN, USA). In brief, the femurs were loaded to the point of failure by compression using a crosshead speed of 0.2 mm/s, during which force and displacement measurements were collected every 0.02 s. From this, force versus displacement curves, yield force (N), stiffness (N/mm), ultimate force (N) and energy to failure (mJ) were calculated using MTestWR™ software following standard equations [29].


### Histology, histomorphometry, and immunohistochemistry (IHC):
  Following μCT, undecalcified distal and midshaft femurs were embedded in polymethyl methacrylate and sectioned to perform dynamic histomorphometry as previously described [20]. Primary measurements such as single-label perimeter (sL.Pm), double-label perimeter (dL.Pm) and interlabel width (Ir.L.Wi) were used to calculate mineral apposition rate (MAR=Ir.L.Wi/7days[μm/day]); mineralizing surface (MS/BS=(0.5*sL.Pm+dl.Pm)/B.Pm*100[%]); and bone formation rate BFR/BS=MAR*MS/BS*365μm3/μm2/year] [20, 30].

  Plastic-embedded, longitudinal, undecalcified distal femur sections (5 μm thick) were stained for tartrate resistant acid phosphatase (TRAP) activity followed by counterstaining with Gill No. 3 hematoxylin (both from Millipore Sigma). For static histomorphometry, an ROI was defined as at least 200 μm from the edge of the growth plate, ending 1mm from the growth plate, and at least 200 μm from the cortical edge. Osteoclast (OC) number (OC.N) and OC surface/bone surface (OC.S/BS) within the ROI were quantified using OsteoMetrics software (OsteoMetrics, Decatur, GA, USA). Static and dynamic histomorphometry followed the guidelines of the American Society for Bone and Mineral Research Histomorphometry Nomenclature Committee [30].

  Immunohistochemical staining was performed as described previously [20]. Briefly, intact long bones were decalcified in 14% EDTA (pH 7.4) and dehydrated before paraffin embedding. Five μm thick sections were deparaffinized and incubated with primary antibody against CaMKK2 (rabbit anti-CaMKK2-NT; cat# 033168, US Biological, Salem, MA, USA; 1:200 dilution) and anti-rabbit secondary (Jackson Immunoresearch, West Grove, PA, USA) and counterstained with Gill No. 1 Hematoxylin (Millipore Sigma). Images were captured using a Leica DMi8 microscope processed with Leica LAS-X software (Leica Microsystems, Wetzlar, Germany).


### Cryopreservation, sectioning, and immunofluorescence (IF):
  Cryopreservation and sectioning were performed as described previously [19, 31]. Briefly, right femurs excised from 8 w and/or 16 w old male and female mice were immediately fixed in 4% PFA for 48 h at 4°C, followed by embedding in optimal cutting temperature (OCT) blocks and flash freezing in a bath of dry ice and n-hexane. Samples were stored at −80°C until cryosectioning. Cryosections were prepared using a gold standard blade (DT315G50, C. L. Stuerky. Inc., Lebanon, PA), collected on an adhesive cryofilm tape (Section lab Co. Ltd., Japan) and stored at −20°C until IF staining [19, 31].

  To perform IF, sections were rehydrated with PBS, permeabilized in 0.2 % Tween 20 in PBS prior to antigen retrieval using a citrate buffer (0.01 M citrate, pH 6.0) for 20 at 50°C. Auto-fluorescence was quenched using Tris-Glycine solution. Following blocking (3% bovine serum albumin (BSA) / 5% normal donkey serum), sections were then incubated with rabbit anti-CaMKK2-NT primary antibody (cat# 033168, US Biological; 1:100 dilution in blocking buffer) followed by incubation with fluorescence-conjugated anti-rabbit secondary antibody (Jackson Immunoresearch; 1:200 dilution in blocking buffer). The sections were mounted using Prolong gold antifade mounting media with DAPI (Invitrogen, Thermo Fisher, Hampton, NH, USA) on a coverslip facing the silver side of the cryofilm tape so that the section in sandwiched between the tape and the coverslip. IF-labeled images were acquired using a Leica DMi8 fluorescence microscope and processed using Leica LAS-X software (Leica Microsystems).


### Primary osteoblast culture:
  Bone marrow cells were isolated from the femurs and tibiae of 8 w old male and female Camkk2Ctrl and Camkk2Ob− mice and plated at a seeding density of 1 × 106 cells per cm2 in α-minimal essential medium (α-MEM; Invitrogen, Thermo Fisher) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; R&D Systems, Minneapolis, MN, USA) and 1 % penicillin and streptomycin (P/S; Invitrogen, Thermo Fisher). Bone marrow cells were cultured for 7 d under hypoxic conditions and replated at a density of 2.8 × 104 cells /cm2 in 24 well dishes containing glass cover slips in osteoblast differentiation media (α-MEM (Invitrogen, Thermo Fisher) containing 10% FBS (R&D Systems) and 1 % P/S (Invitrogen, Thermo Fisher), 50 μM ascorbic acid, and 10 mM β-glycerophosphate (both from Millipore Sigma, Sigma Aldrich).


### Immunocytochemistry:
  Day 5 osteoblasts, grown on glass coverslips, were fixed in 1% PFA, permeabilized in 0.5% Tween-20 (Millipore Sigma)in PBS, blocked for 2 h with 3% BSA and 5% normal donkey serum followed by sequential incubations with CaMKK2 primary antibody and fluorescence-conjugated anti-rabbit secondary antibody. The samples were then mounted on slides using VectaShield Mounting media with DAPI (Vector Laboratories, Newark, CA, USA). Images were acquired using Leica DMi8 fluorescence microscope and processed using Leica LAS-X software.


### Statistical analysis:
  Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism software (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA) and statistical significance was determined at probability values below 0.05 (p<0.05). Normality assumptions were evaluated using histograms and QQ plots. Data sets that passed normality test were analyzed using unpaired, two-tailed Student’s t-test or ordinary one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post-hoc analysis when comparing >2 groups. All values are represented as mean ± standard deviation (SD).


## Results:

### Conditional deletion of CaMKK2 in osteoprogenitors enhances trabecular bone mass in male and female mice in a sex-divergent manner
  We first confirmed the activation of GFP-Cre fusion protein and deletion of CaMKK2 in Osx-positive cells using osteoblast cultures of bone marrow progenitors isolated from Dox-removed (4 w Dox-off) male and female Osx1-GFP-Cre and Osx1-GFP-Cre:Camkk2 flox/flox (Camkk2Ob−) mice. Robust GFP expression was evident in day 5 osteoblasts of both genotypes, indicating Cre recombinase activation, whereas CaMKK2 deletion was only observed in male and female Camkk2Ob− cells (Fig. 1B). Conditional removal of CaMKK2 from Osx-positive cells was verified in vivo by immunostaining long bone sections from Camkk2ctrl (Dox-on Osx-GFP-Cre:Camkk2 flox/flox) and Camkk2Ob− (Dox-off Osx-GFP-Cre:Camkk2 flox/flox) mice. Removal of Dox resulted in GFP expression indicating Osx-Cre activation and a concomitant absence of CaMKK2 immunoreactivity in male and female Camkk2Ob− long bones (Fig. 1C and Supp. Fig. 2).

  Next, we assessed trabecular bone parameters by μCT analysis of male and female distal femurs. We observed bone volume fraction (%BV/TV) to be increased by 27% (p=0.0003) in Camkk2Ob− males after 4 w of Dox removal, compared to age-matched male Camkk2ctrl mice that remained on Dox (Fig. 2A, 2Bi). Male Camkk2ctrl mice that remained on Dox for 12 additional weeks post-weaning displayed a non-significant 18% increase in %BV/TV compared to that in mice that remained on Dox for 4 weeks (Fig. 2A, 2Bi). At 12 w of Cre activation, we observed a slight, but non-significant, decrease in %BV/TV (by 12%) in Camkk2Ob− males, compared to the 4 w Dox-off cohort (Fig. 2Bi). However, %BV/TV was comparable between 12 w Dox-off male Camkk2Ob− and 12 w Dox-on male Camkk2ctrl mice (Fig. 2Bi). We also observed significantly higher trabecular thickness (Tb.Th.) and trabecular number (Tb.N.), and lower trabecular separation (Tb.Sp.) in male Camkk2Ob− mice following 4 w of Dox removal, compared to Camkk2ctrl mice (Fig. 2Bi–Biv). Compared to their respective age-matched Dox-on cohorts, Tb. Th. was significantly higher and Tb. Sp. significantly lower in 12 w Dox-off male Camkk2ctrl and Camkk2Ob− mice (Fig. 2Bii, 2Biv). In contrast, Tb.N. was significantly decreased in male 12 w Dox-off Camkk2Ob− mice, compared to those in 4 w Dox-off Camkk2Ob− males (Fig. 2Bii).

  Female 4 w Dox-off Camkk2Ob− mice also displayed increased trabecular bone volume (by 16%; p=0.019) compared to age-and sex-matched Camkk2ctrl mice (Fig. 2A, 2Ci). Unlike males, %BV/TV was significantly elevated in 12 w Dox-off female Camkk2Ob− mice compared to sex-matched Camkk2ctrl mice that remained on Dox for 12 w post-weaning (Fig. 2A, 2Ci). In contrast, bone volume remained similar between female Camkk2ctrl mice that remained on Dox for 4 or 12 w post-weaning and between female Camkk2Ob− mice at 4 and 12 w of Dox removal (Fig. 2Ci). Similar to males, female Dox-on Camkk2ctrl mice and Dox-off Camkk2Ob− mice displayed age-dependent increase in Tb. Th, regardless of Dox treatment (Fig. 2Cii). In contrast to males, Tb. Th. in 12 w Dox-off female Camkk2Ob− mice was significantly higher than 12 Dox-on female Camkk2ctrl mice (Fig. 2Cii). Female Camkk2Ob− mice Camkk2ctrl mice displayed similar age-dependent decreases in Tb. N and increases in Tb. Sp, regardless of Dox treatment (Fig. 2Ciii–Civ). Thus, conditional removal of CaMKK2 from osteoprogenitors stimulates an acute increase in trabecular bone mass in male and female mice, which was sustained in female but not male mice. Additionally, male Camkk2ctrl mice kept on Dox displayed a modest, albeit non-significant, increase in bone mass that was not observed in females.

  Following previous reports that Osx1-GFP-Cre transgene activation itself may have bone-specific effects [32], we performed μCT-based assessment of cancellous and cortical bone parameters in male and female Osx1-GFP-Cre mice that were kept on Dox for 4 or 12 additional weeks or removed from Dox for 4 or 12 w post-weaning. We did not observe any differences in trabecular %BV/TV or Tb.Th. following 4 weeks of Cre activation in male or female Osx1-GFP-Cre mice, compared to mice that remained on Dox (Supp. Fig. 3Ai–Aii, 3Bi–Bii). Further, we observed an increase in Tb.N. (p=0.014) and a decrease in Tb.Sp. (p=0.036) at 4 w of Cre activation in male but not female Osx1-GFP-Cre mice (Supp. Fig. 3Aiii–Aiv; 3Biii–Biv). Whereas %BV/TV in Osx1-GFP-Cre mice that remained on Dox for 4 or 12 w post-weaning was similar, at 12 w of Cre activation, male Osx1-GFP-Cre mice displayed a significant, 21% reduction in trabecular bone volume, whereas this was not noted in female mice (Supp. Fig. 3Ai, 3Bi). Both male and female Osx1-GFP-Cre mice displayed significantly increased Tb.Sp. and decreased Tb.N., whether they remained on Dox or were weaned off Dox for 12 weeks, compared to the respective 4 w Dox-on and Dox-off cohorts (Supp. Fig.3 Aiii, Aiv, Biii, Biv). In contrast to previous reports indicating decreased body weight and cortical bone accrual in young Osx1-GFP-Cre mice [32, 33], we observed an age-dependent and Dox-independent increase in body weight in male but not female mice (Supp. Fig. 4A). However, female 12 w Dox off Camkk2Ob− mice possessed significantly higher body weight than female 12 w Dox-on Camkk2ctrl mice (Supp. Fig. 4B). Further, cortical bone parameters did not change following Dox removal in either sex of Osx1-GFP-Cre mice (not shown).


### Removal of osteoprogenitor-derived CaMKK2 enhances osteoblast function
  Next, we evaluated the effect of conditional removal of Camkk2 from Osx-positive osteoprogenitors on cancellous bone formation in male and female mice. Dynamic histomorphometry of double alizarin red-labeled distal femur sections from Camkk2ctrl and Camkk2Ob− male mice revealed a 26.8% increase in MAR (p=0.0061) and a 38% increase in BFR (p=0.014) following 4 w of Osx-Cre activation, compared to age and sex-matched Camkk2ctrl mice (Fig. 3A, 3Bii–Biii). The increased mineral apposition and bone formation were sustained at 12 w of Cre activation in male mice, and these mice possessed significantly higher mineralized bone surface, MAR and BFR compared to 12 w Dox-on male Camkk2ctrl mice (Fig. 3A, 3Bi–Biii). Female Camkk2Ob− mice also displayed a 33% increase in MAR (p=0.011) and BFR (p=0.017) following 4 w of Cre activation compared to age and sex-matched Camkk2ctrl mice (Fig. 3A, 3Cii–Ciii). Although MAR and BFR in 12 w Dox-off female Camkk2Ob− mice diminished by 17% and 14%, respectively, compared to those in 4 w Dox-off Camkk2Ob− female mice, these values were significantly higher than those in 12 w Dox-on Camkk2ctrl female mice (Fig. 3Cii–Ciii). In contrast, the mineralizing surface was not different in the female cohorts (Fig. 3 Bi, 3Ci). Notably, removal of Dox in Osx-Cre-GFP male and female mice did not alter MAR or BFR at 4 w or 12 w of Cre transactivation or inactivation (data not shown). These data indicate that ablating CaMKK2 postnatally from osteoprogenitors contributes to enhanced osteoblast function in male and female mice [34].


### Camkk2Ob− mice display a delayed upregulation of osteoclasts
  We performed static histomorphometry on TRAP-stained distal femur sections to assess the impact of ablating osteoprogenitor-derived CaMKK2 on osteoclasts. We did not observe any significant differences in number of osteoclasts (N.Oc/B.Pr) or their attachment to bone surface (Oc.S/BS) following 4 w of Cre activation in male or female mice, when compared to age and sex-matched Camkk2ctrl mice (Fig. 4A–4E). In contrast, we observed a significant reduction in osteoclast numbers and Oc.S/BS in long bones from male Camkk2ctrl mice that remained on Dox for 12 w post-weaning, compared to those that remained on Dox for 4 w post-weaning (Fig. 4A–4C). In contrast, N.Oc/B.Pr and Oc.S/BS were similar in 4 and 12 w Dox-on female Camkk2ctrl mice (Fig. 4A, 4D, 4E). By 12 weeks of Osx-Cre activation, osteoclast numbers became elevated by 72% in male and 54% in female Camkk2Ob− mice, when compared to sex-matched 4 w Dox-off Camkk2Ob− cohorts (Fig. 4A, 4B, 4D). We also noted a 52% increase in Oc.S/BS in female 12 w Camkk2Ob− femur when compared to the 4 w Camkk2Ob− group, whereas these differences were absent in the male Camkk2Ob− mice (Fig. 4A, 4C, 4E). Further, male and female 12 w Dox-off Camkk2Ob− mice possessed significantly higher N.Oc/B.Pr and Oc.S/BS compared to sex-matched 12 w Dox-on Camkk2ctrl mice (Fig. 4B–E). Taken together, results from trabecular bone dynamic and static histomorphometry suggest a coupling effect of osteoclast activation driven by an acute stimulation of bone formation following the conditional deletion of CaMKK2 from osteoprogenitors.


### Effect of CaMKK2 deletion from osteoprogenitors on the cortical bone
  Next, we evaluated the effect of deleting CaMKK2 from osteoprogenitors on femoral cortical bone cross-sectional geometry using μCT [35] in male and female Camkk2Ob− mice as compared to sex- and age-matched Camkk2ctrl mice (Figs. 5A, 6A). In males, compared to 4 w Dox-on Camkk2ctrl mice, total bone area (Tt.Ar), cortical bone area (Ct.Ar) and cortical bone thickness (Ct.Th) in 4 w Dox-off Camkk2Ob− mice increased by 3.6%, 11% and 6% (all p>0.5) respectively. Although Tt.Ar. and marrow area (Ma.Ar) were similar, Ct. Ar and Ct.Th increased by 18% (p=0.014) and 12% (p=0.006) respectively in 12 w Dox-on compared to 4w Dox-on male Camkk2ctrl mice (Fig. 5A, Bi–Biv). In contrast, these primary cortical bone parameters in 12 w Dox-off male Camkk2Ob− mice were similar to 4 w Dox-off males and those in 12 w Dox-on male Camkk2ctrl mice (Fig. 5Bi–Biv). Minimum and maximum moment of inertia (IMin and IMax) were enhanced by 20% and 18% (both p>0.05), respectively, at 4 w post-Dox withdrawal in male Camkk2Ob− mice, compared to age-matched Camkk2ctrl mice, but were similar to those in 12 w Dox-off male Camkk2Ob− mice (Fig. 5Ci–Cii). IMin and IMax were significantly higher in 12 w Dox-on male Camkk2ctrl mice compared to those 4 w Dox-on control mice but were not different from those in 12 w Dox-off male Camkk2Ob− mice (Fig. 5Ci–Cii).

  In females, cortical bone mid-diaphyseal geometric properties were similar between 4 w Dox-off Camkk2Ob− and 4 w Dox-on Camkk2ctrl mice. Marrow area was significantly lower, whereas Ct.Ar and Ct.Th were significantly higher in both 12 w Dox-on Camkk2ctrl and Dox-off Camkk2Ob− female mice compared to 4 w Dox on and Dox-off Camkk2Ob− mice, respectively (Fig. 6Bi–Biv). Female mice displayed age-dependent and Dox-independent increase in IMin but not IMax, whereas IMax was significantly higher in 12 w Dox-off compared to 4 w Dox-off female Camkk2Ob− mice (Fig. 6Ci–Cii).

  Dynamic histomorphometry assessment of bone formation at the cortical bone periosteal surface revealed significantly higher MS/BS (79%) in 4 w Dox-off male Camkk2Ob− compared to 4 w Dox-on Camkk2ctrl femurs, whereas no changes were observed when comparing male 12 w Dox-off and Dox-on cohorts (Supp. Fig. 5A). Male 12 w Dox-on Camkk2ctrl femurs displayed significantly higher MS/BS compared to 4w Dox-on control mice, but this did not translate to increased MAR or BFR (Supp. Fig. 5A–C). Further, whereas MAR was not different between the 4 w Dox-off and Dox-on male cohorts, BFR showed a significant increase (119%) in 4 w Dox-off male Camkk2Ob− compared to 4 w Dox-on Camkk2ctrl mice (Supp. Fig. 5B–C). Although, MAR and BFR were reduced in both 12 w Camkk2ctrl Dox-on and Camkk2Ob− Dox-off male mice compared to their 4 w Dox-on and Dox-off cohorts, MAR and BFR were significantly higher in 12 w Dox-off male Camkk2Ob− mice compared to 12 w Dox-on Camkk2ctrl mice (Supp. Fig. 5B–C), indicating that the acute stimulation of osteoblast function at the male cortical bone following the deletion of osteoprogenitor-derived CaMKK2 is maintained over time. In contrast, female cohorts did not display any significant differences in periosteal cortical bone mineralizing surface, MAR or BFR (Supp. Fig. 6A–C).

  We next performed three-point bending of femurs from 4 w and 12 w Dox-off Camkk2Ob− and Dox-on Camkk2ctrl male and female mice. Ultimate force, stiffness, and energy to fracture parameters of 4 w Dox-off Camkk2Ob− femurs from both sexes were comparable to those of sex and age-matched Camkk2ctrl femurs (Figs. 5Di–Diii and 6Di–Diii). Whereas femurs from 12 w Dox-on Camkk2ctrl male mice displayed significantly higher stiffness compared to the sex-matched 4 w Dox on cohorts, ultimate force and energy to fracture were similar between these cohorts. All three strength parameters assessed were similar between femurs from 12 w Dox-off male Camkk2Ob− mice and those from 4 w Dox-off Camkk2Ob− or 12 w Dox-on Camkk2ctrl male mice (Fig. 5Di–Diii). On the other hand, Osx-Cre activation for 12 w in Camkk2Ob− female mice resulted in significantly higher ultimate force (15%), stiffness (20%) and work-to-fracture (30%), compared to 4 w Dox-off Camkk2Ob−, but these parameters were similar in 4w and 12 w Dox-on control female mice (Fig. 6Di–Diii). Taken together, our data indicate that the conditional removal of CaMKK2 from osteoprogenitors enhances cortical bone osteoblast function in male but not female mice.


## Discussion
  In this study, we aimed to investigate a cell intrinsic role for CaMKK2 in the osteoblast lineage by conditionally targeting it, postnatally, in osteoprogenitors using the reverse Tet-regulated Osx-Cre-GFP mouse line in which the removal of Dox drives Cre recombinase activity in Osterix-positive cells present in the bone marrow and periosteum [25, 36, 37]. Here we report that the conditional loss of CaMKK2 in early osteoblasts stimulates their function which translates into an acute increase in trabecular bone mass in male and female mice. Whereas enhanced osteoblast function was sustained in both male and female mice, the increase in trabecular bone mass was sustained for up to 12 weeks following Cre activation only in female but not male mice. Further, the deletion of CaMKK2 in the osteolineage cells elicited a delayed upregulation of osteoclasts at the trabecular bone, indicating the coupling of bone formation to resorption [38, 39]. Although sustained stimulation of periosteal cortical bone osteoblast function was noted following osteoprogenitor-derived CaMKK2 deletion in male mice, this did not translate into increased cortical bone mass or strength. Altogether, our data indicate a sex-divergent role for CaMKK2 in osteoprogenitors in the regulation of their function and bone mass accrual at the cancellous and cortical bone compartments.

  Osterix-positive cells are present not only in the bone marrow and endosteum, but also in the periosteum where they have been shown to proliferate and contribute to bone formation [36]. Periosteal bone formation was enhanced acutely in male mice following CaMKK2 deletion in osteoprogenitors, but this was not the case in female mice. Modeling activity in the diaphyseal cortical bone is sexually dimorphic in the growing and aging skeleton [40, 41]. Androgens stimulate periosteal bone expansion in males whereas estrogen inhibits periosteal modeling but promotes endosteal bone formation. Thus, at puberty, female mice accumulate cortical bone mass from endosteal contraction with little periosteal expansion, whereas male mice do so through periosteal bone formation than endosteal expansion [40, 41]. This sexual dimorphism at the diaphyseal cortices is also evident in male and female Camkk2Ob− mice, as osteoprogenitor-derived CaMKK2 triggered periosteal bone expansion was present in male mice but not in females. However, this increased periosteal bone formation in male mice did not translate into enhanced cortical bone mass or strength. On the other hand, we observed an increase in IMAX and higher cortical bone strength in female mice following 12 w of CaMKK2 deletion in osteoprogenitors, compared to the same group that was weaned off Dox for 4 w, whereas these parameters did not differ when comparing age-matched mice. Although the mechanistic basis of these anomalies is unclear, we conclude that the deletion of CaMKK2 from osteoprogenitors affects cortical bone periosteal expansion in a sex-divergent manner.

  Following multiple reports of a bone-specific phenotype in Osx-Cre-GFP mice following Cre activation, we assessed trabecular and cortical bone parameters in “Cre-only” male and female mice weaned off Dox for 4 or 12 w in comparison to those that remained on Dox for 8 or 16 w. Trabecular bone mass was unaltered in Osx-Cre-GFP mice of either sex at 4 w post Cre activation, indicating that the acute increase in bone mass observed in Camkk2Ob− male and female mice was due to the conditional deletion of the kinase in osteoprogenitors. Whereas trabecular bone mass remained unaltered in female 12 w Dox-off Osx-Cre-GFP mice, it significantly decreased in males. Alterations in trabecular microarchitecture parameters at 12 w post Cre activation trended similarly in male and female Osx-Cre-GFP and Camkk2Ob− mice. Furthermore, since BFR increased following Cre activation in male and female Camkk2Ob− mice but did not differ between Dox-on and Dox-off Osx-Cre-GFP mice of either sex (data not shown), we conclude that the bone mass accrual observed in Camkk2Ob− mice is a direct effect of the ablation of CaMKK2 in osteoblasts.

  Whereas the increase in trabecular bone formation rate was sustained in Camkk2Ob− mice of both sexes, we observed a decrease in trabecular bone number and an increase in trabecular separation over time in Camkk2Ob− mice of both sexes. We attributed these changes in trabecular microarchitecture to the coupling of bone formation to resorption as a delayed upregulation of osteoclasts was observed in both sexes. Previous studies indicate roles for CaMKK2 in both the formation and resorption arms of bone remodeling, whereby the global deletion of the kinase or its systemic pharmacological inhibition using STO-609 in male and female mice led to an upregulation of osteoblasts and inhibition of osteoclasts, resulting in enhanced bone mass and strength, regardless of age [17, 18, 20]. Thus, ablation of both osteoblast and osteoclast-derived CaMKK2 is necessary to fully recapitulate the higher bone mass phenotype observed in young and older, male and female Camkk2−/− mice [18]. In this study, we posit that osteoblast-osteoclast coupling effect in the long-term, will neutralize any acute increase in osteoblast function and bone mass in Camkk2Ob− mice.

  Global deletion of CaMKK2 enhances osteoblasts and suppresses osteoclasts resulting in enhanced bone mass and strength in male and female mice [17, 18]. However, we recently reported that the conditional deletion of CaMKK2 in osteocytes using the 8 kb Dmp1-Cre mice resulted in enhanced trabecular bone mass only in female mice due to an osteocyte-dependent regulation of osteoclasts via secreted calpastatin [20]. Female but not male Dmp1-Cre::Camkk2flox/flox mice possessed fewer osteoclasts. No differences in osteoblast function or cortical bone parameters were observed in either sex, when CaMKK2 was deleted from osteocytes [20]. In contrast, mice conditionally deleted for CaMKK2 in osteoprogenitors displayed enhanced osteoblast function and an acute increase in trabecular bone mass in male and female mice, but this was sustained for 12 weeks only in female but not male mice. On the other hand, cortical bone formation at the periosteal surface was increased only in male Camkk2Ob− mice, but this did not translate into increased strength. Unlike mice with a deletion of osteocyte-derived CaMKK2, osteoclast numbers were enhanced in both male and female Camkk2Ob− mice.

  A limitation of our study is that we only examined the acute effects of postnatal ablation of osteoprogenitor-derived CaMKK2 on bone mass and strength in male and female mice at 8 and 16 weeks of age, when their skeleton was in growth phase [42]. Examining the skeletal effects in aged Osx1-GFP-Cre:Camkk2 flox/flox mice would have entailed prolonged exposure of the mice to Dox to suppress the Osx-GFP-Cre driver until the mice are older. However, extended exposure to tetracycline results in apoptosis of osteoclasts and inhibition of bone resorption, per studies conducted on male animal models [43–47]. Indeed, male Osx1-GFP-Cre:Camkk2 flox/flox mice that remained on Dox for 12 w displayed a significant reduction in osteoclasts than sex-matched cohorts that were exposed to Dox for only 4 w post-weaning, although this effect was not observed in females. Thus, any skeletal outcome of CaMKK2 deletion in osteoprogenitors in aged male Osx1-GFP-Cre:Camkk2 flox/flox mice would be confounded by the effects of prolonged Dox exposure on osteoclasts and bone resorption.

  In conclusion, we identified a novel cell-intrinsic and sex-divergent role for CaMKK2 in osteoprogenitors in the regulation of osteoblast function and bone formation at the trabecular and cortical compartments of the skeleton in male and female mice. When combined with suppression of bone resorption, CaMKK2 inhibition can be therapeutically leveraged as a bone anabolic therapy.
