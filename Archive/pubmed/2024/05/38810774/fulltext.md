# Structural Adaptation of Secondary p53 Binding Sites on MDM2 and MDMX


## Abstract
  The thermodynamics of secondary p53 binding sites on MDM2 and MDMX were evaluated using p53 peptides containing residues 16–29, 17–35, and 1–73. All the peptides had large, negative heat capacity (ΔCp), consistent with the burial of p53 residues F19, W23, and L26 in the primary binding sites of MDM2 and MDMX. MDMX has a higher affinity and more negative ΔCp than MDM2 for p5317-35, which is due to MDMX stabilization and not additional interactions with the secondary binding site. ΔCp measurements show binding to the secondary site is inhibited by the disordered tails of MDM2 for WT p53 but not a more helical mutant where proline 27 is changed to alanine. This result is supported by all-atom molecular dynamics simulations showing that p53 residues 30–35 turn away from the disordered tails of MDM2 in P27A17-35 and make direct contact with this region in p5317-35. Molecular dynamics simulations also suggest that an intramolecular methionine-aromatic motif found in both MDM2 and MDMX structurally adapts to support multiple p53 binding modes with the secondary site. ΔCp measurements also show that tighter binding of the P27A mutant to MDM2 and MDMX is due to increased helicity, which reduces the energetic penalty associated with coupled folding and binding. Our results will facilitate the design of selective p53 inhibitors for MDM2 and MDMX.


## Introduction
  The tumor suppressor p53 is a guardian of the genome and is mutated in 50–60% of human cancers.1-5 It is negatively regulated by the E3 ubiquitin ligase MDM2 and a homolog of MDM2, MDMX.6-9 The transactivation domain (TAD) of p53 binds to MDM2 and MDMX with a KD in the nanomolar range.10-12 The TAD is divided into two disordered subdomains, TAD1 (1–40) and TAD2 (41–60).13,14 Residues 19–25 of TAD1 fold into an amphipathic helix upon binding MDM2 and residues 18–25 form a helix when bound to MDMX.6,15-19 TAD1 fluctuates between disordered and helical conformations and when a conserved proline at position 27 is mutated to alanine (P27A) the low transient helicity for p53 TAD1 in the unbound state increases.20,21,6 P27A binds more tightly to MDM2, which disrupts p53-mediated cell cycle arrest following DNA damage.6 The tighter binding of P27A to MDM2 was attributed to a smaller energetic penalty for coupled folding and binding.6

  P53 binds MDM2 and MDMX in a hydrophobic pocket composed of α-helices.17,18 In addition to the primary p53 binding sites on MDM2 and MDMX, there is emerging evidence for the presence of secondary binding sites22-24 and evaluating the binding behavior of the secondary sites will be valuable for selective inhibitor design. The p53 binding domains of MDM2 and MDMX are flanked by disordered regions. The N-terminal lid of MDM2 can partially occlude the p53 site and MDMX has a similar lid that requires a post-translational modification.25-27 The C-terminal disordered tail of MDMX appears to have no effect on p53 binding unless the autoinhibitory WW motif is included.28 The role of the MDM2 C-terminal tail binding to p53 is not known.

  Numerous protein folding studies have used heat capacity (ΔCp) measurements to confirm the hydrophobic effect is a major driving force for folding and this approach is now being used to interrogate the role of the hydrophobic effect and estimate the number of residues that are folding when intrinsically disordered regions (IDRs) bind to their partners.29-33 ΔCp is the amount of heat required to change one unit of mass by one unit of temperature.34 ΔCp can be determined using the temperature dependence of ΔH, which can be measured with isothermal titration calorimetry (ITC).35,36 In turn, ΔCp and ΔH can be used to estimate changes in accessible surface area for nonpolar (ΔASAnp) and polar (ΔASAp) residues.32,35,37,38 A more negative ΔCp implies an increase in the burial of nonpolar surface area and/or an increase in the exposure of polar surface area.35 ΔASAnp is then used to calculate solvent entropy (ΔSsolv), which is needed to estimate the number of residues that are folding upon binding.30,32,35

  In this study, we use ITC, NMR, and all-atom molecular dynamics (MD) simulations to investigate the binding of p53 to MDM2 and MDMX using fragments that include a TAD1 sequence commonly used for structural studies (residues 16–29) and two longer sequences (residues 17–35 and 1–73).17,18 Previous studies have shown that including residues 30–35 results in increased binding to MDMX but the effect on MDM2 has not been tested and a comprehensive assessment of binding thermodynamics for MDM2 and MDMX has not been performed.17,23,39,40


## Results

### Thermodynamics of p53 binding to MDM2
  A short segment of p53 TAD1 forms a stable, amphipathic helix when bound to either MDM2 (residues 19–25) or MDMX (residues 18–25).17,18,20 Mutating proline 27 to alanine (P27A) increased the helicity of p53 TAD1 in the unbound state and increased the binding affinity to MDM2 by 10-fold.6 Based on these results we hypothesized that the increase in binding affinity for P27A was due to a reduction in the entropic penalty for forming a stable helix. To test this hypothesis, we used isothermal titration calorimetry (ITC) to measure the thermodynamics of several p53 fragments binding to MDM2 and MDMX. These fragments contained p53 residues 16–29, 17–35, and 1–73. The p5316-29 fragment contains the canonical transactivation motif that was initially identified to bind MDM2 and later MDMX.17,18,41,9 The p5317-35 fragment includes additional residues that are binding a secondary site on MDM2 and MDMX.22,23 The p531-73 fragment contains a second, weak, transactivating motif from residues 41-60.13,42 We also used two fragments of MDM2 containing either residues 17–125 or 25–109 and one fragment of MDMX containing residues 23–111. Both MDM225-109 and MDMX23-111 represent the minimal p53 binding domains while MDM217-125 contains a disordered lid at the N-terminus (residues 17–25) that dynamically occludes the primary binding site and a disordered tail at the C-terminus (residues 110–125) that is the beginning of a long-disordered region.17,27,43

  Table 1 shows the KD, ΔG, ΔH, −TΔS, and −TΔΔS values for the WT and P27A fragments of p53 binding to MDM225-109, MDM217-125, or MDMX23-111. The KD and ΔG values for p531-73 binding to MDM217-125 are consistent with previous measurements from our group and the KD and ΔG values for p5316-29 and P27A16-29 binding to MDM217-125 are consistent with a previous alanine scanning study of a p53 fragment containing residues 17-28.6,44 Table 1 also shows that p5317-35 and P27A17-35 have KD, ΔG, ΔH, and −TΔS values similar to p531-73 and P27A1-73, respectively. The 1–73 and 17–35 fragments bind more tightly to MDM2 than the 16–29 fragments but big differences in −TΔΔS are observed when p5316-29 and P27A16-29 (−2.79 kcal mol−1) are compared with p5317-35 and P27A17-35 (−0.38 kcal mol −1). The large negative value of −TΔΔS between p5316-29 and P27A16-29 is consistent with the increased binding affinity of the P27A mutant resulting primarily from a reduction in the conformational entropy of unbound P27A16-29. The small negative −TΔΔS for the 17–35 fragments suggests there is a process other than the coupled folding and binding of p53 contributing to the entropy change.

  To determine whether the differences in −TΔΔS were due to the presence of the N- and C-terminal disordered regions of MDM2 we performed ITC with p5317-35 and P27A17-35 and a fragment of MDM2 containing residues 25–109. Removing the tails from MDM2 caused a big change in −TΔΔS (−2.22 kcal mol−1) for the 17–35 peptides (Table 1). The magnitude and direction of the change suggests the tails are becoming more disordered when the 17–35 peptides bind to MDM217-125. It is known that the binding of p5317-29 to MDM217-125 displaces the disordered N-terminal lid from the primary binding pocket and we expect the 16–29 and the 17–35 peptides do the same.43 If this is the case, then binding of the 17–35 peptides induces an additional change to MDM217-125 that increases entropy, possibly due to displacement of the C-terminal tail.


### Thermodynamics of p53 binding to MDMX
  The binding affinity of p5316-29 for MDMX23-111 was comparable to MDM225-109 and increased 3-fold relative to MDM217-125 (Table 1). The results for p5316-29 are consistent with previous research on the binding of MDMX24-108 to p5315-29.39 The P27A16-29 affinity for MDMX23-111 was 6-fold lower than MDM225-109 and 2-fold lower than MDM217-125 (Table 1). The addition of residues 30–35 to WT p53 and the P27A mutant increased the binding affinity to MDMX23-111 by 8-fold and 10-fold, respectively, consistent with previous studies where p53 TAD1 was extended by a few residues.23,39

  The tighter binding of p5317-35 and P27A17-35 to MDMX23-111 suggests the presence of a secondary binding site that is either more available, makes more productive interactions than MDM225-109, or leads to stabilization of MDMX. Similar to MDM2, we observed a large difference in −TΔΔS between P27A16-29 and p5316-29 when binding to MDMX. The −TΔΔS between P27A16-29 and p5316-29 binding to MDMX23-111—2.53 kcal mol−1 and it is −1.86 kcal mol−1 binding to the 17–35 peptides (Table 1). We don’t think the N- and C-terminal tails of MDMX have the same effect as MDM2. MD simulations suggest that the N-terminal tail for MDMX can occlude the primary binding site of MDMX, but this requires phosphorylation at Y99.25 According to our recent work, the C-terminal tail has no effect on p53 binding to MDMX until the sequence for the autoinhibitory WW motif is included.28


### Heat capacity of p53 binding to MDM2 and MDMX
  The structures of p5315-29 bound to MDM217-125 and MDMX23-111 show significant burial of nonpolar surface area, including residues F19, W23, and L26.17,18 To determine whether the binding thermodynamics are consistent with these observations, we measured ΔH in relation to T and −TΔS in relation to T for all the p53 fragments binding to MDM217-125 and MDMX23-111 and for the 17–35 fragments binding to MDM225-109 (Figures 1 and 2). All of the parameters of these best fits are in supplementary Appendix A. We estimate heat capacity (ΔCp) as the slope of ΔH versus T (Figure 3 and SI Table 1). We observe large, negative ΔCp values for all p53 fragments binding to MDM217-125, consistent with a process driven by the hydrophobic effect. Interestingly, ΔCp of p5317-35 was less negative than p5316-29 when binding to MDM217-125. Removing the disordered tails of MDM217-125 makes the ΔCp for binding to p5317-35 more negative, indicating the disordered tails play some role in binding and surface area burial for p5317-35. If the tails become more disordered when p5317-35 binds to MDM217-125, we expect that removing the tails will make a positive contribution to −TΔS. Table 1 shows that removing the tails increased −TΔS by 2.4 kcal mol−1. The binding of P27A17-35 and p5317-35 to MDM225-109 have similar ΔCp values, which means there is some difference in the binding mode of the 17–35 peptides to MDM217-125.

  If we compare all the ΔCp values for p53 and P27A binding to MDM2 an interesting trend emerges. p5316-29 has a more negative ΔCp than P27A16-29 when bound to MDM217-125 while the ΔCp for p5317-35 is less and is about the same for P27A17-35. This behavior is related to the presence of the N- and C-terminal tails and when the tails are removed, the ΔCp for p5317-35 and P27A17-35 are very similar and they are less negative than the 16–29 fragments binding to MDM217-125. These results suggest two structural features of MDM2 upon binding either p53 or P27A 1) Displacing the N-terminal lid from the primary binding pocket contributes to the large, negative ΔCp for the 16–29 fragments. We think this is due to hydration of polar and charged residues because Showalter and Bruschweiler showed the lid is more dynamic when bound to p5317-29.43 2) We also think the ΔCp estimates support a model where p53 and P27A bind to slightly different regions of the secondary binding pocket for the 17–35 peptides. This is because p5317-35 binding to MDM217-125 has a less negative ΔCp than P27A17-35. The fact the ΔCp values for both 17–35 peptides binding to MDM225-109 are very similar suggest differences in the interaction with the C-terminal tail are responsible.

  The ΔCp for MDMX binding to the short and intermediate fragments of p53 follow the trend expected for adjacent binding sites, where increasing the length of the peptide leads to a more negative ΔCp. The ΔCp values for the p5316-29 and P27A16-29 binding to MDMX are similar to each other and p5317-35 and P27A17-35 binding to MDMX also have similar but more negative values (Figure 3 and SI Table 1). These results are consistent with additional hydrophobic burial for p53 residues 30–35 interacting with the secondary binding pocket and may explain the increased affinity MDMX has for the 17–35 peptides. It is also known that MDMX becomes more ordered when it binds to p53.45 This additional ordering and the subsequent burial of nonpolar residues could also contribute to more negative ΔCp values.


### MD simulations show different p53 binding modes at the secondary binding sites
  Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations of p5317-35 and P27A17-35 bound to MDM225-109 and MDMX23-109 (MDMX residues 110–111 are missing in the X-ray structure) were performed to evaluate the structural basis for the differences in our ΔCp measurements (Figure 4). According to the simulations, the N-terminal portions of p5317-35 binding to either MDM225-109 or MDMX23-109 are consistent with the X-ray structures of the respective complexes using p5315-29 (Figure 4).17,18 The remaining portion of p53 is mostly extended but residues 33–35 have low levels of helical propensity (SI Figure 1). In the case of MDM2, the C-terminal portion of p53 (residues 30–35) adopts a single dominant conformation and for MDMX the C-terminal portion exhibits substantial flexibility, which may contribute to the affinity enhancement of p5317-35 binding to MDMX versus MDM2 (Table 1).

  As expected, P27A17-35 is more helical when compared to p5317-35 (Figure 4 and SI Figure 1). When bound to MDM225-109, P27A17-35 has two helical regions. The N-terminal portion is similar to p5317-35 and the other region includes residues 28–33, (Figure 4a and SI Figure 1). One interesting feature of the MDM225-109 simulations consistent with the ΔCp measurements is based on the trajectory of p53 residues 30–35. For p5317-35, the trajectory of residues 33–35 points toward what would be the disordered tails of MDM2, and these residues point in the opposite direction for P27A17-35. When bound to MDMX23-109, the P27A mutation elongates the helix of the N-terminal portion, and this elongated helix induces a conformational resonance in the primary and secondary MDMX binding pockets (Figure 4b and SI Figure 1).


### Contact probabilities identify the secondary binding sites of MDM2 and MDMX
  Figure 5 shows the contact probabilities of MDM225-109 and MDMX23-109 with p5317-35 and P27A17-35. Amino acids in MDM225-109 and MDMX23-109 are considered to be in contact with amino acids in p5317-35 or P27A17-35 if the distance between any of their atoms is less than the sum of their respective vdW radii.46 We find that the contacts made by the N-terminal helical portions of p5317-35 in the primary site match those observed in the X-ray structures used to initiate the simulations. The C-terminal portion of p53 (residues 30–35) also makes substantial contacts with MDM225-109 and MDMX23-109. We refer to these regions of MDM225-109 and MDMX23-109 as the secondary binding site. For MDM225-109 the secondary binding site includes residues E25 (0.1), T26 (0.2), V28 (0.4), M50 (0.4), H96 (1.0), R97 (1.0), Y100 (1.0), T101 (0.8), Y104 (0.9), L107 (0.5), V108 (0.3) and V109 (0.6). The numbers in brackets are contact probabilities. For MDMX23-109 the secondary site includes residues V27 (0.8), V49 (1.0), Y99 (1.0), L102 (0.9), R103 (1.0), L106 (0.9), V107 (0.2), T108 (0.9) and L109 (0.1). Both secondary binding sites are larger than the one identified using a p53-like peptide (residues 17–29) that changed residues 27–29 from PEN to SGW.22

  The P27A mutation has a discernible effect on contact probabilities. In MDM2, the P27A mutation changes the contact probabilities of 11 residues by more than 20%, and in MDMX, the mutation changes the contact probabilities of 10 residues by more than 20% (SI Figure 2). The P27A mutation also alters the contacts of the N-terminal helical portions that binds to the primary sites, suggesting that the binding modes in the primary and secondary pockets are correlated. The differences in the contact probabilities of the residues surrounding the secondary binding site also support the possibility that the C-terminal tail of MDM217-125 is displaced when p5317-35 binds but not P27A17-35.


### Structural adaptation of the secondary binding sites
  To determine how p5317-35 and P27A17-35 binding affects the conformational ensembles of MDM225-109 and MDMX23-109, a machine learning method we developed called DiCE (Direct Comparison of Ensembles) was used to directly compare ensembles without any reduction in dimensionality.47-49 DiCE provides a normalized metric for each amino acid (ηaa) that represents conformational shifts in the ensemble. The value of ηaa is closer to 1 as the size of the conformational shift increases. Application of this method reveals that binding of the 17–35 peptides induce a minor adaptive response in MDM225-109 and MDMX23-109 (SI Figure 3). If we assume that conformational changes are in mean positions, and thermal fluctuations are on the order of 0.5 Å, then the highest values of ηaa for MDM2 and MDMX correspond, respectively, to shifts in mean positions by 1.65 Å and 1.1 Å. The minor adaptive responses are localized to the primary and secondary binding sites and appear to result from differences in the amino acid sequences of the C-terminal MDM2 and MDMX helices.

  In both MDM225-109 and MDMX23-109, P27 is anchored to a methionine-aromatic motif through hydrogen bonds. Such motifs are known to stabilize protein structures.50,51 We observe clear differences between the methionine-aromatic motifs of MDM2 and MDMX and how these motifs interact with p5317-35 and P27A17-35. The methionine-aromatic motif in MDM2 includes residues M50, Y100 and Y104 (Figure 6). The distance distribution of MDM2′s methionine-aromatic motif falls within the range (5–6 Å) that could lead to a stabilizing effect of several kcal/mol.50,51 The angle distributions also fall in the appropriate range (20-60°), as shown in SI Figure 4. The geometry of this motif undergoes only minor change upon p5317-35 binding, and so it appears to be structurally prepositioned to bind p5317-35. For MDM2, Y100 hydrogen bonds P27 with a contact probability of 0.93 and Y104 hydrogen bonds S33 with a contact probability of 0.50. These stabilizing interactions at both the mid-fragment and C-terminal portions may explain how MDM2 restricts the intrinsic dynamics of bound p53 but may also explain the restricted access to the secondary binding site. In P27A, the methionine-aromatic motif is mostly disrupted (Figure 6 and SI Figure 4). Y100 hydrogen bonds A27 with a contact probability of 0.77 and there is no longer a hydrogen bond between Y104 and S33. This may explain the increased conformational flexibility of the bound C-terminal portion of P27A17-35.

  The methionine-aromatic motif in MDMX is composed of residues M53 and Y99, and Y99 hydrogen bonds to P27 of p5317-35 with a low probability (0.13). The second aromatic group found in MDM2 is absent in MDMX. This may explain the higher flexibility in the structures of p5317-35 bound to MDMX compared to MDM2. In the event of P27A binding, the M53-Y99 motif is only partially disrupted, there is no hydrogen bond between Y99 and A27, and there is a new hydrogen bond between p53 residue N29 and MDMX residue Y99 (probability, 0.51).

  To determine whether the differences in binding affinity of p5317-35 and P27A17-35 to MDM225-109 and MDMX23-109 are the result of differences in secondary site chemistry we calculated hydrogen bonds and nonpolar surface area burial from the simulations. The number of intra-fragment hydrogen bonds increases when the 17–35 fragments bind to MDM2 and MDMX (SI Table 2). The P27A mutation further increases intrafragment hydrogen bonds, which corresponds to an increase in helicity. However, there are no discernible trends in the numbers of hydrogen bonds the 17–35 peptides make with MDM2 or MDMX to explain the affinity differences (Table 1, SI Table 2 and SI Table 3). The differences also cannot be attributed to the amount of solvent-exposed polar and nonpolar surface areas (SI Table 3). Nevertheless, we do note that MDM225-109 has a quantitatively larger adaptive structural response to P27A mutation than MDMX23-109, which may explain the larger effect that the mutation has on p53 binding affinity to MDM2 (SI Figure 5). The differences in structures we observed in the simulations prompted NMR spectroscopy titration experiments.


### NMR spectroscopy titrations for MDM2 and MDMX
  To assess structural changes at the secondary binding sites of MDM2 and MDMX, we measured the changes in amide 1H and 15N chemical shifts of MDM217-125 and MDMX23-111 in the absence and presence of a 2-fold molar excess of either p5317-35 or P27A17-35. Figure 7a and 7b show the averaged amide 1H and 15N chemical shift changes of MDM217-125 and MDMX23-111 upon binding to either p5317-35 or P27A17-35. SI Figure 6a and 6b shows the chemical shift correlation plots for p5317-35 and P27A17-35 binding to either MDM217-125 or MDMX23-111. Although the chemical shift changes in MDMX23-111 are overall smaller than those of MDM217-125, both show strong chemical shift changes outside the primary binding site along α2′ and support the presence of the secondary binding site. There are changes in chemical shifts greater than 0.1 ppm at residues 100, 103, and 105 in MDM217-125, and residues 98, 101, and 105 in MDMX23-111. The chemical shift changes have a good correspondence with contact probabilities from the MD simulations (Figure 5c and 5d) even though there are small differences in the chemical shift changes between WT p53 and the P27A mutant.

  As expected from previous work, the relatively large chemical shift changes for MDM2 residues 17–30 are due to displacement of the disordered N-terminal lid.27,43 Based on the differences in ΔCp values for p5317-35 and P27A17-35 binding to either MDM217-125 or MDM225-109 we expected larger chemical shifts in the C-terminal tail of MDM2 when bound to p5317-35. The fact that we do not observe large shifts in this region for either p5317-35 or P27A17-35 means any displacement of the C-terminal tail from the secondary binding site occurs without a major disruption in the local electromagnetic environment. This could happen if the C-terminal tail is dynamic and solvent exposed whether or not it occupies the secondary binding site.


### ΔASA from heat capacity and MD simulations
  SI Table 4 provides ΔASA estimates based on the ΔCp measurements. Criteria for choosing the cp and cnp coefficients used to estimate ΔASA are described in the methods. SI Table 5 lists ΔASA estimates from the MD simulations associated with complex formations of p5317-35 and P27A17-35 with either MDM225-109 or MDMX23-109. ASA values from the simulations are calculated using the Double Cubic Lattice Method with a solvent probe radius of 1.4 Å and atomic radii from Shrake and Rupley.52,53 The ΔASA values calculated from the MD simulations for MDM225-109 are similar to those obtained from ΔCp measurements using coefficients from Makhatadze and Privalov, including the trend associated with the P27A mutation (SI Table 4 and SI Table 5).54 However, for MDMX23-109, the values are smaller than those obtained from ΔCp measurements. It is known that MDMX becomes more ordered in the presence of p53 but here we provide the first evidence that this ordering is associated with the burial of nonpolar surface area.45 Since the simulations were initiated with the structure of MDMX bound to p53, it is likely that the effect of MDMX becoming more ordered when bound to p53 was not captured.


### Estimating the conformational entropy of unbound p53
  Based on the −TΔΔS values presented in Table 1, the differences in the binding affinity of p5317-35 and P27A17-35 are the result of changes in the conformational entropy of the disordered p53 peptide due to the P27A mutation. The similarity in the ΔCp values of p5317-35 and P27A17-35 binding to either MDM225-109 or MDMX23-111 also supports a prominent role for conformational entropy in binding. To furthers test whether the increase in transient helicity of unbound P27A reduces conformational entropy we measured 15N backbone relaxation data for non-proline residues of p531-73 and P27A1-73, and estimated order parameters (S2) based on the model-free formalism developed by Lipari and Szabo.55 The 1–73 fragments were used so the 15N-labeled protein samples could be recombinantly expressed. The average values of R1, R2, het-NOE and S2 are given in SI Table 6 and residue specific values are provided in SI Figures 710. Solution state S2 values calculated using the model-free formalism are generally interpreted in terms of N─H bond vector flexibility where a higher S2 value means less flexibility.56 As expected, the S2 values show that P27A has less flexibility in the MDM2/MDMX binding site than WT (Figure 8a and SI Figure 10). This is due to the increase in transient helicity and is consistent with previous work from our group.6 Figure 8b shows S2 values calculated from the MD simulations of unbound p5317-35 and P27A17-35. As expected, these values are smaller than the NMR values for p531-73 and P27A1-73, but the trend is consistent.

  We used the S2 values for p531-73 and P27A1-73 from NMR to estimate changes in conformational entropy based on a method developed by Yang and Kay and later simplified by Wand (see methods).57,58 In order to use the S2 values of the unbound 1–73 fragments for this calculation, we assume the order parameters for residues 17–35 in the bound state are similar for WT and P27A. This assumption is consistent with the high correlation coefficients in SI Figure 6 and with the similar ΔCp values of p5317-35 and P27A17-35 bound to either MDM225-109 or MDMX23-111 (Figure 3).

  The −TΔΔS values measured by ITC and −TΔΔS estimated from the S2 values of unbound p53 and P27A are of the same order. The −TΔΔS from the ITC measurements of P27A17-35 and p5317-35 binding to MDM225-109 is −2.22 kcal mol−1 and −1.86 kcal mol−1 for binding to MDMX23-111 while −TΔΔS for residues 17–35 calculated using the S2 values of the unbound 1–73 fragments was −1.43 kcal mol−1, which we consider an excellent correspondence between the two methods.


### Residues folding upon binding
  To calculate the number of residues that fold upon binding we assume the total entropy change for the system is ΔS=ΔSconf+ΔSsolv+ΔSr∕t, where ΔSconf is the change in conformational entropy due to the folding of IDR residues, ΔSr∕t is the change in rotational and translational entropy for bimolecular interactions (−26.3 cal mol−1 K−1), and ΔSsolv is the change in solvation entropy due to the hydrophobic effect (see eq. (8)).30,32,35,54 If we ignore other contributions to entropy, including (de)protonation or (de)solvation of charges associated with binding, then ΔSconf can be calculated at the isoentropic temperature and then used to calculate the number of residues that fold upon binding assuming the average loss of ΔSconf per-residue is −5.74 cal mol−1 K−1 (SI Table 7).30,32,59-61 All values in SI Table 7 are slightly greater than the number of residues in the 17–35 peptide, which suggests some of the residues that fold upon binding are from MDM223-109 or MDMX23-111. In particular, the higher number of residues calculated to fold upon binding for MDMX23-111 is consistent with our earlier conclusion based on the ΔCp measurements that the folding and stabilization of MDMX23-111 is resulting in the burial of nonpolar residues.


## Conclusions
  We performed a comprehensive thermodynamic evaluation of the secondary p53 binding sites on MDM2 and MDMX. The interpretation of ΔCp measurements for all 6 p53 peptides binding to MDMX23-111 is straightforward. ΔCp becomes more negative when residues 30–35 are added to either the p53 or P27A peptide (Figure 3). This trend does not continue for the 1–73 peptides and our data indicates we have maximized surface area burial and binding affinity with the 17–35 peptides (Table 1 and Figure 3). ΔCp for p5316-29 and P27A16-29 are almost equivalent and so is ΔCp for p5317-35 and P27A17-35. This means that differences in the binding affinities for the p53 and P27A peptides are due to other contributions, like the differences in conformational entropy of the unbound peptides. Regardless of length, unbound P27A is more helical than p53 and will have a smaller energetic penalty associated with folding and binding to MDMX. This is directly reflected in the −TΔΔS values presented in Table 1.

  Like many prolines in disordered regions, P27 is highly conserved, and we previously designed the P27A mutation to maximize helicity and minimize the effects of introducing a new side chain.6,62 Out of almost 30,000 p53 variants found in human tumors, only four mutations have been identified in codon 27.63 Three of these are frameshift mutations and one is a missense mutation that changes the proline to a leucine.63 High conservation and a low frequency of missense mutations in the p53 tumor variant database suggest that mutations at codon 27 may be lethal during development. Interestingly, the human orthologues of p53, p63 and p73, also bind MDM2/MDMX and contain proline residues following the MDM2/MDMX binding motif.64,65

  The ΔCp for all 6 p53 peptides binding to MDM217-125 showed differences that are not explained by changes in nonpolar surface area burial due to increasing the length of the peptide. In fact, the binding of p5316-29 to MDM217-125 is 101.8 cal mol−1 K−1 more negative than the binding of p5317-35 while the ΔCp values for P27A16-29 and P27A17-35 are within the margin of error. This means there is a process that occurs when p5317-35 binds to MDM217-125 that does not occur for p5316-29 or either P27A peptide. Previous research showed the N-terminal lid of MDM2 forms a dynamic helix in the primary binding pocket of MDM217-125 that becomes disordered upon binding of p5317-29.43 We assume this is also happening with the 16–29 and 17–35 peptides. Removing both disordered MDM2 tails has opposite effects on the ΔCp of p5317-35 and P27A17-35 (−27.5 cal mol−1 K−1 versus 53.4 cal mol−1 K−1) but their final values are almost identical. This trend can be explained if we consider displacement of both the N-terminal lid and C-terminal tail of MDM2 is playing a role in binding of the 17–35 peptides, and ultimately full length p53. The C-terminal tail stabilizes the binding of P27A17-35 relative to p5317-35. Based on this model we propose the N-terminal lid of MDM217-125 is displaced in all binding events, but the C-terminal tail is only displaced by p5317-35 and p531-73 and results in a less negative ΔCp (Figure 9).

  Our experimental measurements are largely consistent with the MD simulations. Contact probabilities from the simulations show a good correspondence with chemical shift changes from the NMR spectroscopy titration experiments. The ΔASA values calculated from the simulations are very close to the estimates from ΔCp measurements for MDM225-109 but are lower than the estimates from ΔCp for MDMX23-111. This is probably due to folding that occurs for MDMX23-111 when it binds p53, which could explain why p5317-35 binds MDMX23-111 with higher affinity than MDM225-109 even though the simulations show similar DASA values and hydrogen bond numbers.45 The simulations also suggest a novel mechanism for structural adaptation at the secondary binding sites. Minor differences in the MDM2 and MDMX sequences adjust the position of a methionine aromatic motif that makes it sterically unfavorable for WT p53 to access the secondary site on MDM2 but this is not the case for P27A or either sequence binding to MDMX. We think the results from this study can be used for selective inhibitor design by leveraging the differences between the binding of more physiological p53 fragments to either MDM2 or MDMX.

### Expression and purification of MDM2, MDMX, and p53 for NMR and ITC
  MDM2 cDNA constructs encoding residues 17–125 and 25–109 as well as MDMX cDNA constructs encoding residues 23–111 were expressed using the pGEX-6p-2 vector (GE Healthcare), which has a GST tag, transformed into BL21(DE3) cells. The MDM2 fragments were grown in media consisting of 2.3 mM sodium phosphate dibasic, 22 mM potassium phosphate monobasic, 8.6 mM sodium chloride 2 mM magnesium sulfate, 10 μM calcium chloride, 10 μM iron (III) chloride, 3.8 μM thiamine, 11.1 mM D-glucose, 18.7 mM ammonium chloride, and 143.1 μM ampicillin. The media used for 2D NMR studies replaced the ammonium chloride with 15N ammonium chloride (Cambridge Isotope Laboratories NLM-467). The MDMX fragment was grown in media consisting of 10 mM potassium phosphate monobasic, 40 mM potassium phosphate dibasic, 25 mM sodium chloride, 275.5 μM potassium sulfate, 74 μM iron chloride, 501 nM calcium chloride, 26 nM boric acid, 81 nM manganese chloride, 30 nM cobalt chloride, 9 nM copper chloride, 998 nM zinc chloride, 1 μM sodium molybdate, 527 μM magnesium chloride,.3.8 μM thiamine, 11.1 mM D-glucose, 18.7 mM ammonium chloride, and 143.1 μM ampicillin. The media used for 3D NMR studies replaced the ammonium chloride with 15N ammonium chloride (Cambridge Isotope Laboratories NLM-467) and the D-glucose with 13C D-glucose (Cambridge Isotope Laboratories CLM-13967). Overnight cultures were used to start fresh cultures the next day at OD600 of 0.04 and were grown to an OD600 of 0.6 and then induced with 1 mM IPTG at 288.15 K for 18 h for MDMX23-111, 288.15 K for 24 h or 298.15 K for 5 h for MDM217-125, 288.15 K for 21 h for MDM225-109. Cultures were pelleted and frozen at 193.15 K. Pellets were re-suspended and lysed via French press in 50 mM Tris, 300 mM sodium chloride, 2.5 mM EDTA, 2 mM DTT, and 0.02% sodium azide, pH 7.4 in the presence of ThermoScientific Pierce protease inhibitors (A32963). GST tagged protein was then purified by passing the soluble fraction of lysate through a glutathione Sepharose column (GE 17513201) and eluted with 10 mM reduced glutathione. The GST tag was cleaved by incubation with the GST tagged HRV3C protease overnight at 277.15 K, the glutathione was dialyzed away, and the cleaved tag was removed via a second glutathione Sepharose column. The flowthrough is further purified via a Superdex 75 size exclusion column (GE 28989333) in a buffer of 50 mM sodium phosphate monobasic, 300 mM sodium chloride, 1 mM EDTA, 2 mM DTT, 0.02% sodium azide, pH 7.0.

  p531-73, P27A1-73, and PtoA1-73 cDNA constructs were expressed using pET28a vector (NEB), which has a HIS tag, transformed into BL21(DE3) cells and grown in media consisting of 2.3 mM sodium phosphate dibasic, 22 mM potassium phosphate monobasic, 8.6 mM sodium chloride 2 mM magnesium sulfate, 10 μM calcium chloride, 10 μM iron (III) chloride, 3.8 μM thiamine, 11.1 mM D-glucose, 18.7 mM ammonium chloride, and 51.5 μM kanamycin. ### Isothermal titration calorimetry (ITC) experiments
  ITC experiments were performed on a GE MicroCal VP-ITC in 50 mM sodium phosphate monobasic, 150 mM sodium chloride, 1 mM EDTA, 0.02% sodium azide, 8 mM 2-mercaptoethanol, pH 6.8 at their respective temperatures ± 0.15 K. Prior to titrations, cell and titrating samples greater than 3500 kDa (p531-73, P27A1-73, PtoA1-73, MDM217-125, MDM225-109, and MDMX23-111) were co-dialyzed in the aforementioned buffer for 40–48 h at 277.15 K ± 1 K and then de-gassed. Samples less than 3500 kDa (p5316-29, P27A16-29, p5317-35, and P27A17-35) were lyophilized and resuspended in the macromolecule dialysis ITC buffer. MDM217-125, MDM225-109, and MDMX23-111 were prepared at 5 μM in the cell and titrated with 50 μM of p531-73, P27A1-73, PtoA1-73, p5316-29, P27A16-29, p5317-35, and P27A17-35 (MDM225-109 was titrated with only p5317-35 and P27A17-35). An initial injection of 4.8 μl was followed by 28, 10 μl injections of the p53 peptides. Data were analyzed with a single site binding model using Microcal Origin software (7.0). As typical for processing of ITC data, the integrated values of injections 1 and 2 were removed. In rare cases, an outlier titration point, resulting from an air bubble, that skewed the sigmoidal curve was removed (SI Figure 11). Following baseline correction and integration of the measured heat values, the data were fitted using the non-linear least square curve-fitting algorithm for a single binding site. The values for the KD, ΔG, ΔH, −TΔS, and N from two to four titrations were averaged and the standard deviations were calculated. Controls and sample titrations are shown in SI Figures 12 and 13.

### Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations
  We carried out a total of 52 μs of MD simulations in this study; a complete list of which is provided in SI Table 8. Specifically, we conduct MD simulations of p5317-35 and P27A17-35 complexed individually with MDM225-109 and MDMX23-109. Each of the four complexes is simulated for 2.5 μs. Starting coordinates of the MDM2 – p53 complex are taken from PDB ID: 1YCR.17 This X-ray structure contains a shorter p53 fragment (residues 17–29), and the coordinates of the remaining residues (30–35) are built using PyMoL as a fully elongated chain.70 Starting coordinates of the MDMX – P27A complex are taken from PDB ID 3DAB.18 The X-ray structure of this complex also has a shorter p53 fragment (residues 17–28), and the coordinates of the remaining residues (29–35) are built just like we added missing residues in MDM2 – p53. Note that residues 110 and 111 are not resolved in the MDMX23-109 crystal structure and are not included in the simulations. Starting coordinates of the MDM2 – P27A17-35 and MDMX – P27A17-35 complexes are the same as those of their respective WT complexes, except here proline 27 is substituted with alanine. The N- and C-termini of MDM225-109, MDMX23-109, as well as the p5317-35 and P27A17-35 fragments are capped using ACE and NME, respectively. In addition, we conduct MD simulations of the apo states of MDM225-109 and MDMX23-109. These are simulated for 1 μs. The starting coordinates of apo states are the same as those in their respective complexes with p5317-35 fragments.

  Conformational ensemble shifts are computed using our machine learning based method, DiCE (Direct Comparison of Ensembles), and reported in terms of a normalized metric (ηaa) that varies between 0 and 1 (SI Figure 3). Larger the conformational ensemble shift, the closer the value of ηaa is to 1. To compute ηaa, conformational ensembles for each of the 4 states (apo MDM2, p53-bound MDM2, apo MDMX and P27A-bound MDMX) are constructed by extracting 10,000 snapshots at regular intervals from the final 1 μs of MD trajectories. To remove global rotational and translational effects, all conformations are superimposed to the final snapshots of their respective bound states. Choosing a different reference conformation for superimposition, including that from the apo state does have a minor effect on the computed ηaa, but it does not change any of our conclusions. The structures are superimposed by least square fitting backbone atoms. After fitting, ηaa is computed for each amino acid (aa), and hydrogens are excluded from analysis. For SI Figure 5, backbone deviations (dbb) are transformed to the Hilbert space where ηaa are computed.71

  Finally, we also carry out MD simulations of unbound p5317-35 and P27A17-35 fragments. Since they are intrinsically disordered, they are expected to undergo substantial conformational rearrangements in their unbound states. To enhance conformational sampling, we carry out 10 separate 1 μs long MD simulations for each of the isolated p5317-35 and P27A17-35 fragments. Each MD simulation was started from a different conformation, which were extracted in an unbiased manner from 100 ns long MD simulations of these fragments carried out at a high temperature of 500 K and a pressure of 1.01 bar. These starting structures are shown in SI Figure 16.

  All MD simulations are carried out in explicit solvent and 50 mM of NaCl. For simulations of MDM225-109, MDMX23-109, and their complexes with p5317-35 and P27A17-35 fragments, cubic box sizes are chosen whose lengths are at least 3 nm longer than the longest dimensions of the proteins or complexes. For simulations of unbound p5317-35 and P27A17-35 fragments, we chose a cubic box of length 9 nm as it is 2 nm longer than the end-to-end distance of a fully extended form of P27A17-35 (maximum possible end-to-end distance). Ionization states of amino acids are assigned to default values at neutral pH. In cases where proteins do not have a net neutral charge, the ratio of Na+ and Cl− is adjusted to get box charge neutrality. All simulations are carried out at constant pressure and temperature. Pressure is regulated at 1.01 bar using the Parrinello-Rahman barostat with a coupling constant of 1 ps.72,73 Temperature is regulated at 298 K using the velocity-rescale thermostat with a coupling time constant of 0.1 ps.74 Bonds are constrained, and a leapfrog integrator is used with a time step of 2 fs. Amino acids and water are described using the a99disp force field, which is a derivative of the amber99sb-ILDN force field, but whose dihedrals are tuned to better reproduce structural data of intrinsically disordered proteins and peptides.75,76 We also show that a99disp performs better than amber99sb-ILDN at reproducing the NMR order parameters of unbound p53 and P27A peptides (SI Figure 17). Ions are described using CHARMM22 parameters as prescribed for use with the a99disp.75 However, we do note that these ion parameters are not validated to reproduce the correct balance between ion-water and ion-protein interactions.77 Nevertheless, we do still use these ion parameters, and our main reasons to include them are for them to serve as a buffer and to maintain box charge neutrality. Electrostatics interactions are computed using PME with a direct-space cutoff of 1 nm. van der Waals interactions are computed only for pairs within a cut-off distance of 1 nm. All MD simulations are carried out using GROMACS version 2020.4.

  Second order N─H order parameters are determined as the limiting value of the autocorrelation function in eq. (5)49:  (5) S2=limt→∞〈P2[μ^(0)⋅μ^(t)]〉  where the unit vectors μ^ are orientations of N─H bonds, P2 is the second order polynomial, P2[x]=1∕2(3x2−1), and the angle brackets denote trajectory averages. Note that S2 reflects only the internal motion of the protein, and so prior to calculating correlations, we least square fit the backbone atoms of MDM225-109 and MDMX23-109 onto their 2.5 μs conformations, effectively removing translational and rotational motions.


### DASA calculations
  ΔASA was estimated using eq. (6) and (7):  (6) ΔH∗=(hnpΔASAnp)+(hpΔASAp)   (7) ΔCp=(cnpΔASAnp)+(cpΔASAp)  where ΔH∗ is ΔH at the temperature of enthalpic convergence (295.15 K).38 The coefficients for hnp and hp are −7.35 cal (mol Å2)−1 and 31.06 cal (mol Å2)−1, respectively. These coefficients were chosen because they were determined in the physiological temperature range (298.15 K).78 We used two different sets of cnp and cp coefficients to solve eq. (7). One set of coefficients were based on data from folding studies of 20 globular proteins (cnp and cp 0.502 cal (K mol Å2)−1 and −0.215 cal (K mol Å2)−1 respectively).54 We chose this set of coefficients because the large negative ΔCp values we observed were consistent with burial of a large amount of nonpolar surface area. The second set of cnp and cp coefficients were recalculated by Kragelund and colleagues using a collection of protein complexes where one of the partners is an IDR that folds upon binding (0.327 cal (K mol Å2)−1 and −0.143 cal (K mol Å2)−1, respectively).30 These recalculated coefficients are very similar to those developed by Spolar and Record for proteins folding and binding to DNA.32
