## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (preprint, bioRxiv)
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific claims, methodology, and interpretation as presented in the provided text; no external data or code verification
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors test whether machine learning models, specifically convolutional neural networks (CNNs), can predict cold-responsive differential gene expression in Arabidopsis thaliana using only proximal DNA sequence (upstream and downstream regions). They report moderate predictive accuracy, identify enriched sequence motifs in differentially expressed genes, and conclude that current sequence-only approaches are limited by biological complexity and require additional data types.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract, Introduction, Materials and Methods, Results, Discussion, figure legends (referenced but not shown), supplementary table references
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Figures, tables, supplementary materials, code, and exact model performance metrics are referenced but not provided; this limits quantitative verification of reported results

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript addresses an important and timely question: whether DNA sequence alone can predict gene expression responses to environment. The authors apply a reasonable computational pipeline (motif discovery with STREME, CNN training with hyperparameter grid search) to a published cold-response transcriptome dataset across five Arabidopsis accessions. The work is clearly presented and the authors are appropriately cautious in their conclusions. However, the central finding is modest predictive accuracy (best prAUC ~0.7), and the manuscript does not establish a clear advance over existing approaches. The analysis of what limits prediction is thoughtful but largely speculative. The manuscript would benefit from stronger benchmarking against simpler baselines, more rigorous statistical comparisons, and clearer presentation of the biological implications. The claims are generally supported by the described methods, but the absence of figures and supplementary data prevents full verification.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in plant genomics, regulatory genomics, and evolutionary biology would be interested. The work speaks to the feasibility of predicting expression responses from cis-regulatory sequence, a question relevant to understanding adaptation and to applied breeding for stress tolerance. The negative/limited results are informative for the community developing machine learning approaches for regulatory genomics.
- **Major strengths** 1) Addresses a significant and unresolved problem: predicting expression plasticity from sequence. 2) Uses a multi-genotype design, capturing natural variation. 3) Employs a rigorous training/validation/test split with gene family separation to avoid overfitting. 4) Includes permutation tests to assess significance. 5) Honest reporting of limitations and modest performance. 6) Clear writing and logical structure.
- **Major Concerns** 
  - R1-M1: The central claim of "moderate accuracy" is based on a single selected model per condition, and the manuscript does not report the distribution of performance across the 1,344 hyperparameter combinations. This makes it difficult to assess robustness and whether the reported performance is representative or an outlier.
  - R1-M2: The comparison to random expectation is incomplete. The permutation test establishes that the model performs better than chance, but the manuscript does not compare CNN performance to simpler baselines (e.g., k-mer frequency, motif presence/absence, or linear models). Without such comparisons, it is unclear whether the CNN adds value over existing methods.
  - R1-M3: The biological interpretation of the motif enrichment results is underdeveloped. The authors report overlap with known TFBS but do not test whether these motifs are functionally relevant (e.g., via reporter assays or chromatin data). The claim that motifs "suggest their potential contribution to environmental response" is speculative.
  - R1-M4: The analysis of trans-effects (CBF genotypes) is presented as a test of whether homogenizing major trans variation improves prediction, but the results are described only qualitatively ("did not improve"). No statistical comparison is provided.
  - R1-M5: The manuscript does not report confidence intervals or variability measures for the prAUC estimates, making it impossible to assess the precision of the reported performance.
- **Minor Comments** 
  - R1-m1: The abstract states "14 and 15 motifs" but the Results section reports "14 motifs" for up-regulated and "15 motifs" for down-regulated; this is consistent but should be clarified in the abstract.
  - R1-m2: The description of the "spike" experiment is clear, but the choice of the 5bp sequence AAGGG and its placement at the first 5bp of the upstream region should be justified.
  - R1-m3: The Discussion mentions "language models" as a future direction, but this is not connected to any specific limitation identified in the current CNN approach.
  - R1-m4: The manuscript would benefit from a table summarizing the best-performing model architectures and their hyperparameters for each condition.
  - R1-m5: The GitHub repositories are mentioned, but the manuscript does not state whether the code and data are sufficient to reproduce all results.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1, R1-M2, R1-M4 (as described above)
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - Originality: Moderate. The application of CNNs to predict expression responses from sequence is not entirely novel, but the multi-genotype design and focus on environmental response are somewhat distinctive.
  - Scientific importance: Moderate. The question is important, but the limited predictive success and lack of a clear methodological advance reduce the potential impact.
  - Interdisciplinary readership: Moderate. The work bridges genomics, machine learning, and evolutionary biology, but the technical detail may limit accessibility to a broad audience.
  - Technical soundness: The methods are generally sound, but the lack of baseline comparisons and robustness analyses weakens the conclusions.
  - Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is clearly written, but the machine learning details may be challenging for readers without computational background. A brief explanation of CNNs and prAUC would help.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that "approaches to predict DEGs between specific environments based only on proximate DNA sequences require further development" is supported by the modest results, but the manuscript does not rule out that better models or feature representations could achieve higher accuracy.
- The claim that "the large number of cis regulatory variants across the genome in low frequency can decrease the accuracy in prediction" is speculative and not directly tested.
- The claim that "including more accessions may either marginally increase the noise and not improve the accuracy" is based on a single comparison and lacks statistical support.
- The claim that the CNN models "learned" cis-regulatory motifs is inferred from performance but not directly demonstrated (e.g., via interpretation methods like saliency maps).
