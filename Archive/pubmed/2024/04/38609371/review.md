## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text including abstract, introduction, results, discussion, methods, and figure legends as provided.
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, experimental design, data interpretation, modeling approach, and claims as presented in the provided text. No external data or supplementary materials were accessed beyond what is described in the text.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report single-molecule magnetic tweezers measurements of E. coli RNA polymerase (RNAP) transiting through protein roadblocks (Lac repressor at three operators of differing affinity, and EcoRI Q111 mutant). They find that assisting forces facilitate and opposing forces hinder passage through weaker roadblocks, but that opposing forces promote passage through strong roadblocks when backtracking is fast relative to roadblock dissociation, particularly with GreA. They propose a hybrid model with passive (waiting for dissociation) and active (backtracking-recovery cycling, "battering") pathways, supported by Monte Carlo simulations.
- **Visible evidence base** Main text Figures 1–5, Supplementary Figures 1–11, Supplementary Table 1, Methods section, statistical descriptions.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Raw trajectory data, full statistical output for all fits, detailed simulation fitting procedures (referenced to Supplementary but not fully visible), exact force calibration curves, and complete control experiments (e.g., no-roadblock controls, GreA-only effects) are not fully described in the provided text.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a carefully executed single-molecule study addressing a fundamental question in transcription elongation: how RNAP overcomes protein roadblocks. The experimental system is well-designed, and the force-dependent measurements reveal a non-intuitive result, namely that opposing force can promote passage through strong roadblocks when backtracking-recovery cycling is fast. The hybrid model is a useful conceptual framework. However, several technical and interpretive concerns must be addressed before the conclusions are fully established, particularly regarding the statistical treatment of pause distributions, the interpretation of the "battering" mechanism, and the generality of the proposed model.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in transcription elongation, single-molecule biophysics, gene regulation, and molecular motors. The work addresses a long-standing question of how RNAP overcomes DNA-bound obstacles, which is relevant to understanding transcriptional noise, gene expression control, and the interplay between transcription and chromatin structure. The force-dependent effects and the proposed reciprocating mechanism will be of interest to the biophysics community and to those studying transcription-coupled DNA repair and genome stability.
- **Major strengths** The experimental approach is technically demanding and well-executed, with real-time observation of individual RNAP complexes at roadblocks. The use of multiple roadblock affinities and the systematic variation of force direction and magnitude is thorough. The finding that 0.2 pN can significantly alter passage is striking and well-supported. The hybrid model is simple, testable, and provides a coherent explanation for the seemingly contradictory results across different roadblock strengths. The inclusion of GreA as a modulator of backtracking adds a biologically relevant dimension.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **R1-M1** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes, Axis: Statistical rigor / data interpretation, Claim pointer: The authors claim that pause time distributions are exponential and that characteristic times differ significantly between conditions. Evidence pointer: Figures 2, 3, 4, Supplementary Figures 4–7. Concern: The text states that pause times were "distributed exponentially" but also notes a "long tail" for LacI-O2 under opposing force without GreA, which was "much better fitted by a double exponential." This inconsistency is not fully resolved. The statistical comparison between conditions appears to rely on fitting characteristic times with 90% confidence intervals, but it is unclear whether the differences between conditions (e.g., 0.2 pN vs 0.7 pN) are statistically significant given the sample sizes and the fitting procedure. The number of events (N) is reported for some figures but not all, and the criteria for including or excluding tethers are not fully described. Why it matters: The central claims of the paper depend on quantitative differences in pause lifetimes and passage fractions across conditions. If the statistical treatment is not robust, the conclusions may not be reliable. Resolution test: Provide full statistical comparisons (e.g., likelihood ratio tests for single vs double exponential fits, bootstrap confidence intervals for characteristic times, and explicit p-values for pairwise comparisons between conditions). Report the number of independent experiments and tethers for each condition, and describe the criteria for data inclusion/exclusion in more detail.**
  - **R1-M2** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes, Axis: Model validation / parameter identifiability, Claim pointer: The authors claim that the hybrid model with parameters k1, k2, k3, and P1 recapitulates the experimental data across all conditions. Evidence pointer: Figure 5, Supplementary Figures 8–10, Supplementary Table 1. Concern: The model has four free parameters, and it is not clear whether they are uniquely identifiable from the data. The text states that "globally fitted values" were obtained, but the fitting procedure is only briefly described. It is unclear whether the model can produce the observed distributions with a unique parameter set, or whether different parameter combinations could yield equally good fits. The claim that the model "faithfully reproduced" the data is not quantitatively supported in the main text. Why it matters: The model is a key part of the paper's contribution. If the parameters are not identifiable, the mechanistic interpretation (e.g., the relative rates of backtracking and roadblock dissociation) is not well constrained. Resolution test: Provide a detailed description of the fitting procedure, including the objective function, optimization algorithm, and uncertainty estimates for the fitted parameters. Perform a parameter identifiability analysis (e.g., profile likelihood or bootstrap) to show that the parameters are constrained by the data. Show the simulated distributions overlaid on the experimental data for all conditions.**
  - **R1-M3** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No, Axis: Mechanistic interpretation / alternative explanations, Claim pointer: The authors propose that "reciprocating motion" enables RNAP to "batter through" roadblocks, and that this mechanism may also apply to promoter escape and elongation. Evidence pointer: Discussion section, Figure 5A. Concern: The "battering" mechanism is inferred from the observation that opposing force plus GreA promotes passage through strong roadblocks. However, an alternative explanation is that GreA simply rescues backtracked complexes that would otherwise remain stalled, and that the opposing force increases the probability of backtracking, thereby increasing the chance that GreA can act. The distinction between "battering" (active disruption of the roadblock) and "rescue followed by passive waiting" is not clearly made. The extension of the model to promoter escape and general elongation is speculative and not directly tested. Why it matters: The proposed mechanism is the central conceptual advance of the paper. If the data cannot distinguish between active disruption and passive rescue, the interpretation is weakened. Resolution test: Design an experiment or analysis that can distinguish between these mechanisms, e.g., measuring the force-dependence of the rate of roadblock disruption versus the rate of GreA-mediated rescue, or testing whether the model predicts specific correlations between pause durations and the number of backtracking-recovery cycles. Alternatively, soften the claims about the generality of the mechanism.**
  - **R1-M4** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No, Axis: Control experiments / confounding factors, Claim pointer: The authors state that "roadblock protein re-binding does not affect the observed pause times" based on heparin control experiments. Evidence pointer: Supplementary Figure 3. Concern: The heparin control is mentioned but not described in detail. It is unclear whether heparin was present throughout the experiment or added after roadblock binding, and whether the concentration was sufficient to fully sequester dissociated roadblock proteins. Additionally, the possibility that roadblock proteins can rebind from a locally high concentration near the DNA (e.g., due to nonspecific binding) is not addressed. Why it matters: If roadblock rebinding contributes to the observed pause times, the interpretation of the force-dependent effects could be confounded. Resolution test: Provide a detailed description of the heparin control experiments, including the concentration of heparin used and the timing of addition. Consider additional controls, such as using a roadblock protein with a much lower affinity or a competitor DNA in solution.**
- **Minor Comments**
  - **R1-m1** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Clarity, Affected element: Figure 1C, Evidence pointer: Figure 1C. Issue: The representative traces in Figure 1C are described as showing "pauses at LacI roadblock sites," but the y-axis label and the specific positions of the roadblocks are not clearly indicated. Required correction: Add clear labels to the figure indicating the positions of the promoter, roadblock, and terminator, and specify the direction of transcription (e.g., with an arrow).**
  - **R1-m2** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Completeness, Affected element: Methods, Evidence pointer: Methods section, "Statistics & reproducibility". Issue: The statement "No statistical method was used to predetermine sample sizes" is standard but the authors should also report how many independent biological replicates were performed and whether the results were reproducible across different batches of protein or DNA preparations. Required correction: Add a sentence describing the number of independent experiments and the reproducibility of the results.**
  - **R1-m3** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Interpretation, Affected element: Discussion, Evidence pointer: Discussion section. Issue: The claim that "backtracking significantly helps ECs evict DNA-bound proteins" is strong. The data show that opposing force plus GreA promotes passage, but the mechanism of "eviction" is not directly demonstrated. Required correction: Soften the language to "is consistent with" or "suggests" rather than "significantly helps."**
  - **R1-m4** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Technical detail, Affected element: Methods, Evidence pointer: Methods section, "Magnetic Tweezers Assays". Issue: The force calibration is described as being performed using "the lateral Brownian motion of the bead and the length of the DNA tether," but the specific method (e.g., equipartition theorem, power spectrum) is not stated. Required correction: Provide a brief description of the calibration method and cite the relevant literature.**
  - **R1-m5** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Model presentation, Affected element: Figure 5A, Evidence pointer: Figure 5A. Issue: The model diagram is described in the text but the figure itself is not shown in the provided text. The states and transitions are described in the legend but the visual representation would help the reader. Required correction: Ensure the figure is clear and self-contained, with all states and transitions labeled.**
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** The statistical treatment of pause time distributions needs to be more rigorous, with explicit comparisons between conditions and justification for the choice of exponential vs double-exponential fits. The model fitting procedure needs to be described in sufficient detail to assess parameter identifiability. The heparin control experiments need to be described more fully to rule out roadblock rebinding as a confounding factor.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - Originality: The work is original in its systematic single-molecule analysis of RNAP passage through roadblocks of varying affinity, and the finding that opposing force can promote passage is novel. The hybrid model is a new conceptual contribution.
  - Scientific importance: The question of how RNAP overcomes protein roadblocks is of fundamental importance in transcription. The results have implications for understanding transcriptional regulation, the role of backtracking, and the interplay between transcription and DNA-bound proteins. The work is likely to be of interest to a broad audience.
  - Interdisciplinary readership: The work bridges single-molecule biophysics, transcription biochemistry, and gene regulation. It should appeal to researchers in these fields, as well as to those interested in molecular motors and mechanobiology.
  - Technical soundness: The experimental approach is sound, but the statistical analysis and model fitting need to be strengthened to fully support the conclusions. The lack of detailed control descriptions is a concern.
  - Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is generally well-written and accessible. The abstract and introduction are clear. The results section is dense but understandable. The discussion is thoughtful and places the work in a broader context. Some terms (e.g., "battering") are evocative but may need clearer definition.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The core experimental findings are likely to be of interest, and the model is a useful framework. However, the statistical rigor and model validation need to be improved before the conclusions are fully established.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that "reciprocating motion also enables RNAP to break protein-DNA contacts that hold RNAP back during promoter escape and RNA chain elongation" is speculative and not directly supported by the data presented.
- The claim that the model "faithfully reproduced" the experimental data is not quantitatively demonstrated in the provided text.
- The claim that "as little as 0.2 pN can significantly change transit" is supported by the data, but the physiological relevance of this force magnitude is not discussed in detail.
- The claim that "backtracking significantly helps ECs evict DNA-bound proteins" is an interpretation that is not directly tested; the data are consistent with this idea but do not prove it.
