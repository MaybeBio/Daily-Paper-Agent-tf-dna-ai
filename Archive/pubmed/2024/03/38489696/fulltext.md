# Network pharmacology and molecular docking to explore the mechanism of a clinical proved recipe for external use of clearing heat and removing dampness in the treatment of immune-related cutaneous adverse events


## Abstract
  Immune-related cutaneous adverse events (ircAEs) will undermine the patients’ quality of lives, and interrupt the antitumor therapy. A clinical proved recipe for external use of clearing heat and removing dampness (Qing-Re-Li-Shi Formula, hereinafter referred to as “QRLSF”) is beneficial to the treatment of ircAEs in clinical practice. Our study will elucidate the mechanism of QRLSF against ircAEs based on network pharmacology and molecular docking. The active components and corresponding targets of QRLSF were collected through traditional Chinese medicine systems pharmacology database. GeneCards, online Mendelian inheritance in man, and pharmacogenomics knowledgebase were used to screen the targets of ircAEs. The intersecting targets between drug and disease were acquired by venn analysis. Cytoscape software was employed to construct “components-targets” network. Search tool for the retrieval of interacting genes/proteins database was applied to establish the protein–protein interaction network and then its core targets were identified. Gene ontology and Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes analysis was performed to predict the mechanism. The molecular docking verification of key targets and related phytomolecules was accomplished by AutoDock Vina software. Thirty-nine intersecting targets related to QRLSF against ircAEs were recognized. The analysis of network clarified 5 core targets (STAT3, RELA, TNF, TP53, and NFKBIA) and 4 key components (quercetin, apigenin, luteolin, and ursolic acid). The activity of QRLSF against ircAEs could be attributed to the regulation of multiple biological effects via multi-pathways (PI3K-Akt pathway, cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, JAK-STAT pathway, chemokine pathway, Th17 cell differentiation, IL-17 pathway, TNF pathway, and Toll-like receptor pathway). The binding activities were estimated as good level by molecular docking. These discoveries disclosed the multi-component, multi-target, and multi-pathway characteristics of QRLSF against ircAEs, providing a new strategy for such medical problem.


## 1. Introduction
  In the era of precision medicine, the application of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) has become one of the important strategies in the treatment of malignant tumors due to their outstanding clinical efficacy. Blocking the cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated antigen-4 and programmed death-1/programmed death ligand-1 pathways are the main mechanisms.[1] Because of the cross-reactive tumor antigenicity, where the targeted T-cell antigens exist in both tumor and normal tissue, the use of ICIs is often accompanied by the occurrence of immune-related adverse events.[2] The most frequently and earliest observed adverse event is dermatologic toxicity, with an incidence rate of 72%.[3] Dermatologic complications arise with diverse type, such as maculopapular rash, pruritus, psoriasiform dermatitis, lichenoid eruptions, vitiligo, Stevens-Johnson syndrome/toxic epidermal necrolysis, drug rash with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms, and acute generalized exanthematous pustulosis. Approximately 2% to 10% patients treated with ICIs will develop the severe (grade ≥ 3) immune-related cutaneous adverse events, which may impair their quality of life, induce treatment interruption, or even threaten survival.[4] Increasing studies have supported that the emergence of particular ircAEs is associated with a more beneficial clinical outcome, which underlines the necessity for effective interventions to maintain the intensity of ICIs therapy.[5–7] The current treatments are primarily based on topical steroids, antihistamines and systemic corticosteroids, but with multiple side effects (infection, hypertension, diabetes, etc).[8,9] Even 25% of ircAEs are corticosteroid-refractory.[10] Hence, it is essential to explore more effective and safer treatment measures for patients with ircAEs in order to complement the aforementioned limitations.

  According to the external treatment of traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) theory, ircAE is called “drug toxicity.” Patients with ircAEs always appear one or more symptoms, such as erythema, papules, purpura, blisters, desquamation, pain and pruritus, which can be ascribed to “wind-damp and heat-toxicity syndrome with blood-stasis.” Whereupon a clinical proved recipe for external use of clearing heat and removing dampness (Qing-Re-Li-Shi Formula, hereinafter referred to as “QRLSF”) is empirically originated from Si-Miao-Yong-An decoction, which is a classic formula and has been traditionally utilized externally for centuries to treat sore, carbuncle and ulcer that are similar to the symptoms of ircAEs.[11] Lonicerae Japonicae Flos (Jin-Yin-Hua), Figwort Root (Xuan-Shen), Angelicae Sinensis Radix (Dang-Gui), Coicis Semen (Yi-Yi-Ren), A. Dahurica (Fisch.) Benth. Et Hook (Bai-Zhi) and Licorice (Gan-Cao) are the components of QRLSF. Due to the effects of its components, QRLSF can be applied externally for wind-dispelling, dampness-removing, heat-clearing, detoxifying and blood-activating. Modern researches have proven that these 6 herbs are used for the treatment of cutaneous diseases, and have the properties of anti-inflammation, anti-oxidation, immunoregulation and antiplatelet aggregation,[12–17] which signifies that QRLSF has great potential in the therapy of ircAEs. In daily clinical practice, QRLSF has been successfully applied in our medical center to treat various types of skin toxicity induced by ICIs and also cured a case of Stevens-Johnson syndrome–toxic epidermal necrolysis, which is known as one of the severe ircAEs.[18] However, the mechanism of QRLSF in treating ircAEs still remains unclear.

  It is a great challenge to reveal its mechanism since the pathogenesis of ircAEs is very complex, which may involve multi-targets, and TCM usually works through multi-components. Network pharmacology provides a model of “disease-gene-drug,” which can construct and visualize the interaction network to elucidate the relationship between drug action and molecular targets.[19] This approach is really suitable for investigating the pharmacological evidence of TCM at the biomolecular level. Molecular docking is an effective tool to describe the recognition pattern between micromolecules (ligand) and macromolecular structures (receptor).[20] The active ingredients and disease targets screened by network pharmacology can be chosen as corresponding ligands and receptors. So the integration of network pharmacology and molecular docking benefits to the acceleration of target validation.[21]

  Here, we conducted a network pharmacological model to analyze the potential mechanism of QRLSF against ircAEs. In order to verify the predicted outcome, the molecular docking models with some potential targets were established. The detailed flowchart is shown in Figure 1.


## 2. Methods

### 2.1. Active ingredient screening and corresponding target prediction of QRLSF
  The terms “Lonicerae Japonicae Flos,” “Figwort Root,” “Angelicae Sinensis Radix,” “Coicis Semen,” “A. Dahurica (Fisch.) Benth. Et Hook” and “Licorice” were retrieved by using the traditional Chinese medicine systems pharmacology database (TCMSP) (https://old.tcmsp-e.com/tcmsp.php) to collect the active components of QRLSF.[22] Although oral bioavailability and drug-likeness (DL) are 2 important screening conditions for these ingredients, QRLSF is an external Chinese medicine prescription in clinical practice, which does not involve oral bioavailability related drug metabolism process. Therefore, its therapeutic phytomolecules that met the standard of DL ≥ 0.18 were selected.[23] TCMSP was continued to use to obtain the corresponding targets related to active ingredients of QRLSF. The acquired targets were limited to genes of “Homosapiens” species and “Reviewed” status, and their annotations were standardized through the universal protein resource database (https://www.uniprot.org/).


### 2.2. Collection of ircAEs targets
  The keyword “immune-related cutaneous adverse event” was searched through GeneCards (https://www.genecards.org/), online Mendelian inheritance in man (https://www.omim.org/), and pharmacogenomics knowledgebase (https://www.pharmgkb.org/) to screen the disease targets. The gene contents selected in these 3 databases were combined and the duplicates were deleted. The final relevant targets for ircAEs were acquired.


### 2.3. Target identification of QRLSF against ircAEs
  The screened targets relevant to QRLSF and ircAEs were overlapped by using venn analysis with R language software. The common genes could be identified as the potential targets involved in the process of QRLSF against ircAEs.


### 2.4. Construction of “components-targets” network
  The filtered data with regard to the active components and common targets were input into the Cytoscape software (version 3.8.0) to construct the regulatory network of QRLSF against ircAEs. The nodes represent the active molecules and targets, respectively. The edges connecting them indicate the modulated interrelationship. The active components in this network were ranked by degree values.


### 2.5. Construction of protein–protein interaction (PPI) network
  The common targets of QRLSF and ircAEs were imported into the search tool for the retrieval of interacting genes/proteins database (STRING) (version 11.5) (https://cn.string-db.org/) to create the PPI network. The organism was limited to “homo sapiens” and the medium confidence > 0.4 was set as the minimum required interaction score. The disconnected nodes in the network were hidden. As a result, a tab separated values (TSV) format file of PPI relationship was generated.


### 2.6. Core targets identification
  The data in TSV format obtained from STRING database were imported into Cytoscape software to visualize the PPI network. The topological parameters of this network were analyzed on the basis of Betweenness, Closeness, Degree, Eigenvector, Local Average Connectivity (LAC), and Network through CytoNCA plug-in. After double filtration according to the median value of each parameter, the core target network was screened.


### 2.7. Gene ontology (GO) and Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis
  In order to explore the biological function and signal transduction process of QRLSF against ircAEs, GO and KEGG enrichment analysis was performed to ascertain the biological process (BP), correlative cellular component (CC), molecular function (MF), and KEGG pathway-related evidences by using R language and Bioconductor software. The adjusted P < .05 was considered as the filter criteria and the outcomes were exhibited in the form of bubble. Subsequently, the chord diagrams of BP enrichment and important pathways were displayed. Besides, the corresponding targets related to QRLSF against ircAEs were marked on these pathway maps.


### 2.8. Molecular docking
  The top 4 active ingredients of QRLSF screened from the “components-targets” network which were also corresponding to PPI core network were selected as small molecular ligands. Their two-dimensional (2D) structures were downloaded from the PubChem database (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/), and then converted into three-dimensional (3D) structures and minimized their energy by ChemBio3D software (version 14.0.0.117). The targets from the PPI core network were chosen as macromolecular receptors and their 3D structures were acquired from Protein Data Bank database (https://www.rcsb.org/). The PyMOL Molecular Graphics System (version 2.5.4) was applied to remove the water molecules and original ligands of the downloaded receptors. Both the receptors and ligands were input into AutoDockTools software (version 1.5.7) for conventional processing and saved as “pdbqt” format files. Subsequently, the “grid option” tool was used to determine the parameters of the binding pocket. Ultimately, AutoDock Vina 1.1.2 was run to simulate molecular docking with the lowest binding energy and PyMOL software was utilized for visualization.


## 3. Results

### 3.1. Acquisition of active constituents and corresponding targets of QRLSF
  Through searching TCMSP database with the condition of DL ≥ 0.18, a total of 393 active ingredients in QRLSF were screened out, containing 90 in Lonicerae Japonicae Flos, 25 in Figwort Root, 11 in Angelicae Sinensis Radix, 18 in Coicis Semen, 45 in A. Dahurica (Fisch.) Benth. Et Hook, and 204 in Licorice. Afterwards, 3443 potential targets relevant to these active constituents were identified, including 806 corresponding to Lonicerae Japonicae Flos, 177 to Figwort Root, 103 to Angelicae Sinensis Radix, 76 to Coicis Semen, 224 to A. Dahurica (Fisch.) Benth. Et Hook, and 2057 to Licorice. After screening the Uniprot database with the condition of “Human” organism and “Reviewed” status and removing the duplicates, 151 active ingredients and 110 related targets were selected for further analysis (see Table S1, Supplemental Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/MD/L907 which illustrates the active ingredients and their related targets of QRLSF).


### 3.2. Potential targets of ircAEs
  By retrieving “immune-related cutaneous adverse event” as the keyword, 592 correlative targets were obtained from GeneCards, 19 from online Mendelian inheritance in man, and 1309 from pharmacogenomics knowledgebase database. Finally, 1720 targets corresponding to ircAEs were gained after merging the records of these 3 databases and deleting the duplicates (see Fig. 2A).


### 3.3. Common targets of QRLSF against ircAEs
  After overlapping the targets related to both QRLSF and ircAEs, 39 common targets that might be involved in the process of QRLSF against ircAEs were acquired for subsequent investigations (see Table 1 and Fig. 2B).


### 3.4. Construction of “components-targets” network for analysis
  Based on the previous study, 68 components were identified as main molecules acting on ircAEs (see Table 2). The “components-targets” network was established through inputting 68 phytomolecules and 39 common targets into Cytoscape software to show their complex interaction (see Fig. 3A). A total of 107 nodes and 178 edges were exhibited in the network, of which orange stood for Lonicerae Japonicae Flos, purple stood for Figwort Root, dark blue stood for Angelicae Sinensis Radix, yellow stood for Coicis Semen, red stood for A. Dahurica (Fisch.) Benth. Et Hook, green stood for Licorice, and wathet blue stood for targets. The nodes with more connections represented higher degrees in the network. Quercetin, apigenin, luteolin, and ursolic acid ranked top 4 in the phytomolecules with the degree values of 23, 15, 14, and 14, respectively, indicating that these 4 phytomolecules might be the key ingredients in the treatment of ircAEs (see Fig. 3B).


### 3.5. Establishment of PPI network and identification for its core targets
  The 39 common targets were uploaded into STRING database to obtain the PPI network. Under the certain conditions mentioned in “2.5,” the result was illustrated in Figure 4A, with 35 nodes and 346 edges. In order to determine the core network, a TSV format file of PPI was imported into Cytoscape software for double filtration. Thirteen nodes satisfying the requirement of “Betweenness > 3.67, Closeness > 0.55, Degree > 18, Eigenvector > 0.14, LAC > 11.50, Network > 12.90” (greater than the median value) were selected to build a new subnetwork with 128 edges. The subnetwork was filtered secondly with the condition of “Betweenness > 1.45, Closeness > 0.86, Degree > 20, Eigenvector > 0.28, LAC > 16, Network > 17.53” to screen the final core target network. The core network was composed of 5 nodes and 20 edges, including STAT3, RELA, TNF, TP53, and NFKBIA (see screening process in Fig. 4B–D). These targets were the candidates for molecular docking with the top 4 corresponding key ingredients screened from “components-targets” network.


### 3.6. GO and KEGG enrichment analysis
  The 39 targets involved in treating ircAEs with QRLSF were characterized by GO and KEGG enrichment analysis to explore the mechanism associated with the gene function and signaling pathways. There were totally 1367 GO terms, comprising 1287 items of BP, 10 items of CC, and 70 items of MF. As shown in Figure 5A, the top 10 of each category were displayed in the light of adjusted P value and gene count. Because the pathogenesis of ircAEs did not involve apoptosis, death and related activities of neurons, the result of BP we needed to focus on was mainly enriched in cytokine-mediated signaling pathway, response to peptide, cellular response to peptide, etc. CC was closely related to external side of plasma membrane, transcription regulator complex, membrane raft, membrane microdomain, etc. MF was primarily centralized on DNA-binding transcription factor binding, cytokine receptor binding, chemokine receptor binding, cytokine activity, chemokine activity, etc. Figure 5B displayed the enrichment relationship between BP and corresponding targets. A total of 107 signaling pathways were exported through KEGG enrichment analysis and the top 30 were demonstrated in Figure 5C. The common targets were highly enriched in Epstein-Barr virus infection, human cytomegalovirus infection, lipid and atherosclerosis, hepatitis C, etc., which were other diseases irrelevant to ircAEs. The reason for this phenomenon was that there were the same targets in the pathological process of different diseases. Complying with the results of the current literatures, we should pay more attention to PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, cytokine–cytokine receptor interaction, JAK-STAT signaling pathway, chemokine signaling pathway, Th17 cell differentiation, IL-17 signaling pathway, TNF signaling pathway and Toll-like receptor signaling pathway. Figure 5D showed the enrichment relationship between important signaling pathways and corresponding targets. Figure 6 exhibited the important signaling pathways involved in the process of QRLSF against ircAEs.


### 3.7. The assessment of molecular docking
  Quercetin, apigenin, luteolin, and ursolic acid were the key active ingredients screened from the “components-targets” network, and these phytomolecules were also corresponding to the 5 targets (STAT3, RELA, TNF, TP53, and NFKBIA) in the core network. Therefore, these critical targets were docked with their potential ligands. The docking scores which represented the binding affinities of the key components with the core targets were illustrated in Table 3. The docking loci and modes were presented in Figure 7. The lower score stood for the stronger affinity between the ligand and receptor.[24] The score <−4.25, <−5.0, and <−7.0 kcal/mol signified the existent, good, and strong binding activity.[25] The results displayed that all the docking scores of the phytomolecules with their corresponding targets were <−5.0 kcal/mol, revealing good to strong binding affinities and stable structures. In comparison to the other 3 phytomolecules (luteolin, quercetin, and ursolic acid) (see Fig. 7B–D), apigenin coupled with the site of LEU78, LEU115, and LEU131 in NFKBIA through hydrophobic interactions, displaying the score of −7.4 kcal/mol (see Fig. 7A). Quercetin and ursolic acid displayed stronger binding affinities with RELA which presented the scores of −7.8 and −7.9 kcal/mol (see Fig. 7G and H), respectively, compared to apigenin and luteolin (see Fig. 7E and F). Ursolic acid emerged strong binding affinity (affinity = −8.0 kcal/mol) with STAT3 through bounding to the ALA250 and LEU260 residues with hydrogen bonds and ARG350 residue with salt bridge interaction (see Fig. 7I). The total 4 active ingredients presented strong binding activities with TNF (see Fig. 7J–M) and ursolic acid achieved the best docking score (affinity = −11.4 kcal/mol) (see Fig. 7M). Also, they presented high binding energy with TP53, the docking scores of which ranged from −6.9 to −7.1 kcal/mol (see Fig. 7N–Q). These findings suggested that the key phytomolecules of QRLSF seemed to play a regulatory role in the core target network of ircAEs. Besides, the discoveries of molecular docking were in accordance with the results of network pharmacology selection, validating the effectiveness of network pharmacology in our investigation.


## 4. Discussion
  The frequent occurrence of ircAEs has brought great challenges to the clinical anti-tumor therapy and attracted the attention of the medical community. They are a series of skin inflammatory reactions, which seem to share pivotal mechanisms with non-ICI-related cutaneous disorders.[26] But unlike the cytotoxicity induced by the traditional chemotherapy drugs which is self-limited in duration, the characteristics of ircAEs are delayed and refractory due to the persistent activation of immune system, indicating persistent inflammatory responses.[27] Topics of limited efficacy and side effects of the existing treatment of ircAEs have promoted researchers to reconsider a new alternative therapeutic schedule. TCM occupies a unique role in treating dermatologic toxicity related to precise anti-tumor medication.[28] The previous research suggested that QRLSF might be the potential candidate for treating ircAEs, and its preliminary mechanism was worth exploration.[18] The difficulties in the study of TCM prescriptions consist in the diversity and complexity of their components. Network pharmacology, an integration of the system biology, bioinformatics and network science, can help the researchers to get rid of this dilemma by analyzing the association between drugs and diseases from the perspective of multichannel and multi-target biological network to elucidate the mechanism. Consequently, our study explained the potential mechanism of QRLSF acting on ircAEs systematically.

  In the system of “components-targets” network, quercetin, apigenin, luteolin, and ursolic acid were the ingredients with degree values ranked top 4. Tang J et al have demonstrated that quercetin can attenuate the inflammatory response and oxidative stress to promote the repair of inflammatory skin lesions.[29] It has been confirmed that apigenin is a potential therapeutic agent for immune-related skin diseases due to its significant anti-inflammatory effect and improvement of skin barriers.[30] Luteolin exerts its anti-oxidative and anti-inflammatory activities through regulating the functions of keratinocytes, fibroblasts and immune cells, which may be a candidate for treating immune-related inflammatory dermatosis.[31] López-Hortas L et al have reported that ursolic acid is a natural molecule characterized by antioxidant, antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and immunomodulatory effects, which can enhance the defensive capability of the skin barrier to stimulus.[32] These phytomolecules are speculated as the major chemical ingredients that may play a significant role in the process of QRLSF against ircAEs.

  In the PPI network, STAT3, RELA, TNF, TP53, and NFKBIA were the hub targets on account of more interactions with other nodes, implying they probably generated more outstanding effects. Notably, the overall of them are involved in cutaneous inflammation. Previous studies have proven that STAT3 plays a crucial role in the development of immune-related dermatitis such as psoriasis-like rash.[33,34] The activation of STAT3 will cause excessive inflammatory response and keratinocyte hyperproliferation.[35,36] Kumari S et al have claimed that RELA, an important NF-κB subunit, can regulate immune-related skin inflammatory reaction.[37] Anti-RELA siRNA has been proven as an effective method for treating immune dermatitis.[38,39] TNF, also known as TNF-α, acts as an inducer in the process of skin inflammatory diseases, which involved cytokine release.[40] Its level in skin tissues of patients with immune-related dermatitis has been found to be increased and inhibition of TNF-α will yield a positive effect.[41] Some scholars considered NFKBIA as a regulatory element in various cell types associated with immune-related skin diseases, including keratinocytes, Th17 cells, neutrophils, etc.[42] It has been also regarded as a key factor for the development of immune-inflammatory dermatosis.[43] A current clinical observation has shown that there are abnormal TP53 immunoreactive patterns existing in human cutaneous lesions.[44] All of the above evidences disclose that these 5 core targets may be crucial for QRLSF against ircAEs.

  The results of GO and KEGG analysis demonstrated that the pathophysiological process and signal pathways of other diseases in addition to ircAEs were also involved. The reason may be attributed to the common targets which exist in the development of different diseases. Hence, we will focus on the enrichment results closely related to ircAEs. By analyzing the outcomes of GO enrichment, cytokine-mediated signaling pathway, response to peptide and cellular response to peptide represented the main biological transduction processes, which were perhaps mainly regulated by the core targets as well as some other potential overlapping targets. The key cellular components involved in these specific processes might predominantly located in external side of plasma membrane, transcription regulator complex, membrane raft, membrane microdomain, etc. These targets of QRLSF against ircAEs performed multiple molecular functions which mainly concentrated on DNA-binding transcription factor binding, cytokine receptor binding, chemokine receptor binding, cytokine activity, chemokine activity, etc. The recent literatures have identified that ircAEs are a series of complex pathological processes involving multiple inflammatory cells (CD4+ T cell, macrophage, eosinophils, etc.), inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, TNF-α, CXCL10, etc.), inflammatory peptides (C3a, C3b, etc.) and inflammatory protein reactions (IgG, IgE, etc.),[4,26] which correspond to the main predicted results of GO analysis. Therefore, we can speculate that the complex activity of QRLSF against ircAEs is regulated by the multi-target network via the above biological processes. Moreover, the potential mechanism of QRLSF against ircAEs might be associated with PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, JAK-STAT signaling pathway, chemokine signaling pathway, Th17 cell differentiation, IL-17 signaling pathway, TNF signaling pathway and Toll-like receptor signaling pathway according to the results of KEGG enrichment analysis. The PI3K-Akt signaling pathway plays a major role in regulating immune cells, such as neutrophils, macrophage and monocytes, which are involved in the pathogenesis of immune-related skin lesions.[45] Buerger C. elaborated that the activation of PI3K-Akt and its downstream pathways could accelerate the secretion of TNF-α, which is an important biomarker in immune dermatosis.[46] The targets relevant to PI3K-Akt pathways are regarded as potential candidates for treating inflammatory skin disorders.[47] JAK-STAT signaling pathway is a topic of general interest for dermatology research due to its extensive correlation with multitudinous cytokines (IL-2, IL-4, IL-6, etc.). Multiple cytokine receptors exhibit biological effects through JAK-STAT pathways and blocking it can concurrently suppress the inflammatory process. The inhibitors of JAK-STAT pathway have been applied to explore the treatment of immune-related cutaneous diseases.[48] TNF-α has been proven to be a key component in the pathological development of immune-related diseases, such as skin inflammatory reaction. The activated TNF-α will lead to the subsequent activation of NF-κB pathway and the MAPK cascade along with the release of cytokines (IL-1β, IL-6, etc.), which will cause the immune-related inflammatory dermatosis. Currently, inhibition of TNF signaling pathway has been confirmed to be effective in such diseases.[49,50] Th17 cell differentiation is involved in the pathological process of a wide range of immune-related dermatosis. The recent reports have verified that programmed death-1 inhibitors can cause the pro-inflammatory Th-1/Th-17 response, increasing levels of inflammatory cytokines (IFN-γ, TNF-α, IL-2, IL-6, IL-17, etc.), which may contribute to psoriasiform dermatitis.[51–53] IL-17, a critical cytokine produced by Th17 cells, can recruit the inflammatory cells to infiltrate skin tissues by regulating the expression of chemokines. TNF secreted by these inflammatory cells can further activate the differentiation of Th17 cell and augment the effects of IL-17. The severity of skin lesion is frequently related to the level of IL-17.[54] There is a close relationship between Toll-like receptor signaling pathway and immune dermatitis. The increased levels of TLR1, TLR2, TLR4, TLR7, and TLR9 perhaps promote the pathogenesis of immune-cell mediated inflammatory skin diseases.[55] There are interactions among these important signaling pathways, involving the response of cytokines and their receptors, the regulation of chemokines, and so on. For instance, cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction and chemokine binding to its receptor will activate JAK-STAT, PI3K-Akt, MAPK pathways, etc. The activation of Toll-like receptors or JAK can also transmit signals to PI3K. The differentiation of Th17 cells is induced by STAT3. Therefore, the results of KEGG show an interconnected and complex network mechanism. In addition, some of the key targets in the above significant pathways are also the corresponding targets of the critical phytomolecules of QRLSF that we have screened through the “components-targets” network, indicating that QRLSF may play a pharmacodynamic role via these pathways. In general, we can consider that multiple signaling pathways drive the anti-ircAEs effect of QRLSF.

  The matching activity between the active ingredient and the target is generally evaluated by an important indicator called “binding affinity.” In the present study, the whole screened active compounds and targets could be effectively docked, further verifying the authenticity and effectiveness of the results obtained from our constructed network pharmacology, as well as the pharmacodynamic basis of QRLSF.

  There are also some limitations existing in our study. Owing to the boundedness of filter criteria, we only analyzed the major active components originated from QRLSF, resulting in the limitations of the results. In addition, it is helpful to check the targets screened by network pharmacology via basic study or clinical verifications, which is the keynote of our future work.


## 5. Conclusion
  On the basis of network pharmacology technology, we constructed the interaction network between QRLSF and ircAEs, including 68 active phytomolecules and 39 potential targets. STAT3, RELA, TNF, TP53, and NFKBIA were identified as the core targets, as well as quercetin, apigenin, luteolin, and ursolic acid were screened as the key bioactive ingredients. Depending on GO and KEGG enrichment analysis, the activity of QRLSF against ircAEs might be through PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, JAK-STAT signaling pathway, chemokine signaling pathway, Th17 cell differentiation, IL-17 signaling pathway, TNF signaling pathway and Toll-like receptor signaling pathway, involving in the process of cytokine-mediated signal transduction, response to peptide and cellular response to peptide. The results of molecular docking also confirmed the accuracy of these findings. In brief, there was multi-targets, multi-pathways, and multi-components involved in the process of QRLSF against ircAEs. Our study preliminarily revealed the underlying mechanism of biological effect of QRLSF in the treatment of ircAEs, providing the theoretical basis of the application of TCM in treating cutaneous toxicity induced by anti-tumor immunotherapy and establishing a foundation for further basic experiments in vitro and in vivo.
