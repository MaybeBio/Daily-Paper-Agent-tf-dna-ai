## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, technical soundness, and claims as presented in the provided manuscript
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors present a multi-technique study of the N-terminal low-complexity domain of EWS (EWSLCD), reporting that it is intrinsically disordered, phase separates with a higher propensity than FUSLCD, and that tyrosine residues, particularly in the C-terminal region, are the primary drivers of self-association and phase separation. They further propose that intramolecular contacts in the dilute phase inform on intermolecular interactions in the condensed phase.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract, Introduction, Results, Discussion, Conclusion, Experimental Procedures, figure legends referenced in text
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Figures, tables, and supplementary materials are referenced but not provided; specific numerical data for key claims (e.g., Csat values, relaxation rates) are not fully reported in text

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript addresses an important gap in understanding the molecular determinants of phase separation in the FET protein family, specifically the less-studied EWS LCD. The combination of NMR, microscopy, and simulations is appropriate and potentially powerful. However, the absence of the actual figures and tables makes it impossible to fully evaluate the quality and interpretation of the data. The central claims regarding tyrosine-driven phase separation are plausible and supported by the described experiments, but the quantitative evidence is not fully presented in the text. The comparison with FUSLCD is a strength, but the claim of "molecular diversity" is not fully developed beyond sequence composition differences. The manuscript is likely of interest to the biophysics and phase separation communities, but the current evidence base is insufficient to fully establish all conclusions.

- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying liquid-liquid phase separation, intrinsically disordered proteins, and the FET protein family. The work provides molecular-level insights into EWS LCD behavior that could inform understanding of Ewing sarcoma pathogenesis and potentially guide therapeutic strategies targeting fusion oncoproteins. The methodological approach combining NMR and simulations will also interest biophysicists developing tools for studying IDPs.

- **Major strengths** 1) Addresses a clinically relevant protein (EWS) that is understudied compared to FUS in the phase separation field. 2) Multi-technique approach (NMR, microscopy, simulations) provides complementary perspectives. 3) Mutational analysis directly tests the role of tyrosine residues. 4) Comparison between dilute and condensed phase behavior is conceptually valuable. 5) The finding that EWS phase separates more readily than FUS, despite lower tyrosine density, challenges simple sequence-composition models.

- **Major Concerns** 
  - R1-M1: The manuscript claims that EWSLCD phase separates at lower concentrations than FUSLCD, but the quantitative comparison is not fully presented. The Csat values are mentioned but not systematically reported for both proteins under identical conditions.
  - R1-M2: The interpretation of PRE data as evidence for specific intramolecular contacts is not fully justified. The authors acknowledge challenges with the 5-MSL spin label but do not provide a clear validation of their approach.
  - R1-M3: The claim that dilute-phase intramolecular contacts "inform on" condensed-phase interactions is based on correlation, but the causal link is not established. The authors do not discuss alternative explanations for the observed correlation.
  - R1-M4: The mutational analysis is limited to a small number of constructs. The authors do not systematically vary tyrosine position or density to distinguish between effects of tyrosine number versus placement.
  - R1-M5: The condensed phase simulations are performed with 25 chains for 1 μs, which may be insufficient to achieve equilibrium sampling of phase-separated systems. The authors do not discuss convergence or finite-size effects.

- **Minor Comments** 
  - R1-m1: The abstract states "tyrosine-rich termini exhibit compact conformations" but the text later indicates the C-terminal region is more compact than the N-terminal. This inconsistency should be clarified.
  - R1-m2: The authors state that "27 of the 37 tyrosines in EWSLCD are immediately adjacent to a potential O-linked-N-acetylglucosaminylation site" but do not provide a reference or method for this prediction.
  - R1-m3: The discussion of the yin-yang hypothesis in the Conclusion is interesting but not well integrated with the presented data.
  - R1-m4: The Experimental Procedures section is detailed but lacks specific parameters for some key experiments (e.g., exact centrifugation conditions for pelleting assays).
  - R1-m5: The authors use both "EWSLCD" and "EWS LCD" interchangeably; consistency would improve readability.

- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1, R1-M2, R1-M3, R1-M5

- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - Originality: The work is original in its focus on EWS LCD, which is less studied than FUS. The finding of distinct phase separation behavior between EWS and FUS is novel. However, the general concept of tyrosine-driven phase separation in FET proteins is not entirely new.
  - Scientific importance: The work has potential importance for understanding Ewing sarcoma biology and the molecular grammar of phase separation. However, the clinical relevance is not directly demonstrated.
  - Interdisciplinary readership: The work bridges biophysics, structural biology, and cancer biology, which could appeal to a broad audience. However, the presentation is heavily biophysical and may not be accessible to all.
  - Technical soundness: The combination of techniques is appropriate, but the missing figures and incomplete quantitative reporting prevent full assessment. The simulation methodology appears sound but convergence is not demonstrated.
  - Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is well-written but assumes significant background in NMR and simulation techniques. The clinical context is introduced but not maintained throughout.

- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The core hypothesis is interesting and the approach is appropriate, but the current evidence base is insufficient to fully establish the claims. The authors need to provide complete quantitative data, address the limitations of their PRE analysis, and strengthen the link between dilute and condensed phase behavior.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that EWSLCD phase separates at lower concentrations than FUSLCD is not quantitatively supported in the provided text.
- The assertion that intramolecular contacts in the dilute phase directly inform on condensed-phase interactions is correlative and not mechanistically established.
- The statement that "tyrosine-rich termini exhibit compact conformations" is ambiguous and not consistently supported by the described data.
- The prediction of O-GlcNAc sites on tyrosines is not referenced or validated.
- The claim that the study provides "insights useful for understanding the pathogenesis of oncogenic fusion proteins" is speculative and not directly supported by the data presented.
