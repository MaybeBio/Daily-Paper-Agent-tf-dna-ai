## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence as presented in the abstract; no access to full manuscript, figures, tables, or supplementary materials
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors investigate disease-associated truncated variants of thyroid hormone receptor beta (THRbeta) using in-silico analysis and cell-based assays. They report that point mutations leading to a shortened ligand-binding domain (LBD) cause loss of structural integrity and receptor stability, deviant subcellular localization, compromised transcriptional function, altered heterodimeric interaction with RXR, and affected receptor-chromatin interactions. They further report that the mitotic bookmarking property of some THRbeta variants is affected. The authors conclude that structural and conformational attributes of THRbeta are necessary for normal receptor function and that deviations may contribute to disease predisposition.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no experimental details, numerical data, statistical analyses, or methodological descriptions are provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, all figures and tables, detailed methods, statistical analyses, sequence information for the truncated variants, cell lines used, and any validation data

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a potentially interesting investigation into the functional consequences of THRbeta truncating mutations, a topic with relevance to thyroid hormone resistance syndromes and related disorders. However, the abstract provides insufficient methodological detail and quantitative evidence to evaluate the robustness of the claims. Several conclusions, particularly those regarding structural dynamics and mitotic bookmarking, are stated without supporting data. The work may have value for the endocrine and nuclear receptor communities, but the current evidence base is too limited to establish the case.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying thyroid hormone receptor biology, nuclear receptor structure-function relationships, and genetic disorders of thyroid hormone action would be interested. Clinicians and geneticists investigating thyroid hormone resistance syndromes may also find relevance. The mitotic bookmarking angle could attract cell cycle and chromatin biology researchers.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses a clinically relevant question regarding how naturally occurring truncating mutations in THRbeta lead to dysfunction. The use of multiple complementary approaches, including in-silico analysis, molecular dynamics simulations, and cell-based assays, is commendable. The focus on mitotic bookmarking as a potential disease mechanism is a novel angle that extends beyond classical transcriptional assays.
