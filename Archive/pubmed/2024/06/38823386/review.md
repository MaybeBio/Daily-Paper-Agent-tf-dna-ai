## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a potentially significant advance in understanding Integrator complex architecture and its connection to transcription factor recruitment. The structural data on two sub-complexes and the integrative model of the full complex bound to RNAPII are of clear interest to the transcription and RNA processing communities. However, the evidence base available for this review is limited to the abstract, and several key claims, particularly the functional model involving ZNF655 and transcription attenuation, are not supported by data visible here. The work appears technically ambitious, but the strength of the conclusions cannot be fully assessed without the full manuscript.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Structural biologists studying multi-subunit complexes, researchers in transcription regulation and RNA polymerase II dynamics, and investigators focused on Integrator function in gene expression control. The proposed link between a specific transcription factor and Integrator recruitment would also interest those studying transcriptional attenuation and its dysregulation in disease.
- **Major strengths** The abstract reports novel structures of previously uncharacterized Integrator sub-complexes, which fills a gap in understanding the assembly of this important complex. The integrative modeling approach to place the full complex on the paused RNAPII elongating complex is a notable technical achievement. The in silico screen of over 1,500 transcription factors is a systematic and potentially powerful strategy to identify new regulatory interactions.
- **Major Concerns**  
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1  
  - **Severity** Major  
  - **Blocking** Yes  
  - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
  - **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that ZNF655 is a "direct interacting partner of INTS13 within the fully assembled Integrator" and that this interaction could modulate Integrator stability and regulate transcription attenuation.  
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
  - **Concern** The abstract states that ZNF655 was identified via an in silico protein-protein interaction screen, but no experimental validation (e.g., co-immunoprecipitation, pull-down, or mutagenesis) is described. The term "direct interacting partner" implies a physical interaction, which cannot be established by computational prediction alone.  
  - **Why it matters** The proposed functional model rests entirely on this interaction. If the interaction is not experimentally confirmed, the central claim of the paper regarding transcription factor recruitment via INTS13 is unsupported.  
  - **Resolution test** Provide experimental evidence of a direct physical interaction between ZNF655 and INTS13, such as co-purification or binding assays with purified components, and demonstrate that this interaction occurs in the context of the fully assembled Integrator complex.

## Assessment against Nature-style criteria
- **Originality** The abstract describes new structures of Integrator sub-complexes and a full complex model, which appears original. The identification of a transcription factor interaction partner via a systematic screen adds a novel dimension, though the concept of transcription factor recruitment to Integrator is not entirely unprecedented.
- **Scientific importance** The Integrator complex is central to transcription regulation, and understanding its assembly and recruitment mechanisms is of high importance. The proposed link to transcription factor-mediated targeting could have broad implications for gene-specific regulation.
- **Interdisciplinary readership** The work bridges structural biology, transcription biochemistry, and gene regulation, and would appeal to a broad audience in molecular biology. The abstract is accessible to nonspecialists in structural biology, though some terminology assumes familiarity with transcription mechanisms.
- **Technical soundness** The structural approach appears sound based on the abstract, but the lack of validation details and the absence of experimental confirmation for the key interaction prevent a full assessment. The in silico screen is a reasonable strategy but requires orthogonal validation.
- **Readability for nonspecialists** The abstract is concise and generally clear, but the functional model is stated as a proposal without clear caveats, which could mislead readers into overinterpreting the strength of the evidence.

## Recommendation posture
Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The structural findings are likely valuable, but the central functional claim regarding ZNF655 and transcription attenuation must be experimentally validated. The authors should also provide structural quality metrics and clarify the integrative modeling approach. If these issues are addressed, the work could be suitable for a high-impact venue.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that ZNF655 is a "direct interacting partner" of INTS13 is unsupported by the abstract, as only in silico evidence is described.
- The proposal that INTS13 modulates Integrator stability and regulates transcription attenuation is speculative and not supported by functional data in the abstract.
- The accuracy and reliability of the structural models cannot be assessed without resolution and validation information.
- The generalizability of the proposed mechanism to other transcription factors or loci is not established.
