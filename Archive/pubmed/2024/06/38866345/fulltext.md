Platelet-derived growth factor receptor beta (PDGFRbeta) plays a crucial role in murine haematopoiesis. Baicalein (BAI), a naturally occurring flavonoid, can alleviate disease damage through anti-oxidative, anti-apoptotic, and anti-inflammatory mechanisms. However, whether BAI attenuates oxidative damage in murine haematopoietic cells by PDGFRbeta remains unexplored. In this study, we utilized a tert-butyl hydroperoxide (TBHP)-induced BaF3 cell injury model and an ionising radiation (IR)-induced mice injury model to investigate the impact of the presence or absence of PDGFRbeta on the pharmacological effects of BAI. In addition, the BAI-PDGFRbeta interaction was characterized by molecular docking and dynamics simulations. The results show that a specific concentration of BAI led to increased cell viability, reduced reactive oxygen species (ROS) content, upregulated nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (NRF2) expression, and its downstream target genes heme oxygenase 1 (HO-1) and NAD(P)H Quinone Dehydrogenase 1 (NQO1), and activated protein kinase B (AKT) pathway in cells expressing PDGFRbeta plasmid and experiencing damage. Similarly, BAI elevated lineage(-)Sca1(+)cKIT(+) (LSK) cell proportion, promoted haematopoietic restoration, enhanced NRF2-mediated antioxidant response in PDGFRbeta(+/+) mice. However, despite BAI usage, PDGFRbeta knockout mice (PDGFRbeta(-/-)) showed lower LSK proportion and less antioxidant capacity than the total body irradiation (TBI) group. Furthermore, we demonstrated an interaction between BAI and PDGFRbeta at the molecular level. Collectively, our results indicate that BAI attenuates oxidative stress injury and helps promote haematopoietic cell recovery through regulation of PDGFRbeta.
