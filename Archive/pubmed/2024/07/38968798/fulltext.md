# The secoiridoid glycoside Gentiopicroside is a USP22 inhibitor with potent antitumor immunotherapeutic activity


## Abstract
  Over the past decade, immunotherapies have brought about significant changes in how we approach the treatment of various solid tumors and blood-related cancers. However, the effectiveness of checkpoint blockade therapy has been constrained to a rate of under 30%. A significant challenge in the realm of tumor immunotherapy revolves around comprehending the mechanisms through which regulatory T (Treg) cells induce immunosuppression. We have recently discovered that USP22 (ubiquitin-specific peptidase 22) a deubiquitinating enzyme that is increased in various tumors, is an oncogene and controls Treg immune suppressive activity for tumor evasion, providing a rationale for USP22 targeting to achieve both onco- and immuno-therapeutic efficacies. Herein, we identified the traditional Chinese secoiridoid compound gentiopicroside as a USP22 inhibitor. Gentiopicroside treatment decreased the forkhead box P3 (Foxp3) expression, which subsequently reduced Treg immune suppressive activity. Treatment of cancer cells by gentiopicroside resulted in an increase in histone 2B monoubiquitination (H2Bub) in a USP22-dependent manner and a decrease in programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1) expression, both of which are known as USP22-specific substrates. Docking and molecular dynamic simulation revealed that gentiopicroside stably binds to USP22 catalytic pocket, supporting that gentiopicroside is a USP22 inhibitor. Importantly, administration of gentiopicroside to mice significantly inhibited the growth of syngenetic lung adenocarcinoma. Further analysis of intratumoral immune cells revealed a dramatic increase CD8+ T cell production of IFN-γ and granzyme B (GZMB), confirming that gentiopicroside enhances antitumor immunity. Our study revealed that gentiopicroside is a USP22-specific inhibitor with potent antitumor therapeutic potentials.


## Introduction
  It has been 10 years since its selection as the “2013 Breakthrough of the Year” by Science (1), cancer immunotherapy has transformed into a standard care for therapy in treatment of many advanced cancers. However, the efficacy of cancer immunotherapy is still limited in treatment of most solid tumors and often associated with immunotherapy-related adverse events with multiple tissues/organs autoimmune inflammation. Clinicians have attempted combinations of cancer immunotherapy with either irradiation or chemotherapies to achieve synergistic efficacy and reduce their toxicities. However, one of the obstacles is that many of the chemotherapeutic drugs, if not all, often have immune toxicities, and their administration largely reduces the immune cell frequency in the circulating blood, which consequently impairs antitumor immunity and diminishes immune checkpoint blockade therapeutic efficacy. Therefore, it will be ideal to develop a strategy with both chemo- and immuno-activating therapeutic efficacy in antitumor treatment.

  Ubiquitin-specific peptidase 22 (USP22), a deubiquitinating enzyme, exhibits elevated levels across various tumors and is under the influence of multiple pathways linked to the development of tumors, evasion of the immune system, and resistance to drugs(2–7). Acting as a component of the Spt-Ada-Gcn5 acetyltransferase (SAGA) transcription cofactor complex, USP22 plays a role in facilitating the complex’s access to gene promoters by deubiquitinating specific amino acids on histone tails. This modification prompts gene transcription activation (8–10). SAGA stands as the primary deubiquitinase for H2Bub in human cells, with its activity not being wholly replaceable by other deubiquitinases(11). Programmed cell death 1 ligand 1 (PD-L1), often present on tumor cells, binds with the immune-inhibiting receptor PD-1 to hinder T-cell function. Recent findings suggest that USP22 also contributes to the stability of PD-L1 and CD73 by means of deubiquitination(12). Both our research and that of others have demonstrated USP22’s role in upregulating FoxP3, a specific transcription factor within regulatory T cells (Tregs), thereby promoting immune suppression(13). However, Treg-specific targeting to boost antitumor immunity has not been successfully developed. We have recently discovered that USP22 is a FoxP3-specific deubiquitinase. Genetic USP22 inhibition reduces Foxp3 stability and impairs Treg suppressive function. Mice with Treg-specific USP22 inhibition have an enhanced antitumor immune response(13). These results provide a clear rationale for USP22 inhibition in antitumor immunotherapy.

  Gentiopicroside, a secoiridoid compound derived from Gentiana lutea, known as Qin Jiao Chinese herbal medicine, to treat joint pain(14). Recent studies have shown that gentiopicroside has a variety activities in immune regulation and metabolic response. Gentiopicroside was found to suppress PAQR3 expression, thereby restoring the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway. This restoration contributes to the amelioration of glucose and lipid metabolism disorders(15). It can also modulate the TGR5-β-arrestin 2-NF-κB signaling pathway, which serves as a preventive measure against kidney inflammation in diabetic mice(16, 17). Furthermore, gentiopicroside has been associated with anti-tumor effects through impeding the cell cycle of tumor cells and promoting tumor apoptosis(18). In addition, LiX et al. provided evidence of gentiopicroside’s potent anti-tumor effects on human ovarian carcinoma cells (SKOV3). These effects encompass inducing cell cycle arrest, mediating mitochondrial-driven apoptosis, and inhibiting cell migration(19). However, the molecular mechanisms underlying how gentiopicroside excavates its antitumor activity remain undefined.

  In the current study, we demonstrated that the natural product gentiopicroside is a USP22-specific inhibitor, which partially diminished Treg immune suppressive functions by decreasing the expression of Foxp3 and inhibit tumoral PD-L1 expression. As a consequence, gentiopicroside treatment enhanced antitumor immunity to inhibit tumor growth in mice with syngeneic lung adenocarcinoma.

### Homology modeling and Molecular docking
  The 3D structure of USP22 was not previously reported; therefore, homology modeling was employed to generate the 3D structures of USP22. UBP8, the yeast ortholog of USP22, forms a similar SAGA DUB module and deubiquitinates both H2A and H2B. The yeast UBP8 structure (PDB code: 3MHS) with a high resolution of 1.89 Å has been successfully solved and was selected as a template to generate the homology model of human USP22 using the Swiss-model search(20, 21). Molecular docking analysis was conducted using AutoDock Vina(22). The protein structures underwent purification through the elimination of crystallographic water molecules and were subsequently modified by the inclusion of polar hydrogen atoms, along with the incorporation of Gasteiger charges. The dimensions of the simulation box were set at 50×50×50 (X×Y×Z). The protein/ligand interaction was facilitated using the PyMOL software (23).


### Molecular dynamics simulation
  The conformation of the complexes formed between the compounds and the potential target protein was predicted utilizing AutoDock Vina. Additionally, all molecular dynamics (MD) simulations were conducted using Gromacs 2016 with the Amber99sb force field (24). Following the MD simulation, the output file was analyzed to acquire the root mean square deviation (RMSD) and assess the number and distribution of hydrogen bonds between Gentiopicroside and USP22 throughout the process. The molecular mechanics energies combined with the Molecular Mechanics/Poisson Boltzmann Surface Area (MM/PBSA) methods were employed to calculate the binding free energy of the Gentiopicroside with USP22 target protein. In the MM/PBSA approach, the binding free energy was determined through the following calculation:  ΔGbinding=ΔEMM+ΔGsolv−TΔSMM   ΔEMM=ΔEint+ΔEvdW+ΔEele   ΔGsolv=ΔGPB+ΔGSA


### Cell sorting and in vitro Treg suppression assay
  Foxp3-YFP-Cre mice were treated with gentiopicroside 20 mg/kg, twice a day for 3 days. Then CD4+YFP+ T cells were sorted by MACSQuant Tyto Cell sorter. Then cocultured with cell trace violet stain-labelled conventional T cells at indicated ratio in 96-well plate for another 3 days. The suppressive function of Treg cells was determined by measurement of the proliferation of activated conventional T cells on the basis of labelled cell proliferation dye dilution.


### Flow cytometry analysis
  Spleens and lymph nodes were placed in 5 mL TCM after mice were sacrificed. Single-cell suspensions were prepared by grinding over a nylon membrane (70μm), and lymphocytes were isolated after ACK lysis. The isolated cells were collected and staining of cell surface proteins were performed in FACS (phosphate buffered saline (PBS) + 3% FBS) at 4°C in dark for 30 min, for intracellular staining followed by Fixation/Permeabilization 1 hour in room temperature and intracellular staining with certain antibodies. After staining, cells were washed and resuspended in FACS. Flow cytometric analysis was performed using a BD LSRFortessa X-20 flow cytometer. Data was analyzed using FlowJo. Gating strategies are shown in supplemental figures.


### WST-1 analysis
  Cells were plated in a 96-well tissue culture plate after attachment treated with gentiopicroside for 24 or 48 hours then incubated with the WST-1 reagent for 2 – 4 hours. After this incubation period, OD values were detected with a scanning multi-well spectrophotometer.


### Immunoprecipitation and western blotting
  Cells were lysed in RIPA Buffer with complete Protease Inhibitor Cocktail on ice for 30 min. Lysates were centrifuged at 13,000 rpm for 10 min at 4°C. Samples for immunoprecipitation being rotated with antibodies for 2 hours at 4°C. Protein G beads were added for 2 hours followed by washing with buffer. Samples were boiled at 95°C for 10 min before gel loading. Membranes were blocked with 5% milk in TBST for 1 hour at room temperature. Primary antibodies were added at the manufactures recommended dilutions overnight. Secondary antibodies were incubated for one hour at room temperature on the second day.


### RNA extraction and real-time PCR analysis
  RNA was extracted using an RNA isolation kit (Qiagen). CDNA synthesis (Quantabio) was conducted. Samples were prepared with SYBER Green (Quantabio) and analyzed using the Biorad MyiQ2 Real-Time PCR detector. Primers (5’−3’) used were as follows, Human Foxp3: Forward: GTGGCCCGGATGTGAGAAG; Reverse: GGAGCCCTTGTCGGATGATG; Human β-Actin: Forward: ACAGAGCCTCGCCTTTGCC; Reverse: GATATCATCATCCATGGTGAGCTGG. Mouse Pd-l1: Forward: CACTTGCTACGGGCGTTTAC; Reverse: AGGGCAGCATTTCCCTTCAA; Mouse β-Actin: Forward: CACTGTCGAGTCGCGTCC; Reverse: TCATCCATGGCGAACTGGTG.


### In vivo tumor model
  All the mouse used were 6–8 weeks old C57BL/6 mouses. Mouses were inoculated with 5×105 LLC1 cells subcutaneously to the right flank as reported. Then treated with 4mg gentiopicroside i.p. twice a day. Tumor measurements were performed by a single operator with calipers. Volume was calculated using the following formula L × W2/2.


### Statistical analysis
  Statistical analysis was done using Prism (GraphPad) software. Measurement data are presented as mean±SD. Means were calculated using paired student’s t-test, unpaired student’s t-test, or two-way ANOVA. P < 0.05 indicated statistical significance.


## Results

### Gentiopicroside boosts the anti-tumor response.
  As gentiopicroside been known has variety activities in anti-tumor effects, immune regulation and metabolic response, we accessed the antitumor therapeutic efficacy of gentiopicroside using an LLC1 syngeneic lung adenocarcinoma model. We subcutaneously injected LLC1 cells in C57BL/6 mice and treated each of them with 4mg gentiopicroside twice a day (Figure 1A). While the tumor growth was largely comparable during the early phase of treatment, a statistically significant reduction in tumor volume was observed during the late phase of gentiopicroside treatment. Consistently, we observed a dramatic reduction in tumor size and tumor weight from mice with gentiopicroside treatment (Figure 1B–E), clearly indicate that the gentiopicroside holds potent antitumor activity. Of note, the mice body weight was unaltered during the entire gentiopicroside treatment process, largely excluding the possibility that its therapeutic activity is not due to toxicity to treated mice.

  Analysis of intratumoral immune cells revealed that, while the percentages of infiltrated CD4, CD8 were unaltered (Figure 1F), the levels of both IFN-γ and GZMB were dramatically increased in CD8+ T cells from gentiopicroside treated mice (Figure 1I–J) indicating that gentiopicroside treatment enhances antitumor immunity. To support this, we detected that Treg frequency was much lower despite not yet reaching to any statistical difference (p=0.0833) (Figure 1G–H). It has been shown that PD-L1 expression in immune cells could also affect cytotoxic CD8+ T cell mediated tumor killing in vivo, we then further detected the expression of PD-L1 in CD8+ T cells and Treg cells (Figure 1H–K). However, the treatment of mice at the dose was insufficient to significantly inhibit PD-L1 expression. Collectively, we demonstrated that Gentiopicroside enhances anti-tumor response possibly through regulate immune response at least in LLC1 mouse model.


### Gentiopicroside treatment partially diminishes Treg immune suppressive functions in vivo
  To study the effect of gentiopicroside on FOXP3-downregulation in Treg cells in vivo, we intraperitoneal injected 20 mg/kg gentiopicroside twice a day in wildtype mice for 3 days (Figure 2A). Mice treated at this dose of gentiopicroside did not show any cytotoxicity as their body weights were unaltered (Figure 2B). Gentiopicroside treatment could decrease FOXP3 protein expression in Tregs from both spleen and lymph nodes, without changing the frequency of conventional T cells and Treg cells (Figure 2D, G). Further flow analysis showed that the expression of functional receptors including CD25, CTLA-4, PD-1, PD-L1, ICOS, Helios and GITR, while all of which were unaltered in splenic Tregs, both CD25 and GITA expression levels in Tregs from lymph node were statistically reduced (Figure 2E, H), suggesting that gentiopicroside treatment may inhibit Treg immune suppressive activity due to the reduced FOXP3, CD25 and GITR expression.

  To directly test the function of Treg cells from gentiopicroside treated mice, we sorted the CD4+ FOXP3-YFP+ Tregs from gentiopicroside treated and control mice and performed in vitro Treg suppressive assay by co-culturing with the conventional T cells as reported. Indeed, the proportion of proliferating conventional T cells was higher when co-cultivated with Tregs from gentiopicroside treated mice comparing to that from PBS treated controls. Further analysis revealed a significant higher division index of conventional T cells in the gentiopicroside treated group, indicating that gentiopicroside treatment impaired the suppressive function of Treg (Figure 2I). Taken together, gentiopicroside treatment inhibit Treg immune suppressive functions.


### Docking and molecular dynamic (MD) simulation reveal the USP22-gentiopicroside binding.
  USP22 is a deubiquitinating enzyme and is under the influence of multiple pathways linked to the development of tumors, evasion of the immune system, and resistance to drugs. So we wonder if there is any connection between USP22 and gentiopicroside. To investigate it, we analyzed whether USP22 protein and gentiopicroside form a protein-ligand complex using the optimized crystal structure of USP22 peptidase catalytic domain as we reported recently (26). The interaction between the two molecules was assessed using AutoDock Vina and PyMOL. Indeed, gentiopicroside effectively occupied the catalytic pocket of USP22, displaying a notable binding energy of −9.1 kcal/mol (Figure 3A–B). Notably, gentiopicroside formed four strong hydrogen bonds specifically with USP22 amino acid residues including Asp262, Glu265, His471, and Gly478, highlighting the crucial role of the hydroxy groups in gentiopicroside ‘s inhibitory activity against USP22. Consistently, molecular dynamics (MD) simulation using GROMACS for a duration of 50 ns confirmed the stable RMSD trajectory of gentiopicroside with USP22 (Figure 3C). Notably, several crucial amino acid residues, namely Glu259, Gln260, Gln261, Asp262, Glu265, Phe412, His471, His475, Ser477, and Gly478, were identified in the molecular dynamics simulation (Figure 3D). The interaction between gentiopicroside and each residue was further analyzed using the MM/PBSA methods. The results indicated that gentiopicroside can form a stable complex with USP22, with an energy of approximately −170.69 kJ/mol, as illustrated in Figure 3F. Further analysis reveals that the interaction between gentiopicroside and USP22 involved in the formation of one or two hydrogen bonds (Figure 3E). These findings provide molecular evidence supporting the potential of gentiopicroside as a promising USP22 inhibitor.


### Gentiopicroside inhibits FOXP3 expression in human Treg MT-2 cells.
  MT-2 cell line has the phenotypic and functional characteristics of human Tregs and is a useful tool in the study of human Tregs (25), which was used for our study of the effects of natural products on Treg FOXP3 expression in vitro. Interestingly, our intracellular staining and flow cytometry analysis revealed a significant reduction in the FOXP3 protein expression in MT-2 cells when treated with gentiopicroside, a natural secoiridoid glycoside from gentian species of medicinal plants (14), in a dose dependent manner (Figure 4A). To determine whether gentiopicroside inhibits FOXP3 expression in MT-2 cells is due to cytotoxicity, we determined its effect on MT-2 cell proliferation and viability. As shown in Figure 4B & D, while with a modest reduction in MT-2 cell proliferation at the concentration of 20 μg/ml, their viabilities were not altered, suggesting that gentiopicroside is not toxic to MT-2 cells at the concentration tested. To support this conclusion, we further showed that gentiopicroside treatment did not induce MT-2 cell apoptosis (Figure 4C). Western blotting analysis further confirmed FOXP3 protein reduction in MT-2 cells by gentiopicroside treatment (Figure 4E).

  To investigate the possible molecular mechanisms underlying how gentiopicroside inhibits FOXP3 expression, we analyzed its effect on FOXP3 transcription in MT-2 cells. Real-time-PCR analysis showed that gentiopicroside treatment did not inhibit, but increased Foxp3 mRNA levels in MT-2 cells (Figure 4F), implying that gentiopicroside inhibits FOXP3 expression at post-transcriptional levels. To support this, we further show that gentiopicroside treatment facilitated FOXP3 degradation (Figure 4G), and further addition of the proteasomal inhibitor MG132, largely protected gentiopicroside-induced FOXP3 degradation (Figure 4H). We have recently discovered that the USP22 functions as an FOXP3-specific deubiquitinase to protect it from ubiquitination-mediated proteasomal degradation, implying a possibility that gentiopicroside may inhibit USP22 that leading to elevated FOXP3 degradation. Indeed, gentiopicroside treatment dramatically increased the levels of histone 2B monoubiqutination (Figure 4E), which is known as a substrate of USP22 deubiquitinase. Collectively, our study suggests that gentiopicroside facilitates FOXP3 protein proteasomal degradation possibly through suppressing it deubiquitinase USP22.


### Gentiopicroside inhibits USP22 activity in cancer cells.
  To further test the possibility that gentiopicroside is a USP22-specific inhibitor, we treated colon cancer MC38 cells and analyzed the level of USP22 substrate H2Bub. Consistently, gentiopicroside treatment resulted in a dramatic increase in H2Bub (Figure 5A). We then used a CRISPR approach and generated USP22 KO MC38 cells to study the USP22-dependence of gentiopicroside efficacy. As expected, USP22 targeted deletion resulted in a dramatic increase in H2Bub levels in MC38 cells. Importantly, treatment of USP22-null MC38 cells with gentiopicroside did not further upregulate H2Bub levels (Figure 5B), clearly indicating that gentiopicroside enhances H2Bub in a USP22-dependent manner. The increased H2Bub and reduced PD-L1 expression levels which is another USP22 substrate by gentiopicroside treatment were further confirmed in lung adenocarcinoma LLC1 cells (Figure 5C). This decrease of PD-L1 by gentiopicroside appears to be due to the impaired USP22 activity in protecting PD-L1 from ubiquitination-mediated proteasomal degradation, because gentiopicroside treatment resulted in an increase in PD-L1 ubiquitination and further treatment with the proteasomal inhibitor MG132 largely abolished gentiopicroside-induced PD-L1 protein reduction (Figure 5D & E). In contrast, Pd-l1 mRNA was unaltered by gentiopicroside treatment (Figure 5F). These results further support our conclusion that gentiopicroside is a USP22 inhibitor.

  We and others have demonstrated that USP22 functions as an oncogene through promoting cancer cell cycle progression and induces cancer cell apoptosis (2, 3). Indeed, while neither MC38 nor LLC1 cell proliferation were altered after 24 hours gentiopicroside treatment, after 48 hours treatment gentiopicroside can significantly inhibit the proliferation (Figure 5G & H). In contrast, gentiopicroside treatment at the dose up to 20 μg/ml did not induce their apoptosis (Figure 5I & J), suggesting that gentiopicroside treatment at the concentration tested results in a partial USP22 inhibition that is sufficient to inhibit cell growth but insufficient for apoptosis induction in cancer cells.


## Discussion
  Our study identifies the first natural product gentiopicroside as a USP22 inhibitor that boosts antitumor immunity. This conclusion is verified by the following observations: First, gentiopicroside treatment inhibits Foxp3 expression human Treg cell line MT-2 cells and in mouse primary Tregs in vivo; 2nd, gentiopicroside treatment resulted in the increased H2Bub levels in a USP22-dependent manner; 3rd, gentiopicroside inhibited the tumor cell surface expression of PD-L1 through facilitating ubiquitination-mediated proteasomal degradation; 4th, docking and molecular dynamic simulation revealed that gentiopicroside stably binds to USP22 catalytic pocket; lastly, administration of gentiopicroside to mice enhanced their antitumor immunity with increased CD8+ T cell production of IFN-γ and GZMB, which consequently inhibited the tumor growth.

  The first evidence that implies that gentiopicroside could be a potential USP22 inhibitor is its activity in suppressing Foxp3 protein expression. Multiple ubiquitin-specific peptidases, including USP7 (27), USP21 (28), USP22 (13) and USP44 (29) have been identified as Foxp3-specific deubiquitinase, which protect Foxp3 from ubiquitination-mediated proteasomal degradation. However, genetic deletion of USP22, but not the rest three, resulted in the reduction of Foxp3 expression in Tregs (13). USP7 genetic suppression reduced Treg frequency in peripheral lymphoid organs in mice, and neither USP21 nor USP44 genetic inhibition, while impaired Treg immune suppressive functions, was sufficient to reduce Foxp3 expression in Tregs (28, 29). More importantly, our data further shown that gentiopicroside treatment increased H2Bub levels fully relying on the existence of functional USP22 in cancer cells, as its treatment failed to further increase H2Bub levels. Together with our discovery that treatment of cancer cells with gentiopicroside led to the increased ubiquitination and reduced protein expression of PD-L1, a bona fade substrate of USP22, we conclude that gentiopicroside holds potent activity in suppressing USP22 deubiquitination catalytic activity.

  USP22 has recently emerged as an attractive therapeutic target to treat a broad spectrum of, if not all human cancers. Gene expression survey has shown that the elevated expression of USP22 in correlation with poor prognosis in a variety of human tumors including triple negative breast cancer (TNBC), gastric carcinoma, lung cancer, pancreatic cancer, and melanoma (30–33). At the molecular level, we and others have recently demonstrated that USP22 functions as an oncogene by inhibiting cell apoptosis and promoting cell cycle progression through targeting cyclins, c-Myc, BMI-1, TRF1, and Sirt1, which controls p53 expression (2, 8, 34–37). USP22 promotes chemotherapeutic resistance by inhibiting Bax-mediated apoptosis, improving HSP90 function, inhibiting Estrogen receptor α degradation, and driving EGFR recirculation (38). We have shown that genetic USP22 suppression inhibits cancer cell growth and induces their apoptosis, and thus dramatically reduced tumor progression in mice (34, 35). More recently, we have demonstrated that deletion of USP22 partially diminishes Treg suppressive functions, but in the conventional T cell (both CD4 and CD8) immunity is unaltered (13). Treg-specific USP22 deletion results in a reduction but not a total loss of Foxp3 because USP22 controls Foxp3 protein stability, providing a reasonable window for USP22 suppression to boost antitumor immunity without causing severe autoimmunity. Consequently, mice with Treg-specific USP22 deletion resulted in vigorous tumor rejection (13). In addition, we and other have shown that both Foxp3 expression levels and the immune suppressive functions of Tregs are further elevated after infiltrating into tumor microenvironment, a process called Treg adaptation or fitness (26, 39). Importantly, we discovered that several tumor microenvironmental factors including TGF-β, hypoxia and low glucose levels induce USP22 expression for Treg fitness (26). On the other hand, USP22 promotes its suppressive function of the antitumor immunity through upregulating cancer cell expression of PD-L1 and CD73 (6, 12), two checkpoint receptors responsible for evading antitumor immunosurveillance. USP22 inhibition dramatically enhances antitumor immunity and vigorous tumor rejections (26). Therefore, USP22 is an ideal target with both chemo- and immunotherapeutic targeting efficacies in cancer treatment. Indeed, our data here show that gentiopicroside treatment inhibited cancer cell growth and reduced Treg immune suppressive activities, indicating that gentiopicroside holds a great therapeutic potential in cancer treatment through, at least in part, inhibiting USP22. We are aware, however, as a natural product, the antitumor activity of gentiopicroside is relatively modest. Nevertheless, our current study defines gentiopicroside as the first USP22-specific natural product inhibitor and provide a rationale for its use in antitumor therapy. Future studies to design more potent anti-cancer drugs using gentiopicroside as a lead compound is needed.
