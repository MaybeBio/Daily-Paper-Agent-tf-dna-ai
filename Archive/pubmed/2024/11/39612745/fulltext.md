Cresyl diphenyl phosphate (CDP), a novel organophosphate ester (OPE), has been increasingly detected in various environmental and human samples. However, its toxicity, mechanisms, and health risks remain largely unknown. In this work, we investigated CDP-induced hepatic steatosis through Liver X Receptor alpha (LXRalpha) pathway across the molecular interactions, signaling pathways, cell functions, animal effects, and population risks, and compared them to triphenyl phosphate (TPHP) and tricresyl phosphate (TCRP). Receptor binding results showed that all three OPEs bound to LXRalpha directly in the order of TCRP > CDP > TPHP. Docking results suggested that the three aryl groups played an essential role in the binding of these chemicals to LXRalpha. They also activated LXRalpha-mediated lipogenesis pathway and promoted lipid accumulation in HepG2 cells. The intracellular concentration and LXRalpha-bound concentration of the chemicals in HepG2 cells followed a consistent order of CDP > TCRP > TPHP. In mice, exposure to CDP activated LXRalpha-mediated de novo lipogenesis pathway, leading to hepatic steatosis. Risk assessment results suggested that few populations (5.38 %) face a LXRalpha-mediated hepatic steatosis risk from CDP exposure. Collectively, our results demonstrate that CDP could bind to LXRalpha, activate the subsequent de novo lipogenesis pathway, inducing hepatic steatosis, and increasing adverse health risks.
