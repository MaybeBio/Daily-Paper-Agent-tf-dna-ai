## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (preprint, bioRxiv)
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific claims, methodology, and evidence as presented in the provided text; no external validation performed
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors use AlphaFold3 (AF3) to generate structural ensembles of Taq DNA ligase and human DNA Ligase I (LIG1) across different functional states, claiming that these ensembles capture conformational dynamics that provide mechanistic insights into DNA ligase activity, including substrate positioning effects on catalytic efficiency.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract, Introduction, Results, Discussion, Methods, Data availability statement; figures referenced but not provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Figures 1–4 and Supplementary Figures S1A–D are referenced but not included; raw structural data and analysis code are available via Zenodo/GitHub but not reviewed; no experimental validation data (e.g., mutagenesis, biochemical assays) are presented

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript presents a conceptually interesting application of AlphaFold3 to generate conformational ensembles for DNA ligases, with the goal of extracting mechanistic insights from predicted structural variability. However, the evidence provided is insufficient to support several key claims. The core assertion that AF3 ensembles "capture" physiologically relevant conformational dynamics is not validated against experimental data or established conformational sampling methods. The LIG1 findings, while aligned with published experimental results, are presented as confirmatory rather than novel, and the mechanistic interpretation relies on qualitative visual inspection of predicted models. The manuscript would benefit from quantitative validation, comparison with experimental structures, and a clearer articulation of what is genuinely new beyond the AF3 predictions themselves.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Structural biologists and computational biologists interested in applying deep learning models to study protein dynamics; researchers studying DNA repair mechanisms, particularly DNA ligases; those developing tools for conformational ensemble prediction. The potential to use AF3 for generating ensembles rather than single structures is of broad interest, but the current evidence may not convince specialists in molecular dynamics or experimental enzymology.
- **Major strengths** 1) Addresses a timely and important question: whether AF3 can move beyond single-structure prediction to capture conformational diversity. 2) The systematic approach of modeling multiple functional states (ground, cofactor-bound, DNA-bound) is logical and comprehensive. 3) The extension to human LIG1 with different overhang substrates is a thoughtful test of the method's applicability. 4) Data and code availability is commendable for reproducibility.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Validity of core claim
  - **Claim pointer** "AF3 conformational ensembles indicated LIG1 has lower catalytic efficiency for 5' overhangs due to suboptimal DNA positioning within the catalytic site"
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section, LIG1 subsection; Figure 4 (not provided)
  - **Concern** The claim that AF3 ensembles explain catalytic efficiency differences is based on qualitative visual inspection of predicted models. No quantitative metrics (e.g., distance distributions, contact frequencies, binding free energies) are provided to support the assertion of "suboptimal positioning." The manuscript does not demonstrate that the predicted conformational differences are statistically significant or that they correlate with experimentally measured catalytic rates beyond a single published reference.
  - **Why it matters** The central novelty of the paper is that AF3 ensembles can capture functionally relevant conformational differences. Without quantitative validation, the claim reduces to anecdotal observation of predicted structures, which does not establish the method's utility for mechanistic inference.
  - **Resolution test** Provide quantitative measures (e.g., RMSD distributions, key catalytic distances, contact maps) comparing 3DSB and 5DSB ensembles, with statistical testing. Ideally, correlate these with experimental catalytic efficiencies from the cited literature or new assays.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Methodological validation
  - **Claim pointer** "Modifying the input parameters for AF3 yielded detailed conformational states of a DNA-binding enzyme"
  - **Evidence pointer** Methods, Overview of the structural prediction pipeline; Results, Steps 0–3
  - **Concern** The manuscript does not validate that the conformational ensembles generated by AF3 are physically meaningful. No comparison is made with experimental structures (e.g., known crystal structures of Taq ligase or LIG1), molecular dynamics simulations, or established ensemble generation methods. The claim that "modifying input parameters" produces valid conformational states is asserted without evidence that the resulting diversity is not an artifact of the model's stochasticity or input perturbations.
  - **Why it matters** Without validation, the ensembles could represent noise rather than biologically relevant conformations. The paper's premise depends on the reliability of AF3-generated ensembles, which is not established.
  - **Resolution test** Compare predicted ensembles with known experimental structures (e.g., apo vs. DNA-bound forms) and/or run control analyses (e.g., predicting a protein with known rigid structure to show low ensemble diversity). Quantify whether the observed conformational spread is consistent with experimental B-factors or NMR ensembles.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Novelty and significance
  - **Claim pointer** "These detailed structural ensembles provided novel insights into the enzyme's behavior during DNA repair"
  - **Evidence pointer** Results, Steps 0–3; Discussion
  - **Concern** The "novel insights" are not clearly articulated. For Taq ligase, the findings (e.g., mobility of N-terminal and BRCT domains, closed conformation upon cofactor binding) are consistent with known structural biology of DNA ligases, as the authors themselves note. For LIG1, the finding that 5' overhangs are suboptimally positioned is presented as confirmatory of published experimental data, not as a new discovery. The manuscript does not identify a specific, testable hypothesis or a previously unknown mechanistic detail that emerges from the ensembles.
  - **Why it matters** For a Nature-style publication, the work must offer more than a demonstration that AF3 can reproduce known biology. The field needs new insights or predictions that can be experimentally tested.
  - **Resolution test** Clearly state what is novel about the ensemble-based findings compared to existing structural knowledge. Propose a specific, falsifiable prediction (e.g., a mutation that should alter ensemble dynamics and affect activity) and ideally provide preliminary experimental support.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M4
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Technical rigor
  - **Claim pointer** "We generated 100 structural models (20 distinct seeds, 5 models per seed) for each of the five steps... for a total of 500 structures" (Results) vs. "24 different seeds (5 model output per seed, 120 structural models for each step)" (Methods)
  - **Evidence pointer** Results, first paragraph; Methods, Overview of the structural prediction pipeline
  - **Concern** There is a direct numerical inconsistency between the Results (20 seeds, 100 models per step) and the Methods (24 seeds, 120 models per step). This discrepancy undermines confidence in the reported methodology and reproducibility.
  - **Why it matters** Inconsistencies in core methodological details are unacceptable in a scientific manuscript and suggest lack of careful review.
  - **Resolution test** Correct the inconsistency and ensure all numbers are consistent throughout the text, figure legends, and supplementary materials.

- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 (quantitative validation of LIG1 claim), R1-M2 (validation of ensemble reliability), R1-M4 (numerical inconsistency between Results and Methods)
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: Moderate. The application of AF3 to generate ensembles is timely, but the concept of using deep learning models for conformational sampling is not entirely new, and the manuscript does not introduce a novel method or framework. Scientific importance: Moderate. The findings are largely confirmatory of known ligase biology; the potential for new mechanistic insight is not realized in the current form. Interdisciplinary readership: Limited. The work is primarily of interest to structural biologists and computational biologists; the biological implications are not developed enough to attract a broader audience. Technical soundness: Weak. The lack of validation against experimental data or established methods, combined with methodological inconsistencies, undermines confidence in the results. Readability for nonspecialists: Acceptable. The writing is generally clear, but the significance of the findings is overstated relative to the evidence.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The manuscript addresses an interesting question, but the core claims require substantial additional validation and quantitative analysis. The authors should address the major concerns, particularly the validation of ensemble reliability and the quantitative support for the LIG1 mechanistic interpretation, before the case for AF3-based ensemble analysis of ligase dynamics can be considered established.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that AF3 ensembles capture physiologically relevant conformational dynamics is unsupported without comparison to experimental data or established sampling methods.
- The assertion that "suboptimal DNA positioning" explains LIG1's lower catalytic efficiency for 5' overhangs is based on qualitative inspection and lacks quantitative metrics.
- The statement that "AF3 successfully predicts different conformations" of Taq ligase is presented without defining what constitutes "success" or how this was assessed.
- The claim that the framework is "generalizable" to other enzymes is speculative and not demonstrated.
- The Discussion's statements about "transformative innovations" and "profoundly enhanced" understanding are not supported by the data presented.
