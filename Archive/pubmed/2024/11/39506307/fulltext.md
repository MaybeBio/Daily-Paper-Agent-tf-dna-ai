# Naringenin can
Inhibit the Pyroptosis of Osteoblasts
by Activating the Nrf2/HO-1 Signaling Pathway and Alleviate
the Differentiation Disorder of Osteoblasts Caused by Microgravity


## Abstract
  

  Naringenin (4,5,7-trihydroxyflavone, NAR) is an effective active ingredient in Rhizoma Drynariae, which has many biological functions, encompassing anti-inflammatory and -oxidant functions. Prior research has shown that NOD-like receptor pyrin domain-containing protein 3 (NLRP3) inflammasomes possessed a significant contribution to osteoporosis. However, the NAR impact on bone loss caused by microgravity remains unclear. Classical microgravity simulation methods were used to induce simulated microgravity (SMG) in mice and cells. Microcomputed tomography, immunohistochemical examination, and hematoxylin and eosin staining were implemented to ascertain alterations in bone microstructure and morphology in mice subsequent to NAR gavage. Cellular investigations were implemented encompassing quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction, Western blotting, and immunofluorescence labeling to investigate the molecular mechanism behind NAR resistance to microgravity-induced bone loss. Our research has shown that NAR can significantly enhance the SMG-stimulated alterations in bone microstructure and morphology in mice, mainly by increasing the trabecular thickness, bone volume fraction, and trabecular number while increasing the bone trabecula number. Cell experiments also showed that SMG caused the activation of inflammatory corpuscles of NLRP3 and induced pyroptosis simultaneously, which can be confirmed by the upregulation of protein and mRNA expression levels such as those of NLRP3, cleaved caspase-1, gasdermin D, and apoptosis-associated speck-like protein. The occurrence of pyroptosis further led to the disorder of osteogenic differentiation, which showed that the osteopontin, Runt-related transcription factor 2, bone morphogenetic protein 2, and alkaline phosphatase expression levels were decreased. The intervention of NAR can activate the nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2/heme oxygenase-1 (Nrf2/HO-1) pathway, reverse this phenomenon via controlling the reactive oxygen species generation in cells and correcting mitochondrial malfunction, weaken the pyroptosis of osteoblasts (OBs), and promote osteogenic differentiation. In summary, NAR could hinder the pyroptosis of OBs caused by SMG and promote osteogenic differentiation via activating the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway. This provides a unique view for inhibiting bone loss under weightlessness and confirms the NAR capacity in treating microgravity-stimulated bone loss, giving new ideas and methods for future space medicine development.


## Introduction
  The microgravity environment encountered by astronauts throughout spaceflight directly affects several bodily systems.1 Exposure to a microgravity environment significantly results in bone loss, increasing the risk of fractures and irreparable skeletal damage. This seriously threatens the health of astronauts and limits human exploration of space.2−4 Previous studies have reported that Soviet astronauts who stayed six months in space had an average 10% hindrance in calcaneal bone mineral density (BMD) and a 5% decrease in spine BMD.5 The BMD of astronauts in space diminishes by around 1%–2% monthly, paralleling the yearly bone loss of postmenopausal women.6 Drug therapy and mechanical stimulation have shown good efficacy in treating bone loss caused by weightlessness. However, there are still certain limitations to these methods.7,8 Although chemicals like bisphosphonates can effectively alleviate bone loss under simulated microgravity (SMG), they possess serious side consequences on the human body, such as osteonecrosis of the jaw and headache.9−12 Recent research has reported the increasing attention of scientists toward natural medicines due to their significant biological efficacy and few adverse effects. Therefore, finding safe, natural drugs to prevent and treat bone loss caused by weightlessness will be the emphasis of the next space medicine investigations.

  Pyroptosis is a distinctive cell death kind newly identified in connection with inflammation, whereby inflammasome activation is pivotal to the pyroptotic process. Pyroptosis encompasses both classical and nonclassical inflammasome mechanisms. Classical pyroptosis is intricately connected with the NOD-like receptor pyrin domain-containing protein 3 (NLRP3) inflammasome complex, which comprises NLRP3 and apoptosis-associated speck-like protein (ASC).13 ASC, which functions as a connector protein, involves the pyrin domain (PYD) and the caspase recruitment domain, whereas NLRP3, a receptor protein, is composed of three domains that encompass the PYD. The NLRP3 inflammasome is activated by the interaction between the PYD of ASC and the PYD of NLRP3.14 The activated NLRP3 inflammasome can lead to an inflammatory response by inducing caspase-1 (Cas-1), thereby triggering pyroptosis.15,16

  The traditional Chinese medicine Drynariae Rhizoma is a classic herbal medicine used to treat osteopenia and fractures because it nourishes the kidneys, strengthens the bones, and relieves injuries. Its main active ingredient, naringenin, has been favored by scientists in recent years. Naringenin (4,5,7-trihydroxyflavone, NAR) is a naturally existing dihydroflavone compound. In addition to being widely present in traditional Chinese medicine, citrus fruits and tomatoes are abundant in D. Rhizoma.17 It possesses many biological implications, encompassing anti-inflammatory,18 anticancer,19 and antioxidant functions.20 La et al.21 have found that NAR can inhibit osteoclast generation and bone resorption to improve BMD. However, there is still a lack of research on whether NAR can treat bone loss caused by SMG.

  Herein, we used a well-established SMG animal model created by Jee et al.22 Rats positioned at a head-down tilt demonstrate decreased bone mass and mechanical strength.23,24 Therefore, we used this model to simulate the Earth’s weightlessness state and explore the NAR implications on bone loss and its possible mechanism in vivo. Moreover, the impact of NAR on the function of primary osteoblasts (OBs) cultured in a rotating wall tube bioreactor was ascertained. This study provides a promising treatment option for bone loss resulting from spaceflight.


## Materials and Methods

### SMG Mouse Models and NAR Treatment
  Male C57BL/6 J mice that were two months old were obtained from the Animal Experimental Center of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Harbin Medical University (Harbin, China). They were located in an incubator maintained at 21 °C and a 12 h light/dark cycle. There were 4 groups to which the mice were randomly assigned, each consisting of 5 mice: [1] Control; [2] SMG; [3] SMG + NAR (L); and [4] SMG + NAR (H) groups; the hind limb unloading (HLU) is a previously reported model of bone loss caused by mechanical unloading.22 Surgical tape was attached and tied on nearly 1/3 of the mouse’s tail. The tape was connected to the top of the cage with a metal ring to ensure the mouse’s forelimbs could fully enter the cage and touch the ground. The mouse’s body should be positioned to the horizontal plane at a 30–40° angle, and the hindlimbs cannot have any contact surface and can move freely, allowing them to move freely and obtain food and water for 4 weeks. The Control group was mice without tail suspension in the cage; the SMG group was mice with tail suspension; the mice in the SMG + NAR (L) group25 were given NAR (60 mg/kg) by gavage, while their tails were suspended in the cage; and the mice in the SMG + NAR (H) group26 were given NAR (100 mg/kg) by gavage, while their tails were suspended in the cage. After 4 weeks of tail suspension, euthanasia was performed on the mice, and the bilateral femurs and tibias were gathered. No mice died during the experiment. All protocols were accepted by the Ethics Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Harbin Medical University. The approval number for this investigation is YJSDW2023-060.


### Microcomputed Tomography Analysis
  The fixed distal femur specimens were analyzed with microcomputed tomography (micro-CT) technology (SCANCO, Switzerland) to precisely ascertain the variations in bone mass and microstructure between the groups. The voxel dimension was 17.5 μm, the voltage was established at 70 kV, and the current was 800 μA. Scanned pictures were rebuilt into three-dimensional geometric forms with N-Recon software for a morphometric study. The bone microstructure of each group was assessed by examining the trabecular bone volume (BV/TV), trabecular bone thickness (Tb.Th, mm), trabecular number (Tb.N, 1/mm), and trabecular separation (Tb.Sp, mm).


### Biomechanical Test of the Bone
  The femur’s biomechanical characteristics were assessed with a three-point bending test, and related parameters were recorded.27,28 First, the femur stored in an −80° refrigerator (Eppendorf) was thawed in physiological saline and then tested on a universal testing machine (AGXplus, daojin, China). Ten the two brackets was 16 mm, and the working speed was set at 1 mm/min until the femur was fractured. The obtained data were collected and analyzed: the maximum load (N), roughness (J/mm2), stiffness (N/mm), maximum stress (MPa), and Young’s modulus (GPa).


### Histological Examination
  Histological analysis in this investigation mainly included hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) and immunohistochemical labeling. The mice femurs were decalcified with 10% ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid decalcifying solution (LEAGENE, Beijing, China) for 21 days, following which they were preserved in 4% paraformaldehyde and afterward embedded in paraffin. Post sectioning, the morphology was examined with H&E staining. Following deparaffinization, the tissue slices were blocked with 5% goat serum, treated with primary antibodies, stored at 4 °C in a refrigerator overnight, stained with the DAB chromogenic kit (ZSGB-BIO, Beijing, China) for 5 min, and afterward counterstained with hematoxylin. Slides were photographed using a confocal microscope (ZEISS, Germany).


### OB Extraction and Culture
  Primary OBs were extracted from the skulls of newborn C57BL/6 J mice aged 1 to 2 days. The isolated primary OBs were cultivated in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (Solarbio, Beijing, China) enriched with 10% fetal bovine serum (TIANHANG, Zhejiang, China) and 1% penicillin and streptomycin (Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China), and incubated at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 atmosphere. When the cells were fused to approximately 90%, they were digested with a trypsin cell digestion solution (Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China) and then subcultured. The OBs from the third passage were employed in our subsequent investigations.


### Cell SMG State
  For cell experiments, according to a previously described method, a Rotating Wall Vessel (2D-RWVS) was implemented to replicate the microgravity implications in vitro. The cells were injected on a 6-well plate, which was then coated with a blood film. Following the adhesion of the cells to the wall, they were incubated for 72 h at 30 rpm, after which the cells were harvested for further experimental procedures.


### Cell Counting Kit 8 Experiment
  The OB vitality was estimated with a cell counting kit 8 (CCK-8) experiment. In 96-well plates, OBs were inoculated at a density of 5 × 103 cells. Subsequent to the intervention under varying circumstances, 10 μL of the CCK-8 working solution was distributed into each well (Solarbio, Beijing, China), and it was incubated in the dark at 37 °C for 2 h. Each well absorbance was ascertained at 450 nm with enzyme-labeled equipment (Sigma, Shanghai, China).


### Quantitative Real-Time Polymerase Chain Reaction
  Aligning with the manufacturer’s recommendations, TRIzol reagent (Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China) was implemented to isolate the total RNA of OBs. The RNA was afterward reverse transcribed into cDNA, and quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) was conducted. All experiments were normalized with glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase as the housekeeping gene, and the 2–ΔΔCT approach analyzed the target gene. The primer information is displayed in Table 1.


### Western Blotting
  Initially, the cells were rinsed for two rounds with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), afterward extracting total cellular protein with radioimmunoprecipitation assay lysis buffer (Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China), with levels ascertained with a BCA protein assay kit (SEVEN, Beijing, China). Furthermore, an equivalent quantity of protein was isolated with 12.5% sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis and moved into a poly(vinylidene fluoride) membrane, which was further incubated in 5% skim milk. Subsequent to a 2 h blocking interval, the membranes were rinsed 3 rounds with TBST for 10 min each, after which the designated primary antibody was incubated at 4 °C in a refrigerator overnight. Antibodies utilized include NLRP3, ASC, gasdermin D (GSDMD), GSDMD-N, IL-18, heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1), Runt-related transcription factor 2 (Runx2), and bone morphogenetic protein 2 (BMP-2) at a dilution of 1:2000, nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (Nrf2), cleaved Cas-1, interleukin (IL)-1β, Kelch-like ECH-associated protein, alkaline phosphatase (ALP), and osteopontin (OPN) at a dilution of 1:1000. Thereafter, prior to 1 h incubation with the secondary antibody (goat antirabbit IgG, 1:20,000) at 37 °C, the membrane was rinsed with TBST for 30 min. The bands were ultimately estimated via gel imaging equipment and an ECL luminescent substrate kit. All target genes were performed in 3 separate assays, with β-actin operating as the reference for normalization.


### ALP Staining
  A 5-bromo-4-chloro-3-indolyl phosphate/nitroblue tetrazole ALP chromogenic kit was implemented to conduct ALP staining (Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China) to evaluate the differentiation ability of the OBs. The treated cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min following 3 washes with PBS, incubated in a 5-bromo-4-chloro-3-indolyl phosphate/nitroblue tetrazole working solution for 2 h in darkness, and then photographed using a microscope.


### Alizarin Red S Staining
  Alizarin red S (ARS) staining was implemented to estimate the OB’s mineralization function. After PBS washing, a 15 min fixation with 4% paraformaldehyde was conducted for the treated OBs, rinsed 3 rounds with PBS, and dyed with a 0.5% alizarin red (Solarbio, Beijing, China) solution for 15 min. The staining was concluded upon the red coloration of the calcium nodules. The mineralized nodules were ultimately scanned microscopically, and the documentation was preserved.


### Detection of Reactive Oxygen Species
  Intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) were identified with a 2′,7′-dichlorodihydrofluorescein diacetate (DCFH-DA) detection kit (Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China). Cells were rinsed with PBS after SMG or NAR intervention; then, the DCFH-DA working solution was introduced, incubated in the darkness at 37 °C for 60 min, and afterward rinsed 3 rounds with PBS. An inverted fluorescent microscope was implemented to examine the fluorescence intensity (Zeiss, Germany) and processed with ImageJ software.


### Mitochondrial Function Assays
  In this experiment, the detection of mitochondrial function is mainly accomplished by observing the changes in the mitochondria membrane potential (MMP) and mitochondrial ROS (mtROS). MMP depolarization was estimated with 5,5′,6,6′-tetrachloro-1,1′,3,3′-tetraethyl benzimidazolyl-carbocyanine iodide (JC-1). In accordance with the JC-1 kit instructions (MCE, USA), the cells were inoculated on a six-well plate. Following the cells adherence, different methods were used to intervene, and then, the JC-1 reagent dye was used for 30 min at 37°. Subsequent to the PBS wash, images were captured with an inverted fluorescence microscope (Zeiss, Germany). MitoSOX was used to evaluate changes in mtROS, and MitoTracker was used to track and stain mitochondria. Finally, an inverted fluorescence microscope (Zeiss, Germany) was implemented to quantify mtROS and obtain fluorescence images.


### Immunofluorescence Staining
  The cells were implanted in a 12-well plate, and subsequent processing was conducted once the cells reached a stable state. A 15 min fixation in 4% paraformaldehyde was conducted for the specimens following the intervention. Following this, they were permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100 (Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China) for 10 min and subsequently occluded with a 5% bovine serum albumin solution (Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China) for 30 min. Afterward, the specimens were rinsed three times with PBS, incubated with the primary antibody overnight, and rinsed for 3 additional rounds with PBS. Subsequently, they were treated with a fluorescent secondary antibody at 37 °C for 60 min in the absence of light and stained with DAPI for 3 min. An inverted fluorescent microscope (Zeiss, Germany) was applied for imaging, and the fluorescence intensity was assessed using ImageJ software.


### Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay
  Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) was conducted aligning with the manufacturer’s recommendations (Jianglai Biotechnology, Shanghai, China). In summary, the coated plate was afterward covered and incubated at 37 °C for 60 min followed by introduction of 100 μL of cell supernatant or standard solution and a 60 min incubation with enzyme-conjugated antibody, and then, it was washed 5 times. 3,3′,5,5′-Tetramethylbenzidine dihydrochloride was introduced and incubated in the absence of light for 15 min. Each well optical density was then quantified at 450 nm.


### Detection of Lactate Dehydrogenase Release, Superoxide Dismutase,
and Glutathione
  In a 6-well plate, the primary OBs were dispersed at a density of 5 × 105 and maintained overnight. Then, the SMG of the environment and NAR were used to intervene. Subsequently, the cell supernatant was collected and examined with a lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) detection kit. The superoxide dismutase (SOD), malondialdehyde (MDA), and glutathione (GSH) activities were determined to align with the instructions.


### Statistical Analysis
  All data were examined with GraphPad Prism version 9. In the figures, data are stated as means ± standard deviations. The data were compared across groups with one-way analysis of variance in all statistical investigations, with significance deemed at P < 0.05. Outcomes of statistical significance are reported in the figures as follows: *; #; $; &; %P < 0.05, **; ##; $$; &&; %%P < 0.01, and ***; ###; $$$; &&&; %%%P < 0.001.


## Results

### NAR Impact on the Bone Microstructure
  The HLU mouse model described by Jee et al. was used to simulate bone loss caused by spaceflight microgravity to directly ascertain the NAR implication on bone loss in vivo.22 Micro-CT was employed to estimate alterations in the bone microstructure of the femoral shaft metaphysis as an indicator of bone quality. Figure 1A displays the typical three-dimensional (3D) and two-dimensional pictures of the right femoral shaft metaphyseal cancellous bone. In the Control group, the trabecular bone exhibited a cohesive connection, and the reticular structure was intact. The quantity of trabecula in the SMG group decreased significantly, and this pattern was then inverted after NAR intervention.

  Following 4 weeks of HLU, the BV/TV, Tb.Th, and Tb.N significantly mitigated contrast with the Control group (P < 0.05). Nonetheless, the SMG group exhibited a significant elevation in Tb.Sp (P < 0.01), reporting that HLU inflicted considerable harm to the cancellous bone (Figure 1B,C,E). Following NAR therapy, BV/TV and Tb.N exhibited significant elevations, but Tb.SP showed reduction (P < 0.05). As shown in Figure 1D, the effect of NAR intervention on Tb.Th was not significant (P > 0.05), confirming that the bone microstructure was improved after NAR treatment, and it also played an important role in preserving bone mass in mice.


### NAR Improves the Morphological Characteristics of the Trabecular
Bone and Promotes Osteogenic Differentiation
  H&E staining was implemented to examine the bone trabeculae morphological features in mice. As stated in Figure 2A, the SMG group reported that bone trabecula possessed less density and fewer numbers as opposed to the Control group, but this condition was significantly restored after NAR intervention, which is also consistent with the micro-CT scan results, indicating the protective impact of NAR on bone loss caused by SMG.

  We implemented immunohistochemistry to assess the expression levels of the OPN, Nrf2, and NLRP3 in the metaphysis of the femoral shaft to further validate the function of NAR in facilitating osteogenic differentiation. Figure 2B shows a significant hindrance in the expression of OPN within the SMG group, but this trend was reversed after NAR treatment, and Nrf2 showed a consistent trend, as shown in Figure 2C. However, the NLRP3 expression is opposite (Figure 2D), and the above results also confirm the ability of NAR to promote osteogenic differentiation in another dimension.


### Consequence of NAR on Bone Biomechanics
  We conducted a three-point bending test to ascertain the NAR implications on the biomechanical characteristics of femurs in mice. The SMG group demonstrated significant decreases in biomechanical parameters, encompassing elastic modulus (MPa), elastic load (N), maximum strain (%), and bending stiffness (N·mm–1) (P < 0.05), as opposed to the Control group (Figure 2E–H). The outcomes indicated that SMG diminished the mechanical characteristics of the femur. However, NAR treatment can significantly improve the weakening of mechanical properties caused by SMG. Therefore, our outcomes report that NAR has a significant enhancing consequence on the biomechanics of the femur.


### NAR can Improve SMG-Induced Cytotoxicity and Inhibit Cell Proliferation
  Initially, OBs were intervened with several different dosages of NAR, and cell viability was assessed with CCK-8. In the case of low concentration, the cell viability increased with the increase of NAR concentration, but on the contrary, higher concentrations inhibited cell viability (Figure 3A). Then, NAR was used to pretreat OBs for 12 h, followed by cell simulation in the SMG environment. In comparison to the SMG group, cell viability after NAR pretreatment was significantly enhanced from 5 to 20 μM, reaching its peak at 10 μM. As shown in Figure 3B, the cells pretreated with 5 and 10 μM NAR showed promising potential in reducing the activity of OB cells induced by SMG and were selected for subsequent experiments. In addition, morphological observation of the cells showed that cells in the Control group were long-spindle-shaped and polygonal (Figure 3C). SMG exposure led to changes in cell morphology, mainly manifested by the increase of floating dead cells, and the adherent cells began to shrink and became elongated, while NAR pretreatment could significantly reverse the cell morphology. To further study the cytotoxicity of SMG and the protective effect of NAR on cells, we used an LDH release assay. NAR significantly reduced the release of LDH (Figure 3D), indicating that NAR pretreatment can reduce the cytotoxicity caused by SMG.


### NAR Inhibits the SMG-Induced NLRP3 Inflammasome-Linked Pyroptosis
Pathway and Improves Osteogenic Differentiation
  Prior research has shown that NLRP3 inflammasomes activate downstream Cas-1, secreting proinflammatory cytokines such as IL-1β/-18, mediated by Cas-1.29,30 As illustrated in Figure 4A–H, Western blotting (WB) analysis indicated that SMG intervention resulted in a significant elevation in the NLRP3, ASC, cleaved Cas-1, GSDMD, GSDMD-N, IL-1β/-18 expression levels in OBs (P < 0.05), whereas NAR pretreatment significantly suppressed the NLRP3, ASC, cleaved Cas-1, GSDMD, GSDMD-N, IL-1β/-18 expression (P < 0.05). Moreover, qRT-PCR (Figure 4I–O) and immunofluorescence (IF) staining (Figure 4P) reported that NAR inhibited pyroptosis, which is aligned with the outcomes of WB. In summary, NAR pretreatment can inhibit the pathway of pyroptosis related to inflammatory corpuscles of OBs induced by SMG, and it also shows that NAR has a powerful anti-inflammatory ability.

  SMG intervention stimulates the activation of the pyroptosis pathway related to inflammatory corpuscles and leads to the disorder of osteogenic differentiation, which further affects the changes in BMD. As shown in Figure 5A–E, SMG intervention significantly hindered the ALP, Runx2, BMP2, and OPN expression (P < 0.05), and this trend was significantly elevated after NAR pretreatment. In addition, qRT-PCR (Figure 5F–I) and IF staining (Figure 5J,K) confirmed the above trend. ALP and ARS staining techniques were implemented to ascertain the implications of various treatment modalities on the osteogenic differentiation capacity of the OBs. Figure 5L–N illustrates that ALP released by OBs was stained blue on the seventh day, whereas calcium nodules formed on the twenty-first day were stained red with the ARS staining solution. The findings reported that in contrast with the Control group, SMG intervention mitigated the ALP and calcium nodules synthesis in OBs, but NAR pretreatment could somewhat mitigate the adverse effects of SMG on the osteogenic differentiation capacity of OB. These results show that NAR pretreatment can improve the osteogenic differentiation disorder caused by SMG and a strong ability to promote osteogenic differentiation.


### NAR Reduces ROS Production and Mitochondrial Dysfunction in
OBs Induced by SMG
  We used the ROS-sensitive fluorescent probes DCFH-DA and MitoSOX Red to ascertain the NAR consequences on SMG-stimulated oxidative stress (OS) in OBs via measuring intracellular ROS and mtROS generation, respectively. As seen in Figure 6A,B, SMG exposure significantly elevated ROS generation in OBs contrasted with the Control group (P < 0.05), while NAR pretreatment significantly mitigated intracellular ROS (P < 0.05). Under normal circumstances, ROS are typical metabolic byproducts of mitochondria; nonetheless, their overproduction serves as a significant facilitator of cellular death responding to diverse stimuli.31,32 Mitochondria serve as the primary origin of intracellular ROS generation.33 Mitochondrial dysfunction leads to excessive production of mtROS, which can induce oxidative damage and lead to cell death.34 As shown in Figure 6C,D, SMG intervention promoted the production of mtROS. However, NAR pretreatment could severely attenuate SMG-induced mtROS overproduction, highlighting the powerful antioxidant capacity of NAR.

  Mitochondria serve as the primary loci for the generation of intracellular ROS and superoxide generation. Excessive generation of ROS may result in the degradation of MMP. Consequently, the JC-1 reagent probe was applied to assess alterations in MMP. The green fluorescence in the SMG group was more pronounced than that in the Control group (Figure 6E,F), while the red fluorescence/green fluorescence was weaker, which indicated SMG-induced MMP and mitochondrial dysfunction in OBs. After NAR pretreatment, the red/green fluorescence ratio was significantly reversed compared with SMG. These results indicate that NAR can stabilize the changes in the MMP of OBs induced by SMG. In summary, SMG intervention can lead to OS and mitochondrial dysfunction of OBs, while NAR has antioxidant properties, which can alleviate SMG-induced cell damage.


### NAR Promotes the Nrf2/HO-1 Pathway in SMG-Stimulated OB Pyroptosis
  The Nrf2 signaling pathway largely regulates redox homeostasis according to the cellular ROS levels. Upon activation, Nrf2 separates from Keap 1 and translocates to the nucleus, resulting in enhanced downstream antioxidant enzymes expression.35 Consequently, we examined whether NAR mitigates SMG-induced cellular damage via the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway. WB, PCR, and IF labeling were implemented to examine the OBs-linked protein expression and to investigate the impact of various treatments on the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway. The SMG intervention, in contrast to the Control group, decreased the Nrf2 and HO-1 expression (P < 0.05) while significantly elevating the Keap 1 expression (P < 0.05), indicating that SMG intervention makes the antioxidant system exhausted (Figure 7A–D). However, NAR pretreatment significantly upregulated the Nrf2 and HO-1 expression (P < 0.05), which indicated that NAR has a powerful antioxidant capacity. Moreover, qRT-PCR (Figure 7E–G) and IF staining (Figure 7H,I) verified that NAR can significantly increase the Nrf2 and HO-1 expression in the antioxidant system to resist cell damage caused by SMG intervention.

  Because endogenous antioxidants, encompassing SOD, GSH, and MDA, are vital in mitigating OS, we used the corresponding kits to detect the antioxidant capacity of OBs. As opposed to the Control group, SMG intervention mitigated the SOD (P < 0.05) and GSH (P < 0.05) activities and significantly increased the MDA expression (P < 0.05), indicating that ROS and superoxide were overproduced (Figure 7J–L). However, NAR pretreatment effectively reversed these changes and also showed antioxidant characteristics. In summary, NAR can significantly improve the antioxidant capacity of OBs under SMG intervention through the Nrf2/HO-1 signaling pathway.


### Nrf2 Deficiency Reversed the Protective Effect of NAR on SMG-Induced
OB Pyroptosis
  We used the specific inhibitor ML385 of Nrf2 in this experiment to further confirm the contribution of the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway in SMG intervention leading to OB pyroptosis. First, NAR was used to pretreat OBs, and then, ML385 was added or not to the system of SMG and NAR intervention to observe the changes in pyroptosis-related indicators. Figures 8A,B demonstrate that the SMG + ML385 group significantly elevated the NLRP3, ASC, cleaved Cas-1, GSDMD, and GSDMD-N levels (P < 0.05). ELISA findings (Figure 8C) indicated a significant rise in the IL-1β/-18 expression levels (P < 0.05). These results further verified that NAR inhibits OB pyroptosis through the Nrf2/HO-1 signaling pathway.


### NAR Protects OBs from SMG-Induced Osteogenic Differentiation
Disorder by Activating the Nrf2/HO-1 Pathway
  The above outcomes have confirmed that the Nrf2 deficiency leads to an increase in pyroptosis. To explore the role of Nrf2 in OB differentiation and whether NAR affects osteogenic differentiation through the Nrf2/HO-1 signaling pathway, NAR and ML385 were used to treat the OBs. Figure 8D–G illustrates that in comparison to the SMG + NAR group, the ALP, RUNX2, OPN, BMP2, Nrf2, and HO-1 expression levels in the SMG + NAR + ML385 group were significantly declined (P < 0.05). In conclusion, the incorporation of ML385 diminished the protective impact of NAR on osteogenic dysfunction induced by SMG intervention, hence further substantiating that NAR facilitates OB differentiation via the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway.


## Discussion
  Osteoporosis is a prevailing skeletal condition marked by mitigated bone density and structural deterioration, leading to brittle bones susceptible to fractures.36,37 Bone loss resulting from microgravity poses a significant hazard to astronaut health and is a primary challenge for extended space missions.38 Therefore, this study aimed at finding potential natural drugs with higher biological activity and fewer side effects. Studies have found that NAR can maintain bone mass by improving the BMD bone microstructure and promoting OB differentiation. This discovery may provide a new idea and method for treating bone loss caused by microgravity.

  With the increasingly prominent role of ROS in osteoporosis, studies have found that senile, menopausal, and secondary osteoporosis are all related to OS.39,40 Moreover, OS is one of the most studied events when bone tissue reacts to microgravity.41 Increasing evidence shows that excessive ROS produced in the bone interfere with OB differentiation and prevent OB activity.42 Therefore, decreased OB differentiation and bone formation are the main causes of microgravity-induced osteoporosis.43,44 OS resulting from high ROS generation impairs mineral tissue homeostasis and bone remodeling by restricting bone resorption.45−49 Previous studies have also confirmed that ROS is an important stimulus to trigger inflammasome activation of NLRP3,50,51 and mitochondrial dysfunction can also produce a large number of ROS52 which has been fully confirmed in our study. Therefore, it is necessary to additionally investigate whether the protective consequence of NAR on the activation of inflammatory corpuscles of NLRP3 in OBs mediated by SMG is related to the regulation of ROS.

  Pyroptosis is a nonconventional programmed cell death pathway akin to apoptosis and characterized by inflammatory soluble cell death pathway.53,54 It is often initiated by the NLRP3 inflammasome and Cas-1.55 Pyroptosis frequently happens with the Cas-1 activation since active Cas-1 cleaves the GSDMD protein into GSDMD-N, forming pores in the cell membrane and the subsequent secretion of proinflammatory cytokines, encompassing IL-18/-1β.56,57 In this study, by constructing classic animal and cell models mediated by SMG, it is confirmed that when SMG causes an elevated in intracellular ROS, the expression of NLRP3 inflammasome-linked genes is also elevated. Mitochondria, being the primary source of intracellular ROS production,33 are crucial for energy generation and are intimately involved in cell death and inflammasome activation.58 The mitochondrial dynamics equilibrium is critical for homeostasis of mitochondrial function. Mitochondrial dysfunction leads to mtROS overloading and MMP change, damaging mitochondrial protein and DNA and excessive mtROS production and NLRP3 inflammasome activation.59−61 Previous studies have shown that the MMP decrease is an early event in apoptosis. Once the MMP is determined to be exhausted, cells initiate the apoptosis pathway.62 Shen et al.63 have found that bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells showed reduced osteogenesis-linked gene expression, weakened ALP activity, and reduced mineralized nodule formation after mitochondrial dysfunction, thus weakening the damage of pyroptosis mediated by NLRP3 inflammation to OBs.

  NAR is a polyphenolic substance that exists in citrus fruits. It has garnered the interest of researchers across several disciplines due to its excellent bioavailability, little cytotoxicity, and notable anti-inflammatory and antioxidant characteristics.64−67 The Nrf2/HO-1 pathway is a critical intracellular mechanism for inhibiting ROS. Nrf2 promotes antioxidant activity by elevating the HO-1 protein expression.68 Prior research has shown that NAR may modulate the OS via the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway. Pan et al.25 have recently confirmed that NAR can regulate osteoarthritis caused by iron overload by controlling the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway and influencing OS. Zhang et al.69 have shown that NAR regulates the endoplasmic reticulum stress via the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway to alleviate renal ischemia-reperfusion injury. In this study, while SMG caused mitochondrial dysfunction and intracellular ROS overload in OBs, we exhibited that the Nrf2 and HO-1 gene expression was mitigated; this is consistent with the previously confirmed conclusion.12,70 However, NAR reversed this phenomenon after an early intervention. Loboda et al.71 have confirmed in their recent study that the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway is essential for mitigating intracellular OS and safeguarding cells from ROS damage. Many investigations have verified that the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway regulates redox homeostasis according to the cell ROS levels. After intracellular ROS overload, Nrf2 dissociates from Keap 1 and translocates to the nucleus, activating the antioxidant response element. This increases the downstream antioxidant enzymes HO-1 and NAD(P)H quinone dehydrogenase 1 expression and maintains cellular homeostasis.35,72 Therefore, dissociating the Nrf2-Keap1 complex is a protective mechanism of cells, which helps eliminate intracellular overloaded ROS to resist OS and inflammatory damage.73 Herein, we found that the intervention of NAR inhibited the NLRP3 inflammasome expression and elevated the OBs proliferation and differentiation, reversing the decrease of bone mass in C57BL/6 J mice caused by SMG. We introduced Nrf2-specific inhibitor ML385 to further explore whether NAR regulates OB pyroptosis and thus affects osteogenic differentiation by affecting the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway. In the study, we found that ML385 treatment weakened the antioxidant and -inflammatory features of NAR, which not only mitigated the Nrf2 and HO-1 proteins expression but also mitigated the osteogenic differentiation while increasing the expression of inflammatory corpuscles of NLRP3, reversing the positive effect of NAR on bone loss. Huang et al.74 have demonstrated that stimulating the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway can inhibit NLRP3 inflammation and pyroptosis, which is aligned with the results of this study. In summary, this investigation proves the efficacy of NAR in mitigating bone loss stimulated by microgravity via stimulation of the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway. This signaling pathway is critical in reversing SMG-induced pyroptosis of OB and mitochondrial dysfunction while enhancing osteogenic differentiation capacity. These results confirm the potential of NAR in treating bone loss under SMG and offer unique perspectives into the function of NAR in OBs.
