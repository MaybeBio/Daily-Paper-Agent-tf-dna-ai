At present, a major effort in biophysical studies has been paid towards exploring the interactions and release of therapeutic payloads to the specific site leaving behind healthy cells unaffected and hence, lower the drug-induced toxicity. For the purpose, interaction of beta-bound CUR with calf thymus DNA (ctDNA) has been examined intensely using a series of biophysical methods like absorption, steady state fluorescence emission, and circular dichroism together with molecular docking study. The experimental analysis divulge that CUR interacts with both beta-CD (although with different molar ratio) and DNA. However, the binding affinity of CUR with the target (DNA) is higher than it does with the beta-CD. When beta-CD-carried (10 mM) CUR (muM) (inclusion complex) comes near DNA (15-372 muM), CUR gets out from beta-CD's void and approaches to binds with the DNA. The relocation of the probe occurred due to competitive binding of the CUR between beta-CD and the DNA. The present investigation may provide a simple yet probable route for the transfer of encapsulated therapeutic payload of beta-CD to the most relevant biomolecular target DNA.
