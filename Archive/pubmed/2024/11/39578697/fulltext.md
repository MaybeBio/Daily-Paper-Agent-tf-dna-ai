# miRStart 2.0: enhancing miRNA regulatory insights through deep learning-based TSS identification


## Abstract
  MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are small non-coding RNAs that regulate gene expression by binding to the 3′-untranslated regions of target mRNAs, influencing various biological processes at the post-transcriptional level. Identifying miRNA transcription start sites (TSSs) and transcription factors’ (TFs) regulatory roles is crucial for elucidating miRNA function and transcriptional regulation. miRStart 2.0 integrates over 4500 high-throughput datasets across five data types, utilizing a multi-modal approach to annotate 28 828 putative TSSs for 1745 human and 1181 mouse miRNAs, supported by sequencing-based signals. Over 6 million tissue-specific TF–miRNA interactions, integrated from ChIP-seq data, are supplemented by DNase hypersensitivity and UCSC conservation data, with network visualizations. Our deep learning-based model outperforms existing tools in miRNA TSS prediction, achieving the most overlaps with both cell-specific and non-cell-specific validated TSSs. The user-friendly web interface and visualization tools make miRStart 2.0 easily accessible to researchers, enabling efficient identification of miRNA upstream regulatory elements in relation to their TSSs. This updated database provides systems-level insights into gene regulation and disease mechanisms, offering a valuable resource for translational research, facilitating the discovery of novel therapeutic targets and precision medicine strategies. miRStart 2.0 is now accessible at https://awi.cuhk.edu.cn/∼miRStart2.


## Introduction
  MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are a class of endogenous, non-coding RNAs ∼22 nucleotides in length (1). Since their discovery in mammals in 2001 (2), miRNAs have become a focal point of genetic research, reshaping the understanding of gene regulatory networks and their roles in health and disease. miRNAs are pivotal in post-transcriptional regulation of mRNAs, often through sequence-specific base pairing that leads to mRNA destabilization or translation inhibition (3). These small RNAs are involved in a wide range of physiological processes, and their dysregulation is linked to numerous diseases (4).

  Understanding how miRNAs are regulated at the transcriptional level, particularly by characterizing their transcription start sites (TSSs) and associated promoter regions, is essential for fully integrating miRNAs into gene regulatory networks. Despite significant advances in miRNA function (5,6), research on miRNA gene promoters and their transcriptional regulators has lagged due to the complex miRNA biogenesis. miRNA biogenesis begins with the transcription of primary miRNAs (pri-miRNAs) by RNA polymerase II (Pol II) (7). These pri-miRNAs have a 5′ cap, a polyadenylated tail and vary in length. The Drosha–DGCR8 complex cleaves them to produce precursor miRNAs (pre-miRNAs), which are then exported to the cytoplasm, further processed by Dicer and loaded onto Argonaute proteins, forming the RNA-induced silencing complex (8). Unlike protein-coding genes, the TSS of a pri-miRNA transcript can be far upstream from the mature miRNA sequence (9).

  To accurately locate miRNA TSSs, several sequencing methods have been developed. Cap analysis of gene expression sequencing (CAGE-seq) is a high-throughput technique that captures and quantifies the 5′ ends of capped RNAs, providing precise genome-wide TSS identification (10). Similarly, transcription start site sequencing (TSS-seq) focuses on sequencing the 5′ cap region, with sensitivity in distinguishing between multiple TSSs within a gene (11). Additionally, profiling transcriptional activation markers through chromatin immunoprecipitation sequencing (ChIP-seq) for H3K4me3 and RNA Pol II, as well as DNase I hypersensitivity sequencing (DNase-seq), supports TSS identification by mapping open chromatin regions accessible to transcription factors (TFs) (12).

  With the availability of these data, recent computational methods using deep learning, such as DeepTSS (13) and D-miRT (14), have emerged to predict miRNA TSSs in a cell-specific manner. primirTSS (15), developed by Li et al., analyzes H3K4me3-enriched regions and DNase-seq data to identify putative TSSs. Tools such as microTSS (9) integrates CAGE-seq and RNA-seq data with chromatin features to identify TSSs across various cell types. Databases such as miRTrans (16) and DIANA-miRGen v4 (17) further annotate cell-specific miRNA TSSs using ChIP-seq, CAGE-seq and DNase-seq data across multiple tissues and cell lines.

  Initially developed in 2011, miRStart was the first to annotate human miRNA TSSs, combining machine learning and high-throughput data to provide evidence for miRNA transcriptional regulation (18). In this update, miRStart 2.0 delivers high-quality miRNA TSS annotations and associated TFs by integrating high-throughput datasets with a robust deep learning model, ensuring high accuracy in both human and mouse. Curated TF data further support tissue-specific investigations of upstream regulatory effects on miRNAs. The interactive web browsers enable users to visualize these annotations, providing deeper insights into miRNA gene regulation for researchers.


## System overview and database content
  miRStart was initially developed in 2011 to identify miRNA TSSs using high-throughput experimental data. Over the past decade, advancements in miRNA annotation and next-generation sequencing technologies have expanded the scope and depth of miRNA research. In this major update, miRStart 2.0 integrates a high-resolution deep learning-based signal classification model with extensive gene transcription data from leading databases such as FANTOM (19), DBTSS (20) and ENCODE (21). Our deep convolutional neural network (CNN) model employs a multi-modal approach, incorporating data from CAGE-seq, TSS-seq, ChIP-seq (H3K4me3, Pol II) and DNase-seq, to differentiate miRNA TSS signals from background noise, ensuring robustness across both high- and low-resolution data while avoiding potential issues such as overfitting. With this model, the miRStart 2.0 database covers over 3000 human samples and 1000 mouse samples, identifying 28 828 TSSs for over 1700 human miRNAs and 1100 mouse miRNAs, supported by sequencing-based transcription initiation signals. Additionally, the database integrates 50 126 experiments from ChIP-Atlas (22) and identified 6 200 615 tissue-specific TF–miRNA associations across miRNA promoter regions, supported by DNase hypersensitive sites and conservation data. This comprehensive dataset is accessible through a user-friendly web interface, allowing interactive search, visualization and exploration of miRNA gene regulation in diverse biological contexts. Figure 1 provides an overview of the design and content of miRStart 2.0.


## Methods and results

### Collection of TSS-relevant datasets
  In order to comprehensively identify the TSS annotation pattern, various types of high-throughput sequencing data were integrated from public databases, including CAGE-seq from the Functional Annotation of the Mammalian Genome (FANTOM 5) project (23), TSS-seq from DataBase of Transcriptional Start Sites (DBTSS 10.1) (20) and ChIP-seq data from the ENCODE database (21), which includes H3K4me3 histone modifications, RNA Pol II binding sites and DNase-seq data. These datasets curated >2800 human and 700 mouse samples across different cell lines and tissues. For experimentally validated TSSs information, a total of 59 977 and 41 037 protein-coding transcript (PCT) TSSs with high confidence level were downloaded from GENCODE v46 for human and GENCODE M35 for mouse (24). Moreover, 24 miRNAs with non-cell-specific TSSs and 61 hESC-specific miRNAs TSSs were collected as the benchmark data (9,25). All data have been downloaded as or converted to hg38 and mm39 by UCSC liftOver tool (26).


### miRNA TSSs annotation
  We developed a deep learning-based CNN model for miRStart 2.0 to identify miRNA TSSs by integrating multi-omics data. To optimize the model’s architecture and parameters, we tested various structures and dataset configurations (Supplementary Table S1). The final model, consisting of two convolutional layers and three fully connected layers, was trained using experimentally validated TSS data from PCT. Positive and negative regions (2200 bp length) relative to each TSS were divided with a window size of 20 bp. Sequencing data from CAGE-seq, DBTSS-seq, DNase-seq, H3K4me3 and Pol II ChIP-seq were mapped onto these regions, yielding a matrix with 550 features from 59 777 verified TSSs (Supplementary Figure S1).

  For miRNA TSS identification, we compiled data on 1745 human and 1181 mouse pre-miRNAs from the miRBase v22.1 database (27). Previous studies have shown that the distance between miRNA TSSs and their corresponding pre-miRNAs varies, with most located within 100 kb upstream of the pre-miRNA (9,18,25,28). Using the miRStart 2.0 model, we scanned up to 100 kb upstream for each pre-miRNA. Peak signals within this region that exceeded a threshold of 0.7 were flagged as putative TSSs and curated in our database, resulting the identification of 13 597 human miRNA TSSs and 15 231 mouse miRNA TSSs from pooled data samples. In human, predicted TSSs predominantly cluster near the 5′ end of pre-miRNAs, with a marked decrease in frequency as distance increases, whereas in mouse, TSSs exhibit a more variable distribution without a clear positional preference (Supplementary Figure S2).

  To evaluate miRStart 2.0′s performance, we compared it with other recent methods, such as primirTSS (2020) (15), D-miRT (2021) (14) and DeepTSS (2022) (13), using an independent test dataset of 2000 PCT samples. miRStart 2.0 consistently outperforms these methods across key metrics for both human and mouse datasets, demonstrating its accuracy and robustness in TSS identification (Figure 2A and B). We further validated the model by benchmarking it against experimentally verified TSS data from 24 non-cell-specific miRNAs (Supplementary Table S2) and 61 hESC-specific miRNAs (Supplementary Table S3), as collected by Hua et al. (25). Incorporating data annotated by Hua et al., we compare the relative distances between the closest identified TSSs and experimentally validated TSSs, with successful identifications considered to be within 2 kb of the validated TSS.

  As shown in Figure 2, miRStart 2.0 correctly identified the validated TSSs for miRNAs in both non-cell-specific and hESC datasets, with 83.3% and 93.4% of predictions exactly matching the validated TSSs (0 bp distance), respectively (Figure 2C and D). The remaining miRNAs were predicted within 500 bp of the validated TSS, highlighting the model’s accuracy. In contrast, other tools predicted TSSs with wider variations, including ranges of 100–500, 500–2000 and beyond 2000 bp. The box plot (Figure 2E and F) demonstrates that miRStart 2.0 provides more accurate and consistent miRNA TSS predictions, with fewer outliers, lower mean distances and smaller interquartile ranges compared to other methods. These findings affirm miRStart 2.0’s superior accuracy in TSS prediction, reinforcing the reliability of its annotations, making miRStart 2.0 database a valuable resource for systematic research across diverse contexts.


### Identification of TF–miRNA regulations
  miRStart 2.0 enhances the annotation of TF regulations linked to miRNA TSSs by integrating tissue-specific ChIP-seq and DNase-seq data from the ChIP-Atlas database (22). Using a stringent score threshold of 200, the platform curates 1675 TFs in human and 899 in mouse datasets. To refine TF binding site identification, miRStart 2.0 incorporates chromatin accessibility data from DNase-seq and conservation information from the UCSC Genome Browser (29), pinpointing regions that are highly supportive of TF binding. All data were mapped to the hg38 and mm39 genome assemblies via the UCSC liftOver tool (26).

  Through BEDTools (30), overlaps between ChIP-seq peak regions and miRNA promoters were identified, revealing experimentally validated TF binding sites within miRNA promoter regions. The platform also allows users to customize promoter regions, ranging from 10 000 bp upstream to 1000 bp downstream of each miRNA TSS, to explore potential TF interactions. This analysis resulted in over 6 million TF–miRNA regulatory interactions. Statistical evaluation of tissue-specific data revealed that in both humans and mice, blood tissue, which consists primarily of immune cells, had the highest number of TF–miRNA interactions. In humans, this was followed by breast tissue, while in mice, pluripotent stem cells had the second most interactions. Conversely, embryonic tissue in humans and spleen tissue in mice exhibited the fewest TF–miRNA interactions (Supplementary Figure S3).

  miRStart 2.0 compiles these interactions with detailed annotations, providing a comprehensive resource for TF–miRNA regulation. The platform offers an interactive network visualization, highlighting experimentally validated TFs within an miRNA regulatory framework, facilitating deeper insights into the regulation of miRNAs across tissues and species.


### Summary of database contents and statistics of miRStart 2.0
  miRStart 2.0 significantly improves the reliability of miRNA upstream regulation, focusing on global miRNA TSS identification and tissue-specific TF–miRNA interactions. Table 1 outlines these advancements, comparing miRStart 2.0 to miRStart 1.0 and other miRNA TSS databases. miRStart 2.0 features a greater number of miRNA annotations and TF–miRNA interactions for both human and mouse datasets. It identifies 6 200 615 experimentally supported TF–miRNA associations derived from 28 828 TSSs for 2926 miRNAs, based on over 60 000 high-throughput sequencing experiments and an enhanced deep learning model. This integrative dataset enhances the accuracy of miRNA TSS identification while providing detailed tissue-specific TF binding information, expanding the platform’s utility and relevance. Moreover, we conducted a comprehensive comparison of the TSS for 322 miRNAs identified by miRStart 2.0 with those reported in a previous review study (31) that incorporated data from 14 different datasets, as shown in Supplementary Table S4.


## Web interface
  The integrated data in miRStart 2.0 database is accessible through a user-friendly web interface (Figure 3). Users can search for information by selecting either the ‘By miRNA’ or ‘By Transcription Factor’ option, with both human and mouse data supported. In miRNA search mode, results are displayed across three pages. The first page, ‘miRNA Information’, provides detailed information about the selected miRNA, including basic annotation (such as pre-miRNA information, miRNA classifications, hosted protein-coding gene or lncRNA if applicable and associated mature miRNAs), identified TSS lists (including the score of TSSs predicted by model and TSS supporting evidence) and visualizations of TSS signals processed by our model and in real sequencing data. The second page lists TF–miRNA interactions based on the identified miRNA TSSs. The associated cell groups are linked to the SRA database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sra) for detailed experimental settings, and the conservation scores can be further linked to UCSC Genome Browser (29) for visualization of cross-species TF conservation. To assess TF–miRNA interaction confidence, we provide three features: ‘Significance’, based on MACS2 results, reflecting peak statistical reliability; overlap with DNase I hypersensitive sites, indicating active regulatory regions; and conservation scores, representing evolutionary importance. These factors are combined into a normalized summation, forming a ‘Confidence’ score to help users identify the most reliable TF–miRNA interactions. The third page features a network illustration of tissue-specific TF–miRNA regulations, with different tissues highlighted in different colors.

  In the ‘Transcription Factor’ search mode, all TF–miRNA interactions for the selected TF are displayed, along with supporting evidence from DNase hypersensitive sites and conservation across species. The data presentation is similar to the TF page in the ‘By miRNA’ module. Users can explore specific miRNAs regulated by the chosen TF by clicking on them for more detailed information. Detailed usage tutorials are available on the ‘Help’ page for guidance.


## Case study
  miRStart 2.0 employs a multi-modal approach to accurately annotate miRNA TSSs, enabling precise mapping of miRNA regulatory regions. As shown in Supplementary Table S5, 20 out of 24 non-cell-specific miRNAs’ TSSs are accurately annotated by miRStart 2.0. For example, miRStart 2.0 predicted two TSS sites for miR-21 at chr17:59838821 and chr17:59838204 were confirmed by studies from Cai et al. (32) and Ozsolak et al. (33), respectively. Similarly, miRStart 2.0 identifies the shared TSS at chr13:91349345 for the miR-17-92 cluster (miR-17, miR-18a, miR-19a, miR-19b-1, miR-20a and miR-92a-1) and the shared TSS at chr19:13866339 for miR-181c and miR-181d, both validated by Ozsolak et al. (33). Additional examples include TSS of miR-155 at chr21:25562145, confirmed by Kluiver et al. (34), the TSS of miR-122 at chr18:58446262, verified by Chien et al. (18), the TSS of miR-146b at chr10:102435812 validated by Taganov et al. (35) and the TSS of miR-34a at chr1:9182338, as confirmed by Chang et al. (36). These examples highlight the predictive accuracy of miRStart 2.0, providing a reliable framework for identifying miRNA TSSs based on sequencing data.

  To understand the post-transcriptional effects of miRNAs, inhibition of mature miRNA transcripts is commonly used to investigate their roles in specific conditions (37). Recently, CRISPR/dCas9-based methods, which target miRNAs at the genomic level, offer a more precise approach, particularly for studying miRNA clusters or those with high sequence similarity (38,39). miRStart 2.0 serves as a critical foundation for such experiments by integrating diverse datasets like CAGE-seq and TSS-seq, allowing for accurate identification of both canonical and non-canonical miRNA TSSs. These detailed TSS annotations guide the design of single guide RNAs for CRISPR inhibition, improving the specificity of repression for miRNAs with similar sequences and allowing for precise evaluation of the endogenous regulatory elements involved in miRNA transcription. By providing a reliable reference for miRNA TSS mapping, miRStart 2.0 is valuable for fine-tuned experimental validation.

  Understanding the upstream regulation of miRNAs is critical for deciphering their roles in cancer. In breast cancer, aberrant miRNA expression is closely linked to tumor development and progression. Using miRStart 2.0, we identified the TSSs for the key onco-miRNA miR-21 and mapped the TFs located within 2000 bp upstream and 200 bp downstream of these TSSs. Focusing on breast tissue-specific data, we identified 10 TFs—MED1, BRD4, SIX4, JUN, JUNB, ESR1, TEAD1, FOS, ZNF592 and FOSL2, which were likely involved in regulating miR-21 expression. Among these TFs, MED1, BRD4 and SIX4 were found in one promoter region, while JUN, JUNB, ESR1, TEAD1, FOS, ZNF592 and FOSL2 were associated with another distinct promoter. TEAD1 showed the strongest evidence for regulation.

  Inferred from previous studies, the identified TFs suggest a multi-layered regulatory network. For example, JUN and its AP-1 complex partners (JUNB, FOS and FOSL2) may enhance miR-21 expression in response to stress signals (40), while TEAD1 could collaborate with AP-1 to drive oncogenic pathways via Hippo signaling (41). ESR1’s involvement suggests a link between estrogen signaling and miR-21’s role in hormone-responsive breast cancers (42). Furthermore, potential TF synergies were identified. TEAD1 and AP-1 may work together to amplify miR-21’s oncogenic impact, while BRD4 and MED1 could enhance chromatin accessibility (43), enabling further TF binding and persistent miR-21 transcriptional activation even under adverse conditions such as chemotherapy (44). The presence of these 10 TFs in the regulatory regions of miR-21 highlights a complex interaction network, where co-activators (BRD4 and MED1) may collaborate with specific TFs (TEAD1, JUN and ESR1) to drive miR-21 expression. This likely contributes to tumorigenesis, metastasis and therapy resistance, highlighting the pivotal role of miR-21 in breast cancer. TF interactions, especially within the AP-1 complex, suggest coordinated regulation that amplifies miR-21’s oncogenic effects, offering potential therapeutic targets to disrupt these regulatory networks.


## Discussion
  miRStart 2.0 represents a significant advancement in miRNA TSS identification, offering a comprehensive database with enhanced accuracy and expanded functionalities. The updated model was meticulously trained and validated using high-throughput datasets, integrating both single-resolution and broad-spectrum data to refine TSS predictions. While cell-specific models, which primarily rely on CAGE-seq data, can perform well in specific contexts, they often suffer from limited data types, overfitting and reduced applicability across varied biological environments. By incorporating multi-cell data from various sources, miRStart 2.0 overcomes these limitations, offering a more versatile and robust tool for miRNA TSS identification across a wide range of tissues and conditions. Comparative analysis against existing tools demonstrated miRStart 2.0’s superior performance, particularly its ability to pinpoint miRNA TSSs with greater proximity to experimentally validated sites.

  miRNA expression is closely associated with their TSSs, with different regulatory patterns depending on whether the miRNA is intragenic or intergenic (9). Intragenic miRNAs may be co-transcribed with their host genes, sharing the same TSSs and regulatory elements, although some possess independent promoters, allowing for more flexible expression. Intergenic miRNAs, located outside of protein-coding genes, rely on independent TSSs for autonomous transcriptional control (45). The position of the TSS, along with chromatin modifications, greatly influences miRNA expression, shaping condition-specific expression patterns and impacting miRNA function in gene regulation and cellular processes. By leveraging integrated data types and a deep learning model, miRStart 2.0 can identify precise and complete TSSs, independent of miRNA type, providing a robust framework for analyzing miRNA upstream regulation. The database now includes detailed annotations for over 1700 human and 1100 mouse pre-miRNAs.

  It is well established that TFs play a key role in regulating miRNA expression by binding to specific promoter regions of miRNA genes. This binding can either promote or repress miRNA transcription, depending on the cellular context and the specific TFs involved (46). The specificity of TF binding is closely related to the specificity of miRNA expression, as TFs often bind to distinct promoter regions in a tissue- or condition-specific manner (47). This interplay is particularly evident when both the TF and the corresponding miRNA are co-expressed in the same tissue to perform specialized functions. Tissue-specific TFs tend to participate in a higher number of interactions in organs such as the liver and bone, where distinct regulatory networks are essential for maintaining tissue-specific functions and responses (48). Regarding this issue, miRStart 2.0 integrates over 50 000 tissue-specific TF ChIP-seq experiments, providing a broad annotation of TF–miRNA regulations, supported by DNase hypersensitive sites and conservation data. Its user-friendly web interface allows researchers to explore miRNA and TF regulatory relationships with detailed annotations and visualizations.

  Looking forward, miRStart 2.0 sets the stage for further exploration of miRNA transcriptional regulation, enabling the discovery of novel regulatory elements and their roles in gene expression. miRStart 2.0 database allows systematic exploration of global miRNA regulatory networks with targeted insights into miRNA–TF interactions, offering additional granularity for precise cellular contexts. As a key resource, miRStart 2.0 will be invaluable for researchers seeking to elucidate the complexities of miRNA regulation and its implications in health and disease.
