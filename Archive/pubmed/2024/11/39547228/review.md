## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a significant advance in the field of transcription-coupled DNA repair by establishing a robust cell-free system that recapitulates TC-NER, and by identifying STK19 as a core factor that bridges stalled Pol II to TFIIH. The combination of biochemical reconstitution, high-resolution cryo-EM, and structure-guided mutagenesis provides compelling evidence for the proposed mechanism. The work is technically rigorous and the conclusions are largely supported by the data presented. However, several concerns regarding the interpretation of the STK19-XPD interaction, the quantitative aspects of the repair assays, and the generalizability of the findings warrant careful consideration.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in the fields of DNA repair, transcription, and genome stability will find this work of high interest. The cell-free TC-NER system provides a powerful new tool for dissecting the molecular mechanisms of this pathway, and the identification of STK19 as a key factor fills an important gap in our understanding of how Pol II stalling is coupled to repair. The structural data will also be of interest to structural biologists studying multi-protein complexes involved in genome maintenance.
- **Major strengths** 1) Development of a novel cell-free TC-NER assay that recapitulates all key features of the pathway, providing a much-needed experimental system for mechanistic studies. 2) High-resolution cryo-EM structure (1.9 Å) of the TC-NER complex, revealing the architecture of STK19 binding to Pol II and CSA. 3) Structure-guided mutagenesis that links specific STK19 interfaces to functional outcomes in repair. 4) The identification of STK19 as an essential TC-NER factor, resolving previous ambiguity about its role. 5) The proposed model for how STK19 positions TFIIH for lesion verification is conceptually novel and testable.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Evidence strength for mechanistic claim
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that "STK19 positions TFIIH ahead of Pol II for lesion verification" based on molecular modeling and mutagenesis.
  - **Evidence pointer** Figure 5, Results section "Mutations at the predicted STK19-XPD interface disrupt TC-NER"
  - **Concern** The central mechanistic claim that STK19 positions TFIIH for lesion verification relies on a composite model generated by aligning the STK19-XPD AF-M prediction onto the cryo-EM structure, and on mutagenesis of the predicted interface. However, no direct biochemical or structural evidence is provided for the STK19-XPD interaction in the context of the full TC-NER complex. The mutagenesis data show that disrupting the predicted interface impairs repair, but this could be due to indirect effects on complex stability or other interactions. Direct evidence for the STK19-XPD interaction, such as co-immunoprecipitation, pull-down assays, or cross-linking, is needed to substantiate this key claim.
  - **Why it matters** The proposed mechanism of STK19 positioning TFIIH is the central conceptual advance of the paper. Without direct evidence for the STK19-XPD interaction, the model remains speculative and the interpretation of the mutagenesis data is ambiguous.
  - **Resolution test** Provide direct biochemical evidence for the STK19-XPD interaction (e.g., co-IP, pull-down, or cross-linking) in the context of the TC-NER complex. Alternatively, determine a cryo-EM structure of the TC-NER complex bound to TFIIH to directly visualize the STK19-XPD interface.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Quantitative rigor of repair assays
  - **Claim pointer** The authors report that STK19 mutants impair TC-NER to varying degrees, with the combined mutant inhibiting repair ~4-fold.
  - **Evidence pointer** Figure 5E, Results section "Mutations at the predicted STK19-XPD interface disrupt TC-NER"
  - **Concern** The quantification of repair efficiency appears to rely on gel-based assays with limited dynamic range. The authors report a maximum repair efficiency of ~32%, and the differences between mutant and wild-type conditions are relatively modest (e.g., 50-60% for single mutants, ~4-fold for the combined mutant). It is unclear whether these differences are statistically significant across biological replicates, and whether the assay is sufficiently sensitive to detect subtle but biologically relevant changes in repair efficiency.
  - **Why it matters** The conclusions about the importance of specific STK19 interfaces for repair depend on the reliability and sensitivity of the repair assay. If the assay has high variability or limited dynamic range, the reported differences may not be robust.
  - **Resolution test** Provide detailed statistical analysis (e.g., mean ± SD, number of replicates, statistical tests) for all repair assays. Consider using a more quantitative readout, such as qPCR-based detection of repair products, to confirm the observed differences.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Generalizability of the cell-free system
  - **Claim pointer** The authors state that the cell-free system "recapitulates all features expected of TC-NER" and can be used to study the mechanism.
  - **Evidence pointer** Figures 1–2, Results sections "Cell-free TC-NER in egg extract" and "Cell-free repair requires all canonical TC-NER factors, as well as STK19"
  - **Concern** The cell-free system relies on Xenopus egg extracts, which may not fully recapitulate the chromatin context and regulatory mechanisms present in human cells. The authors use a naked plasmid DNA template, which lacks nucleosome structure and other chromatin-associated factors that may influence TC-NER. Additionally, the requirement for recombinant human CSB (rather than Xenopus CSB) raises questions about species-specific differences.
  - **Why it matters** The physiological relevance of the findings depends on how faithfully the cell-free system recapitulates TC-NER in vivo. If the system lacks important regulatory layers, the conclusions may not be directly applicable to human cells.
  - **Resolution test** Discuss the limitations of the naked DNA template and consider experiments with chromatinized templates. Validate key findings in a human cell-based system, or at least demonstrate that the Xenopus and human factors are functionally interchangeable.

  - **Concern ID** R1-m2

  - **Affected element** Discussion section
  - **Evidence pointer** Discussion, "STK19: lynchpin between TFIIH and stalled Pol II"
  - **Issue** The discussion does not fully address alternative interpretations of the data, such as the possibility that STK19's role is not solely to position TFIIH but also to stabilize the TC-NER complex or regulate Pol II ubiquitylation.
  - **Required correction** Add a paragraph discussing alternative models and how the current data distinguish between them, or what additional experiments would be needed.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m3
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Methodological detail
  - **Affected element** Methods section
  - **Evidence pointer** Methods, "Cell-free TC-NER assay"
  - **Issue** The methods for the repair assay are described in detail, but the exact composition of the "TC-NER cocktail" and the concentrations of each factor are not clearly specified in the main text.
  - **Required correction** Provide a table or a clear list of the components and concentrations of the TC-NER cocktail in the methods or supplementary material.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** 1) Direct biochemical evidence for the STK19-XPD interaction is lacking (R1-M1). 2) The quantitative robustness of the repair assays needs to be demonstrated with appropriate statistical analysis (R1-M2). 3) The physiological relevance of the naked DNA template should be discussed and ideally validated with chromatinized templates (R1-M3).
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** **Originality:** High. The development of a cell-free TC-NER system and the identification of STK19 as a key factor represent a significant conceptual advance. **Scientific importance:** High. The work addresses a long-standing question in the field and provides a new tool for future studies. **Interdisciplinary readership:** Moderate. The work will primarily appeal to DNA repair and transcription specialists, though the structural and biochemical approaches may attract a broader audience. **Technical soundness:** Generally strong, but the lack of direct evidence for the STK19-XPD interaction and the limited quantitative analysis of repair assays are notable weaknesses. **Readability for nonspecialists:** The manuscript is well-written but assumes significant background knowledge in DNA repair and transcription. The figures are complex and may be challenging for nonspecialists.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The manuscript presents a significant advance and the core conclusions are likely correct, but the central mechanistic claim regarding STK19-XPD interaction requires direct experimental support, and the quantitative rigor of the repair assays needs to be strengthened.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that STK19 "positions TFIIH ahead of Pol II for lesion verification" is not directly supported by experimental evidence; it is inferred from molecular modeling and mutagenesis.
- The statement that the cell-free system "recapitulates all features expected of TC-NER" may be overstated given the use of naked DNA and the absence of chromatin context.
- The assertion that STK19 is a "core TC-NER factor" is supported by the data, but the precise molecular function beyond XPD interaction remains speculative.
- The claim that the cryo-EM structure at 1.9 Å provides "unambiguous" placement of STK19 is not fully verifiable without access to the map and model validation metrics.
