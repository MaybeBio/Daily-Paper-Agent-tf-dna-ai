# Pharmacological targets and validation of remdesivir for the treatment of COVID-19-associated pulmonary fibrosis: A network-based pharmacology and bioinformatics study


## Abstract
  The objective of this study was to employ bioinformatics and network pharmacology methodologies to investigate the targets and molecular mechanisms of remdesivir in the treatment of coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19)-associated pulmonary fibrosis (PF). Several open-source databases were utilized to confirm the shared targets of remdesivir, COVID-19, and PF. Following this, a comprehensive analysis incorporating function enrichment, protein-protein interaction (PPI), transcription factor (TF), and molecular docking was conducted to investigate the potential mechanisms underlying the effectiveness of remdesivir in the treatment of COVID-19-associated PF. The initial validation of these findings was performed using publicly available histological and single-cell sequencing databases. The functional enrichment analysis revealed a strong association between remdesivir and viral defense, inflammatory response, and immune response. The key pathways identified in the study were transforming growth factor (TGF-β), PI3K-Akt, mTOR, MAPK, epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) tyrosine kinase inhibitor resistance, HIF-1, and Toll-like receptor signaling pathways. Additionally, the PPI analysis demonstrated the network relationships of 13 important targets, while the TF analysis provided valuable insights into the regulatory networks of these targets. Among the identified TFs, RELA was found to be the most significant. To validate our findings, we utilized publicly available histological and single-cell sequencing databases, successfully confirming the involvement of 8 key targets, including AKT1, EGFR, RHOA, MAPK1, PIK3R1, MAPK8, MAPK14, and MTOR. Furthermore, molecular docking studies were conducted to assess the interaction between remdesivir and the identified key targets, thus confirming its effective targeting effects. Remdesivir has the potential to exert antiviral, anti-inflammatory, and immunomodulatory effects in the context of COVID-19-associated PF.


## 1. Introduction
  Coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19), caused by the severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2), was declared a global pandemic by the World Health Organization in 2020. As of April 2023, COVID-19 has affected over 762 million people and caused 6.89 million deaths, posing a significant threat to human health. Severe COVID-19 cases can trigger an “inflammatory factor storm” that leads to acute respiratory distress syndrome (100.0%) and respiratory failure (83.3%).[1] This process is often accompanied by the development of pulmonary fibrosis (PF).[2] Several clinical studies have reported that approximately 17.5% to 33.9% of COVID-19 patients exhibit PF changes as indicated by CT scans or lung histopathology, which are closely associated with SARS-CoV-2 infection.[3–5]

  PF is a progressive disease that can be attributed to various factors. It is characterized by an excessive proliferation of fibroblasts and the deposition of extracellular matrix (ECM), ultimately leading to respiratory failure and mortality. Patients who are left untreated face a median survival time of only 3 to 5 years.[6,7] However, the currently available therapeutic options for delaying the progression of PF are limited, with guidelines recommending only pirfenidone and nintedanib.[8] Therefore, there is an urgent need to explore new therapeutic agents to address PF associated with COVID-19.

  Remdesivir, the first antiviral drug approved by both the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and the European Medicines Agency (EMA) for the treatment of COVID-19, demonstrates its antiviral effect by inhibiting nucleic acid synthesis.[9] Clinical studies have provided evidence that COVID-19 patients treated with remdesivir experience faster recovery, lower mortality rates, and reduced reliance on prolonged oxygen therapy or mechanical ventilation.[10,11] Notably, both bleomycin-induced PF mouse models and cellular assays have confirmed that remdesivir exhibits anti-PF effects and shows greater efficacy compared to nintedanib.[12] Furthermore, our previous clinical study suggests that remdesivir has the potential to delay the onset of PF in COVID-19 patients.[13] However, the pharmacological targets and mechanism of action of remdesivir for the treatment of COVID-19-associated PF remain unclear.

  In light of the aforementioned background, our study employed bioinformatics and network pharmacology techniques to identify the core targets and signaling pathways associated with remdesivir. Our objective was to explore the potential mechanisms underlying the effective treatment of COVID-19-associated PF with remdesivir through a comprehensive analysis of function enrichment, protein-protein interaction (PPI), transcription factor (TF), and molecular docking. Additionally, we validated our findings using histological and single-cell sequencing public databases. The results of this study will contribute to the discovery of potential therapeutic targets for remdesivir and provide a stronger theoretical basis for its therapeutic application in the context of COVID-19-associated PF. ## 2. Materials and methods

### 2.1. Screening for common targets of remdesivir in COVID-19 and PF
  The potential targets of remdesivir were retrieved from several databases, including Drugbank (https://go.drugbank.com/), Swiss Target Prediction (http://www.swiss), SEA Search Server (https://sea.bkslab.org/), PharmMapper (http://www.lilabecust.cn/pharmmapper/), and Chembl (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chembl/). The predicted results were filtered using the following criteria: “Human” setting, Normalized Fit Score ≥ 0.5 in the PharmMapper database, and confidence ≥ 0.8 in the Chembl database. Additionally, the proteins obtained from the search results were converted into genes using the Uniprot database (https://www.uniprot.org/). The targets associated with COVID-19 and PF were identified through a comprehensive search across multiple databases, including GeneCards (https://www.genecards.org/), PubChem (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/), OMIM (https://omim.org/), and NCBI (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/). In the target screening process, the PubChem and NCBI databases were limited to human targets. In contrast, GeneCards and OMIM were utilized as human gene databases without such restrictions. Additionally, a relevance score of ≥ 1 was set in the GeneCards database to ensure the selection of relevant human targets. Subsequently, duplicate entries were removed, and the common targets of remdesivir, COVID-19, and PF were identified using a Venn diagram analysis.


### 2.2. Functional enrichment analysis of common targets
  To further investigate the biological functions of the potential targets of remdesivir, we utilized several R language packages, including “ClusterProfiler,” “org.Hs.e.g.,” “ggplot2,” “ReactomePA,” “igraph,” and “ggraph.” These packages were used for gene ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses, as well as network graph visualization.


### 2.3. PPI network analysis and screening of important targets
  We utilized the STRING database (http://string-db.org) to construct a PPI network specifically for the “human” species. The network was built with a high confidence level of 0.700 and showed only interactions with query proteins. The relationships between potential targets were visualized using Cytoscape 3.9.1 software, and key sub-network was filtered out based on degree values. In order to conduct cluster analysis of the PPI network, we utilized the molecular complex detection (MCODE) plugin within the Cytoscape platform. The modules with the highest MCODE score values were identified and considered as the key sub-network.[14] Finally, we overlapped the 2 key sub-networks to identify the important target proteins of remdesivir for the treatment of COVID-19-associated PF.


### 2.4. TF analysis of important targets
  We utilized the TRRUST database (https://www.grnpedia.org/trrust/) to identify the transcriptional regulatory relationships of important targets within the “human” species, specifically those with a P value < .05. To construct the TF-target network, we employed the Cytoscape 3.9.1 software.


### 2.5. Validation of important target expression at tissue and single-cell levels
  We used the GTEx database (https://gtexportal.org/home/) to examine the expression levels of the important targets in human lung tissues and lung cells. Based on the results of the study, we classified targets with a median TPM value of > 30 in lung tissue or a percentage of > 10% in lung cells as key targets.


### 2.6. Molecular docking analysis of key targets
  We retrieved the 3D structures of key target proteins, along with their corresponding ligands, from the RCSB PDB database (https://www.rcsb.org/). The protein structures were specifically restricted to the human species. The 3D structure of remdesivir, in the SDF file format, was obtained from the PubChem database and converted to the PBD file format using Open Babel 3.1.1 software. To prepare the protein structures for docking, we used PyMOL software to remove water molecules and ligands from the proteins. Additionally, Autodock 1.5.7 software was employed to perform hydrogenation and charge assignment of the proteins. The parameters for the receptor protein docking site were set to the location of the active pocket of the original small molecule ligand binding. For docking simulations, we utilized Autodock Vina to dock the receptor proteins with remdesivir. Finally, the results were visualized using PyMOL software.


## 3. Results

### 3.1. Identification of potential targets for remdesivir treatment in COVID-19-associated PF
  We identified a total of 283 potential targets for remdesivir, 1981 targets related to COVID-19, and 4463 targets related to PF. The Venn diagram in Figure 2 illustrates the intersection of these target sets, revealing 62 common targets. These common targets represent potential candidates for remdesivir in the treatment of COVID-19-associated PF.


### 3.2. GO and KEGG enrichment analysis of potential targets
  In this study, we obtained a total of 1210 significant enriched GO terms (P-adjust < 0.05). These terms were categorized into 1132 terms for biological process (BP), 26 terms for cell composition, and 52 terms for molecular function (Supplementary File, http://links.lww.com/MD/N268). The top 12 terms are illustrated in Figure 3A. Regarding BPs, the enriched processes included response to lipopolysaccharide, neutrophil-mediated immunity, T cell activation, regulation of inflammatory response, collagen metabolic process, mesenchymal cell differentiation, positive regulation of epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT), and fibroblast proliferation (Fig. 3B). Additionally, we identified 128 significant terms (P-adjust < 0.05) for KEGG pathway enrichment analysis. These pathways included TNF signaling pathway, apoptosis, transforming growth factor (TGF-β) signaling pathway, PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, mTOR signaling pathway, MAPK signaling pathway, epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) tyrosine kinase inhibitor resistance, HIF-1 signaling pathway, Toll-like receptor signaling pathway, and other signaling pathways (Fig. 3C). The bubble plot in Figure 3D displays the most significant 20 KEGG pathways. To provide a concise representation of the findings, we filtered the top 3 items from both GO and KEGG analyses and constructed a network diagram (Fig. 3E).


### 3.3. Construction of PPI network and identification of important targets
  The PPI relationships of 62 potential targets linked to remdesivir for COVID-19-associated PF were obtained from the STRING database. A visual PPI network was constructed using Cytoscape 3.9.1 software, which consisted of 62 nodes representing targets, 157 edges representing protein-protein interactions, and red circles representing central targets, with larger circles indicating the importance of the targets in the PPI network. The network was then screened based on a degree value of > 5.06 (mean), resulting in the identification of 19 protein targets with degree values higher than the mean (ranging from 6 to 22) (Fig. 4A). Furthermore, cluster analysis of the PPI network was performed using the MCODE plugin, revealing the presence of one functional module cluster (K-core = 2). This cluster contained 13 nodes and 59 edges, with an MCODE score of 9.833 (Fig. 4B). We took the 2 key sub-networks and performed an intersection analysis. As a result, 13 important targets were identified, including AKT1, EGFR, RHOA, CASP3, ALB, MAPK1, IL2, MAPK8, PIK3R1, MAPK14, LCK, JAK3, and MTOR (Fig. 4C).


### 3.4. TF analysis of important targets
  The TF-target network consisting of 13 important targets was composed of 12 TFs, 6 targets, and 32 edges (Fig. 5A). The TFs were ranked based on their P value significance, in the following order: RELA, NFKB1, PML, STAT3, JUN, TP53, VDR, HDAC1, ETS1, EGR1, AR, SP1 (Table 1).


### 3.5. Validation of important targets
  The gene-tissue expression heatmap showed that the majority of the important targets exhibited high expression levels in lung tissues, particularly RHOA, AKT1, PIK3R1, MAPK14, and MAPK1 (Fig. 5B). The multi-gene single-cell expression data map, presented in a matrix format (Fig. 5C), The results revealed that the following targets were detected in more than 10% of cells in the respective cell types: EGFR, MAPK8, MAPK1, RHOA, MAPK14, and MTOR in epithelial cells (alveolar type I/II); PIK3R1, EGFR, MAPK8, MAPK14, and MAPK1 in fibroblasts; and MAPK1, MAPK8, MAPK14, RHOA, PIK3R1, and MTOR in immune cells (alveolar macrophages). In total, 8 key target ensembles demonstrated high expression in tissues or cells of the lung, including AKT1, EGFR, RHOA, MAPK1, PIK3R1, MAPK8, MAPK14, and MTOR.


### 3.6. Molecular docking of key targets
  The key targets (AKT1, EGFR, RHOA, MAPK1, PIK3R1, MAPK8, MAPK14, MTOR) were selected for molecular docking with remdesivir. A lower binding energy indicates a better binding effect, with a binding energy of less than −5 kcal/mol generally accepted as indicative of good binding activity. The molecular docking results revealed that remdesivir exhibited binding energies ranging from −6.6 to −9.2 kcal/mol to the 8 key targets (Table 2). Additionally, the compound formed 1 to 4 hydrogen bonds with each target (Fig. 6).


## 4. Discussions
  This study represents the first attempt to analyze the potential targets and molecular mechanisms of remdesivir for the treatment of COVID-19-associated PF using bioinformatics, network pharmacology, and molecular docking. Our functional enrichment analysis revealed that remdesivir is closely associated with viral defense, inflammatory response, and immune response. Furthermore, the PPI analysis identified 13 important targets and their interaction networks, while the TF analysis revealed their regulatory networks. To validate our results, we utilized histology and single-cell sequencing public databases and confirmed the involvement of 8 key targets. Additionally, we performed molecular docking of these key targets and confirmed that both remdesivir and the targets exhibited well-targeted interactions. These findings suggest a potential role for remdesivir in the treatment of COVID-19-associated PF, providing a stronger theoretical basis for the discovery and application of potential therapeutic targets for remdesivir.

  It is well-known that the TGF-β signaling pathway plays a crucial role in PF by inducing the differentiation of fibroblasts into myofibroblasts and promoting collagen secretion.[15] In cellular experiments conducted by Li et al, it was demonstrated that remdesivir inhibits TGF-β1-induced activation of lung fibroblasts and alveolar EMT.[12] Mechanistically, cytokines such as TGF-β can activate the PI3K/AKT/mTOR and MAPK signaling pathways.[16,17] The PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway is involved in the development and progression of PF by upregulating collagen expression, promoting cell proliferation, and influencing apoptosis.[18,19] Similarly, the MAPK signaling pathway regulates various cellular processes including proliferation, differentiation, apoptosis, and immunity, and is implicated in PF through multiple pathways.[17,20,21] Our KEGG pathway enrichment analysis confirmed that the TGF-β signaling pathway, PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, mTOR signaling pathway, and MAPK signaling pathway play critical roles in COVID-19-associated PF. Furthermore, our analysis revealed that EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor resistance, the HIF-1 signaling pathway, and the Toll-like receptor signaling pathway are also implicated in the development of COVID-19-associated PF. EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor resistance has been associated with the development of EMT, which may contribute to the pathogenesis of interstitial lung disease.[22,23] A sustained inflammatory response can trigger the activation of myofibroblasts and the fibrotic response, with the HIF-1 signaling pathway and Toll-like receptor signaling pathway playing crucial roles in promoting inflammation. HIF-1 expression was found to be significantly elevated in COVID-19 patients, promoting SARS-CoV-2 replication and exacerbating the inflammatory response. Notably, specific silencing of HIF-1 has been shown to attenuate PF in bleomycin-induced mice.[24,25] Toll-like receptor signaling can activate innate and adaptive immune responses and produce pro-inflammatory cytokines.[26] Collectively, remdesivir may exert antiviral, anti-inflammatory, immunomodulatory, anti-proliferative, and apoptotic effects through these pathways to inhibit the development of COVID-19-associated PF.

  Our GO enrichment results provide further evidence of the involvement of remdesivir in the pathogenesis of COVID-19 and PF. In addition to the inflammatory and immune processes in BPs, collagen metabolism process, smooth muscle cell proliferation, ECM disassembly, mesenchymal cell differentiation, positive regulation of EMT and fibroblast proliferation, which are closely related to the development of PF, are also enriched by remdesivir-related targets. In PF, the abnormal accumulation of ECM proteins leads to the formation of scar tissues in the lung. The ECM, composed of collagen, elastin, glycoproteins, and proteoglycans, serves as a structural scaffold for cells and is crucial for imparting mechanical stability and elastic retraction required for normal lung function.[27] ECM remodeling is a highly regulated process in tissue homeostasis and injury repair, and dysregulation of ECM remodeling may result from imbalances in ECM synthesis, catabolism, organization, or post-translational modifications.[28,29] EMT has also been shown to play a direct role in the development of PF.[30] Furthermore, the excessive immune response triggered by viral infection may serve as a trigger for the induction and acute exacerbation of PF.[31,32]

  Aberrant activation of TFs and subsequent aberrant expression of pathogenic genes play a crucial role in disease progression. In our study, we identified 12 TFs from the 13 important targets, and RELA was found to be the most significant. RELA is a subunit of the TF nuclear factor-κB (NF-κB), which is involved in critical cellular processes such as inflammation, immunity, cell adhesion, cell proliferation, cell differentiation, and cell apoptosis.[33] Notably, RELA not only promotes the production of TGF-β1 but also mediates fibroblast-to-myofibroblast transition (FMT) itself.[34,35] Furthermore, NF-κB inhibitors have been shown to inhibit key pro-fibrotic pathways, including proliferation, FMT, fibronectin deposition, and IL-6 secretion.[36]

  In addition to the association with PF at the TF level, we also found that key targets such as AKT1, PIK3R1, MTOR, MAPK1, MAPK8, MAPK14, RHOA, and EGFR exhibited high expression levels in lung tissues, as well as in lung cellular components including epithelial cells, fibroblasts, and alveolar macrophages. Multiple cytokines and inflammatory mediators are involved in lung epithelial cell injury, which is an important part of lung tissue inflammation.[37] Fibroblasts, acting as the main “effector” cells in fibroplasia, are pivotal in providing structural support and facilitating post-injury tissue repair through the secretion and regulation of the ECM.[37,38] Alveolar macrophages, on the other hand, are essential in the pathogenesis of PF by initiating immune responses and generating reactive oxygen species (ROS).[39] Through molecular docking analysis, we confirmed that all these identified targets exhibit strong binding affinity with remdesivir, indicating their potential as treatment targets for COVID-19-associated PF. Akt is a serine/threonine protein kinase that plays a central role in many signaling pathways. It encompasses 3 isoforms: AKT1, AKT2, and AKT3, of which AKT1 and AKT2 have been more extensively studied in the context of PF.[40] AKT1 serves as a regulator of apoptosis and also plays a role in modulating both systemic and local inflammation by influencing the differentiation, proliferation, and migration of immune cells.[41,42] PIK3R1 encodes the major regulatory subunit of class I PI3K, known as P85α.[43] Increased phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K) has been observed in the lungs of a mouse model of bleomycin-induced PF.[44] PI3K affects key processes in the inflammatory response to injury and infection,[45] and PI3K inhibitors have been shown to reduce inflammation and improve lung function.[46] mTOR plays an important role in protein translation, apoptosis, and cell metabolism.[19] MAPK1 (ERK2), MAPK8 (JNK1), and MAPK14 (P38) are members of the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) superfamily. MAPK8 and MAPK14 are primarily activated by pro-inflammatory cytokines and environmental stresses, and they play crucial roles in apoptosis. On the other hand, MAPK1 is predominantly activated by pro-growth factors and is essential for cell differentiation and proliferation.[17] It is worth noting that MAPK14 inhibitors have demonstrated effective inhibition of virus replication, including SARS-CoV-2, and modulation of the immune response in patients.[21] Additionally, EGFR inhibitors have shown anti-SARS-CoV-2 viral and antifibrotic effects based on viral fibrosis scores.[47] RHOA serves as a key mediator in several pro-fibrotic processes, and overexpression of connective tissue growth factors and myofibroblast formation are dependent on RHOA.[48,49] Collectively, these findings suggest that remdesivir exerts its therapeutic effects on COVID-19-associated PF primarily through these key targets.

  Despite the promising findings from our study, there are still limitations that need to be addressed in future research. Firstly, our study utilized a network pharmacology approach to analyze the mechanisms of multiple pathways involved in remdesivir for delaying the progression of COVID-19-associated PF. However, further in vivo and ex vivo experiments are necessary to validate these findings. Secondly, the conclusion that remdesivir slows the progression of COVID-19-associated PF should be corroborated through additional clinical trials. Nevertheless, given the current prevalence of COVID-19 and the limited availability of PF therapeutics, our study provides valuable insights into the potential mechanisms and application of remdesivir in the treatment of COVID-19-associated PF.


## 5. Conclusions
  This study represents a pioneering effort to integrate bioinformatics, network pharmacology, and molecular docking to uncover the potential mechanisms underlying the antiviral, anti-inflammatory, and immune modulation effects of remdesivir on COVID-19-associated PF. Our findings suggest that these effects may be mediated through the modulation of key targets, including AKT1, EGFR, RHOA, MAPK1, PIK3R1, MAPK8, MAPK14, and MTOR. Additionally, this process may involve RELA TF and crucial signaling pathways, such as TGF-β, PI3K-Akt, mTOR, MAPK, EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor resistance, HIF-1, and Toll-like receptor. These findings highlight the potential of remdesivir as a promising candidate for the treatment of COVID-19-associated PF. However, it is important to note that further clinical trials, as well as in vivo and in vitro experimental studies, are necessary to validate and confirm these findings.
