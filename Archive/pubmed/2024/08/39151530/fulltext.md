# Network pharmacology and molecular docking analysis predict the mechanisms of Huangbai liniment in treating oral lichen planus


## Abstract
  This study explored the mechanism of Huangbai liniment (HB) for the treatment of oral lichen planus (OLP) through network pharmacology and molecular docking techniques. The study identified HB’ active ingredients, therapeutic targets for OLP, and associated signaling pathways. The chemical composition of HB was screened using the HERB database. The disease targets of OLP were obtained through the GeneCards and OMIM databases. A protein–protein interactions network was constructed with the String platform. Topological analysis was performed using Cytoscape software to identify core targets. Gene ontology and Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes pathway enrichment analysis were performed using the Hiplot database, and the active ingredients and core targets were verified by molecular docking. Date analysis showed that the active composition of HB in the treatment of OLP were quercetin, wogonin, kaempferol, and luteolin. This survey identified 10 potential therapeutic targets, including TNF, CXCL8, IL-6, IL1B, PIK3R1, ESR1, JUN, AKT1, PIK3CA, and CTNNB1. Molecular docking revealed stable interactions between OLP’ key targets and HB. These key targets were predominantly involved in the PI3K–Akt signaling pathway, AGE–RAGE signaling pathway, TNF signaling pathway, and HIF-1 signaling pathway. HB plays a crucial role in the treatment of OLP, acting on multiple targets and pathways, particularly the PI3K–Akt signaling pathway. It regulated biological processes like the proliferation of epithelial cells and lymphocytes and mediates the expression of transcription factors, cytokines, and chemokines. Therefore, this study provides a theoretical basis for the clinical trial and application of HB in the therapy of OLP.


## 1. Introduction
  Oral lichen planus (OLP) is a chronic inflammatory condition of the oral mucosa, characterized by white reticular lesions, mucosal thickening, roughness, and pain.[1,2] Approximately 1.4% OLP case progress to oral squamous cell carcinoma, classified as oral potentially malignant disorder (OPMD) by the World Health Organization (WHO).[3] OLP exhibits a high recurrence rate, with vesicular/ulcerative OLP causing significant pain and distress for patients.[4,5] Although corticosteroids and immunosuppressants are commonly used as first-line drugs, evidence-based standardized treatment for OLP remain limited.[6] Long-term corticosteroid use may lead to severe side effects including Cushing syndrome, secondary Candida infections, and mucosal atrophy.[7,8]

  The pathogenesis of OLP involves dysregulation of T-lymphocyte-mediated immune response.[9] Oral keratinocytes present antigens to both CD4 + helper cells and CD8 + cytotoxic T lymphocytes. Additionally, activated helper T cells initiate pro-inflammatory cascades, causing proliferation, activation, and the apoptosis of basal keratin-forming cells.[6,10] Autoimmune responses also contribute to OLP pathogenesis, with CD8 + cytotoxic T lymphocytes recognize and upregulating the expression of MHC class I and II antigens, adhesion molecules, and chemokines, thereby exacerbating chronic inflammation.[9] OLP’s complex and diverse pathogenesis presents challenges in clinical treatment.

  Research indicates that traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) offers a promising and effective treatment for OLP.[11] Chinese medicines treatments can modulate the immune balance and significantly improve the clinical symptoms of OLP.[11–13] Huangbai liniment (HB), a compound herbal formulation, contains Cortex Phellodendri, Fructus Forsythiae, Flos Lonicerae, Herba Taraxaci and Scolopendra.[14] Phellodendron and Forsythia exhibit anti-inflammatory, anti-infective, and immunomodulatory effects,[15,16] whereas Honeysuckle and Dandelion possess antioxidant and antiviral properties.[17,18] The Centipede is renowned for medicinal properties, particularly its strong anti-inflammatory and reparative abilities, making it especially effective for treating ulcers and erosions.[19] HB demonstrates therapeutic potential in managing chronic inflammatory diseases including atopic dermatitis,[14] diabetic ulcers,[20] recurrent stomatitis,[21] and ulcerative colitis.[22] In vitro experiments showed that HB enhanced TREG cell expression, suppressed TH17 cells, rebalanced TREG/TH17 cell. It ameliorated the inflammatory response by reducing pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, IFN-γ, and IL-17), and augmenting anti-inflammatory cytokine IL-10.[15] HB is known to inhibit oxidative damage and stimulate cell proliferation, effectively promoting diabetic ulcer healing. HB could influence multiple biological processes including inflammation, angiogenic dysfunction, oxidative damage, apoptosis, and extracellular matrix secretion and remodeling abnormalities.[20] A significant advantages of HB is its lack of to specific strains or drug resistance, rendering it suitable for prolonged clinical adjuvant therapy.[22] However, the molecular mechanism by which HB treats OLP remain unreported.

  To identify potential HB targets for OLP treatment, we utilized network pharmacology to delineate HB’s active chemical components and corresponding proteins targets (Fig. 1). A protein–protein interaction (PPI) network pharmacological analysis was performed to definite core targets associated with HB’s chemical composition and OLP disease targets. Molecular docking, supplemented by gene ontology (GO) and Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) pathway analyses, elucidated the binding mechanisms between HB’s primary targets and OLP’s active compounds (Fig. 3). This research aimed to uncover HB’s potential mechanisms of HB in OLP treatment through network-based pharmacological. The insights offer direction for subsequent pharmacological and in vitro experiments.


## 2. Materials and methods

### 2.1. Screening and target prediction of HB
  To identify the pharmacodynamic components of HB (Cortex Phellodendri, Fructus Forsythiae, Flos Lonicerae, Herba Taraxaci, and Scolopendra), we utilized the HERB database (http://herb.ac.cn/),[23] integrating multiple TCM databases such as SymMap, TCMID, TCMSP, and TCMD. The chemical composition of HB was screened using SwissADME (http://www.swissadme.ch/).[24] Filtering criteria comprised adherence to Lipinski rule (molecular weight < 500, rotatable bonds ≤ 10, hydrogen bond acceptors ≤ 10, H-bond donors ≤ 5, Log Po/w ≤ 5) and pharmacokinetic properties, notably high gastrointestinal absorption and blood–brain barrier permeability. Swiss Target Prediction (http://www.Swisstargetprediction.ch/)[24] was used to forecast potential targets of chemical composition, requiring a “probability” >0.


### 2.2. Screening of targets for OLP
  To identify targets related to OLP, we retrieved data using the keywords “Oral lichen planus” to from both the GeneCards database (https://www.genecards.org/)[25] and the OMIM database (https://www.omim.org/).[26] Duplicate were eliminated to derive the final set of OLP-specific targets.


### 2.3. Construction of HB–OLP interaction networks
  Based on the target data, HB and OLP targets were analyzed using Venny2.1 (https://bioinfogp.cnb.csic.es/tools/venny/index.html) to create a Wayne diagram illustrating the overlap between therapeutic and disease-related targets. Subsequently, we constructed the HB–OLP interaction networks utilizing Cytoscape 3.8.0 software (www.cytoscape.org/),[27] based on the identified.


### 2.4. Construction of PPI network
  Target genes were uploaded to the STRING platform (https://cn.string-db.org/),[28] with the specifying “Homo sapiens,” to acquire protein interaction information. The network was developed by selecting interactions with a confidence score above 0.900 and excluding unconnected nodes. Subsequently, protein interactions detail relevant to specific medicines and diseases were integrated into Cytoscape 3.8.0 software, for topological analysis via the Network Analyzer tool. The ten genes with the most connections were identified as key targets.


### 2.5. GO enrichment and KEGG pathway analysis
  Common target genes associated with both medicine and diseases underwent enrichment analysis for GO biological process, molecular function, and cell component enrichment analysis, alongside KEGG pathway analysis, using the HIPLOT platform (https://hiplot-academic.com/).[29] A threshold P < .05 was set. Higher enrichment score indicates greater relevance of the pathway to disease progression. Pathways containing more genes are represented by larger bubbles. The top 20 entries based on P-values were selected for visualization in both GO and KEGG enrichment analysis


### 2.6. Molecular docking validation
  Obtain the 3D structure of the target protein related to the disease from the PDB database (https://www.rcsb.org/),[30] using it as the protein receptor, and select small molecular compounds with a resolution finer than 2.5 Å as the benchmark. Retrieve the 3D structure of HB’s active ingredient from PubChem database (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/)[31] to use as the small molecule ligand. PyMOL 2.4.0 software[32] was used to dehydrate and hydrogenate the protein receptor and small molecule ligand, converting them into the “PDBQT” format. Molecular docking analysis was conducted using AutoDockTools.[33] The molecular docking results were visually examined with PyMol 2.4.0 software, yielding the relevant docking pattern diagram. Previous literature suggests that a binding energy is lower than −5.0 kcal/mol indicates strong binding force.


## 3. Results

### 3.1. Acquisition of chemical ingredients and targets of HB
  HB consists of 5 Chinese herbal medicines: Cortex Phellodendri, Flos Lonicerae, Fructus Forsythiae, Herba Taraxaci, and Scolopendra. The chemical composition of HB was searched by HERB database, and then the chemical structure was obtained, with the SDF structure was derived from PubChem database. Potential active components and targets were screened and predicted using SwissADME and Swiss Target Prediction platform, respectively. Target with a “Probability” score of <0 were identified as potential targets. After merging and removing duplicates, 1193 potential drug targets were identified (see Fig. 2A).


### 3.2. Screening of HB for the treatment of OLP
  Databases OMIM and GeneCards were queried for “Oral lichen planus.” From OMIM 6 disease targets, and from GeneCards, 567 disease targets were identified post-screening. Combined, the databases yielded 571 unique disease targets after duplicates were eliminated. Potential HB targets and OLP target genes were compared using Venny2.1, generating a Wayne diagram. This comparison identified 131 intersecting targets were identified (see Fig. 2A).


### 3.3. Therapeutic target renin and PPI network construction
  Utilizing the STRING database, we constructed a PPI network using the STRING database, visualized through Cytoscape 3.8.0, comprising 102 nodes and 227 edges (Fig. 2B). We employed the CytoHubba plug-in for topological analysis, identifying Compound HB’s core targets for OLP treatment. The color scale, ranging from lighter to darker hues, represented the Degree value’s magnitude. The identified top 10 targets included TNF, CXCL8, IL6, IL1B, PIK3R1, ESR1, JUN, AKT1, PIK3CA, and CTNNB1, respectively (Fig. 2C). These targets play critical roles within the protein network, with their modifications potentially having significant effects.


### 3.4. HB therapeutic OLP “active ingredient–target of action” network
  We used Cytoscape software to create depicting potential active ingredients and their targets in HB for treating OLP. The network graph contained 468 nodes and 3382 edges. In the graph, yellow denotes the active ingredients from the 5 TCM, and blue signifies the targets (Fig. 2D). PPI data for these targets, sourced from the STRING database with a confidence threshold above 0.9, yielded 227 protein interaction. This network demonstrates how the active ingredients from specific herbs interact with OLP-related targets. The most prominent active ingredients were flavonoids, such as including quercetin, wogonin, kaempferol, and luteolin, among others. These active ingredients are likely crucial in producing the medicinal effects.


### 3.5. GO enrichment and KEGG pathway analysis
  GO functional enrichment analysis performed on the intersecting targets using the HIPLOT platform, identified 2468 enriched entries. Of these, 2201 entries fell under biological processes, meeting the significance criterion of P-value < .05. Potential therapeutic targets primarily related to responses to lipopolysaccharide, as well as epithelial cell proliferation, leukocyte proliferation, lymphocyte proliferation, and bacterial-origin molecular responses (Fig. 3A). Molecular function analysis identified 193 entries, predominantly involving protein serine/threonine/tyrosine kinase activity, transcription factor, cytokine and chemokine binding (Fig. 3B). The cell component category comprised 74 entries, affecting cellular composition via the outer plasma membrane, membrane rafts, microregions, transcriptional regulatory complexes, and receptor complexes (Fig. 3C). The top 20 entries, based on P-value, were selected for visualization and analysis. KEGG enrichment analysis on the intersecting targets disclosed 153 regulatory pathways, notably including those related to OLP like PI3K–Akt, AGE–RAGE, TNF, and HIF-1 signaling pathway (Fig. 3D). These pathways were visualized in a bubble diagram.


### 3.6. Molecular docking results
  The study sought to confirm the molecular docking results outcomes by initially selecting the top 10 key targets with the greatest degree of freedom in the PPI network. These targets underwent molecular docking with the top 20 most active ingredients as identified in the ingredient–target network. Docking scores evaluated the binding efficacy between targets and ingredients. Scores below −4.25 kJ/mol suggested binding activity; those below −5.0 kJ/mol indicated good binding activity, and scores below −7.0 kJ/mol denoted strong binding activity. The average docking score was −7.365 kJ/mol, with half of the scores being −7.0 kJ/mol higher. 6,7-Dihydroxy-4’-methoxyisoflavone showed the highest intermolecular stability with PIK3CA, achieving a docking score of −12.60 kJ/mol. Results were visualized with a heatmap (Fig. 4), revealing that HB’s core target and main active ingredients exhibit good binding ability, suggesting a synergistic effect on the core target through multi-component action. High scores in HB (quercetin, wogonin, kaempferol, and luteolin) and their targets were visualized using PyMol 2.4.0 (Fig. 5).


## 4. Discussion
  OLP is a common autoimmune disease affecting the oral mucosa, characterized by the destruction of epithelial basal cells and infiltration by subepithelial T lymphocytes.[34,35] In OLP, T-lymphocyte activation, proliferation, and apoptosis of T lymphocytes are crucial for maintaining local immune system balance.[36,37] The development of OLP is a complex process, and frequently necessitates ongoing treatment due to a high recurrence rate. Current primarily target drugs that modulate the local oral mucosa or the systemic immune response in OLP patients.[38] Common treatments for OLP symptoms and promote short-term healing include Corticosteroids, calcium-modulated phosphatase inhibitors, immunosuppressants, and thalidomide.[39] However, for severe erosive/ulcerative OLP, these treatments may not cure the disease and could result in significant immunosuppression, opportunistic infections, and osteoporosis.[40] HB, a TCM, demonstrates antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, and immune-regulating characteristics with fewer toxic side effects than hormonal medications in some immune diseases.[14] The precise mechanism of HB’s effect on OLP remains unclear.

  This study used a network pharmacology approach to investigate HB therapy’s multi-target potential for OLP. It analyzed the intricate medicine–protein/gene-pathway network. Screening identified 1193 active components in HB, and revealed 571 differential genes with OLP. Network analysis indicated that TNF, CXCL8, IL6, IL1β, PIK3R1, ESR1, JUN, AKT1, PIK3CA, and CTNNB1 were associated with T-lymphocyte activation, oral mucosal keratinocyte proliferation, and epithelial apoptosis in OLP.

  Research indicates elevated levels of TNF-α and IL-1β in the saliva, OLP keratinocytes and local monocytes. TNF-α plays a critical role in the T-cell-mediated immunopathological response at OLP lesions. Its gene polymorphisms, linked to a higher OLP risk, suggest its significant regulatory function in OLP.[41,42] IL-6, primarily secreted by lymphocytes, macrophages, and endothelial cells, shows increased levels in the serum and saliva of OLP patients. This elevation may contribute to the proliferation and apoptosis of basal keratinocytes in OLP.[43] Phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3-K), a heterodimeric enzyme involved in T-cell receptor activation and adaptive immunity signaling, T-cell proliferation, and regulation of CD8 + T cells.[44] AKT functions as downstream effector of PIK3R1, influencing cell growth and proliferation.[45] CXCL8, a critical pro-inflammatory factor, is released by various cells, including T lymphocytes, monocytes, macrophages, and fibroblasts. Its expression can increase by 10 to 100-fold when stimulated by cytokines like TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, and IFN-γ.[46] The CXCL8-CXCR1/2 axis recruits and activates neutrophils and granulocytes at inflammation sites, causing intense and chronic immune responses with tissue damage. It also regulates angiogenesis, immune cell infiltration and cell migration during inflammation. The CXCL8 axis contributes to various inflammatory diseases via signaling pathways like PI3k/Akt, MAPKs, and NF-κB.[47,48] c-Jun N-terminal kinase (JNK), a MAPK family member, activates various substrates like transcription factors, articulins, and signaling proteins. It plays roles in multiple cellular processes including inflammation, apoptosis, cell proliferation, migration, and differentiation.[49] Maintaining c-Jun and Jun-B balance and inhibiting the AGE–RAGE signaling pathway, can repaired the skin barrier and reduce skin eczema’s inflammatory response.[50] ESR1, a gene of significant size and complexity, is governed by numerous regulatory elements. Its transcriptional regulation crucially influences the dysregulation of estrogen receptor (ER) expression. In premenopausal and perimenopausal women, a decrease of ER and an increase of ERα in the ovaries can elevate pro-inflammatory factors and thereby accelerating the onset of chronic inflammation associated with OLP.[51,52] CTNNB1 encodes β-catenin, a key molecule in the Wnt signaling pathway, crucial for cell membrane structure, cell proliferation and intracellular homeostasis regulation.[53] Microenvironmental factors, including inflammatory factors and cytokines, can induce abnormalities in WNT signaling pathways, contributing to the transformation of oral precancerous lesions, like oral leukoplakia, OLP, and oral submucous fibrosis into oral cancer.[54] Wnt3-positive abnormal cytoplasm and nuclei are present in vesicular or ulcerative OLP with the highest malignant potential, with similar expression patterns during the dysplastic change within OLP.[55]

  Based on the identified core targets, identified 20 associated core components. quercetin, wogonin, kaempferol, and luteolin emerged as potential key active compounds for OLP treatment with HB. Quercetin regulates the immune response by targeting IL-6 and IFN-γ to affecting the Th1/Th2 balance. It significantly influences T-lymphocyte apoptosis and migration in OLP patients.[56] TNF-α can activate the NF-κB signaling pathway by regulating the NF-κB induced kinase (NIK)/IκB kinase (IKK-α) axis.[57] Wogonin suppresses TNF-α, IL-6 production, and NF-κB or extracellular regulated kinase1/2 by LPS.[58] Furthermore, it modulates the Wnt/β-Catenin signaling pathway, inhibiting cancer cell proliferation and invasion.[59] Kaempferol effectively reduces tumor growth, induce apoptosis and autophagy, and inhibit the MAPKS pathway.[60] Similarly, luteolin demonstrates anti-cancer effects by inhibiting cell proliferation, activating NF-kB signaling, and inducing apoptosis via protein kinase B/Akt, p38, and c-Jun N-terminal kinase pathways.[61] Molecular docking studies reveal a strong affinity of lignans for core targets, with luteolin achieving the highest docking score for each target, thus offering a solid foundation for this study.

  The treatment of OLP with HB primarily involves the response to lipopolysaccharides, epithelial cell proliferation, leukocyte proliferation, and lymphocyte proliferation. The primary targets are closely associated with protein serine/threonine/tyrosine kinase activity, transcription factors, cytokines, and chemokines. It involves multiple pathways related to inflammation and metabolic processes are involved. KEGG analysis revealed that the PI3K–Akt, AGE–RAGE, and TNF signaling pathway are among the main therapeutic targets, with the PI3K–Akt pathway being particularly important. Abnormal AKT pathways were observed in OLP, and aberrant PI3K/AKT/MTOR signaling disrupt the cytokine network, leading to imbalance by altering T-cell and keratinocyte interactions.[45] The PI3K/AKT pathway regulates metabolic pathways, cell proliferation, survival, gene expression, and protein synthesis in oral and lingual squamous cell carcinomas. Inhibiting of PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling can prevent epithelial-mesenchymal transition in oral squamous cell carcinoma.[62]


## 5. Conclusion
  Network pharmacology and molecular docking studies show that compound HB interacts with several targets, such as TNF, PIK3R1, AKT1, primarily affecting the PL3K/AKT signaling pathway. HB’s therapeutic effects are mediated through 20 active compounds that regulate epithelial cell and lymphocyte proliferation, inhibit protein kinase activity, and modulate transcription factors, cytokines, and chemokines. This study investigates HB’s multi-target potential in treating OLP, identifying relevant targets and pathways for further investigation. However, it is important to note the study’s limitations. The derived information obtained depends on databases, whose completeness and accuracy are debatable. Conclusions from network pharmacology should be corroborated with serological tests and pharmacokinetics studies for a more robust quantitative analysis. Ideally, inflammation should be further investigated through validation with animal and cellular experiments.
