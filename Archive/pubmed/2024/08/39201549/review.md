## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (review article)
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, structural coherence, evidence quality, and claims as presented in the provided text
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** This review argues that the AT-hook DNA-binding domain is a versatile motif that enables nuclear factors to interact with the DNA minor groove at AT-rich sequences, and that beyond canonical DNA binding, AT-hooks also mediate RNA interactions, protein-protein interactions, enzymatic activities, and are modulated by post-translational modifications. The authors survey approximately one hundred AT-hook-containing proteins identified via literature search, with detailed discussion of HMGA, PATZ1, ZFAT, LEDGF/p75, AKNA, and BRG1/BAF, and propose an "AT-hook network" hypothesis wherein competition among AT-hook proteins may have functional consequences.
- **Visible evidence base** Full text including abstract, sections 1.1-1.4, 2.1-2.11, and 3 (Conclusions), with references cited numerically
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Figures 1-4 and Supplementary Table S1 are referenced but not provided; reference list is not provided; no search methodology details for the literature review are given

## Reviewer

- **Overall assessment** This is a competent and readable review that consolidates known information about AT-hook domains across a range of nuclear factors, with a useful emphasis on underappreciated functions such as RNA binding and enzymatic activity. The manuscript is strongest when discussing the HMGA family, where the authors demonstrate clear expertise. However, the review suffers from an unclear scope definition, a non-transparent literature search methodology, and a tendency to overstate conclusions from limited or indirect evidence. The central hypothesis of an "AT-hook network" is intriguing but is presented as speculation without a clear framework for how it could be tested. The manuscript would benefit from a more systematic approach to the literature survey and a clearer distinction between established findings and hypotheses.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying DNA-binding domains, chromatin architecture, transcription factor biology, and epigenetic regulation would find this review useful. Clinically oriented investigators interested in HMGA proteins as cancer targets or in minor groove binders as therapeutic agents would also benefit. The review provides a convenient entry point for those new to the AT-hook field and highlights lesser-known AT-hook-containing proteins that may warrant further investigation.
- **Major strengths** The review fills a gap by consolidating information on AT-hook-containing proteins beyond the well-studied HMGA family. The discussion of non-canonical AT-hook functions (RNA binding, enzymatic activity, protein interactions) is valuable and timely. The authors appropriately highlight the potential of minor groove binders as therapeutic tools and discuss their limitations honestly. The figures, as described, appear to provide useful schematic overviews of domain architectures and the proposed network hypothesis.
- **Major Concerns** The concerns below identify issues with scope definition, evidence quality, and overinterpretation that should be addressed before the review's conclusions can be considered fully established.
- **Minor Comments** Several smaller issues relate to clarity, consistency, and completeness that would improve the manuscript's quality.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** The lack of a described search methodology (R1-M1) and the unsupported claim regarding the number of AT-hook-containing proteins (R1-M2) are the primary technical issues. The speculative network hypothesis (R1-M3) is not a technical failing per se but requires reframing.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**  
  *Originality*: Moderate. The review consolidates existing knowledge and offers a novel framing (the "AT-hook network"), but does not present new primary data or a fundamentally new synthesis.  
  *Scientific importance*: Moderate. AT-hook domains are relevant to chromatin biology and disease, but the field is niche, and the review does not establish that the topic has broad implications beyond specialist audiences.  
  *Interdisciplinary readership*: Limited. The content is primarily of interest to molecular biologists and biochemists; the clinical sections are brief and do not substantially broaden the appeal.  
  *Technical soundness*: Adequate in the descriptive sections, but the lack of a transparent search methodology and the overstatement of the protein count are weaknesses.  
  *Readability for nonspecialists*: Generally good. The writing is clear, and the concepts are introduced progressively. However, some sections assume familiarity with chromatin terminology.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The review has merit and fills a niche, but the search methodology must be described, the protein count claim must be qualified, and the speculative sections must be clearly framed as hypotheses rather than established conclusions.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Methodology transparency
- **Claim pointer** The authors state: "using a literature search, we identified about one hundred AT-hook-containing proteins"
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract; Section 2; Supplementary Table S1 (not provided)
- **Concern** The manuscript does not describe the search strategy, inclusion criteria, or date of the literature search. The claim of "about one hundred" proteins is presented without methodological context, making it impossible to assess completeness, reproducibility, or potential bias in the selection.
- **Why it matters** A review's value depends on the systematicity and transparency of its literature coverage. Without a described methodology, readers cannot determine whether the protein list is comprehensive, representative, or skewed toward well-studied examples. This is particularly important given that the authors acknowledge that for many proteins, only the presence of the domain has been noted without functional characterisation.
- **Resolution test** The authors should add a methods paragraph or subsection describing the search terms, databases used, date of search, inclusion and exclusion criteria, and how the final list of proteins was curated. Ideally, the search strategy should be provided in sufficient detail to be reproducible.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence quality
- **Claim pointer** The claim of "about one hundred AT-hook-containing proteins" is presented as a definitive count.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract; Section 2; Supplementary Table S1 (not provided)
- **Concern** The count of approximately one hundred proteins is stated without qualification regarding how many of these have been functionally validated as AT-hook-containing proteins versus predicted from sequence homology. The authors themselves note that for many proteins, only the presence of the domain has been indicated, not functional characterisation. The distinction between bioinformatically predicted and experimentally validated AT-hooks is critical for the review's credibility.
- **Why it matters** If a substantial fraction of the counted proteins are only predicted to contain AT-hooks, the number may be misleading. Conversely, if the count is limited to experimentally validated examples, the search may have missed relevant proteins. The ambiguity undermines confidence in the review's foundational claim.
- **Resolution test** The authors should clarify the basis for inclusion in Supplementary Table S1, distinguishing between experimentally validated AT-hooks, sequence-predicted AT-hooks, and AT-hook-like motifs. The count should be qualified accordingly, and the limitations of the identification approach should be acknowledged.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** No
- **Axis** Interpretation and speculation
- **Claim pointer** The authors propose an "AT-hook network" wherein "subtle changes in the presence of AT-hook-containing factor coordinately impact the functionality of the network"
- **Evidence pointer** Section 3 (Conclusions); Figure 4 (not provided)
- **Concern** The "AT-hook network" hypothesis is presented as a plausible framework, but the evidence supporting it is indirect. The authors cite competition among paralogous transcription factors as a precedent, but do not provide direct evidence for competition among AT-hook proteins specifically. The claim that AT-hook proteins could "displace" each other from DNA is speculative and not supported by experimental data presented in this review.
- **Why it matters** The network hypothesis is positioned as a forward-looking conclusion, but without a clear articulation of testable predictions or existing supporting evidence, it risks being perceived as unsupported speculation. This weakens the review's concluding message.
- **Resolution test** The authors should either (a) present any available experimental evidence for competition or displacement among AT-hook proteins, or (b) clearly reframe this section as an explicitly speculative "open question" with proposed experimental approaches to test the hypothesis. The distinction between established findings and hypotheses must be unambiguous.

- **Concern ID** R1-M4
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** No
- **Axis** Scope and depth consistency
- **Claim pointer** The review claims to cover "about one hundred AT-hook-containing proteins" but provides detailed discussion of only a small subset.
- **Evidence pointer** Section 2 (Sections 2.1-2.11); Supplementary Table S1 (not provided)
- **Concern** The manuscript provides detailed treatment of HMGA, PATZ1, ZFAT, LEDGF/p75, AKNA, and BRG1/BAF, but the remaining proteins from the claimed count of approximately one hundred are only listed in the supplementary table. The criteria for selecting which proteins received detailed discussion are not stated. This creates an imbalance between the broad claim and the depth of coverage.
- **Why it matters** Readers may wonder whether the selected proteins are representative or whether the selection introduces bias. A review that claims broad coverage but provides depth on only a few examples should justify the selection criteria, particularly if the goal is to support general conclusions about AT-hook function.
- **Resolution test** The authors should state their criteria for selecting proteins for detailed discussion (e.g., depth of literature, disease relevance, functional diversity). Alternatively, they could provide a summary table in the main text that categorises all identified proteins by family, function, and evidence level, allowing readers to see the full landscape at a glance.

### Minor Comments

- **Concern ID** R1-m1
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Clarity
- **Affected element** Section 1.1
- **Evidence pointer** Section 1.1, paragraph 2
- **Issue** The sentence "The AT-hook DNA-binding domain (DBD), hereafter referred to as 'AT-hook', is a peculiar peptide motif of 10-15 amino acid residues that can fit into the narrow minor groove of DNA and cause bends in the DNA structure" uses "peculiar" in a way that may be ambiguous to non-specialist readers. The term could be interpreted as either "distinctive" or "strange."
- **Required correction** Replace "peculiar" with a more precise term such as "distinctive" or "characteristic" to avoid ambiguity.

- **Concern ID** R1-m2
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Consistency
- **Affected element** Section 1.1
- **Evidence pointer** Section 1.1, paragraph 3
- **Issue** The text states that "Most AT-hook motifs have a GRP core sequence" but later refers to "the central RGR core" in the context of HMGA proteins. The relationship between GRP and RGR is not explicitly clarified. This could confuse readers who are not familiar with the field.
- **Required correction** Add a brief clarification that the GRP core is the consensus sequence, while in HMGA proteins the core is often referred to as RGR, and explain the relationship (e.g., "the GRP core, which in HMGA proteins is typically RGR").

- **Concern ID** R1-m3
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Completeness
- **Affected element** Section 2.2 (PATZ1)
- **Evidence pointer** Section 2.2, paragraph 2
- **Issue** The text describes PATZ1's role in various cancers and its interaction with p53, but does not explicitly state whether the AT-hook domain is required for the p53 interaction or whether this interaction is DNA-dependent. This is relevant to the review's theme of AT-hook multifunctionality.
- **Required correction** Add a sentence clarifying whether the AT-hook is directly involved in the p53 interaction or whether this is mediated by other domains, and whether this has been experimentally tested.

- **Concern ID** R1-m4

- **Required correction** Add a citation to the structural study that supports this claim, or soften the language to indicate that this is based on structural models.

- **Concern ID** R1-m5

- **Concern ID** R1-m6
- **Severity** Minor

- **Issue** The concluding section introduces the "AT-hook network" concept and the possibility of "liquid/liquid phase separation processes" in the final paragraph. These concepts are not introduced earlier in the review, making the conclusion feel abrupt for readers unfamiliar with these topics.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim of "about one hundred AT-hook-containing proteins" is not verifiable without the search methodology and Supplementary Table S1.
- The statement that AT-hook motifs are "essential" for the binding of SA1 to telomeres, D1 to centromeric heterochromatin, and Cbx2 to major satellites is presented without discussion of experimental evidence or potential caveats.
- The claim that HMGA1's lyase activity is "essential" for efficient removal of DNA lesions is presented as established, but the text does not provide details on the experimental systems or whether this has been confirmed in vivo.
- The proposal of an "AT-hook network" with competitive displacement among AT-hook proteins is speculative and not supported by direct evidence presented in this review.
- The statement that eRNA binding and AT-hook involvement in phase separation "may well represent a new scientific frontier" is forward-looking speculation and should be clearly labelled as such.
- The claim that PATZ1 "can either activate or inhibit apoptosis, depending on the cellular context" is presented without discussion of the experimental evidence or the specific conditions that determine the outcome.
- The text states that "the absence of the N-terminal and C-terminal extensions of AT-hooks has a strong influence on the DNA-binding affinity" but does not specify which extensions or which AT-hooks were tested, making the claim difficult to evaluate.
