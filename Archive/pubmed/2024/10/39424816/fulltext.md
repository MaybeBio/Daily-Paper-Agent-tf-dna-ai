# Prognostic significance of ER-to-PR difference in ER+/HER2- early breast cancer


## Supplementary Information
  The online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-74608-w.


## Introduction
  Breast cancer is one of the most common malignant tumors among women worldwide, and it has the highest morbidity and mortality rates among female malignancies1. ER+/HER2- breast cancer accounts for approximately 60% ~ 70% of all breast cancers2. While ER+/HER2- breast cancer generally has a more favorable prognosis compared to HER2-positive and triple-negative breast cancer, there remains considerable prognostic variability among patients. Therefore, it is essential to enhance the current prognostic evaluation tools for patients with ER+/HER2- breast cancer.

  Currently, the assessment of postoperative recurrence risk in ER+/HER2- breast cancer primarily depends on the clinicopathological characteristics of patients and multigene assays3. Traditional prognostic factors, including tumor size, lymph node status, tumor grade, ki67 index and molecular subtypes4,5, provide valuable insights for managing patients with ER+/HER2- breast cancer. The advent of multigene assays, such as Oncotype DX6,7, Mammaprint8, PAM509,10, EndoPredict11, Breast Cancer Index12, and RecurIndex13, has introduced additional tools for prognostication and treatment planning in ER+/HER2- breast cancer. Although the clinical validity of these multigene assays has been confirmed by extensive research, adjuvant treatment decisions for breast cancer patients after surgery still predominantly depend on precise clinical and pathological evaluations. Moreover, given the high cost and limited accessibility of multigene testing, there is a pressing need to identify more accessible and cost-effective prognostic markers to help clinicians more accurately assess recurrence risk and guide treatment strategies.

  The expression of the estrogen receptor (ER) and progesterone receptor (PR), which are easily accessible, is routinely evaluated in breast cancer patients to predict their response to endocrine therapy and guide treatment decisions14,15. Based on ER and PR expression, patients are classified into Luminal A or Luminal B16, single or double hormone receptor-positive17 and ER-low positive breast cancer18 subtypes based on the expression of ER and PR, each associated with distinct prognoses. However, the prognostic significance of the ER-to-PR difference (EPD) in ER+/HER2- early breast cancer (EBC) has not been well-established. In this study, we define the ER-to-PR difference as the numerical differenc between the expression levels of ER and PR, as determined by immunohistochemical staining. We then investigate the prognostic value of EPD in ER+/HER2- EBC, with the aim of refining existing risk stratification strategies and enhancing the accuracy of clinical decision-making.


## Methods

### Study cohort
  This study included a large cohort (n = 2873) of patients with ER+/HER2- EBC at stages I-III, who had not received any treatment prior to surgery, from West China Hospital (WCH) between 2007 and 2017. Clinical pathology, treatment, and prognosis data were collected, including patient diagnosis, age, menopausal status, tumor size, lymph node status, histological grade, tumor stage, and the expression levels of ER, PR, HER2 and Ki67. According to the 2020 ASCO/CAP guidelines for estrogen receptor (ER) and progesterone receptor (PR) testing in breast cancer, cases with 1–10% staining should be reported as ER low positive due to their distinct biological characteristics, which are more akin to those of triple-negative breast cancer. This subtype typically exhibits higher heterogeneity and greater aggressiveness, and the benefit of endocrine therapy for these patients remains controversial. Consequently, this study only included ER/HER2- EBC patients with ER expression ≥ 10%. The validation cohort (n = 467) was obtained from the published data of Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center (FUSCC) (2009–2016) from Xi Jin et al.19, and all the patients had equivalent clinical information. ### X-tile
  To investigate the potential prognostic value of the EPD for ER+/HER2- EBC, X-tile software20 (Yale University, New haven, CT, USA) was utilized to determine the optimal cutoff value for patient stratification.


### Follow-up
  All patients with breast cancer were enrolled in the Breast Cancer Management Information System of the West China Hospital of Sichuan University21,22. Clinical, pathological and treatment information was prospectively collected by professional physicians. Each patient undergo outpatient or telephone follow-up every 3 to 4 months during the first 2 years, every 6 months for 3 to 5 years, and annually thereafter following the initial diagnosis. Patients enrolled in this retrospective study were followed until their death or until May 31, 2023. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the time from the date of initial surgery to the time of death from any cause. Disease-free survival (DFS) was defined as the time from the first surgery to the occurrence of any type of recurrence (local, regional or distant) or death from breast cancer.


### Predictive models and metrics evaluation
  To evaluate the prognostic ability of the EPD, we trained six different predictive models, including Cox Proportional Hazards (CoxPH), Elastic Net Regression (Elastic Net), Ridge Regression (Ridge), Random Survival Forests (RSF), Support Vector Machine (SVM) and eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost). In this study, DFS data were used as outcome measures. External datasets were used to validate the reliability of the models. The AUC value and Concordance Index (C-index) were used as evaluation metrics.


### Statistical analysis
  X-tile software was utilized to identify the optimal cutoff values. The chi-square test was employed to assess differences in the distribution of variables between groups. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis was utilized to generate DFS and OS curves, and the log-rank test was employed to compare the differences between groups. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were also conducted to identify clinicopathological parameters associated with DFS, and to investigate the prognostic significance of EPD. ROC curves were plotted to assess the ability of the predictive models, and AUC values were calculated to evaluate the predictive ability of the models. All statistical analyses were two-sided, and P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Relevant statistical analyses were performed using SPSS.26, Prism 9.5.0, and R 4.3.2.


## Results

### Patient baseline characteristics
  A total of 3340 patients with ER+/HER2- EBC were included in this study, with 2,873 patients in the training cohort from WCH and 467 patients in the validation cohort from FUSCC. Detailed characteristics of all patients were shown in Table S1. In the WCH and FUSCC cohorts,1791 patients (62.34%) and 204 patients (43.68%), respectively, were under 50 years of age. Clinical stage I was observed in 26.63% and 27.19% of patients, stage II in 52.42% and 48.61%, and stage III in 16.32% and 24.20%, respectively. Luminal A subtype was present in 24.33% and 12.21% of patients, while Luminal B subtype accounted for 74.77% and 87.79% in the WCH and FUSCC cohorts, respectively. Patients who received adjuvant chemotherapy-endocrine therapy (CT-ET) Luminal A subtype was present in 24.33% and 12.21% of patients, while Luminal B subtype accounted for 74.77% and 87.79% in the WCH and FUSCC cohorts, respectively. The median follow-up time was 94 months for WCH cohort and 83 months for FUSCC cohort.


### Associations between clinicopathological characteristics and EPD
  X-tile software was used to determine the optimal cutoff value of the EPD for predicting DFS in patients with ER+/HER2- EBC. Based on the WCH cohort, the optimal cutoff value for EPD was identified as 10% (Fig. 2). Patients were then categorized into two groups: EPD-low (ER-PR ≤ 10%) and EPD-high (ER-PR > 10%) for further analysis. The EPD-low group was observed to exhibit a higher likelihood of having age < 50 years (P < 0.001), tumor size ≤ 2 cm (P = 0.002), LN negative (P = 0.010), clinical stage I (P < 0.001), grade 1–2 (P < 0.001), Ki67 ≤ 20% (P < 0.001) and luminal A (P < 0.001) (Table 1). Similar results were observed in FUSCC cohort, except for no difference in tumor size, LN status and clinical stage (Table S2).

### Prognostic impact of the EPD on DFS and OS
  In WCH cohort, patients in the EPD-high group had poorer DFS and OS compared to those in the EPD-low group (HR: 1.701, P < 0.001 and HR: 1.772, P = 0.001) (Fig. 3A). The 7-year DFS rates for the EPD-high and EPD-low groups were 80.9% and 94.5%, respectively (P < 0.001), while the 7-year OS rates were 96% and 97.5%, respectively (P = 0.031) (Fig. 3B). Similar results were observed in the FUSCC cohort, where the EPD-high group also demonstrated poorer DFS and OS compared to the EPD-low group (Figure S1). We further stratified all patients based on molecular subtypes, as well as ER and PR status (Fig. 3C). Kaplan-Meier analysis showed that patients with ER ≤ 50% had significantly lower DFS compared to those with ER expression > 50% (HR: 1.964, P < 0.001). Similarly, stratification based on PR expression showed that patients with PR ≤ 50% or PR ≤ 20% had lower DFS compared to those with higher PR expression (HR: 1.510, P = 0.002 or HR: 1.383, P = 0.035). Additionally, patients with luminal B had lower DFS compared to those with luminal A, although the difference was not statistically significant. (P = 0.072).

### Prognostic impact of the EPD in differert subgroups
  Based on PR expression and ki67 index, ER+/HER2- breast cancer patients were classified into luminal A or luminal B subtypes. As shown in Fig. 4A, the EPD effectively stratified luminal A (HR: 2.068, P = 0.049) and luminal B (HR: 1.576, P = 0.002) tumors into two distinct prognostic subgroups. Additionally, EPD could stratify the T1-2N0-1 subgroup (HR: 1.851, P < 0.001) (Fig. 4B). Considering systemic treatment, patients in EPD-high group who received CT-ET had poorer DFS (HR:1.693, P < 0.001), while there was no significant difference in DFS between patients with EPD-low and EPD-high who received only endocrine therapy (ET) (P = 0.086) (Fig. 4C). Similar results were observed in the FUSCC cohort (Figure S2A-B). Furthermore, when evaluating surgical modalities, EPD-high patients had poorer DFS compared to EPD-low patients, regardless of whether they underwent breast-conserving surgery (HR: 1.607, P < 0.001) or modified radical mastectomy (HR: 3.002, P = 0.035) (Figure S2C). In the T1-2N0-1 EPD-low subgroup, there was no significant difference in DFS between patients who received CT-ET and those who received ET alone (P = 0.136) (Fig. 4D).

### The combination of the EPD and clinicopathological parameters can effectively predict the DFS of ER+/HER2- EBC
  To determine whether the EPD is an independent prognostic factor for ER+/HER2- EBC, we performed univariate Cox regression analysis on all clinicopathological parameters (Fig. 5A). We also conducted a correlation analysis for ER, PR and EPD, which revealed a strong correlation between EPD and PR (|r| = 0.8, P < 0.001), indicating potential multicollinearity. Subsequently, tumor size, lymph node status, histological grade, Ki-67 index, ER, and EPD were incorporated into a multivariate Cox regression analysis. The results revealed that, after adjusting for clinicopathological parameters, EPD remained an independent prognostic factor (HR: 1.496, P = 0.004), as did ER (HR: 0.426, P = 0.007) (Fig. 5B).

We assessed the predictive performance of these models using AUC values and the C-index. Based on AUC values and C-index.   Furthermore, we stratified the WCH cohort into CoxPH-low and CoxPH-high risk groups based on the median risk score derived from the CoxPH model. In the overall population, the CoxPH-high risk group demonstrated poorer DFS and OS compared to the CoxPH-low risk group, with similar findings observed in the T1-2N0-1 subgroup (Fig. 6C). Within the T1-2N0-1 CoxPH-low subgroup, there was no significant difference in DFS between patients who received CT-ET and those who received ET alone (P = 0.487) (Fig. 6D). These results were further validated in the FUSCC cohort (Fig. 6E-F).

## Discussion
  Traditional molecular prognostic markers of ER+/HER2- breast cancer mainly include ER, PR, ki67 index and molecular subtypes23. While these markers can provide some insight into the biological behavior of tumor cells, their accuracy and consistency in predicting patient prognosis are limited. This study demonstrates that the EPD not only reflects the biological behavior of tumor cells but also serves as a significant biomarker for evaluating the prognosis of ER+/HER2- EBC.

  ER and PR play critical roles in the development and progression of breast cancer24. Previous studies have established that both ER and PR are critical biomarkers in breast cancer, with their expression levels being predictive of response to endocrine therapy and overall prognosis3,22,24. Additionally, the role of cytoskeletal components such as F-actin has been shown to be significant in the progression and metastasis of breast cancer, further highlighting the importance of understanding the underlying molecular mechanisms25,26. Several studies have demonstrated that ER+/PR- breast cancer has worse survival outcomes compared to ER+/PR + breast cancer27. However, the relationship between ER and PR, particularly the discordance in their expression, has not been fully explored as a combined prognostic indicator. In this study, we found that EPD could effectively predict the prognosis of ER+/HER2- EBC, with the optimal cut-off value of 10%. Patients with EPD-high exhibited lower DFS and poorer OS compared to those with EPD-low. Our study addresses this gap by focusing on the EPD, which reflects the relative balance between ER and PR expression levels. Compared to existing markers such as the individual ER or PR levels, the EPD provides a more comprehensive measure, potentially capturing subtle dysfunctions in the ER signaling pathway that may not be apparent when considering these markers in isolation. This aligns with recent efforts to refine prognostic tools and therapeutic targets within this subtype. For example, the development of machine learning models to identify effective ERα and ERβ inhibitors demonstrates the ongoing innovation in targeting resistant breast cancer28. Additionally, the role of novel molecular markers like TUBA1B, which has been shown to influence immune responses and cancer progression, further emphasizes the complexity of breast cancer biology and the importance of comprehensive biomarker analysis in improving patient outcomes29.

  By comparing the stratification effects in ER+/HER2- EBC, we found that the stratification effect of EPD was superior to that of molecular subtypes and PR alone, but slightly inferior to ER in WCH cohort. This may be attributed to the fact that EPD comprehensively considers the expression levels of both ER and PR, thereby overcoming the limitations of single-indicator evaluation. The EPD represents the numerical difference between the expression levels of ER and PR, which are both crucial components of the hormone receptor pathway in breast cancer. ER is a well-established nuclear receptor that regulates cell growth, differentiation, and apoptosis by binding to estrogen30, while PR is a downstream target of ER signaling and modulates the effects of estrogen in breast tissues31,32. The underlying biology supporting the prognostic significance of EPD lies in the interplay between ER and PR signaling pathways. In cases where PR expression is low relative to ER (resulting in EPD-high), this may indicate a dysfunction or attenuation in the ER signaling pathway. Such dysfunction can lead to reduced sensitivity to endocrine therapies, as these therapies often rely on the intact functioning of the ER pathway to exert their effects. Consequently, patients with a higher EPD may experience poorer outcomes due to the compromised efficacy of endocrine therapy, which is crucial for managing ER+/HER2- breast cancer. Furthermore, the heterogeneity in the expression of ER and PR reflects the diverse biological behavior of tumors within the ER+/HER2- subtype. Tumors with EPD-high are more likely to exhibit aggressive characteristics and may be less responsive to conventional therapies, further explaining their association with a poorer prognosis. This hypothesis is consistent with previous studies that have observed that low PR expression (relative to ER) is often associated with a higher risk of recurrence and worse survival outcomes in breast cancer patients22,24. The independent prognostic value of EPD, even after adjusting for other clinicopathological factors in our multivariate analysis, underscores its potential as a biomarker that encapsulates both the degree of ER pathway activation and the subsequent impact on PR expression. By providing an integrated view of hormone receptor signaling, EPD adds a unique dimension to risk stratification in ER+/HER2- EBC patients, enhancing the precision of prognosis and treatment planning.

  Our study identifies the EPD as a novel independent prognostic marker, validated through both traditional statistical methods and machine learning models using a large, well-characterized cohort from WCH, and externally validated with data from FUSCC. A significant strength of EPD is its clinical applicability, offering a cost-effective and accessible tool derived from routine immunohistochemistry, making it particularly valuable in settings with limited resources for advanced genomic testing. However, the retrospective design and single-center training cohort may limit the generalizability of our findings. Additionally, the lack of functional validation of the biological mechanisms underlying EPD’s prognostic significance warrants further investigation. Future research should focus on prospective, multicenter studies to confirm EPD’s prognostic value, explore its integration with other molecular markers, and assess its role in clinical trials to refine personalized treatment strategies in ER+/HER2- breast cancer.


## Conclusion
  In conclusion, our study highlights the potential of EPD as a novel and independent prognostic marker in ER+/HER2- EBC. Due to its accessibility and cost-effectiveness, EPD could become a valuable tool in clinical practice, aiding in the refinement of risk stratification and the guidance of treatment decisions. However, further research is necessary to validate its clinical utility and explore its integration into comprehensive prognostic models.
