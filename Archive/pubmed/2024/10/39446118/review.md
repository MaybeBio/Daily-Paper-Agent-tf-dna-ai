## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence as presented in the abstract; no access to full text, figures, tables, or supplementary materials
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The study investigates the prevalence of the rs61330082 C/T variant and serum visfatin levels in Mexican individuals with metabolic syndrome (MetS), combining clinical measurements, dietary assessment, PCR-RFLP genotyping, ELISA, and molecular docking. The authors report that the TT genotype was present in 10% of the population, that women with the CT+TT genotype had higher triglycerides and VLDL-C, that no significant association was found between MetS and the dominant model (OR = 1.41, 95% CI = 0.61-3.44, p = 0.53), and that PAX5 was identified as a transcription factor binding to the polymorphic site.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no numerical data beyond those stated, no methodological details, no figures or tables
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, detailed methods, participant characteristics, genotype frequencies by group, visfatin data, docking parameters and results, dietary pattern analysis details, statistical analysis outputs, ethics statement, and any supplementary information

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a potentially interesting exploratory study linking a genetic variant to lipid parameters in a Mexican population with metabolic syndrome. However, the evidence provided is insufficient to evaluate the robustness of the claims. The primary finding regarding triglycerides in women is plausible but under-supported by the limited data shown. The lack of significant association with MetS itself, combined with the modest sample size, weakens the overall case. The bioinformatics component is mentioned but not described in sufficient detail to assess its validity. The manuscript may have merit as a preliminary report, but the current abstract does not establish the case convincingly.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in genetic epidemiology, metabolic syndrome pathophysiology, and nutrigenetics may find interest. The focus on a Mexican population adds population-specific value, and the combination of genotyping with bioinformatics could appeal to those studying transcription factor binding in metabolic traits. Clinicians interested in personalized risk factors for dyslipidemia might also take note, though the clinical applicability is limited at this stage.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses a relevant public health issue in a specific population. The use of multiple approaches (genotyping, serum protein measurement, dietary assessment, and molecular docking) is integrative. The reporting of a non-significant association for the primary outcome is transparent and avoids overclaiming.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Statistical validity  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that women carrying the CT+TT genotype had higher serum levels of triglycerides and VLDL-C.  
- **Evidence pointer** Results section, abstract; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract reports a sex-stratified finding without providing effect sizes, confidence intervals, or p-values for the triglyceride and VLDL-C comparisons. The only statistical result shown is the non-significant OR for MetS. It is unclear whether the sex-specific analysis was pre-specified or exploratory, and whether multiple testing corrections were applied.  
- **Why it matters** Without these details, the reader cannot judge the strength or reliability of the main positive finding. A post-hoc subgroup analysis with a small sample size could easily produce spurious associations.  
- **Resolution test** Provide the full statistical output for the sex-stratified analysis, including adjusted and unadjusted models, confidence intervals, p-values, and a statement on multiple testing. If the finding persists after adjustment for confounders and correction for multiple comparisons, the claim would be supported.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Missing evidence  
- **Claim pointer** The title and objectives emphasize serum visfatin levels, but the abstract does not report any visfatin results.  
- **Evidence pointer** Results section, abstract; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract mentions that visfatin was assessed by ELISA but provides no data on serum levels, comparisons between genotypes, or associations with MetS. This is a central component of the study as framed by the title.  
- **Why it matters** The absence of visfatin data makes the study incomplete as presented. The reader cannot evaluate whether the visfatin hypothesis was tested or supported.  
- **Resolution test** Include visfatin levels by genotype and MetS status, with appropriate statistical comparisons, in the abstract or clearly state that these results are reported in the full manuscript.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Methodological transparency  
- **Claim pointer** The authors state that molecular docking was used to infer the binding of transcription factors in the polymorphic region and identified PAX5.  
- **Evidence pointer** Methods and Results sections, abstract; location not provided  
- **Concern** No details are provided on the docking methodology, including software, scoring functions, structural templates, or how the polymorphic site was modeled. The identification of PAX5 is presented without supporting scores or validation.  
- **Why it matters** Molecular docking results are highly dependent on methodological choices. Without details, the claim that PAX5 binds the polymorphic site cannot be assessed for reliability or biological relevance.  
- **Resolution test** Describe the docking protocol in the full manuscript, including software versions, parameters, and validation steps. Provide docking scores or binding affinities for PAX5 and ideally for other transcription factors tested as controls.

- **Concern ID** R1-M4  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** No  
- **Axis** Sample size and generalizability  
- **Claim pointer** The study included 197 individuals, and the TT genotype was present in only 10% of the population.  
- **Evidence pointer** Methods and Results sections, abstract; location not provided  
- **Concern** The sample size is modest, and the TT genotype frequency of 10% means that the number of TT homozygotes is approximately 20. The sex-stratified analysis for the dominant model would further reduce group sizes, raising concerns about statistical power and precision.  
- **Why it matters** Small subgroup sizes can lead to unstable estimates and limit the generalizability of the findings to the broader Mexican population.  
- **Resolution test** Report the number of individuals in each genotype group by sex, provide power calculations, and discuss the limitations of the sample size in the full manuscript.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that women with the CT+TT genotype have higher triglycerides and VLDL-C is unsupported in the abstract due to missing effect sizes and p-values.
- The claim that PAX5 binds the polymorphic site is unsupported without docking details or scores.
- The conclusion that T allele carriers have a higher risk of high triglycerides is not directly supported by the data shown, as the only reported OR is for MetS, not for triglycerides.
- The prevalence of the TT genotype at 10% is stated but not contextualized against Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium or population reference data.
- Any claim regarding visfatin levels is not assessable because no visfatin results are reported in the abstract.
