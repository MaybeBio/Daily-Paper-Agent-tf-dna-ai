# Heat shock protein 22 alleviates doxorubicin-induced kidney injury by suppressing oxidative stress and apoptosis


## Supplementary Information
  The online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-75277-5.


## Introduction
  Doxorubicin (DOX)-induced kidney injury is a chronic progressive condition that ultimately leads to uremia, significantly impacting a patient’s quality of life1. The use of DOX in cancer chemotherapy has been associated with complications such as kidney injury, which further result in adverse events2. In the past few years, research on DOX-induced kidney injury has been limited, resulting in a scarcity of specific treatments for this condition. Thus, there is an urgent need to explore novel treatments for DOX-induced kidney injury.

  Heat shock protein 22 (HSP22), a small member of the heat shock protein family, has demonstrated protective effects in various conditions including endothelial injury, cerebral hemorrhage, and myocardial injury3–5. Yu et al. demonstrated that HSP22 reduces reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels, thereby suppressing diabetes-induced endothelial injury3. Additionally, Fan et al. demonstrated that HSP22 mitigates DRP1-triggered mitochondrial apoptosis and decreases oxidative stress, thereby alleviating early brain injury in subarachnoid hemorrhage rats4. Studies on myocardial injury have indicated that HSP22 overexpression attenuates hypoxia-induced oxidative phosphorylation dysfunction and reduces superoxide production, which eventually reduces the degree of myocardial injury5. However, the effects of HSP22 on DOX-induced kidney injury remain unclear. In this study, we establish a DOX-induced kidney injury model to investigate the impact of HSP22 on the occurrence and progression of this condition.


## Materials and methods

### Mice and reagents
  Male C57BL/6J mice, aged 6 weeks, were obtained from the Hunan SJA Laboratory Animal Co., Ltd. A total of 24 mice were used in the study. HK2 cells (Human renal cortex proximal tubule epithelial cells), acquired from the Chinese Academy of Medical Science, were used for in vitro experiments. The adeno-associated virus carrying the HSP22 gene (ad-HSP22) was obtained from Shanghai Genechem Co., Ltd. DOX (CAT NO. 25316-40-9), which was purchased from Sigma Co., Ltd., was used to induce kidney injury in the experimental model.


### The mice model of kidney injury and ethical approval
  All procedures involving mice were approved by the Nanchang University Animal Ethical and Welfare Committee (Approval number: NCULAE-20221031137) and adhered to the National Research Council’s Guidelines for the Care and Use of Experimental Animals. Mice were housed under a 12-hour light/dark cycle with a temperature of approximately 25 °C and humidity around 55%. Standard laboratory diet and water were provided ad libitum. After acclimatization, mice were randomly divided into four groups: CON (saline injection, n = 6), ad-HSP22 (ad-HSP22 injection, n = 6), DOX (DOX injection, 20 mg/kg, n = 6), and ad-HSP22 + DOX (ad-HSP22 injection for 4 weeks followed by DOX injection for 5 days, n = 6, 20 mg/kg). Finally, mice were euthanized using 2% isoflurane anesthesia for subsequent experiments.


### Measurement of serum creatinine (SCr) and blood urea nitrogen (BUN) levels
  SCr and BUN levels are crucial indicators of renal function. Elevated levels indicate severe kidney6. Whole blood was collected from euthanized mice, and serum was extracted using a super centrifuge at 3500 g for 25 min at 4℃. SCr and BUN levels were measured using a BS-800 Chemistry Analyzer (Mindray Co., Ltd.). Kidney tissues isolated from mice were stored at -80℃ for further analysis.


### Hematoxylin & Eosin (H&E) staining
  Kidney tissues were immediately fixed in 4% formaldehyde upon excision. Different concentrations of alcohol were used to dehydrate these kidney tissues, which were then embedded in paraffin (5 μm). H&E staining was performed according to standard protocols, and pathological changes were examined using an optical microscope.


### Dihydroethidium (DHE) staining
  The degree of oxidative stress in kidney tissues was assessed using DHE staining. Frozen kidney tissue sections were incubated in DHE solution at 37℃ for 35 min. The sections were then counterstained with DAPI (1 mg/mL) for 10 min to label cell nuclei. After washing with water, the sections were analyzed for oxidative stress levels using a laser-scanning confocal microscope.


### Terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase dUTP nick end labeling (TUNEL) staining
  Paraffin-embedded kidney tissue sections were prepared for TUNEL staining to detect apoptosis. A specific fluorescent TUNEL kit (Roche Co., Ltd.) was used according to the manufacturer’s instructions.


### Immunohistochemical (IHC) staining
  After deparaffinization, the paraffine-embedded kidney tissue sections were deparaffinized and rehydrated through graded alcohol concentrations. Immunol staining was performed for approximately 10 min. Subsequently, sections were incubated with primary antibodies at 4 °C for about 24 h, followed by incubation with HRP-polymer secondary antibodies for 20 min and DAB solution for 10 min. Finally, the nuclei of kidney tissues were counterstained with hematoxylin for 1 min. The protein expressions were evaluated using an optical microscope.


### HK2 cell treatments
  HK2 cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle’s Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin/streptomycin under 5% CO2 at 37℃. Subsequently, the HK2 cells were pre-treated with ad-HSP22, followed by exposure to DOX at a concentration of 1 µg/mL. Next, these HK2 cells were randomly divided into four groups: the CON group received normal saline without additional interventions; the ad-HSP22 group received ad-HSP22 without any additional interventions; the DOX group received DOX (1 µg/mL) without any additional interventions; the ad-HSP22 + DOX group received ad-HSP22 followed by DOX exposure (1 µg/mL). Additionally, the molecular mechanisms were investigated as follows: CON group received normal saline; DOX group received DOX (1 µg/mL) without any additional interventions; ad-HSP22 + DOX group received ad-HSP22 followed by DOX exposure (1 µg/mL); ad-HSP22 + DOX + ML385 group received ad-HSP22, ML385 (Nrf2 inhibitor), and DOX (1 µg/mL) sequentially.


### Flow cytometry
  The kit of flow cytometry was used in the present study (CAT NO. ab14085; Abcam, Cambridge, MA, USA). The experimental procedures of flow cytometry assay were as follows: First, the HK2 cells were incubated with Annexin V-FITC. Then, using FITC signal detector to analyze the Annexin V-FITC binding by flow cytometry and PI staining conducted by the phycoerythrin emission signal detector. At last, the apoptotic rates of HK2 cells were measured by fluorescence microscopy.


### Western blotting
  The total protein was extracted from the kidney tissues of mice by using a protein extraction kit (Beyotime Biotechnology Co., Ltd.). Then, the total protein was divided into different target proteins via 10% sodium-dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel-electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE). Next, these target proteins were transferred onto PVDF membranes (EMD-Millipore Co., Ltd.). These membranes were then incubated with primary antibodies for > 8 h at 4℃, followed by incubation with secondary antibodies for 1 h at room temperature. Finally, a scanner and an advanced chemiluminescence solution were used to detect these target protein bands. The following primary antibodies were used: anti-HSP22 (CAT NO. ab151552; 1:1000; Abcam, Cambridge, MA, USA), anti-NOX-2 (CAT NO. ab310337; 1:1000; Abcam), anti-NOX-4 (CAT NO. ab154244; 1:1000; Abcam), anti-SOD-1 (CAT NO. CST-65778SF; 1:1000; Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, MA, USA), anti-SOD-2 (CAT NO. CST-13141 S; 1:1000; Cell Signaling Technology), anti-Bax (CAT NO. ab32503; 1:1000; Abcam), anti-Bcl-2 (CAT NO. ab182858; 1:1000; Abcam), anti-Nrf2 (CAT NO. ab92946; 1:1000; Abcam), and anti-HO-1 (CAT NO. ab52947; 1:1000; Abcam). The following secondary antibodies were used: goat anti-mouse-IgG (CAT NO. 15014; Proteintech Rosemont, USA) and goat anti-rabbit-IgG (CAT NO. B900210; Proteintech Rosemont). The Image-Lab (version 4.0.1) software was used to analyze the data of the target proteins’ bands.


### Statistical analyses
  Results are presented as means ± standard deviations (S.D). Statistical analysis was conducted using GraphPad Prism (v.7.0) sofware. Analysis of variance (ANOVA) was employed to compare results among different groups, with statistical significance set at p < 0.05 (*p < 0.05, **p < 0.01 and ***p < 0.001).


## Results

### HSP22 alleviates DOX-induced kidney injury in mice
  Figure 1A outlines the experimental procedures conducted in this study. HSP22 protein overexpression was successfully achieved in the experimental mice. Compared to the CON group, DOX treatment significantly reduced HSP22 expression in the DOX group, while it was significantly overexpressed in the ad-HSP22 + DOX group than the DOX group (Fig. 1B, C, and D). Compared with the CON group, DOX significantly increased the serum levels of SCr and BUN in the DOX group, while they were significantly decreased by HSP22 overexpression in the ad-HSP22 + DOX group when compared to the DOX group (Fig. 1E and F). H&E staining revealed that DOX caused severe renal structural damage and cellular edema compared to the CON group, which was mitigated by HSP22 overexpression in the ad-HSP22 + DOX group compared to the DOX group (Fig. 1G). These findings demonstrate that HSP22 alleviates DOX-induced kidney injury in mice.

  Fig. 1A An overview of the animal treatment protocols conducted in the present study. B and C the protein expression of HSP22 was investigated by western blotting in the kidney tissues (n = 6). D IHC staining was conducted to evaluate the protein expression of HSP22 in the kidney tissues (n = 6). E and F The serum levels of SCr and BUN were detected (n = 6). G H&E staining was conducted for evaluating the pathological changes in the kidney tissues (n = 6). ns means no statistically significant, **p < 0.01 and ***p < 0.001. Data represent the mean ± SD of at least 3 times of separate experiments.


### HSP22 reduced DOX-induced renal oxidative stress in mice
  DHE staining was performed to assess oxidative stress in kidney tissues. Compared to the CON group, DOX significantly increased oxidative stress in the DOX group, which was significantly decreased by HSP22 overexpression in the ad-HSP22 + DOX group compared to the DOX group (Fig. 2A). Additionally, expressions of oxidative stress-related proteins were evaluated. DOX upregulated pro-oxidative stress proteins NOX-2 and NOX-4 compared to the CON group, whereas their expression was significantly decreased in the ad-HSP22 + DOX group compared to the DOX group (Fig. 2B, C, and D). Conversely, anti-oxidative stress proteins like SOD-1 and SOD-2 were reduced in the DOX group but were upregulated following HSP22 overexpression in the ad-HSP22 + DOX group compared to the DOX group (Fig. 2B, E, and F). These results indicate that HSP22 reduces DOX-induced renal oxidative stress in mice.

  Fig. 2A DHE staining was conducted to assess the changes of oxidative stress in the kidney tissues (n = 6). B–D, the expressions of pro-oxidative stress proteins, such as NOX-2 and NOX-4, were detected by western blotting (n = 6). B, E and F, the expressions of antioxidant proteins, such as SOD-1 and SOD-2, were detected by western blotting (n = 6). ns means no statistically significant, *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01 and ***p < 0.001. Data represent the mean ± SD of at least 3 times of separate experiments.


### HSP22 decreased DOX-induced renal apoptosis in mice
  TUNEL staining was used to assess apoptosis levels in kidney tissues. DOX significantly increased apoptosis compared to the CON group, but this effect was significantly decreased by HSP22 overexpression in the ad-HSP22 + DOX group compared to the DOX group (Fig. 3A). Additionally, the expressions of apoptosis-related proteins were evaluated. DOX upregulated the expressions of pro-apoptosis protein, like Bax, compared to the CON group, whereas its expression was significantly reduced in the ad-HSP22 + DOX group compared to the DOX group (Fig. 3B, D, and E). Conversely, the anti-apoptotic protein Bcl-2 was reduced by DOX but restored by HSP22 overexpression in the ad-HSP22 + DOX group compared to the DOX group (Fig. 3C, D, and F). These results indicate that HSP22 decreases DOX-induced renal apoptosis in mice.

  Fig. 3A TUNEL staining was performed for evaluating the changes of apoptosis in the kidney tissues (n = 6). B, D and E the protein expression of pro-apoptosis protein Bax was investigated by IHC staining and western blotting in the kidney tissues (n = 6). C, D and F, the protein expression of anti-apoptosis protein Bcl-2 was investigated by IHC staining and western blotting in the kidney tissues (n = 6). ns means no statistically significant, *p < 0.05 and **p < 0.01. Data represent the mean ± SD of at least 3 times of separate experiments.


### HSP22 reduced DOX-induced oxidative stress and apoptosis by activating the Nrf2/HO-1 signaling pathway
  IHC staining and western blot were performed to assess changes in Nrf2 and HO-1 protein expression. Compared to the CON group, DOX reduced Nrf2 and HO-1 expression, whereas their levels were significantly elevated in the ad-HSP22 + DOX group compared to the DOX group (Fig. 4A, B, C, D, and E). These findings suggest that HSP22 reduces DOX-induced oxidative stress and apoptosis by activating the Nrf2/HO-1 signaling pathway in kidney tissues.

  Fig. 4A and B IHC staining was conducted to evaluate the expressions of Nrf2 and HO-1 in the kidney tissues (n = 6). C–E, the protein expressions of Nrf2 and HO-1 were investigated by western blotting in the kidney tissues (n = 6). ns means no statistically significant, *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01 and ***p < 0.001. Data represent the mean ± SD of at least 3 times of separate experiments.


### HSP22 reduced DOX-induced oxidative stress and apoptosis in HK2 cells
  HSP22 protein overexpression was successfully achieved in HK2 cells. Compared to the CON group, DOX reduced HSP22 expression in the DOX group, whereas HSP22 was overexpressed in the ad-HSP22 + DOX group (Fig. 5A and B). The expressions of pro-oxidative stress proteins, NOX-2 and NOX-4, were significantly elevated in the DOX group compared to the CON group, and they were reduced in the ad-HSP22 + DOX group than the DOX group (Fig. 5C, D, and E). Conversely, anti-oxidative stress proteins SOD-1 and SOD-2 showed opposite trends to NOX-2 and NOX-4 (Fig. 5C, F, and G). The apoptotic rates in HK2 cells were assessed by flow cytometry assay. The results showed that DOX increased the apoptotic rates of HK2 cells in the DOX group when compared to the CON group, but HSP22 inhibited this effect in the ad-HSP22 + DOX group (Fig. 5H). Pro-apoptosis protein Bax was upregulated in the DOX group compared to the CON group and downregulated in the ad-HSP22 + DOX group, while anti-apoptosis protein Bcl-2 exhibited opposite changes (Fig. 5I, J, and K). This study also investigated the Nrf2/HO-1 signaling pathway. Compared to the CON group, DOX significantly reduced the expressions of Nrf2 and HO-1, which were restored by HSP22 overexpression in the ad-HSP22 + DOX group (Fig. 5L, M, and N). These results suggest that HSP22 reduces DOX-induced oxidative stress and apoptosis potentially by activating the Nrf2/HO-1 signaling pathway in HK2 cells.

Data represent the mean ± SD of at least 3 times of separate experiments.


### ML385, an inhibitor of Nrf2, reverses the effects of HSP22 on DOX-induced kidney injury in HK2 cells
  ML385, an inhibitor of Nrf2, was used to assess whether the inhibition of Nrf2 reverses the renal protective effect of HSP22 on DOX-induced kidney injury in HK2 cells. Western blot analysis confirmed successful inhibition of Nrf2 expression by ML385 (Fig. 6A and B). Compared to the CON group, DOX reduced HSP22 expression in the DOX group, which was restored in the ad-HSP22 + DOX group, and further elevated in the ad-HSP22 + DOX + ML385 group (Fig. 6C and D). NOX-2 and NOX-4 were significantly increased in the DOX group compared to the CON group but significantly decreased in the ad-HSP22 + DOX group. However, NOX-2 and NOX-4 levels were elevated in the ad-HSP22 + DOX + ML385 group compared to the ad-HSP22 + DOX group (Fig. 6C, F, and G). Conversely, HO-1, SOD-1, and SOD-2 showed opposite trends to NOX-2 and NOX-4 (Fig. 6C, E, H, and I). Bax expression was significantly upregulated in the DOX group compared to the CON group, decreased in the ad-HSP22 + DOX group, and increased in the ad-HSP22 + DOX + ML385 group, while Bcl-2 showed opposite changes (Fig. 6J, K, and L). These findings indicate that ML385 can reverse the effects of HSP22 on DOX-induced kidney injury in HK2 cells by inhibiting the Nrf2 pathway.

## Discussion
  DOX is a widely used anthracycline antibiotic in clinical chemotherapy for various malignant tumors7. Its mechanism of action involves inhibition of DNA and RNA synthesis in tumor cells, accompanied by increased oxidative stress levels, leading to cell death. Therefore, DOX is classified as a periodic non-specific drug8,9. Due to its non-selective cytotoxicity, DOX not only targets tumor cells but also targets normal cells from various organs, such as renal cells10. Although significant efforts have been made to improve DOX-induced kidney injury, effective therapeutic options remain scarce11. Thus, exploring and developing safe and effective treatments for DOX-induced kidney injury is crucial.

  HSP22 belongs to the small molecule heat shock protein family and is known to increase in response to various stressors, enhancing the body’s ability to resist external stimuli12. Additionally, HSP22 exhibits multiple biological functions such as anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, anti-apoptotic, molecular chaperone, and protein kinase activity13,14. Previous studies have demonstrated the protective effects of HSP22 in conditions like endothelial injury, cerebral hemorrhage, and myocardial injury. However, its role in DOX-induced kidney injury remains unclear. In this study, we investigated the effects of HSP22 on DOX-induced kidney injury and elucidated its underlying molecular mechanisms. We found that HSP22 alleviates DOX-induced kidney injury by inhibiting oxidative stress and apoptosis, primarily through the activation of the Nrf2/HO-1 signaling pathway (Fig. 7).

  Fig. 7Graphical abstract of the effects of HSP22 on DOX-induced kidney injury. HSP22 alleviates DOX-induced kidney injury by inhibiting oxidative stress and apoptosis, and the molecular mechanism involved activation of the Nrf2/HO-1-signaling pathway.

  SCr and BUN levels are critical biomarkers reflecting kidney injury15,16. Our study observed significant increases in SCr and BUN levels in serum upon DOX administration, indicative of kidney injury. Importantly, HSP22 overexpression reversed these effects, demonstrating its protective role against DOX-induced kidney injury in mice.

  Oxidative stress is a pivotal factor contributing to kidney injury17. DHE staining revealed that HSP22 overexpression reversed the increase in total superoxide levels induced by DOX. The pro-oxidative stress proteins, like NOX-2 and NOX-418, were significantly elevated by DOX, but attenuated by HSP22 overexpression. Conversely, antioxidant proteins SOD-1 and SOD-219 were significantly reduced by DOX, with their levels restored by HSP22 overexpression. Consistent results were observed between the in vitro and in vivo experiments, suggesting that HSP22 effectively mitigates DOX-induced renal oxidative stress.

  Apoptosis also plays a crucial role in kidney injury pathology20. TUNEL staining showed that DOX significantly upregulated the degree of apoptosis, which was notably suppressed by HSP22 overexpression. Levels of the pro-apoptotic protein Bax21 were elevated by DOX and decreased by HSP22 overexpression, while the anti-apoptotic protein Bcl-222 showed reduced expression with DOX treatment, but this effect was reversed by HSP22 overexpression. Similar findings were observed in both in vitro and in vivo experiments, demonstrating that HSP22 effectively reduces DOX-induced renal apoptosis.

  The Nrf2/HO-1 signaling pathway plays an important role in kidney injury progression23. The present study explored the effects of HSP22 overexpression on Nrf2/HO-1 activation. The results revealed that HSP22 overexpression significantly suppressed the DOX-induced reduction of Nrf2 and HO-1. Furthermore, the use of ML385, an inhibitor of Nrf2, confirmed that the inhibition of Nrf2 reversed the protective effects of HSP22 on DOX-induced kidney injury. Therefore, HSP22 reduces DOX-induced renal oxidative stress and apoptosis through the activation of the Nrf2/HO-1 signaling pathway.

  However, there still esists some limitaions in the present study. Therefore, more research and experiments are required for an in-depth investigation to efficiently elaborate the pathogenesis of DOX induced kidney injury.


## Conclusion
  This study demonstrates that HSP22 alleviates DOX-induced kidney injury by suppressing oxidative stress and apoptosis. The underlying molecular mechanism involves the activation of the Nrf2/HO-1 signaling pathway. These results highlight HSP22 as a potential novel therapeutic target for treating DOX-induced kidney injury.
