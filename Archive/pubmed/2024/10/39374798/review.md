## Reviewer

- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a well-structured and ambitious scope for a review on eTF-dFNA synthetic biosensors. The topic is timely and relevant to the synthetic biology and biosensing communities. However, the abstract is largely descriptive and programmatic, with no specific examples, comparative data, or critical synthesis visible. The claim of being the "first comprehensive and systematic dissection" requires verification against existing literature, which is not possible from the abstract alone. The writing is clear but contains some terminological imprecision, such as "diversed" and "binding" in the title, which should be corrected.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers and practitioners in synthetic biology, biosensor engineering, molecular diagnostics, food safety, and environmental monitoring would find this review valuable. The integration of engineered transcription factors with functional nucleic acids addresses a growing need for more versatile and sensitive biorecognition elements, which is of direct relevance to those developing point-of-care diagnostics and field-deployable sensors.
- **Major strengths** The abstract clearly delineates the scope and structure of the review, which is helpful for readers. The proposed taxonomy of design strategies for eTFs (irrational, rational, semi-rational) and dFNAs (SELEX, modifying, predicting) is logical and suggests a systematic treatment. The stated coverage of applications across multiple domains indicates broad relevance.
- **Major Concerns** The abstract does not provide any specific evidence, examples, or comparative analysis to support the claimed advantages of eTF-dFNA biosensors. The novelty claim of being the "first" comprehensive review is unverifiable from the abstract. The abstract lacks critical discussion of limitations or challenges, despite stating that limitations are addressed, which raises questions about the depth of the analysis.
- **Minor Comments** The title contains grammatical errors ("diversed" should be "diverse" or "diversified"; "binding" is awkwardly placed). The abstract uses vague terms such as "exceptional performance" and "substantial advantages" without quantitative or qualitative specifics. The phrase "inject new vitality" is informal and not typical of rigorous scientific writing.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** None identified from the abstract alone, but the lack of specific content prevents any technical assessment.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**  
  - Originality: The abstract claims novelty, but this cannot be assessed without a literature comparison.  
  - Scientific importance: The topic is important, but the abstract does not demonstrate how the review advances beyond existing knowledge.  
  - Interdisciplinary readership: The scope spans multiple fields, which is promising, but the abstract does not show how the content will be accessible or relevant across disciplines.  
  - Technical soundness: Not assessable from the abstract; no methods, data, or critical analysis are presented.  
  - Readability for nonspecialists: The abstract is generally readable, but some jargon (e.g., SELEX, eTF-dFNA) is introduced without explanation, which may hinder nonspecialist comprehension.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The abstract outlines a promising review, but the absence of substantive content prevents a supportive recommendation. A full manuscript would be required to assess the quality and novelty of the work.
