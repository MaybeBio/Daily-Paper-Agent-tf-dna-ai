Hypoxia-inducible factor-2 (HIF-2) serves as the pivotal transcription factor in cellular responses to low oxygen levels, particularly concerning the regulation of erythropoietin (EPO) production. A docking-based virtual screening on crystal structures of HIF-2alpha inhibitors unexpectedly identified 3-phenyl-5-methyl-isoxazole-4-carboxamide derivative v19 as a hit of HIF-2alpha agonist. Further structural optimizations of compound v19 led to the discovery of a series of HIF-2alpha agonists with novel scaffolds. The most promising compounds 12g and 14d exhibited potent HIF-2alpha agonistic activities in vitro with EC(50) values of 2.29 muM and 1.78 muM, respectively. Molecular dynamics simulations have revealed their capacity to allosterically enhance HIF-2 dimerization, which shed light on their mechanism of action. Moreover, compound 14d demonstrated a favorable pharmacokinetic (PK) profile, boasting an impressive oral bioavailability value of 68.71 %. These findings strongly suggest that compound 14d is an auspicious lead compound for the treatment of renal anemia.
