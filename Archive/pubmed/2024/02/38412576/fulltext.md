## Introduction
  According to the latest report from the world health organization, breast cancer has ranked first in the incidence of malignant tumor [1]. Breast cancer can be generally classified into HER2 related subtype [2,3], Luminal A subtype [4,5], Luminal B subtype [6,7] and basal-like subtype [8,9]. The four subtypes [10] were defined based on the overexpression of HER2, ER, PR, and Ki67. It has great implications for personalized medicine to determine the subtypes, which exhibit distinct symptom characteristics in metastasis, recurrence, and sensitivity to different treatments.

  The immunohistochemical method serves as one of the most common tools in screening breast cancer at an early stage, due to its competitive advantages in aspects of high reliability, short waiting time and low cost. However, clinical immunohistochemical detection still relies on manual interpretation, which is severely restricted by individual subjective experiences as well as the finite eye resolution and human calculating speed.

  Taking advantage of the high speed and strong ability in complex data processing, artificial intelligence (AI) doctor has become the common development direction of engineering science and modern medicine, in aspects of image interpretation, comprehensive diagnosis, and prognosis analysis [11,12]. Chiagoziem developed a hybrid workflow by fusing the ensemble convolutional networks and the transformer encoder mechanism, which identified pneumonia from chest X-ray images with an accuracy up to 99.21 % [13]. David Capper [14] and his coworkers generated 2873 cases of genome-wide DNA methylation profiles representing 91 types of WHO-defined central nervous system tumors to construct and train a random forest algorithm based central nervous system classification, which achieved superior accuracy over participating pathologists after testing 1104 confirmed cases. Scott Mayer McKinney [15] curated up to 25856 cases of breast tumor from the UK and 3097 cases from the USA to construct a deep learning based artificial intelligence system for screening mammography, which outperformed all participating radiologists, leading to a reduction of 5.7 % and 1.2 % (USA and UK) in false positives and 9.4 % and 2.7 % in false negatives.

  As above, AI aided tumour diagnosis has been widely used in analyzing the detection results of genetic sequencing [16], computerized tomography [17], magnetic resonance imaging [18], and such detecting methods[19], which also exhibited great potential in realizing automatic interpretation of immunohistochemical images of breast tumor. The interpretation of pathological sections of breast cancer involves comprehensively considering a number of different features with unclear correlations. Compared with logistic regression, linear regression, decision trees, support vector machine and such commonly used feature extraction algorithms, convolutional neural network (CNN) [20,21] shows significant advantages in aspects of high accuracy, strong learning ability, and great performance in handling multi-features with unknown relationships, which is widely applied in various complex image recognition systems [22] due to its outstanding ability in image classification. Zizhao Zhang et al [23] presented an artificial intelligence powered pathology whole-slide diagnosis method using CNN and recurrent neural networks, which successfully detected 913 cases of haematoxylin and eosin stained bladder cancer samples with an excellent mean accuracy of 94.6 %, compared with 84.3 % for 17 independent pathologists. Therefore, CNN can be utilized as a powerful tool for automatically interpreting breast cancer immunohistochemical images with pluralistic tissue structures and complex background noises[24].

  Here, a holistic intelligent diagnosis system for detecting breast tumor was established based on CNN, to interpret immunohistochemical images and make suggestions the molecular subtyping. 104, 198, 198, and 60 cases of breast tumor with different HER2, ER, PR, and Ki67 overexpression levels were expanded to a ten-thousand-scale of training set and a thousand-scale of testing set. Detected by individual CNN based interpreting modules, the overexpression rates of HER2, ER, PR, and Ki67 were accurately graded with satisfactory sensitivity and specificity, which greatly overcame the limitations of conventional manual interpretation in efficiency, precision, and repeatability. On this basis, the intelligent diagnosis system was developed according to the classification standard of breast tumors, to provide suggestions on molecular subtyping by comprehensively analyzing the overexpression levels of HER2, ER, PR, and Ki67.


## Materials and methods

### Dataset
  104 cases of breast tumor with HER2 overexpressed, 198 cases with ER, PR and 60 cases of Ki67 overexpressed respectively collected from Nanjing medical university affiliated cancer hospital were immunohistochemical stained with DAB and taken photographs under a microscope (Fig. S1).

  The microscopic images of HER2 with a positive degree of “-” were 73 cases, “+” and “++” were 24 cases, and “+++” were 26 cases (Table S1). The negative samples were divided into 60 and 13 cases for the training set and testing set respectively. For positive samples, 20 cases of each positive degree were chosen randomly for the training set, and the rest were put into the testing set. The lowest resolution of the original pathological images was 768 by 960 pixels. Considering the inherent characteristics of breast tumor pathological images, the pathological images were segmented into subgraphs of 100 by 100 pixels so as not to lose efficient features. Therefore, the maximum expansion multiple of the training data for each positive degree was(1)em=10000/(7×9×n)

  And the maximum expansion multiple of the testing data of each positive degree was(2)em=1000/(7×9×n)where em was the maximum expansion multiple, and n was the number of original images. According to the calculation, the maximum expansion multiples of negative images were 2.6 and 1.2 for the training set and testing respectively, images of “+” and “++” were 7.9 and 3.9, and images of “+++” were 7.9 and 2.6. After rotated, the subgraphs on the edges couldn't fill up 100 by 100 pixels, which lead to a loss in quantity. And given the operability of subsequent rotation, the practical expansion multiples of negative samples were set to 3 and 2 for training set and testing respectively, images of “+” and “++” were 10 and 5, and images of “+++” were 10 and 4, to obtain enough efficient data.

  The rotating step was calculated by(3)s=360/epwhere s was the rotating step, and ep was the practical expansion multiple. Reverse mappings were introduced in the rotation [25], so each pixel of the rotated image could map to the original image, which ensured minimum distortion.

  After rotation, the images were cut to 100 by 100 pixels, and the subgraphs could not fill up 100 by 100 pixels or were not following the positive degree of the original images were eliminated. Breast tumor with ER, PR, and Ki67 overexpressed were processed with similar operating procedures. For ER, PR, and Ki67. Unevenly distributed data might result in unreasonable detection bias, so 10000 cases of training data and 1000 cases of testing data were kept for each positive degree (Fig. S2, Table S2).


### CNN based Interpreting module
  The CNN architecture consisted of four convolutional layers, four pooling layers, two fully connected layers, and one output layer, and employed TensorFlow to code.

  The original breast tumour sections were interpreted by 3 independent pathologists, and the interpreting results were averaged as ground truth.

  The immunohistochemical images of HER2, ER, PR, and Ki67 were trained and tested by individual CNN based interpreting modules, and the interpreting performance was evaluated by comparing the result of each case with the ground truth. ### Construction of the holistic diagnosis system for breast tumour
  The diagnosis system was designed with a front interaction layer, a service layer, and a data storage layer, which consisted of 8 modules, including a display module, data-entry module, process module, business logic service, transmission service, message service, log management service, data processing service, and system monitoring service.

  In the subtyping module, the interpreting results of HER2, ER, PR, and Ki67 were called and encoded in order into a low-dimensional feature representation. According to the classification standard in Table S3, the molecular subtype would be inferred based on the encodings through a logical judgment. The results were saved at the data storage layer to further optimize the modules.


## Results and discussions

### Construction of interpreting module for detecting immunohistochemical images
  The holistic intelligent breast tumour diagnosis system included four interpreting modules for each tumour maker and a subtyping module for encoding the interpreting results and making determinations on molecular subtype (Fig. 1).Fig. 1Method framework. The holistic intelligent breast tumour diagnosis system included two main modules. The immunohistochemical images of HER2, ER, PR, and Ki67 were input into individual interpreting modules, where the multi-features of immunostaining were automatically extracted to output the pathology interpretation results. In the subtyping module, the interpretation results of HER2, ER, PR, and Ki67 were encoded in a low-dimensional feature representation to divide into different molecular subtypes, referring to the clinical classification criteria for breast tumour.Fig 1

  104 cases of breast tumor sections with four different expression levels of HER2 were used to construct and optimize the interpreting module. The sections were immunohistochemically stained by 3, 3′-diaminobenzidin (DAB) and manually graded, where the intensity and distribution of brown immunostaining were positively related to the HER2 overexpression rate (Fig. S1). The microscopic images with different positive degrees were randomly divided into the training set and testing set as shown in Table S2. Considering the inherent characteristics of breast tumor pathological images, and then rotated and segmented into subgraphs of 100 by 100 pixels, which could basically preserve the efficient features (Fig. S2). An evenly distributed data of 10000 cases of training data and 1000 cases of testing data for each positive degree respectively was obtained, to guarantee a sufficient body of data volume based on controlling unreasonable detection bias (Fig. S3).

  Impurities appearing in pathological sections could cause diverse and complex background noises. The clinical immunohistochemical method was typically implemented by manual operation. Human intelligence could easily distinguish the tumor regions from different kinds of background noises, however, it was hard to give a concise and meanwhile complete description of the characteristics of background noises and the differences between noises and tumor regions. CNN was able to automatically extract feature information from pathological images and refine the feature association, which could realize accurate interpretation by learning large-scale training data instead of working by artificial rules. As a result, CNN was employed to develop interpreting modules for grading the overexpression level of HER2, ER, PR, and Ki67.

  The interpreting module for detecting HER2 were coded by TensorFlow and consisted of four convolutional layers, four pooling layers, two fully connected layers, and one output layer (Fig. 2a). The feature information was extracted at the convolutional layers, and the dimensionality of the data was reduced to restrain over-fitting (Fig. 2b). The four pooling layers all adopted max-pooling and corresponded to the four convolutional layers respectively (Fig. 2c). The features extracted were mapped into sample space, and the convolutionally output two-dimensional images were translated into one-dimensional vectors. ### Interpretation for immunohistochemical images of HER2
  The breast tumor sections included 30 cases of “-”, 24 of “+” and “++”, and 26 of “+++” (Table S1), which were used to construct and optimize the interpreting module. The microscopic images with different positive degrees were randomly divided into the training set and testing set as shown in Table S2.

  According to the statistics results, this interpreting module successfully realized an effective and reliable locational, quantitative, and qualitative detection for HER2 overexpressed breast tumor sections. Referencing the diagnosis from 3 independent pathologists, the interpreting module exhibited a satisfactory accuracy of 96.3 %, with a total overhigh and overlow rate of 1.9 % and 1.8 % respectively (Fig. 2a,b, Table S4), and the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) was up to 0.99 (Fig. 2c).

  The accuracy of conventional manual-interpretation varies considerably by different operators. As a comparison, the clinical manual-interpreted IHC was crudely assessed with the data collected from several influential hospitals and medical colleges by Edith A.Perez, where HER2 overexpressed breast tumor were detected by conventional immunohistochemical method, fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) and reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction, respectively [28]. Since FISH is considered the gold standard for detecting HER2, we took the results of FISH as a reference to evaluate the accuracy of the clinical manual-interpreted immunohistochemical method and statistically obtained a sensitivity of 93.6 %, a specificity of 67.5 %, and a Youden index of 0.61. Analyzed the results in Table S5, the HER2-overexpressed breast tumour was accurately graded by the CNN based interpreting module with a sensitivity of 99.8 %, a specificity of 96.9 %, a misdiagnosis rate of 3.1 %, a missed diagnosis rate of 0.2 %, a positive predictive value of 99.0 %, a negative predictive value of 99.4 %, and a Youden index of 0.97, which exhibited higher reliability over the conventional manual interpretation (Fig. 2g, Table 1).Table 1Accuracy evaluation of the CNN based interpretation method for detecting immunohistochemical images of breast tumor.Table 1HER2ERPRKi67AverageSensitivity99.8 %94.9 %98.0 %97.7 %97.6 %Specificity96.9 %95.1 %96.3 %96.1 %96.1 %Misdiagnosis Rate3.1 %4.9 %3.7 %3.9 %3.9 %Missed Diagnosis Rate0.2 %5.1 %2.1 %2.3 %2.43 %Positive Predictive Value99.0 %97.5 %98.1 %96.2 %97.7 %Negative Predictive Value99.4 %90.3 %95.9 %97.7 %95.8 %Youden Index0.970.900.940.940.94

  Besides that, the CNN based interpreting module was compared with the other two classic classifiers. The pixel level features (PLF) based module (Fig. S4) extracted the color spatial features, local binary pattern histogram Fourier features, run-length texture features, and gray-level co-occurrence matrix features, and utilizing support vector machines (SVM) to classify breast tumour immunohistochemical images with different HER2 positive grades. The object level features (OLF) based module (Fig. S5) extracted the number, area, circumference, average radius, and fractal dimension of the target object, features of the ellipse with the same second central moment as the target object, and convex polygon within the interior of the object, and also realized the interpretation by SVM classifier. The agreement between ground truth and the grading results obtained by PLF and OLF was 77.6 % and 76.7 %, respectively. Combining the two algorithms could improve the agreement to 82.6 %, which was still lower than the CNN based interpreting module (Fig. 4a).


### Interpretation for immunohistochemical images of ER, PR, and Ki67
  Besides HER2, the overexpression rates of ER, PR, and Ki67 were also commonly detected through the immunohistochemical method. For the sake of making an effective diagnosis on molecular subtypes, the immunohistochemical images of four tumour markers were graded as Fig. 3. HER2 was graded into 0, 1+, 2+, and 3+, according to the intensity and distribution of brown immunostaining just as the standard clinical criteria. For ER and PR, the molecular subtypes were only determined by the number of positive cells. Therefore, we defined ER and PR into three positive grades, where the positive number below 1 % was defined as negative (Neg), the positive number positive ratio of 10 % and 20 % were used as the thresholds to define low (Low) and high (High) overexpression for ER and PR respectively. Ki67 was only defined as low positive (immunostaining < 14 %) and high positive (immunostaining > 14 %).Fig. 3Grading criteria of interpreting module for HER2, ER, PR, and Ki67: The immunohistochemical images of HER2 were graded into 0 (N), 1+ (L), 2+ (M), and 3+ (H), according to the standard clinical criteria. The immunohistochemical images of ER were graded into negative (N, positive number < 1 %), low overexpression (L, 1 % < positive number < 10 %), and high overexpression (H, positive number > 10 %). The immunohistochemical images of PR were graded into negative (N, positive number < 1 %), low overexpression (L, 1 % < positive number < 20 %), and high overexpression (H, positive number > 20 %). The immunohistochemical images of Ki67 were defined into low positive (L, immunostaining < 14 %) and high positive (H, immunostaining > 14 %). .Fig 3

  The individual interpreting modules for ER, PR, and Ki67 were developed with a similar design as HER2. Utilizing similar data extending methods, 10000 cases of training images and 1000 cases of testing data for each positive grade were prepared for each tumour marker.

  The CNN based interpreting module was demonstrated to be applicable for ER, PR, and Ki67, with an accuracy of 92.0 %, 95.6 %, and 96.9 %, and the total under and over-valued rates were all kept within 5.0 % (Fig. 4b, Table S6, S7, S8). ### Automatic diagnosis for the molecular subtype
  In terms of the positive degrees of HER2, ER, PR, and Ki67, breast cancer could be generally divided into four subtypes, HER2 related subtype, Luminal A subtype, Luminal B subtype, and basal-like subtype. After obtaining the grading results of four tumour markers, a classification analysis service was added to determine the molecular subtypes according to the clinical classification standard of breast cancer (Fig. 5a, Table S3).Fig. 5Holistic intelligent breast tumour diagnosis system for subtyping breast tumour: (a) The molecular subtyping standard of breast tumour. (b) The architecture of subtyping module. (c) Framework of subtyping module. (d) The holistic intelligent breast tumour diagnosis software for grading HER2, ER, PR, and Ki67 of breast tumour images, and providing suggestions on molecular subtypes .Fig 5

  The architecture of the subtyping module was divided into three layers, the front interaction layer, the service layer, and the data storage layer (Fig. 5b). The front interaction layer controlled the input of immunohistochemical images and the whole diagnosis process. At the service layer, the interpreting modules were called to provide grading results of the corresponding tumour markers. The grading results were input as a defined letter in the order of HER2, ER, PR, and Ki67 (Fig. 3), and encoded in a low-dimensional feature representation. After that, the subtype would be inferred according to the overexpression levels of four biomarkers through a logical judgment (Fig. 5c,d) and the classification results were saved at the data storage layer to further optimize the modules.

  This holistic intelligent breast tumour diagnosis system could automatically read and analyze immunohistochemical results of breast cancer instead of manual operation and successfully provided accurate and reliable grading results as well as suggestions on molecular subtyping. This system would help in evaluating prognosis and making choices on drugs and therapeutic plans, which effectively lightened the workload and eased the diagnosis difficulty for doctors.


## Conclusions
  In summary, a convolutional neural network based holistic intelligent breast tumour diagnosis system for automatically interpreting immunohistochemical images and making suggestions on molecular subtypes has been established. Hundred-scale cases of breast tumor sections with different overexpression levels of HER2, ER, PR, and Ki67 were expanded to ten-thousand-scale of training sets and thousand-scale of testing sets. The positive grades for four tumour markers were accurately interpreted with an average sensitivity of 97.6 % and an average specificity of 96.1 %, among those, the sensitivity and specificity for interpreting HER2 were up to 99.8 % and 96.9 %. On this basis, the interpreting results were encoded in a low-dimensional feature representation and classified into four molecular subtypes. This holistic intelligent diagnosis system could greatly overcome the limitations of conventional manual interpretation in efficiency and stability. Since immunohistochemical methods serve as a common detection tool, there is reason to expect to realize artificial intelligent aided diagnosis for more other cancers.
