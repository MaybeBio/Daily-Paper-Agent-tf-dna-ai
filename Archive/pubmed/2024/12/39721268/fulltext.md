# Paeoniflorin inhibits APEC-induced inflammation in HD11 cells through the NF-κB signaling pathway by activating CB2R


## Abstract
  Avian pathogenic Escherichia coli (APEC) is a bacterial pathogen that threatens poultry reproduction by inciting systemic inflammation and leading to chicken colibacillosis. The endocannabinoid system (ECS) is an immunomodulator system that regulates inflammatory responses. In this study, we aimed to investigate the anti-inflammatory effect of paeoniflorin on APEC-infected HD11 cells and its underlying mechanism. The results showed that paeoniflorin significantly reduced the expression levels of pro-inflammatory factors (IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α), M1-type macrophage-associated markers (IL-12, iNOS, CD86), and chemokines (CXCL8, CXCL12, CCL1, CCL5, CCL17). Additionally, paeoniflorin significantly reduced the expression of MAGL and restored that of DAGL and CB2R, thereby activating the ECS. Furthermore, we found that paeoniflorin and CB2R exhibited stable conformations through molecular docking and molecular dynamics simulations. The addition of the CB2R inhibitor AM630 notably diminished paeoniflorin's inhibitory effects on the phosphorylation levels of proteins in the NF-κB signaling pathway and on inflammatory responses. These results indicate that the anti-inflammatory effects of paeoniflorin on APEC-induced HD11 cells may be mediated by the inhibition of the NF-κB signaling pathway through the activation of CB2R. This work has the potential to provide a new agent for the control and prevention of chicken colibacillosis, as well as contribute to modern research in traditional Chinese medicine.


## Introduction
  Avian pathogenic Escherichia coli (APEC) causes avian colibacillosis, a syndrome of severe respiratory and systemic diseases, which seriously threatens the development of poultry breeding. The common forms of localized infection caused by APEC as a primary pathogen include omphalitis, reproductive tract infections, and yolk sac infections. However, the most studied form is systemic colibacillosis with respiratory origin, leading to colisepticemia (Guabiraba and Schouler, 2015). APEC first colonizes the trachea and air sacs, inducing inflammatory responses, and then crosses into bloodstream, causing systemic infection. Therefore, inhibiting inflammation in the respiratory tract and then preventing APEC from invading the bloodstream may be an effective way to prevent and control colibacillosis. Resident cells, such as epithelial cells and macrophages in lungs and air sacs, are the first line to defense against the APEC infection.

  The endocannabinoid system (ECS) is a neuroimmune and immunomodulatory system that consists of endogenous cannabinoids such as 2-arachidonoylglycerol and anandamide, their cognate receptors (CB1R and CB2R), and the enzymes involved in their synthesis (such as NAPE-PLD and DAGL) and inactivation (such as FAAH and MAGL). CB2R is expressed by immune cells and their wide peripheral distribution among these cells makes them targets for modulating inflammatory processes (Osafo et al., 2021). The study found that CB2R is only expressed in very small amounts in the brains of healthy individuals, but during neuroinflammation, CB2R is upregulated in the central nervous system (Young and Denovan-Wright, 2022). In LPS-induced acute lung injury, 2-AG is increased by inhibiting the metabolic enzyme MAGL, and cytokine and chemokine levels are reduced, thereby improving the inflammatory response and alleviating lung injury (Costola-de-Souza et al., 2013). Therefore, the activation of CB2R can improve the inflammatory response and reduce the inflammatory damage. Previous studies have reported that the CB2R agonist GP1a markedly reduces proinflammatory M1 macrophage polarization, increases anti-inflammatory M2 polarization, reduces edema development, and improves neurobehavioral outcomes during traumatic brain injury in mice. In contrast, the CB2R antagonist AM630 can worsen its outcomes (Braun et al., 2018). According to the above findings, CB2R may also be an important target for regulating inflammation induced by APEC.

  Paeoniae Radix Alba, a widely used traditional Chinese medicine, is known for its efficacy in treating lung diseases and diarrhea. Paeoniflorin is a monoterpenoid glycoside compound, which has anti-inflammatory (Huang et al., 2021; Zhang and Wei, 2020) and immune-regulatory properties (Zhou et al., 2020), which is one of the main pharmacodynamic components and a key quality evaluation index of Paeoniae Radix Alba. This study aims to explore whether paeoniflorin exerts its anti-inflammatory effects by activating CB2R in macrophages.


## Materials and methods

### Reagents
  Paeoniflorin (CAS: 23180-57-6, purity>99%) was purchased from Chengdu Must Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Chengdu, China). Luria-Bertani (LB) medium was purchased from Qingdao Hopebio Biological Co., Ltd. (Qingdao, China). The CCK8 and BCA kit were sourced from Thermo Fisher, Inc. (USA). Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) kit was obtained from Wuhan Saipei Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Wuhan, China). FastStart™ Universal SYBR® Green (ROX) was bought from Roche, Inc. (USA). Trizol was acquired from Accurate Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Hunan, China). The reverse transcription kit was purchased from Beijing TransGen Biotech Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). RIPA lysate from Beyotime (Shanghai, China). PVDF membranes and gentamicin sulfate salt from Sigma, Inc. (USA). P65, p-IκB, GAPDH and Anti-rabbit secondary antibodies were obtained from Beijing Bioss Biotechnology Co., Ltd (Beijing, China). p-P65 and IκB were purchased from Affinity Biosciences Co., Ltd (USA)


### Cultivation and growth condition of APEC
  A single colony of APEC (CVCC1418, from the Chinese Veterinary Culture Collection Center (CVCC)) was placed in a sterile conical flask containing LB medium and inoculated into a shaking table until it reached logarithmic growth, centrifuged, and the bacterial suspension was then adjusted to the required concentration with RPMI 1640 medium as required by the experiment. Subsequently, APEC was co-cultured with paeoniflorin at concentrations ranging from 0 to 400 μg/mL and incubated on a shaker for 0 to 14 hours, and the absorbance of each group was measured at OD600 every 2 hours, and the growth curve was drawn.


### Culture and treatment of HD11 cells and detection of the cell viability
  HD11 cells were cultured in RPMI 1640 medium containing 10% FBS in a cell incubator at 37°C and 5% CO2. When the cells reached 80%-90% confluence, they were digested with pancreatic enzymes and the cell suspension was collected for subsequent experiments. A 96-well plate was seeded with 105 cells per well in a volume of 100 μLper well and incubated for 2 hours. After cell adhesion, the medium was discarded and 100 μL of maintenance medium containing the drug (12.5-200 μg/mL of paeoniflorin) was added. After 24 hours, 10 μL of CCK8 solution was added to each well, and the plate was incubated at 37°C for an additional hour. Cell viability was determined by measuring OD450. All tests were repeated three times.


### HD11 phagocytosis and detection of LDH
  HD11 cells were seeded in a 6-well plate at a concentration of 1×106 cells/well. After cell adhesion, paeoniflorin at concentrations of 25-100 μg/mL was added and cultured in an incubator of 37°C and 5% CO2 for 24 hours, inoculated with APEC at an MOI of 51, cultured for 2 hours at 41°C, and washed with PBS. Gentamicin sulfate was added with 200 μg/mL each well, cultured at 41°C for 1 hour, washed with PBS, and 1 mL of 1% Triton X-100 was added to lyse cells for plate counting. The phagocytic index of APEC by HD11 cells in each group was calculated. All experiments were carried out in triplicate. HD11 cells were attached to the 6-well plate at a concentration of 1×106 cells/well, and the medium was discarded after the cells had fully adhered to the wall. The paeoniflorin group with a concentration of 100 μg/mL served as the drug control group and cultured for 24 hours. The APEC+Paeoniflorin group was inoculated with APEC at an MOI of 51 and incubated at 41°C for 4 hours. The supernatant of the medium was collected and the precipitation was removed by centrifugation. Subsequently, the procedures outlined in the LDH assay kit were followed to perform the assay.


### AM630 inhibition
  HD11 cells were seeded into 6-well plate with 1×106 cells/well. Once the cells had adhered, the medium was aspirated and replaced. The AM630 group was treated with a maintenance medium containing 10 μM of AM630, the HD11 group was added with an equal volume of maintenance medium, and after incubating for 4 hours, the paeoniflorin group was treated with 100 μg/mL of paeoniflorin and cultured in a 5% CO2 incubator at 37°C for 24 hours. Except for the control group, all groups were challenged with APEC at an MOI of 51. Subsequent experimental procedures were conducted after incubating the cells at 41°C for 4 hours.


### RT-qPCR
  The RNA of the cells was extracted and reverse-transcribed into cDNA for RT-qPCR. The reaction conditions of qRT-PCR were as follows: initial denaturation at 95°C for 10 min; 95°C for 15 s, and annealing extension at 60°C for 60 s, and 40 cycles. Subsequently, RT-qPCR was used to detect the expressions of ECS-associated genes (CB2R, MAGL and DAGL), associated markers of M1 polarization (IL-12, iNOS and CD86), inflammatory factors (IL-1β, IL-6 and TNF-α), and chemokines (CXCL8, CXCL12, CCL1, CCL5 and CCL17) in lung tissue samples. ### Molecular docking
  To utilize the PubChem database (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) and the protein databases (https://www.rcsb.org/), the 3D structures of CB2R (PDB ID: 6KPC, 3.20 Å resolution), (PDB ID: 8GUR, 2.84 Å resolution) combined with paeoniflorin was obtained. Using CB2R as receptor and paeoniflorin as ligand, AutoDockTools was used for hydrogenation and other pretreatment, AutoDockVina was used for semi-flexible molecular docking to find the optimal binding site, and affinity energy parameters were used as criteria to evaluate their stability. Finally, PyMOL software was used for visual analysis.


### Molecular dynamics simulation
  The molecular dynamics simulation of paeoniflorin and CB2R was carried out with GROMACS software, and procedures such as adding a box and minimizing energy were carried out. The time step was 2 fs. The molecular dynamics (MD) simulation was obtained every 100 ps, and the kinetic simulation was completed after a total of 16,000 ps. The stability of the MD simulation was evaluated by RMSF (Å), Energy (kcal/mol), and RMSD (nm).


### Western blotting
  After the medium was discarded, the wells were cleaned three times with pre-cooled PBS. 50 μL of RIPA lysate was added into each well and was cracked on ice for 30 min, and then the cell lysate was transferred to a centrifuge tube, centrifuged at 4°C at 12 000 r/min for 15 min, and the supernatant was collected. The protein was quantified using a BCA kit. The protein sample were adjusted the concentration of to the same level, then add 5×Loading buffer at 100°C to denature the protein. The total protein was separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membrane. The PVDF membrane was blocked in the sealing solution for 2 hours, incubated with specific primary antibodies at 4°C overnight. The following primary antibodies (1:1000) were used: P65 (bs-0645R), p-P65 (Thr254), IκB (AF5002), p-IκB (bs-2513R) and GAPDH (bsm-33033M). Subsequently, the membrane was washed with TBST and incubated with anti-rabbit secondary antibody (bs-0295G, 1:2000). Protein bands were visualized by chemiluminescence, and protein density was analyzed by ImageJ software and normalized to GAPDH.


### Statistical analysis
  The data are expressed as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). Data among all treatments were analyzed by One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey's multiple comparisons test using GraphPad Prism software version 8.1 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA). P<0.05 or P<0.01 indicates significant differences.

The cytotoxicity of paeoniflorin was detected by CCK8 assay. Compared with the control group, the cell viability was significantly decreased (P<0.01) at a paeoniflorin concentration of 200 μg/mL. In contrast, paeoniflorin within 100 μg/mL had no obvious cytotoxicity to HD11. ## Discussion
  Macrophages, serving as the first line of immune response, recognize pathogens and defend against invading microorganisms (Puertollano et al., 2011). As observed in the APEC infection model, APEC infection causes a rapid increase in macrophage numbers (Ariaans et al., 2008). The HD11 chicken macrophage cell line, which is a well-established cell line with many similarities to normal chicken macrophages (Beug et al., 1979), is widely used as a model for infection and immunobiological studies in vitro (Slawinska et al., 2016). Paeoniflorin, a water-soluble monoterpene glycoside, serves as the active ingredient of Paeoniae Radix Alba. Literature has shown that paeoniflorin has diverse pharmacological activities including anti-inflammatory, antioxidant (Chen et al., 2011), antithrombotic, anticonvulsive (Hino et al., 2012), analgesic (Zhang et al., 2009), cardioprotective (Chen et al., 2018), neuroprotective (Mao et al., 2012), antitumoral (Lu et al., 2014), and immune-regulatory effects (Zhou et al., 2020). Given the paeoniflorin possesses anti-inflammatory, antioxidant and immunomodulatory effects, this study explored the anti-inflammatory effect of paeoniflorin on APEC-induced HD11 cells. We found that paeoniflorin (25-100 μg/mL) significantly increased the phagocytic capacity of HD11, reduced LDH release, and decreased the expression of inflammatory cytokines. These results suggested that paeoniflorin inhibited the inflammation and cell damage induced by APEC.

  Macrophages are highly plastic and are divided into two distinct subpopulations: M1-type and M2-type macrophages (Li et al., 2009). M1-type macrophages exhibit elevated pro-inflammatory cytokine expression and enhanced antigen presentation, and play an important role in promoting inflammation, resisting microbial infection and fighting tumors (Sun et al., 2022). M1-type macrophages release large amounts of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α, IL-12 and IL-23 (Verreck et al., 2004). Our findings indicated that APEC induction led to an increase in markers associated with M1-type polarization (IL-12, iNOS, CD86) in HD11 cells, whereas paeoniflorin significantly down-regulated M1-type polarization after APEC infection. After APEC infection, the lung tissues (bronchi and bronchioles) are infiltrated mainly by granulocyte and monocyte (Dwars et al., 2009). Chemokines are a large superfamily of molecules that coordinates the migration of white blood cells to recruit different immune cells to the site of infection and determine the course of many inflammatory and immune responses (Sallusto and Lanzavecchia, 2000). The results demonstrated that paeoniflorin treatment reduced the expression of chemokines (CXCL8, CXCL12, CCL1, CCL5, CCL17).

  The ECS has emerged as an effective anti-inflammatory mechanism, which controls the intensity of inflammatory response and prevents the occurrence of pathological inflammation (Chiurchiù, 2016; Chiurchiù et al., 2015). The physiological role of 2-AG in inflammatory and/or immune responses is mediated through its interaction with the cannabinoid receptor CB2R (Carayon et al., 1998; Munro et al., 1993). A decrease in CB2R activation and M1 macrophage infiltration leads to significant downregulation of gene and protein levels of M1-related markers and cytokines, including IL-6, IL-12, CD86, and iNOS (Du et al., 2018). CB2R activation elicits anti-inflammatory effects by inhibiting leukocyte recruitment, reducing the synthesis and release of chemokines, reactive oxygen species and pro-inflammatory cytokines (Stasiulewicz et al., 2020). Cannabinoids can activate CB2R in AtT20 cells, and the CB2R antagonist AM630 can modulate CB2R activation (Udoh et al., 2019). The agonist AM1241 activates CB2R, thereby inhibiting related inflammatory cytokines such as IL-6 and TNF-α and improving the outcome of BLM-induced pulmonary fibrosis, while AM630 treatment has the opposite effect (Parlar et al., 2021). In a mouse model of skeletal muscle ischemia reperfusion (IR) injury, M1-type macrophage infiltration and related marker protein expression were enhanced in CB2R-knockout (KO) mice (Jiang et al., 2020). In addition, increased levels of MMP9 and chemokines CCL2, CCL4, and CXCL10 in CB2R−/− mice significantly enhanced recruitment of neutrophils and monocytes (Kapellos et al., 2019). Our results also showed that paeoniflorin treatment increased the expression of CB2R, as well as affected the metabolic synthetase of endocannabinoids. Consequently, we hypothesize that paeoniflorin may activate CB2R.

  To further verify our findings, we initially employed molecular docking, which revealed that the binding energies of paeoniflorin and CB2R (6KPC and 8GUR) were 7.902 kcal/mol and 8.903 kcal/mol, respectively, with hydrogen bond distances between donor and acceptor atoms being less than 3Å. Subsequently, we confirmed the molecular docking results through molecular dynamics simulations, which indicated that paeoniflorin and CB2R had a stable binding conformation. Next, we treated HD11 cells with the CB2R inhibitor AM630 to investigate whether paeoniflorin activates CB2R in macrophages. Treatment with AM630 significantly attenuated the activation of CB2R by paeoniflorin. Activation of NF-κB can exacerbate early immune and inflammatory responses by releasing a variety of inflammatory mediators (Kannian et al., 2020). Kupffer cells transfected with CB2R siRNA and stimulated with LPS not only exhibited overactivation of inflammatory cytokines but also activated the NF-κB signaling pathway (Louvet et al., 2011). In this experiment, we also found that the phosphorylation level of proteins associated with the NF-κB signaling pathway was higher following CB2R inhibition compared to the paeoniflorin-treated group. After AM630 treatment, the inhibitory effects of paeoniflorin on the expression of APEC-induced inflammatory factors, M1-type polarization related markers and chemokines were diminished. These findings further demonstrate that paeoniflorin exerts anti-inflammatory effects by activating CB2R in macrophages, thereby inhibiting the NF-κB signaling pathway in vitro. Although there are significant differences between the in vitro and in vivo environments for chickens, we also found that paeoniflorin (25, 50, and 100 mg/kg) increased the survival rate of APEC-infected chickens and inhibited acute inflammation by affecting the ECS and suppressing the NF-κB signaling pathways (Shen et al., 2024). That provides the basis for the clinical use of paeoniflorin. However, low bioavailability is a common problem for many effective natural compounds and paeoniflorin is no exception (Yu et al., 2019). How does low-profit paeoniflorin play an anti-inflammatory role? According to the previous studies, degradation by gut microbiota enzymes may be one of the main reasons for the low bioavailability of paeoniflorin (Yu et al., 2019), and paeoniflorin is capable of modulating the composition of gut microbiota or gut microbial production to alleviate colitis (Fan et al., 2020), peripheral neuropathy (Xu et al., 2024) and so on. Therefore, we speculate that although paeoniflorin has a low bioavailability in the body, it may still exert a variety of pharmacological effects through mechanisms such as regulating the composition of the intestinal flora and its metabolic products.


## Conclusion
  In conclusion, paeoniflorin inhibits APEC-induced inflammation in HD11 cells by activating CB2R and consequently suppressing the NF-κB signaling pathway. This study suggests that paeoniflorin may serve as a novel agent for the inflammation regulation targeting ECS especially CB2R.
