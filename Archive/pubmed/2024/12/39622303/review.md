### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence quality
- **Claim pointer** "Fifty-one compounds showed greater disruption of EGR-1 DNA binding than that induced by AB1711" and "compound 36 ... showed the best activity"
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract provides no numerical data, assay conditions, replicates, or statistical analysis to support the claim that 51 compounds outperformed AB1711 and that compound 36 was the best. Without quantitative readouts (e.g., IC50 values, percent inhibition, or binding affinity constants), the ranking of compounds is unverifiable.
- **Why it matters** The central claim of the manuscript is the identification of a superior compound. If the activity data are not presented with appropriate metrics and error analysis, the conclusion cannot be evaluated or reproduced.
- **Resolution test** Provide dose-response curves, IC50 or EC50 values with confidence intervals, and statistical comparisons (e.g., p-values) for all 70 compounds against the reference AB1711.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Technical soundness
- **Claim pointer** "Pharmacophores were derived based on comparative molecular field and comparative molecular similarity index analyses" and "Pharmacophores derived from quantitative structure-activity relationships matched well with the results obtained from in silico docking"
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract states that CoMFA and CoMSIA analyses were performed and that the resulting pharmacophores matched docking results, but no model statistics (e.g., q2, r2, standard error), validation procedures, or docking scores are provided. The claim of a "good match" is subjective without quantitative comparison.
- **Why it matters** Computational models are only meaningful if their predictive power and statistical robustness are demonstrated. Without these details, the pharmacophore and docking results are anecdotal and cannot support the mechanistic interpretation.
- **Resolution test** Report CoMFA/CoMSIA model statistics (cross-validated q2, non-cross-validated r2, F-values), and provide docking scores or binding energies for compound 36 and key comparators, with a clear description of the docking protocol and scoring function.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence quality
- **Claim pointer** "To determine the role of compound 36 in cells, further experiments, including electrophoretic mobility shift and reverse-transcription polymerase chain reaction assays, were carried out. These findings demonstrated that compound 36 is a good disruptor of EGR-1-DNA binding."
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract mentions EMSA and RT-PCR assays but provides no results, no quantitative outcomes, and no description of what was measured (e.g., EGR-1 target gene expression levels, binding shift percentages). The conclusion that compound 36 is a "good disruptor" in cells is unsupported.
- **Why it matters** The cellular assays are intended to bridge the biochemical and computational findings to a functional context. Without data, the claim of cellular activity is speculative, and the relevance of the compound as a potential therapeutic lead is not established.
- **Resolution test** Present representative EMSA images with quantification, and RT-PCR data showing changes in EGR-1 target gene expression with appropriate controls (e.g., housekeeping genes, vehicle treatment) and statistical analysis.
