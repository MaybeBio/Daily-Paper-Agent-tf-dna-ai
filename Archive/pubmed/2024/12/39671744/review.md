## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a broad set of claims spanning antioxidant activity, metal binding, DNA interaction, and protein transport for acacetin and its Cu(II) complex. The topic is of interest to the bioinorganic and flavonoid research communities. However, the abstract alone provides insufficient quantitative detail to evaluate the strength of the evidence. Several claims are qualitative and lack supporting numbers, error estimates, or methodological context. The connection between the various assays and the stated conclusions is not fully transparent from the abstract. The work appears potentially valuable, but the case is not established from the supplied material.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in bioinorganic chemistry, particularly those studying metal-flavonoid interactions; investigators interested in the role of redox-active metal ions in modulating antioxidant and DNA-binding properties of natural polyphenols; and scientists working on drug delivery or transport mechanisms involving serum albumin. The combination of spectroscopic, computational, and biochemical assays may appeal to a multidisciplinary audience.
- **Major strengths** The study integrates multiple complementary techniques (UV-Vis, EPR, DFT, gel electrophoresis, molecular docking, molecular dynamics, fluorescence spectroscopy) to address a coherent question about the influence of Cu(II) on acacetin's biological properties. The use of both experimental and computational approaches is commendable. The abstract reports a clear and potentially important finding that Cu(II) coordination reduces the radical-scavenging ability of AC while enhancing DNA binding, which could have implications for understanding the dual pro-oxidant and antioxidant behavior of flavonoids.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
  - **Claim pointer** "AC appears as a potent scavenger against the model ABTS radical cation by itself, but this ability is significantly limited upon Cu(II) coordination."
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided
  - **Concern** The abstract states that AC is a potent ABTS scavenger and that this ability is significantly limited upon Cu(II) coordination, but no quantitative data are provided, such as IC50 values, rate constants, or percentage inhibition values. The term "significantly" implies statistical testing, yet no error bars, replicates, or p-values are mentioned.
  - **Why it matters** Without quantitative measures and statistical support, the reader cannot assess the magnitude of the effect or whether the difference between free AC and Cu(II)-AC is meaningful. This is a central claim of the paper, and its strength directly affects the overall conclusions about how Cu(II) modulates AC's antioxidant activity.
  - **Resolution test** Provide numerical values for ABTS scavenging activity (e.g., percentage inhibition or IC50) for AC and Cu(II)-AC, with standard deviations and statistical comparison (e.g., t-test or ANOVA). If the difference is not statistically significant, revise the claim accordingly.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
  - **Claim pointer** "Absorption titrations and gel electrophoresis revealed effective binding to calf thymus (CT)-DNA with a stronger interaction for the Cu(II)-AC complex."
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided
  - **Concern** The abstract claims stronger DNA binding for the Cu(II)-AC complex compared to AC alone, but no binding constants, stoichiometry, or quantitative comparison are provided.
