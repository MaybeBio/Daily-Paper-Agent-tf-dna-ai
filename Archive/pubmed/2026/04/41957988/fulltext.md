Caffeic acid phenethyl ester (CAPE), a dietary phenolic compound derived from propolis, exhibits cardiovascular and anti-inflammatory effects; however, its mechanisms in endothelial cells remain unclear. In this study, we demonstrated that CAPE induces eNOS phosphorylation and NO production in TNF-alpha-stimulated endothelial cells. Mechanistically, CAPE-induced NO production is mediated by Akt, PKA, and ERK signaling pathways. In silico docking and pharmacological inhibition confirmed that CAPE directly engages the beta2-adrenergic receptor (beta2AR) to activate the Galphas/cAMP/Epac axis. Moreover, CAPE suppresses NF-kappaB activation and inflammatory cytokine expression through NO-dependent mechanisms. Ex vivo studies using aortic rings further validated that CAPE stimulates eNOS activation and NO production via beta2AR/Galphas signaling. Collectively, these findings indicate that CAPE enhances endothelial function and exerts anti-inflammatory effects through the beta2AR-mediated signaling, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic candidate for endothelial dysfunction and inflammation-related cardiovascular diseases.
