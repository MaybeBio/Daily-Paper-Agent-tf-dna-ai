# Identification of common transcriptional responses to salinity of two halophytes: Bruguiera gymnorrhiza and Populus euphratica


## Abstract
  Salinity stress is a major environmental factor that limits plant growth and development. This study identified common differentially expressed genes (DEGs) of two halophyte species, Populus euphratica and Bruguiera gymnorrhiza, using a meta-analysis of retrieved microarray datasets. A total of 27 commonly up-regulated DEGs were identified, including NAC1, GBSS1, NIR1, APR1, and ChlAKR, with the most positive fold changes. Additionally, 37 commonly down-regulated DEGs, such as GASA1, GLT1, MSS1, and APS1 showed the most negative fold changes. Gene Ontology analysis indicated that these DEGs are mainly involved in responses to stimuli, chemicals, or metals. Several transcription factors responsive to salinity stress were identified, including up-regulated families such as NAC, bZIP, ERF, MYB, and LBD, while NAC was the only down-regulated family. Gene network analysis highlighted five hub genes: NIR1, APR1, APR3, HY5, and MS2, of which only MS2 was down-regulated. Among microRNA families, miR5658 (up-regulated) and miR169 (down-regulated) were the most frequent. Promoter analysis revealed cis-acting elements binding to 11 conserved motifs, including AP2/EREBP and C2H2 classes, potentially regulating key responses to abiotic stress in halophytes. These findings may provide insights that could inform strategies for genetic engineering of crops. The relative expression of the NIR1 gene in P. euphratica leaves was significantly induced by NaCl, with higher expression under 500 mM peaking at 48 h (10-fold). Expression decreased at 72 h but remained above control levels, showing a time- and concentration-dependent response.


## Introduction
  Plants continuously face to biotic and abiotic stresses during their lifespan. Salinity stress is one of the main adverse environmental factors reducing growth and productivity1. High salinity leads to ionic imbalance, high osmotic stress, and secondary stress2. In addition, high levels of salinity can cause changes in plant performance, including morphological, physiological, biochemical, and molecular functions. Plants under saline conditions also produce secondary metabolites that are involved in defending against environmental stresses. For example, halophytes are salt-resistant plants that can complete their life cycles in regions with high salt concentrations3.

  In the last decade, the molecular responses to various environmental stresses have attracted the attention of many scientists who work in the field of plant biotechnology. They have showed that some regular and complex systems are involved in response to salinity stress including the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) cascade, the ROS signaling pathway, the abscisic acid (ABA) signaling pathway, and Ca2+-mediated signaling pathway4. Halophytes can be used as models to understand molecular responses of plant to salinity2. In addition, novel genes related to salt tolerance identified from halophytes can be used in genetic manipulation of main crop genomes to increase crop production and enhance global food security. Recently, many studies on salt-responsive genes in plants have achieved good results, most of which are based on microarray or RNA-seq data. Meta-analysis is a powerful strategy that is designed to overcome the limitations in the gene expression profiling studies5. These approaches strive to find stronger and more reliable predictions by collecting and analyzing different data that have a common goal than those that are based on limited information. For example, Wang et al.. (2018) reported a total of 5273 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified between sensitive and resistant genotypes of rice at the seedling stage to salinity6. Meta-analysis identified 829 DEGs responsive to salinity stress in Arabidopsis thaliana7. Soltani et al. (2023) reported 308 hormonal and 1,232 biotic DEGs in barley8. A meta analysis study revealed 2,336 DEGs involved in different biotic and abiotic stresses in tomato9. In other study, it was found that the number of DEGs in roots was more than in leaves under both salinity and drought stresses in Arabidopsis10. The results of meta-analysis of transcriptome and metabolome showed that hub genes including CDS, PAL, CIN, RAS, TAT, C4H, and MYB58 are responsible for the biosynthesis of important secondary metabolites that are responsive to the different11–13. This idea that the halophytes use unique molecular mechanisms to deal with the salinity has been considered before. Therefore, the study of transcriptome of these plants may shed some light on the role of key tolerance mechanisms in these plants to discover novel regulatory genes. We decided to decipher the gene networks and to identify the key genes related to salinity tolerance in two halophytes Bruguiera gymnorrhiza and Populus euphratica. Halophyte B. gymnorrhiza is one of the most important and widespread mangrove species in Asia and the Pacific. One of the halophytes that has been extensively studied for its salinity tolerance mechanisms. Inorganic ions such as Na+ and Cl− play important roles in the osmotic adjustment of leaves under NaCl stress in this plant14. It has been also shown that due to the up-regulation of the gene encoding RuBisCO activase enzymes in leaves, this plant grew under 200 mM NaCl treatment14. Another halophyte, P. euphratica is a tree species that grows naturally in arid and semi-arid regions along rivers, which belongs to the Salicaceae family. The natural distribution of this tree covers many countries and its natural habitats are in Asia, Africa and a small part of Europe15. Geographical and climatic diversity in the range of distribution of this species has caused morphological and genetic differences. P. euphratica is one of the salinity-tolerant poplar species which is used as a model plant to investigate salinity response mechanisms in trees16. Studies on salt-tolerant mechanisms in P. euphratica will help to increase the salinity tolerance in this species in arid and semi-arid regions. The aim of this study was to integrate the transcriptome datasets of P. euphratica and B. gymnorrhiza to find common DEGs and key genes responsive to salinity.


## Materials and methods

### Data collection and DEG identification
  The microarray data of P. euphratica (GSE52305) and B. gymnorrhiza (GSE10942) were retrieved from GEO (Table 1). We used FlexArray software to find out the genes that were expressed differentially between salt stress and control condition. Up- and down-regulated genes with log FC > 1 or log FC < − 1 and FDR < 0.05 were considered significant differentially expressed genes (DEGs). IDs of these genes were converted to TAIRID using the gprofiler. Venn diagram for DEGs involved in salinity response was plotted to identify genes common for both plants. Finally, common genes were used to map gene network and to identify ontology and characteristics of these genes.

### Normalization
  Expression Console software version 1.3.1 (https://www.thermofisher.com/affymetrix-expression-console-software.html) was used to normalize the data based on the Robust Multiarray Average (RMA) algorithm and the RMA signal values were transformed into FlexArray software version 1.6.3. (http://genomequebecmcgillca/FlexArray) to identify DEGs between stress and control conditions. The DEGs were defined with a false discovery rate less than 5% using Benjamini–Hochberg method.


### Gene ontology and KEGG pathway and hub genes
  In order to study the function of significant DEGs, functional enrichment analysis was performed using the DAVID online tool (https://david.ncifcrf.gov/) with a P-value level of 0.05. Common genes were grouped according to all three main groups of biological processes (BP), cellular components (CC) and molecular function (MF). The Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes (KEGG) was downloaded from the KEGG database (https://www.kegg.jp/) FDR < 0.05 was set as the cut-off criterion17.


### Protein-protein interaction (PPI) network
  To understand the interaction between the DEGs, STRING (https://www.string-db.org) database was used. Confidence score for interactions was medium score (above 0.15). The gene network obtained from STRING was imported into Cytoscape software version 3.9.1 (https://apps.cytoscape.org/apps/stringapp) for further analysis. In addition, hub genes were identified in the gene network with the Cytohubba Plugin and based on Maximal Clique Centrality (MCC).


### Identification of TFs, PKs and miRNA families
  To identify plant transcription factors (TFs) and protein kinases (PKs), the common DEGs were applied into iTAK database (http://itak.feilab.net/cgi-bin/itak/index.cgi). iTAK is a program to identify TFs, transcriptional regulators (TRs), and PKs. The psRNATarget tool was used to predict miRNAs that potentially target the common DEGs.


### Promoter analysis
  A BLAST against the Ensemble database was performed to find the upstream flanking region of DEGs (1500 bp). Then, to identify the conserved cis-acting elements, the MEME database (Multiple Em for Motif Elicitation, https://meme-suite.org/meme/tools/meme) software version 5.5.3 was used. After counting the motifs, the abundance of regulatory elements in the promoter region of DEGs was investigated. Additionally, we used the TomTom tool to compare the obtained CREs from the target genes against the motifs presented in the Jasper database with one threshold E-value cut-off of 0.05. Gene ontology for motifs was used by the Gomo tool to identify the biological role of each of the identified motifs in the promoter region.


### Screening of important genes based on the random forest method
  A random forest analysis was conducted to identify key genes. To identify the most important gene features among DEGs, normalized expression values of DEGs across control and salt-stressed samples were used as input for a Random Forest classifier with 500 decision trees (ntree = 500) and mtry set to the square root of the number of input variables, implemented in the R package ‘randomForest’. The Gini index of the random forest model was used to evaluate the importance of each gene in discriminating between normal and stressed samples. In this algorithm, a higher Gini index value indicates a greater contribution of the gene to the classification. Using this approach, the top 100 genes with the highest Gini index values were selected as candidate feature genes.


### Plant material and salt treatment
  One-year-old P. euphratica seedlings were collected from the Mahabad region in northwestern Iran (West Azerbaijan Province) and planted in 10-L pots filled with a 1:1 sand–soil mixture. The seedlings were maintained in the greenhouse of the School of Agriculture, Shiraz University, under controlled conditions of 20–25 °C. After a three-week period of irrigation and acclimation, salt stress was imposed by irrigating the plants with 2 L of NaCl solution at concentrations of 250 or 500 mM, along with a non-stress control, each with three replicates. Upper leaves were collected at 0, 12, 24, 48, and 72 h after salt treatment. Plant materials were immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at − 80 °C until RNA extraction.


### qRT-PCR
  Total RNA was extracted from the samples using a column-based RNA isolation kit (DENA Zist Asia, Iran) following the manufacturer’s instructions. RNA quantity and quality were assessed using a Nanodrop spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). To remove genomic DNA contamination, the extracted RNA was treated with DNase I (Thermo Scientific, USA). First-strand cDNA was then synthesized using the Easy cDNA Synthesis Kit (Parstous, Iran) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Real-time quantitative PCR (qRT-PCR) was employed to analysis the expression pattern of the NIR1 gene, which shown the highest positive fold change among the up-regulated DEGs, in the leaves of P. euphratica in response to salinity stress. qRT-PCR was performed on an Applied Biosystems™ StepOne™ Real-Time PCR System using the RealQ Plus 2x Master Mix Green with high ROX™ (Ampliqon, Denmark) following the manufacturer’s protocol. Three biological replicates were included for each treatment, and PeACT1 was used as the internal reference gene. The forward and reverse primer sequences for PeNIR1 and PeACT1 are provided in Table 2. Relative expression levels of the PeNIR1 gene were calculated using the 2-ΔΔCt method after normalization to PeACT1. Statistical analysis was performed by analysis of variance (ANOVA), followed by Tukey’s test to identify significant differences among treatment means (p ≤ 0.05). The PCR conditions were as follows: an initial denaturation at 95 °C for 5 min, followed by 35 cycles of 94 °C for 45 s, 52 °C for 45 s, and 72 °C for 45 s, with a final extension at 72 °C for 10 min.

## Results and discussion

### Meta-analysis and identification of DEGs
  One of the most important challenges of agriculture all over the world is crop yield loss due to salinity stress18. The response of plants to high levels of salinity is a complex process that includes morphological, physiological, and metabolic changes19. In this study, the transcriptome data of two halophytes, P. euphratica and B. gymnorrhiza, were analyzed in response to salinity stress. One of the ways to overcome salinity stress is to increase the amino acids proline and glycine, and P. euphratica copes with salinity by accumulating these amino acids in its tissues20. In addition, P. euphratica uses different mechanisms to cope with salinity stress such as a deeper root system, antioxidant production to prevent ROS damage, along with limiting photosynthetic capacity by reducing stomatal size and increase their density to avoid evaporation21. B. gymnorrhiza is one of the most important and widespread mangrove species in Asia and the Pacific. mangroves Due to their adaptability, they have the ability to tolerate high salinity and they have morphological adaptations to survive and are believed to have a specialized mechanism for salt exclusion21. Mangroves are equipped with a wide range of free radical scavenging systems that make them tolerate to high salinity22. Due to their specific adaptation to salinity stress, their genes can be a very important tool for plant breeding in the future. In this study, up- and down-regulated genes under salinity stress were identified and annotated, and common genes between two plants in two conditions were selected. In this study, we found up- and down-regulated genes with significant adjusted P-value (FDR < 0.05) and a fold change value greater than one (Fig. 1). The gene IDs were converted to Arabidopsis orthologues using the gprofiler (https://biit.cs.ut.ee/gprofiler/convert). Finally, 3,782 DEGs were identified (FDR < 0.05). The results of the Venn diagram showed that In P. euphratica, 1,960 DEGs were up-regulated and 1471 DEGs down-regulated, while in the B. gymnorrhiza, 144 DEGs were up-regulated, and 207 DEGs down-regulated (Fig. 2). Based on the results, 64 common DEGs between the two plant species datasets were identified whereas 27 DEGs were found to have the most positive fold change, respectively such as NAC1 - NAC DOMAIN CONTAINING PROTEIN 1, GBSS1- Granule bound starch synthase 1, NIR1-Ferredoxin-nitrite reductase, (APX- Ascorbate peroxidase) and (JAZ4- JASMONATE-ZIM-DOMAIN PROTEIN 4) were up-regulated. In addition, a total of 37 DEGs which have the most negative fold change, respectively such as (GASA1 - Gibberellin-regulated protein 1), (GLT1 - Glutamate synthase 1), (MSS1 - Sugar Transport Protein 13) and AT3G22890 (APS1 - ATP SULFURYLASE 1) were down-regulated.

  Common genes involved in salinity tolerance can be considered as potential candidates in plant breeding programs. NAC proteins are plant-specific TFs that control plant development, senescence, morphogenesis, and abiotic stress tolerance23. NAC1 (NAC021) is a transcriptional activator that plays an important role in growth and salt tolerance24. Studies have shown that the members of NAC family, has the potential to be involved in salt stress response and adaptation in quinoa25. JAZ proteins are other important transcriptional repressors in the jasmonic acid (JA) signaling process and play ubiquitous roles in plant growth, defense, and metabolism26. These proteins play roles in plant responses to biotic and abiotic stresses, for example, it has been reported that overexpression of OsJAZ8 in rice led to improved salt tolerance in transgenic seedlings27. In addition, overexpression of Apple MdJAZ2 improved the tolerance of transgenic Arabidopsis to salinity stress28. Ascorbate peroxidase (APX) is one of the antioxidant enzymes that plays an important role in scavenging H2O2 and balancing the content of ROS in plant cells, which is effective as a defense system of plants against various stresses29. Previous results have showed that three cytosolic genes BnaAPX1, BnaAPX2, BnaAPX9, and two putative microsomal genes BnaAPX18 and BnaAPX25 were up- regulated in Brassica napus under high salt concentrations29.

  APX expression was significantly higher in both shoots and roots for citrus rootstocks grown under salinity stress compared to the control30. GASA proteins also that have important roles in biotic and abiotic stress responses and in hormonal signal transmission and the regulation of plant growth and development31. For instance, TdGASA1 gene was up-regulated after exposure of durum wheat seedlings to the salinity stress32. The highest OsGASA1 transcript level in rice was induced by NaCl treatments33. ATP sulfurylase (ATPS) is a key enzyme for the sulfate assimilation pathway in plant plastids and cytosol. The results have shown that ATPSs such as SbATPSa and SbATPSb were up-regulated in leaves and down-regulated in roots in Sorghum bicolor under salinity stress34. In contrast, the expression of ATPS under salinity stress in shoots and roots in Brassica rapa was not significantly different compared to the control35.

### Gene enrichment analysis of common DEGs and KEGG pathway data
  Gene ontology analysis of 64 common DEGs showed that in Biological Processes (BP), they were significantly enriched including response to stimulus, chemicals, stress, organic substance, and abiotic stimulus (Fig. 3). For Molecular Function (MF), DEGs were significantly enriched in iron-sulfur cluster binding, metal cluster binding, ion binding, cation binding, and catalytic activity (Fig. 3). In addition, for Cell Component (CC), the DEGs were significantly enriched in plastids, chloroplasts, intracellular membrane-bounded organelles, and membrane-bounded organelle (Fig. 3). Analysis of metabolic pathways using KEGG showed that these genes are associated with metabolic pathways including nitrogen metabolism, secondary metabolite biosynthesis, cysteine and methionine metabolism, and selenocompound metabolism (Table 3).

Previous studies have shown that the effects of chemicals such as phytohormones including abscisic acid (ABA), salicylic acid (SA), jasmonic acid (JA), and ethylene are important in the stress signaling processes. Plants have a set of osmotic regulatory genes, the expression of these genes is stimulated by ABA, and then causes the accumulation of Ca2+ ions in the cytosol, which act as secondary messengers triggering the expression of salt-response genes36. Also, SA counteracted the damage caused by salinity stress through regulating osmolyte assimilation accompanied with ionic homeostasis by lowering the production of chlorogenic acid37.   KEGG analysis of the common DEGs revealed several major metabolic pathways. Nitrogen metabolism includes several physiological and biochemical processes including nitrogen uptake, transport and assimilation. Nitrogen metabolism plays an important role in the response to abiotic stresses in plants39. The secondary metabolite production is affected by external factors such as stresses, stimuli, and hormones. The accumulation of secondary metabolites in the plant is contributes to the defense response against microbial contamination and environmental stress40. However, it is not yet fully understood how environmental stresses affect the production of these substances. Substantial evidence shows that under stress conditions, the production of some of these compounds increase dramatically40.


### Protein–protein interaction and hub genes
  The whole PPI network shown in Fig. 4 contains 64 nodes and 119 edges. The cytoHubba of the Cytoscape software was used to select the significant hub genes among the DEGs. The five most significant hub genes were identified using the Maximal Clique Centrality (MCC) algorithm Finally, a PPI network was constructed to identify the five most significant hub genes.

  These included the genes NIR1 (Ferredoxin-nitrite reductase), APR1 (5’-adenylylphosphosulfate reductase1), APR3 (5’adenylylphosphosulfate reductase 3) and HY5 (elongated hypocotyl 5) which were up-regulated and gene MS2 (methionine synthase 2) which was down-regulated (Fig. 4). All five genes play an important role in abiotic stresses. Nitrite reductase (NIR) plays an important role in nitrate assimilation in plants. NiR activity has been shown to be decreased or increased under salinity stress41. Previous studies show that NiR1 enzyme activity decreased in salinity-sensitive plants, but it has increased in salinity-tolerant plants. Considering that two plants in this study are halophytes, our results are consistent with previous results. It has been previously reported that NR and NiR were significantly upregulated in the halophyte Zostera marina following exposure to NaCl42. It has been also shown that the expression of NiR in Morus alba leaves increased significantly compared to controls under salinity stress that accompanied by a significant increase in NiR activity41.

  HY5 (ELONGATED HYPOCOTYL 5) is a member of the basic leucine zipper (bZIP) family of transcription factors43. bZIPs are involved in responses to abiotic and biotic stresses43. Under NaCl stress, the physiological responses of plants to deal with salinity are regulated by the plant’s internal molecular mechanism. It has been shown that under salinity stress, bZIP gene expression is significantly upregulated44. Liao et al.. (2023) has shown that transgenic Arabidopsis overexpressing GmbZIP, a bZIP gene from soybean, showed better salinity tolerance compared to wild-type45. The APR1 and APR3 are considered as the key regulatory enzymes of sulfate assimilation46. It has been previously shown that salinity stress increases APR activity and its mRNA levels up to 3-fold for all three isoforms APR1, APR2, and APR3 in A. thaliana47.

  Plants respond to environmental stresses through the activation of some regulatory genes. In this research, we identified several TFs among DEGs (Fig. 5), each of which has an important role in abiotic stresses. The NAC family is a type of TFs that is expressed in different tissues and at different developmental stages. There are some reports have been shown the members of this family has key roles in response to biotic and abiotic stresses48. The overexpression of NAC genes such as ONAC022 and PeNAC036 improved salinity tolerance in transgenic rice and Arabidopsis, respectively49.

  Lateral organ boundary domain (LBD) proteins are plant-specific TFs that play important roles in growth, development and metabolic processes. Some studies also reported that LBD is involved in response to different biotic and abiotic stresses50. Ethylene response factor (ERF), one of the most important transcription factor family of AP2/ERF superfamily, is involved in plant development and responses to biotic and abiotic stresses. It has been previously reported that overexpression of ERF76 increases height, root length, and fresh weight under salinity stress in tobacco plants51. In addition, the expression of OsERF922 in rice was induced about 5-fold by NaCl treatment52.

  MYB is another large transcription factor family that play a key role in abiotic stress tolerance53. Cheng et al.. (2013) has been reported that overexpression of sheepgrass, LcMYB1, increases tolerance to oxidative stress related to salinity stress in transgenic Arabidopsis54. Zhu et al.. (2015) also showed that overexpression of OsMYB91 in rice increased salinity tolerance by inducing transcription of salt-related genes55.

### Identification of responsive of PKs to salinity stress
  Protein kinases play important roles in the plant response to different stresses through stress sensing and signal transduction56. PKs have also been shown to be involved in many aspects of cellular regulation and metabolism. To identify PKs, DEG sequences were used in the BLASTX against the iTAK database. Two PKs were identified among up-regulated DEGs and classified into CDPK (calcium-dependent protein kinases) and RLK-Pelle (receptor-like kinase/Pelle), whereas no PKs were found in the down-regulated DEGs. CDPKs play important roles in carbon and nitrogen metabolism, water and ion transport from the membrane, regulation of the cytoskeleton, stomatal movement, and regulation of growth and development. In addition, CDPKs play important roles in defense responses to abiotic stresses, wounds and fungi. Receptor-like kinase (RLK)/Pelles play important roles in plant development or related defense responses, and expansion of this family may be important for plant-specific adaptations57.


### Identification of miRNAs
  MicroRNAs are an extensive class of single-stranded non-coding RNA, which impact plant development and response to biotic and abiotic stresses58,59. miRNAs can affect gene regulation at the post-transcriptional stage under salinity stress60. Many salinity-responsive miRNAs have been found in plants. The psRNATarget tool was used to find putative miRNAs associated with DEGs (Fig. 6). In this study, we identified 826 miRNAs belonging to 122 conserved families in up-regulated DEGs and 470 miRNAs belonging to 101 conserved families in down-regulated DEGs. The miR5658 family possesses the highest frequency (33 members), followed by miR156, miR5021, and miR854 in up-regulated DEGs and the miR169 family possesses the highest frequency (24 members), followed by miR854, miR869, and miR773 in down-regulated DEGs. It was found that 48 target genes were common across P. euphratica and B. gymnorrhiza of which miR169 was the most frequent. miR5658 were identified from Tamarix which appeared to be the potential key regulators of the post-transcriptional under salinity stress61. Two plants Medicago truncatula and Medicago sativa had different expression patterns for Mir156 and showed different levels of salinity tolerance62. It has been reported that miR169 is closely related to various plant growth processes63. The miR169 family plays an important role in the response to abiotic stress. In this study, we observed that the miR169 family has the highest frequency (24 members) among down-regulated DEGs, which has been previously reported in maize under salinity stress64. Also, in poplar under salinity stress, most of miR169 showed significantly up-regulated65. It has been previously reported that the expression of miR169 was significantly up-regulated in P. euphratica under salinity stress66. Although our analysis is based solely on computational prediction and does not include small RNA sequencing or experimental validation of miRNA expression, the results suggest that these conserved miRNA families may play important regulatory roles in salinity stress tolerance. Experimental validation using small RNA-seq or qRT-PCR will be essential in future studies to confirm their expression patterns and regulatory functions.

### Cis-acting elements in promoters
  The 1,500 bp upstream flanking region67 of DEGs was used to predict conserved and consensus cis-regulatory (CREs). Using the MEME tool, 11 significant motifs were identified with lengths ranging from 29 to 50 nucleotides (Table 4). Transcription factors such as AP2/EREBP, C2H2, bZIP and FOX factors families were identified in the promoter region of key genes. The majority of motifs are known as CREs are related to C2H2 zinc finger motifs. Based on the enrichment of AP2/EREBP, C2H2, bZIP, and FOX motifs in the promoter regions of DEGs and the well-known roles of their corresponding transcription factors in abiotic stress responses, we hypothesize that these motifs serve as direct cis-regulatory targets of the respective transcription factors, forming specific regulatory networks. Plant-specific AP2/EREBPs are a large group of transcription factors that regulate key functions in response to several abiotic stresses68. The results showed that TtAP2/ERF belonging to the DREB subfamily in Triticum durum were significantly induced under both drought and salt stresses69. C2H2 zinc finger proteins are transcription factors that regulate genes involved in plant growth and development, as well as genes involved in plant stress response70. C2H2 zinc finger proteins play an important role in activating the ABA signal transduction pathway to induce the expression of stress-related genes under salt stress. bZIP TFs are one of the major families of transcription factors that play crucial roles in regulating gene expression under various abiotic stresses71. Different members of the plant bZIP family play important roles in a wide array of biological events, from developmental stages like flower development, seed maturation, dormancy, and senescence, to critical responses against abiotic stresses such as salt, drought, cold, osmotic, and mechanical damage, and in mediating ABA signaling44. FOX transcription factors have a conserved forkhead box (FOX) domain, a DNA-binding region with a winged helix structure44. FOX transcription factors regulate plant development (root, flowering, seed) and are increasingly linked to drought, salinity, and heat stress responses. The expression of certain FOX transcription factor genes has been shown to increase in response to salinity stress, suggesting their role in activating downstream genes that contribute to tolerance72. Gene Ontology indicated that these motifs are involved in regulation of transcription, DNA-dependent, and the transmembrane receptor protein tyrosine kinase signaling pathway. In addition, these motifs are involved in molecular functions, including TF activity, protein serine/threonine kinase activity, and RNA binding.

### Random forest algorithm
  A random forest algorithm was used to identify key gene features among the DEGs. The findings highlighted five significant genes, AGT1, GASA1, LHT1, CA2, and GSR2. Aminotransferases are enzymes that catalyze the transamination reactions and play significant roles in cell metabolisms. Overexpression of AtAGT1 enhances salt tolerance by affecting ABA-dependent signaling73. Overexpression of AtAGT1 in duckweed has been reported to increase salinity tolerance in transgenic plants74. Gene expression data demonstrated that AtAGT1 is involved in a complex metabolic network in roots and AtAGT1 overexpression in roots was also shown to be involved in salinity stress tolerance. In plants, the gibberellic acid-stimulated Arabidopsis (GASA) gene family plays important regulatory roles in plant growth and development, response to hormones, and resistance mechanisms to biotic and abiotic stresses75. GASA1, is a representative gene of GA signal response and transduction, has been mentioned in many reports76. GASA1 is involved in reproductive growth, leaf senescence, biotic and abiotic stress regulatory networks. GASA1 expression also responded to abscisic acid, brassinosteroid and auxin74. The LHT family is known as a lysine-histidine transporter which includes neutral and acidic amino acid transporters with high affinity that affects nitrogen uptake and tolerance to abiotic stresses in plants77. The results showed that overexpression of NtLHT1 in tobacco enhances the tolerance to salinity and drought stresses77. Carbonic anhydrases (CAs) play essential roles in all photosynthetic organisms and are zinc metalloenzymes that catalyze the interconversion of CO2 and HCO378. The CA family is induced under multiple abiotic stresses and phytohormones. Previous studies showed that in Oryza sativa, βCA2 is a gene involved in salinity tolerance79. Glutamine synthetase (GS) is a key enzyme in nitrogen metabolism in organisms that catalyzes the synthesis of glutamine from ammonia and glutamate with the energy of the reaction provided hydrolysis of ATP to ADP80. They are classified into 3 morphological categories: GS1, GS2 and GS3, where GS2 is mainly found in eukaryotes. The results have shown that GS genes play an important role in tolerance to abiotic stresses. It has been reported that overexpression of GS2 gene in rice increased photorespiration capacity that can prevent the over-reduction of electron transport systems and enhance NaCl tolerance in transgenic plants81.


### qRT-PCR analysis
  The results showed that the relative expression of the NIR1 gene was significantly affected by different concentrations of NaCl and treatment duration in P. euphratica leaves (P ≤ 0.05). At time zero, treatments with 250 and 500 mM showed 1.27- and 1.37-fold up-regulate compared to the control, respectively; however, these increases were not statistically significant. Under 250 mM, the gene expression increased from 12 h after stress application and was significantly higher than the control at 24 and 48 h, with the highest expression observed at 48 h, reaching 6.4-fold relative to the control. At 72 h, although expression decreased, it still showed a significant difference compared to the control and relative to the 250 mM treatment. Under 500 mM, the increase in gene expression was more pronounced. The expression reached approximately 8-fold compared to the control at 24 h and peaked at 48 h about 10.2-fold relative to control, which was significantly higher than other treatments. However, at 72 h, a significant decrease in gene expression was observed; although expression remained higher than the control, the difference compared to 250 mM was not statistically significant (Fig. 7). Statistically significant differences among treatments are indicated by different letters (P ≤ 0.05). In mature leaves of Zostera marina, the expression of ZmNiR was significantly upregulated, reaching 13-fold of the control 3 h after exposure to 40% salinity stress, highlighting its crucial role in early salinity stress respons82. It has been also shown that the expression of NiR in Morus alba leaves increased significantly compared to controls under salinity stress that accompanied by a significant increase in NiR activity41.

  Nitrite reductase (NIR) is a key enzyme in the dissimilatory denitrification pathway, responsible for reducing NO2- to NO. Regulation of nitrogen metabolism plays a vital role in plant salinity tolerance, and the interaction between salinity and nitrogen status forms a complex network that affects nearly all physiological and metabolic processes in the plant, which requires further investigation83. A comprehensive analysis of the expression pattern of the NIR1 gene in response to varying NaCl concentrations was performed. The qRT-PCR results revealed that the expression levels of NIR1 in Populus euphratica were significantly up-regulated at different time points under salinity stress, indicating a potential role of this gene in the plant’s adaptive response to high salinity condition. Considering that this tree is salt-tolerant, previous studies have also shown that under salinity stress, the activity of the NiR enzyme decreases in salt-sensitive plants, while it increases in salt-tolerant plants. Therefore, NiR is speculated to play a crucial role in the adaptation of eelgrass to high salinity82.

## Conclusions
  Halophyte plants use different mechanisms such as hormone signaling pathways (abscisic acid and auxin), MAPK and osmolyte synthesis to tolerate salinity stress. Identification of genes involved in salinity tolerance in these plants can provide valuable information for plant breeding and genetic engineering. The objective of this study was to identify common DEGs between two halophyte P. euphratica and B. gymnorrhiza under salinity stress. Our study identified up-regulated genes in response to stress in these halophytes. By identifying important genes with up-regulation, and comparison with past results, we identified major genes potentially involved in salinity tolerance. Gene network analysis reveals complex relationships between genes and identifies hub genes. Our results revealed many hub genes encoding TFs (e.g., NAC, bZIP, ERF, MYB, and LBD), kinases (e.g., CAMK), and miRNAs (e.g., miR169) responding to salinity stress. Identification of important miRNAs can lead to novel approaches to improve plant tolerance to salinity stress. promoter analysis revealed that key DEGs are regulated by conserved cis-elements associated with major stress-responsive transcription factor families, particularly C2H2, AP2/EREBP, bZIP, and FOX. These regulatory motifs highlight coordinated transcriptional control mechanisms underlying salinity stress tolerance in plants. Regulation of nitrogen metabolism is crucial for plant salt tolerance, as the interaction between salinity and nitrogen status influences a wide range of physiological and metabolic processes. qRT-PCR analysis showed that NIR1 expression in Populus euphratica was significantly up-regulated at multiple time points under salinity stress, suggesting its involvement in adaptation to high salinity conditions.
