BACKGROUND: Inflammation and oxidative stress are core pathological processes in the development of numerous chronic diseases, mutually reinforcing each other to create a vicious cycle. Consequently, identifying natural compounds capable of concurrently mitigating both processes, along with the elucidation of their mechanisms, is crucial for therapeutic development. OBJECTIVE: This study aims to investigate the anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects, along with their molecular mechanisms, of the novel sesquiterpene lactone compound 3-n-Propionyl Zaluzanin C (NPZC), isolated from Saussurea involucrata. STUDY DESIGN: Utilizing an integrated approach of network pharmacology, cellular assays, molecular docking, and zebrafish models, this study systematically evaluated the bioactivities of NPZC and elucidated its underlying mechanisms of action. METHODS: Network pharmacology was utilized to screen potential targets and pathways. The effects of NPZC were then interrogated in cellular models of LPS-induced inflammation (RAW264.7) and H(2)O(2)-induced oxidative stress (HepG2), quantifying inflammatory mediators, ROS levels, antioxidant enzyme activity, and related protein expression. The interaction between NPZC and key targets was validated by molecular docking and cellular thermal shift assays (CETSA). Finally, the protective efficacy of NPZC was confirmed in a zebrafish model, ameliorating both AAPH-induced oxidative damage and LPS-triggered inflammatory phenotypes. RESULTS: NPZC effectively suppressed the release of LPS-induced inflammatory mediators (TNF-alpha, IL-6, NO and COX-2), scavenged H(2)O(2)-induced ROS, and enhanced the activity of antioxidant enzymes (CAT, SOD and GSH). Mechanistic studies indicate that NPZC does not directly inhibit the NF-kappaB pathway. Instead, it activates the NRF2 signaling pathway, promoting NRF2 nuclear translocation and upregulating downstream antioxidant genes (HO-1 and NQO1). Consequently, NPZC enhances cellular antioxidant capacity and indirectly suppresses NF-kappaB-mediated inflammation. The direct binding of NPZC to both NRF2 and KEAP1 was confirmed by molecular docking and CETSA. These actions were corroborated in vivo, where NPZC alleviated oxidative damage and inflammatory phenotypes in zebrafish, concomitant with the upregulation of NRF2 pathway. CONCLUSION: NPZC disrupts the vicious cycle of inflammation and oxidative stress by activating the NRF2 signaling pathway, thereby enhancing cellular antioxidant defenses and indirectly suppressing the NF-kappaB pathway. This study not only elucidates the modern scientific basis for the traditional medicinal effects of S.involucrata but also provides novel candidate compounds and theoretical foundations for developing natural anti-inflammatory and antioxidant drugs targeting NRF2. Future studies would clarify the relationship between the NPZC and NRF2 using point mutation analysis and surface plasmon resonance. Mechanistic validation will be further enhanced through multi-omics approaches, with confirmed conversion potential subsequently validated in mammalian models.
