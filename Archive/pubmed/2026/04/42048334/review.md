All statements regarding experimental results, statistical analyses, and mechanistic conclusions are assessed based on the written description alone. No raw data, figure panels, or statistical outputs were available for independent verification.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors claim that ulinastatin (UTI) attenuates sepsis-associated acute lung injury (SALI) and capillary leakage by activating the PI3K/Akt pathway, leading to FoxO1 phosphorylation and nuclear export, thereby suppressing FoxO1-dependent angiopoietin-2 (Ang-2) transcription. This mechanism is proposed based on in vivo CLP rat models and in vitro LPS-stimulated HUVECs, with molecular docking supporting a direct UTI-PI3K interaction. The protective effects are reported to be abolished by the PI3K inhibitor wortmannin.

Statistical methods are described but no numerical data, effect sizes, or p-values are provided in the text.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** All figures and figure legends, raw data, statistical outputs, docking scores beyond the single reported value, antibody validation details, and supplementary information files. The absence of these materials prevents independent verification of the reported findings and the strength of the evidence.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript addresses a clinically relevant question regarding the mechanism of action of ulinastatin in sepsis-associated acute lung injury. The proposed pathway (PI3K/Akt/FoxO1/Ang-2) is plausible and builds on existing literature. However, the evidence as presented in the text is largely descriptive, with no quantitative data, effect sizes, or statistical measures reported. The molecular docking analysis is superficial and does not establish a direct binding interaction. The claim that UTI directly binds PI3K is not supported by the evidence provided. The manuscript would benefit from substantial revision, including presentation of quantitative results, additional experimental validation, and tempering of mechanistic claims.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in critical care medicine, sepsis pathophysiology, endothelial biology, and acute lung injury would find the results relevant. Clinicians using ulinastatin in sepsis management may also be interested in the proposed mechanism. The potential clinical implication of UTI as a steroid-sparing agent could attract broader interest in intensive care research.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses a clinically important problem with a well-established animal model (CLP) and relevant in vitro system (LPS-stimulated HUVECs). The proposed mechanism is biologically plausible and connects to a known pathway (PI3K/Akt/FoxO1/Ang-2) implicated in vascular stability. The use of a pharmacological inhibitor (wortmannin) to test pathway dependence is appropriate. The comparison with dexamethasone provides a clinically relevant benchmark.
- **Major Concerns** The concerns are listed below with full details.
- **Minor Comments** The minor comments are listed below with full details.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 (no quantitative data), R1-M2 (docking does not establish direct binding), R1-M3 (wortmannin specificity and dose justification), R1-M4 (dexamethasone mechanism inconsistency).
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: The proposed mechanism is partially novel, as the specific link between UTI, PI3K activation, and FoxO1-dependent Ang-2 suppression in SALI has not been previously reported. However, the individual components of this pathway are well established, and the novelty is incremental rather than transformative. Scientific importance: The findings could have clinical relevance for sepsis management, but the evidence presented does not yet establish the mechanistic claim with sufficient rigor. The potential impact is moderate. Interdisciplinary readership: The topic is relevant to critical care, pharmacology, and vascular biology, but the current presentation is unlikely to attract broad interdisciplinary interest beyond these fields. Technical soundness: The experimental design is generally appropriate, but the lack of quantitative data, the superficial docking analysis, and the incomplete validation of the proposed mechanism undermine technical soundness. Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is generally readable, but the results section relies heavily on qualitative descriptions without numerical context, which limits clarity for readers who cannot access the figures.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The manuscript addresses a relevant question and the proposed mechanism is plausible, but the evidence as presented is insufficient to support the core claims. Substantial revision with quantitative data, additional validation, and tempered conclusions would be required before the case is established.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence quality and reporting
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that UTI attenuates lung injury, reduces Ang-2 expression, and activates the PI3K/Akt/FoxO1 pathway, with protective effects abolished by wortmannin.
- **Evidence pointer** Results sections "UTI attenuates sepsis-associated acute lung injury (SALI) and endothelial barrier dysfunction in vivo and vitro", "UTI improved endothelial barrier function by protecting the junction proteins", "The effects of UTI on the expression of angiopoietin-2 (Ang-2) and activation of the phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K)/protein kinase B (PKB/AKT)/Forkhead box protein O1(FoxO1) signaling pathway", "The protective effect of UTI on SALI and the endothelial barrier is mediated by the PI3K/Akt/FoxO1 pathway"; figures 1-6 referenced but not provided
- **Concern** The results are described exclusively in qualitative terms. No numerical data, effect sizes, confidence intervals, or p-values are reported anywhere in the text. Statements such as "significantly increased", "obviously reduced", and "markedly downregulated" are used without quantitative support. The statistical methods section describes appropriate tests, but the results of these tests are never presented in the text.
- **Why it matters** Without quantitative data, the reader cannot assess the magnitude of the observed effects, the variability within groups, or the reliability of the conclusions. This is a fundamental requirement for evaluating the strength of the evidence and for reproducibility.
- **Resolution test** The authors must report quantitative results for all key experiments, including mean values, standard deviations, sample sizes, and exact p-values or effect sizes. This could be presented in the text or in figure panels with corresponding statistical annotations.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Mechanistic claim support
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that UTI has a "stable interaction and favorable binding affinity to PI3K" based on molecular docking, and that this binding activates the PI3K/Akt pathway.
- **Evidence pointer** Results section "The effects of UTI on the expression of angiopoietin-2 (Ang-2) and activation of the phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K)/protein kinase B (PKB/AKT)/Forkhead box protein O1(FoxO1) signaling pathway", Figure 3C referenced but not provided
- **Concern** The molecular docking analysis is described in a single sentence with one docking score. No details are provided regarding the docking methodology, the protein structure preparation, the ligand conformation, the binding site, or the significance of the score. A docking score of -7.47 is presented without context or comparison to known binders. Most critically, computational docking alone does not demonstrate a direct physical interaction in a biological system.
- **Why it matters** The claim that UTI directly binds PI3K is central to the proposed mechanism. If this interaction is not experimentally validated, the mechanistic model is substantially weakened. The current evidence is insufficient to support a direct binding claim.
- **Resolution test** The authors should either provide experimental evidence of direct UTI-PI3K binding (e.g., surface plasmon resonance, isothermal titration calorimetry, pull-down assays, or cellular thermal shift assay) or temper the claim to state that docking suggests a potential interaction that requires experimental validation.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Experimental rigor
- **Claim pointer** The authors use wortmannin as a PI3K inhibitor to demonstrate that the protective effects of UTI are mediated by the PI3K/Akt/FoxO1 pathway.
- **Evidence pointer** Results section "The protective effect of UTI on SALI and the endothelial barrier is mediated by the PI3K/Akt/FoxO1 pathway", Figures 4-6 referenced but not provided
- **Concern** Wortmannin is a broad-spectrum PI3K inhibitor that also affects other PI3K-related kinases and has known off-target effects. The manuscript does not report dose-response experiments, specificity controls, or validation that the wortmannin concentration used effectively inhibits PI3K without causing nonspecific cellular toxicity. Additionally, the timing of wortmannin administration relative to UTI treatment is not described in sufficient detail.
- **Why it matters** The conclusion that the protective effects of UTI are mediated specifically through PI3K depends on the specificity and appropriate use of the inhibitor. Without proper controls, alternative explanations for the observed effects cannot be excluded.
- **Resolution test** The authors should provide dose-response data for wortmannin, demonstrate that the chosen concentration inhibits PI3K activity (e.g., by measuring downstream Akt phosphorylation), and include controls for off-target effects. Alternatively, genetic knockdown or knockout of PI3K subunits could strengthen the conclusion.

- **Concern ID** R1-M4
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** No
- **Axis** Internal consistency
- **Claim pointer** The authors report that dexamethasone (DEX) does not alter FoxO1 phosphorylation but does cause FoxO1 nuclear export, and suggest acetylation as a possible alternative mechanism.
- **Evidence pointer** Results section "The effects of UTI on the expression of angiopoietin-2 (Ang-2) and activation of the phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K)/protein kinase B (PKB/AKT)/Forkhead box protein O1(FoxO1) signaling pathway", Figure 4A and 4B referenced but not provided
- **Concern** The observation that DEX causes FoxO1 nuclear export without changing its phosphorylation state is presented without experimental support. The suggestion of acetylation as a mechanism is speculative and not tested. This creates an inconsistency in the narrative, as the authors do not explain how DEX achieves nuclear export through a different mechanism while producing similar functional outcomes to UTI.
- **Why it matters** The comparison with DEX is used to highlight the distinct mechanism of UTI. However, the unexplained discrepancy in the DEX mechanism weakens the clarity of the argument and raises questions about whether the observed nuclear export in the DEX group is real or an artifact.
- **Resolution test** The authors should either provide experimental evidence for the proposed acetylation mechanism (e.g., acetylation-specific antibodies, acetylation inhibitors) or remove the speculation and present the DEX data as an observation requiring further investigation.

### Minor Comments

- **Concern ID** R1-m1
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Reporting quality
- **Affected element** Animal model description
- **Evidence pointer** Materials and methods section "CLP‐induced polymicrobial sepsis model and treatment"
- **Issue** The text states that 48 rats were used and 30 survived to the 24-hour endpoint, with 18 requiring early euthanasia. However, the number of rats per experimental group is not specified, and it is unclear how many animals were allocated to each condition (sham, CLP, CLP+UTI, CLP+DEX, CLP+UTI+wortmannin, etc.).
- **Required correction** Provide the number of animals per group and clarify whether the survival numbers are consistent across groups or whether differential mortality affected group sizes and statistical power.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that UTI directly binds to PI3K is unsupported. Molecular docking alone is computational prediction and does not constitute experimental evidence of a physical interaction.
- The claim that UTI "disrupts a critical positive feedback loop" driven by FoxO1 is not fully supported, as the manuscript does not demonstrate the existence of this loop in the experimental system prior to UTI treatment.
- The suggestion that UTI could serve as a "steroid-sparing agent" or an alternative to therapeutic plasma exchange is speculative and goes beyond the data presented.
- The statement that DEX may regulate FoxO1 acetylation in endothelial cells is presented without experimental evidence and is therefore unsupported.
- The overall conclusion that the PI3K/Akt/FoxO1 pathway is the "primary conduit" for UTI action is not fully established, as alternative mechanisms are not excluded and the evidence relies heavily on a single pharmacological inhibitor.
