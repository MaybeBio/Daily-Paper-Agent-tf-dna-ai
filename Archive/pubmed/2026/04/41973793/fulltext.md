# Majoon Ushba alleviated IL-17A sensitized keratinocyte ferroptosis via JAK-2-STAT-3 signaling axis and reversed imiquimod induced psoriasiform inflammation


## Context
  Psoriasis is a relapsing autoimmune disease exacerbated by aberrant interleukin (IL)-17 A activity. Majoon Ushba, a unani polyherbal formulation implicated in clinical cases of psoriasis lacks immunopharmacological validation.


## Objective
  The study aims to investigate the pre-clinical efficacy of Majoon Ushba and its therapeutic role in mitigating IL-17A-induced keratinocytes ferroptosis via ablation of JAK-2/STAT-3 pathway.


## Materials and Methods
  HaCaT cells were stimulated with IL-17A to assess the activation of the JAK-2-STAT-3 pathway. The STAT-3 inhibitor, S3I-201, was used to confirm the role of the STAT-3 axis in keratinocyte ferroptosis. Majoon Ushba pretreatment was assessed to determine its efficacy in alleviating keratinocyte ferroptosis. An imiquimod (IMQ)-induced psoriasis mouse model was used to evaluate the pre-clinical efficacy of Majoon Ushba. Furthermore, prior high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) profiling was leveraged for in-silico docking analysis to identify the binding affinities of key phytoconstituents with IL-17RA and STAT-3.


## Results
  Majoon Ushba alleviated the IL-17A/JAK-2-STAT-3 axis, improved GPX4 expression, and regulated lipid peroxidation. Subsequently, Majoon Ushba also reversed the expression of pathogenic mediators and led to a reduction in serum cytokine levels of IL-17A, IL-23, and IFN-γ. An in-silico docking analysis suggested favorable binding affinities for key phytoconstituents of Majoon Ushba against IL-17RA and STAT-3. Aligned with pre-clinical and in vitro results, the computational findings offer initial evidence that the polyherbal formulation may influence the IL-17A/JAK-2-STAT-3 signaling pathway.


## Discussion and Conclusion
  In conclusion, our preliminary findings reveal a plausible mechanistic basis for the anti-psoriatic efficacy of Majoon Ushba, warranting larger clinical trials in psoriasis patient cohorts.


## Introduction
  Psoriasis is an autoimmune skin disease that affects nearly 2-3% of the world’s population (Rapalli et al. 2020). The clinical features of psoriasis include localized inflammation, scaly patches, and aggressive itching. The external ramifications of psoriasis often sustain mental stigma in patients, significantly impacting their socialization patterns (Greb et al. 2016). Current pharmacological modalities used to treat psoriasis include vitamin D supplements, nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), and corticosteroids. Furthermore, targeted biologics, especially those targeting IL-17A, offer improved resolution, reinforcing the pivotal role of IL-17A in psoriasis. However, current treatment modalities do not offer complete resolution of disease features and are associated with adverse events, limiting their clinical application (Batta et al. 2023). Thus, there is a consistent need to develop and validate novel compounds for the management of psoriasis. From the current standpoint of psoriasis therapeutic landscape, several small molecule inhibitors are being validated in pre-clinical disease models (Ridha-Salman et al. 2025). Previously, our lab validated the blockade of IL-17A cytokine signaling in human epidermal keratinocytes using natural small-molecule inhibitors diindolylmethane and cyanidin (Srikanth and Rasool 2022). However, the translation of these small-molecule inhibitors into clinical practice has been challenging because of practical constraints in the process development. This prompted us to investigate the therapeutic implications of Majoon Ushba, a polyherbal formulation routinely used by Unani practitioners in clinics for skin ailments. The traditional formulation is a potent coalition of the useful parts of 14 medicinal plants, as listed in the Unani Pharmacopeia of India, Part II, and Volume-I (formulations) (Table 1). Despite the proven clinical efficacy of this polyherbal formulation in resolving the external features of psoriasis (Lone et al. 2011), its pharmacological effects have not been scientifically validated. The present study elucidated the mechanism by which Majoon Ushba operates downstream of IL-17A cytokine signaling, highlighting its therapeutic potential in ameliorating the physical and molecular characteristics of psoriasis. In the present study, we developed a strategy to utilize Majoon Ushba as a topical treatment in an IMQ-induced psoriasis model. This approach is based on the rationale that psoriasis medications are predominantly formulated as topical creams or ointments, which offer enhanced efficacy owing to their direct application to the skin (Salman et al. 2024).

  Janus kinase (JAK) and signal transducer and activator of transcription (STAT) are key downstream signaling proteins of IL-17A cytokine signaling (Ellinghaus et al. 2012). Our earlier report linked the activation of the JAK-2-STAT-3 signaling pathway to the expression of key pathogenic mediators of psoriasis, including Cyr61, HMGB1, and VEGF (Srikanth and Rasool 2022). STAT-3 has been identified as a genetic risk locus that is upregulated in patients (Tsoi et al. 2012). Therapeutic modalities targeting STAT-3 activation have shown promising outcomes in an IMQ-induced model of psoriasis (Sudha Yalamarthi et al. 2022; Cai et al. 2023). This prompted us to study the ability of Majoon Ushba to regulate the JAK-2-STAT-3 signaling axis downstream of IL-17A.

  Impaired keratinocyte turnover or dysregulated keratinocyte homeostasis is a classical feature of psoriasis, resulting in increased epidermal thickness, parakeratosis, or acanthosis (Ortiz-Lopez et al. 2022; Albanesi et al. 2018). Keratinocyte ferroptosis compromises the homeostatic machinery of the epidermis. Targeting ferroptosis in keratinocytes offers distinct advantages in restoring homeostatic balance and blocking the autoimmune feedforward loop facilitated by the release of damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) (Shou et al. 2021). Keratinocyte ferroptosis amplifies secondary immune responses and drives dendritic cell overactivation. Additionally, studies have leveraged IL-17A signaling blockade to target the ferroptotic program (Guo et al. 2022; Song et al. 2023). This provides a strong rationale for investigating the therapeutic implications of Majoon Ushba in alleviating psoriatic ferroptosis by inhibiting IL-17A signaling.

  Dysregulated iron homeostasis and diminished GPX4 antioxidant machinery are the central hallmarks of ferroptosis (Li et al. 2021; Dixon et al. 2012). Elevated hepcidin levels in psoriatic lesions are accompanied by a corresponding decline in serum iron levels in patients with psoriasis. Increased iron levels have been observed in psoriatic lesions in control patients for nearly 50 years (Forslind et al. 1999; Pinheiro et al. 2015; Molin and Wester 1973). Iron chelators, including deferoxamine, have demonstrated promising outcomes in alleviating psoriasiform inflammation (Abboud et al. 2024). Thus, there is explicit evidence confirming the role of iron in psoriasis-like inflammation. We speculated that enhanced iron intake via the transferrin receptor could contribute to improved ferritin load, sensitizing cells toward ferroptosis. Another key contributor to ferroptosis is a compromised antioxidant machinery, GPX4, and an impaired GSH:GSSG ratio, indicating a retarded ability of cells to neutralize excess reactive oxygen species (ROS). Thus, we investigated the effects of Majoon Ushba on iron homeostasis and cellular redox machinery by regulating IL-17A levels to alleviate ferroptosis in keratinocytes.

  In summary, the current study aimed to validate Majoon Ushba as a potent drug against psoriasis by investigating its therapeutic effects, including the regulation of key pathogenic mediators and alleviation of psoriatic ferroptosis via the IL-17A–JAK-2/STAT-3 signaling axis.


## Methodology

### Chemicals and antibodies
  Majoon Ushba (MU) was purchased from the pharmacy of the National Institute of Unani Medicine (Bangalore, India). The STAT-3 inhibitor S3I-201 and canonical ferroptosis inhibitor (ferrostain-1) were purchased from MedChem Express (NJ, USA). Primary antibodies targeting JAK-2 (#A11497), pJAK2 (#AP0531), STAT-3 (#A1192), pSTAT3 (#AP0705), GPX4 (#A1933), TfR (#A5865), ACSL4 (#A6826), and β-actin (#AC026) utilized in western blotting, and CYR61 (#A1111), HMGB1 (#A19429), and VEGF (#A21647) for immunofluorescence, along with HRP- (#AS014), and FITC-conjugated secondary antibodies (#AS011), were sourced from ABclonal (MA, USA). Alexa Fluor 555 (#4413) was purchased from Cell Signaling Technology (Danvers, MA, USA). HiMedia (Maharashtra, India) supplied all other chemical reagents utilized in our molecular biology experiments and for buffer preparation.


### In vitro treatment regimen
  In the present study, we used an immortalized human epidermal keratinocyte (HaCaT) cell line to model human psoriasis. HaCaT or human keratinocyte cell lines were obtained from the National Center for Cell Science, Pune, India. The cells were maintained in complete 10% high-glucose Dulbecco’s modified eagle medium (DMEM) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 µg/mL streptomycin at 37 °C and 5% CO2, allowing them to adhere. To ensure biological relevance, the cells were first differentiated by exposure to 2 mM calcium chloride (CaCl2) for 4 h to mimic the epidermal structure and differentiation state of the skin. Subsequently, psoriasis-like skin inflammation was established by incubating the differentiated cells with 100 μM IMQ for 24 h, as previously described (Srikanth and Rasool 2022). Undifferentiated HaCaT keratinocytes were used as negative controls (Supplementary Figure 1A).

  For in vitro assays, the differentiated cells were either pretreated with MU (200 µg/mL) or S3I-201 (50 µM) for 24 h or left untreated. Subsequently, the cells were stimulated with or without 100 ng/mL IL-17A for 24 h. The in vitro IL-17A stimulation dose was predetermined based on prior evidence suggesting a 100 ng/ml concentration to improve the expression of downstream targets including vital inflammation markers in HaCaT keratinocytes, while the stimulation time (24h) has deemed necessary to effect the expression of late ferroptosis markers (Yuan and Cao 2023; Efimova et al. 2020).HaCaT keratinocytes; baseline/negative controlHaCaT Keratinocytes + CaCl2 (∼4h) + IMQ (∼24h); in vitro psoriasis modelHaCaT Keratinocytes + CaCl2 (∼4h) + IMQ (∼24h) + IL-17A (∼24h); positive controlHaCaT Keratinocytes + CaCl2 (∼4h) + IMQ (∼24h) + MU (∼24h) + IL-17A (∼24h); treatment controlHaCaT Keratinocytes + CaCl2 (∼4h) + IMQ (∼24h) + S3I-201 (∼24h) + IL-17A (∼24h); reference control


### Cell viability
  Cell viability in HaCaT cells was determined using an MTT assay. HaCaT cells were seeded in 96-well plates (1 × 104 cells/well) and allowed to attach for 24 h prior to treatment. After attachment, the cells were treated with Majoon Ushba at concentrations ranging from 10 to 400 μg/mL for a 24-hour period. Following treatment, 3-(4,5-Dimethylthiazolyl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide (MTT) reagent (5 mg/ml in PBS) (Sigma, USA) was added to each well and incubated for 4 h, allowing metabolically active cells to reduce tetrazolium salt to insoluble formazan crystals. The crystals were then solubilized by adding 100 μL μL DMSO. The absorbance of the resulting solution, which is indicative of cell viability, was measured at 570 nm.


### Colony formation assay
  The cells were seeded and cultured for 24 h to allow establishment. Following this, the cells were either pretreated with the tested concentrations of Majoon Ushba or 50 μM S3I-201, or maintained as untreated controls, followed by stimulation with or without 100 ng/ml IL-17 for 24 h. Mature or formed colonies were then fixed with methanol and stained with crystal violet (10 g/L; Fisher Scientific, Fair Lawn, NJ, USA). Quantification was performed by imaging the colonies under an inverted microscope (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan) at 20 × magnification and counting the number of distinct colonies formed. Quantification was performed by imaging the colonies under an inverted microscope (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan) at 20× magnification and counting the number of distinct colonies formed.


### Detection of intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS)
  Cells (2 × 105) were uniformly seeded in a 6-well plate and allowed to establish themselves in the wells. 2′,7′-Dichlorofluorescein diacetate (DCFH-DA) is a cell-permeable, nonfluorescent probe. Upon oxidation by various intracellular ROS, it becomes highly fluorescent (DCF), allowing for the direct and quantifiable measurement of cellular oxidative stress. For ROS detection, the cells were washed with PBS to remove residual culture medium. DCFH-DA was introduced to the cell suspension and was then incubated for 30 min at 37 °C in the dark to allow probe uptake. Prior to imaging, the cells were washed with PBS and serum-free media, and an EVOS M5000 fluorescence microscope was used for image acquisition and visualization.


### Assessment of change in mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP)
  JC-1, a lipophilic carbocyanine dye, was used to measure MMP. Briefly, HaCaT cells were cultured in 6-well plates with JC-1 for 15 min at 37 °C. The dye entered HaCaT cells and accumulated in the mitochondria in a potential-dependent manner. The mitochondrial membrane potential was assessed by measuring the fluorescence properties of JC-1. Red fluorescence (585/590 nm excitation/emission) indicated potential-driven dye aggregation in healthy mitochondria, whereas green emission (514/529 nm excitation/emission) indicated monomeric dye in the cytosol when the mitochondrial membrane was depolarized. MMP was imaged using a confocal microscope (Olympus Fluoview FV3000, Olympus, Japan).


### Measurement of intracellular iron
  The intracellular iron content in HaCaT cells was determined using FerroOrange to detect and measure intracellular ferrous iron (Fe2+). Briefly, cells were seeded in a 6-well plate, pre-washed with serum-free medium, and incubated with the probe in the dark for 30 min. An Olympus Fluoview FV3000 fluorescence microscope (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan) was used to detect intracellular iron.


### Lipid peroxidation assay
  C11–BODIPY is a fluorescent probe primarily used to detect lipid peroxidation in cell membranes. Cells were seeded in 6-well plates and labeled with C11-BODIPY and incubated at 37 °C for 30 min. Lipid peroxidation was quantitatively detected and analyzed using an Olympus Fluoview FV3000 microscope (Olympus).


### Malondialdehyde (MDA) assay
  The cellular or tissue concentration of malondialdehyde was determined using an MDA assay kit (Elabscience, USA), following the manufacturer’s instructions. The assay involved the interpretation of test values with standard MDA dilution values by measuring the absorbance at 695–700 nm.


### Assessment of GSH/GSSG ratio
  The ratio of reduced glutathione (GSH) to oxidized glutathione (GSSG) was estimated using a GSSG assay kit (#HY-K0311; MedChem Express, Princeton, NJ, USA). This assay is based on the formation of a chromogenic product, 5-thio-2-nitrobenzoic acid, from the substrate 5,5′-dithio-bis (2-nitrobenzoic acid). Absorbance was measured at 412 nm, which is directly correlated with the volume of GSH in the sample.


### Western blotting
  For immunoblotting, total protein was extracted from HaCaT cells using RIPA lysis buffer (G Biosciences, USA) containing protease and phosphatase inhibitors, and the concentration of the extracted protein was determined using the Bradford method. The prepared lysate was subjected to SDS-PAGE and subsequently transferred onto a polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membrane (Immobilon transfer membrane, Merck Millipore, Ireland) using a wet transfer system. To minimize nonspecific antibody binding, the membranes were blocked with 10% BSA for 2 h at room temperature. The blots were washed with TBST and incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies specific for JAK-2, phospho-JAK2, STAT-3, phospho-STAT3, GPX4, TfR, and ACSL4. After primary antibody incubation, the membranes were washed twice with TBST and incubated with an HRP goat anti-rabbit IgG secondary antibody (1:10000) (#AS014) for 2h at room temperature, with gentle shaking. The blots were washed twice with TBST and once with TBS to remove unbound antibodies and reduce nonspecific background signals. Finally, the blots were developed using a sensitive chemiluminescence (ECL) solution (ABclonal, USA), and a Chemidoc (VILBER) system was used for image acquisition.


### Quantitative RT-PCR analysis
  Total RNA was extracted from HaCaT cells and mouse skin tissue excised from three animals per group using TRIzol (RNAiso Plus, Takara A) and reverse-transcribed into cDNA using the Bio-Rad iScript cDNA synthesis kit (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA). For qRT-PCR, the EvaGreen Supermix PCR kit (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA) was used for the GPX4, TfR, ACSL4, CYR61, VEGF, and HMGB1 primers (Table 2A and B). The thermal cycling protocol involved an initial denaturation step (95 °C for 15 min), followed by 40 cycles of denaturation (94 °C for 15 s), annealing (60 °C for 30 s), and extension (72 °C for 30 s). Each sample was analyzed in triplicate to ensure reliable results. Relative gene expression levels were determined by normalizing to GAPDH using the established 2− ΔΔCt method.


### Immunofluorescence
  Cells cultured on sterile coverslips were fixed with 2% paraformaldehyde and permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100 in PBS for 15 min. To reduce nonspecific antibody binding, the cells were blocked for 1 h with 1% goat serum (CST, Danvers, MA, US). Cells were incubated with primary antibodies against phospho-STAT3 (#AP0705), Cyr61 (#A1111), HMGB1 (#A19529), and VEGF (#A21647) overnight at 4 °C. The primary antibody was diluted (1:50–1:200) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. After washing with PBST, secondary antibodies, including Alexa Fluor 555 and FITC-conjugated conjugates, were added for 1 h at room temperature. Hoechst staining was used to counterstain the nuclei, and the coverslips were mounted. Images were acquired and visualized using an EVOS M5000 fluorescence microscope. The corresponding mean fluorescence intensity was calculated using Image J.


### Animal and ethical provisions
  The animal experimental protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Vellore Institute of Technology (VIT), Vellore, India (Reg. No.: VIT/IAEC/27/Sep 24/03). BALB/c mice (4–6 weeks old, 20 ± 2 g, of either sex) were obtained from the animal facility of the Vellore Institute of Technology. BALB/c mice were chosen because of their improved sensitivity to the psoriasiform model used in the current study. After two weeks of acclimatization, the mice were kept in polypropylene cages at 25 ± 2 °C under a 12-hour light/12-hour dark cycle with access to pathogen-free rodent feed and water. At the end of the experimental timeline from January to February 2025, the animals were euthanized by CO2 inhalation, filling 50% of the chamber volume per minute. Care was taken to perform this in the most humane way possible, minimizing stress, fear, or pain to the animals. All experimental procedures were approved by the Institutional Animal Ethical Committee (IAEC) and strictly adhered to the CPCSEA guidelines.


### Psoriasis establishment and treatment regime
  To induce psoriasis, the dorsal portion of BALB/c mice was shaved with an electric clipper, followed by the application of an epilator cream under anesthesia. Anesthesia was induced using an isoflurane respirator, maintaining an inhalational anesthetic concentration of 3–5%. The experimental mice were randomly divided into five groups (n = 6/group, fixed-simple randomization to eliminate selection bias), and a daily topical application of IMQ cream (62.5 mg/kg body weight) was administered to the shaved area for seven consecutive days. Group I: The healthy, non-psoriatic group was designated as the control. Group II: Psoriasis control group consisting of mice that received IMQ induction but no therapeutic intervention. Group III: To evaluate the therapeutic potential of Majoon Ushba, a separate group of psoriatic mice was orally administered 1000 mg/kg body weight of the formulation, based on previous studies (Ganesan et al. 2016). Group IV: To assess the therapeutic potential of Majoon Ushba cream, a separate group of psoriatic mice received daily topical application of the cream. Group V: As a standard drug control, a reference group of psoriatic mice was treated with 1.15% derobin. Treatment was performed daily for 6 h after IMQ induction. Psoriasis progression was observed periodically. Furthermore, severe loss of body weight, lethargy, dehydration, and loss of righting reflex were observed at regular intervals throughout the course of the study as humane endpoints. Briefly, a 20% reduction in body weight from baseline (recorded prior to the induction) was considered a critical humane endpoint. However, all experimental animals displayed disease progression and behavioral patterns that did not warrant elimination from the study design. Notably, prior care was also taken to eliminate potential confounding factors, including diet, environmental factors, and treatment regimen, that may inadvertently influence outcome measures.


### Psoriasis area and severity index (PASI) scoring
  To assess the severity of psoriasiform inflammation, visual examinations and assessments were performed in a double-blinded manner by research personnel unaware of the experimental groupings or treatment to score the experimental animals (n = 6 per group) on a scale of 0–4 based on disease severity. Briefly, the experimental mice were marked on the tail with markers of different colors, and distinct codes were assigned to each color-marked mouse in a cage to prevent observer bias. The following parameters, including erythema, scaling, and skin thickness, were evaluated from day 0 to day 7 prior to assigning individual scores. Subsequently, the cumulative PASI was calculated as the sum of the individual assessments on a scale of 0–12. The scoring parameters were based on the extent of the disease: 0 = none; 1 = slight; 2 = moderate; 3 = marked; and 4 = very marked.


### Histopathology and immunostaining studies
  Skin samples from the psoriatic regions were collected after euthanasia on day 7 and preserved in 10% formalin, followed by decalcification for 2–3 weeks in 10% EDTA. After fixation, the skin samples were embedded in paraffin and cut into 5-µm-thick sections using a microtome. Skin sections were stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) to assess tissue morphology. Histopathological evaluation was performed in a blinded manner using a standardized semiquantitative scoring system (scale 0–5) to assess key pathological features, such as epidermal thickness and immune cell infiltration. The histopathological sections were labeled with the adjacent coding for each mouse before scoring.

  For immunohistochemistry, the sectioned tissues were deparaffinized in xylene, dehydrated using graded ethanol concentrations, and incubated in 0.3% hydrogen peroxide. For antigen retrieval, the slides were immersed in a retrieval solution at 92 °C for 20 min. Tissues were then blocked for 1 h with 5% normal goat serum at room temperature for 2 h, followed by overnight incubation with primary antibodies against CYR61, HMGB1, and VEGF. After washing, the slides were incubated for 2 h with an anti-rabbit HRP-conjugated secondary antibody at room temperature. Stained sections were developed using the ImmPACT DAB Substrate Kit Peroxidase (HRP) (#SK-4105) (VectorLabs, Newark, CA) and counterstained with Mayer’s hematoxylin solution (#51275) (Sigma-Aldrich). After development and counterstaining, sections were dehydrated and mounted with DPX (dibutyl phthalate polystyrene xylene) (#06522) (Sigma-Aldrich). All slides were photographed under a photomicroscope at 40 × magnification (Tokyo, Japan). Subsequently, the stained images (n = 6 per group) were quantified using ImageJ (Fiji) with the image deconvolution option to isolate the DAB signal and interpret the mean gray value (χ). The mean gray value was then converted to reciprocal staining intensity (RSI) using the formula 255- χ to represent intensity levels on a linear scale.


### Assessment of inflammatory cytokines profile
  Serum was isolated from whole blood samples of experimental mice, euthanized on day 7 (at the end of the psoriasis experimental timeline), and used to assess the concentrations of pro-inflammatory cytokines, including IFN-γ, IL-17, and IL-23, using ELISA, following the manufacturer’s instructions.


### Molecular docking
  Based on previous HPLC results (Ganesan et al. 2016), the key phytoconstituents of Majoon Ushba were identified (Table 3) for molecular docking of IL-17RA and STAT-3. Prior to docking, all water and ligand molecules present in the IL-17RA structure were removed from the structure. Initially, the grid was constructed using AutoDock Tools, targeting the residues for IL-17RA [Asp121 to Glu125, Ser257 to Asp262, Thr163 to Ser167] and STAT-3 [Ile597, Leu607, Thr622, Ile634]. Active residues were selected based on previous studies. Subsequently, molecular docking was performed using GNINA (McNutt et al. 2021)– an improved version of Autodock Vina (Trott and Olson 2010). Hydrogen atoms were added to the ligands using GNINA based on custom settings. Docking was performed using site-specific grid parameters generated from Auto Dock (for IL-17RA, X_center, Y_center, Z_Center: −39.185, 40.256, −35.598; for STAT-3, X_center, Y_center, Z_Center: 22.087, 13.729, −38.148), with a default exhaustiveness of eight. Furthermore, the top nine positions were identified by docking. The best-docked binding pose of the ligand with respect to the proteins was characterized for interactions between the protein and ligand using a Protein-Ligand Interaction Profiler (Salentin et al. 2015). The entire workflow of docking with GNINA to Protein-ligand interaction profiler was performed using ScientiFlow – Scientific workflow automation tool (ScientiFlow: scientiFlow: scientific Workflow Automation Platform, 2015n.d). Finally, stable protein-ligand complexes were processed using Biovia Discovery Studio to visualize their interactions.


### Phytochemical fingerprinting
  The polyherbal formulation of Majoon Ushba was characterized using LC-HRMS. Briefly, 100 mg of the dried extract of Majoon Ushba was extracted by ultrasonication in 5 mL of ethanol or water. The extracts were subsequently filtered through a 0.22-μm syringe filter to remove coarse particles and collected in individual vials. The phytochemical compounds present in the aqueous and ethanolic extracts were assessed using Waters Xevo G2-XS-Q ToF High Resolution Mass spectrometer, which takes full advantage of the step wave ion optics, XS collision cell, Quant ToF, ultra-fast electron multiplier, and hybrid ADC detector electronics. An ES ion source in the positive and negative ionization modes was used. The mass parameters were set in the positive mode: source temperature, 120 °C; capillary voltage, 1 kVcone gas flow rate, 10 L h-1; desolvation gas flow rate, 800 L h-1; and injection volume, 1 µL. Mass spectra were detected in the range between m/z 100 to1200. Data acquisition and integration were performed using MassLynx4.2 software.


### Statistical analysis
  Results are presented as the mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM) from three independent replicates. Statistical comparisons between the experimental groups were conducted using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), followed by Bonferroni’s multiple comparison post hoc test. Prior to analysis of the datasets, potential outliers were assessed via the ROUT function (Q = 1%), with zero outliers detected across all experimental groupings; all original datapoints were retained for analysis. Normality was verified using the Shapiro–Wilk test; all experimental endpoints followed a Gaussian distribution (p > 0.05). Homogeneity of variances was verified using the Brown–Forsythe test to ensure that standard deviations were not significantly different between the groups (p > 0.05). All analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism (version 8.0.2; GraphPad, MA, USA), with P values < 0.05 indicating statistical significance. Statistical significance is denoted as follows: NS (not significant), *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001.

  Subsequently, for in vivo assessments utilizing IMQ-induced psoriasis mouse models, we performed a priori power analysis, following one-way ANOVA design with an effect size of 0.40 (moderate-to-large effects) and a significance level of α = 0.05, involving six experimental groups. The analysis revealed n = 6 mice per group, which provides for a statistical power of 35% to observe any biological differences between the groups. Although this is lower than the ideal 80% power threshold, it remains within an acceptable range for exploratory preclinical findings and corroborates previous findings using similar models and endpoints. Furthermore, for qualitative scoring systems, such as the PASI scoring and histopathological assessments, weighted Cohen’s kappa (κw) value was calculated to measure inter-rater variability. In subsequent experiments representing in vivo findings, data are presented as the mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM) of six independent biological replicates (n = 6 mice). While each dot represents one mouse, the experiments were performed in technical replicates for each animal before averaging to yield a single value.


## Results

### Majoon Ushba alleviates the IL-17A/JAK-2/STAT-3 signaling axis to prevent the hyperproliferative phenotype of HaCaT cells
  To investigate the pharmacological activity of Majoon Ushba in altering the IL-17A-sensitized JAK-2-STAT-3 signaling axis, we performed relative protein assessments in HaCaT cells. Prior to treating the cells with Majoon Ushba, the cytotoxicity of the polyherbal formulation was evaluated in IMQ-induced psoriatic keratinocytes, and the optimal in vitro dosage was assessed to be 200 μg/mL (Supplementary Figure 1B). In-vitro treatment with Majoon Ushba reversed the expression profile of JAK-2 and STAT-3 pathway proteins, effectively preventing the phosphorylation and nuclear translocation of STAT-3, similar to its inhibitor S3I-201 (Figure 1(A, B)). Treatment with Majoon Ushba or blockade of IL-17A sensitized STAT-3 signaling axis also regulated the hyperproliferative phenotype of HaCaT cells (Figure 1(C)).


### Majoon Ushba restores GPX4 antioxidant machinery and mitochondrial membrane potential
  To assess whether Majoon Ushba diminished the classical features of keratinocyte ferroptosis, we used appropriate markers. As indicated by earlier reports, Majoon Ushba prevents the accumulation of intracellular reactive oxygen species. Similarly, treatment with the STAT-3 inhibitor S3I-201 regulated intracellular ROS levels (Figure 2(A)). Mitochondrial membrane potential was assessed, as hyperpolarization of the mitochondrial membrane is a consequence of increased ROS accumulation. As expected, Majoon Ushba restored the mitochondrial membrane potential, demonstrating trends similar to those with S3I-201 (Figure 2(B)). Subsequently, the relative mRNA and protein expression of the compromised antioxidant machinery in ferroptosis, GPX4, was assessed. Surprisingly, Majoon Ushba successfully improved the GPX4 antioxidant machinery, effectively contributing to enhanced ROS quenching activity (Figure 2(C, D)). Similarly, treatment with Majoon Ushba improved the ratio of reduced glutathione to oxidized glutathione (GSH: GSSG), indicating a restored cellular redox status. Notably, S3I-201 showed a similar pattern of influence on the expression profile of GPX4 and redox status, implying that STAT-3 inhibition is a potential cue (Figure 2(E)). Intriguingly, we performed similar investigations after silencing of the IL-17R gene (Supplementary Figure 1C) to substantiate the implications of IL-17A cytokine or IL-17A signaling inhibition in restoring intracellular ROS levels and mitochondrial membrane potential. As expected, silencing of IL-17R regulated the levels of intracellular ROS and mitochondrial membrane potential, concurring with the treatment timeline for Majoon Ushba (Figure 2(F, G)).

  Previously, the concentration of IL-17A adjudged within in vitro experimental conditions for ferroptosis readouts was determined using a concentration-dependent study of IL-17A cytokine stimulation for 24 h, followed by MDA estimation. These assessments revealed that IL-17A could provide a significantly measurable response at 100 ng mL−1 (Supplementary Figure 2A).


### Majoon Ushba desensitizes cells to iron overloading and lipid peroxidation
  Another aspect of keratinocyte ferroptosis is iron overload and subsequent lipid peroxidation. Treatment with Majoon Ushba abrogated the expression of the transferrin receptor (TfR) (Figure 3(A, B)), and acyl-CoA synthetase long chain family member 4 (ACSL4) (Supplementary Figure.2B-C) at the gene and protein levels. Briefly, the raw data of protein expression analysis by western blotting are provided as Supplementary Figure 4. Treatment with S3I-201 also yielded similar results, underscoring the implications of the IL-17A/JAK-2-STAT-3 signaling pathway in orchestrating intracellular iron trafficking via the transferrin receptor (Figure 3(A, B)). Subsequently, treatment with Majoon Ushba and S3I-201 restored intracellular iron status and diminished lipid peroxidation (Figure 3(C, D)), while also regulating the expression of malonaldehyde (MDA), a by-product of lipid peroxidation (Figure 3(E)). Concurrently, silencing of IL-17R indicated diminished iron overload and lipid peroxidation (Figure 3(F, G)).

  In parallel, to override the specificity of S3I-201 for STAT-3 inhibition, the assessment of these parameters was performed upon STAT-3 knockdown (Supplementary Figure 3A). STAT-3 knockdown attenuated the formation of colonies characteristic of HaCaT cells (Supplementary Figure 3B). Similarly, downstream assessments for relative ferroptosis readouts were measured using ferrostatin-1, a canonical ferroptosis inhibitor. Treatment with ferrostatin alleviated the gene expression of GPX4 and TfR, similar to that upon STAT-3 knockdown (Supplementary Figure 3C-D). STAT-3 gene silencing and ferroptosis treatment also reduced intracellular ROS levels and mitochondrial membrane potential (Supplementary Figure 3E-F). Concurrently, the estimation of the ratio of reduced glutathione to oxidized glutathione suggested a similar trend for both treatments (Supplementary Figure 3G). Attenuation of ferroptosis also appeared to alleviate lipid peroxidation status and intracellular labile iron pool (Supplementary Figure 3H-I). The levels of MDA, an end-product of lipid metabolism, were also attenuated upon STAT-3 knockdown or ferroptosis inhibition (Supplementary Figure 3J).


### Majoon Ushba impedes the inflammatory microenvironment and expression of psoriasis pathogenic mediators
  Psoriasiform inflammation is driven by excessive production of inflammatory cytokines, release of DAMP molecules, and aberrant expression of pathogenic proteins. To investigate whether Majoon Ushba could alter the expression profile of prominent pathogenic markers Cyr61, VEGF, and DAMP protein HMGB1, we performed relative gene and protein assessments. Interestingly, treatment with Majoon Ushba diminished the expression of Cyr61, HMGB1, and VEGF, following a pattern similar to that exhibited by S3I-201 treatment at both genes (Figure 4(A–C)) and protein levels (Figure 4(D–F)).


### Majoon Ushba alleviates IMQ-induced psoriasiform inflammation in an animal model
  To investigate the pre-clinical efficacy of Majoon Ushba, we employed a popular in-vivo model to assess psoriasis disease severity, including topical application of IMQ, a TLR7/9 agonist, on the shaved backs of mice on the indicated days (Figure 5(A)). Furthermore, we considered formulating a topical dose of Majoon Ushba in a PEG-cream base to assess the impact of topical application over oral administration. Subsequently, 3% Majoon Ushba was constituted in a PEG cream base (Table 4). The preclinical efficacy of Majoon Ushba was scored in comparison to that of derobin 1.15%, a topical formulation comprising coal tar and salicylic acid in a cream base. It should be noted that the prevailing findings occurred from a smaller sample size (n = 6/group) and could be interpreted merely as preliminary findings. However, a larger cohort with strong power could enable statistically rigorous readouts.

  Briefly, skin images were captured on the indicated days, demonstrating diminished physical features of psoriasis, including scaly skin and erythema, upon treatment with Majoon Ushba. Notably, topical application of Majoon Ushba enhanced the preclinical efficacy against oral treatment. Similarly, preliminary validation suggested that the topical formulation of Majoon Ushba fared in terms of efficacy similar to that of derobin 1.15% w/w (Figure 5(B)). The application of IMQ resulted in a significant reduction in body weight, indicating disease establishment. However, administration of Majoon Ushba or derobin 1.15% w/w rescued significant changes in body weight from baseline measurements. The percentage change in body weight also served as a humane endpoint observation for the current study (Figure 5(C)). Subsequently, the experimental mice were qualitatively scored on a scale of 0–4 for features, including erythema, scaling, and skin thickness, by two independent researchers in a double-blind fashion. The combined scores of the three parameters were then summarized in a final PASI assessment. The cumulative assessments demonstrated a similar efficacy to that observed earlier upon physical imaging (weighted Cohen’s kappa [κw] = 0.95; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.89–1) (Figure 5(D)). Splenomegaly is often associated with increased immune cell involvement in systemic diseases. We assessed spleen weight in experimental mice to determine whether Majoon Ushba also exerts immune-modifying effects. Our assessments indicated a decrease in relative spleen weight following treatment with Majoon Ushba, suggestive of diminished splenomegaly, aligning with the effects seen with derobin 1.15% (Figure 5(E, F)). Furthermore, we measured the levels of inflammatory cytokines, including IL-17, IL-23, and IFN-γ, in the collected serum samples. Majoon Ushba treatment appeared to attenuate the expression of these cytokines (Figure 5(G–I)).

  Subsequently, treatment with Majoon Ushba was associated with a reduction in skin thickness. Furthermore, histological scoring performed in a double-blinded manner assessed acanthosis, parakeratosis, and cellular infiltration on a scale of 0–5, suggesting the pre-clinical efficacy of Majoon Ushba. The topical formulation of Majoon Ushba showed pronounced efficacy relative to the oral form of the drug, while showing comparable efficacy against derobin (weighted Cohen’s Kappa (κw)=0.84; 95% CI: 0.72–0.96) (Figure 5(J–L)). However, these findings require further validation in larger cohorts.


### Majoon Ushba impedes the JAK-2-STAT-3 signaling axis and regulates the expression of psoriasis-related mediators in an experimental animal model
  To corroborate our in vitro assessments, we performed relative protein assessments of JAK-2 and STAT-3 signaling pathway proteins in the skin of experimental mice, within the constraints of our current sample size. Consistent with our observations, treatment with Majoon Ushba was associated with a reduction in the phosphorylation of JAK-2 and STAT-3 pathway proteins, suggesting an attenuation of the IL-17A signaling axis (Figure 6(A)). Furthermore, our immunohistochemical assessments yielded a measurable reduction in the proliferation of epidermal keratinocytes upon treatment with Majoon Ushba. This finding also paralleled the altered phosphorylation status of STAT-3 in the lesional skin of psoriatic mice, when treatment with Majoon Ushba was associated with a decrease in the expression of p-STAT-3 (Figure 6(B)). In line with these findings, relative gene expression assessments suggested a reduction in the expression of key pathogenic mediators of psoriasis–Cyr61, HMGB1, and VEGF (Figure 6(C–E)).


### Key phytoconstituents of Majoon Ushba show antagonistic properties against IL-17R and STAT-3
  Based on our prior high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC)-based analytical assessment of Majoon Ushba, we identified four phenolic acids: gallic, p-coumaric, vanillic, and ferulic acids, and three flavonoids: catechin, morin, and quercetin (Table 3). The preliminary findings inspired us to strategize in silico docking of the identified compounds against IL-17R (Figure 7(A–C)) and STAT-3 protein targets (Figure 7(D–F)). Our docking analysis revealed excellent binding affinities for catechin, morin, and quercetin against IL-17R and STAT-3 (Table 5).

  Furthermore, to strengthen the characterization of the polyherbal formulation, we performed LC-HRMS-based characterization of the aqueous (Figure 8) and ethanolic extracts (Figure 9), which revealed a significant range of bioactive compounds, suggesting the pharmacological efficacy of Majoon Ushba (Table 6).


## Discussion
  Majoon Ushba, a polyherbal Unani formulation has been routinely used in clinical settings for the management of skin ailments including psoriasis (Lone et al. 2011). Our previous reports have provided scientific validation of the polyherbal formulation in the management of rheumatoid arthritis, a diarthrodial joint autoimmune disease (Ganesan et al. 2016). Previous studies have also underscored its efficacy in the clinical settings of psoriasis and tinea corporis (Ahmad and Mobeen 2023). However, there is little rationale or scientific validation to support its use in psoriasis management. The lack of scientific validation continues to limit its widespread clinical use. To address this scientific gap, we investigated the implications of Majoon Ushba for psoriasis using in vitro, in vivo, and in silico approaches.

  Targeting the IL-23/IL-17 axis is a popular approach that has yielded remarkable results in psoriasis remission in the past. However, targeted biologics against IL-17A are associated with serious adverse events and secondary inefficacies, resulting in poor patient compliance and increased dropout rates (Alunno et al. 2015). Thus, several small-molecule inhibitors targeting IL-17RA have been developed to circumvent the limitations of existing treatment modalities. Our laboratory has, to date, validated two small-molecule inhibitors against IL-17RA for psoriasis resolution: diindolylmethane and cyanidin (Patent No:566404). Our previous report also identified the JAK-2-STAT-3 signaling axis of IL-17A as a promoter of the expression of key pathogenic mediators in psoriasis, including Cyr61, VEGF, DAMP protein-HMGB1.The IL-17A-JAK-2/STAT-3 signaling axis promotes keratinocyte hyperproliferation and contributes to increased skin thickness in IMQ-induced animal models of psoriasis (Srikanth and Rasool 2022).

  Impaired keratinocyte turnover and dysregulated homeostasis are key features of psoriasis. Several treatment modalities have targeted the impaired keratinocyte machinery to alleviate psoriasiform inflammation (Ortiz-Lopez et al. 2022; Benezeder et al. 2020). Recently, keratinocyte ferroptosis has been identified as a pivotal player in sustaining the autoimmune loop in psoriasis (Shou et al. 2021; Vats et al. 2021). This led us to speculate that Majoon Ushba could hinder the IL-17A-JAK-2/STAT-3 signaling axis to counter keratinocyte ferroptosis and prevent the expression of key inflammatory mediators.

  Our preliminary in vitro assessments revealed that Majoon Ushba alleviated the phosphorylation of Janus kinase (JAK)-2 and STAT-3 pathway proteins, which are otherwise activated upon IL-17A stimulation. Treatment with Majoon Ushba also regulated the proliferative phenotype of human epidermal keratinocytes and HaCaT cells. The in vitro treatment efficacy was similar to that observed upon inhibition of STAT-3 using S3I-201. These findings prompted us to further investigate the therapeutic ramifications of Majoon Ushba downstream of IL-17A signaling with reference to the JAK-2-STAT-3 signaling axis.

  As we intended to delve further, we understood that IL-17A cytokine stimulation diminished the antioxidant activity of glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4) via STAT-3 activation. Therapeutic blockade of STAT-3 activation restored GPX4 expression and alleviated intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) accumulation. Interestingly, our in-vitro observations with S3I-201 were comparable to the efficacy of Majoon Ushba. These findings concur with those of previous studies reporting that STAT-3 blockade restores GPX4 expression (Zhang et al. 2024; Zhao et al. 2022). Subsequently, Majoon Ushba improved the ratio of reduced glutathione to oxidized glutathione, restoring cellular redox status. An increase in labile iron concentration and lipid peroxidation is a critical hallmark of ferroptosis sensitivity. To understand the implications of IL-17A/STAT-3 signaling in this aspect, we measured the levels of intracellular iron and transferrin receptor expression. Prior reports have suggested excessive accumulation of iron in psoriatic lesions with a corresponding decline in blood samples. However, the exact reason or pathological implications underlying iron overloading in psoriatic lesions remain unknown. Our initial results demonstrated excessive accumulation of labile iron, with a corresponding increase in lipid peroxidation. Concurrently, the expression of the transferrin receptor was also found to be highly elevated after IL-17A stimulation. Interestingly, S3I-201 dramatically reduced the expression of the transferrin receptor and downregulated labile iron levels, underscoring the role of STAT-3 activation in negatively influencing iron homeostasis. Notably, STAT-3 activation improves labile iron levels by promoting NCOA4-dependant ferritinophagy (Zhang et al. 2024). Similarly, treatment with Majoon Ushba diminished intracellular iron levels and transferrin receptor expression, while preventing excessive lipid peroxidation. The in-vitro findings using the HaCaT cell line strongly suggest the role of the IL-17A/JAK-2-STAT-3 signaling axis in orchestrating ferroptosis sensitivity via impaired antioxidant expression, heightened iron accumulation, and lipid peroxidation. Treatment with Majoon Ushba also decreased the expression of malonaldehyde (MDA), suggesting the therapeutic efficacy of this polyherbal formulation in limiting lipid peroxidation. To further reaffirm our study hypothesis and pathway validation, STAT-3 knockdown and ferroptosis pathway inhibitor were used to assess critical ferroptosis readouts. Interestingly, treatment with ferrostain-1 and STAT-3 knockdown demonstrated a measurable improvement in alleviating lipid peroxidation, ROS accumulation, and intracellular iron pools. The findings concur with early revelations linking the JAK-2-STAT-3 pathway to promoting ferroptosis in non-tumors. Precisely, STAT-3 activation has been shown to hamper iron homeostasis via the NCOA4/FTH1 axis in obesity induced cardiac injury (Zhu et al. 2023; Xie et al. 2025). The findings emphasize the pathological implication of STAT-3 activation in psoriasis disease severity.

  To further demonstrate the therapeutic efficacy of Majoon Ushba, we investigated whether the polyherbal formulation could alleviate the expression of psoriasis pathogenic mediators, Cyr61, VEGF, and HMGB1. We have previously demonstrated the role of the JAK-2-STAT-3 signaling axis in promoting the expression of these mediators upon IL-17A cytokine signaling. Cysteine-rich angiogenic inducer 61 (Cyr61) promotes IL-8 production and neutrophil recruitment, exacerbating psoriasiform features. Pro-inflammatory proteins released by keratinocytes also trigger epidermal hyperplasia (Wu et al. 2017). In addition to Cyr61, vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) facilitates autocrine regulation of epidermal hyperplasia. VEGF is a key link between inflammation and angiogenesis in psoriasis (Gong et al. 2020). High Mobility Group Box protein 1 (HMGB1) is a DAMP protein released from stressed keratinocytes that facilitates immune cell activation and Th17 cell differentiation (Zhu et al. 2024). Collectively, targeting these pathogenic mediators has been shown to resolve psoriasiform inflammation and rescue disease progression. Our results demonstrate that Majoon Ushba effectively diminished the expression of Cyr61, VEGF, and HMGB1, further reinforcing its therapeutic implications in psoriasis.

  The in-vitro results provide a compelling basis for exploring the therapeutic potential of Majoon Ushba in an experimental psoriasis model. We employed a widely recognized IMQ-induced psoriasis model to examine the effects of this polyherbal formulation on psoriasis. We formulated Majoon Ushba as a cream for topical application. The development of a 3% topical formulation of Majoon Ushba could be attributed to the improved clinical efficacy of topical formulations in psoriasis. Recent research has thrown spotlight on improved pre-clinical outcomes for topical formulations in an IMQ-induced psoriasis mice model (Abbas et al., 2025; Ali et al. 2025). Furthermore, Majoon Ushba was employed in its original form and administered orally to a different cohort. The preclinical efficacy of the drug was compared with that of the topical application of derobin 1.15%, which contains dithranol, coal tar, and salicylic acid. Our in vivo validation demonstrated the significant therapeutic efficacy of Majoon Ushba, which effectively mitigated the characteristics of psoriasiform inflammation, including erythema, scaling, and increased skin thickness. Qualitative analysis also indicated reduced PASI scores for Majoon Ushba. Notably, the topical application of Majoon Ushba was comparable to that of derobin 1.15%. Furthermore, treatment with Majoon Ushba reduced splenomegaly, suggesting its systemic effect in restoring immune homeostasis. Indeed, the topical formulation of Majoon Ushba effectively alleviated the expression of key inflammatory cytokines including IFN-γ, IL-17 and IL-23, demonstrating the pre-clinical efficacy of the topical application, suggesting the vitality of topical formulations, in line with previous reports (Alaa Hamza Abbas ZMHMAAQAZHR-SHAMAMAAWJAAKRAJHA-AZHASKHMHA-T 2025; Farooq Ali et al., 2025). Notably, treatment with the polyherbal formulation also limited the risk of adverse events, thus providing greater flexibility over drug dosage.

  Subsequently, the expression of psoriasis pathogenic mediators in mouse skin sections was evaluated using qPCR. The levels of Cyr61, VEGF, and HMGB1 were reduced. Additionally, Majoon Ushba mitigated the phosphorylation of JAK-2 and STAT-3, thereby inhibiting their expression in psoriatic skin lesions. Majoon Ushba also decreased keratinocyte proliferation, leading to reduced epidermal thickness. Briefly, the study also envisages the idea of utilizing different molecular signatures investigated herein, including GPX4, p-STAT-3, and intracellular iron levels, as reliable readouts to assess treatment endpoints and outcome measures. For instance, elevated p-STAT-3 levels serve as a reliable biomarker for disease severity in psoriasis as it drives keratinocyte hyperproliferation and dictates an inflammatory milieu in the skin (Jiang et al. 2015). Interestingly, intracellular iron levels in keratinocytes also aggravate psoriasis inflammation. The mechanism of which was obscure until recently could be attributed to its role in keratinocyte ferroptosis. Alternatively, the serum concentration of hepcidin appears to be low in psoriasis patients relative to skin lesions. Thus, intra-lesional iron level and serum hepcidin levels can be rationalized as potential biomarkers in psoriasis (Ponikowska et al. 2015; Zhang et al. 2025).

  To further enhance the scientific rationale in this area, following up on our findings, we conducted docking studies targeting IL-17R and STAT-3 using the principal phytoconstituents of Majoon Ushba, as identified in our previous reports (Table 3). The in-silico docking analyses revealed that Catechin, Morin, and Quercetin exhibit significant binding affinities for IL-17R and STAT-3 (Table 5). These findings supplement the earlier research suggesting that the pharmacological effects of Majoon Ushba may be mediated through the JAK-2-STAT-3 signaling pathway. Nevertheless, the docking evaluations only indicate a tentative link to the pathway and should not be overly emphasized for their direct pharmacological significance to the potential bioactive compound.


## Conclusions
  The present study elucidates a plausible mechanism underlying the pharmacological action of Majoon Ushba, specifically through the IL-17A/JAK-2-STAT-3 signaling pathway in keratinocytes, which improves the expression of ferroptosis-associated markers by modulating iron overload and excessive accumulation of intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS). However, the current study has certain limitations that may warrant caution regarding its translational implications. First, the IMQ-induced psoriasis mouse model, which is popularly used to mimic psoriasis, may not completely recapitulate human psoriasiform inflammation. The prevailing limitation endorses the need for clinical validation of Majoon Ushba in clinical settings. In particular, the IMQ-induced psoriasis model, although routinely used for the preclinical validation of small molecules, largely involves an innate immune response pinpointing TLR7-/8 activation, which tends to resolve after discontinuation of IMQ application. This draws attention to the immune phenotype of human psoriasis, that evolves around adaptive immune responses of Th17 and Th1 cells. and constantly persisting under favorable conditions (Abbas et al., 2025). Thus, the efficacy of Majoon Ushba demonstrated in the study herein needs to be carefully weighed against the backdrop of model limitations. Subsequently, while the pre-clinical efficacy of Majoon Ushba is demonstrated upon various assessments, the study fails to measure ferroptosis readouts in mice models. For instance, the lack of therapeutic cross-profiling for Majoon Ushba against ferrostatin-1-treated mice limits the scope of in vivo findings as purely compensatory relative to in vitro validation.

  Additionally, this study identified catechin, morin, and quercetin as candidate molecules that could potentially contribute to the pharmacological efficacy of Majoon Ushba. The identification of these compounds was based on prior high-resolution liquid chromatography (HPLC) analysis, which helps to qualitatively assess key phytoconstituents. Thus, the in-silico docking analysis of the three compounds with IL-17RA and STAT-3 is purely speculative and lends credence to our hypothesis. Additional LC-HRMS-based characterization of the aqueous and ethanolic extracts enables the ascertainment of the degree of standardization across different batches of the polyherbal formulation and supports reproducibility for future studies on Majoon Ushba. However, it should be noted that complex polyherbal formulations could include several bioactive compounds that provide synergistic or antagonistic interactions.

  Overall, this study unveils previously unknown mechanistic implications for Majoon Ushba in treating psoriasis by using human epidermal keratinocytes (HaCaT cell line) and preclinical disease models. However, we stress that the modest power of this study could benefit from larger clinical trials in psoriasis patient cohorts.
