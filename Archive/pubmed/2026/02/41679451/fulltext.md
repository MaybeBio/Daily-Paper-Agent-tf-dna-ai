Acetamiprid (Ace) is a known toxic environmental pollutant. However, its immunotoxicity remains inadequately characterized. This study used zebrafish investigate the innate immunotoxicity and its underlying mechanisms. In this study, molecular docking and thermal shift experiments provided preliminary evidence suggesting the potential agonistic activity of Ace against Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4). To further confirm this conjecture, this work analyzed the changes of immunotoxicity-related indexes in zebrafish stressed by Ace at individual, protein, and gene levels. The results showed that Ace altered the phenotypic characteristics of immunocytes, increased NADPH oxidase activity, induced oxidative stress, and triggered mitochondrial pathway-mediated apoptosis in immunocytes. Additionally, it significantly altered the expression of key proteins and genes involved in the TLR4/MYD88/NF-kappaB and JAK-STAT signaling pathways. Finally, the addition of a TLR4 inhibitor effectively mitigated these adverse effects, highlighting the crucial role of TLR4/MYD88/NF-kappaB signaling and its crosstalk with the JAK-STAT pathway in Ace-induced immunotoxicity. Overall, the evidence from this study reveals a new mechanism of immunotoxicity induced by environmental concentrations of Ace, providing critical insights into the ecological and health risks of Ace.
