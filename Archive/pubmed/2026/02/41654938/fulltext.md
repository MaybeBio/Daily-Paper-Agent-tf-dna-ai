# TFAP2A transcriptionally regulates B3GALT2 to affect gouty arthritis progression through pyroptosis: a study based on machine learning and multi-omics integration analysis


## Background
  Gouty arthritis (GA) is an inflammatory joint disease driven by monosodium urate (MSU) crystal deposition. The NOD-like receptor thermal protein domain associated protein 3 (NLRP3) inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis is central to GA pathogenesis, yet potential regulatory targets remain limited. The role of β-1,3-galactosyltransferase 2 (B3GALT2), a glycosyltransferase, is entirely unknown in GA. This study aims to identify novel biomarkers for GA and investigate the role and regulatory mechanism of B3GALT2.


## Methods
  Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) analysis was performed on the GSE160170 dataset. Machine learning algorithms, including least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression and random forest (RF), were applied to identify key candidate genes. B3GALT2 expression and its diagnostic value were validated in clinical peripheral blood samples from GA patients and healthy controls. In vitro GA models were established using THP-1 cells stimulated with lipopolysaccharide (LPS) and MSU. Gain- and loss-of-function experiments assessed the effects of B3GALT2 and transcription factor activator protein-2 A (TFAP2A) on pyroptosis. Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP)-qPCR and dual-luciferase reporter assays were used to verify the transcriptional regulation of B3GALT2 by TFAP2A.


## Results
  B3GALT2 was identified as a key down-regulated gene in GA through integrated bioinformatics and machine learning. Clinically, B3GALT2 expression was significantly decreased in GA patients, showed high diagnostic accuracy, and was negatively correlated with inflammatory markers, including erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), C-reactive protein (CRP), and interleukin-6 (IL-6). Functionally, B3GALT2 overexpression in LPS/MSU-induced cell models inhibited NLRP3 inflammasome activation (reducing NLRP3, apoptosis-associated speck-like protein containing a caspase recruitment domain (ASC), caspase1 p20) and pyroptosis (decreasing N-terminal gasdermin-D (GSDMD-N), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) release, and IL-1β/IL-18 secretion). Mechanistically, TFAP2A was predicted and experimentally confirmed to bind directly to the B3GALT2 promoter, activating its transcription. Importantly, the anti-pyroptotic effects of TFAP2A overexpression were largely abolished upon B3GALT2 knockdown.


## Conclusion
  This study identifies B3GALT2 as a promising diagnostic biomarker for GA and further reveals a novel TFAP2A/B3GALT2 axis that plays a critical protective role in GA by suppressing NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis. These findings provide new insights into GA pathogenesis and highlight potential therapeutic targets.

## Introduction
  Gouty arthritis (GA) is an inflammatory joint disorder caused by monosodium urate (MSU) crystal deposition, leading to intense pain, redness and swelling driven by innate immune responses [1], with NOD-like receptor thermal protein domain associated protein 3 (NLRP3) inflammasome activation and subsequent pyroptosis playing a central role in amplifying the inflammatory cascade [2]. Early diagnosis and specific treatments for GA are still limited [3], necessitating new biomarkers and therapeutic targets. In recent years, the lipid rafts in the cell membrane have garnered significant attention as a key platform for inflammatory signal transduction. Lipid rafts are cholesterol- and sphingolipid-rich membrane microdomains that segregate and aggregate various signaling molecules, including Toll-like receptors, thereby efficiently initiating and amplifying intracellular inflammatory signals [4]. Notably, NLRP3 inflammasome assembly and activation are believed to be closely linked to the lipid raft microenvironment. For instance, in autoimmune diseases like antiphospholipid syndrome, antibody-mediated signaling pathways rely on lipid rafts to aggregate receptors, ultimately driving NLRP3 inflammasome activation—a core mechanism underpinning inflammatory cascade amplification [5]. In GA, the inflammatory signals triggered by MSU crystals recognized by macrophages may similarly depend on the lipid raft microenvironment, though the precise regulatory mechanisms remain incompletely elucidated. Pyroptosis, a pro-inflammatory form of programmed cell death, is critically involved in the pathogenesis of GA. It is mediated by the activation of inflammasomes, particularly the NLRP3 inflammasome, which recruits apoptosis-associated speck-like protein containing a caspase recruitment domain (ASC) and activates caspase1. Active caspase1 cleaves both pro-interleukin-1β (IL-1β)/pro-IL-18 into their mature forms and gasdermin D (GSDMD). The N-terminal GSDMD (GSDMD-N) forms pores in the cell membrane, leading to cytokine release, cell swelling, and eventual lysis—a hallmark of pyroptosis [2, 6]. In GA, MSU crystals are potent activators of the NLRP3 inflammasome, making the pyroptotic pathway a key therapeutic target. However, the regulatory mechanisms, especially those involving glycosylation-related genes like β-1,3-galactosyltransferase 2 (B3GALT2), remain largely unexplored. This study aims to investigate whether B3GALT2 affects GA progression by modulating this specific cell death pathway.

  Glycosylation, a key post-translational modification [7], involves enzymes like B3GALT2 [8]. Beyond its canonical role in glycoprotein synthesis, emerging evidence indicates that B3GALT2 participates in critical cellular processes such as inflammation regulation, oxidative stress response, and neuronal survival [9–11], suggesting its potential involvement in signal transduction pathways. Bioinformatics analysis identified B3GALT2 as a potential biomarker, but its role in GA remained unreported.

  Transcription factors like transcription factor activator protein-2 A (TFAP2A) regulate disease processes [12], including neuropathic pain [13] and cancer [14]. Whether TFAP2A regulates B3GALT2 in GA is unknown.

  This study integrated bioinformatics analysis of the GSE160170 dataset with machine learning algorithms, including least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression and random forest (RF), to identify B3GALT2 as a core down-regulated gene in GA. This study validates its diagnostic value, demonstrates its role in inhibiting NLRP3 inflammasome activation and pyroptosis, and reveals that TFAP2A transcriptionally activates B3GALT2, forming a protective axis in GA.


## Methods

### Database analysis
  Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were screened from the GSE160170 dataset, containing primary gout patient (n = 6) and healthy subject (n = 6) peripheral blood mononuclear cells, using the Limma method with false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05 and |fold change|> 1.5.


### Machine learning
  LASSO regression (ten fold cross-validation) and RF model (Mean Decrease Gini) were used for feature selection. The intersection of results identified the key gene.


### Human samples
  Peripheral blood samples were collected from GA patients and age- and sex-matched healthy volunteers at The Second Affiliated Hospital of Henan University of Chinese Medicine. Healthy controls had no history of gout, hyperuricemia, autoimmune diseases, or severe systemic infections. Participants with malignant tumors, severe liver or kidney dysfunction, or recent use of immunosuppressants/anti-inflammatory drugs (within 2 weeks) were excluded. All participants provided written informed consent. This research was approved by the Ethics Committee of The Second Affiliated Hospital of Henan University of Chinese Medicine. ### GA model establishment
  The human monocytic leukemia cell line (THP-1) (Fenghuishengwu, Changsha, China) was maintained in the Roswell Park Memorial Institute-1640 (Procell, Wuhan, China) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (Procell) and 1% penicillin–streptomycin (Procell). THP-1 cells were differentiated using 100 ng/mL phorbol 12-myristate 13-acetate (PMA, Proteintech, Wuhan, China) for 24 h. Subsequently, the cells were treated with lipopolysaccharide (LPS, 500 ng/mL, Solarbio) for 4 h and then stimulated with MSU crystals (200 μg/mL, InvivoGen, Toulouse, France) for 5 h to establish the in vitro GA models (LPS/MSU) based on previous reports [15–17]..


### Flow cytometry
  After treatment, cells were collected, resuspended, and assessed for caspase1 activity and reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels using the FAM FLICA™ Caspase-1 Kit (Bio-rad, Hercules, California, USA) and the C11 BODIPY 581/591 Lipid Peroxidation Fluorescence Probe (ABclonal, Wuhan, China). After staining with propidium iodide (PI) at 37℃ for 5 min or incubated with C11 BODIPY 581/591 for 1 h, cell analysis was conducted using flow cytometry.


### Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) release detection
  LDH release was detected using the LDH Assay Kit (Beyotime). Briefly, cells were treated with LDH release reagent for 1 h. After centrifugation at 400 g for 5 min, the supernatant was collected for detection.


#### Enzyme linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)
  Following the manufacturer’s instructions of the Human IL-18 and Human IL-1β ELISA Kits (Both from Elabscience, Wuhan, China), the concentrations of IL-18 and IL-1β in the cell culture supernatant was determined.


#### Bioinformatics analysis
  The JASPAR (https://jaspar.elixir.no) was used to predict transcription factors and their binding sites within the B3GALT2 promoter sequence.


#### Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP)-qPCR
  B3GALT2 enrichment by TFAP2A was examined using the ChIP Assay Kit (Beyotime). After cross-linking with 1% formaldehyde at 37℃ for 10 min, cells were treated with glycine solution. Subsequently, cells were lysed and sonicated to shear genomic DNA into fragments of approximately 400 bp. Following treatment with Protein A + G Agarose/Salmon Sperm DNA, samples were incubated with anti-TFAP2A (ab108311, Abcam, Cambridge, UK) or control IgG (ab172730, Abcam) at 4℃ overnight. The immune complexes were precipitated using Protein A + G Agarose/Salmon Sperm DNA. Purified DNA was analyzed by qPCR.


#### Dual-luciferase reporter assay
  The B3GALT2 promoter sequences, either wild-type (B1-WT) or containing a mutated TFAP2A binding site 1 (B1-MUT), were cloned into the pGL3-luciferase reporter vector (GeneCreate). Cells were co-transfected with B1-WT or B1-MUT reporter vectors and oeNC or oeTFAP2A for 48 h. Luciferase activity was quantified using the Dual Luciferase Reporter Gene Assay Kit (Beyotime).


#### Statistical analysis
  Data were analyzed and visualized using GraphPad Prism software (GraphPad Prism, San Diego, California, USA). Student’s t test and analysis of variance were used to identify the differences between two or more groups. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Pearson correlation analysis was used to evaluate the correlation between B3GALT2 mRNA levels and erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), C-reactive protein (CRP) and IL-6.

## Discussion
  GA is a severe clinical manifestation of hyperuricemia (HUA), resulting from abnormal uric acid (UA) metabolism, leading to MSU deposition in joints, triggering acute or chronic inflammation [16]. Through integrated bioinformatics analysis and experimental validation, this study systematically reveals, for the first time, the pivotal role of B3GALT2 in GA. It elucidates the molecular mechanism whereby B3GALT2, regulated by the transcription factor TFAP2A, negatively modulates GA inflammation by inhibiting NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis. These findings provide important new insights into the pathological mechanisms of GA and propose a potential diagnostic and therapeutic target.

  Research indicated that the glycosylation-related molecule B3GALT2 exhibited elevated expression in the trigeminal ganglion in LPS-induced rat pulp inflammation models. Its knockdown reduced the expression of inflammatory factors tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) and IL-6, as well as Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4) and nuclear factor kappa-B (NF-κB) [18]. B3GALT2 has been identified as a therapeutic target for ischemic stroke, exerting neuroprotective effects by modulating cerebral ischemia-induced oxidative stress and NLRP3 inflammasome activation [11]. This suggests that B3GALT2 may influence NLRP3 activity through regulating its upstream activators. Bioinformatics analysis also revealed B3GALT2 as a survival predictor in bladder urothelial carcinoma [19] and lung adenocarcinoma [20]. In mitral regurgitation, B3GALT2 was identified as a biomarker associated with immune-related pathways [21]. These studies indicate that B3GALT2 participates in the progression of various diseases. Herein, through comprehensive bioinformatics analysis and clinical sample validation, the value of B3GALT2 as a diagnostic biomarker for GA was confirmed. In this study, data indicated that B3GALT2 mRNA levels were significantly down-regulated in the peripheral blood of GA patients, and ROC curve analysis confirmed its high diagnostic value. More importantly, B3GALT2 expression levels showed significant negative correlations with classic GA inflammatory markers (ESR and CRP) and the key cytokine IL-6 [22]. This suggests that low B3GALT2 expression correlates with increased inflammatory severity in GA, indicating its potential role not only as a diagnostic marker but also as an inhibitory molecule in the GA pathological process.

  This study is the first to link B3GALT2 with NLRP3 inflammasome activation and pyroptosis. Pyroptosis is an inflammatory form of cell death typically triggered by the NLRP3 inflammasome, leading to cellular lysis and cytokine release [6]. In GA pathogenesis, MSU crystals activate the NLRP3 inflammasome, leading to caspase1 activation, pyroptosis, and inflammatory mediator release [23]. In vitro studies demonstrated that B3GALT2 overexpression significantly suppressed the protein expression of NLRP3, ASC, caspase-1 p20, and GSDMD-N, reduced pyroptosis, and decreased the release of LDH, IL-1β, and IL-18. These findings collectively indicate that B3GALT2 deficiency is a key factor in the excessive amplification of inflammation in GA. Restoring B3GALT2 expression effectively blocks this core pyroptosis pathway, mitigating inflammatory responses. These findings position B3GALT2 as a novel negative regulator of NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis in GA. The observed down-regulation of B3GALT2 in GA patients and models may create a permissive environment for unchecked inflammasome assembly. Overexpression of B3GALT2 potently suppressed the entire pyroptotic cascade, from upstream inflammasome components (NLRP3, ASC) to downstream executors (caspase1 activation, GSDMD cleavage) and terminal events (LDH release, IL-1β/IL-18 secretion). This comprehensive inhibition suggests that B3GALT2 acts at a pivotal node upstream of NLRP3 activation. Given its role as a glycosyltransferase, this study hypothesizes that B3GALT2 might modulate the glycosylation state of key proteins involved in inflammasome priming or assembly (such as NLRP3 itself or upstream sensors), thereby altering their stability, interaction, or activity. This potential link between glycosylation and pyroptosis regulation represents a novel and intriguing mechanistic avenue for future research in inflammatory diseases.

  Through investigation into the upstream causes of B3GALT2 dysregulation, TFAP2A was found to play a pivotal role. TFAP2A, highly expressed in multiple cancers [24], promoted proliferation and metastasis by regulating target genes in cancers, including esophageal [25], hepatocellular [26], pancreatic [27], and triple-negative breast cancer [28]. Additionally, Li et al. revealed that TFAP2A also exerted anti-inflammatory effects in cancer [29]. A study suggested that impaired TFAP2A binding to the NRBP1 promoter, leading to elevated NRBP1 expression, might be associated with gout development [30]. This study confirmed that TFAP2A specifically bound to a distinct region of the B3GALT2 promoter and positively regulated its transcription. In the GA model, TFAP2A expression was down-regulated, and its overexpression rescued B3GALT2 expression. Crucially, rescue experiments demonstrated that the inhibitory effects of TFAP2A on NLRP3 inflammasome and pyroptosis were dependent on B3GALT2. Following B3GALT2 knockdown, the anti-inflammatory and anti-pyroptotic effects of TFAP2A overexpression were significantly attenuated. While these data clearly demonstrate that B3GALT2 overexpression suppresses NLRP3 inflammasome activation and pyroptosis, the precise upstream mechanisms by which B3GALT2 exerts this inhibitory effect warrant further investigation. As a β-1,3-galactosyltransferase, B3GALT2 catalyzes the glycosylation of specific glycoproteins, which can profoundly alter their function, localization, and stability. It is plausible that B3GALT2-mediated glycosylation modulates key signaling molecule upstream of NLRP3. For instance, in cerebral ischemia models, B3GALT2 has been shown to alleviate oxidative stress and NLRP3 activation [11]. In this study, B3GALT2 also abolished the increase in ROS levels in GA models, suggesting a potential link between B3GALT2 and ROS homeostasis. In the context of GA, MSU crystals are known to induce mitochondrial dysfunction and potassium efflux, both of which serve as critical upstream signals for NLRP3 inflammasome assembly [23]. This study hypothesizes that B3GALT2 may regulate the glycosylation state of membrane receptors or ion channels involved in MSU crystal recognition (such as TLR4) or cellular ion flux, thereby attenuating the priming and activation signals for NLRP3. Alternatively, B3GALT2 could influence the stability or activity of mitochondrial proteins, mitigating crystal-induced mitochondrial damage and subsequent ROS release. Elucidating the specific glycoprotein targets of B3GALT2 in monocytes/macrophages and their impact on these upstream pathways will be a crucial focus of the future research to fully delineate the mechanistic impact of B3GALT2 in GA pathogenesis. This clearly delineates a TFAP2A/B3GALT2 regulatory axis whose dysfunction in GA leads to loss of pyroptosis inhibition and uncontrolled inflammation.

  This study first reveals the role of the TFAP2A/B3GALT2 axis in suppressing NLRP3 inflammasome activation and pyroptosis in GA. A potential mechanism warranting further investigation is whether B3GALT2 influences liposome function or composition by regulating glycosylation processes. As a glycosyltransferase, B3GALT2’s products can modify membrane proteins or lipids, thereby altering liposome stability and the capacity of signaling molecules to recruit. In cerebral ischemia models, B3GALT2 has been demonstrated to exert protective effects by suppressing oxidative stress and NLRP3 inflammasome activation [11]. Given the known role of lipid rafts as a critical assembly platform for NLRP3 inflammasomes [4, 5], this study hypothesizes that the TFAP2A/B3GALT2 axis may block MSU crystal-induced inflammatory signal amplification by maintaining lipid raft homeostasis and disrupting the proper membrane assembly of NLRP3 inflammasomes. This hypothesis offers a novel perspective for understanding the molecular mechanisms in this study; however, whether and how B3GALT2 regulates lipid rafts requires further experimental validation. While this study provides novel insights, several potential confounding factors warrant consideration. First, the sample size, though sufficient for initial biomarker validation, is moderate. Larger, multi-center cohorts are needed to confirm the diagnostic efficacy of B3GALT2 across diverse populations. Second, demographic factors such as age and sex were matched between GA patients and healthy controls, and common comorbidities like hypertension and diabetes showed no significant difference in distribution. However, other unmeasured variables (such as dietary habits, genetic background, concomitant medications for comorbidities) could potentially influence gene expression profiles or disease severity. Future studies with more detailed clinical data collection and stratification analysis are recommended to account for these factors. Third, the in vitro experiments, while controlled for specific stimuli (LPS/MSU), may not fully recapitulate the complex microenvironment of an inflamed joint in vivo. Animal models will be essential to validate the in vivo functionality of the TFAP2A/B3GALT2 axis and to assess the impact of systemic metabolic and immune factors. Fourth, the specific downstream molecular mechanisms by which B3GALT2 influences NLRP3 inflammasome activity remain incompletely elucidated, and whether its enzymatic products directly or indirectly participate in signal regulation requires further investigation.

  In summary, this study has for the first time identified and characterized the critical role of B3GALT2 in GA. It demonstrates its potential as a diagnostic biomarker and reveals a novel TFAP2A/B3GALT2 signaling axis that exerts protective effects by negatively regulating NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis. Enhancing the function of this pathway may provide novel directions for developing future therapeutic strategies against GA.
