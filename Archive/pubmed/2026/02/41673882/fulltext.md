## Introduction
  Ankylosing spondylitis (AS) stands as a chronic autoimmune inflammatory disease [1], commencing with sacroiliac joint ossification, extending to the axial skeleton, and potentially culminating in spinal deformities. Characterized by persistent back pain and new bone formation, AS can progress to spinal fusion or ankylosis, with chronic pain and stiffness constituting primary contributors to the overall disease burden [2]. In the advanced stages, chronic inflammation may precipitate joint stiffness, fusion, and spinal kyphosis, contributing significantly to the elevated disability rate in AS patients [3]. The prevalence of AS exhibits variations across diverse populations, influenced by geographical and environmental factors [4]. Additionally, genetic and environmental determinants play roles in AS pathogenesis [5]. Current treatment modalities predominantly involve nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and biologics, aiming to ameliorate clinical symptoms and enhance patients’ quality of life [6]. Nevertheless, challenges persist, notably in the form of side effects from nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and the substantial cost of biologics. Furthermore, the mechanism underlying AS ossification remains elusive. Consequently, there is an urgent need to unravel the intricacies of AS's mechanism and offer insights as a reference for improved AS treatment.

  With the rapid advancement of technology, an increasing array of detection techniques plays a pivotal role in unraveling disease mechanisms, facilitating diagnosis, and enhancing treatment strategies. Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) stands out as a method capable of characterizing the gene expression levels of thousands of individual cells simultaneously, offering both high throughput and high resolution [7]. This technique enables the detection of the transcriptional state of each cell, providing groundbreaking insights into cell dynamics and heterogeneity, with the potential to explore intercellular variation [8]. The application of scRNA-seq technology has proven successful in studying various disease mechanisms, yielding significant outcomes [9]. In parallel, proteomics emerges as a valuable tool for discerning the true expression of proteins within tissues [10].

  During the surgical procedure, bone marrow blood is extracted through the nail tract [11]. Bone marrow serves as the origin for various cell types [12]. Immune cells play pivotal roles in the mechanisms of human diseases [13]. Earlier studies have delineated the potential mechanism of action of inflammatory cell subsets in AS [14], as well as the osteogenic differentiation of mesenchymal stem cells [15, 16]. This study employs multiomics methods to analyze and investigate the potential underlying mechanisms of ankylosing spondylitis, contributing valuable insights for treatment.


## Materials and methods

### Study design
  This study received approval from the Ethics Committee of our hospital and adhered to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. Approval Number: 2024-E080-01. All participants provided informed consent. Patients diagnosed with AS, meeting the modified New York criteria [17] and requiring kyphosis correction, constituted the study group. For the control group, blood samples were obtained from patients matched in gender and age who underwent spinal internal fixation for spinal fractures or thoracolumbar fusion due to intervertebral disc herniation. Exclusion criteria encompassed AS with arthritis, systemic lupus erythematosus, or other immune diseases; immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS); noninfectious conditions like tumors and bone diseases; use of glucocorticoids or other immunosuppressants; and the presence of cardiovascular or severe primary diseases involving the liver, kidneys, stomach, and hematopoietic system. Pregnancy was also considered an exclusion criterion.


### Single-cell RNA sequence
  Bone marrow blood was obtained from the vertebral screw channel and immediately placed on ice for rapid transfer to the laboratory. Mononuclear cells were isolated by Ficoll density gradient centrifugation. After centrifugation, a distinct white layer containing mononuclear cells was collected and washed twice with PBS. Red blood cells were removed using a red blood cell lysis buffer. After cell counting, the samples were cryopreserved through gradual temperature reduction and sent for sequencing and analysis under dry ice protection. We employed 10X Genomics to construct single-cell sequencing libraries [18]. Quality control involved the use of an Agilent bioanalyzer [19]. KAPA Library Quantification Kit was utilized for quantifying high-quality libraries, followed by submission for sequencing [20] on HiSeq4000 for the single-cell sequencing experiments [21]. Cell Ranger version 6.0.2 software was employed to inspect and initially assess the raw sequencing data [22].

  For gene data quality control, the following criteria were applied: each gene should be expressed in at least 500 cells, the number of genes detected in each cell should be more than 500 but less than 10,000, and the proportion of mitochondrial genes should be less than 10%.

  The FindVariableFeatures function was used to identify genes with significant coefficients of variation between cells, and JackStraw (repeated 1000 times) was employed to screen principal components (PCs) for cell segmentation. The top 20 PCs were selected for TSNE cluster analysis using the RunTSNE function and the FindNeighbors function.

  Marker genes were identified through the 'FindAllMarkers' function and likelihood ratio tests of single-cell gene expression. DEG analysis, comparing each cluster between the two sample groups, utilized the Wilcoxon rank sum test with parameters set as follows: min.pct = 0.25, logFC not less than 2, and adjPval less than 0.05.

  CellMarker (http://xteam.xbio.top/CellMarker/index.jsp) [23] was consulted for the most significantly differentiated genes of each PC for cell annotation. In cases where annotation results were unavailable, Enrichr (https://maayanlab.cloud/Enrichr/) [24] was utilized to query all significantly differentiated genes of the PC according to cell type. Subsequent to acquiring cell annotations, the limma function was used to analyze the DEGs of each cell subpopulation in the AS group and the control group. ClusterProfiler package 4.8.3 was then employed for Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analyses, followed by construction of the PPI network using STRING (https://string-db.org/) and Cytoscape. Mutual communication between cells was analyzed using CellChat 1.6.1.


### RNA chip and mass spectrometry analysis
  Total RNA from the interspinous ligament was isolated and assessed using an Agilent Human ceRNA chip. Differential analysis employed Student’s t-test, with statistical significance indicated by a p-value less than 0.05.


### Public open source databases
  The GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) provides chip data for numerous diseases, offering gene chip and clinical data resources. GeneCards database (https://www.genecards.org/) [25] and Comparative Toxicogenomics Database (CTD) (https://ctdbase.org/) [26] contain comprehensive gene information, enabling the search for disease-related genes under specific conditions. The ceRNA network, crucial in disease mechanisms, has been extensively studied in recent years [27], contributing to a deeper understanding of disease mechanisms. miRcode (http://www.mircode.org/) [28] and circbank (http://www.circbank.cn/) [29] databases house substantial transcriptome data for predicting miRNAs and circRNAs, respectively. The CIBERSORTx platform(https://cibersortx.stanford.edu/) enables the estimation of the relative abundance of constituent cell types within a mixed cell population based on gene expression data, providing further validation for our findings.


### Exploration of traditional Chinese medicine treatment for AS
  HERB (http://herb.ac.cn/) is a traditional Chinese medicine database grounded in high-throughput experiments and reference guidance [30]. HERB facilitates the retrieval of active ingredients in traditional Chinese medicine and allows queries based on disease status, including treatment targets. PubChem (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) [31] was used to query the 2D structure of active ingredients, and ChemBio3D Ultra 14.0 calculated the minimum free energy, converting it into a three-dimensional structure. The AlphaFold Protein Structure Database (https://alphafold.ebi.ac.uk/) [32] provided the three-dimensional structure of the target protein, processed through PyMOL 2.5 to eliminate solvent and organics. Finally, AutoDock Vina identified the active pocket of the target protein and executed the molecular docking of the target protein with drug ingredients.


### Immunohistochemical verification
  To enhance result evaluation, we specifically chose interspinous ligaments from patients diagnosed with AS and degenerative diseases for immunohistochemical examinations. The interspinous ligament was procured, followed by soaking and fixation in 10% formalin. Subsequent procedures included paraffin embedding, sectioning, antigen retrieval, antibody hybridization, and sealing. The Anti-STAT4 Polyclonal Antibody was diluted at a ratio of 1:400. All antibodies used were sourced from Shanghai Jizhi Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd. in Shanghai, China.


### Validation in a chronic inflammatory model
  We selected eight male Sprague–Dawley (SD) rats of comparable age and weight, and randomly divided them into two groups. The experimental group received weekly intraperitoneal injections of lipopolysaccharide (LPS, 2.5 mg/kg), while the control group received equal volumes of phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) for 10 consecutive weeks. At the end of the 10-week period, the rats were sacrificed, and paraspinal ligament muscle tissues were collected. Total RNA was extracted from approximately 50 mg of ligament muscle, followed by reverse transcription and quantitative PCR (qPCR) analysis. The primers used for STAT4 were as follows: Forward-TGAGAGCCATCTTGGAGGGA; Reverse-CCCGTTCTGTCGGTCTTGAA. The internal reference gene β-actin was amplified using the following primers: Forward-CTGTGTGGATTGGTGGCTCT; Reverse-CAGCTCAGTAACAGTCCGCC.


## Results

### Single-cell transcriptomics profile of immune cells
  In this investigation, single-cell RNA sequencing was performed on bone marrow blood obtained from 3 patients with AS and 3 healthy controls (Fig. 1A). All participants were male, with a mean age of 40.7 years in the AS group and 40 years in the healthy control group. Following rigorous quality control and filtration, we acquired 59,566 bone marrow blood cells (BMBCs). Principal component analysis (PCA) on these cells yielded 23 clusters (Fig. 1B). Cell annotation, conducted through CellMarker, identified distinct cell types, including 1 type of B-cell (B cell), 4 types of T cells (naive CD4+ T cells, naive CD8+ T cells, T helper 1 (Th1) cells, and effector CD8+ memory T (Tem) cells), 3 types of dendritic cells (plasmacytoid dendritic cells, CD141+ CLEC9A+ dendritic cells, and CD1C-CD141- dendritic cells), and 1 type of natural killer cell (natural killer cell). However, one cluster failed to be annotated after using CellMarker. Finally, we annotated the cluster as M2 macrophages using the Enrichr website. M1 macrophages were notably absent in the enrichment analysis, leading to the identification of a total of 10 distinct cell subpopulations (Fig. 1B). ### The percentage of effector CD8+ memory T (Tem) cells is significantly increased in AS
  Upon comparing cell data across different types, we observed a significant increase in T cells in the AS group, constituting 68.03% compared to 51.97% in the control group. Further detailed analysis unveiled a noteworthy elevation in the percentage of effector CD8+ memory T (Tem) cells, standing at 20.97% in the AS group and 7.52% in the control group (Fig. 2A). Consequently, an in-depth investigation into effector CD8+ memory T (Tem) cells was subsequently undertaken.Fig. 2The proportion of cells. A Cell subpopulation among the BMBCs of AS patients and healthy controls. B Violin plots of cell dubtype abundances in GSE25101 estimated by CIBERSORTx

  We further obtained transcriptomic data from the GSE25101 dataset, which includes peripheral blood samples from 16 AS patients and 16 HCs. Based on the cell-type-specific gene signatures and annotations derived from our single-cell analysis, we conducted deconvolution using CIBERSORTx. The results were consistent with our single-cell findings, showing an overall higher abundance of CD8⁺ T cells in AS patients. Notably, the abundance of effector CD8⁺ memory T (Tem) cells was higher in AS compared to HC, although the difference did not reach statistical significance (Fig. 2B).


### Cell communication pathways in AS
  In-depth exploration of cellular communication in AS involved extracting gene expression profiles related to signaling pathway ligands and receptors through CellChat. The findings underscored the intricate communication within various immune systems implicated in AS. A comprehensive analysis revealed the participation of 32 cellular communication pathways, encompassing 46 ligands, 44 receptors, and a complex network of 64 ligand–receptor interaction modes (Fig. 3A). The top five most prevalent ligand‒receptor interaction modes included MIF- (CD74+ CD44), MIF- (CD74+ CXCR4), ADGRE5-CD55, SELPLG-SELL, and IL16-CD4 (Fig. 3B).Fig. 3Cellular interaction and communication. C Ligand‒receptor interactions involved in the interaction between effector CD8+ memory T (Tem) cells and other cells. D, E, G, H are the MIF, ADGRE5, RESISTIN, and ICAM signaling pathway networks, respectively. F Outgoing and incoming signaling patterns

  For effector CD8+ memory T (Tem) cells, acting both as sources and targets, a nuanced investigation revealed 51 ligand–receptor modes of action when originating from effector CD8+ memory T (Tem) cells and 37 modes when targeted. Notably, MHC-II emerged as a consistently prominent pathway, irrespective of the cellular role of effector CD8+ memory T (Tem) cells. However, MIF- (CD74+ CXCR4), MIF- (CD74+ CD44), and ADGRE5-CD55 exhibited robust communication patterns with various other cells (Fig. 3C–E).

  Further analysis of output and input signals in effector CD8+ memory T (Tem) cell subpopulations unveiled ICAM, RESISTIN, and ADGRE5 as predominant output signals, while ICAM emerged as a key contributor to input signals (Fig. 3F–H). Particularly, the ICAM pathway displayed significant involvement, with ICAM2 identified as the exclusive ligand and ITGAM and ITGB2 as the corresponding receptors in this communication pathway.


### Differences in effector CD8+ memory T (Tem) cells percentage between AS and HCs
  Analysis of effector CD8+ memory T (Tem) cell subpopulations in AS patients and healthy controls (HCs) revealed 465 differentially expressed genes (DEGs), with 281 upregulated and 184 downregulated, meeting criteria of p < 0.01 and |logFC|> 1. Among them, 194 DEGs, comprising 131 upregulated and 63 downregulated genes, met criteria of adjusted p < 0.01 and |logFC|> 2 (Fig. 4A).Fig. 4Differential gene expression analysis and related functional analysis of effector CD8+ memory T (Tem) cells in AS patients and HCs. A Volcano plot of DEGs in effector CD8+ memory T (Tem) cells from AS patients and HCs. B GO functional enrichment analysis of 465 DEGs. C, D GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis of DEGs. E The top subnetwork of 163 DEGs was subjected to PPI analysis via MCODE. F The top 10 genes obtained from cytoHubba

  Functional analyses of the involved genes included Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses using the cluster Profiler package. GO enrichment analysis highlighted significant enrichment in biological processes (BP) (Fig. 4B, C), focusing on cytoplasmic translation, regulation of immune cell proliferation, and immune response aspects. Analysis of cell subpopulation proportions indicated a significant increase in effector CD8+ memory T (Tem) cells in AS, consistent with GO enrichment findings related to lymphocyte activation and T-cell regulation (GO:0051251, GO:0050863). KEGG analysis identified concentration in genes related to hsa05171 Coronavirus Disease-COVID-19 and hsa03010 Ribosome, with additional enrichment in pathways associated with T lymphocytes and inflammatory factors (Fig. 4D).

  To understand the role of key genes in effector CD8+ memory T (Tem) cells, we selected genes with higher differential expression (adj p < 0.01, |logFC|> 2). The top 100 upregulated and all 63 downregulated DEGs were used to construct a protein–protein interaction (PPI) network via STRING and further analyzed using Cytoscape. The most concentrated cluster, with a score of 38.32, was obtained through MCODE analysis (Fig. 4E). Global PPI network analysis using cytoHubba revealed the top 10 genes by Maximum Correlation Centrality (MCC) ranking, identifying ribosomal protein-encoding gene families (Fig. 4F). Among them, RPL10 and RPS19 were downregulated in AS, while others were upregulated, suggesting a crucial role of the ribosomal protein family in effector CD8+ memory T (Tem) cells in AS.


### STAT4 as an immune-related gene in AS
  AS is a chronic inflammatory disease, prompting a comprehensive exploration of key genes through public databases. Identification of AS-related genes involved searches in the GeneCards database, yielding 2367 genes associated with ankylosing spondylitis. Additionally, the Comparative Toxicogenomics Database provided 1247 genes related to immune system diseases. Based on the criteria of adjusted P-value < 0.01 and |logFC|> 2, we identified differentially expressed genes between AS and HC in the effector CD8⁺ memory T (Tem) cell subset. Utilizing a Venn diagram, we extracted genes related to effector CD8+ memory T (Tem) cells, resulting in 24 genes from the differential gene set of effector CD8+ memory T (Tem) cells, GeneCards database, and Comparative Toxicogenomics Database (Fig. 5A). Notably, among these genes, STAT4 emerged.Fig. 5AS immune-related DEGs and functional analysis. A Venn diagram of ankylosing spondylitis effector CD8+ memory T (Tem) cell-related immune genes. B GO and KEGG pathway functional enrichment analysis of 24 genes. C Heatmap of STAT4 expression in the GSE73754 and GSE25101 cohorts

  Further functional analyses via GO and KEGG revealed that these 24 genes were intricately linked to T-cell- and immune-related pathways. These pathways encompassed biological processes such as T-cell-mediated cytotoxicity, T-cell-mediated immunity, and positive regulation of T-cell-mediated immunity. Additionally, KEGG pathways included human T-cell leukemia virus 1 infection, Th1 and Th2 cell differentiation, and Th17 cell differentiation (Fig. 5B).

  Accessing the GEO database, which harbors extensive microarray data for various diseases, we retrieved datasets GSE73754 and GSE25101. GSE73754 comprised 52 AS patients and 20 healthy controls, while GSE25101 included 16 active AS patients and 16 sex- and age-matched controls. Differential expression analysis of these datasets indicated that STAT4 was expressed at significantly lower levels in AS patients compared to healthy controls (p < 0.05) (Fig. 5C). This finding aligned with our single-cell analysis of the expression of effector CD8+ memory T (Tem) cell subsets.


### Insights into networks in AS
  Verification of STAT4 expression at reduced levels in bone marrow blood effector CD8+ memory T (Tem) cells was conducted using peripheral blood data from 68 AS patients and 36 healthy controls available in the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. Recognizing the pivotal role of ceRNA networks in disease mechanisms, an in-depth analysis of the potential regulatory mechanism governing STAT4 was pursued. Prediction of miRNAs interacting with STAT4 was performed using the miRcode database, yielding 58 miRNAs associated with STAT4.

  Additionally, spinal ligament tissue was procured from three AS patients and three sex- and age-matched individuals with spinal degeneration who underwent thoracolumbar surgery at the same site. Agilent RNA chip sequencing on the ligament tissue provided miRNA and circRNA data. Employing Venn diagram analysis, four miRNAs (hsa-miR-484, hsa-miR-574-5p, hsa-miR-425-5p, and hsa-miR-4317) were identified in both our ligament Agilent miRNA chip data and the miRcode database (Fig. 6A). Subsequent prediction of circRNAs interacting with these four miRNAs through circBank and comparison with Agilent circRNA chip data yielded 11 circRNAs interacting with STAT4 through four miRNAs.Fig. 6ceRNA data acquisition, analysis process and construction of the ceRNA network. A Design and experimental process of miRNA and ceRNA chip construction and construction of the ceRNA network. B The intersections of STAT4 downstream target genes. C The target genes expression in GSE73754. D ceRNA network regulating the STAT4 gene

  We recognize that STAT4, as a transcription factor, is likely to play a critical regulatory role. To investigate its downstream targets, we queried two transcription factor–target gene databases, hTFtarget and TRRUST, and identified three overlapping target genes: IL2RA, IRF1, and SOCS3. Further analysis using the CTD and GeneCards databases revealed that all three genes are associated with AS (Fig. 6B). We then examined the expression of these genes in the GSE73754 dataset using violin plots and found that IRF1 was significantly downregulated in AS patients compared to controls (Fig. 6C). Based on these findings, we hypothesize that the decreased expression of STAT4 in AS may lead to reduced expression of IRF1, potentially contributing to disease pathogenesis.

  The construction of a network using Cytoscape (Fig. 6D) highlighted the regulatory dynamics. While miRNAs suppress the transcriptional expression of genes, circRNA acts as a sponge, absorbing miRNA and modulating the transcriptional expression of genes. Integrating the regulatory mechanism of the network with the actual RNA expression in ligaments, we propose three regulatory pathways in AS: hsa_circ_0036579/hsa-miR-574-5p/STAT4/IRF1, hsa_circ_0063062/hsa-miR-574-5p/STAT4/IRF1, and hsa_circ_0006696/hsa-miR-574-5p/STAT4/IRF1.


### Investigating the therapeutic potential of guava leaves in AS
  The predominant treatments for AS currently involve nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and biological agents. However, the former is associated with numerous side effects, while the latter poses financial constraints. Traditional Chinese medicine, known for its minimal side effects and cost-effectiveness, emerges as a significant player in disease management. Guava, a common subtropical fruit, stands out due to the accessibility of its leaves. Previous research indicates that guava leaves exhibit immunomodulatory and anti-inflammatory effects [33, 34]. This study delves into the potential binding interaction between guava leaves and STAT4, a key factor in AS pathology. The composition of guava leaves, comprising eleven components, was queried through HERB, and the 2D structure was retrieved from PubChem.

  Utilizing AutoDock Vina, we conducted simulations to examine the binding between protein receptors and the medicinal ingredient ligands. Stable binding was defined by an affinity energy of less than − 5 kcal/mol, with lower values indicating stronger binding. Our findings reveal that eleven components of guava leaves exhibit stable binding to STAT4 through multiple modes. In the optimal binding configurations, strictinin (− 11.4 kcal/mol), amritoside − 10.4 kcal/mol), and guaijaverin (− 9.3 kcal/mol) emerged as the top three ingredients, showcasing high binding stability (Table 1). These results affirm that guava establishes a stable interaction with STAT4, employing multiple components and modes (Supplement 1). ## Discussion
  AS is a chronic immune-inflammatory disorder involving diverse immune cells, yet its precise pathogenesis remains elusive. Current treatments predominantly rely on nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and costly biological agents like TNF-α. Despite their efficacy, these approaches are accompanied by notable side effects. This study employed single-cell transcriptomics to delineate immune subpopulations and systematically explored pivotal genes and associated pathways through the integration of public databases. The ceRNA transcriptome was subsequently employed to scrutinize gene regulation via the ceRNA network. Ultimately, the investigation delved into the potential therapeutic impact of traditional Chinese medicine on AS.

  Our study revealed a significantly higher percentage of effector CD8+ memory T (Tem) cells in AS patients compared to healthy individuals. Prior research has documented elevated CD8+ T cells, including immature subsets, in AS [14] and various other diseases [35, 36]. Previous studies have shown that patients with AS exhibit distinct CD8⁺ T cell subsets with osteogenic and cytotoxic potential [37, 38]. The clonal expansion of CD8⁺ T cells has also been implicated in the pathogenesis of spondyloarthritis [39]. In addition, peripheral blood single-cell RNA sequencing has similarly demonstrated an increased abundance of CD8⁺ T cell subsets in AS [40]. However, to date, no other studies have reported single-cell RNA sequencing analysis of bone marrow blood in AS patients. Effector CD8+ memory T (Tem) cells, characterized by rapid and enhanced immune responses upon re-exposure to specific pathogens, may be influenced by paracrine signaling from cells such as MIF − (CD74+ CXCR4), MIF − (CD74+ CD44), and ADGRE5–CD55. Notably, ICAM emerged as a key player in both outgoing and incoming signals, suggesting its integral role in this immune context.

  In examining the differential expression of effector CD8+ memory T (Tem) cells in AS patients and controls, a noteworthy reduction in STAT4 expression was observed in AS patients. This downregulation of STAT4 expression within the effector CD8+ memory T (Tem) cell subset was validated in the GSE73754 and GSE25101 cohorts. Prior studies have reported a similar decrease in STAT4 expression in immune-related disorders [41, 42], with gain-of-function variants implicated in specific conditions [43]. To unravel the potential regulatory mechanisms behind STAT4 downregulation, miRNA and circRNA sequencing was conducted on ligament tissue. Given ethical considerations, interspinous ligaments from degenerative patients served as age-matched controls. The downregulation of hsa_circ_0036579, hsa_circ_0063062, and hsa_circ_0006696 is speculated to elevate hsa-miR-574-5p, subsequently inhibiting STAT4 expression. Although miR-574-5p is highly expressed in lung cancer [44], the regulatory roles of the identified circRNAs in AS are novel, presenting a unique contribution to the understanding of ceRNA networks in this context. However, further investigations are warranted.

  CellMarker failed to annotate classical M1 or M2 macrophages, and one cluster remained unannotated. Although the Enrichr website identified M2 macrophages in this cluster, the absence of typical M2 macrophage markers raises uncertainties about the accuracy of this assignment. This study performed single-cell sequencing analysis on bone marrow-derived samples. According to the methods we described, hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs) and neutrophils were not identified in our cell annotation. Previous studies analyzing single-cell data from bone marrow aspirates have also not reported the presence of HSCs or neutrophils [45]. This discrepancy may be due to differences in cell composition across different anatomical sites of bone marrow or potential limitations in current cell annotation methods.

  Considering the side effects and costliness of current AS treatment modalities, traditional Chinese medicine emerges as a viable alternative. Our prior investigation revealed the potential therapeutic efficacy of C. ramulus in AS [46]. Notably, STAT4 exhibits reduced expression in effector CD8+ memory T (Tem) cells. Guava leaves, widely accessible in daily life, boast anti-inflammatory, antidiarrheal, antihypertensive, antiobesity, and other beneficial effects [47]. Our analysis explored the binding potential of guava leaves to STAT4, revealing that 11 components bind through various modes, predominantly at the core of STAT4’s 3D-dimensional structure. This central binding enhances stability and impedes STAT4 degradation. Previous studies underscore strictinin’s inhibition of viral replication [48] and STAT6 phosphorylation via IL-4Rα [49], while recent research touts guava leaves for their potential in diabetes treatment [50].

  In this study, we began with single-cell RNA sequencing data and identified a significant upregulation of effector CD8⁺ memory T (Tem) cells in ankylosing spondylitis (AS). This finding was preliminarily validated using CIBERSORTx-based deconvolution analysis. Further investigation revealed that STAT4 expression was downregulated in this cell subset. We then explored the upstream and downstream regulatory mechanisms of STAT4 and confirmed its low expression in the paraspinal ligament muscle of both AS patients and a chronic inflammation rat model. Finally, we analyzed potential traditional Chinese medicine compounds targeting STAT4. Together, our findings provide novel insights into the pathogenesis and potential treatment strategies for AS. However, this study also has several limitations. First, the number of cases included in our single-cell RNA-seq analysis was relatively small. Second, we lacked mechanistic validation at the cellular level. Third, the predicted therapeutic compounds targeting STAT4 have not yet been experimentally verified. In future studies, we aim to further investigate the pathogenic mechanisms of AS and explore potential therapeutic interventions in greater depth.


## Conclusion
  Our comprehensive multiomics analysis unveiled the intricate mechanisms and associated genes of effector CD8+ memory T (Tem) cells in AS. Notably, STAT4 exhibited significant downregulation, prompting the construction of a ceRNA network to elucidate the underlying regulatory mechanisms. Additionally, molecular docking investigations showcased the potential of guava leaves in AS treatment. These findings offer novel perspectives for AS diagnosis and therapeutic interventions.
