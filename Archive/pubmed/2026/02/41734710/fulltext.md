BACKGROUND: Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) is a major metabolic disorder lacking effective pharmacological therapy. Medicinal plants represent valuable sources of hepatoprotective agents. Piper attenuatum Buch.-Ham. has been used traditionally for liver ailments, yet its potential against NAFLD remains unknown. PURPOSE: To isolate and characterize bioactive metabolites from P. attenuatum and evaluate their effects on hepatic lipid accumulation and related molecular pathways. METHODS: Bioassay-guided fractionation of the hexane extract of P. attenuatum stems afforded six compounds, including a novel ishwarane derivative, Atsuattenone. Structures were elucidated by NMR, MS, and single-crystal X-ray diffraction. Lipid accumulation and cell viability were assessed in linoleic acid-loaded HepG2 cells. Gene expression was analyzed by qRT-PCR, and molecular docking was performed against hepatocyte nuclear factor 4alpha (HNF4alpha) and peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma (PPARgamma). RESULTS: Atsuattenone markedly reduced intracellular triglyceride accumulation without cytotoxicity, while other isolates showed weak or adverse effects. qRT-PCR analysis revealed that Atsuattenone significantly downregulated PPARalpha and SREBP1 mRNA, while upregulating FABP4 and the lipolytic genes ATGL, HSL, and MGL. It also decreased AMPKalpha expression in a dose-dependent manner. Docking analysis indicated moderate affinity for HNF4alpha (-6.9 kcal/mol), a transcriptional regulator of PPARalpha, but weak binding to PPARgamma (-5.5 kcal/mol), suggesting an indirect, non-classical regulatory mechanism. CONCLUSION: P. attenuatum is identified as a new source of lipid-regulating metabolites. Atsuattenone acts as a promising natural modulator that attenuates steatosis by enhancing lipolytic gene expression, suppressing lipogenesis, and modulating HNF4alpha- and AMPKalpha-linked transcriptional pathways, offering potential for NAFLD prevention and therapy.
