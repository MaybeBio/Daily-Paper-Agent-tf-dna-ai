### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that "Key SAR insights highlight the critical contributions in distinct structural motifs, including planar aromatic cores for pi-pi stacking, cationic side chains for electrostatic interactions and precisely positioned electron-donating or electron-withdrawing groups that determine inhibitory potency and selectivity."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
- **Concern** The claim that specific structural motifs determine potency and selectivity is presented without any quantitative support. No IC50 values, selectivity indices, or comparative data across analogues are provided. The abstract does not cite specific compounds or studies that demonstrate these relationships.  
- **Why it matters** A central purpose of a SAR review is to correlate structure with activity in a way that readers can use for design. Without quantitative examples, the claim is an assertion rather than an evidence-based conclusion, and the framework cannot be evaluated or applied.  
- **Resolution test** Provide at least 2 to 3 representative examples per scaffold class with chemical structures, associated potency values, and a clear description of how specific structural changes altered activity. This would allow readers to assess the validity of the SAR claims.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Claim substantiation  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract states that "In silico docking studies highlighted the key interactions of the corresponding ligand molecules with the amino acid residues present within the active pocket of the MYC target."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
- **Concern** The docking studies are described generically. No information is given on which software, scoring functions, protein structures, or validation methods were used. The phrase "active pocket of the MYC target" is ambiguous, as MYC is intrinsically disordered and does not have a well-defined pocket in its free state. The relevance of docking to a disordered protein is not addressed.  
- **Why it matters** Docking results are only meaningful if the target conformation and methodology are appropriate. For a disordered protein like MYC, docking to a single static structure may be misleading. Without methodological detail, the computational claims cannot be assessed for validity.  
- **Resolution test** Specify the docking protocol, including the protein structure or model used, the software and scoring function, and any validation steps such as redocking or correlation with experimental data. Clarify how the disordered nature of MYC was handled.
