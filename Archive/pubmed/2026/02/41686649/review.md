## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, conclusions) without figures, tables, or supplementary material.
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific soundness, methodological clarity, validity of claims, and reproducibility based solely on the provided text. Figures, tables, and supplementary files referenced but not supplied are treated as unavailable for verification.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors present iDLDDG, a deep learning framework that integrates embeddings from three protein language models (ESM2, ESM1v, ProtTrans) via a multi-channel transformer architecture to predict protein stability changes (ΔΔG) from missense mutations in DNA-binding proteins. The model is trained and evaluated on a newly compiled dataset of 324 mutations across 73 complexes, partitioned into MPD276 (training) and MPD48 (independent test), with reverse-augmentation to balance the data. An entropy-based algorithm is used to select an optimal input sequence length of 181 residues. The authors report state-of-the-art performance, with a 10-fold cross-validation PCC of 0.755 on MPD276 and 0.632 on the independent test set, and claim the framework is the first to rigorously differentiate double-stranded (DSB) and single-stranded (SSB) DNA-binding protein mutation mechanisms.
- **Visible evidence base** Text descriptions of dataset construction, model architecture, entropy analysis, ablation study, and performance comparisons. Numerical results are reported in the text (e.g., PCC values) but the corresponding tables and figures are not provided.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** All figures (Figs 1–8), tables (Tables 1–5), supplementary tables (Tables S1–S4), supplementary figures (Figs S1–S4), and supplementary text (Text S1) are referenced but not included. The GitHub repository link is provided but not assessed. Without these, the quantitative claims, statistical significance, and comparative analyses cannot be independently verified.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript addresses a relevant problem in computational biology: predicting ΔΔG for missense mutations in DNA-binding proteins, with a novel emphasis on distinguishing DSBs and SSBs. The use of multiple PLM embeddings and a transformer-based multi-channel architecture is methodologically reasonable and timely. However, the current evidence base is insufficient to fully evaluate the claims. Key quantitative results are presented without the underlying tables and figures, making it impossible to assess statistical robustness, compare against baselines rigorously, or verify the stated performance improvements. The entropy-based sequence length selection is an interesting contribution but its justification and generalizability require more rigorous analysis. The manuscript is clearly written and accessible, but several technical details and validations are missing.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Computational biologists and bioinformaticians working on protein stability prediction, mutation effect prediction, and protein–DNA interaction modeling. Researchers developing deep learning methods for biological sequence analysis, particularly those using protein language models, would also find the approach relevant. The distinction between DSB and SSB mutations could be of interest to researchers studying DNA-binding protein function and disease-associated mutations.
- **Major strengths**
  - The problem is well-motivated and practically important.
  - The explicit separation of DSB and SSB mutations is a thoughtful and potentially valuable design choice.
  - The integration of three complementary PLM embeddings is a reasonable strategy to capture diverse sequence features.
  - The entropy-based method for selecting input sequence length is an original idea that addresses a practical modeling issue.
  - The writing is generally clear and accessible to a broad audience.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **R1-M1** (Blocking: Yes): The core performance claims are not verifiable from the provided material. All tables and figures containing quantitative results are missing. The reported PCC values (e.g., 0.755, 0.632) and comparisons with other methods (e.g., PEMPNI, mSAMPDI, PremPDI, mCSM-NA) cannot be checked for statistical significance, variance, or fairness of comparison. Without these data, the central claim of "state-of-the-art performance" is unsupported.
  - **R1-M2** (Blocking: Yes): The dataset construction and partitioning are described only at a high level. The criteria for including mutations, the redundancy reduction process, and the exact composition of the training and test sets are not detailed. The claim that MPD48 "was constructed to exclude any data used in the training of iDLDDG" is critical for avoiding data leakage, but the procedure for ensuring this is not described. The use of reverse augmentation is mentioned but its potential to introduce bias or artificial correlations is not discussed.
  - **R1-M3** (Blocking: No): The entropy-based selection of 181 residues is presented as a key methodological contribution, but the analysis is incomplete. The relationship between entropy, information density, and model performance is not rigorously established. The choice of 181 as "optimal" appears to be based on a single criterion (PCC from 10-fold CV), but the sensitivity of this choice to dataset composition, model architecture, or random seed is not explored. The claim that this "enhances predictive accuracy and computational efficiency" is not supported by a systematic comparison across different sequence lengths.
  - **R1-M4** (Blocking: No): The ablation study (Table 2) is described in text but the results are not shown. The claim that the full combination of three embeddings yields the highest PCC and lowest RMSE is plausible but unverifiable. The observation that the MAE for the full combination is slightly higher than ESM1v alone is noted but not explained, which raises questions about the consistency of the performance metrics.
  - **R1-M5** (Blocking: No): The comparison with existing methods is limited to a single table (Table 3) and figures (Figs 5 and 6) that are not provided. The choice of baselines (PEMPNI, SAMPDI, PremPDI, mCSM-NA) is reasonable, but the conditions of comparison (e.g., whether the same training/test splits were used, whether hyperparameters were optimized for each method) are not stated. The reported "superior performance" is therefore not established.
- **Minor Comments**
  - **R1-m1** (Axis: Clarity): The abstract states the model predicts "protein stability changes" but the introduction and methods focus on "protein–DNA binding affinity." These are related but distinct quantities. The manuscript should clarify whether ΔΔG refers to folding stability or binding affinity, as this affects interpretation and comparison with other methods.
  - **R1-m2** (Axis: Reproducibility): The description of the model architecture is incomplete. The number of transformer layers, hidden dimensions, dropout rates, learning rates, and optimization details are not provided. The pseudo-Huber loss is mentioned but the value of δ is not given. These details are essential for reproducibility.
  - **R1-m3** (Axis: Statistical rigor): The manuscript reports PCC, MAE, and RMSE but does not provide confidence intervals, standard deviations across folds, or statistical tests for comparing methods. Given the small dataset size (324 mutations), such measures are necessary to assess the reliability of the reported improvements.
  - **R1-m4** (Axis: Data description): The manuscript states that the dataset was compiled from "diverse sources" but does not list the specific sources or the number of mutations from each. This information is important for assessing potential biases and for comparison with other datasets in the field.
  - **R1-m5** (Axis: Generalizability): The claim that iDLDDG "provides a foundation for prediction of pathological mutations" is speculative. The manuscript does not include any analysis of disease-associated mutations or clinical relevance. This claim should be tempered or supported with additional analysis.
  - **R1-m6** (Axis: Clarity): The description of the "multi-channel architecture" is vague. It is unclear how the three embedding matrices are combined (e.g., concatenation, attention-based fusion) and how the "multi-scale structural and evolutionary information" is integrated. A clearer schematic or textual description is needed.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established**
  - R1-M1: Missing quantitative results (tables and figures) for all performance claims.
  - R1-M2: Insufficient detail on dataset construction and leakage prevention.
  - R1-M3: Incomplete justification of the entropy-based sequence length selection.
  - R1-M4: Unverifiable ablation study results.
  - R1-M5: Unclear comparison conditions with baseline methods.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**
  - **Originality**: The idea of explicitly separating DSB and SSB mutations and using entropy analysis for sequence length selection has some novelty. However, the overall approach (PLM embeddings + transformer) is a common paradigm in current bioinformatics. The originality is moderate and would be strengthened by a clearer demonstration of the unique benefits of the DSB/SSB distinction.
  - **Scientific importance**: Predicting ΔΔG for DNA-binding protein mutations is a relevant problem with potential applications in disease variant interpretation and protein engineering. The importance is real but incremental, as many similar predictors exist. The manuscript does not demonstrate a major advance in accuracy or applicability.
  - **Interdisciplinary readership**: The topic is of interest to computational biologists, bioinformaticians, and possibly structural biologists. The writing is accessible, but the lack of detailed methods and results limits its appeal to a broader readership.
  - **Technical soundness**: The methodological framework is plausible, but the missing results and incomplete details prevent a full assessment. The entropy-based analysis and the ablation study are not rigorously validated. The statistical treatment of results is insufficient.
  - **Readability for nonspecialists**: The abstract and introduction are clear and well-written. The methods section is more technical but generally understandable. The lack of figures and tables makes it difficult for any reader to follow the results.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The manuscript addresses a relevant problem and presents a reasonable methodological framework, but the core quantitative claims are unverifiable without the missing tables, figures, and supplementary materials. The authors should be invited to resubmit a revised version that includes all data, detailed methods, and rigorous statistical analysis.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim of "state-of-the-art performance" is unsupported without the comparative results in Table 3 and Figs 5 and 6.
- The claim that the entropy-based algorithm "determined 181 residues as optimal" is not rigorously justified without a systematic sensitivity analysis.
- The claim that iDLDDG "provides a foundation for prediction of pathological mutations" is speculative and not supported by any disease-related analysis.
- The claim that the model "significantly surpasses existing methods" is unverifiable without statistical tests and confidence intervals.
- The claim that the multi-channel architecture "effectively integrates diverse protein representations" is not demonstrated without the ablation study results (Table 2).
- The claim that the dataset is "comprehensive" and "from diverse sources" is not verifiable without a detailed breakdown of the data sources and composition.
