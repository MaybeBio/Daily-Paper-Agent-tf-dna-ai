ETHNOPHARMACOLOGICAL RELEVANCE: Yinhuo Decoction (YHD), a classical Chinese herbal formula for yin-nourishing and kidney-tonifying, has been traditionally prescribed for depression and female climacteric syndrome, and shows therapeutic potential for menopausal depression (MD). While modern pharmacological studies confirm its potent anti-inflammatory properties, the role of YHD in regulating microglial polarization and its key active components remains unclear. AIM OF THE STUDY: This study aimed to identify the key active components and molecular targets of YHD responsible for anti-neuroinflammation and to elucidate its underlying mechanisms. MATERIALS AND METHODS: The ovariectomy (OVX) + chronic unpredictable mild stress (CUMS)-induced murine model of menopausal depression was established. Mice were randomly assigned to six groups (n = 15 per group): Sham, OVX + CUMS, E2 (0.13 mg/kg), and YHD (11.7、23.4、46.8 g/kg). The YHD components in cerebrospinal fluid were qualitatively analyzed using UHPLC-LTQ-Orbitrap MS. Combined with network pharmacology, the core components, targets of its anti-neuroinflammatory effect were screened. Molecular docking, cellular thermal shift assays (CETSA) under intervention with specific concentration (100 muM) of active monomers, and affinity ultrafiltration techniques were used to analyze the interaction between YHD components and key targets. The therapeutic effects of YHD active ingredients were explored in lipopolysaccharides/adenosine triphosphate (LPS + ATP)-induced cell models. RESULTS: Treatment with YHD significantly alleviated depressive-like behaviors in OVX + CUMS mice, regulate M1/M2 phenotypic balance of microglia and reduced neuroinflammation. Chemical profiling of cerebrospinal fluid identified seven YHD derived components, and network pharmacology analysis suggested the PI3K/Akt/NF-kappaB signaling pathway as a key mechanistic target. This prediction was confirmed in vivo. In LPS + ATP stimulated BV2 microglia, the core monomeric constituents, including echinacoside, schisandrin B, and verbascoside, recapitulated the pathway modulating effects observed in vivo. Molecular docking, cellular thermal shift assay (CETSA), and affinity ultrafiltration studies further demonstrated direct binding of all three compounds to PI3K, with verbascoside exhibiting the highest affinity. In addition, the PI3K inhibitor LY294002 can reverse the activation of the PI3K/Akt pathway and the inhibition of the NF-kappaB signal by verbascoside. CONCLUSIONS: The findings of this study suggest that YHD promotes microglial polarization from M1 to M2 in OVX + CUMS mice and that the underlying mechanism involves of the PI3K/AKT/NF-kappaB pathway.
