ETHNOPHARMACOLOGICAL RELEVANCE: Perimenopausal obesity and depression are highly prevalent and debilitating comorbidities in aging women, yet effective integrative treatment options remain limited. Chaihu Shugan San (CHSGS), a classic traditional herbal formula, has long been utilized in clinical practice for treating perimenopausal symptoms and mood disorders. However, whether CHSGS exerts therapeutic efficacy in perimenopausal obesity with depression (POD), and the mechanisms underlying its actions remain unclear. AIM OF THE STUDY: To evaluate the therapeutic effects of CHSGS on POD and to elucidate its underlying molecular mechanisms. METHODS: A POD mouse model was established by combining ovariectomy (OVX), chronic unpredictable mild stress (CUMS), and a high-fat diet (HFD). CHSGS efficacy was assessed through biochemical analysis, organ indices, and a battery of behavioral assays. Serum and hepatic triglycerides (TG), total cholesterol (TC), and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) were quantified, and hepatic steatosis was evaluated using hematoxylin-eosin and Oil Red O staining. RNA sequencing was performed to identify differentially regulated pathways, followed by qRT-PCR and Western blot validation. UPLC-HRMS and network pharmacology were used to identify core bioactive constituents and putative targets. Molecular docking further characterized interactions between major compounds and key proteins. RESULTS: POD mice developed obesity, loose stools, and pronounced anxiety- and depression-like behaviors. CHSGS treatment markedly alleviated these pathological manifestations, reduced hyperlipidemia, and improved hepatic steatosis and glucose intolerance. CHSGS decreased systemic and hepatic TG, TC, and LDL-C levels, and improved histopathological scores. Transcriptomic profiling indicated that the metabolic benefits of CHSGS are associated with modulation of the PGC-1alpha/CYP8B1/PPARgamma axis. Subsequent qRT-PCR and Western blot analyses confirmed that CHSGS reversed POD-induced dysregulation of the PGC-1alpha/CYP8B1/PPARgamma pathway. Integrated analyses further revealed that CHSGS upregulated the tight-junction protein Occludin, thereby enhancing intestinal barrier integrity, reducing systemic inflammation, and mitigating depression-like behaviors. Molecular docking identified glycyrrhizic acid, naringin, and naringenin as principal bioactive constituents with strong affinities for the core targets. CONCLUSION: CHSGS ameliorates POD through a coordinated, multi-target mechanism involving restoration of hepatic lipid metabolism via regulation of the PGC-1alpha/CYP8B1/PPARgamma pathway, and reinforcement of intestinal barrier function through Occludin-mediated suppression of systemic inflammation. These findings highlight CHSGS as a promising holistic therapeutic approach for POD, reflecting the multi-component and multi-pathway pharmacological characteristics of traditional formulas.
