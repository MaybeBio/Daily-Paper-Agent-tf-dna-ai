# Natural flavonoid dihydroquercetin blocks Trim14-JAK1-STAT3 signaling and relieves pain-depression comorbidity in rheumatoid arthritis


## Abstract
  Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is frequently complicated by chronic pain, anxiety and depression, yet the neuroimmune mechanisms linking peripheral inflammation to central affective dysfunction remain elusive. Here we demonstrate that the natural flavonoid dihydroquercetin (DHQ) blocks Trim14 and relieves pain-depression comorbidity in RA. Serum Trim14 is elevated in RA patients and correlates with clinical symptom severity. We identify Trim14 as a contributing factor of JAK1/STAT3 signaling in spinal dorsal horn neurons, promoting both nociceptive sensitization and affective symptoms in collagen-induced arthritis (CIA) mice. Intrathecal Trim14 siRNA attenuates synovial inflammation, inflammatory pain and anxiety-depressive behaviors. Molecular docking and molecular dynamics simulations reveal that DHQ occupies the Trim14 PRYSPRY substrate-recognition pocket with sub-micromolar affinity. Long-term DHQ treatment improves both nociceptive and affective symptoms by inhibiting spinal Trim14-JAK1-STAT3 axis in CIA mice. These findings suggest Trim14 as a potential neuroimmune target and DHQ as a natural medicine modulator for pain-depression comorbidity in chronic inflammatory disease.


## Introduction
  Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a chronic autoimmune disease affecting 0.5–1% of the global population, characterized by progressive joint inflammation, synovial destruction, and persistent pain [1]. Critically, 80% of RA patients suffer from chronic pain despite adequate anti-inflammatory treatment, and 40% experience comorbid anxiety or depression—substantially higher rates than the general population [2]. This pain-depression comorbidity severely impairs quality of life and increases healthcare burden, yet the underlying neuroimmune mechanisms remain elusive. Although inflammatory responses appear to be associated with the progression of RA [3], the precise pathogenesis of RA remains unclear.

  Janus kinase/signal transducer and activator of transcription (JAK/STAT) represent a central signaling axis in RA pathogenesis, mediating cytokine-driven synovial inflammation and joint destruction [4]. Beyond peripheral tissues, neuronal JAK/STAT activation in the spinal cord contributes to nociceptive plasticity in diverse chronic pain models, including inflammatory arthritis, bone cancer pain, and neuropathic injury [5]. However, the molecular mechanism by which JAK/STAT is regulated to promote RA development remain partially unclear [6].

  Tripartite motif-containing protein 14 (Trim14), an E3 ubiquitin ligase implicated in innate immune regulation and interferon signaling, has recently been identified as a modulator of JAK/STAT pathway activation [7]. The Trim14 protein comprises a B-box, a coiled-coil region, and a C-terminal PRYSPRY domain, and human Trim14 protein exhibits a highly conserved sequence homology with its mouse counterpart, and Trim14 transcriptional patterns are conserved between humans and mice [8]. While Trim14 is established in peripheral immune responses, its expression and function within the nervous system have not been characterized. We hypothesized that spinal dorsal horn neuronal Trim14 promotes RA chronic pain and comorbid anxiety-depression through JAK1/STAT3 pathway activation.

  The spinal dorsal horn (SDH), serving as a critical relay for nociceptive signal transmission, exhibits neuronal hyperexcitability that can influence higher brain functions through multiple ascending pathways [9–11]. Neuroimmune interactions constitute the core mechanism promoting pain-depression comorbidity. Peripheral joint inflammation activates SDH neurons through peripheral-central sensitization mechanisms, releasing pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, IL-6 and TNF-α) and neuromodulators that enhance excitatory synaptic transmission to trigger a pathological vicious cycle of periphery-SDH-brain [12–14]. However, the role of Trim14 in the transition from RA-related chronic pain to affective disorders requires further study.

  Given the close correlation between the Trim 14 and the JAK/STAT in RA [15–17], we hypothesize that Trim14 regulates both nociception and affective behaviors through JAK1/STAT3 signaling [18]. Dihydroquercetin (DHQ, also known as taxifolin) is a natural flavonoid with anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and neuroprotective properties, recently shown to inhibit JAK/STAT signaling and improve RA [19–21]. We further investigated whether DHQ could improve nociception and affective symptoms in CIA mice by inhibiting JAK/STAT signaling. Overall, we integrated clinical serology, behavioral pharmacology, genetic silencing, and computational biophysics to demonstrate that the natural flavonoid DHQ blocks Trim14 with sub-micromolar affinity, relieving pain-depression comorbidity in rheumatoid arthritis.


## Materials and methods

### Ethical approval
  Random sampling methods were adopted in this study. Serum samples were collected from inpatients and outpatients in the Department of Rheumatology and Immunology of the Affiliated Zhangjiagang Hospital of Soochow University from April 2023 to October 2023. Inclusion: 2010 ACR/EULAR criteria, DAS28-CRP > 3.2, disease duration > 6 months. Exclusion: other autoimmune diseases, severe organ dysfunction, biologic use within 3 months. Disease activity: DAS28-CRP scores (RA group mean 4.8 ± 0.6). Medication history: All patients on methotrexate (mean 12.5 mg/week), no biologics, glucocorticoids, analgesics, antidepressants. NRS using 10-point scale, HAMA-14/HAMD 24-item versions, scored by two trained physicians independently, blinded to group, cutoff > 3 for pain, cutoff > 7 for anxiety/depression. All RA patients met the American rheumatism association classification criteria. Age- and sex-matched healthy controls (n = 10) were recruited via poster advertisement. Screening included: (i) structured interview excluding chronic pain (>3 months), inflammatory/autoimmune disease; (ii) MINI 7.0.2 excluding current/lifetime psychiatric diagnoses; (iii) medication review excluding analgesics, psychotropics, or corticosteroids within 4 weeks. First-degree family history of major psychiatric disorders was additionally excluded.


### Animal models
  DBA/1 mice purchased from Shanghai SLAC Laboratory Animal Co, Ltd., 7-week-old males, 18–22 g body weight. For the duration of this study, mice were maintained on a 12 h light / 12 h dark cycle without restriction of food and water. DBA/1 was used to establish the CIA model. After 1 week of feeding, in the first stage, bovine collagen type II (chondrex, 20022, USA) was mixed with Complete Freund’s Adjuvant (sigma, F5881, Germany) in equal proportion to the final concentration of 1 mg/mL. Each mouse was injected 0.1 mL subcutaneously into the root of the tail. Three weeks later, the mice were reimmunized with the same amount of bovine type II collagen mixed with Incomplete Freund’s Adjuvant. After that, continuous observation, weighing, CIA modeling score and behavioral experiments were performed. In the second stage, mice were divided into Control group, CIA group, Trim14-NC group and Trim14-siRNA (siBDM0001, RIBO, China) group. Trim14 siRNA was locally injected at day 21 once a week until sacrifice [22] and mice in Trim14-NC group were injected with 0.9% NaCl. Animal behavior testing conditions: temperature 22 °C, humidity 60%, lighting 60 lux, noise < 60 dB, 30-min habituation before testing, timing 9:00–12:00 AM (light phase), avoiding circadian effects. Randomization: Stratified by body weight, random number table. Exclusion criteria: CIA score < 4 (model failure).


### Synovitis evaluation and immunohistochemical staining of knee joint
  The right knee was excised, fixed in 10% formaldehyde, and decalcified with a rapid decalcification solution for one week before embedding in paraffin. Using a microtome, 5-mm-thick sections were cut from the tissue. After dewaxing and rehydration, the sections were stained with anti-mouse IL-1β antibody (1: 400, ab9722, Abcam, UK). The right knee was also fixed into 4% paraformaldehyde, paraffin-embedded, and stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) for pathological analysis due to the pathological assessment of synovial tissue.


### Paw withdrawal frequency threshold
  The mechanical pain threshold of mice was measured using 0.07 and 0.4 g von Frey filaments (Stoelting, USA). The middle part of the hind paw of each mouse was stimulated vertically with the cilia filament. A positive response was recorded when mice withdrew their paws or lifted their legs. The positive response frequency was recorded during the 10 consecutive stimuli, with an interval of 5 s. The paw withdrawal frequency (PWF) = (number of effective responses /10) × 100% was calculated to evaluate the mechanical pain threshold.


### Paw withdrawal thermal latency
  The right plantar of mice in each group was irradiated with a moving thermal radiation source (Ugo Basile, Italy), and the irradiation was stopped when the mice quickly lifted or licked their feet. The duration of thermal radiation, or paw withdrawal latency (PWL), was recorded for 5 times for each mouse with an interval of 5 min, and the average value was calculated. To avoid foot burns in mice, stimulate the time set to 15 s, if no response occurred within 15 s, the test was terminated to prevent tissue damage.


### Western blotting
  The L3–L5 segments of the spinal dorsal horn (SDH) were lysed with protease inhibitor cocktail (P001, NCM Biotech, China) in RIPA lysis buffer (P0013B, Beyotime, China). The cleavage products were centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 20 min at 4 °C to remove the supernatant. Protein samples were prepared by adding 5 × loading and transferred to nitrocellulose (NC) transfer membrane (HATF0010, Millipore, USA) by SDS-PAGE. The NC membrane was incubated at room temperature for 2 h in 5% BSA, rinsed with TBST, and then incubated with an antibody at 4 °C overnight. The primary antibodies used include Trim14 (1: 1000, bs-16724R, Bioss, China), JAK1 (1: 1000, 66466-1-Ig, Proteintech, China), STAT3 (1:3000, 60199-1-Ig, Proteintech, China), p-STAT3 (1:2000, ab76315, Abcam, UK), GAPDH (1:5000, 60004-1-Ig Proteintech, China). TBST was used to wash the membrane three times for 10 min, followed by 2 h of incubation with the HRP Goat anti-Mouse IgG secondary antibody (1:5000, #31430, Invitrogen) or HRP Goat anti-Rabbit IgG secondary antibody (1:5000, #31460, Invitrogen). The blots were detected using ImageQuant LAS4000 Mini image analyzer (GE Healthcare, Buckinghamshire, UK) and analyzed using ImageJ software. Each experiment was conducted at least six times.


### Immunofluorescence staining
  Mice were anesthetized with 1% sodium pentobarbital, perfused with normal saline and 4% formaldehyde, and L3–L5 spinal cord tissues were harvested. After sampling, the samples were immersed in 4% formaldehyde for 4 h and then placed overnight at 4 °C in 20 and 30% sucrose dissolved in 0.1 M PBS, respectively. Spinal cord sections of 15 μm thickness were processed 3 times with 0.3% Triton-X-100 for 10 min each time, and then blocked with goat serum for 1 h at room temperature. The primary antibody (rabbit antibody, 1:100; mouse antibody, 1:100) was incubated in a cold wet box at 4 °C overnight, and the fluorescent secondary antibody (donkey anti-mouse IgG Alexa Fluor® 488, 1:800, ab150129, Abcam; donkey anti-rabbit IgG Alexa Fluor® 555, 1:800, ab150074, Abcam) was incubated in the dark after washing three times with PBS sealed with DAPI to dry. The plates were viewed and photographed under a confocal microscope.


### ELISA assay
  Human Trim14 ELISA Kit (EH13190) was purchased from FineTest. The serum samples from 10 RA patients and 10 normal controls were operated according to the instructions of the human Trim14 ELISA kit. The measurement principle was based on a sandwich technique, and the final reaction solution was read for OD absorbance at 450 nm using a microplate reader. Analytical statistics were plotted using OD absorbance correlation data.


### RNA interference of Trim14
  Trim14 siRNA sequences were effective and reliable at the cellular and animal levels [23], and purchased from New England Biolabs. Injection dilution method: Dilute the siRNA with enzyme-free water to a final concentration of 5 nM, 10 μL of this solution was added to 5 μL of enzyme-free water and 5 μL of transfection reagent (EntransterTM-in vivo, Engreen Biosystem Co, Ltd.) to form a 20 μL solution that was administered to each mouse. The Trim14 siRNA sequences were as follows: 5′-CCTGTCAAGAACTTCTTTA-3′.


### Molecular docking and molecular dynamics simulation
  The TRIM14 PRYSPRY domain structure (PDB: 6JBM) and dihydroquercetin (PubChem: 442588) were prepared for docking using AutoDock Vina 1.2.3 with exhaustiveness = 32. The top-ranked pose (ΔG = −8.44 kcal/mol) was subjected to triplicate 100-ns molecular dynamics simulations (AMBER 24, ff14SB/GAFF2, TIP3P, 298 K, 1 atm). Binding free energies were calculated by MM/GBSA (igb = 2) on 90–100 ns trajectories.


### Drug administration
  The CIA mice were randomly divided into 2 groups: vehicle (Veh) and dihydroquercetin (DHQ) group. The CIA mice were administered DHQ (100 mg/kg) continuously for two weeks from day 22 to day 35 once daily via oral gavage. DHQ was stored in powder form at −20 °C. When used, it was dissolved and diluted with normal saline (NS) to a working concentration. Dihydroquercetin (purity ≥ 98%) used in this study was extracted from the roots of artificially cultivated Larix olgensis (Changbai larch) by Jilin Jianwei Natural Biotechnology.


### Open field test
  The mice were placed in a chamber measuring 40 × 40 × 35 cm and observed for a duration of 10 min to monitor their movement. Following each test session, the chamber was meticulously cleaned using 75% ethanol and thoroughly dried. The Anymaze video tracking system (manufactured by Stoelting, Wood Dale, IL, USA) was used to measure the overall distance covered by the mice in the open field.


### Elevated plus maze test
  The maze was elevated 40 cm above the floor, which comprised of four arms (two arms devoid of walls and two arms enclosed by 15 cm high walls), measuring 30 cm in length and 5 cm in width. Individual mice were placed at the center of the maze, facing the enclosed arms, and their behavior was recorded for a duration of 5 min. The time spent on both the open and closed arms was documented and subsequently analyzed through the implementation of the Anymaze video tracking system (Stoelting, USA).


### Forced swimming test
  The mice were subjected to a 6-min swim session in clear cylinders (30 cm height × 20 cm diameter) filled with 15 cm of water (25 °C). Sessions were recorded using a digital video-camera. Immobility time, defined as passive floating with no additional activity other than that necessary to keep the animal’s head above the water, was scored for the last 4 min of the 6-min test by a trained observer.


### Tail suspension test
  The mice were suspended by the tail on a station (50 cm above the floor) for 6 min using adhesive tape placed 2 cm from the tip of the tail; the test was videotaped from the side and the immobility time of the mice was measured for the last 4 min period. Animals were considered immobile when there were no initiated movements. The cumulative immobility time was recorded by an investigator who was blinded to the experimental groups.


### Sucrose preference test
  The mice were singly housed and habituated to two bottles filled with drinking water (50-ml tubes with fitted ball-point sipper tubes) filled with drinking water for 1 day, and then mice were given access to a two-bottle choice of water vs 1% sucrose solution for the consecutive 2 days. Bottles were daily weighed and interchanged (left to right, right to left) to avoid biases from side preference. After this period, mice were left undisturbed, and their 12 h overnight fluid consumption was measured the next morning. Sucrose preference was calculated as a percentage (amount of sucrose consumed × 100/total volume consumed) [24].


### Novelty suppressed feeding test
  The mice were singly housed and food-deprived for twenty-four hours in home-cages. One normal chow diet pellet was placed on an inverted weighing-boat platform (10 × 10 × 1.5 cm) in the center of an open-field arena (40 × 40 cm). The mice were introduced into a corner of the arena. The time needed for the mice to take a bite of food was recorded over a 10-min period by a trained observer. After the test, the mice were returned to their home cage containing pre-weighed food pellets, and latency to start biting the pellet, as well as consumption was recorded for a period of 10 min.


### Statistical analysis
  All experiments (behavioral tests, drug administration, molecular detection) and data analyses were carried out following the double-blind principles. The normality of all the data was verified before analysis. Results are expressed as mean value ± standard error (SEM). Two-way ANOVA followed by Sidak multiple comparisons test was conducted. Graphs were created with Graphpad Prism 10 (GraphPad Software, USA). P < 0.05 was considered a statistically significant difference.


## Results

### RA patients overexpressed Trim14 and exhibited anxious-depressive states
  The serum samples of 10 RA patients and 10 normal controls were collected from the Department of Rheumatology and Immunology. The results showed that the concentrations of Trim14 and IL-1β in RA patients were higher than that in normal controls (Con) at OD450 nm (Fig. 1A–E). We further observed that RA patients exhibited higher Numerical Rating Scale (NRS), Hamilton Anxiety Scale (HAMA) and Hamilton Depression Scale (HAMD) scores compared to normal controls (Fig. 1F–H). These results indicated that RA patients displayed chronic inflammatory pain and anxious-depressive states compared to normal controls. To investigate the clinical relevance of elevated Trim14 in RA patients, we performed Pearson correlation analyses between serum Trim14 levels and clinical parameters. Serum Trim14 exhibited positive correlations with NRS, HAMA and HAMD in RA patients (Fig. 1I–K). ### Trim14 siRNA attenuated inflammatory pain-induced depression-like behaviors in CIA mice
  After Trim14 siRNA was injected intrathecally into the L3–L5 SDH once a week on day 21, we further investigated the effects of CIA and Trim14 on depression-like behaviors. To detect the desire to survive, we tested the Con, CIA, NC and Trim14 siRNA mice at day 35 by forced swimming test and tail suspension test. The results showed that the immobility time was increased in CIA mice, but recovered in Trim14 siRNA mice (Fig. 7G–J). We further performed sucrose preference test and novelty suppressed feeding test to examine the anhedonia in Con, CIA, NC and Trim14 siRNA mice at day 35. The results displayed that the sucrose preference was reduced and eating latency was prolonged in CIA mice, but reversed in Trim14 siRNA mice (Fig. 7K–P). ## Discussion
  In this study, we first found that serum Trim14 was abnormally elevated in RA patients. Then we demonstrated that Trim14 exacerbates chronic inflammatory pain in RA by potentiating the JAK1/STAT3 signaling axis. After injection of Trim14 siRNA, we showed that Trim14 siRNA reduced paw swelling, synovitis, chronic inflammatory pain and anxious-depressive states in CIA mice. Lastly, long-term DHQ treatment relieves nociceptive and affective symptoms by inhibiting the Trim14-JAK1-STAT3 axis in CIA mice. Trim14 showed a possible therapeutic target in rheumatoid arthritis.

  RA is characterized by progressive and aggressive disease, synovitis and pannus are the main pathological changes of RA [28]. JAK/STAT signal transduction pathway is closely related to the regulation of synovial cell proliferation signal to nuclear gene expression in RA, which can widely regulate cell proliferation, differentiation, apoptosis and immune inflammatory response. Studies have shown that JAK are activated when inflammatory factors are linked to their receptors. JAK creates docking sites for STAT proteins. After phosphorylation and dimerization, STAT is transferred to the nucleus to bind to DNA and promote the transcription of proinflammatory genes such as IL-1β [29–31]. Therefore, JAK/STAT signaling is closely related to inflammatory response in the pathogenesis of rheumatoid arthritis.

  TRIM14 (Tripartite Motif-containing protein 14) is an E3 ubiquitin ligase involved in regulating innate immune responses [32]. Previous studies have shown that the mRNA expression levels of IL-1β in Trim14 deficient mice are suppressed on chronic periodontal inflammation [33]. The pathophysiology of RA is characterized by synovial cell proliferation and persistent synovial inflammation. Under the stimulation of IL-1β, immune cells are activated to secrete more pro-inflammatory factors to further promote synovial inflammation [34]. Therefore, the continuous expression of Trim14 may promote synovial inflammation in RA.

  At present, the drugs used in clinical practice can be divided into several categories: disease-improving antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs), non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) and glucocorticoids (GCs) biological agents, natural products [35], small-molecule targeted drugs and radionuclide drugs. Small molecule targeted drugs that inhibit signal transduction by specifically acting on a certain signal molecule in the pathogenic signal transduction pathway of RA are a new direction for the treatment of RA, and JAK kinase inhibitors that block the JAK/STAT signaling pathway by chemical technology are representative of them. Tofacitinib, baricitinib and upadacitinib have been approved for clinical use and have achieved good therapeutic effects. Recent studies have shown that JAK inhibitors can effectively reduce residual pain in RA patients [36–38]. Therefore, the in-depth study of Trim14 may have a potential promotion effect on the treatment of RA in clinical practice.

  Our study reveals a significant elevation of serum Trim14 in RA patients compared to healthy controls, with positive correlations between Trim14 levels and multiple clinical parameters including pain intensity (NRS), anxiety symptoms (HAMA), and depressive symptoms (HAMD). These findings establish Trim14 as a clinically relevant biomarker that bridges peripheral inflammation with central symptomatology in RA. We note that the anxiety/depression-like behaviors observed in CIA models represent disease-induced secondary anxiety/depression, rather than primary anxiety/depression. Trim14 siRNA alleviates these affective symptoms through amelioration of the primary pathological drivers-chronic inflammatory pain in CIA. This mechanism suggests that the anxiolytic/antidepressant effects are downstream consequences of pain relief, rather than direct modulation of central anxiety/depression circuits. SDH is a critical relay for nociceptive signals projecting to thalamus, amygdala, and anterior cingulate cortex. Hyperexcitability of SDH neurons can influence higher brain functions through thalamo-cortical and spino-parabrachial-amygdala pathways [9–11]. Intrathecal siRNA injections can indeed reach the brain ventricles and brain regions via cerebrospinal fluid circulation [39]. While intrathecal injection primarily targets the spinal cord, potential roles of Trim14 in the brain require further investigation.

  Local intra-articular therapies are clinically established and well-tolerated, and demonstrating their capacity to improve both peripheral and central disease manifestations supports a broader therapeutic strategy. Local joint inflammation can affect the spinal cord through peripheral-central sensitization, and conversely, spinal modulation can influence peripheral inflammation. The therapeutic efficacy of locally delivered Trim14 siRNA likely involves multiple convergent pathways. First, peripheral joint Trim14 knockdown directly reduces synovial inflammation and nociceptor sensitization at the source, decreasing afferent drive to the spinal cord. Second, the resulting reduction in neurogenic inflammation and circulating inflammatory mediators indirectly attenuates spinal neuroimmune activation. Third, retrograde signaling along sensitized nociceptive fibers may directly modulate spinal neuronal gene expression. Finally, improved peripheral joint status reduces guarding behavior and mechanical stress, indirectly normalizing central pain processing.

  The involvement of JAK/STAT signaling extends beyond RA to diverse chronic pain conditions, including bone cancer pain and neuropathic pain, where spinal JAK2/STAT3 activation drives nociceptive sensitization [40–42]. Our identification of Trim14 as an upstream regulator of JAK1/STAT3 suggests a conserved neuroimmune mechanism across pain etiologies, while highlighting disease-specific differences in JAK isoform utilization that may inform targeted therapeutic strategies. Previous studies have focused primarily on cytokine-driven JAK/STAT activation, our work implicates the proteasome system and interferon signaling, pathways in which Trim14 plays established regulatory roles, in the modulation of pain-depression comorbidity.

  This study identifies Trim14 as a potential druggable neuroimmune node and DHQ as a prototype natural-product modulator that relieves pain-depression comorbidity by direct target blockade. Our computational analyses reveal sub-micromolar engagement of the Trim14 PRYSPRY domain, with persistent hydrogen bonds to GLU-423 and SER-343 trapping the Trim14 in a substrate-incompetent conformation. This structural mechanism, validated by 100-ns molecular dynamics and convergent genetic and pharmacological relief, positions Trim14-JAK1-STAT3 signaling as a tractable interface between peripheral inflammation and affective circuitry. The relief paradigm demonstrated here carries translational implications. First, DHQ achieves CNS-relevant concentrations after oral administration, circumventing blood-brain barrier limitations of synthetic JAK inhibitors. Second, the identified binding hot spots (GLU-423/SER-343/ALA-342) provide a roadmap for structure-guided optimization. Third, the convergence of siRNA-mediated and DHQ-mediated relief establishes causality for Trim14 in neuroimmune affective dysfunction, supporting clinical translation for mood disorders associated with chronic inflammation.

  We acknowledge that our study provides associative rather than causal evidence for the Trim14-JAK1-STAT3 axis in RA-related pain and affective behaviors. While we observed parallel reductions in Trim14, JAK1, and p-STAT3 following Trim14 siRNA or DHQ treatment, and demonstrated functional rescue through behavioral improvements, we recognize that these findings do not exclude contributions from alternative pathways or off-target effects. Specifically, Trim14 is known to regulate multiple signaling cascades beyond JAK/STAT, including NF-κB activation, interferon signaling, and selective autophagy. Furthermore, the pharmacological and genetic interventions employed in our study lack absolute specificity. Trim14 siRNA may have partial off-target effects on related TRIM family members; DHQ exhibits broad bioactivity including antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and ion channel modulatory properties beyond JAK/STAT inhibition. Future studies employing conditional genetic approaches, pathway-specific rescue experiments, and unbiased proteomic or transcriptomic analyses will be essential to dissect the relative contributions of JAK/STAT versus alternative Trim14 effector pathways, and to validate the therapeutic potential of this axis in RA and related conditions.

  Several limitations should be acknowledged when interpreting these correlations. First, our human sample size (n = 10 per group) is modest, reflecting the exploratory nature of this biomarker analysis. While the effect sizes are statistically significant, validation in larger, multi-center cohorts is essential to establish Trim14’s diagnostic and prognostic utility. Second, the cross-sectional design precludes causal inference; longitudinal studies tracking Trim14 levels through disease flares, treatment changes, and symptom evolution would clarify its predictive value. Third, we did not assess potential confounders such as sleep quality, chronic fatigue, or cognitive function that may mediate the Trim14-symptom relationship. Fourth, the absence of cerebrospinal fluid or synovial fluid Trim14 measurements limits our ability to determine the tissue source of circulating Trim14 and its relevance to central nervous system pathology. Notwithstanding these limitations, our correlation findings have important translational implications. Our data suggest that Trim14 could guide personalized selection of JAK inhibitors or identify candidates for novel Trim14-targeted therapies, such as the DHQ-based approach demonstrated in our animal studies.


## Conclusion
  In this study, human serum Trim14 of rheumatoid arthritis patients were elevated, and Trim14-JAK1-STAT3 in the synovium and SDH of CIA mice were upregulated. Trim14 siRNA alleviated JAK1/STAT3 expression, paw swelling, inflammatory factor, and pain-depression comorbidity in CIA mice. Furthermore, long-term DHQ treatment relieved both nociceptive and affective symptoms by inhibiting the Trim14-JAK1-STAT3 axis in CIA mice.
