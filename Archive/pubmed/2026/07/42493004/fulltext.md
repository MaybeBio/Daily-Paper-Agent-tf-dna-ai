Bisphenol A (BPA) is an important environmental pollutant, which may indirectly affect insecticide susceptibility by interfering with key regulatory pathways in insects. However, the molecular mechanism of the cross-tolerance to chitin synthesis inhibitors (CSIs) in BPA-exposed Aedes albopictus remains poorly understood. Here, we show that BPA causes abnormal chitin synthesis by regulating estrogen-related receptor (ERR), imparting cross-tolerance to CSIs in Ae. albopictus. Crucially, BPA significantly modulated AalERR transcription and displayed potent insecticidal activity in Ae. albopictus, with an LC(50) of 0.011 mg/L. The increased chitin content in BPA-exposed groups with resistance ratio of 12.260 and the increased susceptibility after silencing AalERR with LC(50) of 0.542 mg/L shifted to 0.313 mg/L of Ae. albopictus to hexaflumuron, suggesting that AalERR played a crucial role in mediating the resistance to chitin synthesis inhibitors by modulating chitin synthesis. Moreover, isothermal titration calorimetry (ITC) confirmed high-affinity binding between BPA and AalERR, while Glu303 was interaction amino acid residue by molecular docking and site-directed mutation. Consequently, BPA promotes cross-tolerance to CSIs in Ae. albopictus by modulating AalERR signaling, suggesting a novel mechanism of cross-tolerance and AalERR as a key regulatory pathway for conferring CSIs resistance. Selectively controlling AalERR activity with a specific modulator will provide critical insights for developing new strategies to combat insecticide resistance in Ae. albopictus.
