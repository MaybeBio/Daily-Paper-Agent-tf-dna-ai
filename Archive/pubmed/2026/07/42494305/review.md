## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence presented in the abstract; no access to full text, figures, tables, or supplementary materials
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report a novel NR3C1 frameshift variant (c.923dupG, p.Ala309Serfs*6) in two affected males across successive generations with familial glucocorticoid resistance syndrome (GRS). The variant is absent from population databases, co-segregates with the phenotype, and is classified as pathogenic by ACMG/AMP criteria. Clinical phenotyping, steroid metabolomic profiling, adrenal CT imaging, structural prediction via AlphaFold3, tissue and single-cell expression analyses, in silico virtual gene perturbation, and a systematic literature review of 66 previously reported GRS cases were performed. The authors propose that N-terminal truncating variants may be associated with cardiovascular complications, though they frame this as hypothesis-generating.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only. No figures, tables, methods details, or supplementary data were provided for verification.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, detailed steroid profiling data, segregation analysis results, AlphaFold3 structural outputs, ACMG/AMP scoring details, expression analysis methods and results, virtual perturbation methodology and outputs, literature review search strategy and case-level data, and any statistical analyses.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract describes a clinically and genetically well-characterized family with a novel NR3C1 frameshift variant and a distinctive steroid profile. The core clinical and genetic findings are plausible and potentially valuable for the GRS literature. However, the abstract makes several claims that cannot be evaluated without the full manuscript, particularly regarding structural prediction, in silico perturbation, and the proposed genotype-phenotype association with cardiovascular events. The latter is appropriately framed as hypothesis-generating, which is commendable. The main limitation of the current submission is that the evidence base provided is insufficient to assess the technical rigor of the analyses or the strength of the conclusions.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Clinicians and researchers in endocrinology, particularly those focused on adrenal disorders, glucocorticoid signaling, and rare genetic causes of hypertension. Geneticists and molecular biologists studying NR3C1 structure-function relationships and nuclear receptor signaling would also find the variant characterization relevant. The steroid profiling data may interest clinical biochemists involved in diagnostic metabolomics.
- **Major strengths** The report addresses a rare and frequently misdiagnosed condition with a clear clinical message. The combination of clinical phenotyping, steroid profiling, genetic segregation, and structural prediction represents a comprehensive approach. The variant is novel, absent from population databases, and co-segregates with the phenotype, which are strong genetic findings. The authors appropriately acknowledge the hypothesis-generating nature of the cardiovascular association.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** The variant was classified as pathogenic according to ACMG/AMP criteria.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract states that the variant was classified as pathogenic by ACMG/AMP criteria but provides no details on which criteria were applied, how they were weighted, or whether the classification was based on PVS1, PM2, PP1, PP3, or other evidence. The classification is a central claim that supports the clinical significance of the variant.  
- **Why it matters** ACMG/AMP classification requires transparent documentation of the evidence used. Without this, the pathogenicity claim cannot be independently verified, and the clinical recommendation to test for this variant in other patients is not fully supported.  
- **Resolution test** In the full manuscript, provide a detailed ACMG/AMP scoring table listing each criterion, the evidence used, and the final classification. The scoring should be consistent with current guidelines.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Technical validity  
- **Claim pointer** AlphaFold3 was used to predict the structures of wild-type and mutant GRalpha proteins, and structural prediction indicated premature termination within the N-terminal transactivation domain.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract claims that AlphaFold3 predicted the structure of the mutant protein, but AlphaFold3 is not designed to predict the structure of a truncated protein resulting from a frameshift with a premature stop codon. The mutant protein is expected to be 314 amino acids in length, and the claim of "premature termination" is a direct consequence of the frameshift, not a structural prediction. The added value of AlphaFold3 in this context is unclear.  
- **Why it matters** If the structural prediction is presented as supporting evidence, it must be methodologically sound. Overstating the role of AlphaFold3 may mislead readers about the nature of the evidence. The loss of downstream domains is a logical consequence of the frameshift and does not require structural modeling.  
- **Resolution test** Clarify in the full manuscript what AlphaFold3 was actually used for, and present the structural data as confirmatory rather than predictive. Provide the predicted structures and explain how they inform the interpretation of the variant's impact.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** In silico virtual perturbation analysis suggested that NR3C1 dysfunction may extend beyond a linear defect in glucocorticoid signaling, potentially influencing neuroendocrine communication, extracellular matrix regulation, protein translation, and developmental pathways.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract reports broad biological pathway implications from an in silico virtual knockout analysis, but provides no details on the method, the data source, the significance thresholds, or the validation of these findings. The pathways listed are highly diverse and the claim is speculative.  
- **Why it matters** These claims go beyond the clinical and genetic findings and could be misinterpreted as established biology. Without methodological transparency and appropriate caveats, the abstract overreaches.  
- **Resolution test** In the full manuscript, describe the virtual perturbation method in detail, including the software, input data, and statistical criteria. Present the pathway results with appropriate effect sizes and uncertainty, and clearly state that these are exploratory hypotheses requiring experimental validation.

- **Concern ID** R1-M4  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** No  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** Literature review identified 66 reported GRS cases encompassing substantial phenotypic variability, with cardiovascular events documented in some carriers of N-terminal or truncating variants.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract states that cardiovascular events were documented in some carriers of N-terminal or truncating variants, but does not specify how many cases, what types of events, or whether this association was statistically evaluated. The claim is presented as a potential genotype-phenotype correlation.  
- **Why it matters** The proposed association between N-terminal truncating variants and cardiovascular complications is a novel claim that could influence clinical surveillance. It must be supported by a systematic and transparent review methodology, including case-level data and an assessment of ascertainment bias.  
- **Resolution test** In the full manuscript, provide a detailed table of the 66 cases with variant location, phenotype, and cardiovascular events. Describe the search strategy, inclusion criteria, and any statistical comparisons performed. Clearly state the limitations of the analysis.
