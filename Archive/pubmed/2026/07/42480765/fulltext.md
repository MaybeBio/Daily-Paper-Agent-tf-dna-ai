The KEAP1-NRF2 axis is the main regulator of the cellular antioxidant response. Recent studies identified the deubiquitinase USP25 as a key stabilizer of KEAP1, but the molecular basis of their interaction is unclear. In this study, the result of gel filtration and isothermal titration calorimetry (ITC) show that the USP25 catalytic domain binds directly to the KEAP1 Kelch domain with high affinity. Using mutational analysis of the canonical Kelch binding pocket and simple sequence searching, we identify a non-canonical ETGQ motif in USP25. AlphaFold 3 structural modeling and structure-guided mutagenesis show that this ETGQ motif is required and sufficient for binding to the Kelch beta-propeller. Because of this interaction, the closely related homolog USP28 does not regulate the KEAP1-NRF2 axis. This study defines the molecular architecture of the USP25-KEAP1 interface. It also provides a clear structural framework for the rational design of protein-protein interaction inhibitors aimed at treating oxidative stress-related diseases.
