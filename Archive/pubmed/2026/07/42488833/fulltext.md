# Huashi Baidu formula may attenuate adriamycin-induced myocardial injury via modulating NRF2-ATF3-SRXN1 axis-mediated ferroptosis


## Objective
  Myocardial injury is a fatal adverse effect of adriamycin (ADR), which greatly limits its clinical application. Numerous studies have shown that Huashi Baidu formula (HBF) may exert a satisfactory effect on myocardial function repair. This study aims to reveal the underlying mechanisms and main bioactive compounds (BACs) of HBF against myocardial injury.


## Methods
  Firstly, this study established an ADR-induced mouse model to assess the protective effects of HBF against myocardial injury. Subsequently, transcriptomic profiling was conducted to screen the differentially expressed genes among the HBF treatment, ADR-induced model, and normal control groups. Following the construction and analysis of protein–protein interactions, gene set enrichment analysis, and co-expression matrix, the key targets of HBF against myocardial injury were screened and further validated based on the ADR-induced mouse model. An integrative approach combining network mapping, molecular docking, and molecular dynamics simulation was performed to identify the representative BACs.


## Results
  Based on an ADR-induced myocardial injury mouse model, HBF significantly ameliorated myocardial injury by improving body weight loss, decreasing mortality, and rescuing severe cardiac fibrosis. A total of 361 ADR-related genes and 42 HBF therapeutic effect-related genes were identified with the thresholds P < 0.05 and fold change (FC) > 1.2 or < 0.833. Transcriptomic profiling-based networks demonstrated that the nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (NRF2)-cyclic AMP-dependent transcription factor ATF-3 (ATF3)-sulfiredoxin-1 (SRXN1) axis-mediated ferroptosis was the key target of HBF against myocardial injury. In vivo validation showed that HBF treatment effectively inhibited serum enzymatic biomarkers of general myocardial injury (aspartate aminotransferase, lactate dehydrogenase, and creatine kinase), myofibroblast activity (transforming growth factor beta 1 and α-smooth muscle actin), inflammatory cytokines (tumor necrosis factor-α and interleukin-6), oxidative damage (superoxide dismutase, malondialdehyde, accumulated iron content, and TUNEL-positive cells). Mechanically, HBF may restore the dysregulation of the NRF2-ATF3-SRXN1 signal axis, leading to inhibition of ferroptosis-associated protein expression. Emodin, rhein, and lactiflorin were identified as the underlying representative BACs of HBF against myocardial injury due to the strong binding affinities between candidate BACs and key targets [Kelch-like ECH-associated protein 1 (KEAP1), ATF3, SRXN1, system Xc- (SLC3A2/SLC7A11), glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4), ferroportin (FPN), glutathione synthetase (GSS)]. Molecular dynamics simulations further verified these findings, revealing the stable binding among the three components and the key targets.


## Conclusion
  HBF may attenuate ADR-induced myocardial injury via modulating NRF2-ATF3-SRXN1 axis-mediated ferroptosis, and emodin, rhein, and lactiflorin are potential representative BACs.


## Introduction
  Myocardial injury is rising as an increasing concern due to its clinical challenges, including irreversible cardiomyocyte loss (Taggart, Wereski, Mills, & Chapman, 2021), limited pathological remodeling (Azevedo, Polegato, Minicucci, Paiva, & Zornoff, 2016), and direct contribution to alarmingly high mortality rates across cardiovascular diseases (Taggart, Wereski, Mills, & Chapman, 2021). The causes exhibit a multifactorial etiopathogenesis, including iatrogenic triggers (e.g., chemotherapeutic agents like anthracyclines and immune checkpoint inhibitors) and complex comorbidities (diabetes mellitus, chronic kidney disease, sepsis) (Taggart, Wereski, Mills, & Chapman, 2021). Doxorubicin, also known as adriamycin (ADR) (Chen et al., 2014), is an anthracycline antibiotic (Ichikawa et al., 2014) that is clinically used as a promising chemotherapeutic agent for the treatment of various malignant tumors, such as leukemia, lymphoma, sarcoma or breast cancer (Chen et al., 2014, Ichikawa et al., 2014, He et al., 2022, Luo et al., 2023). Although it has a broad spectrum of antitumor activity, ADR has limited clinical applications due to cumulative cardiotoxicity, leading to left ventricular dysfunction or even heart failure (Herrmann, 2020). Current therapeutic approaches, such as β-blockers, angiotensin converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors, and aldosterone antagonists (Taggart, Wereski, Mills, & Chapman, 2021), primarily address secondary hemodynamic consequences rather than intervening in core pathogenic processes, including dysregulated cardiomyocyte death, calcium homeostasis disruption, mitochondrial bioenergetic failure, or sarcomeric protein degradation. Approximately 30%−40% of patients with myocardial injury have limited therapeutic effects, unable to effectively halt the progression of myocardial injury or fully restore myocardial function (Taggart, Wereski, Mills, & Chapman, 2021).

  Huashi Baidu formula (HBF) is an approved traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) for the treatment of corona virus disease 2019 (COVID-19) by the National Medical Products Administration of China (NMPA) (Wang et al., 2022), exerting satisfactory clinical efficacy. Previously, the team employed ultra-high performance liquid chromatography with quadrupole time-of-flight mass spectrometry (UPLC-Q/TOF-MS/MS) to characterize the chemical profiles of HBF both in vitro and in vivo. Its antiviral and anti-inflammatory effects have been verified through a series of in vivo and in vitro experiments (Xu et al., 2023). Clinical evidence-based research demonstrates that HBF significantly improves abnormal changes in organ function indicators in COVID-19 patients (Xiong et al., 2022), reducing the levels of lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), serum ferritin (SF), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), and creatinine (Cre) (Liu et al., 2021, Shi et al., 2021, Zhao et al., 2021, Xiong et al., 2022), which signifies it may have immunomodulatory, anti-inflammatory, and microcirculation-promoting effects. Notably, the HBF can significantly improve myocardial function, including lowering the elevated heart rate, expression levels of creatine kinase (CK) and creatine phosphokinase (CPK) isoenzyme (Shi et al., 2021, Wang et al., 2021). The previous study has demonstrated that the cardioprotective effects of HBF against myocardial injury (Li et al., 2024). However, its key bioactive compounds (BACs) and molecular mechanisms remain to be unraveled.

  In the current study, an ADR-induced animal model was constructed to verify the therapeutic efficacy of HBF against myocardial injury. After gene expression profiling-based network analysis, target prediction, and topological feature calculation, this study screened the key candidate targets of HBF for reversing the ferroptosis during the development and progression of myocardial injury. Then, this study carried out a series of validations in vivo to assess the regulation of HBF on the nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (NRF2)-cyclic AMP-dependent transcription factor ATF-3 (ATF3)-sulfiredoxin-1 (SRXN1) axis-mediated ferroptosis. The binding affinities of candidate BACs to key targets of HBF were assessed via molecular docking, followed by molecular dynamics (MD) simulations for further validation. Subsequently, in silico ADME models were utilized to screen for representative BACs capable of ameliorating myocardial injury by rescuing ferroptosis, with a focus on their intestinal permeability and systemic bioavailability.


## Material and methods

### Ethics approval and consent to participate
  The study was approved by the Research Ethics Committee of Institute of Chinese Materia Medica, China Academy of Chinese Medical Sciences, approval No. 2021B127 [certificate number of the facility: SYXK (Beijing) 2023-0077, Beijing, China]. All in vivo studies were carried out following the institutional guidelines of the China Academy of Chinese Medical Sciences and in compliance with the National Institute of Health (NIH) Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals.


### Preparation of HBF
  The TCM prescription was acquired from Guangdong Yifang Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd. (Foshan, Guangdong, China, National Drug Approval Number C20210002, Lot No. J2211023). Accordingly, the TCMs consist of 14 Chinese herbs (Xiong et al., 2022), see the details in supplementary materials Section 1.


### Mouse experiments
  A total of 69 specific-pathogen-free (SPF) male C57BL/6J mice [(21.00 ± 1.00) g, 6–8-week-old] were purchased from Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co., Ltd. [certificate number: SYXK (Beijing) 2019–0003, Beijing, China] and randomly assigned into the discovery cluster (n = 9, for transcriptome sequencing) and validation cluster (n = 60, for experimental validation). Detailed information on in vivo experiments as shown in supplementary materials Section 2.


### Survival and weight analyses
  After ADR induction, the survival of animals was monitored and mice of each group were weighed every day for 7 consecutive days. The survival and weight curves were delineated.


### Viscera index and histology
  The cardiac index was calculated by the weight of the heart relative to body weight. Cardiac tissues were deparaffinized for hematoxylin and eosin (H & E) and Masson trichrome staining according to routine protocols (Seo et al., 2018, Cheng et al., 2022) (Supplementary materials Section 3).


### Gene expression profiling
  The cardiac tissues were used for transcriptome sequencing. This study identified ADR-related and HBF therapeutic genes using the DESeq2 package in R (Love, Huber, & Anders, 2014) with the P < 0.05 and fold change (FC) > 1.2 or < 0.833. The datasets presented in the current study can be found in NCBI SRA data: PRJNA1285419 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sra/PRJNA1285419). Detailed information was shown in supplementary materials Section 4.


### Network construction and analyses
  After the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) screening, this study chose ADR-related genes reversed by HBF treatment for further analysis. The protein–protein interaction (PPI), gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) analysis, and co-expression matrix were constructed and analyzed based on our previous study (Li et al., 2020, Li et al., 2020, Li et al., 2021, Li et al., 2022, Li et al., 2022, Kanehisa et al., 2025, Mootha et al., 2003, Subramanian et al., 2005). Detailed information was shown in supplementary materials Section 5.


### Putative targets of HBF
  Previously, a total of 343 in vitro and 126 in vivo chemicals of HBF were characterized by UPLC-Q/TOF-MS/MS system (Xu et al., 2023). The known targets of HBF were acquired from the known database, including Herbal Ingredients’ Targets Platform (Yan et al., 2022) (Hit 2.0, http://hit2.badd-cao.net/), Bioinformatics Annotation Database for Molecular mechanism of Traditional Chinese Medicine (Kong et al., 2024) (BATMAN-TCM, http://bionet.ncpsb.org.cn/batman-tcm/, updated on 2023–10-07), and the Encyclopedia of Traditional Chinese Medicine 2.0 Databases (ETCM 2.0, http://www.tcmip.cn/ETCM2/front/#/, updated on 2022–06) (Zhang et al., 2023).


### Serum cardiac injury marker detection
  To further evaluate the cardiac injury, commercial enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits and biochemical testing kits were used to estimate AST and CK levels, as well as LDH release in plasma according to the manufacturer’s protocol (Table S1). Assays were performed on polystyrene 96-well plates. The AST, CK, and LDH concentrations were quantified against a standard curve calibrated with known amounts of protein. Each value is a mean of duplicated measurement.


### Cytokine analysis and lipid peroxidation measurement
  Superoxide dismutase (SOD) activity and malondialdehyde (MDA) content were measured using the testing kits. Simultaneously, tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) and interleukin-6 (IL-6) both in plasma and cardiac tissues were detected by ELISA according to the manufacturer's protocol. The testing kits used were shown in Table S1. Assays were performed on polystyrene 96-well plates. The SOD, MDA, TNF-α and IL-6 concentrations were quantified against a standard curve calibrated with known amounts of protein. Each value is a mean of duplicated measurement.


### Iron ion detection
  For cardiac tissues, the samples were washed with 2 mL of cold normal saline twice, and then, the normal saline was suck up. Normal saline (200 μL/g) was added to cardiac tissue samples to prepare tissue homogenates, and the homogenates were centrifuged to obtain the collected supernatants for iron-ion colorimetric detection. Cardiac tissue iron ion detection was performed using the Iron assay kit according to the manufacturer’s protocol (Table S1).


### Terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase-mediated dUTP biotin nick end labeling (TUNEL) staining
  Cardiac tissues were collected from mice when the experiment terminated and fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde 24 h before paraffin embedding. Samples were cut into 4-μm-thick longitudinally oriented sections and processed for cell death. Cardiomyocyte death was assessed using an in situ TUNEL staining according to the manufacturer’s protocol (Servicebio, Wuhan, China) and images were observed and collected with NIKON ECLIPSE C1 microscope with a DS-U3 camera system (Nikon, Japan).


### Immunofluorescence staining
  Persistent or severe myocardial injury can trigger excessive extracellular matrix deposition and maladaptive repair processes, ultimately progressing to myocardial fibrosis. Since myocardial fibrosis is a key determinant of long-term cardiac dysfunction and adverse remodeling, herein this study also evaluated fibrotic changes to more comprehensively reflect the cardiac damage. Transforming growth factor beta 1 (TGF-β1) and α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA) were further used to assess cardiac fibrosis. To detect the expression levels of phospholipid hydroperoxide glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4), NRF2, ATF3, and SRXN1 in the cardiac tissues, immunofluorescence staining was carried out as described previously (Li et al., 2020, Li et al., 2021, Li et al., 2022). ImageJ software (1.54 g, NIH, Bethesda, USA, https://imagej.net/ij/) was used for quantification. Protocols and antibodies were shown in supplementary materials Section 6 and Table S2, respectively.


### Western blotting analyses
  Total protein was extracted from cardiac tissues for immunoblotting analysis (Li et al., 2020; Li et al., 2021; Li et al., 2022). Briefly, proteins were separated by electrophoresis on SDS-polyacrylamide gels and transferred moist to nitrocellulose filter membranes. Membranes were incubated with primary antibodies against ferroportin (FPN), GPX4, glutathione synthetase (GSS), solute carrier family 3 member 2 (SLC3A2), and solute carrier family 7 member 11 (SLC7A11) overnight at 4 °C (Supplementary materials Table S3). After washing, the membranes were incubated with secondary antibody for 1 h. The images were captured on the Tanon 4800 (Shanghai, China). Western blotting bands were quantified using ImageJ software (1.54 g, NIH, Bethesda, USA, https://imagej.net/ij/) and normalizing to β-actin (1:5 000, Bioss Biotechnology, Beijing, China) as a loading control.


### Molecular docking simulation
  To screen the underlying representative BACs contained in HBF, the binding affinities between core targets and candidate BACs were evaluated using AutoDock Vina (Version 1.1.2). The three-dimensional structures of proteins (ATF3, KEAP1, SRXN1, System XC-, GSS, GPX4, FPN) and ligands were retrieved from the RCSB Protein Data Bank (PDB, https://www.rcsb.org/, supplementary materials Table S4) (Burley et al., 2022) and Chem Spider databases (https://www.chemspider.com/), respectively. Molecular docking was carried out using the AutoDock Vina and AutoDock package. Finally, the complexes were visualized by PyMOL version 1.7.2.1 (https://www.pymol.org/).


### Molecular dynamics (MD) simulation
  MD was simulated using the Gromacs2022 program on 100 ns (Van Der Spoel et al., 2005). The force field parameters of AMBER14SB (Maier et al., 2015), generalized amber force field (GAFF) (Wang, Wolf, Caldwell, Kollman, & Case, 2004), and TIP3P model (Nayar, Agarwal, & Chakravarty, 2011) were conducted for proteins, ligands, and water molecules, respectively, under constant temperature and pressure conditions and periodic boundary boundaries. Detailed methods refer to supplementary materials Section 7.


### Statistical analyses
  All statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism 8.2.1 Software (San Diego, CA, United States, https://www.graphpad.com/). Data are expressed as the Mean ± SEM and were analyzed by one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with Tukey’s test for multiple comparisons, considering P < 0.05 as statistically significant.


## Results

### HBF treatment significantly alleviates cardiac fibrosis in ADR-induced myocardial injury model mice
  To evaluate the pharmacological effects of HBF against cardiac fibrosis, an ADR-induced myocardial injury mouse model was established (Fig. 1A), manifesting body weight loss, increased mortality, and severe cardiac fibrosis (Fig. 1). In the HBF-treated group, the body weights were significantly increased from the second day after ADR induction (Fig. 1B). In addition, the mortality was decreased in the HBF treatment groups, especially in HBF-H group (Fig. 1C). And the heart index was markedly elevated after HBF treatment in a dose-dependent manner (Fig. 1D). Myocardial tissues from the normal control group illustrated well-organized cardiomyocytes with distinct cellular architecture and minimal collagen deposition in the extracellular matrix (Fig. 1G and H). After ADR induction, this study observed the manifestations of myocardial injury, evidenced by tissue edema, vacuolar degeneration, disorganized and ruptured fibers, irregular nuclei, and interstitial fibrosis (Fig. 1E–H). A clear segregation among groups was revealed (Fig. 2A–D). Then, the PPI network of “ADR-related targets-HBF effective targets” was constructed, and KEGG enrichment pathways were analyzed, which showed that HBF may improve myocardial injury via regulating cellular processes, imbalance of the “immune-inflammation” system, material and energy metabolism disorder, etc. (Fig. 2E). Among them, ferroptosis was a major cell metabolism-related form of cell death, occupying a central position within the PPI network.   Among the above DEGs, there were 16 ADR-related genes reversely regulated by the treatment of HBF, such as ATF3, BTG2, CCRL2, DUSP1, HAND2, HBA2, HBB, HMOX1, HSPB1, IFI27L2A, JUNB, LYZ2, NR4A1, SQSTM1, SRXN1, and TMSB4X. To further analyze the function of differentially expressed transcripts, DEGs were mapped with the FerrDb database (Zhou et al., 2023) (Version 2, http://www.zhounan.org/ferrdb/current/). A total of 8 genes were screened as shared targets for the ADR-related genes reversely regulated by the treatment of HBF and ferroptosis-related genes (Fig. 2G). These data demonstrated that HBF may regulate ferroptosis partially via the NRF2/ATF3/SRXN1 signal axis.

### HBF improves cardiac fibrosis via suppressing NRF2/ATF3/SRXN1-mediated ferroptosis signal axis
  To explore the molecular mechanisms, in vivo validations based on the ADR-induced myocardial injury mouse model were conducted based on the above findings. GPX4 is recognized as an essential endogenous regulator of ferroptosis (Friedmann Angeli et al., 2014). It is well-established that the downregulation of FPN, which plays a pivotal role in iron efflux, culminates in iron accumulation (Dixon et al., 2012), leading to ferroptosis (Li et al., 2022). Reducing GSS and system Xc- (SLC3A2 and SLC7A11) (Wu et al., 2022) inhibit glutathione (GSH) synthesis and subsequently promote ferroptosis (Peng et al., 2022). Thus, this study detected the expression levels of the key proteins, which are positively associated with ferroptosis sensitivity. The GPX4, FPN, GSS, SLC3A2, and SLC7A11 proteins were significantly declined in the ADR-induced mouse model group, while HBF treatment remarkably boosted the five proteins (Fig. 5A–F). Moreover, the GPX4-positive cells were decreased in the cardiomyocytes of ADR-induced mice, and HBF dose-independently improved the expression level of GPX4 protein (Fig. 5G and H). ## Discussion
  Accumulated evidence shows that ADR-associated myocardial injury, a potentially fatal side effect and significantly limiting the clinical application (Long et al., 2018), critically involves ferroptosis-induced cardiomyocyte oxidative injury, ultimately leading to myocardial fibrosis (Zou et al., 2021). Thus, suppressing the ferroptosis process may be a promising therapeutic strategy for anti-ADR cardiotoxicity. The current study employed an integrative approach combining transcriptome profiling, network calculation and analysis, and in vivo validations and identified the NRF2/ATF3/SRXN1 signaling axis as a crucial target of HBF for inhibiting ferroptosis-mediated cardiac fibrosis based on the ADR-induced mouse model. Moreover, a series of computer-based drug screening methods, including molecular docking, in silico ADME model calculation, as well as MD simulation was addressed. Three compounds-emodin, rhein, and lactiflorin, were identified as the key representative BACs contained in HBF on the targets for the strong binding affinities and their stabilization between small molecules and proteins.

  Ferroptosis is a type of programmed cell death characterized by its dependence on iron and the accumulation of lipid peroxides (Yang et al., 2022). The core mechanisms involve dysregulated redox homeostasis, aberrant iron metabolism, and collapse of membrane lipid repair systems (Liu et al., 2022). As the key subunit of system Xc-, the plasma membrane transporter SLC3A2 exchanges intracellular glutamate for extracellular cystine, enabling cellular cystine acquisition (Koppula, Zhuang, & Gan, 2021). Intracellular cystine rapidly converts to cysteine, which is a rate-limiting precursor of GSH synthesis (Koppula et al., 2022). GSS is a critical enzyme for GSH synthesis, which then adds glycine to the C-terminal of γ-glutamylcysteine. GSH serves as an essential cofactor for GPX4 (Zhang et al., 2021), which catalyzes the reduction of toxic lipid hydroperoxides (LOOH) (Tuo et al., 2022) to non-toxic lipid alcohols (Li et al., 2020), thereby protecting cellular membranes from oxidative damage. The inhibition or loss of GPX4 and depletion of GSH expression leads to lipid peroxides, such as MDA, which ultimately facilitates the ferroptosis process (Wang et al., 2017, Lee et al., 2020). FPN, as the sole known cellular iron exporter, is essential for cellular iron export to mediate efflux of ferrous iron (Fe2+) from cells and mitigate iron-induced cytotoxicity (Lakhal-Littleton et al., 2015). Downregulation of FPN promotes intracellular ferrous iron accumulation (Mironov et al., 2020), exacerbating Fenton reactions that convert LOOH into highly reactive radicals (Jeong et al., 2015). After that, oxidation of polyunsaturated fatty acid (PUFA)-containing phospholipids generates cytotoxic aldehydes (Chen, Kang, Kroemer, & Tang, 2021), including MDA and 4-hydroxynonenal (Lee, You, & Roh, 2022). MDA subsequently cross-links proteins and DNA, which impairs membrane fluidity and organelle function, culminating in plasma membrane rupture. This study demonstrated that the HBF could effectively prevent cardiac fibrosis partially via suppressing ferroptosis, exhibiting activating system Xc- and reducing iron accumulation, promoting GSH biosynthesis, and up-regulating GPX4 level.

  As a master regulator of antioxidant responses, increasing evidence demonstrates that NRF2 upregulates GSH synthesis-related genes, thereby preventing ferroptosis triggered by GPX4 inactivation (Zhu et al., 2021). Concurrently, overexpression of NRF2 may be a potential treatment for diseases that prevent ferroptosis through transcriptionally rescuing downstream related proteins, such as system Xc- and FPN. NRF2 transcriptionally regulates ATF3 through direct promoter binding. More critically, ATF3 promotes ferroptosis through mediating repression of SLC7A11 expression and suppression of system Xc- (Wang et al., 2020). Notably, SRXN1, as a key sulfiredoxin, is one of the targets of NRF2 (Vizioli et al., 2020), representing the oxidative stress response which is a major pathological process of ferroptosis (Wink, Hiemstra, Huppelschoten, Klip, & van de Water, 2018). Consistent data were obtained based on the ADR-induced mouse model. After HBF administration, lowered NRF2 and SRXN1 levels, and enhanced AFT3 levels in the cardiac tissues were reversed (Fig. 8). Unlike synthetic single-target drugs or isolated natural compounds, HBF's efficacy stems from the multi-target, synergistic pharmacology (Shen et al., 2024). These data show that HBF could simultaneously regulate the GPX4-centered antioxidant system via KEAP1/NRF2-ATF3-SRXN1 mediated SLC7A11-GSH signal and maintain intracellular iron homeostasis via upregulating FPN (Soriano-Castell, Currais, & Maher, 2021). Subsequently, the release of damage-associated molecular patterns triggered by ferroptosis is diminished, leading to a significant attenuation of the associated inflammatory response.   Given the complex constituents and polypharmacology of TCMs (Guo et al., 2022), herein three representative BACs (emodin, rhein, and lactiflorin) were preliminarily identified as follows: (1) Bioactive constituents were traceable to distinct herbal ingredients within the formula: emodin and rhein from DH, and lactiflorin from CS; (2) They demonstrated strong and stable binding affinities with multiple target proteins (NRF2/ATF3/SRXN3 and ferroptosis-related proteins). While ADME profiling in silico suggested drug-like potential for the three BCAs, their poor aqueous solubility poses a significant challenge to bioavailability. Nonetheless, future studies involving structural modifications and advanced drug delivery systems hold promise for overcoming this limitation, potentially paving the way for their development into novel therapeutics for cardiovascular events (China Association of Chinese Medicine, 2025, Li et al., 2025). Also, more in vivo experiments need to be conducted to further clarify and verify the anti-ferroptosis activities of the key BACs.


## Conclusion
  In summary, these data offer evidence that HBF attenuates ADR-induced myocardial injury via modulating the NRF2-ATF3-SRXN1 axis-mediated ferroptosis. Emodin, rhein, and lactiflorin are implicated as key BACs in HBF, potentially mediating its anti-fibrotic effects during myocardial injury. These results underscore the molecular basis of the TCM formula and support its modern development.
