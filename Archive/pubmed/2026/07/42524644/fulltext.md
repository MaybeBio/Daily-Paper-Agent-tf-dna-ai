# Machine learning identifies neutrophil-related signatures for diagnostic value in neonatal sepsis


## Abstract
  Neonatal sepsis (NS) is one of the leading causes of neonatal mortality. The nonspecific clinical manifestations and the limited timeliness of existing biomarkers (such as C-reactive protein) highlight the urgent need for highly accurate diagnostic tools. Neutrophils, as key effector cells of innate immunity, are closely involved in the progression of NS. This study integrated training (GSE69686) and validation (GSE25504) datasets from the GEO database. Neutrophil infiltration characteristics were analyzed utilizing CIBERSORT, and weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) was introduced to determine neutrophil-related co-expression modules. Three machine learning algorithms—LASSO, SVM-RFE, and RF—were implemented to cross-screen core diagnostic genes. A combined diagnostic model was distributed based on these genes. NetworkAnalyst was utilized to predict miRNA–TF regulatory networks, and GSVA was conducted to interpret biological functions. Three algorithms identified IL1R2 and METTL7B as core diagnostic genes; the model showed strong reliability. IL1R2 high expression correlated with reduced CD8+ T cells, regulatory T cells, and neutrophils (p<0.05). METTL7B high expression linked positively to B cells and negatively to NK cells/neutrophils. The two genes synergistically cause immune cell dysfunction. Six miRNAs and 15 transcription factors (e.g., NFKB1/RELA, STAT3) regulating these genes were found, involved in inflammation and metabolic reprogramming. Integrating neutrophil infiltration and triple-machine-learning, this study first proposed an IL1R2/METTL7B two-gene panel. The model had high accuracy and generalizability, potentially contributing to NS pathogenesis via immune dysfunction and metabolic reprogramming, supporting rapid diagnostics and targeted interventions.


## Introduction
  Neonatal sepsis (NS), a systemic inflammatory response syndrome triggered by pathogenic infections, is one of the leading causes of neonatal mortality worldwide. It is estimated that sepsis affects approximately 3 million newborns annually, with severe cases exhibiting a mortality rate of 9–20%.(1) Due to the immaturity of the neonatal immune system, clinical manifestations are often nonspecific, while conventional biomarkers such as C-reactive protein (CRP) and blood culture are limited by delayed responsiveness and other constraints.(2,3) Therefore, there is an urgent clinical need to identify rapid and reliable early diagnostic indicators.

  Neutrophils serve as essential cellular mediators of host defense against infection and constitute the first line of microbial control.(4) In the early stages of pathogen invasion, neutrophils play a central defensive role through mechanisms including chemotaxis, phagocytosis, reactive oxygen species (ROS) release, and neutrophil extracellular trap (NET) formation. During infection, immature neutrophils are rapidly released from the bone marrow into the bloodstream, leading to significant alterations in cell volume, density, and membrane protein expression.(5) Neutrophils exhibit dysregulated activity in sepsis and may contribute to its pathogenesis.(6) In this context, neutrophil parameters such as volume, conductivity, and scatter (VCS) have been proposed as potential screening tools for NS.(7) Moreover, innovative diagnostic parameters related to neutrophils (Mon_XW and Neu_Y) demonstrate superior diagnostic performance relative to conventional markers like CRP and procalcitonin (PCT).(8) These findings collectively underscore the promising potential of neutrophil-related parameters as rapid diagnostic biomarkers. However, current research still faces significant challenges: the mechanisms linking neutrophil characteristics to pathogen types remain unclear, and more importantly, single biochemical markers are inadequate for comprehensively capturing the complex immunopathological network of sepsis.

  With the deepening application of artificial intelligence technology in the medical field, machine learning algorithms have demonstrated pronounced potential in improving the accuracy of disease diagnosis. In research on NS, integrated multi-algorithm strategies are becoming a key pathway to overcome the limitations of traditional diagnostic methods. LASSO regression addresses overfitting in high-dimensional data by constructing a penalty function that compresses the variable space, thereby effectively identifying key features from large-scale datasets.(9) SVM-RFE algorithm leverages the principle of maximum margin hyperplanes and iteratively removes features with the smallest weights to optimize the feature subset.(10) Meanwhile, RF, based on ensemble learning, constructs multiple decision trees and aggregates feature importance scores to evaluate the contribution of variables to classification.(11) Each of these three algorithms has distinct advantages: LASSO excels in feature compression and regularization,(12) SVM-RFE offers greater robustness against overfitting,(13) and RF exhibits high tolerance for high-dimensional nonlinear data.(14) When a single model faces performance limitations, a cross-algorithm validation strategy can effectively enhance biological plausibility and model robustness. For instance, in a related study, the combined use of LASSO, RF, and SVM-RFE identified 14 senescence-related signature genes linked to heart failure and suggested rimonabant and lovastatin as potential therapeutic agents.(15) In this context, algorithm integration strategies hold irreplaceable value in addressing the complex diagnostic challenges of NS. Therefore, there is an urgent need to systematically decipher the dynamic changes of neutrophils in sepsis through multi-omics integration and machine learning modeling, ultimately enabling the construction of a multidimensional combined diagnostic model.

  To overcome the limitations of single-method approaches in identifying diagnostic biomarkers, this study innovatively integrated multi-omics data with a machine learning-based analytical pipeline. First, the CIBERSORT algorithm was introduced to characterize neutrophil-specific immune infiltration patterns. Subsequently, weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) was applied to construct co-expression modules and identify those most closely linked to disease severity. For biomarker selection, a triple-machine-learning algorithm consensus strategy was adopted, retaining only genes identified by all three algorithms as core diagnostic markers. To ensure clinical applicability, the model parameters were optimized through cross-validation on the training set, and generalizability of the model was assessed in an independent validation dataset. In summary, the multi-modal research framework—spanning from “immune infiltration profiling” to “network module analysis” and finally “machine learning-based screening”—not only offered a novel strategy for discovering neutrophil-related biomarkers but also established a technical foundation for early and precise diagnosis of NS. This research aims to develop a diagnostic model that combines high timeliness and reliability, ultimately enabling accurate identification of high-risk septic neonates in early infection stages through minimal peripheral blood testing, thereby providing technological support for reducing neonatal mortality.


## Materials and Methods

### Data collection and processing
  Training dataset “GSE69686” (Platform: GPL20292) and validation dataset “GSE25504” (Platform: GPL6947) of NS were retrieved and downloaded from the GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/). The training set consisted of 64 NS samples and 85 healthy controls. The validation set included 26 NS samples and 37 healthy controls.


### Identification of differentially expressed genes (DEGs)
  DEGs between the NS group and the control group in the training set GSE69686 were identified using the “limma” package.(16) We employed the commonly used efficient screening criteria in transcriptome studies: |logFC| >1 and adj.P.Val <0.05.


### Immune cell infiltration analysis
  The CIBERSORT algorithm was applied to estimate the immune cell subset infiltration levels in the training set samples. Based on the infiltration scores, differences in neutrophil infiltration between healthy controls and NS patients were compared (p<0.05).


### WGCNA
  The “WGCNA” package(17) was introduced to construct a co-expression network from the training set data, aiming to determine gene modules significantly linked to immune infiltration. First, sample and gene quality were assessed implementing the goodSamplesGenes function in the “WGCNA” R package. Next, an appropriate soft threshold was selected via pickSoftThreshold. Subsequently, modules were merged with the minimum module gene number set to 50 and a module similarity cut height threshold of 0.2. Finally, the module most strongly linked to neutrophils and with a p value less than 0.05 was selected for further analysis.


### Acquisition of the initial diagnostic gene set
  The DEGs linked to NS obtained from differential expression analysis were intersected with key neutrophil-related module genes identified through WGCNA, resulting in an initial diagnostic gene set relative to neutrophil infiltration in NS.


### Screening of diagnostic genes
  Diagnostic genes were screened by three machine learning algorithms—LASSO, SVM-RFE, and RF—applied to the training set. LASSO regression was implemented through the “glmnet” package(18) to compress model complexity and address multicollinearity, thereby selecting key genes. The SVM-RFE algorithm was performed with the “e1071” package(19) combined with 10-fold cross-validation; the gene subset corresponding to the minimum cross-validation error was selected. A RF model was constructed utilizing the “randomForest” package,(20) and genes ranked in the top 33% by importance were retained. The intersection of genes identified by these three methods was defined as the final diagnostic gene set for NS.


### Testing and validation of diagnostic genes
  The diagnostic performance of the candidate genes was evaluated in the training set by the “pROC” package. The reliability of these genes was further assessed in the validation set. ### Immune microenvironment analysis
  Based on the immune infiltration results generated by CIBERSORT, correlation analysis between the diagnostic genes and immune infiltration scores was performed through the “stats” and “corrplot”(22) packages. Heatmaps and lollipop plots were generated to visualize the associations.


### Regulatory network prediction for diagnostic genes
  The NetworkAnalyst web platform (https://www.networkanalyst.ca/) was introduced to predict miRNAs targeting the diagnostic genes (based on the TarBase ver. 9.0 database) and transcription factors (TFs) (from the JASPAR database). Interaction networks between diagnostic genes and miRNAs, as well as between diagnostic genes and TFs, were constructed.


### GSVA enrichment analysis of diagnostic genes
  To investigate biological function differences between groups with high and low expression of the diagnostic genes, GSVA was applied to quantify enrichment scores for KEGG pathways and GOBP terms.


## Results

### Acquisition of the initial diagnostic gene set
  This investigation employed a systematic analytical approach to identify core diagnostic genes for NS. First, differential expression analysis of the training set identified 45 significantly DEGs linked to sepsis (Fig. 1A). Subsequently, CIBERSORT-based immune infiltration analysis determined neutrophils as the dominant immune cell population in the NS cohort (Fig. 1B), suggesting that alterations in neutrophils may play a critical role in sepsis pathogenesis. Statistical analysis further confirmed that neutrophil levels were remarkably higher in the NS group relative to the healthy control group (Fig. 1C), supporting above hypothesis. To explore co-expression modules associated with neutrophils in sepsis, WGCNA was performed using a soft threshold of 19, a minimum module size of 50 genes, and a merge cut height of 0.2. The Cyan module showed the strongest positive correlation with neutrophil infiltration scores (cor = 0.66, p<0.05) (Fig. 1D). Genes within this module were significantly co-expressed with neutrophil signatures (cor = 0.29, p<0.05) (Fig. 1E). Finally, the intersection of genes from the Cyan module and the previously identified DEGs yielded 43 core candidate diagnostic genes (Fig. 1F).


### Screening of diagnostic genes using LASSO regression, SVM-RFE, and RF
  To identify the most predictive feature genes, this investigation employed a combination of three machine learning algorithms for feature selection. First, LASSO regression applied L1 regularization to automatically shrink coefficients of irrelevant variables. Through 10-fold cross-validation, the optimal lambda (λ) value was determined to be 0.00053, resulting in the selection of 24 key genes (Fig. 2A and Supplemental Table 1*). Second, RF identified the top 14 genes based on importance ranking (Fig. 2B and Supplemental Table 2*). Subsequently, SVM-RFE with 10-fold cross-validation pinpointed 4 genes with the minimum error (Fig. 2C). Finally, the intersection of genes selected by all three methods yielded two core diagnostic genes: IL1R2 and METTL7B (Fig. 2D). These two genes demonstrated extremely high statistical significance in the initial differential expression analysis. The adjusted p values (adj.P.Val) after correction have reached 5.17 × 10−14 and 7.31 × 10−14, respectively.


### Validation and evaluation of diagnostic genes
  To assess the reliability of diagnostic genes, a logistic regression model was constructed and evaluated by an external cohort. ROC analysis confirmed strong diagnostic performance in the training set, with IL1R2 (AUC = 0.863), METTL7B (AUC = 0.850), and the combined model (AUC = 0.892) all exhibiting high discriminative power (Fig. 3A). Consistent results were observed in the validation set, where the combined model achieved an AUC of 0.913 (IL1R2 AUC = 0.909, METTL7B AUC = 0.801) (Fig. 3B). These findings indicated that the model based on IL1R2 and METTL7B possessed strong generalizability. Furthermore, expression levels of both genes were notably higher in the NS group relative to healthy controls in both the training and validation sets (p<0.0001) (Fig. 3C and D).


### Immune microenvironment analysis of diagnostic genes in the training dataset
  A pronouncedly positive correlation was observed between the expression levels of IL1R2 and METTL7B, supported by high statistical significance and reliable linear fitting (r = 0.67, p<0.001) (Fig. 4A). Subsequently, the correlations between IL1R2/METTL7B expression and immune cell infiltration were investigated. IL1R2 expression was negatively correlated with infiltration of T cells CD8, T cells CD4 naive, T cells regulatory (Tregs), and neutrophils, while positively correlated with infiltration of Mast cells activated (Fig. 4B). METTL7B expression was negatively correlated with infiltration of NK cells activated, T cells regulatory (Tregs), T cells CD8, and neutrophils, but positively correlated with infiltration of B cells naive and B cells memory (Fig. 4C). To further explore the relationship between IL1R2, METTL7B, and neutrophils, we conducted a correlation analysis of the diagnostic genes and neutrophil marker genes (S100A8, S100A9, and S100A12) as well as a functional regulatory gene (CSF3R). IL1R2/METTL7B was significantly positively correlated with these neutrophil markers (Fig. 4D). This finding strongly suggests that the high expression of these two genes signifies an increase in neutrophil numbers. Moreover, it appears to be closely related to the activation or dysfunction of neutrophils in the sepsis state.


### Construction of the miRNA–TF–Hub gene regulatory network
  To further elucidate the potential mechanisms linking IL1R2 and METTL7B to sepsis, we investigated miRNAs and TFs correlated to these diagnostic genes. Through the NetworkAnalyst platform and the JASPAR database, a regulatory network connecting the diagnostic genes with miRNAs and TFs was constructed (Fig. 5). A total of 61 miRNAs with potential targeting capabilities were identified, among which six miRNAs—hsa-miR-107, hsa-miR-103a-3p, hsa-miR-34a-5p, hsa-miR-210-3p, hsa-miR-29c-3p, and hsa-miR-26a-5p—were found to simultaneously regulate both IL1R2 and METTL7B. In addition, eight TFs—USF1, NFKB1, RELA, ELK1, NFYA, GATA2, TEAD1, and USF2—were associated with IL1R2, while seven TFs—ZNF354C, FOS, HNF1B, JUN, IRF2, STAT3, and NR0C1—showed strong regulatory relevance to METTL7B.


### GSVA enrichment analysis of diagnostic genes
  Through GSVA, we investigated differences in biological functions between groups with high and low expression of the diagnostic genes. The screening threshold was set at a pathway enrichment significance level (p value) <0.05. The top 10 most remarkably upregulated and downregulated pathways (based on the smallest p values) were selected for further analysis. GO enrichment analysis for IL1R2 pointed out that significantly upregulated terms were primarily related to metabolic regulation and inflammatory processes, such as “Negative regulation of interferon 1 alpha production” and “Ump catabolic process”. Remarkably downregulated processes involved immune cell signaling regulation, including “Negative regulation of B cell receptor signaling pathway” and “Negative regulation of T cell receptor signaling pathway” ​(Fig. 6A). KEGG enrichment results for IL1R2 indicated heightened activity in pathways such as “Galactose metabolism” and “Ppar signaling pathway” (Fig. 6B). In the case of METTL7B, GO analysis showed notably enriched upregulated terms in inflammatory control and metabolic processes, such as “Negative regulation of interleukin 1 alpha production” and “Urea catabolic process”. Downregulated terms were mainly involved in cellular development and activity regulation, including “Negative regulation of dendrite development” and “Regulation of growth rate” ​(Fig. 6C). KEGG enrichment for METTL7B highlighted metabolic pathways, with significantly upregulated pathways involving metabolic processes and signal transduction, such as the “Pentose phosphate pathway” and “Ppar signaling pathway”. Significantly downregulated pathways were primarily linked to immune function, metabolic regulation, and cell adhesion, including “Antigen processing and presentation” and “Autoimmune thyroid disease” ​(Fig. 6D). In summary, the expression levels of IL1R2 and METTL7B primarily influence intracellular metabolic regulation, inflammatory processes, and signal transduction.


## Discussion
  This investigation confirmed IL1R2 and METTL7B as highly efficient diagnostic biomarkers for NS by integrating multi-omics features of neutrophils with a triple machine learning algorithm, and further established a combined diagnostic model. This discovery not only provided an innovative tool for early and precise detection of NS, but also offered deeper insights into the central role of neutrophil-mediated immune metabolic imbalance in sepsis.

  During sepsis, neutrophils are closely involved in the host inflammatory response against invading pathogens, primarily through phagocytosis, degranulation, and the release of NETs.(23,24) By analyzing the intersection of neutrophils and sepsis, two diagnostic genes, IL1R2 and METTL7B, were determined. IL1R2 is a key regulator of innate immune responses. Its high expression may inhibit IL-1β signaling,(25,26) thereby suppressing inflammatory responses in immune cells,(27) which is consistent with our observation of its notable inhibitory effect on T-cell subsets. IL1R2 gene is positively correlated with neutrophil biomarkers.(28) Moreover, in the peripheral blood of septic patients, expanded immature neutrophils highly express IL1R2, and their proportion is positively linked to early mortality.(29) METTL7B, a member of the mammalian METTL family located on chromosome 12, exerted alkylthiol methyltransferase activity.(30) METTL7B can respond to inflammatory signals via Janus Kinase 1,(31) and is involved in drug resistance mechanisms in lung adenocarcinoma(32) and malignant progression in glioblastoma.(33) Additionally, research in macrophages suggests that METTL7B contributes to the pathogenesis of sepsis by modulating inflammatory responses.(34) However, a direct link between neutrophils and METTL7B has not been reported. Consistently, we found that IL1R2/METTL7B was significantly positively correlated with neutrophil markers. This finding strongly suggests that the high expression of these two genes reflects an increase in the number of neutrophils coupled with potential activation or dysfunction of neutrophils in the sepsis state. In conclusion, this investigation is the first to reveal that METTL7B may remodel the NS immune microenvironment by suppressing NK cell activity and promoting B cell differentiation. Notably, the synergistic high expression of both genes suggests a novel pattern of immunometabolic dysregulation in sepsis: combined dysfunction of T cells, NK cells, and B cells mediated through IL1R2/METTL7B.

  The diagnostic performance of IL1R2/METTL7B was exceptionally high (AUC >0.85). Through miRNA-TF network, key miRNAs and TFs closely linked to these diagnostic genes were screened, leading to the establishment of a comprehensive regulatory network. More importantly, both genes were found to be co-regulated by six specific miRNAs, which have all been previously reported in sepsis research. For instance, miR-107-3p is upregulated in septic patients, and its knockdown may protect against sepsis-induced endothelial injury by targeting KLK5.(35) miR-103a-3p alleviates sepsis-related liver injury by targeting HMGB1.(36) Suppression of miR-29c-3p in animal models reduces sepsis-induced cardiac dysfunction and inflammatory responses.(37) miR-26a-5p attenuates septic acute kidney injury by targeting IL-6 to inhibit renal inflammation.(38) Serum levels of miR-34a-5p and miR-210-3p exert pronounced potential as biomarkers for sepsis.(39,40) However, no existing literature has reported a direct regulatory relationship between these miRNAs and IL1R2/METTL7B.

  Further investigation pointed out that relevant TFs also play non-negligible roles. NFKB1 and RELA are core components of the canonical NF-κB pathway; their activation drives the expression of proinflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β, amplifying inflammatory responses in sepsis.(41,42) RelB may confer protection against Escherichia coli-induced sepsis via the non-canonical NF-κB pathway.(43) ELK1 acts as a downstream effector of the MAPK pathway, activating inflammatory genes involved in acute lung injury inflammatory response(44) and accelerating IL-8 secretion.(45) GATA2 has been bioinformatically correlated to sepsis, though functional validation is lacking.(46) TEAD1 is a key effector of the Hippo signaling pathway, critically involved in organ development, epithelial homeostasis, tissue regeneration, wound healing, and immune regulation.(47) USF1 and USF2 participate in metabolic and stress responses and modulate T cell activation.(48) STAT3 is a central inflammatory regulator; it mediates ferroptosis in sepsis-associated acute respiratory distress syndrome(49) and facilitates METTL7B-dependent negative regulation of PD-L1 expression via the JAK/STAT3 pathway.(50) AP-1 family members (FOS and JUN) form heterodimers that amplify inflammatory signaling.(51) Downregulation of IRF2 reduces levels of IL-1β, IL-18, IL-6, and TNF-α in vitro and in vivo, alleviating sepsis-induced acute kidney injury.(52) The functions of NR0C1, ZNF354C, and HNF1B in sepsis remain poorly understood, with limited direct evidence supporting their roles in immunity, warranting further investigation. These findings not only reinforce the reliability of the diagnostic model based on IL1R2 and METTL7B, but also highlight their hub roles within the miRNA-TF regulatory network. This work lays a foundation for developing integrated “genetic testing + targeted intervention” strategies: for instance, a dual-gene qPCR-based detection kit for rapid screening, while targeting shared miRNAs may simultaneously suppress both genes and reverse immune dysregulation.

  Through systematic investigation of IL1R2 and METTL7B, our work established a highly accurate predictive model for NS. By constructing a miRNA-TF regulatory network, we further elucidated the central contribution of neutrophil-related molecular features to NS: aberrant overexpression of IL1R2 and METTL7B disrupted the transcriptional equilibrium within neutrophils, thereby driving inflammatory cascades and metabolic dysregulation. However, this investigation is subject to several limitations. The data were sourced exclusively from the GEO database, and prospective cohort validation is lacking. Although bacterial infection is a major cause of sepsis, the influence of different pathogens on marker expression was not analyzed. Moreover, the proposed mechanisms of IL1R2 and METTL7B are based primarily on bioinformatics predictions and literature mining and require experimental validation. Future work will include validation in multiple independent cohorts. The role of IL1R2/METTL7B in sepsis-induced organ damage could be further explored through organoid models. Additionally, the findings support the development of serum miRNA-based liquid biopsies for diagnostic purposes, as well as miRNA-targeted therapeutics aimed at suppressing IL1R2 and METTL7B expression. Secondly, given that the public data set used did not provide unified CRP or PCT test data, we were unable to directly compare the diagnostic efficacy of IL1R2/METTL7B with these traditional biomarkers. Future research designs should include these conventional indicators and build a multimodal joint diagnostic model to clarify the incremental diagnostic value of the new markers. Finally, although the model performed well in differentiating sepsis from healthy status, its specificity in differentiating sepsis from other non-sepsis neonatal inflammatory diseases, such as severe pneumonia, early-stage necrotizing enterocolitis, has not been verified. Moreover, NS may exhibit heterogeneity in different onset times (early-onset sepsis and late-onset sepsis), different pathogenic organisms (Gram-positive bacteria, Gram-negative bacteria, and fungi), different blood sampling times, and host immune responses. Future research needs to further validate the clinical specificity of IL1R2 and METTL7B in larger prospective cohorts that include different clinical subtypes or specific pathogen infections.

  Employing a machine learning-driven multi-omics integration strategy, this work is the first to propose IL1R2/METTL7B as a dual-gene biomarker signature. This signature not only provides a clinically translatable, high-precision diagnostic tool for NS, but its regulatory network also deeply implicates neutrophil-mediated immunometabolic reprogramming, opening new avenues for understanding the pathogenesis of NS and developing targeted therapeutic strategies.
