## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence as presented in the abstract; no access to full text, figures, tables, or supplementary materials
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report that periplocin, a compound from Periplocae Cortex, inhibits multiple myeloma (MM) cell proliferation in vitro and in a murine xenograft model. They propose a mechanism whereby periplocin binds STAT3, prevents JAK2/STAT3/AKT/mTOR pathway activation, reduces STAT3 transcriptional activity, and decreases STAT3 binding to the MYC promoter, thereby downregulating MYC expression. They further link high MYC expression to poor prognosis in MM patients using a public database.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; methods and results are summarized without numerical data, statistical values, or experimental details
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, all figures and tables, RNA-seq data, docking scores, CETSA data, flow cytometry histograms, Western blot images, xenograft tumor growth curves, survival analysis plots, and statistical reporting

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a coherent and potentially interesting mechanistic hypothesis for periplocin in MM. However, the evidence as summarized is insufficient to establish the central claims. Key quantitative data, statistical analyses, and experimental controls are absent. The link between STAT3 binding, MYC regulation, and functional outcomes is plausible but not rigorously demonstrated from the provided material. The study may be of interest to researchers in cancer pharmacology and MM biology, but the current abstract does not provide enough detail to assess technical soundness or reproducibility.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in multiple myeloma biology, natural product pharmacology, and STAT3/MYC signaling pathways would be interested. The potential repurposing of a traditional medicine-derived compound for MM treatment could attract translational oncology investigators. Those studying transcription factor targeting as a therapeutic strategy may also find the proposed mechanism relevant.
- **Major strengths** The study combines multiple orthogonal approaches, including in vitro assays, an in vivo model, transcriptomics, molecular docking, CETSA, reporter assays, and CUT&Tag PCR. This multi-method design is commendable. The focus on a specific molecular axis, STAT3 to MYC, provides a clear mechanistic narrative. The use of a patient survival correlation adds translational relevance.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** "Periplocin exhibited encouraging anti-MM activity in both cellular and animal models."  
- **Evidence pointer** Methods and Results sections, abstract; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract reports anti-MM activity but provides no quantitative data, such as IC50 values, percent inhibition, tumor volume changes, or statistical significance. No effect sizes or confidence intervals are given.  
- **Why it matters** Without quantitative results, the magnitude and reliability of the anti-tumor effect cannot be evaluated. This is a foundational claim upon which the entire mechanistic narrative depends.  
- **Resolution test** Provide dose-response curves, viability percentages, tumor growth curves, and statistical comparisons for both in vitro and in vivo experiments.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Mechanistic evidence  
- **Claim pointer** "Periplocin prevented the JAK2/STAT3/AKT/mTOR signaling cascade from being activated."  
- **Evidence pointer** Western blotting, Results section; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract states that periplocin prevents activation of this pathway but does not specify which phosphorylation sites were measured, at what time points, or in which cell lines. No data on phosphorylation levels are provided.  
- **Why it matters** The claim of pathway inhibition is central to the proposed mechanism. Without specific phospho-protein data and appropriate controls, the conclusion is unsupported.  
- **Resolution test** Show Western blot images with quantified phospho-STAT3, phospho-JAK2, phospho-AKT, and phospho-mTOR levels, including loading controls and statistical analysis.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Target validation  
- **Claim pointer** "Molecular docking and CETSA showed the interaction between periplocin and STAT3."  
- **Evidence pointer** Molecular docking, CETSA, Results section; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract claims a direct interaction between periplocin and STAT3 based on docking and CETSA. However, docking results are computational predictions and CETSA alone does not prove specific binding. No binding affinity values, CETSA melting curves, or negative controls are provided.  
- **Why it matters** Direct target engagement is a critical claim. If periplocin does not directly bind STAT3, the proposed mechanism is invalid. The current evidence is insufficient to establish this interaction.  
- **Resolution test** Provide docking scores and binding poses, CETSA melting curves with a known binder as positive control and a non-binder as negative control, and ideally a biophysical assay such as surface plasmon resonance or isothermal titration calorimetry.

- **Concern ID** R1-M4  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Transcriptional regulation  
- **Claim pointer** "Periplocin significantly suppressed STAT3 transcriptional activity" and "effectively inhibited the binding of STAT3 to the MYC promoter DNA region."  
- **Evidence pointer** Dual-luciferase reporter assay, CUT&Tag PCR, Results section; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract reports suppression of STAT3 transcriptional activity and reduced STAT3 binding to the MYC promoter, but no quantitative data are given. The reporter assay details, such as the reporter construct and normalization method, are absent. CUT&Tag PCR results are described without enrichment values or statistical significance.  
- **Why it matters** These experiments are intended to link STAT3 inhibition to MYC downregulation. Without quantitative results and appropriate controls, the causal chain is not established.  
- **Resolution test** Provide luciferase activity values with normalization, CUT&Tag enrichment scores with input controls, and statistical comparisons for both assays.

- **Concern ID** R1-M5  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Clinical relevance  
- **Claim pointer** "High expression of MYC was closely related to poor prognosis in MM."  
- **Evidence pointer** Gene Expression Profiling database, Results section; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract states a correlation between MYC expression and poor prognosis but does not specify the database, cohort size, statistical method, hazard ratio, or p-value.  
- **Why it matters** This correlation is used to support the clinical relevance of the proposed mechanism. Without proper survival analysis details, the claim is not verifiable.  
- **Resolution test** Provide the database name, cohort characteristics, survival curves, hazard ratio, confidence interval, and p-value.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim of direct physical interaction between periplocin and STAT3 is unsupported without quantitative binding data and appropriate controls.
- The claim that periplocin inhibits the JAK2/STAT3/AKT/mTOR pathway is unsupported without phospho-protein data.
- The claim that periplocin suppresses STAT3 transcriptional activity and MYC promoter binding is unsupported without quantitative reporter and CUT&Tag data.
- The claim that MYC expression correlates with poor prognosis in MM is unverifiable without database and statistical details.
- The overall conclusion that periplocin targets STAT3 to suppress MYC and inhibit MM proliferation is not established from the abstract alone.
