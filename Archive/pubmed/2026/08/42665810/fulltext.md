# Identification and preliminary functional characterization of MsPAL2, a key salt-tolerance gene during seed germination in Medicago sativa


## Background
  Alfalfa (Medicago sativa) is an important legume forage crop. Salt stress severely impairs seed germination, compromising field establishment and subsequent seedling growth. However, the key genes underlying alfalfa seed responses to salt stress during germination remain largely unidentified, hindering targeted genetic improvement of salt tolerance within the genus Medicago.


## Results
  In this study, transcriptomic sequencing was performed on germinated and ungerminated seeds of alfalfa treated with 150 mmol/L NaCl, using the ‘Zhongmu No. 4’ reference genome. Through integrated KEGG pathway enrichment, gene expression fold-change analysis, and Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA), we identified the gene MsPAL2 (Msa1079410), which encodes the key rate-limiting enzyme in phenylpropanoid metabolism, as a critical salt-tolerance gene. Functional characterization via virus-induced gene silencing (VIGS) revealed that silencing MsPAL2 did not affect seed germination under normal conditions but significantly reduced the germination rate under salt stress. These findings suggest that MsPAL2 acts as a positive regulator of alfalfa seed germination under salt stress. Promoter analysis and AlphaFold3 prediction collectively revealed MYB-binding cis-elements and identified MYB transcription factors as candidate upstream regulators of MsPAL2, laying the groundwork for elucidating its regulatory network.


## Conclusions
  Phenylpropanoid metabolism constitutes a key mechanism underlying salt tolerance in alfalfa. Through transcriptome analysis, we identified MsPAL2, a phenylpropanoid pathway gene, as a critical regulator of alfalfa seed germination under salt stress, providing valuable genetic resources for molecular breeding of salt-tolerant alfalfa.

## Background
  Alfalfa (Medicago sativa), known as the “king of forage”, is the most widely cultivated perennial legume forage crop worldwide, with a cultivation area exceeding 30 million hectares [1]. It plays a vital role in advancing the livestock industry, reclaiming saline-alkali soils, and restoring degraded ecosystems [2]. However, the exacerbation of soil salinization has become one of the most serious challenges confronting global agricultural production [3]. Salt stress has emerged as a primary abiotic stress factor limiting yield increases in alfalfa. Across the plant’s life cycle, seed germination is the developmental stage at which alfalfa exhibits the greatest sensitivity to salt stress [4]. Salt stress during this critical phase directly leads to decreased seed germination rates, reduced seedling vigor, and uneven emergence in the field, ultimately resulting in substantial yield losses [5].

  Salt stress delays germination initiation by inhibiting seed water uptake [5, 6]; it also induces excessive accumulation of Na+ and Cl−, disrupting intracellular ion homeostasis [3, 7]. Additionally, it promotes reactive oxygen species (ROS) accumulation, leading to oxidative damage that directly impairs cell membrane integrity and damages biomacromolecules [8]. To cope with salt stress-induced damage, plants have evolved multiple tolerance strategies, including the activation of antioxidant defense systems, the accumulation of osmotic regulators, and the reprogramming of secondary metabolic pathways [9]. Among these, the phenylpropanoid metabolic pathway represents one of the most critical secondary metabolic pathways in plant responses to salt stress, producing a diverse array of metabolites, including flavonoids, lignin, and tannins [10, 11]. These phenylpropanoid metabolites enhance salt tolerance through mechanisms such as flavonoid-mediated ROS scavenging [10, 12]. Phenylalanine ammonia-lyase (PAL, EC 4.3.1.24) is the first rate-limiting enzyme in the phenylpropanoid metabolism pathway; it catalyzes the non-oxidative deamination of L-phenylalanine to form trans-cinnamic acid, thereby determining the allocation of carbon flux from primary to secondary metabolism [13, 14]. Numerous studies have demonstrated that salt stress rapidly induces PAL gene expression and enzymatic activity in various plant species, thereby enhancing plant salt tolerance through multiple mechanisms, including ROS scavenging, cell wall strengthening, and osmotic balance regulation [15]. Recent studies have confirmed that exogenous melatonin treatment can enhance the salt tolerance of jujube leaves by activating PAL activity and promoting flavonoid accumulation [16], indicating that PAL-mediated phenylpropanoid metabolism is one of the key mechanisms underlying plant salt tolerance.

  In alfalfa, seven MsPAL genes (MsPAL1–MsPAL7) have been identified through genome-wide screening, and these genes exhibit distinct expression patterns and respond specifically to various abiotic stresses. Notably, MsPAL4 and MsPAL6 showed the most significant upregulation under salt stress, suggesting their important roles in alfalfa salt tolerance [17]. Elevated expression of MsPAL1 has been further confirmed to significantly enhance salt tolerance by increasing flavonoid accumulation and boosting antioxidant enzyme activity, supporting the critical role of PAL family members in alfalfa salt adaptation [18]. However, current understanding of PAL family gene functions in alfalfa remains largely confined to the seedling and mature plant stages [17, 18]. The specific roles of these genes during seed germination under salt stress—the developmental stage most vulnerable to salinity—remain uncharacterized, representing a critical gap in our understanding of alfalfa salt tolerance. Furthermore, the PAL family exhibits considerable functional diversification across different species [19]. Therefore, elucidating the core functions of specific alfalfa PAL family members in salt tolerance during the germination stage represents a critical and pressing scientific question.

  Transcriptome sequencing (RNA-seq) is a core technology for characterizing genome-wide transcriptional reprogramming in plants under abiotic stress [20, 21]. Virus-induced gene silencing (VIGS), a rapid and high-throughput reverse genetics tool, effectively overcomes the bottlenecks of stable genetic transformation in alfalfa, which stem from its autotetraploid genome and recalcitrant regeneration [22]. A Tobacco rattle virus (TRV)-based VIGS system has been successfully established and optimized in alfalfa, enabling rapid transient silencing and functional characterization of stress-responsive genes [23]. Based on the theoretical foundation and methodologies, this study conducted the following: comparative transcriptomic analysis of germinated and ungerminated alfalfa seeds under salt stress conditions; identification of modules and hub genes significantly associated with germination-stage salt tolerance using GSEA, with particular focus on PAL gene family members; and silencing of candidate PAL gene using a TRV-mediated VIGS system, followed by systematic evaluation of seed germination rates under salt stress. The results indicate that silencing MsPAL2 (Msa1079410) significantly reduced alfalfa seed germination rates under salt stress. This represents the first study to validate the role of a PAL family member specifically during the germination stage, thereby providing direct genetic evidence for the role of a PAL family member in salt tolerance during this critical developmental window.


## Materials and methods

### Test materials
  The alfalfa (Medicago sativa) variety used was ‘Zhongmu No. 1’. The seeds used in the experiment were harvested in 2020 from the Shangba Base of Daye Seed Industry Co., Ltd. in Jiuquan City, Gansu Province (98°46′E, 39°37′N, elevation 1,414 m), and had an initial moisture content of approximately 10%. Healthy seeds—those that are plump, uniform in size, and free of pests and diseases—were selected in advance and set aside. The Agrobacterium tumefaciens strain GV3101, used for genetic transformation, and the virus-induced gene silencing (VIGS) vectors pTRV1 and pTRV2 are all maintained in our laboratory.


### Sample collection and transcriptome sequencing
  Alfalfa seeds were treated with 150 mmol·L⁻¹ NaCl solution for 48 h, after which seeds with visible radicle protrusion (≥ 1 mm) were collected as the germinated group (G), while seeds showing no radicle emergence were collected as the non-germinated group (NG). Both groups included three biological replicates, each consisting of approximately 10 seeds. The collected samples were rapidly frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at − 80 °C for future use. Beijing Biomarker Technologies Co., Ltd. (Biomarker Technologies, Beijing, China) performed next-generation reference-based transcriptome sequencing using the Illumina sequencing platform, constructed cDNA libraries, and generated raw sequencing data.


### Bioinformatics and differential gene analysis
  Fastp software was used to filter raw sequencing data, removing adapter sequences, reads with high N content, and low-quality reads, to obtain high-quality clean data (clean reads), and calculate the Q30 score and GC content. Clean reads were aligned to the alfalfa reference genome using HISAT2. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using DESeq2 with the following screening thresholds: a fold change (FC) of ≥ 1.5 and a false discovery rate (FDR/P-value) of < 0.01. Next, Baimake Cloud Platform (Biomarker Technologies, Beijing, China) was used to perform KEGG (Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes) pathway enrichment analysis on the DEGs.

  To further identify gene sets that undergo coordinated changes in response to salt stress, gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was employed [24]. GSEA was performed in R (version 4.6.0) within RStudio. All expressed genes were pre-ranked by log₂ fold change derived from the DESeq2 differential expression analysis comparing germinated (G) versus non-germinated (NG) groups. The KEGG pathway gene sets (Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes, https://www.genome.jp/kegg/) were used as the reference gene sets. GSEA was implemented via the fgsea algorithm with gene-set permutation, and the normalized enrichment score (NES) was calculated for each gene set. P-values were adjusted using the false discovery rate (FDR) method, with minimum and maximum gene-set sizes set to 3 and 2,000, respectively. Gene sets with |NES| > 1 and adjusted P-value (FDR) < 0.05 were considered significantly enriched.

  MYB family transcription factors among the upregulated DEGs (log₂FC > 0, P < 0.01) were annotated using PlantTFDB v5.0 (http://planttfdb.gao-lab.org/). Cis-acting regulatory elements in the 2000-bp upstream promoter region of MsPAL2 were predicted using PlantCARE (http://bioinformatics.psb.ugent.be/webtools/plantcare/html/). The binding potential between each MYB-annotated transcription factor protein and the MsPAL2 promoter DNA sequence was predicted de novo using AlphaFold3 [25], and the protein-DNA interaction confidence score (ipTM) was extracted for each MYB candidate. The top three MYB candidates ranked by ipTM were selected and visualized in a Cytoscape (v3.9.1) interaction network, with edge thickness scaled to the AlphaFold3 ipTM score.


### Construction and genetic transformation of VIGS vectors
  To verify the function of the candidate gene MsPAL2 in the response to salt stress, gene silencing was performed using the TRV-mediated VIGS system. Based on the CDS sequence of MsPAL2, a specific silencing fragment approximately 300 bp in length was designed. The fragment was cloned into the pTRV2 vector via homologous recombination to construct the pTRV2-MsPAL2 silencing vector. The pTRV1 and pTRV2 empty vectors, as well as the pTRV2-MsPAL2 plasmid, were transformed into GV3101, respectively. Agrobacterium in the logarithmic growth phase was collected, resuspended in the infection solution (10 mmol·L⁻¹ MES, 10 mmol·L⁻¹ MgCl₂, 200 µmol·L⁻¹ acetyl eugenol, pH 5.6), and adjusted to an OD₆₀₀ to 1.0. Equal volumes of the bacterial culture containing pTRV1 and the bacterial culture containing pTRV2 or pTRV2-MsPAL2 were mixed and allowed to stand at room temperature for 2 h. Alfalfa seeds were soaked in the aforementioned mixed bacterial solution, after assisting infection through vacuum treatment, the seeds were cultivated using the paper germination method until germination. The experiment included six treatment groups: distilled water control (H₂O-CK), distilled water empty vector control (H₂O-TRV2), distilled water MsPAL2 silencing group (H₂O-TRV2-MsPAL2), 150 mmol·L⁻¹ NaCl stress control (NaCl-CK), empty vector control group under NaCl stress (NaCl-TRV2), and MsPAL2 silencing group under NaCl stress (NaCl-TRV2-MsPAL2). Each treatment group included three biological replicates, with approximately 50 seeds per replicate. Seed germination rate and phenotype were assessed 24 h after imbibition. At the same time, RNA was extracted and the relative expression levels of MsPAL2 were detected by real-time quantitative PCR (qRT-PCR) using the alfalfa Actin gene as an internal control, with the H2O-CK group normalized to 1, to verify the silencing efficiency.


## Results

### Phenotypic responses of alfalfa seed germination under salt stress
  To investigate the effects of salt stress on alfalfa seed germination, ‘Zhongmu No. 1’ seeds were treated with either distilled water (CK) or 150 mmol·L⁻¹ NaCl. Under control conditions, seed germination was rapid and uniform, whereas 150 mmol·L⁻¹ NaCl stress significantly inhibited seed germination (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1A). Physiological observations revealed that, under salt stress, ungerminated seeds (NG) exhibited an intact seed coat with slight cracking and no radicle protrusion or elongation, whereas germinated seeds (G) showed clear radicle protrusion and elongation (Fig. 1B). These results indicate that 150 mmol·L⁻¹ NaCl is an effective stress level capable of significantly distinguishing between the “germination” and “inhibition” states.

Different lowercase letters indicate significant differences between treatments (P < 0.05). Both groups included three biological replicates (n = 3), each consisting of approximately 100 seeds. B Phenotypic photographs of non-germinated (NG) and germinated (G) seeds under 150 mmol·L-1 NaCl treatment. Scale bar = 1 cm


### Overview of transcriptome sequencing and analysis of differentially expressed genes
  Illumina paired-end transcriptomic sequencing was performed on germinated (G) and non-germinated (NG) seeds treated with 150 mmol·L⁻¹ NaCl (three biological replicates each, for a total of six samples). The number of clean reads per sample remained stable, ranging from 1.94 × 10⁷ to 2.52 × 10⁷, yielding a total of approximately 6.02 × 10⁹ to 7.53 × 10⁹ bp of high-quality base sequences (clean bases). The Q30% exceeded 97.79% across all samples, with a maximum of 98.96% and an overall average of 98.52%, indicating exceptionally high base-calling accuracy. Additionally, the alignment rate of reads to the reference genome ranged from 84.39% to 94.43% across all samples. The PCA results showed clear separation among different treatment groups, indicating significant differences in gene expression between G and NG seeds under salt stress conditions (Fig. 2A). Using DESeq2 with thresholds of |FC| ≥ 1.5 and P < 0.01, numerous differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified in the G vs. NG comparison, including 8,363 up regulated and 7,077 down regulated genes (Fig. 2B). The clustering heatmap further indicated that samples from the G group and the NG group were clearly divided into two major clusters, with highly consistent expression patterns within each group and distinct differences between them (Fig. 2C). These results suggest that successful germination under salt stress is closely associated with extensive transcriptional reprogramming.

G represents the germination group, and NG represents the non-germination group. B Volcano plot of DEGs for G vs. NG. Red, green, and gray indicate significantly upregulated (Up), significantly downregulated (Down), and no significant difference (Unchange) genes, respectively. C Clustering heatmap of DEG expression patterns. In the row labels, orange represents the G group and blue represents the NG group


### GO functional annotation and enrichment analysis of differentially expressed genes
  A GO enrichment analysis was performed on the DEGs (Fig. 3). In the Cellular Component (CC) category, significantly enriched terms included “chloroplast thylakoid membrane,” “cytosol,” “photosystem II/I,” and “apoplast” (Fig. 3A). In the Biological Process (BP) category, in addition to basic metabolic processes such as “one-carbon metabolic process” and “fatty acid biosynthesis”, stress-related terms—including “hydrogen peroxide catabolic process,” “response to oxidative stress,” “cellular redox homeostasis,” and “cinnamic acid biosynthetic process”—were significantly enriched (Fig. 3B). In the Molecular Function (MF) category, “structural constituent of cell wall”, “naringenin-chalcone synthase activity”, “oxidoreductase activity”, and “peroxidase activity” were significantly enriched (Fig. 3C). Notably, “cinnamic acid biosynthetic process”, “naringenin-chalcone synthase activity”, “peroxidase activity”, and “structural constituent of cell wall” are directly associated with phenylpropanoid/flavonoid metabolism and cell wall reinforcement. Together with “response to oxidative stress” and “hydrogen peroxide catabolic process”, these findings suggest that transcriptional reprogramming in germinating seeds under salt stress is concentrated on antioxidant defense and phenylpropanoid secondary metabolism, providing a basis for subsequent targeting of the phenylpropanoid metabolic pathway.

### KEGG pathway enrichment and GSEA analysis identifying the phenylpropanoid pathway as a key salt tolerance mechanism
  To identify key salt-tolerance mechanisms at the pathway level, KEGG pathway enrichment analysis was performed on DEGs from the G vs. NG comparison. The results showed that DEGs were significantly enriched in pathways such as “carbon metabolism,” “biosynthesis of amino acids,” “flavonoid biosynthesis,” “pyruvate metabolism,” and “phenylpropanoid biosynthesis” (Fig. 4A). Notably, the two pathways directly related to phenylpropanoid metabolism—“flavonoid biosynthesis” and “phenylpropanoid biosynthesis”—both ranked among the most significantly enriched pathways, exhibiting the lowest q-values (indicated in red) and a substantial number of enriched genes, suggesting that phenylpropanoid metabolism plays a central role in the response to salt stress. To exclude the potential influence of arbitrarily defined DEG thresholds on pathway identification, we further evaluated the genome-wide ranked expression profiles using gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA), which does not rely on DEG screening. The GSEA results were consistent with KEGG: the phenylpropanoid biosynthesis gene set showed significant positive enrichment in the germination group (G). Its enrichment score curve rose rapidly to a peak at the front end of the ranked gene sets, with members of this gene set highly concentrated in the upregulated region (Fig. 4B), indicating that this pathway was strongly and coordinately upregulated in seeds that successfully germinated. Further analysis of DEGs within the phenylpropanoid biosynthesis pathway revealed that the vast majority of genes were significantly upregulated (red), while only a few were downregulated (blue), indicating a clear overall trend of upregulation (Fig. 4C).

  Within this pathway, Msa1079410, which encodes phenylalanine ammonia-lyase, exhibited the highest fold change among all differentially expressed genes (log₂FC = 7.24, corrected P = 1.3 × 10⁻⁷) (Table S1). Phylogenetic analysis with previously reported PAL family members placed Msa1079410 in the closest phylogenetic proximity to MsPAL2, and the gene was therefore designated MsPAL2 [17] (Fig. S1). In summary, using two independent methods—KEGG enrichment and GSEA—we identified the phenylpropanoid biosynthesis pathway as one of the most critical salt stress response pathways during the seed germination stage of alfalfa and identified MsPAL2 (Msa1079410) as a candidate gene within this pathway.


### VIGS-mediated silencing of MsPAL2 exacerbates the phenotype of germination inhibition under salt stress
  To verify the role of MsPAL2 in salt tolerance during germination, a pTRV2-MsPAL2 silencing vector was constructed, and transient gene silencing was performed using the TRV-mediated VIGS system, with six treatment groups established. qRT-PCR analysis showed that the relative expression level of MsPAL2 in the TRV2-MsPAL2 treatment group was significantly reduced compared with both the H₂O control and the TRV2 empty vector control (P < 0.05), with no significant difference between the H₂O and TRV2 groups (Fig. 5A). These results confirm that the VIGS silencing system is specific and effective, and that vector infection itself does not affect MsPAL2 expression. Germination rate statistics showed that under non-stress conditions, although germination rates of the H₂O-CK, H₂O-TRV2, and H₂O-TRV2-MsPAL2 groups showed a slight downward trend, they generally remained at a high level, indicating that silencing MsPAL2 has no substantial effect on germination in the absence of salt stress. Under 150 mmol·L⁻¹ NaCl stress, the germination rates decreased across all groups, and the germination rate in the NaCl-TRV2-MsPAL2 silencing group was significantly lower than that in the two control groups, NaCl-CK and NaCl-TRV2 (P < 0.05) (Fig. 5B, C). The above results indicate that the loss of MsPAL2 function specifically impairs seed germination under salt stress, providing genetic evidence that MsPAL2 is a positive regulator essential for salt tolerance during germination in alfalfa.

### In silico identification of candidate MYB upstream regulators of MsPAL2
  The MsPAL2 promoter harbors 3 MYB‑binding and 6 MYC‑binding sites, implying MYB and MYC transcription factors as candidate upstream regulators (Fig. 6A). Consistent with this, the MYB family was highly enriched among the upregulated differentially expressed genes under salt stress, and MYB family members are extensively involved in plant physiological responses to drought and salt stress, further supporting our focus on MYB‑mediated transcriptional regulation of MsPAL2 (Fig. 6B). After filtering out light‑responsive MYB members mainly involved in photomorphogenesis, de novo protein‑DNA interaction prediction via AlphaFold3 yielded four top candidates with the highest ipTM scores: Msa0036810, Msa0547960, Msa0867150 and Msa1351790, all showing comparable interaction confidence (Fig. S2). BLASTp searches against the genome‑wide Medicago sativa MYB dataset revealed that Msa0036810 and Msa1351790 correspond to MsMYB61, Msa0547960 corresponds to MsMYB48, and Msa0867150 corresponds to MsMYB12 [26]. A MsPAL2‑centered protein‑DNA interaction network was subsequently generated, with edge thickness proportional to the AlphaFold3‑predicted ipTM values (Fig. 6C), providing an important foundation for subsequent investigation into the regulatory mechanisms of MsPAL2 in response to salt stress during seed germination.

## Discussion
  Soil salinization is one of the major abiotic stresses constraining global agricultural production and the expansion of forage cultivation. Seed germination represents the developmental stage most sensitive to salt stress, directly determining seedling establishment rates and ultimate yields in saline soils [3, 7]. In this study, we employed alfalfa (Medicago sativa) cultivar ‘Zhongmu No. 1’ as the study material and integrated in situ transcriptomic sequencing with KEGG/GSEA pathway analysis and VIGS functional validation. Our findings reveal that the phenylpropanoid metabolic pathway, particularly its rate-limiting enzyme gene MsPAL2, plays a pivotal role in salt tolerance during germination. These results provide novel insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying salt tolerance in leguminous forage crops during germination.

  In this study, 150 mmol·L⁻¹ NaCl significantly inhibited the germination of alfalfa seeds and induced extensive gene differential expression, with a total of 15,440 DEGs identified. This finding is consistent with previous transcriptomic studies on salt-stressed seeds during germination in alfalfa, rice, and Arabidopsis, which reported similarly widespread transcriptional reprogramming [27]. The inhibition of germination by salt stress is generally attributed to a threefold effect: delayed water uptake caused by osmotic stress, ionic toxicity resulting from the accumulation of Na⁺ and Cl⁻, and oxidative damage caused by ROS burst [28]. GO enrichment analysis in this study revealed that DEGs were highly concentrated in categories such as “redox processes,” “response to oxidative stress,” “antioxidant activity,” and “secondary metabolite biosynthesis,” which align with the aforementioned stress mechanisms—germinating seeds must rapidly establish antioxidant defenses and reprogram secondary metabolism to maintain redox homeostasis [29]. Notably, significant enrichment of “cell wall”-related entries in the GO enrichment analysis suggests that cell wall remodeling (such as lignin deposition) may also be involved in the salt tolerance response during the germination stage, consistent with the role of lignin synthesis downstream of the phenylpropanoid pathway [30, 31].

  In this study, the phenylpropanoid biosynthesis pathway was identified as the most significantly activated metabolic pathway under salt stress using two independent methods: KEGG enrichment and GSEA. As one of the most important secondary metabolic pathways in plants, the phenylpropanoid pathway produces flavonoids, lignin, phenolic acids, and anthocyanins, all of which play a key role in plants’ responses to various abiotic stresses [11]. Under salt stress, phenylpropanoid metabolites enhance salt tolerance primarily through three mechanisms: flavonoids and phenolic acids act as potent non-enzymatic antioxidants that directly scavenge ROS and alleviate oxidative damage [32]; lignin deposition reinforces the cell wall, limiting Na⁺ transport via the apoplast and maintaining cellular structural integrity [33] ; certain phenolic compounds participate in osmotic regulation and signal transduction [11]. Furthermore, studies on exogenous treatments such as melatonin and salicylic acid have confirmed that activation of the phenylpropanoid pathway and the promotion of flavonoid accumulation constitute a key mechanism by which these substances enhance plant salt tolerance [34]. This is highly consistent with our findings that the phenylpropanoid pathway was synergistically upregulated in seeds that successfully germinated under salt stress.

  Among the enzymes in this pathway, phenylalanine ammonia-lyase (PAL) serves as the key rate-limiting enzyme linking primary and secondary metabolism, determining carbon allocation to the phenylpropanoid pathway and functioning as a critical node in phenylpropanoid metabolism [14]. The PAL gene family typically exists as a polygenic family in plants, with members exhibiting distinct functional specialization. In Arabidopsis, AtPAL1 and AtPAL2 play dominant roles in flavonoid accumulation, while AtPAL3 and AtPAL4 are more involved in lignin synthesis [19]. In alfalfa, seven MsPAL members have been identified, with tissue-specific expression patterns varying in response to different stresses; notably, MsPAL6 was the most significantly upregulated under salt stress [17]. This study further provides direct genetic evidence through VIGS-mediated loss-of-function experiments: under non-stress conditions, silencing MsPAL2 had no significant effect on germination; however, under salt stress, the germination rate in the silenced group decreased significantly. This suggests that MsPAL2 is not essential for germination itself, but rather serves as a positive regulator required for the response to salt stress during germination. This result is consistent with previous findings, which demonstrated that overexpression of MsPAL1 enhances alfalfa salt tolerance by increasing flavonoid accumulation and boosting antioxidant enzyme activity [18], thereby strongly supporting the central role of MsPAL genes in alfalfa salt tolerance. Similar studies have been reported in other species, for example, silencing or inhibiting PAL reduces stress tolerance in tomato and wheat [35, 36], further illustrating that PAL-mediated phenylpropanoid metabolism is a conserved and critical pathway for plant salt tolerance. Notably, VIGS-mediated gene silencing is transient, mosaic in expression, and exhibits variable efficiency, with effects that are non-heritable [37]; these limitations render it suitable only for preliminary gene screening, whereas robust functional validation of MsPAL2 requires a stable transgenic system.

  Building on the functional evidence for MsPAL2, this study further explored its upstream transcriptional regulation. PlantTFDB annotation of the upregulated DEGs identified multiple salt-responsive MYB transcription factors co-expressed with MsPAL2, and PlantCARE analysis detected MYB-binding cis-elements in the MsPAL2 promoter. Using AlphaFold3-based de novo prediction of protein-DNA binding, four MYB candidates — Msa0036810 and Msa1351790 (MsMYB61), Msa0547960 (MsMYB48), and Msa0867150 (MsMYB12) — exhibited the highest and comparable binding confidence to the MsPAL2 promoter among all co-expressed MYB family members (Fig. 6C). The parallel upregulation of these candidates alongside MsPAL2 under salt stress, combined with AlphaFold3-predicted binding capacity, indicates that they serve as upstream transcription factors regulating MsPAL2 in response to salt stress. Future studies can further substantiate this regulatory relationship through yeast one-hybrid, EMSA, or luciferase reporter assays.


## Conclusion
  In this study, we employed alfalfa (Medicago sativa) cultivar ‘Zhongmu No. 1’ as the experimental material and elucidated the regulatory mechanisms underlying salt tolerance during seed germination through transcriptomics, gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA), and virus-induced gene silencing (VIGS) techniques. Treatment with 150 mmol·L⁻¹ NaCl significantly inhibited alfalfa seed germination over a 48-hour period. Transcriptome analysis identified 15,440 differentially expressed genes in response to salt stress, with KEGG and GSEA analyses convergently identifying the phenylpropanoid metabolism pathway as the most significantly activated metabolic pathway under salt stress. VIGS-mediated silencing of MsPAL2, a key rate-limiting enzyme gene in this pathway, resulted in a significant decrease in germination rates under salt stress but had no effect under non-stress conditions, confirming that MsPAL2 is specifically required for salt tolerance during germination. Promoter analysis further revealed MYB-binding cis-elements upstream of MsPAL2, and AlphaFold3-based prediction identified four MYB transcription factors as candidate upstream regulators, providing a foundation for future dissection of the MsPAL2 regulatory network. Collectively, these findings reveal the pivotal role of phenylpropanoid metabolism in salt tolerance during alfalfa germination and establish MsPAL2 as a promising candidate gene for molecular breeding of salt-tolerant varieties. Furthermore, the integrated analytical framework employed herein provides a methodological reference for identifying key genes associated with other abiotic stress traits in forage crops.
