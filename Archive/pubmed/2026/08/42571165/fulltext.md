# A Comprehensive In-Silico Pipeline for the Discovery of Non-toxic, Stable Antimicrobial Peptides From Databases for Targeting Multi-Drug Resistant K. pneumoniae


## Abstract
  The rapid emergence of multidrug-resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae has significantly reduced the effectiveness of conventional antibiotics, highlighting the need for alternative therapeutic strategies. This study employed a comprehensive in silico pipeline to identify antimicrobial peptides (AMPs) targeting the essential DNA replication initiator protein DnaA. A total of 28,361 peptide sequences were collected from publicly available AMP databases and sequentially filtered based on peptide length, net charge, GRAVY score, instability index, antimicrobial activity, toxicity, hemolytic potential, aggregation propensity, sequence similarity and favorable amphipathic properties. Four peptides satisfied all selection criteria and were subjected to structural prediction, membrane-binding analysis, protein–DNA docking, protein–peptide docking, and Normal Mode Analysis. Protein–DNA docking identified the functional DNA-binding residues of DnaA, while peptide docking demonstrated that all four peptides interacted within this region. Peptide 3 exhibited the strongest predicted interaction, with a binding energy of −61.8±5.1, a buried surface area of 1151.7±30.8 Å2, and seven hydrogen bonds with key DnaA residues. Normal Mode Analysis further supported the structural stability of the peptide–protein complexes. These findings identify four promising AMP candidates targeting DnaA and provide a computational framework for peptide prioritization against multidrug-resistant K. pneumoniae. However, the proposed interactions remain computational predictions and require experimental validation.


## 1. Introduction
  Antimicrobial resistance (AMR) is no longer a distant concern. Currently, it is a pressing global health challenge that threatens years of progress in infectious disease management. As multidrug-resistant (MDR) organisms continue to evolve, the clinical effectiveness of many conventional antibiotics is losing its activity. 1  The consequences are serious because of longer hospital stays, increased treatment failures, rising mortality, and greater healthcare costs. 2  Among the pathogens driving this crisis, Klebsiella pneumoniae has emerged as one of the most problematic. MDR strains are now common in hospital settings and are responsible for pneumonia, bloodstream infections, urinary tract infections, and sepsis. 3  The rise of carbapenem-resistant and extensively drug-resistant variants has further narrowed treatment options, making the search for alternative strategies increasingly urgent.

  In this context, antimicrobial peptides (AMPs) have drawn considerable attention. Unlike many traditional antibiotics that target specific enzymes or metabolic pathways, AMPs often act by disrupting bacterial membranes or interfering with essential intracellular components.4,5 This broader mode of action may reduce the speed at which resistance develops. At the same time, the rapid expansion of peptide databases and machine learning–based predictive tools has made it possible to screen large peptide libraries more efficiently than ever before.6,7 However, only a small proportion of known AMPs possess the right balance of potency, selectivity, structural stability, and low toxicity required for therapeutic development. Empirical selection approaches frequently yield candidates that are hemolytic, unstable, or unsuitable for systemic use. A more systematic and rational screening strategy is therefore necessary.

  While membrane disruption remains a central antimicrobial mechanism, targeting essential bacterial proteins offers an additional and potentially synergistic strategy. The chromosomal replication initiator protein DnaA is necessary for bacterial DNA replication, as it binds to specific DnaA-box sequences within the origin of replication to initiate the replication process.8,9 Interfering with this interaction could effectively halt bacterial proliferation at its earliest stage. Unlike many conventional antibacterial targets, DnaA remains relatively underexplored. Only a limited number of small-molecule inhibitors have been reported with no clinically approved DnaA-targeted therapeutics. Moreover, no experimentally validated antimicrobial peptide has been reported to specifically target the DnaA protein of Klebsiella pneumoniae. Therefore, identifying peptides with the potential to interact with this essential protein may provide a promising starting point for the development of alternative antibacterial strategies, although experimental validation is required to confirm their activity. Recent advances in protein structure prediction tools such as AlphaFold have made it feasible to generate high-confidence structural models even in the absence of experimentally determined data. 10  This has opened new opportunities for structure-based peptide screening and rational interaction analysis.

  Although computational approaches for AMP discovery are increasingly common, relatively few studies combine multi-step physicochemical filtering, cross-platform activity prediction, toxicity and hemolysis assessment, membrane-binding evaluation, structural modeling, and targeted docking into a single integrated framework focused specifically on MDR K. pneumoniae. Moreover, the possibility that AMPs might inhibit the DnaA DNA-binding domain has not been explored.

  Therefore, the present study employed a comprehensive in silico pipeline to identify promising antimicrobial peptides from databases for targetting against MDR K. pneumoniae. Peptide sequences were collected from established AMP databases and systematically filtered according to physicochemical criteria, predicted antimicrobial activity, toxicity, hemolytic potential, stability, and aggregation tendency. The top candidates were modeled structurally and assessed for membrane-binding propensity. In parallel, the three-dimensional structure of the DnaA protein was predicted and validated. Protein–DNA and protein–peptide docking analyses were done to examine potential competitive inhibition. Finally, normal mode analysis was conducted to evaluate the dynamic stability of the resulting complexes.


## 2. Method

### 2.1. Collecting Antimicrobial Peptides
  APD6 (Antimicrobial Peptide Database) (https://aps.unmc.edu/, accessed on 22 December 2025), 11  DRAMP 4.0 (Data Repository of Antimicrobial Peptides) (https://dramp.cpu-bioinfor.org/, accessed on 22 December 2025), 12  and DBAASP V3 (Database of Antimicrobial Activity and Structure of Peptides) (https://dbaasp.org/home, accessed on 22 December 2025) 13  were used to collect the antimicrobial peptide sequences. These are publicly available and widely used databases. After collection, all peptide sequences from those databases were combined and saved in a single file.


### 2.2. Peptide Filtering
  All peptide sequences were analyzed using a Python script based on the Biopython library to calculate their physicochemical properties.14,15 Those physicochemical properties were applied to filter peptide sequences. Net charge was selected more than 3 to ensure sufficient positive charge to expect interaction with membrane of Gram-negative bacterial. Literature showing that positive charge is critical for antimicrobial activity and optimal activity typically observed in moderately to strongly cationic peptides. 16  Most AMPs possess a net positive charge ranging from +1 to +9 and can form well-defined cationic domains. 17  A cutoff of > +3 was chosen because previous experimental studies reported meaningful antimicrobial activity in peptides with moderate to high positivity, including net charge variance from -5 to +10 has been established that both the overall net charge value and the exact number of positively charged residues are fundamental drivers of antimicrobial activity. 18  While minimalist cationic lipopeptides evaluated across +1 to +4 range indicate that a baseline charge of at least +2 is necessary to trigger significant biological activity when balanced with complementary traits. 19  Furthermore, a large positive charge threshold of approximately ≳4 has been identified as a key requirement for achieving both peak antimicrobial activity and host-cell selectivity, particularly because larger charges are required to maintain membrane-disrupting efficacy under high-salt, physiological conditions where ionic shielding occurs. 20  Consequently, establishing a screening cutoff of > +3 was selected to prioritize the highly cationic peptides. GRAVY (Grand Average of Hydropathy) score was used to assess the overall hydropathic balance of candidate peptides. 21  Positive GRAVY value indicates increasing hydrophobicity and negative value indicates increasing hydrophilicity. 22  Because the initial dataset contained a large number of peptide sequences, a relatively stringent threshold (GRAVY ≥0.3) was intentionally selected to prioritize peptides with greater membrane-interacting potential. This threshold was used as a screening criterion for candidate prioritization rather than as a universal requirement for antimicrobial activity. The instability index was also calculated, and peptides with values below 40 were included to prioritize sequences predicted to be stable under physiological conditions. 23  Moreover, peptides with a sequence length between 10 and 20 amino acid residues were retained. Because, short peptides with only few amino acids are usually cannot create the necessary secondary structure and they can be quickly degraded in biological system. Besides, long peptides create challenges in synthesizing with good yield and handling. 24


### 2.3. Evaluation of Functional and Safety Profiles
  The primary objective of this computational pipeline was to establish a high-reliability filtering workflow using established, publicly available prediction servers. Because individual machine-learning models are trained on different datasets and utilize varying algorithmic architectures, relying on a single tool can introduce software-specific biases or false-positive results. To overcome this limitation and maximize the reliability of our findings, we implemented a three-tool consensus validation strategy for each evaluated property (antimicrobial activity, toxicity, and hemolysis). A peptide sequence was only considered a trusted positive candidate if it successfully passed the prediction thresholds of all three independent tools simultaneously. This approach ensures that our final selections do not rely on the quirks of a single algorithm, but rather on a robust consensus across multiple independent predictive platforms.


#### 2.3.1. Activity Prediction
  To check whether the collected peptides had antimicrobial properties, three online tools with pre-trained models were used. Antimicrobial Peptide Scanner vr.2 (https://www.dveltri.com/ascan/v2/, accessed on 25 December 2025), 25  Collection of Anti-Microbial Peptides (https://camp.bicnirrh.res.in/, accessed on 25 December 2025), 26  and Database of Antimicrobial Activity and Structure of Peptides (https://dbaasp.org/home, accessed on 25 December 2025) 13  were the tools utilized for detection. These tools predicted whether a peptide is AMP-positive or not based on its sequence and features. Only peptides that showed a positive result in all three checks were selected for the next step. Based on this filtering process, it was ensured to have peptides with strong antimicrobial properties.


#### 2.3.2. Toxicity Prediction
  The toxicity of AMP-positive peptides was analyzed after their identification by three online tools. ToxinPred 3 (https://webs.iiitd.edu.in/raghava/toxinpred3/, accessed on 26 December 2025), 27  CSM-Toxin (https://biosig.lab.uq.edu.au/csm_toxin/, accessed on 26 December 2025), 28  SpepToxPred (https://www.wang-subgroup.com/TasToxPred/TasToxPred, accessed on 26 December 2025) 29  were the tools utilized for identification. All peptides that were positive AMP sent to the three servers and toxicity or non-toxicity was predicted for peptides in the three servers. Peptides that were non-toxic in all three tests only have been chosen for next steps. This step was meant to remove peptides more likely to be toxic for host cells and confirm to that the final peptides would be potential antimicrobial active peptides with non-toxic property.


#### 2.3.3. Final Selection
  The non-toxic-peptides were subsequently analyzed to be potentially good antimicrobials and not start damaging red blood cells. The peptides that were predicted as non-toxic from three different online tool selected for hemolytic activity prediction. Because non-hemolytic peptides won’t rupture red blood cells. HemoPI2 (https://webs.iiitd.edu.in/raghava/hemopi2/, 26 December 2025), 30  DBAASP V3 (Database of Antimicrobial Activity and Structure of Peptides) (https://dbaasp.org/home, accessed on 26 December 2025), 13  and dbAMP 3.0 (Database of Antimicrobial Peptides) (https://awi.cuhk.edu.cn/dbAMP/, 26 December 2025) 31  were the online tools used to predict non-hemolytic peptides. Peptides that were predicted as non-hemolytic were checked for aggregation level using PeptideCalc (https://www.novoprolabs.com/tools/calc_peptide_property, 26 December 2025) online peptide property calculator. For peptides with low aggregation were calculated for hydrophobic moment, isoelectric point, and hydrophobicity to check do the peptides maintain favorable amphipathic properties to effectively interact with the biological membranes. ExPasy ProtParam (https://web.expasy.org/protparam/, accessed on 26 December 2025) 32  and HeliQuest (https://heliquest.ipmc.cnrs.fr/cgi-bin/ComputParamsV2.py, accessed on 26 December 2025) 33  tools were used here. Also, peptides with more than 85% similarity were removed.


### 2.4. Peptide’s 3D Structure Prediction
  The 3D structure of the resulted peptides was obtained by Pep-Fold4 (https://bioserv.rpbs.univ-paris-diderot.fr/services/PEP-FOLD4/, 27 December 2025) webserver. PEP-FOLD4 is a de novo peptide structure prediction server. It is able to simulate peptides of up to about 40 amino acids in aqueous environment. It applies a new pH-dependent force field that allows for greater accuracy than seen in earlier versions. By taking pH and salt changes into consideration, this tool can capture conformational shifts more effectively. 34  The solvent pH was assumed to be 7.5 and the ionic strength of the solution was considered as 150 mM in this work. These values were chosen to match the pH and NaCl concentration found in the human body.


### 2.5. Peptide-membrane Binding Prediction
  To explore the membrane-binding properties of peptides identified against Klebsiella pneumoniae, the PMIpred web server (https://pmipred.bioinformatics.cn, 27 December 2025) was applied. 35  This platform uses a transformer-based neural network trained on coarse-grained molecular dynamics simulations with the Martini force field to estimate peptide–membrane interaction energetics. For each sequence, the PMIpred web tool calculated binding free energy (ΔΔF) with the adjusted free energy (ΔΔF_adj) value, which provided a basic understanding of the peptide-membrane binding through the computational tool. Predictions were carried out selecting negatively charged membrane.


### 2.6. Protein’s Structure Prediction and Analysis

#### 2.6.1. Structure Prediction
  As the experimental structure of the chromosomal replication initiator protein (DnaA) for the bacteria was not present, the 3D structure was generated/predicted using the AlphaFold (https://alphafoldserver.com/, 31 December 2025) web server. Currently, this server runs on the AlphaFold 3 model. This model can generate precise biomolecular structures. By applying structure-based templates and multiple sequence alignment, this model learns to fold residues. 36  Bacterial protein sequence was collected from the NCBI (National Center for Biotechnology Information) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/, 31 December 2025). 37


#### 2.6.2. Protein Structure Measurement
  The predicted model was checked by ProSa-Web (https://prosa.services.came.sbg.ac.at/prosa.php, 31 December 2025) and Errat (https://saves.mbi.ucla.edu/, 31 December 2025) online tools. 38  Those tools helped to identify probable errors in the structure. The energy plot from ProSa-Web showed local structural mistakes.39,40 Moreover, the Ramachandran plot for that model was examined by the Ramplot (https://ramplot.in/index.php, 3 January 2026) online tool. 41  Because a structure with more than 90% amino acids inside the favorable regions is considered to have better reliability. 42  Again, the intrinsic disordered regions were calculated by the AIUpred (https://aiupred.elte.hu/, 3 January 2026) online tool. 43


#### 2.6.3. Finding out DNA Binding Domain
  Moreover, the DnaA protein binds with the DnaA-box of chromosomal DNA. For this action, the protein used a specific DNA binding domain, which is called domain IV. As that protein has no experimental data of its domain IV location, the InterPro (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/interpro/, 3 January 2026) online tool was used to identify the domain. 44  Furthermore, the 9 base-pair DnaA-box was identified through the Orifinder (https://tubic.org/Ori-Finder2022/public/index.php, 3 January 2026) online tool. 45


### 2.7. Docking

#### 2.7.1. Protein-DNA Docking
  The chromosomal replication initiator protein (DnaA) was docked with the bacterial DnaA-box through HDOCK (https://hdock.phys.hust.edu.cn/, 4 January 2026) and the pyDockDNA (https://model3dbio.csic.es/pydockdna, 4 January 2026) online tool. These tools were designed for protein-DNA docking. HDOCK works on a hybrid algorithm for docking. It uses both ab initio (free docking) and combined template-based docking. 46  Besides, pyDockDNA works using FTDock. By FTDock, the tool generates probable protein-DNA binding poses and evaluates the poses by a tuned (mainly for protein-DNA interactions) scoring function. 47


#### 2.7.2. Protein-Peptide Docking
  Docking of the target protein and peptide structures via Haddock (https://wenmr.science.uu.nl/haddock2.4/, 5 January 2026) online server was done. Because HADDOCK is a docking tool that uses Ambiguous Interaction Restraints (AIRs) to guide modeling from residues that were marked as active or passive. It works in three simple steps: rigid-body docking, a semi-flexible simulated annealing step, and a final refinement in water. These steps let side chains and the backbone move. The models are scored by their energy and by how well similar solutions cluster together. 48


### 2.8. Normal Mode Analysis
  Normal Mode Analysis (NMA) was performed to investigate the internal motions and flexibility of a protein–peptide complex. 49  The analysis was carried out using the iMODS (https://imods.iqf.csic.es/, accessed on 8 January 2026) web server, which provides a fast and intuitive platform for visualizing molecular dynamics based on internal coordinates. 50  The structure of the complex was uploaded in PDB format. During setup, the HA (Heavy Atom) option was selected to include all non-hydrogen atoms in the calculation. This model offers a detailed representation of atomic interactions and is suitable for analyzing both proteins and small peptides.


## 3. Results

### 3.1. Peptide Collection and Primary Selection
  A total of 28,361 peptide sequences were collected from three public databases: APD6 (3,302 peptides), DBAASP (19,516 peptides), and DRAMP (5,443 peptides). To avoid repetition, the duplicate entries were removed from the combined dataset. Peptides containing unknown amino acids, sequences with gaps or dashes, were also excluded, and 19187 peptides remained. This cleaning step helped to prepare a high-quality dataset for further analysis.

  Based on the four primary criteria that were sequence length (10–20 amino acids), net charge (>3), GRAVY score (>=0.3), and instability index (<40), a total of 726 peptides were selected from the cleaned dataset. These peptides showed a suitable balance of charge, hydrophobicity, and predicted stability, making them strong candidates for further antimicrobial analysis.

### 3.7. NMA of Protein-Peptide Complex
  Normal mode analysis was performed to evaluate the dynamic stability of the four protein–peptide complexes. The eigenvalue distribution for the first 20 modes is shown in Figure 15. In all four systems, eigenvalues increased gradually with mode index. No abnormal spikes or irregular patterns were observed. This trend indicates stable and physically reasonable structural behavior. The first eigenvalue of Protein+Peptide-4 was 1.601613×10-5. This was the lowest among the four complexes, suggesting significantly higher flexibility. Protein+Peptide-1 showed the highest first eigenvalue at 1.403702×10-4, indicating comparatively greater rigidity. Protein+Peptide-2 and Protein+Peptide-3 displayed intermediate values of 1.347063×10-4 and 1.343891×10-4, respectively, reflecting moderate stiffness. Although minor differences were observed, the overall eigenvalue profiles suggest comparable dynamic stability across the systems. Overall, the NMA results support the structural integrity of the protein–peptide complexes and indicate stable motion profiles without significant instability.Figure 15.Normalized eigenvalue distribution across 20 modes for the protein–peptide complex

## 4. Discussion
  At the present time, many antibiotics have become less useful because of the rapid growth of multidrug-resistant (MDR) Klebsiella pneumoniae. 51  This critical condition has created the need for an urgent treatment option against the superbug. Due to high death rates and carbapenem resistance enzymes, K. pneumoniae has become the main priority pathogen based on recent reports. 52  The present study followed a structure-based full computational approach to find out potential antimicrobial peptides (AMPs) that may work against the bacteria. This study explored the possibility that selected antimicrobial peptides could interact with the intracellular replication initiator protein DnaA in addition to exhibiting membrane-active properties. Although computational analyses suggested favorable binding, experimental validation is required to determine whether these interactions translate into functional inhibition of bacterial DNA replication.

  The drop-down of a large number of peptide (28,361) sequences into only four sequences presents by the strictly followed filtering criteria. Because of toxicity, hemolysis activity, instability, and excess aggregation, many peptides are reported as failures when moving forward to real-life use.30,53 For these reasons, the study followed strict filtering criteria. In the first step, the peptides that were kept only had a length of 10-20 amino acids, a net charge of >+3, a GRAVY score of >=0.3, and an instability score less than 40. Short peptides containing only a few amino acids generally fail to form stable secondary structures and are rapidly broken down in biological environments. On the other hand, longer peptides are difficult to synthesize efficiently and can be challenging to manage in terms of yield and handling. 24  Moreover, the minimum positive charge and hydrophobicity were confirmed for the selected peptides. Because antimicrobial peptides need minimum positive charge to attach with the negatively charged Gram-negative bacterial membranes and their hydrophobic character helps them to insert inside the lipid bilayer, which often lead to pore formation.54-56

  The antimicrobial probability was checked for the identified 726 peptides from the first step filtration. Only peptides predicted as active (434 peptides) by all three independent tools were kept for toxicity analysis. The toxicity analysis predicted 156 peptides as non-toxic, which was 35.9% of the total identified antimicrobial peptides. Moreover, the hemolytic activity prediction showed only 19 non-hemolytic peptides. The non-hemolytic activity of the peptides suggested safety for red blood cells in the human body. To keep diversity, highly similar peptides were removed from the list. Because peptide aggregation reduces the efficacy of a peptide and triggers an immune response, peptides showing high aggregation tendency were excluded.53,57 Only four candidate peptides were left after all those filtrations.

  The Pep-Fold4 structure prediction tool predicted the peptide structures. This latest version was especially designed to predict small peptides in a pH and salt concentration. 58  The helical wheel diagrams showed that the amino acids of the peptides were clustered on two sides. The hydrophobic residues (Leu, Val, Iso, Phe) clustered on one side of the wheel and the positively charged residues (Lys, Arg) on the opposite side. This creates a clear split between the water-hating and the positively charged faces. A hydrophobic face and a cationic face in a peptide make it amphipathic and a key feature of membrane-active peptides.59,60 Furthermore, PMIpred predicted all four peptides in the binding region of the negatively charged membrane. The prediction showed good binding of the peptides to the membrane. The potential binding suggests that positive amino acids of the peptides were electrostatically attached to the anionic head groups of membrane and this attraction could potentially lead to pore formation on the membrane. Because the peptides containing arginine and lysine bind electrostatically with the negative lipids.61-63 The physicochemical characteristics and membrane-binding predictions suggest that these peptides possess features commonly associated with membrane-active AMPs. However, the actual membrane-disrupting activity cannot be confirmed without experimental assays.

  Most classical cationic antimicrobial peptides primarily act on negatively bacterial membranes.64,65 Targeting the essential replication initiator protein represents a hypothetical mechanism of action which was suggested by the computational docking analysis. The DnaA is an essential protein for starting the chromosomal replication in bacteria.66,67 The predicted peptide binding within the DNA-binding domain raises the possibility of competitive interference with DnaA-box recognition. However, docking alone cannot establish functional inhibition. Besides, the predicted structure of the DnaA protein showed promising results in validation. The predicted structure provided an Errat quality factor value of more than 97, which means a good quality model. 68  Energy plots presented that the structure predicted was stable. The Ramachandran plot exhibited that more than 97% of the structure falls under the favored area, which means a stable structure. 69  Moreover, the AIUpred graph showed that the structure was not fully rigid or flexible. Overall, the structure showed a positive result.

  The protein-DNA docking through HDock and pyDockDNA identified Arg399, Arg405, Arg407, Asp433, Thr435, Thr436, His434, and Arg442 as key interacting residues of protein. Mainly, Arg399, Asp433, His434 and Thr435 were the residues directly connected with the bases of the DNA in the major and minor grooves, and the rest of the amino acids were connected to the DNA’s backbone. The identified Arg399, Asp433, His434, and Thr435 residues are significant and properly matched with the experimental data of E. coli. 70  These are the residues from the HTH motif and create direct bonds with DNA bases. From that experimental data on E. coli DnaA-DNA-box binding, the other amino acids were also found and those residues act as anchors that stick tightly with the negatively charged DNA backbone.

  The spatial overlap between predicted peptide-binding residues and the DNA-binding pocket is consistent with a possible competitive binding mechanism. However, docking provides only a structural hypothesis and cannot demonstrate competitive inhibition. Peptide 3 demonstrated the promising docking score (−61.8±5.1). It also showed the largest buried surface area (1151.7±30.8 Å2). Importantly, it formed interactions with Arg399, Asp433, and Thr436 residues that are directly involved in DNA recognition. This overlap strengthens the argument that Peptide 3 may obstruct DnaA-box binding. Peptide 2 showed the docking strength of −55.1±2.5, but showed lowest buried surface area than other four peptides. Peptide 1 showed the weakest interaction metrics. However, docking scores alone do not prove biological inhibition.

  The Normal Mode Analysis (NMA) was performed using the iMODS server to evaluate the collective motions and structural flexibility of the protein-peptide complexes. The Variance plots for all four complexes (Protein-peptide 1, 2, 3, and 4) showed that the majority of the structural variance is captured within the first few low-frequency modes, which is characteristic of stable and well-defined protein systems. Specifically, the cumulative variance (indicated by green bars) reached approximately 80-90% within the first 10-12 modes, suggesting that these modes adequately describe the significant global motions of the complexes. Overall, the consistent distribution of eigenvalues and variance across the twenty modes confirms the mechanical stability and structural integrity of all four protein-peptide complexes upon binding.

  As this current study was fully computational, there must be limitations. Computational work may provide a foundation, but it cannot replace experimental analysis. Experimental analysis shows how the protein and peptide will work in real life. The in-silico method is useful for first-step analysis before lab. Because working on thousands of peptides is so time-consuming and costly. Hence, in silico work helps to find out or predict potential ones. First of all, the present study is totally in silico, and no experimental tests were done to see the biological activity of the peptides. The structure of the DnaA protein was predicted using AlphaFold, but that structure was not validated using X-ray crystallography or the cryo-EM technique. The peptide selection was focused on Gram-negative bacteria. There must be other potential peptides that will work against gram-positive bacteria. Human toxicity and the hemolytic activity of the peptides were calculated by machine learning tools based on real-life data. But real-life toxicity and hemolytic activity checking can find out rare or unexpected effects. In addition, the machine-learning prediction tools used in this study were trained on different datasets and algorithmic architectures, which may introduce dataset-specific biases and false-positive predictions despite the consensus strategy adopted. Likewise, membrane-binding predictions were performed using simplified membrane models that do not fully capture the structural complexity of Gram-negative bacterial envelopes. Therefore, the computational predictions presented here should be considered as a prioritization strategy for candidate selection rather than confirmation of antimicrobial efficacy Moreover, docking, binding free energy, and membrane-binding predictions showed potential activity, but it is not proven that the peptides will work the same in experiments or real life. Furthermore, MD simulation was not performed. Though NMA analysis presented a stable formation, it did not perform a simulation to show stability over time.


## 5. Conclusion
  This study presents a comprehensive and stringent in silico workflow for the identification of antimicrobial peptides targeting the essential DNA replication initiator protein DnaA of multidrug-resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae. Starting from 28,361 peptide sequences, sequential physicochemical, functional, safety, and structural filtering reduced the dataset to four high-confidence candidates with favorable antimicrobial, non-toxic, non-hemolytic, stable, and membrane-binding properties. Protein–DNA docking identified the key residues involved in DnaA-box recognition, while protein–peptide docking demonstrated that all four peptides occupy the DNA-binding region, suggesting their potential to interfere with bacterial DNA replication. Among them, Peptide 3 exhibited the better predicted interaction and the most favorable docking characteristics. Normal Mode Analysis further supported the structural stability of the peptide–protein complexes. Overall, this computational framework provides an efficient strategy for prioritizing promising antimicrobial peptides against MDR K. pneumoniae. Nevertheless, these findings remain predictive and require comprehensive in vitro and in vivo validation to confirm antimicrobial efficacy, target inhibition, safety, and therapeutic potential.
