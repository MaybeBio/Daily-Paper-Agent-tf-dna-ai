## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence as presented in the supplied abstract; no full text, figures, tables, or supplementary materials were provided
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report a fully in silico comparison of metal-free His(6) and tri-metal model complexes [Zn(3)(His(6))](6+), [Ni(3)(His(6))](6+), and [Cu(3)(His(6))](6+) against the B-DNA dodecamer 1BNA, using molecular docking and molecular dynamics. They report that metal-free His(6) yields a dominant major-groove pose with adenine-associated contacts and a Vina score of -8.9 kcal/mol, while the tri-metal models yield dominant minor-groove poses with guanine-associated contacts and scores of -9.5, -13.5, and -10.8 kcal/mol for Zn(II), Ni(II), and Cu(II), respectively. The Ni(II)- and Cu(II)-containing models show lower RMSD levels than the metal-free complex. The authors explicitly restrict conclusions to the defined computational models and the 1BNA sequence.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no methodological details, trajectory analyses, docking parameters, force fields, or statistical validations are provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, methods section, all figures and tables, simulation parameters, force field choices, docking validation, replicate information, and any statistical analyses

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a clearly scoped computational comparison with an appropriate caveat that the results are model-specific and hypothesis-generating. The central observation, that metal coordination shifts predicted groove preference from major to minor groove and alters nucleobase contacts, is potentially interesting for the field of metal-peptide-DNA interactions. However, the abstract alone provides insufficient evidence to evaluate the technical soundness of the docking and MD protocols, the robustness of the reported scores and RMSD values, or the biological relevance of the chosen models. The claims are internally consistent but cannot be independently assessed without the full methods and results.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers working on histidine-rich peptide applications in affinity purification, metal-mediated DNA recognition, and computational prediction of peptide-DNA interactions. The work may also interest those developing force field or docking approaches for metal-containing peptide systems, given the explicit modeling choices described.
- **Major strengths** The authors clearly delimit the scope of their claims, explicitly stating that the results do not establish experimental binding affinities or universal selectivity. The comparison across three metal ions with a metal-free control is a reasonable design. The acknowledgment that the formulas represent computational representations rather than solution-phase speciation is a commendable transparency measure.
- **Major Concerns** None identified from the supplied material, as the abstract is appropriately cautious and no specific technical claims can be falsified from the abstract alone. However, the lack of methodological detail prevents any positive assessment of technical soundness.
- **Minor Comments** None identified from the supplied material.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** The case is not established from the abstract alone. The following are required to assess the claims: full docking protocols, force field parameters for the metal centers, MD simulation lengths and equilibration details, RMSD calculation methods, replicate numbers, and any convergence or statistical analyses. Without these, the reported scores and RMSD differences cannot be evaluated.

A supportive posture would be contingent on the methods and results meeting standard computational rigor.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The reported docking scores (-8.9, -9.5, -13.5, -10.8 kcal/mol) are presented without any indication of variance, replicate number, or docking protocol validation, making them unverifiable from the abstract.
- The claim that Ni(II)- and Cu(II)-containing models display lower RMSD levels than the metal-free complex is unsupported without trajectory length, convergence criteria, or statistical comparison.
- The statement that metal-free His(6) produces a "dominant major-groove pose" and tri-metal models produce "dominant minor-groove poses" is unverifiable without clustering methods or population analyses.
- The biological relevance of the tri-metal models, as acknowledged by the authors, is not established, and the abstract correctly avoids overclaiming in this regard.
