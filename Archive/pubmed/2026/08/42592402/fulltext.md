# Exploring Potential Stress Granule-Related Biomarkers in Childhood Asthma Using Integrated Bioinformatics and Machine Learning


## Objective
  Asthma is a common chronic lung disease in children, but the role of stress granules (SGs) in its pathogenesis remains unclear. The present study aims to investigate overlapping genes and regulatory mechanisms between childhood asthma (CA) and SGs via integrated bioinformatics and machine learning.


## Methods
  Machine learning algorithms were applied to screen for potential biomarkers among the candidate genes. Their expression levels were validated, followed by ROC analysis. Multiple bioinformatics analyses, including mRNA-miRNA and transcription factor regulatory networks, were performed to explore their potential functions. Finally, RT-qPCR was used to validate the expression differences between CA and healthy control blood samples.


## Results
  Through machine learning, 11 candidate genes were initially selected, with 3 potential biomarkers (HNRNPA2B1, RPE, TAF15) determined. These biomarkers showed strong diagnostic performance (AUC > 0.7). GeneMANIA and Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) revealed that their functions were enriched in biological processes such as NADPH regeneration. Immunoinfiltration analysis identified four types of differentially infiltrating immune cells: CD56dim natural killer cell, Central memory CD8 T cell, Immature B cell, and Monocyte. Additionally, RT-PCR validation confirmed significantly elevated mRNA expression of HNRNPA2B1, RPE, and TAF15 in CA patients compared to healthy controls, consistent with the bioinformatics predictions.


## Conclusion
  This study screened out three potential biomarkers related to SGs in CA, offering new insights into disease pathogenesis and potential molecular targets for improved therapy.


## Introduction
  Asthma is one of the most prevalent chronic respiratory diseases in children, characterized by heterogeneous and reversible airway inflammation, hyperresponsiveness, and remodeling. It manifests clinically as recurrent episodes of wheeze, shortness of breath, chest tightness, and cough.1 The onset of asthma most commonly occurs in early childhood, with global epidemiological data indicating that approximately 14% of children worldwide are affected.2 Recurrent asthma attacks significantly impair children’s quality of life, hinder normal growth, and impose substantial socioeconomic burdens on families and healthcare systems.3 As a complex heterogeneous disease, childhood asthma (CA) arises from intricate interactions between genetic predisposition and environmental triggers, such as allergens, respiratory infections, and air pollution.4,5

  Inhaled corticosteroids serve as the core treatment for asthma. They can effectively reduce the frequency of asthma exacerbations and lower hospitalization rates.6 Nevertheless, long-term administration, especially at high doses, may lead to various adverse reactions, including influences on growth velocity.7,8 Meanwhile, commonly used clinical biomarkers for asthma include exhaled nitric oxide (FeNO), blood eosinophil count, and serum total IgE. However, these biomarkers have limited sensitivity and specificity in childhood asthma (CA), making it difficult to accurately reflect the heterogeneity of airway inflammation. Consequently, they often fail to effectively predict acute exacerbations or guide individualized treatment.9–11 Therefore, identifying novel molecular biomarkers is of great significance for the precise diagnosis and treatment of CA.

  Recently, the role of cellular stress response mechanisms in inflammatory diseases has garnered increasing attention. As a key component, stress granules (SGs) are dynamic, membraneless cytoplasmic condensates formed in eukaryotic cells under various stressors, including oxidative stress, viral infection, and heat shock.12,13 Their formation is primarily driven by liquid-liquid phase separation (LLPS) and involves the collective accumulation of untranslated mRNAs, RNA-binding proteins (RBPs), translation initiation factors, and other components. Functioning as central hubs for mRNA triage and stress signaling, SGs play a pivotal role in regulating gene expression post-transcriptionally to facilitate cellular adaptation.14 Notably, the dynamic balance of SG assembly and disassembly is intimately linked to the pathogenesis of various diseases, including neurodegenerative disorders, cancer, and inflammatory conditions.15,16 Specifically, SGs may influence the response of airway epithelial cells to oxidative stress and viral infection by sequestering or releasing specific inflammation-related mRNAs and signaling proteins.17 In parallel, SGs can modulate the stability of immune-related mRNAs through post-transcriptional regulatory mechanisms, potentially affecting the translation efficiency of Th2-type cytokines and the function of immune cells.18 Therefore, dysfunction of SGs is likely closely associated with airway hyperresponsiveness and remodeling in CA. However, the specific link between SGs and CA—particularly the identity and function of SG-related molecules—remains largely unexplored. Elucidating these biomarkers would provide a critical foundation for discovering new therapeutic targets and personalized treatment strategies for CA.

  In recent years, machine learning methods have been widely applied in the construction of asthma prediction models and the screening of biomarkers. For example, Huang et al accurately predicted the risk of asthma attacks in the US population using a feature importance statistical method.19 Another narrative review systematically summarized various machine learning algorithms for predicting asthma exacerbations and discussed their application prospects.20 These studies indicate that integrating machine learning with bioinformatics strategies can effectively identify key molecular characteristics of asthma, offering unique advantages in handling high-dimensional omics data. However, to date, no study has applied machine learning methods to the integrated analysis of SGs-related genes and CA.

  To bridge this knowledge gap, this study employs an integrated bioinformatics and machine learning approach. We aimed to: (1) identify candidate genes overlapping between CA and SG pathways from public datasets; (2) conduct functional enrichment analyses to decipher their biological roles; (3) construct protein-protein interaction networks; and (4) apply robust machine learning algorithms to filter and identify the most promising SG-related biomarkers for CA. The findings were subsequently validated in independent cohorts and through experimental RT-PCR, offering a systematic investigation into the potential CA-SG axis.


## Materials and Methods

### Data Collection
  During this study, we retrieved datasets relevant to CA from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). Among them, the dataset GSE2701121,22 (platform GPL6244) consisted of 17 CA samples and 18 healthy control samples (Control), and was designated as the training set. The validation set GSE40732 (platform GPL16025)23 (Supplementary Table 1) encompassed 194 blood samples, including 97 CA samples (CA) and 97 healthy control samples (Control). Both datasets were chip data, and we directly downloaded the normalized series matrix files from GEO using the GEOquery package (version 2.68.0),24 with no additional data preprocessing performed. Additionally, the SG-related gene dataset GSE99304 (platform GPL18573)25 (Supplementary Table 2) was obtained, which included three SGcoreRNA and three TotalRNA samples from U-2 OS cells following arsenite-induced stress granule formation.


### Differential Expression Analysis
  In this study, to screen for differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between CA samples and control samples in the training set, the “limma” package (version 3.56.2)26 was used to perform differential expression analysis on the expression matrix of the training set (p-value < 0.05 and |log2FC| > 0.25). For SG-related DEGs, this study used the “DEseq2” package (version 1.40.2)27 to conduct differential expression analysis on SGcoreRNA blood samples and TotalRNA blood samples (ie, SG samples and normal samples) Similarly, with a threshold of p-value < 0.05 and |log2FC| > 0.25, SG-related DEGs were identified.

  To visualize the distribution of the two sets of DEGs, the “ggplot2” package (version 3.5.1)28 was used to draw a volcano plot for visualizing. In addition, to further present the expression levels of these DEGs, the “ComplexHeatmap” package (version 2.16.0)29 was used.


### Identification and Functional Analysis of Candidate Genes
  To accurately screen for candidate genes, this study utilized the VennDiagram package (version 1.7.3)30 to overlap the two sets of DEGs. To comprehensively analyze the functions of the candidate genes and the biological processes they are involved in, this study employed the “clusterProfiler” package (version 4.15.0.3)31 to perform Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses (p < 0.05). The top 10 pathways from the enrichment results (ranked by p values from lowest to highest) were carefully selected for visualization, enabling a more intuitive and in-depth exploration of the biological significance of the candidate genes.


### Construction of the Protein-Protein Interaction (PPI) Network
  To delve deeply into the interaction relationships among the proteins encoded by the candidate genes, this study constructed a PPI network with the aid of the STRING database. The specific procedures are as follows: Import the candidate genes into the STRING database (URL: http://www/string-db.org/),32 set the species as “Homo sapiens”, and configure the interaction score threshold to be greater than 0.4. Based on these conditions, the PPI network was constructed. After the construction was completed, the obtained TSV file was imported into the Cytoscape software to visually display the results of the PPI network.


### Identification and Validation of Key Genes
  To more precisely screen candidate genes related to the disease, this study utilized the cytohubba plugin in Cytoscape software and applied the Maximal Clique Centrality (MCC) algorithm. We selected the top 50 genes from numerous genes and designated them as key candidate genes. Considering the need to screen key genes closely related to CA from gene expression data and further improve the prediction performance of the model, the research team conducted Random Forest (RF), Lasso, and Boruta analyses on these key candidate genes to screen feature genes that are of great significance for CA diagnosis.

  The RF algorithm can construct a classification model. During the model construction process, it can not only distinguish and classify the importance of genes to the model, clarify the key role of individual genes in the classification, but also screen important genes through a scoring and ranking method. This algorithm has excellent adaptability to complex data and significant advantages in handling noisy, non-linear, and other high-dimensional genomic data. In the set of key candidate genes, the researchers performed RF analysis using the “randomForest” package (version 4.7.1.2)33 (seed = 123). We built 500 decision trees (ntree = 500) with default node size. Model stability was monitored via out-of-bag (OOB) error, and the point with minimum OOB error was selected for feature importance evaluation. Feature importance was measured by Mean Decrease Gini, and genes with importance score > 0.2 (empirically determined based on the importance distribution) were retained.

  LASSO, as a regression analysis algorithm, has the functions of variable selection and regularization, which enable it to perform excellently in avoiding model overfitting and improving prediction accuracy. In the analysis of the key candidate gene set, the researchers used the “glmnet” package (version 4.1.8)34 to conduct LASSO regression analysis (seed = 123,456). We performed logistic regression (binomial family) with 10-fold cross validation (cv.glmnet) to estimate the optimal penalty parameter λ. The λ value within one standard error of the minimum (lambda.1se) was chosen to avoid overfitting. Genes with non-zero coefficients at lambda.1se were selected.

  As a global feature selection technique using Random Forest, Boruta assesses feature significance by comparing the importance of genuine features against randomly created shadow features. The researchers used the Boruta package (version 8.0.0)35 to conduct a Boruta analysis on the key candidate gene set (seed = 1234). We ran 500 iterations (maxRuns = 500). Boruta compares the importance of real features against randomly generated shadow features. The TentativeRoughFix function was applied to resolve tentative features, and features confirmed as important (green boxplots) were retained.

  Finally, the researchers used the “Venn Diagram” package (version 1.7.3) to locate the shared gene set across the outputs of the three algorithms mentioned earlier. The genes in the intersection were identified as candidate biomarkers for CA and will be used in subsequent in-depth analyses.


### Validation of Potential Biomarker Expression Levels
  To validate the expression characteristics of candidate biomarkers in CA, this study analyzed the inter-group expression differences of candidate biomarkers using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test based on CA and normal blood samples from the training set GSE27011 and the validation set GSE40732. A strict significance threshold of p < 0.05 was set to screen genes that showed significant differences and consistent expression trends in both datasets. Visual analysis was performed using the ggplot2 package. Box plots of candidate biomarkers were drawn in the training set and the validation set, respectively to visually display their expression distributions in CA samples and normal samples. Finally, only candidate biomarkers that showed significant differences and consistent expression patterns in the two independent datasets were included for subsequent functional analysis and model construction.


### Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) Curve Analysis
  ROC curve is created by setting different binary classification thresholds, with vertical-axis values representing the true positive rate (TPR) and horizontal-axis values indicating the false positive rate (FPR). The area under the curve (AUC) serves as a core indicator for evaluating diagnostic efficacy. The closer the AUC is to 1, the higher the diagnostic accuracy of the biomarker; indicators with an AUC > 0.7 are generally considered to have good clinical diagnostic value. To assess the ability of the above-screened potential biomarkers to distinguish CA blood samples from normal samples, this study performed ROC curve analysis on the target biomarkers using the “pROC” package (version 1.18.5)36 based on the training set GSE27011. By plotting ROC curves and calculating AUC values, we systematically evaluated their efficacy in CA diagnosis.


### Nomogram Construction
  A nomogram, also referred to as an alignment diagram, functions as a visualization method developed from multivariate regression analysis. The tool combines multiple predictive metrics and visualizes scaled linear segments on a single plane, with segment lengths determined by the metrics’ proportional contributions to the model, intuitively presenting the interrelationships among variables within the predictive model. To evaluate the predictive value of all potential biomarkers for CA, this study constructed a nomogram model using the “rms” package (version 6.8.1) (R package version 6.8-0) based on CA blood samples and normal blood samples from the training set GSE27011.

  In the model evaluation phase, a calibration curve was plotted using the “rms” package. The relationship between predicted probability values and true probability values was analyzed, and a curve slope close to 1 was taken as the criterion for high prediction accuracy. The ROC curve was plotted with the “pROC” package, and the area under the curve (AUC) was used to assess the diagnostic predictive value of the nomogram model. Additionally, decision curve analysis (DCA), a common method for evaluating clinical prediction models, was employed. Decision curves were plotted using the “ggDCA” package (version 1.1)37 to comprehensively assess the clinical utility of the nomogram.


### Identify Transcription Factors and microRNAs (miRNAs)
  Through the NetworkAnalyst 3.0 platform, the regulatory networks of transcription factors (TFs) - genes and genes - microRNAs (miRNAs) were constructed by utilizing the JASPAR database and the TarBase database. JASPAR is an open-access, manually curated database of transcription factor binding site (TFBS) patterns, which stores high-quality transcription factor binding patterns in the form of position frequency matrices (PWMs) and is widely used in gene regulation research and bioinformatics analysis. Tarbase is the primary experimentally validated database of miRNA-mRNA interactions. Cytoscape (version 3.8.2)38 was used to visualize these interactions.


### GeneMANIA Analysis and Subcellular Localization
  To further analyze the interaction relationships and potential functional associations between the screened potential biomarkers and their functionally similar genes, in this study, the screened potential biomarkers were imported into the GeneMANIA online database (http://genemania.org/). Meanwhile, the species was precisely set as “Homo sapiens” to construct a high-precision gene interaction network diagram.

  To clarify the specific localization of the potential biomarkers within the cells, based on the screened potential biomarkers, this study conducted predictive analysis of subcellular localization by means of the GeneCard database (https://www.genecards.org/).


### Single-Gene Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) of Potential Biomarkers
  To further understand the biological functions and pathways in which the screened potential biomarkers are involved in the disease, based on all samples in the training set, the “psych” package (version 2.4.6.26) (https://www.researchgate.net › publication) was used to perform Spearman correlation analysis between each potential biomarker and all the remaining genes to obtain the correlation coefficients. The correlation coefficients were then ranked in descending order. Subsequently, using the ranking results, the “clusterProfiler” package was employed for GSEA of pathway enrichment. In this study, “c2.cp.all.v2022.1.Hs.symbols.gmt” was used as the background gene set to conduct GSEA enrichment analysis on the two potential biomarkers, respectively. Pathways with |NES| > 1, p-value < 0.05, and p.adjust < 0.25 were selected as significantly enriched pathways, and the top 5 pathways with the smallest p-values among the selected ones were visualized.


### Immune Infiltration Analysis
  To reveal the molecular association mechanism between immune cells and the occurrence and development of CA, in this study, based on the training set GSE27011, the single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) method was employed to assess the infiltration levels of immune cells in samples from the CA and normal control (Control) groups, and obtain the immune infiltration level scores. The heatmap was drawn with the help of the “pheatmap” package (version 1.0.12)39 to systematically present the differences in the infiltration patterns of different immune cell types in the two groups of samples.

  To accurately identify the differences in immune cell infiltration between CA and normal blood samples, the study adopted the “rstatix” package (version 0.7.2) (https://CRAN.R-project.org/package=rstatix) and screened the differential immune cells with statistical significance (p < 0.05) between the groups through the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Furthermore, the “ggplot2” package was used to draw boxplots to intuitively display the abundance distribution of the differential immune cells in the two groups of samples.

  To explore the synergistic relationships between differential immune cells, as well as between potential biomarkers and differential immune cells, in this study, based on the training set GSE40732, the Spearman correlation analysis in the “psych” package (version 2.4.6.26) (https://www.researchgate.net › publication) was used to systematically analyze the correlations among the above factors in the CA and normal samples. Finally, the correlations were visually presented in the form of a correlation heatmap.


### Candidate Drug Evaluation
  In order to explore potential drugs for childhood asthma (CA), drug compound prediction was carried out using the Drug Signatures Database (DsigDB, http://dsigdb.tanlab.org/) based on the obtained biomarkers. And Cytoscape (version 3.8.2) was used to visualize the interactions between drug compounds and biomarkers.

  The top 10 compounds in terms of both the p-value and the adjusted p-value were selected. The Enrichers platform was utilized to explore associations between drug molecules and biomarker genes, with data derived from the Drug Signatures Database.


### Case Data and Blood Samples
  To primary explore whether the differences in potential biomarkers between CA blood samples and normal blood samples align with bioinformatics analysis results, we collected fresh blood samples from Shanghai Children’s Hospital (Shanghai Jiao Tong University, Shanghai, China) in october 2025. These samples were drawn from six CA exacerbation patients and six individuals undergoing routine health check-ups (serving as normal controls), aged 5–14 years. Each group comprised three boys and three girls, with no statistically significant differences observed between the groups. All blood samples were anticoagulated with heparin, stored at 4°C, and subsequently processed for RNA extraction. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki (as revised in 2013). The study was approved by the ethics committee of Shanghai Children’s Hospital (Ethical Approval Number: 2025R155-E01), and informed consent was obtained from both the parents or legal guardians of all minor participants and the minor participants themselves.

  RT-qPCR was used to verify the mRNA expression levels: The primers were designed by Shanghai Sangong Biotech. Total RNA was extracted from the blood using RNAeasy™ Blood RNA Extraction Kit (Chinese, Beyotime, R0091S). Using the cDNA Synthesis Kit (Japan, Takara, RR047A) to convert mRNA into cDNA according to the standard protocol. The obtained cDNA was analyzed using the SYBR Select Master Mix (American, Thermo Fisher Scientific, location) to detect the expression levels of target genes by RT-qPCR. The expression levels of potential biomarkers between CA blood and control blood were calculated using 2−ΔΔCt, and the differences between the two groups were compared using the t-test. The process was repeated 3 times. ### Statistical Analysis
  The data were analyzed using SPSS19.0, and all group data are presented as the mean ± SEM. The statistical analyses were conducted using Student’s t-test or a non-parametric test. Data conforming to a normal distribution with homogeneous variances were analyzed using Student’s t-test; otherwise, non-parametric tests (eg, Wilcoxon rank-sum test) were applied Statistical significance was set at P < 005.

### Screen Core Disease-Related Genes
  A set of 196 candidate genes was input into the STRING database (http://www.string-db.org/) to generate a protein-protein interaction (PPI) network, aiming to characterize the functional relationships among proteins encoded by these genes. The resulting PPI network, depicted in (Supplementary Figure 1), comprised 136 nodes and 196 edges, visually illustrating the intricate functional associations between proteins.

  To further screen core disease-related genes, the Maximal Clique Centrality (MCC) algorithm within the cytohubba plugin of Cytoscape software was employed to select the top 50 genes from the PPI network as key candidate genes. Subsequently, three analytical methods—RF (Random Forest), Lasso, and Boruta—were integrated to screen characteristic genes associated with CA diagnosis, aiming to identify core molecular markers.

  Using the key candidate gene set, random forest analysis was performed with the “randomForest” package Optimal parameters were determined by minimizing model error (Figure 3A), and genes were filtered using an Importance score threshold of > 0.2, yielding 31 characteristic genes (Gene Set 1, Figure 3B), including TAF15, TAS2R20, and NOTCH2. Meanwhile, Lasso algorithm was applied for regularization-based screening of key genes, with parameters optimized via 10-fold cross-validation, resulting in 16 characteristic genes (Gene Set 2, Figure 3C and D). Further importance assessment of these 16 key genes via Boruta analysis (Figure 3E) revealed: blue boxplots representing the importance distribution of shadow features, red boxplots indicating excluded non-critical features, and green boxplots marking features confirmed as important through permutation tests (ie, their importance scores were significantly higher than shadow features), ultimately identifying 22 characteristic genes (Gene Set 3, green section). ### HNRNPA2B1, RPE, and TAF15 Were Identified as Potential Biomarkers

## Discussion
  CA is a prevalent pediatric disease characterized by chronic airway inflammation, hyperresponsiveness and remodeling.4 Its pathogenesis involves immune dysregulation (such as Th2-type cytokine-dominant inflammatory responses), epithelial cell damage, and abnormal neural regulation.40 Emerging evidence indicates that environmental stressors (eg, allergens and oxidative stress) exacerbate airway inflammation and accelerate asthma pathogenesis by activating intracellular stress-response pathways Specifically, SGs—membraneless organelles assembled via LLPS—are induced by stressors such as oxidative stress and viral infection. SGs dynamically assemble via LLPS of mRNA, RNA-binding proteins, and translation initiation factors. By regulating mRNA translation, modulating RNA metabolism, and reprogramming signal transduction pathways, SGs enable cellular adaptation to environmental stressors.41 This study systematically investigates the role of SG-related potential biomarkers in childhood asthma through integrated bioinformatics and experimental validation, aiming to identify novel therapeutic targets. We identified three promising potential SG-associated biomarkers (HNRNPA2B1, RPE, and TAF15) for CA.

  This study systematically investigated the expression characteristics of SGs-related genes in CA by integrating bioinformatics analysis with preliminary RT-qPCR validation. We identified three SGs-related genes—HNRNPA2B1, RPE, and TAF15—that exhibited significantly elevated expression levels in the blood of CA patients. It should be noted that these findings are exploratory, based on a limited sample size derived from public datasets. Therefore, at this stage, these genes can only be considered potential association markers rather than clinically validated diagnostic indicators. The AUC value obtained from ROC analysis (>0.7) merely reflects the discriminatory performance within the current dataset. The true clinical diagnostic value of these genes will require further validation in prospective, large-scale, multi-center cohort studies.

  Our data indicate that HNRNPA2B1 is upregulated in children with asthma. Previous studies have shown that the protein encoded by this gene is a core component of SGs and is involved in RNA processing and metabolism. During lymphocyte differentiation, HNRNPA2B1 regulates RNA splicing and stability, thereby influencing the expression of key immune regulators such as GATA3 and IL-4/5/13.42 Based on these observations, we speculate that abnormal expression of HNRNPA2B1 may contribute to persistent inflammation in airway tissues. Moreover, under conditions of oxidative stress or viral infection, HNRNPA2B1 translocates from the nucleus to the cytoplasm to promote SG assembly;43,44 this process may enhance the mRNA stability and splicing efficiency of Th2 cytokines.45 However, these mechanistic insights are primarily derived from studies in other cell types or disease contexts, and their precise roles in childhood asthma (CA) await direct experimental validation.

  Unlike HNRNPA2B1 and TAF15, RPE, whose full name is ribulose-phosphate 3-epimerase, is a metabolic enzyme in the pentose phosphate pathway that primarily functions to supply NADPH, thereby protecting cells from oxidative stress damage.46,47 This study is the first to report the upregulation of RPE in the blood of children with asthma (CA). Currently, no direct evidence links RPE to asthma pathogenesis. However, indirect evidence suggests that co-expression of RPE and SOL3 may promote IFN-γ production,48 and IFN-γ plays a complex role in immune regulation in asthma. Furthermore, RPE may be involved in antibacterial and antifungal responses,49,50 and such infections are common triggers of asthma exacerbations. Based on these clues, we cautiously speculate that the upregulation of RPE may represent a compensatory response to asthma-related oxidative stress or infection. It should be emphasized that this hypothesis remains highly preliminary, and the specific role of RPE in CA requires direct validation using cell or animal models.

  TAF15 belongs to both the TBP-related factor family and the hnRNP family. Literature review indicates that the chromosomal region 17q21.31, where TAF15 is located, is an important susceptibility locus for CA. Genes within this region, such as ORMDL3 and GSDMB, have been confirmed to be associated with asthma pathogenesis.51,52 TAF15 is genomically adjacent to this region; therefore, its expression changes may be in linkage disequilibrium with known asthma risk variants. A recent study on hypoxia-related genes, based on the same GEO datasets (GSE27011 and GSE40732), also identified TAF15 as one of 11 model genes, further supporting its potential diagnostic value in CA.53 Although that study focused on hypoxia-related mechanisms, which differs from the stress granule perspective of the present work, both independent investigations identified TAF15 as a key gene. Moreover, similar to HNRNPA2B1, TAF15 is involved in RNA splicing and metabolism, and may participate in asthmatic airway inflammation by regulating the alternative splicing of immune-related genes.54,55 Despite these genetic and functional correlations suggesting that TAF15 warrants further study, its direct role in the pathogenesis of CA cannot be determined at present.

  Our GSEA results showed that all three potential biomarkers were enriched in RNA metabolic pathways, such as “processing of capped intron-containing pre-mRNA”. This finding is consistent with previous evidence that abnormal RNA splicing is involved in the pathogenesis of asthma,56–60 suggesting that these three genes may contribute to the asthmatic process by modulating RNA metabolism. However, GSEA is based on correlations in gene expression profiles and cannot be interpreted as evidence of functional causality.

  Using ssGSEA analysis, this study revealed that the scores of CD56dim NK cells, central memory CD8⁺ T cells, immature B cells, and monocytes in CA samples were significantly different from those in control samples. These findings are consistent with previously reported immune profile alterations in asthma. For instance, some studies have shown that the frequency of CD56dim NK cells is an independent predictor of CA severity,61 and this subset can exert direct cytotoxic effects by recognizing stress molecules on the surface of damaged airway epithelial cells.62 The changes in CD56dim NK cells observed in the asthma group in this study may reflect the host immune response to recurrent airway injury. More interestingly, correlation analysis indicated that central memory CD8⁺ T cells were negatively correlated with the expression levels of HNRNPA2B1 and TAF15. We speculate that as RNA-binding proteins, HNRNPA2B1 and TAF15 may influence the differentiation or maintenance of CD8⁺ T cells toward the central memory phenotype by regulating the alternative splicing of key transcription factors (eg, TCF1, Eomes) or chemokine receptors (eg, CCR7) Although these correlation analyses cannot establish causality, they provide potential entry points for future investigations into the regulatory axis between “stress granule-related RNA-binding proteins and specific immune cell subsets”.

  Through Drug-compound targeting prediction, we found that quercetin could potentially interact with HNRNPA2B1, RPE, and TAF15 simultaneously, whereas compounds such as valproic acid could target two of these genes. It is worth noting that these results are entirely based on computational predictions and can only serve as initial clues for subsequent drug screening studies. Quercetin is a natural flavonoid with antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and immunomodulatory activities. Previous studies have explored its protective effects in animal models of asthma, such as reducing airway hyperresponsiveness and mucus secretion.63 Our prediction suggests that quercetin may partially affect the assembly kinetics of stress granules by upregulating or downregulating the expression of RNA-binding proteins such as HNRNPA2B1. This represents an unexplored mechanism that warrants verification in asthma cell models. Valproic acid is a histone deacetylase inhibitor that primarily affects epigenetic regulation.64 Its predicted association with HNRNPA2B1 and TAF15 suggests a regulatory hierarchy involving “epigenetic modification-RNA-binding protein expression”. However, whether valproic acid reduces airway inflammation through this pathway in childhood asthma has not yet been reported.

  This study is the first to identify HNRNPA2B1, RPE, and TAF15 as potential SG-related biomarkers in CA, thereby providing novel molecular clues for investigating the mechanisms of CA. Nevertheless, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the sample sizes of both the training set (GSE27011) and the RT-qPCR validation cohort were small, which may affect statistical power and the generalizability of the findings. Additionally, although the training and validation sets were derived from different microarray platforms and were not combined for analysis, technical heterogeneity between platforms may still have some impact on the consistency of the results. Second, while the nomogram model demonstrated good calibration and discriminatory ability, its clinical applicability requires further evaluation in prospective cohorts. Moreover, as an exploratory bioinformatics study, the preliminary findings need to be systematically validated using larger, prospective cohorts that encompass diverse asthma phenotypes and severity strata. Future research should prioritize multicenter, large-scale clinical cohort studies that include different age groups, allergic statuses, and treatment responses across CA subtypes, in order to comprehensively evaluate the diagnostic and prognostic value of the above-mentioned potential markers. Concurrently, we will combine cellular and animal models to deeply investigate the specific molecular mechanisms by which these potential markers regulate SG dynamics and the immune microenvironment, thereby providing more solid experimental evidence for the precise diagnosis and treatment of CA.


## Conclusion
  In summary, by integrating bioinformatics and machine learning approaches, this study preliminarily identified three SG-related potential biomarkers for CA: HNRNPA2B1, RPE, and TAF15. These candidates exhibited relatively consistent expression trends across the training and validation sets and demonstrated moderate diagnostic discriminatory ability (AUC > 0.7). RT-qPCR further confirmed that their expression levels in the blood of CA patients were significantly higher than those in healthy controls. However, given that this study was primarily based on transcriptomic data and bioinformatics analyses, and the RT-qPCR validation involved a limited sample size, definitive conclusions regarding causality or clinical application cannot be drawn. Therefore, the above findings should be considered hypothesis-generating, and the identified genes should be regarded as potential SG-related markers rather than validated clinical biomarkers.
