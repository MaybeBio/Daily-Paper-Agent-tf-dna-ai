Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is a chronic intestinal disorder that has become a global public health concern. Postbiotics offer advantages in biosafety and reproducibility over probiotics. Here, we demonstrated that live Allobaculum stercoricanis (A. stercoricanis) and its two postbiotic preparations horizontal line inactivated bacterial cells (POST) and cell-free fermentation supernatant (CFS) horizontal line effectively alleviated dextran sulfate sodium (DSS)-induced colitis in mice. Integrated gut microbiome and serum metabolomics analyses indicated that these interventions may act through modulating bile acid metabolism and Toll-like receptor signaling pathways. Further investigations combining postbiotic metabolomics, network pharmacology, molecular docking, and molecular dynamics simulations identified alpha-muricholic acid (alpha-MCA) as a candidate functional metabolite, with the farnesoid X receptor (FXR) as a potential target. We hypothesize that alpha-MCA may contribute to ameliorating intestinal inflammation through engagement of FXR and suppression of the TLR4/NF-kappaB signaling pathway. These findings provide new insights into postbiotic-based therapeutic strategies for IBD.
