# Interplay of diffusive and active motion governs cohesin extrusion and CTCF barrier function


## Significance statement
  Cohesin folds chromosomes into loops that help regulate gene expression, yet its diverse behaviors and sensitivity to mechanical forces remain largely unexplained. Here, we develop a theory for how competition between active DNA capture and passive sliding governs loop growth, shrinkage, and motion. This framework reconciles seemingly conflicting experimental observations, and predicts how weak forces generated by cohesin on naked DNA in vitro translate into tensions strong enough to drive large-scale genome folding on native chromatin. Jointly considering cohesin mechanics and biochemistry further enables us to explain how the DNA-binding protein CTCF modulates extrusion activity. By connecting single-molecule measurements with chromosome organization mechanisms, we thus provide a quantitative foundation for how physical conditions and regulatory proteins collectively shape genome folding.
