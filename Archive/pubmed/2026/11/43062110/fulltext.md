Alcoholic liver disease (ALD) is a major global health problem with few effective therapies. Resveratrol (RES) has demonstrated hepatoprotective effects, but its molecular mechanisms remain incompletely understood. We hypothesized that RES protects against ethanol-induced liver injury by activating the AMPKalpha/Nrf2 signaling pathway and upregulating downstream antioxidant enzymes. To test this hypothesis, a comprehensive approach was used, including in vivo and in vitro experiments, molecular docking, and molecular dynamics simulations. Using C57BL/6 mice and their primary hepatocytes, the therapeutic efficacy of RES in acute alcoholic liver injury. The results showed that RES markedly reduced liver histopathological damage and lessened ethanol-induced hepatocyte injury. These effects may be attributable to its antioxidant activity. RES increased AMPKalpha phosphorylation, as indicated by a higher p-AMPKalpha/AMPKalpha ratio, and promoted Nrf2 nuclear translocation. Concurrently, RES upregulated mRNA and protein levels of heme oxygenase 1 (HO-1) and NADPH quinone oxidoreductase 1 (NQO-1). In primary hepatocytes from Nrf2-/- mice co-cultured with ethanol, HO-1 and NQO-1 levels remained largely unchanged, indicating a critical role for Nrf2 in RES action. Molecular docking studies suggested that RES binds effectively to AMPKalpha and kelch-like ECH-associated protein 1 (Keap1), with docking scores below -5 kcal/mol. Molecular dynamics simulations showed that the RES-Keap1 complex was highly stable, yielding a calculated binding free energy (Deltatotal) of -21.26 +/- 4.41 kcal/mol by MMPBSA analysis. In conclusion, RES improved hepatic function and reduced damage in ALD, potentially by modulating AMPKalpha, Nrf2, HO-1, and NQO1, and thus warrants further investigation for therapeutic application.
