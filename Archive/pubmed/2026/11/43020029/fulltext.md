Astrocytes shape synapses and circuits, yet their diversity within the human basal ganglia remains poorly defined. Integrating single-nucleus transcriptomic and epigenomic profiling with spatial transcriptomics, we identify three anatomically organized astrocyte subgroups-striatal gray matter (GM), extra-striatal GM, and white matter (WM)-distinguished by neurotransmitter transporter expression and synapse-associated programs that reflect afferent input. These molecular distinctions generalize brain-wide, separating telencephalic from non-telencephalic astrocytes, with LHX2 marking telencephalic astrocyte identity across species. Within the striatum (STR), dorsal and ventral populations align with distinct microcircuits; a ventral population maps to the nucleus accumbens mediodorsal shell alongside specialized neuronal subtypes, a pattern conserved in nonhuman primates. A deep learning sequence model identifies subgroup-specific enhancer codes and recovers established transcription factor motif logic from published enhancer-AAV datasets, providing a foundation for targeted viral vector design. These findings define intersecting axes of human astrocyte specialization and offer a framework for cell-type-specific dissection of basal ganglia circuits.
