# MAMTF: a method for predicting transcription factor binding sites combining state-space modeling and adaptive feature fusion


## Background
  Transcription factor binding sites (TFBSs) are short sequences on DNA that can be recognized and bound by specific transcription factors (TFs). Accurate identification of TFBSs is crucial for decoding gene regulatory mechanisms and understanding cellular functions. Traditional machine learning-based methods rely on manually designed features, making it difficult to fully uncover the complex patterns within DNA sequences. In recent years, deep learning methods have demonstrated significant advantages in TFBSs prediction. However, existing methods still have shortcomings in terms of local and global feature extraction as well as feature fusion.


## Results
  To address these issues, we propose a deep learning method, MAMTF, designed to integrate local and global DNA features to improve the performance of TFBSs prediction. This method uses a multiscale convolutional neural network to extract local sequence features, employs a bidirectional Mamba architecture to capture long-range dependencies, and designs a cross-gated fusion module to achieve deep interaction between different features. On 165 ChIP-seq datasets encompassing 29 transcription factor (TF) categories, MAMTF achieves a macro-averaged ACC (0.819), ROC-AUC (0.895) and PR-AUC (0.900) over the 29 TF categories, showing promising prediction ability.


## Conclusions
  Experimental results show that MAMTF outperforms other six methods in predicting TFBSs, exhibiting optimal and stable performance on 29 TF categories, providing a new tool for accurate TFBSs prediction.


## Background
  Transcription factors (TFs) are a class of proteins that can bind specific DNA sequences and modulate gene expression via transcriptional regulation [1]. TFBSs are short sequences on DNA that can be recognized and bound by a specific transcription factor; they are normally 5–20 base pairs in length and predominantly located in regulatory regions such as gene promoters and enhancers [2–4]. TFBSs interact with the DNA-binding domains of TFs through base sequence specificity, forming protein-DNA complexes that enable TFs to activate or repress the expression of target genes [5, 6]. Therefore, accurate identification of TFBSs is a critical foundation for decoding gene regulatory networks and understanding the mechanisms of cellular function regulation [7]. In precision medicine and drug development, accurate prediction of TFBSs facilitates the discovery of candidate regulatory targets and improves diagnostic and therapeutic strategies for diseases [8, 9]. Consequently, the development of efficient and accurate TFBSs identification methods is of great theoretical value and broad application prospects.

  With the rapid development of high-throughput sequencing technology and the continuous accumulation of large-scale genomic data, researches on TFBSs have become increasingly in-depth [10]. Among relevant technologies, chromatin immunoprecipitation sequencing (ChIP-seq) employs specific antibodies to enrich DNA fragments bound to target TFs [11]. These enriched fragments are then subjected to high-throughput sequencing to locate potential binding sites across the whole genome [12]. Although ChIP-seq offers high reliability in TFBSs identification, it still has certain limitations. This method relies on high-quality and specific antibodies, requires strict experimental conditions, and is costly and time-consuming. Additionally, ChIP-seq typically provides only broad binding regions, making it difficult to precisely locate specific binding sites [13, 14]. Therefore, it is necessary to develop more rational and efficient methods for TFBSs prediction.

  Computational methods, which do not rely on complex experimental conditions, have been widely applied to TFBSs identification tasks [15]. Early methods were primarily based on traditional machine learning models, such as Hidden Markov Models (HMM) [16, 17], Support Vector Machines (SVM) [18–20], and Random Forests [21–23]. These methods improved prediction performance to some extent and achieved good results in related tasks. However, such methods typically rely on manually designed features or prior sequence biases, making it difficult to automatically extract deep features from raw DNA sequences. Additionally, their ability to improve performance and utilize data is somewhat limited when faced with ever-growing large-scale datasets.

  Since deep learning was introduced into bioinformatics, such algorithms have gradually exhibited superior performance for TFBSs identification. Deep learning methods can automatically capture multi-level features from raw sequences and effectively integrate local and global information [24], significantly improving the accuracy of TFBSs prediction compared to traditional methods [25]. For instance, convolutional neural network (CNN)-based models have been extensively adopted in this prediction task [26]. DeepBind utilizes CNNs to automatically extract sequence features from large-scale experimental data, enabling efficient identification of binding sites [27]. DSAC, combines CNNs with self-attention mechanisms to extract local motif features and global dependencies [28], thereby enhancing feature expressiveness and predictive performance. Furthermore, DeepSTF introduces DNA shape features in addition to sequence information and integrates structures such as CNNs, transformer encoders, and LSTMs to achieve multimodal feature fusion and more precise binding site identification [29]. Wei et al. developed a multi-modal representation learning framework based on cross-attention networks that integrates DNA sequence, structure, and shape information, achieving improved prediction accuracy through cross-modal feature interaction [30]. Beyond DNA-protein binding, SSCRB proposes a lightweight sequence and structural feature-based attention model, effectively capturing multi-scale circRNA features through attention mechanisms [31]. However, existing deep learning methods still have certain limitations. Existing methods often struggle to balance the modeling of local features and global dependencies, resulting in insufficient extraction of sequence information. In addition, the methods for feature fusion are relatively simple, making it difficult to effectively uncover deep interactions between features.

  Based on the research background described above, we propose MAMTF, a deep learning architecture that integrates multi-scale convolutional neural networks, spatial-channel attention mechanisms, bidirectional Mamba sequence modeling, and a cross-gated fusion module, successfully achieving accurate prediction of TFBSs. To verify the efficacy of the proposed model, we adopt the benchmark datasets released by Zhang et al. [32], and performed comparative analyses with various mainstream methods. The experimental results demonstrate that MAMTF outperforms six comparison methods across multiple evaluation metrics, thereby validating the effectiveness of the model design.


## Methods
  Figure 1 shows the overall architecture and workflow of MAMTF model. MAMTF includes three core modules as detailed below: (1) Data processing: Raw input DNA sequences are preprocessed and encoded to transform nucleotide strings into numerical matrices compatible with deep learning input, preparing data for downstream feature extraction. (2) Feature Extraction: Multi-scale convolutions and attention-enhancement mechanisms are employed to extract features from the encoded sequences, uncovering potential sequence patterns across different scales. A dual-branch sequence modeling structure is constructed to model features at both local and global levels, enhancing the expressive power of information in key regions and obtaining discriminative feature representations. (3) Feature Fusion and Prediction: Different features are adaptively fused through a cross-gated fusion module to achieve complementary information representation. The fused features are imported into the prediction head, followed by global pooling and fully connected layers to output prediction probabilities for TFBSs identification.

### Data preprocessing
  MAMTF takes 101-base-pair DNA sequences as input. Each base is encoded as a 4-dimensional one-hot vector, forming an input matrix X of shape 101 × 4. Since the standard one-hot encoding only reflects the discrete categorical information of the bases and is unable to express potential correlations between them. Therefore, we employ a lightweight linear embedding layer to perform feature mapping within the 4-dimensional space and implement initial feature transformation. This embedding layer reorganizes input features through a learnable linear transformation. It keeps the feature dimension unchanged and enables the model to capture implicit nucleotide relationships, thereby providing a more expressive input representation for subsequent feature extraction. Finally, a Reshape operation is applied to adjust the feature format. After these steps, the original DNA sequence is transformed into a three-dimensional feature tensor Xe of shape 1 × 4 × 101, which serves as the input to the feature extraction module.

### Fusion and classification
  TF-DNA binding is jointly determined by local sequence motifs and global regulatory context, but their relative importance varies across different binding sites. A strong core motif may dominate the binding decision, whereas a weak motif may require additional support from distal contextual signals. Therefore, the fusion mechanism must dynamically adjust the contribution of local and global features on a per-sample basis.

  Existing methods generally employ simple feature fusion strategies, such as concatenation or element-wise addition. are insufficient because they fuse features in a static manner: concatenation merely places two feature sets side-by-side without interaction, and addition forces equal contribution regardless of the actual binding context. To address this issue, we design a feature fusion module based on a cross-gated mechanism. The architecture of the cross-gating fusion module is illustrated in Fig. 3.

## Results
  To validate the effectiveness of the MAMTF method, we comprehensively evaluated its predictive performance using three metrics: ACC, ROC-AUC, and PR-AUC. The MAMTF method formulates TFBSs prediction as a binary classification problem. ACC is the most fundamental evaluation metric, used to measure the degree of agreement between the model’s predictions and the true labels. It represents the proportion of correctly classified samples out of the total number of samples. A higher ACC value indicates that the model has better overall classification performance. ROC-AUC is used to evaluate the model’s overall discriminative ability at different classification thresholds. Its value ranges from 0 to 1. The closer the value is to 1, the stronger the model’s ability to distinguish between positive and negative samples. Additionally, considering the potential class imbalance in the TFBSs datasets, we use PR-AUC as a supplementary evaluation metric. A higher PR-AUC indicates that the model performs better in identifying positive samples, maintaining high predictive accuracy while ensuring high recall. In summary, by analyzing the values of these three evaluation metrics, we can comprehensively assess the model’s performance in the TFBSs prediction task.


### Data availability and model implementation
  Build training, validation, and test setsWe evaluated the model on the same 165 ChIP-seq datasets used in the study by Zhang et al., which cover 29 transcription factors from different cell lines. Positive samples were generated by extracting 101 bp sequences from the human reference genome (hg19) centered on peak coordinates from the raw ChIP-seq BED files, with sequences marked as unknown (‘N’) removed. Negative samples were created by applying the uShuffle algorithm to the positive sequences with double-preservation shuffling. uShuffle maintains the nucleotide composition and local k-mer statistics of positive sequences while randomizing their positional arrangement, thereby reducing artificial biases caused by differences in sequence composition between positive and negative samples. Finally, the dataset was split into a training set and an independent test set in an 8:2 ratio. For each training set, split it into a training subset and a validation subset in an 8:2 ratio. Although they can capture motif information to some extent, their ability to model long-range dependencies is limited. CRPTS [36] and DLBSS [37] improve prediction performance by incorporating DNA shape information, but their feature extraction and fusion strategies are relatively simple, and they still fail to fully capture the core biological signals within the sequences. While DSAC optimizes the model structure by adopting a dual-branch architecture combining CNN and self-attention mechanisms, it still faces certain limitations in modeling global dependencies within sequences and in its feature fusion strategy, failing to achieve deep interaction between local and global information. BCDB employs a bidirectional convolutional deep learning framework to capture discriminative sequence patterns for TFBS prediction [38], but it has a insufficiency in comprehensive sequence feature characterization.

  In summary, MAMTF effectively models long-range dependencies and local motif features in DNA sequences and achieves deep feature fusion through a cross-gated fusion mechanism. Experimental results on 165 ChIP-seq datasets also confirm its advantages over existing methods.

### Hyperparameter experiments
  To determine the optimal training configuration for the MAMTF model, we conducted hyperparameter experiments on the validation set across the 165 datasets, focusing on the auxiliary loss weight, the number of training epochs, and the initial learning rate. Figure 4 shows the results of the hyperparameter experiments.

  Fig. 4Hyperparameter experiment results of MAMTF on 165 ChIP-seq datasets. The blue, red, and green curves represent the ACC, ROC-AUC, and PR-AUC values

  The auxiliary loss weight controls the extent to which the auxiliary loss contributes to the total loss in multi-supervised learning. We tested three settings for the auxiliary loss weight: 0.01, 0.1, and 0.5. The experimental results on the validation set show that the model performs best when the auxiliary loss weight is set to 0.1. To determine the optimal number of training rounds, we tested three settings: 10, 15, and 30 rounds. The results on the validation set show that the model did not reach optimal performance after 10 training rounds, fully converged without overfitting after 15 rounds, and exhibited a slight decline in validation set metrics after 30 rounds, indicating the onset of overfitting. Finally, we tested three learning rates: 1e-4, 1e-3, and 1e-2. The results on the validation set showed that the model converged stably and achieved optimal performance at a learning rate of 1e-3.

  The optimal configuration identified through hyperparameter tuning on the validation set was then applied to retrain the model on the combined training and validation data, and the final performance was evaluated on the held-out test set. In summary, the default training configuration for MAMTF is: auxiliary loss weight of 0.1, 15 training epochs, and an initial learning rate of 1e-3. Under this configuration, the model achieved the best performance on the 165 ChIP-seq datasets. The optimal configuration identified through hyperparameter tuning was applied to all experiments.

## Discussion
  Although MAMTF has demonstrated good predictive performance, there are still several areas worthy of further research. Currently, the model uses only DNA sequences as input features. In the future, it may be worthwhile to incorporate multimodal biological information, such as DNA conformation features and chromatin accessibility, to further enhance the model’s ability to characterize TF binding mechanisms. Additionally, the existing model relies solely on one-dimensional linear sequence information during the feature extraction stage and has not yet fully utilized higher-order sequence features or positional encoding information in DNA. Future research could explore more refined sequence feature encoding strategies to enhance the model’s ability to extract deep biological information from raw sequences. Furthermore, future studies could evaluate MAMTF using more stringent benchmark strategies, such as genomic background negative sampling and chromosome-level partitioning, to further assess its generalization ability in real genomic scenarios.


## Conclusion
  This study proposes MAMTF, a deep learning method for TFBSs prediction. Addressing the limitations of existing deep learning methods, MAMTF comprehensively captures binding motifs of varying lengths through multi-scale convolutions. The spatial-channel attention module adaptively refines feature channels and sequence positions, while the bidirectional Mamba module models global sequence context in both directions with linear time complexity. Finally, the cross-gated fusion module deeply integrates local CNN features with global Mamba features. Experiments on 165 ENCODE ChIP-seq datasets demonstrate that MAMTF outperforms six other deep learning methods across three metrics: ACC, ROC-AUC, and PR-AUC. This validates the effectiveness of MAMTF’s core modules and the soundness of its overall architectural design.
