Lithium carbonate (Li(2)CO(3)) is a first-line pharmacotherapy for bipolar disorder (BPD) in female patients of childbearing age, although it is associated with embryonic teratogenicity. Lithium exposure in batteries and pharmaceuticals has been a toxicity factor for women's reproductive health in energy, environment, and fitness fields. Neural tube defects (NTDs) are the severest reproductive outcome of maternal Li(2)CO(3) exposure. Currently, there is a lack of effective prevention for Li(2)CO(3) neurotoxicity. Our previous study revealed that lithium-induced murine NTDs correlated with enhanced proliferation and impaired inositol biosynthesis. In this study, 400 mg/kg inositol was used to treat the lithium-induced murine NTDs after calculating the toxicity using ADMETlab 3.0 and Deep-PK. We found that inositol supplementation ameliorated fetal neural phenotypes, crown-rump length, and body weight and rescued aberrant cell proliferation and dysregulated the GSK3beta/beta-catenin/CyclinD1 pathway in both animal and cellular models. The affinity of GSK3beta-inositol interaction was analyzed and validated using molecular dynamics and surface plasmon resonance. GSK3beta-edited cell models further revealed that inositol altered GSK3beta post-transcriptional status and phosphorylation, consequently modulating the GSK3beta/beta-catenin/CyclinD1 signaling cascade. Overall, our study clarified that inositol alleviates lithium-induced NTDs via the GSK3beta/beta-catenin/CyclinD1 pathway, revealed the direct molecular interaction between inositol and GSK3beta, and highlighted the potential of inositol as a therapeutic prevention against lithium-induced embryonic neurotoxicity for childbearing female BPD patients.
