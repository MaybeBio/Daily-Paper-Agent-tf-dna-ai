BACKGROUND: The aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR) regulates neurodevelopment, immune responses, and cellular homeostasis. Despite increased availability of endogenous ligands during aging and neurodegeneration, AhR transcriptional activity may decline through mechanisms that remain unclear. We investigated AhR dysfunction and its association with the cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor p27 in senescent human astrocytes. METHODS AND RESULTS: SVG p12 human astrocytes were exposed to hydrogen peroxide (50 or 100 microM, 30 min) to induce senescence-associated features and evaluated over 72 h. The AhR ligand indole-3-propionic acid (IPA; 200 microM) was administered after oxidative stress. Cellular assays included at least three independent experiments. AhR localization and signaling, p27 association, and senescence-associated phenotypes were evaluated using microscopy, gene expression analysis, protein assays, and molecular docking. Senescence was associated with cytoplasmic redistribution of AhR and reduced expression of its target gene, cytochrome P450 1A1 (CYP1A1). Increased cytoplasmic p27 accumulation coincided with greater AhR colocalization, and co-immunoprecipitation supported their association. Molecular docking suggested that acidic conditions could stabilize the AhR-p27 complex. IPA increased nuclear AhR localization, CYP1A1 expression, and proliferation while reducing senescence-associated beta-galactosidase activity. CONCLUSIONS: These findings suggest that p27-associated cytoplasmic retention may impair AhR signaling during astrocyte senescence despite ligand availability. IPA treatment after oxidative stress attenuated the development of senescence-associated features. Modulating AhR localization and its association with p27 may offer a therapeutic avenue for age-related neurological disorders, although causality and therapeutic relevance require further investigation.
