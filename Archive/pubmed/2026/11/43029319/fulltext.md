We characterize a virulent COPD isolate, P. aeruginosa RS85, as a potential inducer of NET, and demonstrate the ameliorating impacts of isoliquiritigenin (ISL) on bacterial quorum sensing (QS) and neutrophil responses. Low doses of ISL exhibited anti-quorum sensing (AQS) activities on P. aeruginosa RS85 and PAO1, while its high dose was found to be bactericidal. Significantly high amounts of ROS and NETosis were observed in neutrophils when treated with the live cells and secretome of clinical P. aeruginosa, whereas P. aeruginosa PAO1-JP2 elicited markedly reduced responses. RS85 secretome promoted pulmonary NET formation, recruitment of inflammatory cells, alveolar destruction, and emphysema-like pathology in mouse lungs in vivo. The introduction of ISL inhibited cell- and secretome-mediated ROS and NETosis to the levels observed in PAO1-JP2. ISL treatment constrained NETosis, the co-localization of myeloperoxidase and citrullinated histone H3, lung inflammatory damage and structural damage in vivo. The molecular docking of ISL with bacterial QS (LasR and RhlR) and neutrophil NADPH oxidase assembly (p47phox and NOX2) proteins showed strong ligand binding, substantiating interference in QS signaling and ROS generation pathways. This study identifies potential QS-regulated secretory virulence in clinical P. aeruginosa driving NETosis, and demonstrates the ISL-mediated amelioration of NETs and bacterial QS.
