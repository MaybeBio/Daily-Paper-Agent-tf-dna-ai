Ionizing radiation (IR) exposure in deep space environments has emerged as a serious health concern, as it induces persistent oxidative stress and chronic inflammation, ultimately reducing lifespan. Dietary intervention with natural bioactive compounds presents a promising radioprotective strategy. In this study, we demonstrate that ellagic acid (EA) effectively restores lifespan and enhances stress resistance in Caenorhabditis elegans after extremely high-dose IR exposure. Specifically, EA mitigates reactive oxygen species (ROS) accumulation and reduces oxidative damage levels in C. elegans exposed to high-dose IR, through upregulating antioxidant and innate immune gene expression, and enhancing antioxidant enzyme activity. Molecular docking analyses suggested that EA can probably interact with the transcription factors DAF-16 and SKN-1. Through GFP reporter and loss-of-function experiments, we demonstrated that EA promotes the nuclear accumulation of DAF-16 and SKN-1, thereby triggering downstream antioxidant and immune pathways that collectively enhance stress resistance and mitigate lifespan reduction in C. elegans exposed to high-dose IR. Overall, this work identifies EA as a promising plant-derived natural polyphenolic compound and establish a mechanistic basis for its potential development as a radioprotective intervention.
