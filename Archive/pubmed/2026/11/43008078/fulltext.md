OBJECTIVES: Cisplatin resistance in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) is closely associated with inflammation. This study investigates whether tiliroside, a bioactive flavonoid exhibiting potent anti-inflammatory properties, can sensitize both NSCLC and its cisplatin-resistant variants to cisplatin treatment. METHODS: The reversal of cisplatin resistance and synergistic interactions between tiliroside and cisplatin were evaluated in vitro using cell proliferation, apoptosis, and comet assays, and in vivo via mouse xenograft models. Underlying mechanisms were elucidated through transcriptomic analysis and rescue experiments, while direct target engagement was characterized using molecular docking, cellular thermal shift assays, and biolayer interferometry. KEY FINDINGS: Tiliroside synergistically reversed both intrinsic and acquired cisplatin resistance in vitro and in vivo. Transcriptomic analysis revealed upregulated IL-17A and IL-17RA expression, alongside activated IL-17 signaling, in cisplatin-resistant A549 cells. Target investigations demonstrated that tiliroside directly binds to IL-17RA, downregulating its expression and subsequently inhibiting the downstream IL-17/JAK2/STAT3 pathway. Furthermore, human recombinant IL-17A and IL-6 counteracted the inhibitory effects of tiliroside on STAT3 activation and its ability to enhance cisplatin sensitivity. CONCLUSIONS: IL-17RA represents a promising clinical target for overcoming cisplatin resistance in NSCLC. Tiliroside could serve as a valuable chemosensitizer by directly targeting IL-17RA to inhibit JAK2/STAT3 signaling.
