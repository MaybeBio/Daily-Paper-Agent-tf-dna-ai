Thromboinflammation is a central pathological process contributing to secondary brain injury after ischemic stroke (IS). Panax notoginseng saponins (PNS) exert pleiotropic anti-inflammatory, antithrombotic, and vascular effects, but the molecular convergence between PNS-associated pharmacology and stroke-relevant conventional agents remains incompletely characterized. We integrated network pharmacology, bulk transcriptomic datasets, single-cell RNA sequencing, regulatory-network analysis, and structure-based molecular docking to prioritize candidate targets shared by PNS and an exploratory screening panel of 16 stroke-relevant pharmacological agents. Predicted drug and PNS targets were intersected with IS-associated differentially expressed genes (DEGs), followed by network analysis, independent cross-dataset expression assessment, gene-set enrichment analysis (GSEA), transcription-factor (TF) prediction with co-expression analysis, myeloid-cell sub-clustering, and GNINA docking. The screening framework yielded 79 shared predicted pharmacological targets and 10 disease-associated candidates: ITK, CDK2, C5AR1, VDR, S1PR2, KIF11, CCR1, FAP, PRKCD, and MAOA. CCR1 and C5AR1 were prioritized because they combined high network connectivity with consistent disease-associated expression across the analyzed datasets and strong links to thromboinflammatory pathways. BATF2 and SPI1 expression positively correlated with both C5AR1 and CCR1 in GSE56267. Myeloid sub-clustering further showed that C5ar1 was broadly distributed across stroke-associated microglial and monocyte/macrophage states, whereas Ccr1 was more restricted to activated inflammatory and infiltrating myeloid populations. GNINA docking of all fourteen network-defined receptor-ligand systems identified five complexes with docking affinity scores between - 8.06 and - 10.94 kcal/mol (ginsenosides Rd and Re at CCR1; notoginsenoside R1, ginsenoside Rg1, and ginsenoside Rd at C5AR1). Collectively, these analyses prioritize CCR1 and C5AR1 as convergent candidate thromboinflammatory nodes shared by PNS-associated and stroke-relevant pharmacological target spaces. The findings are hypothesis-generating and do not establish pharmacological synergy, direct receptor coupling, or causal therapeutic mechanisms.
