OBJECTIVES: This study aimed to assess the nephroptotective activity of Malva parviflora L. leaves extract (MPLE) against the potent anticancer, cisplatin. METHODS: MPLE was chemically profiled using liquid chromatography-quadrupole time-of-flight tandem mass spectrometry and different in vitro analyses. Then, its activity was assessed in vivo model at low and high dose levels (250 and 500 mg/kg). Finally, molecular docking elucidated the key bioactive metabolites in MPLE. KEY FINDINGS: 101 metabolites of diverse chemical classes were identified in MPLE. MPLE had total phenolics and total flavonoids of 10.6 +/- 0.2, 6.5 +/- 0.1 mug gallic acid equivalent/mg extract, mug quercetin equivalent/mg extract, respectively. Moreover, it was effective in different in vitro antioxidant assays. In vivo model, MPLE reduced the elevation in creatinine, blood urea nitrogen, cystatin C and renal content of neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin and kidney injury molecule-1. Mechanistically, MPLE halted oxidative stress, inflammation, and necroptosis by targeting peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor-alpha (PPAR-alpha), HMGB-1/TLR-4, and downregulation of TNFR-1/RIPK1/RIPK3/MLKL axes, respectively. The ameliorative activity was further supported by the histopathological examination. Molecular docking elucidated that 8-hydroxy flavonoids, e.g. gossypetin and hypolaetin, had a high affinity, to the molecular targets PPAR-alpha, tumor necrosis factor alpha, and tumor necrosis factor receptor 1. CONCLUSIONS: MPLE is a multi-target nephroprotective agent against cisplatin. Further pharmacological and clinical studies are needed before its integration in clinical studies.
