OBJECTIVES: Scoparone (SCO) has demonstrated hepatoprotective potential in previous studies, whereas its molecular mechanisms against alcohol-associated liver disease (ALD) remain unclear. This study investigated the protective effects and underlying mechanisms of SCO in ethanol-induced HepG2 cells. METHODS: Network pharmacology and molecular docking were employed to predict the potential targets and signaling pathways of SCO against ALD. Ethanol-induced HepG2 cells were used to validate the predicted mechanisms by biochemical assays, flow cytometry, immunofluorescence staining, quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction and western blotting. KEY FINDINGS: Network pharmacology identified 109 overlapping targets between SCO and ALD, with the phosphoinositide 3-kinase/protein kinase B (PI3K/Akt) signaling pathway emerging as a key pathway. SCO enhanced alcohol metabolism, attenuated oxidative stress, restored mitochondrial membrane potential, and suppressed ethanol-induced apoptosis. Mechanistically, SCO activated PI3K/Akt signaling, thereby enhancing nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2/heme oxygenase-1 (Nrf2/HO-1) antioxidant signaling and suppressing the phosphorylated forkhead box O1 (FoxO1)/FoxO1-associated apoptotic signaling. These protective effects were markedly reversed by the PI3K inhibitor LY294002. CONCLUSIONS: SCO protects against ethanol-induced hepatocyte injury by activating the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway, thereby enhancing Nrf2/HO-1-mediated antioxidant defense and suppressing FoxO1-associated apoptotic signaling. These findings provide preliminary mechanistic evidence supporting further preclinical investigation of SCO in alcohol-associated liver injury.
