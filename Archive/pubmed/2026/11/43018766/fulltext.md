Current neuroblastoma (NB) risk classification relies partly on tumor tissue-derived information, which may be unavailable in patients who cannot undergo biopsy or surgical resection. Therefore, this study aimed to develop a non-invasive prediction model for NB risk classification by integrating a liquid biopsy biomarker with routinely available clinical indicators. A total of 141 treatment-naive patients with NB were included and divided into a training cohort (n = 100) and a held-out testing cohort (n = 41). Six pretreatment predictors, including age, sex, lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), neuron-specific enolase (NSE), image-defined risk factors (IDRFs), and circulating plasma MYCN amplification status, were incorporated into 11 machine-learning algorithms. Model development and selection were performed exclusively in the training cohort using repeated nested cross-validation with patient-level out-of-fold predictions. Random forest (RF) achieved the best overall performance, with an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) of 0.952 (95% CI, 0.906-0.998), an accuracy of 91%, and a Brier score of 0.079. Decision curve analysis showed positive net benefit across the prespecified threshold range of 15%-50%, while SHapley Additive Explanations (SHAP) analysis identified IDRFs, NSE, and LDH as the leading contributors to model predictions. After model locking, the RF model retained good discrimination in the held-out testing cohort, with an AUC of 0.890 (95% CI, 0.791-0.989), a sensitivity of 84.2%, and a Brier score of 0.140. CONCLUSION: These findings support the feasibility of combining circulating MYCN status with routine clinical variables to provide a non-tissue-based approach for assisting NB risk classification. WHAT IS KNOWN: * Current NB risk classification incorporates clinical, imaging, histopathological, and molecular information, some of which requires tumor tissue and may be unavailable before biopsy or surgery. WHAT IS NEW: * This study developed and independently tested a non-invasive machine-learning model integrating circulating plasma MYCN amplification status with five routinely available clinical indicators, achieving an AUC of 0.952 during nested cross-validation in the training cohort and an AUC of 0.890 in the held-out testing cohort.
