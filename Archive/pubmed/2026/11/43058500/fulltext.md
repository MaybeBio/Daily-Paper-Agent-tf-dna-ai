# Single-cell and spatial transcriptomics reveal glycolytic remodeling and ENO1-associated metabolic vulnerability in hepatoblastoma


## Background
  Hepatoblastoma (HB) is the most common malignant liver tumor in children; however, the contribution of glycolytic metabolic reprogramming to HB development and progression remains incompletely characterized.


## Methods
  We integrated single-cell RNA sequencing and spatial transcriptomic datasets of HB from the GEO database. Glycometabolic pathway activity was quantified using scMetabolism, and pseudotime and transcription factor activity analyses were performed to characterize regulatory programs associated with glycolytic reprogramming. CellChat and spatial transcriptomics were applied to examine predicted tumor–immune communication. hdWGCNA combined with differential expression analysis was used to prioritize glycolysis-associated genes. ENO1 was further evaluated by in silico network perturbation, two independent shRNAs in vitro, and computational compound prioritization followed by molecular docking and a 100-ns molecular dynamics simulation.


## Results
  The single-cell atlas resolved 10 major cell types and revealed elevated glycolysis/gluconeogenesis pathway scores in malignant epithelial cells. Pseudotime analysis showed increasing glycolytic activity along the inferred epithelial trajectory. CellChat predicted enhanced MDK–NCL communication between Glyco_High tumor cells and neutrophils, while spatial analysis showed regional concordance between MDK/NCL co-expression and high-glycolysis areas. hdWGCNA identified three glycolysis-associated candidate genes: ENO1, AKR1A1, and AHCY. In silico ENO1 perturbation was associated with altered inferred regulatory activity of approximately 2.1% of genes. Two independent ENO1-targeting shRNAs produced concordant reductions in proliferation, colony formation, lactate production, and glucose consumption in HepG2 and Huh6 cells. BRD-K14653796 was computationally prioritized, and the modeled ENO1–compound complex remained conformationally stable under the specified simulation conditions.


## Conclusions
  This study characterizes glycolytic heterogeneity in HB at single-cell and spatial resolution and nominates ENO1 as a candidate metabolic vulnerability in CNV-high putative malignant epithelial cells. It also highlights a computationally predicted MDK–NCL-associated tumor–immune communication pattern and provides testable hypotheses for future preclinical validation.


## Introduction
  Hepatoblastoma (HB) is the most common primary malignant liver tumor in children and predominantly affects patients younger than 4 years of age [1]. Although surgical resection combined with cisplatin-based systemic chemotherapy has substantially improved outcomes in patients with early-stage disease, children with high-risk HB characterized by metastatic, recurrent, or chemoresistant tumors continue to have limited therapeutic options and poor long-term survival [2]. Furthermore, evidence from adult liver malignancies indicates that tumor–vascular spatial relationships and surgical anatomy are clinically informative: vascular involvement or compression may be associated with microvascular invasion and other invasive pathological features, whereas vascular resection can influence operative complexity and transient postoperative liver injury [3, 4]. A major barrier to improving outcomes is the limited understanding of the molecular drivers underlying HB progression. Moreover, HB exhibits pronounced histopathological and molecular heterogeneity [5], and conventional treatment strategies based on clinical stage and pathological classification fail to adequately capture this biological diversity. Therefore, more refined multi-omic approaches are urgently needed to delineate the key molecular events driving HB progression and to identify actionable therapeutic vulnerabilities in high-risk disease.

  Metabolic reprogramming is a hallmark of cancer that enables tumor cells to remodel metabolic networks in response to physiological or pathological stressors, thereby sustaining proliferation, survival, and stress adaptation [6]. This process is particularly relevant to HB because the liver is intrinsically a central metabolic organ. Alterations in metabolic pathways can directly influence tumor growth, cellular viability, and therapeutic resistance. Previous studies have shown that stabilization of HIF-1α, overexpression of c-Myc, and recurrent CTNNB1 mutations may cooperate to promote this metabolic phenotype by upregulating key enzymes and transporters, including GLUT1, HK2, PKM2, and LDHA, thereby redirecting metabolic flux toward lactate production [7–9]. However, the biological impact of glycolysis extends beyond the support of tumor cell proliferation. Glycolytically active tumor cells can shape an immunosuppressive microenvironment by secreting lactate, remodeling the extracellular matrix, and releasing specific signaling molecules [10]. Accordingly, the direct modulation of infiltrating immune cells through ligand–receptor networks has emerged as an important but incompletely explored aspect of tumor immunology. Defining the regulatory mechanisms underlying metabolic reprogramming in HB may clarify disease pathogenesis and reveal therapeutically exploitable metabolic vulnerabilities.

  Although recent single-cell studies have begun to characterize HB and liver tumor biology [11, 12], the spatial organization, immune microenvironmental relevance, and targetable vulnerabilities of glycolytic heterogeneity in malignant HB epithelial cells remain incompletely understood. Here, we integrated scRNA-seq, spatial transcriptomics, ligand–receptor inference, hdWGCNA, and in vitro functional assays to define glycolysis-associated epithelial states, explore their tumor–immune communication programs, and nominate ENO1 as a candidate metabolic vulnerability in HB.


## Materials and methods

### Data sources and preprocessing
  HB scRNA-seq data were obtained from the GEO dataset GSE186975, which contains both tumor and normal liver tissue samples. Data preprocessing was performed using Seurat v4.3.0. Cells were retained according to the following quality-control criteria: nFeature_RNA > 500, nCount_RNA > 1,000, mitochondrial gene fraction (percent.mt) < 15%, and log10GenesPerUMI > 0.80. Only genes detected in at least 10 cells were retained for downstream analysis. The filtered expression matrix was normalized for library size using NormalizeData with default parameters. Highly variable genes were identified using FindVariableFeatures with nfeatures set to 2,000, followed by linear scaling and standardization using ScaleData. To correct inter-sample batch effects, Harmony was applied for data integration using sample-specific batch variables. In addition, 10x Genomics Visium spatial transcriptomic data from two HB tissue sections (GSE261958) were incorporated and processed using an independent workflow for downstream analyses. For disease-specific clinical evaluation, the normalized expression matrices of GSE131329 and GSE75271 were harmonized and combined after gene-symbol matching and batch-effect correction. Clinical association analyses were conducted using only samples with available annotation for the corresponding endpoint.


### Dimensionality reduction, clustering, and cell annotation
  Using Harmony-corrected embeddings, we used the first 15 dimensions to construct a nearest-neighbor graph with FindNeighbors. Clustering was performed using FindClusters with the Leiden algorithm at a resolution of 0.2. UMAP coordinates were computed in the same Harmony-corrected space using RunUMAP to visualize cell clusters and evaluate batch integration. The 14 initial clusters were manually annotated by integrating COSG-derived cluster-specific markers with established lineage markers.


### Assessment of glycometabolic pathway activity
  scMetabolism was used to score the activity of 17 glycometabolic pathways at single-cell resolution with KEGG gene sets as references, including glycolysis/gluconeogenesis, the tricarboxylic acid cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation. Pathway activity was quantified using the AUCell algorithm, which calculates cell-level enrichment scores for each pathway from the gene expression matrix. These scores were used for downstream cell grouping and spatial distribution analyses.


### Reclustering of epithelial subsets and identification of malignant cells
  Hepatocyte-annotated cells were extracted and reanalyzed after renormalization, highly variable gene selection, and Harmony-based batch correction. Clustering at a resolution of 0.6 identified six epithelial subsets (Epi1–Epi6). Large-scale CNV profiles were inferred using inferCNV, with normal non-epithelial cells as references. Epi5 showed the most pronounced CNV alterations and was therefore defined as the CNV-high putative malignant epithelial population (Tumor Epi). Epithelial cells from normal liver tissue with low CNV signals were defined as Normal Epi. Tumor Epi cells were further divided into Glyco_High and Glyco_Low groups according to the median glycolysis/gluconeogenesis score for downstream analyses.


### Stemness assessment and pseudotime analysis
  CytoTRACE was used to estimate the differentiation potential of individual cells. Monocle3 was applied to construct pseudotime trajectories for Tumor Epi cells. The trajectory root was defined as cells with the highest CytoTRACE score among Normal Epi cells, as this population showed the most transcriptionally diverse (lowest differentiation) state, consistent with a progenitor-like starting point. Pseudotime values were correlated with glycolysis scores using Spearman correlation to assess the relationship between metabolic activity and pseudotime position.


### Inference of transcription factor activity
  Differential transcription factor (TF) activity between Glyco_High and Glyco_Low cells was inferred using the DoRothEA regulon database, including TF–target interactions with A, B, and C confidence levels, together with the univariate linear model (ULM) algorithm implemented in decoupleR. The run_ulm function was applied using shared genes, and differentially active regulators were identified using the Wilcoxon test (P < 0.05).


### Cell-cell communication analysis
  CellChat was used to systematically infer ligand–receptor interactions between Glyco_High or Glyco_Low epithelial subsets and microenvironmental cell types. Normalized expression matrices were used as input, and the default ligand–receptor database was applied. Analyses focused on communication pathways between glycolytically active epithelial cells and immune cells.


### Spatial transcriptomic validation
  After preprocessing and unsupervised clustering of 10x Genomics Visium spatial transcriptomic data from two HB tissue sections (GSE261958), RCTD was used for spatial cell-type deconvolution with single-cell annotations used as references. scMetabolism-derived glycolysis scores were mapped to each spatial spot to visualize the spatial distribution of glycolytic activity across tissue sections. Candidate ligand–receptor pairs identified by CellChat were evaluated using spatial co-expression analysis to assess regional concordance, and the spatial expression patterns of three key genes were also visualized.


### Weighted gene co-expression network analysis using hdWGCNA
  hdWGCNA was performed on epithelial cells from the Glyco_High and Glyco_Low groups. Metacells were constructed using k = 20 and max_shared = 10 to ensure that each metacell belonged to a single metabolic group. After normalization, a signed network was constructed using a soft power of 8, and gene modules were identified. Correlations between modules and the glycolytic phenotype were calculated to identify glycolysis-specific modules, from which hub genes with |kME| > 0.8 were extracted. Hub genes were intersected with upregulated differentially expressed genes between Glyco_High and Glyco_Low groups to define core target genes. Upstream transcription factors (TFs) were inferred using DoRothEA.


### In vitro functional assays for ENO1
  To validate the function of the core target ENO1, scTenifoldKnk was first used for single-cell-level in silico knockout analysis. Lentiviral shRNA-mediated knockdown was then performed in HepG2 and Huh6 cells. A three-in-one ENO1 shRNA construct was generated using the following sequences: sh1, CCCAACATCCTGGAGAATAAA; sh2, AGAATGTCATCAAGGAGAAAT; and sh3, GAAAGCTGGCTACACTGATAA. The vector contained a ZsGreen fluorescent reporter and a puromycin resistance gene. A non-targeting shRNA (shNC) was used as the control, and knockdown efficiency was assessed by Western blotting using an anti-ENO1 antibody (Proteintech, 11024-1-AP). Cell viability was measured using the CCK-8 assay (MCE, HY-K0301), and long-term proliferative capacity was assessed by colony formation assays. Culture supernatants were collected, and lactate production and glucose consumption were measured using a lactate assay kit (Nanjing Jiancheng, A019-2-1) and a glucose assay kit (Nanjing Jiancheng, A154-1-1), respectively, to assess changes in glycolytic flux.


### Drug prediction and molecular dynamics simulation
  DrugReflector was used to predict candidate small-molecule compounds based on genes differentially expressed between Glyco_High and Glyco_Low cells (|log2FC| > 0.5, P < 0.05). Candidate compounds were docked to the ENO1 protein structure using AutoDock Vina, and the optimal binding conformation was selected. A 100 ns all-atom molecular dynamics simulation was performed using GROMACS 2022.2. The Amber14SB force field was used for the protein, and the GAFF2 force field with AM1-BCC partial charges was used for the ligand. The system was solvated with TIP3P water and neutralized with 0.15 M NaCl. After energy minimization and NVT/NPT equilibration, production simulations were performed at 298 K and 1 bar with a 2 fs integration time step. Electrostatic interactions were treated using the particle mesh Ewald method, and hydrogen bonds were constrained using LINCS. RMSD, RMSF, SASA, hydrogen bonds, and the radius of gyration were analyzed after simulation; MM-PBSA binding energy estimation was performed when necessary.


### Statistical analysis
  All statistical analyses were performed using R v4.3.1 and GraphPad Prism v9.0. Two-group comparisons of continuous variables used the Wilcoxon rank-sum test or two-sample t-test based on data distribution. Multiple-group comparisons used the Kruskal–Wallis test or one-way ANOVA. Correlations were assessed using Spearman rank correlation. All tests were two-sided; P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Multiple testing correction was applied using the Benjamini–Hochberg method where indicated. Effect sizes (fold change, Spearman r, Cohen’s d) are reported alongside P values for all major comparisons.


## Results

### Single-cell transcriptomic atlas and glycolytic metabolic features of hepatoblastoma
  The overall workflow comprised single-cell atlas construction, metabolic characterization, inference of malignant evolutionary trajectories, analysis of microenvironmental interactions, and validation of key targets (Fig. 1A). To systematically resolve the cellular composition of HB, we performed quality control, batch correction, and unsupervised clustering on the GSE186975 dataset, identifying 10 major cell types: hepatocytes, macrophages, T cells, neutrophils, endothelial cells, erythroid cells, cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), plasma cells, cholangiocytes, and mast cells (Fig. 1B). The specificity of canonical cell type marker gene expression was confirmed by dot plot analysis (Fig. 1C). To characterize the global metabolic features of the tumor ecosystem, we quantified the activity of 17 core metabolic pathways at single-cell resolution using scMetabolism. Hepatocytes exhibited the highest glycolysis/gluconeogenesis activity score compared with other cell types (Fig. 1D). UMAP visualization further showed that regions with high glycolytic activity overlapped with tumor-derived hepatocyte clusters (Fig. 1E), and hepatocytes and macrophages exhibited the highest glycolytic activity scores among all cell types (Fig. 1F).

### Identification of malignant epithelial cells and analysis of metabolic heterogeneity
  To further dissect epithelial heterogeneity in HB, hepatocyte-annotated cells were extracted from the global single-cell atlas and reclustered separately, yielding six transcriptionally distinct epithelial subsets (Epi1–Epi6) (Fig. 2A). Because no universal somatic mutation marker is available to directly assign malignant identity at single-cell resolution in HB, we applied inferCNV, using normal non-epithelial cells as references, to infer large-scale chromosomal copy-number variation (CNV) patterns across epithelial subsets.

  Epi5 showed the most prominent CNV abnormalities and the highest CNV score among all epithelial subsets; therefore, it was designated as the malignant epithelial population (Tumor Epi) (Figs. 2B-C). By contrast, the remaining subsets exhibited weaker CNV signals that were closer to a diploid state, suggesting that they may represent CNV-low epithelial cells or transitional epithelial states. To avoid oversimplified classification of potential intermediate states as normal cells, subsequent tumor-normal comparisons defined Epi5 as Tumor Epi and epithelial cells derived from normal liver tissue with low CNV signals as Normal Epi. Tumor Epi showed significantly increased glycolysis/gluconeogenesis pathway activity, indicating marked glycolytic metabolic reprogramming in malignant epithelial cells (Fig. 2D, red label). Tumor Epi cells were then stratified into Glyco_High and Glyco_Low groups based on the median glycolysis score. Proportion analysis revealed that Glyco_High cells dominated the Tumor Epi compartment, whereas Normal Epi was mainly composed of Glyco_Low cells (Figs. 2E-F). AUCell score comparison showed significantly higher total glycolytic activity in Tumor Epi than in Normal Epi (P < 2.22 × 10⁻¹⁶; Fig. 2G), consistent with the notion that enhanced glycolysis is a metabolic feature of malignant epithelial cells in HB.


### Association between glycolytic activity, malignant evolutionary trajectories, and transcriptional regulation
  To examine the relationship between metabolic reprogramming and tumor evolution, we first assessed cellular differentiation potential using CytoTRACE. CytoTRACE scores, which reflect per-cell transcriptional complexity as a proxy for differentiation potential, were higher in Normal Epi than in Tumor Epi (Fig. 3A), suggesting that Tumor Epi cells have undergone greater transcriptional convergence consistent with a more lineage-committed malignant state. We next constructed a pseudotime trajectory linking Normal Epi and Tumor Epi using Monocle3 (Fig. 3B and C). Glycolytic activity progressively increased along the trajectory (Fig. 3D), indicating a progressive co-occurrence of glycolytic activity and trajectory advancement.Dynamic gene expression analysis revealed progressive downregulation of hepatocyte functional genes, such as ALB and CRABP2, and gradual upregulation of tumor-associated genes, such as GCNT3, along pseudotime (Fig. 3E). To identify upstream transcriptional regulators, we inferred differential TF regulatory activity using DoRothEA/decoupleR. HIF1A showed the highest inferred regulatory activity in Glyco_High cells; HNF4G, CEBPD, PPARA, and FOXO3 also showed higher inferred activity in Glyco_High, whereas RFX5, ATF1, and KLF5 displayed higher inferred activity in Glyco_Low cells (Fig. 3F). TF ranking showed that HNF4A, HIF1A, and KLF4 were positioned within the high-activity region in Glyco_High cells, suggesting their involvement in the transcriptional programs associated with the high-glycolysis phenotype (Fig. 3G).

### CellChat predicts increased MDK–NCL-associated communication between Glyco_High tumor cells and neutrophils
  We hypothesized that metabolic heterogeneity may be associated with differential intercellular communication programs and applied CellChat to predict ligand–receptor interactions. The inferred overall outgoing communication probability was higher in Glyco_High cells than in Glyco_Low cells (Fig. 4A). Higher predicted communication probabilities were observed primarily toward endothelial cells, CAFs, and neutrophils (Fig. 4B). The MDK–NCL pair showed the highest predicted communication probability between Glyco_High cells and neutrophils among the evaluated ligand–receptor pairs (Fig. 4C, red box). A chord diagram visualized the inferred MDK-associated communication pattern involving neutrophils and macrophages (Fig. 4D).To assess the spatial context of this predicted interaction, we analyzed spatial transcriptomic data from two HB tissue sections. MDK and NCL showed overlapping spatial expression within tumor parenchymal regions, with double-positive regions visualized in purple; these regions also showed regional overlap with areas of high glycolytic activity. These findings support a spatial association between MDK/NCL co-expression and high-glycolysis regions but do not demonstrate functional intercellular signaling.

### hdWGCNA identifies glycolysis-associated core modules and pan-cancer prognostic relevance
  To identify key genes associated with the glycolytic phenotype, we performed high-dimensional weighted gene co-expression network analysis (hdWGCNA) in Glyco_High and Glyco_Low cells. Seven co-expression modules were identified (Fig. 5A). The Green module showed a strong positive correlation with the Glyco_High phenotype, and its hub genes, including GLG1, ACAT2, CCT3, and ENO1, were preferentially expressed in Glyco_High cells (Fig. 5B). Intersection of the 155 hub genes from the Green module with genes upregulated in Glyco_High cells prioritized three candidate genes: ENO1, AKR1A1, and AHCY (Fig. 5C).All three genes were expressed at higher levels in Tumor Epi than in Normal Epi. Pseudotime analysis showed that the expression of these genes progressively increased along malignant progression (Fig. 5D). Spatial transcriptomic analysis further confirmed the in situ enrichment patterns of selected candidates, including ENO1 and AKR1A1, whereas AHCY displayed broadly distributed spatial expression and was therefore not included in the main spatial visualization (Fig. 5E). In a combined HB bulk-transcriptomic cohort, ENO1 expression was numerically higher in patients with an event than in event-free patients (P = 0.057) and in patients with metastatic disease than in those without metastasis (P = 0.092) (Fig. 5F). Although neither comparison reached statistical significance, both showed a concordant directional pattern.

### ENO1 regulatory network perturbation and in vitro functional validation
  We first performed in silico regulatory network perturbation of ENO1 using scTenifoldKnk. This computational approach models gene network perturbation through tensor decomposition; results represent predicted changes in inferred gene regulatory activity, not actual transcriptomic consequences of gene deletion. In silico ENO1 perturbation was associated with significantly altered inferred activity of approximately 2.1% of genes (Fig. 6A–C), including predicted downregulation of NEK2, FGA, FGG, FGL1, and liver-enriched secretory proteins (ALB, APOH, APOC1). KEGG pathway enrichment of genes showing altered inferred activity showed enrichment in complement and coagulation cascades, cholesterol metabolism, insulin resistance, pyruvate metabolism, and fatty acid degradation (Fig. 6D), consistent with a broad network role for ENO1 in tumor metabolic homeostasis. We subsequently evaluated ENO1 function using two independent shRNAs targeting distinct ENO1 sequences, sh2-ENO1 and sh3-ENO1, in HepG2 and Huh6 cells. Both shRNAs effectively reduced ENO1 expression and produced concordant phenotypes (Fig. 6E). ENO1 knockdown significantly inhibited cell proliferation and colony formation and reduced lactate production and glucose consumption in both cell lines (Fig. 6F–H).

### Exploratory computational prioritization of candidate compounds and molecular dynamics analysis
  Based on the differential gene-expression profile between Glyco_High and Glyco_Low cells, DrugReflector was used to computationally prioritize candidate compounds. BRD-K14653796 was ranked as the top candidate compound and was selected for subsequent molecular docking and molecular dynamics analyses, followed by BRD-K32636001 and BRD-K58519325 (Fig. 7A). Molecular docking predicted a plausible binding pose of BRD-K14653796 within a putative ENO1 binding pocket, with modeled hydrogen-bond interactions involving Lys119 and Arg411 and a hydrophobic interaction involving Leu129 (Fig. 7B). To assess the conformational stability of the modeled complex under the specified simulation conditions, a 100-ns all-atom molecular dynamics simulation was performed. Following initial equilibration, the protein backbone and ligand RMSD values remained within relatively narrow ranges, suggesting limited conformational drift during the simulation (Fig. 7C). The radius of gyration, residue-level RMSF, and solvent-accessible surface area also remained relatively stable, consistent with limited global and local structural fluctuations of the modeled complex (Fig. 7D–F). Recurrent hydrogen-bond interactions were observed during the trajectory (Fig. 7G), and superposition of representative conformations showed no obvious ligand dissociation within the simulated timescale (Fig. 7H). Collectively, these analyses indicate that the modeled ENO1–BRD-K14653796 complex remained conformationally stable under the specified simulation conditions.

## Discussion
  HB is a rare pediatric malignancy; however, its distinctive biology and the paucity of effective therapies for high-risk patients underscore the clinical importance of characterizing its molecular underpinnings. By integrating single-cell and spatial transcriptomics with in vitro experiments, this study provides a characterization of metabolic heterogeneity in HB and identifies candidate associations between glycolytic activity, transcriptional programs, immune microenvironmental communication, and ENO1 expression. These findings offer insights into metabolic reprogramming in HB.

  The Warburg effect has traditionally been regarded as a general metabolic feature of cancer cells [13]. However, our single-cell analysis showed that glycolytic activation in malignant HB epithelium was heterogeneous. Pseudotime trajectory analysis revealed a statistically significant positive association between glycolytic activity and pseudotime progression, accompanied by downregulation of ALB and upregulation of GCNT3. This pattern is consistent with a co-occurrence of metabolic and transcriptional changes along the malignant evolutionary trajectory, though the causal directionality cannot be established from pseudotime analysis alone. The Glyco_High subset was highly enriched within malignant epithelial cells, suggesting that high-glycolysis metabolic states may be associated with more advanced tumor cell states [14]. Given that HB originates from embryonic hepatic precursor cells, its normal counterpart may exhibit relatively high basal glycolytic activity [6], potentially contributing to the predominance of the Glyco_High phenotype in malignant HB epithelium. Whether this implies greater susceptibility to glycolytic inhibition compared with adult liver cancer remains to be investigated.

  DoRothEA/decoupleR analysis identified HIF1A as the TF with the highest inferred regulatory activity in Glyco_High cells. HIF-1α is a canonical master regulator of glycolysis and can directly activate the transcription of glycolytic enzyme genes, such as HK2, PKM2, and LDHA [15]. Notably, approximately 70%–80% of HB cases harbor activating CTNNB1 mutations [16]. Prior work has shown that β-catenin can stabilize HIF-1α by suppressing VHL-mediated ubiquitination and degradation [17], suggesting a potential molecular link between Wnt/β-catenin signaling and aerobic glycolysis. Although CTNNB1 mutational status was not directly assessed in this study, the marked activation of HIF1A in Glyco_High cells was consistent with this mechanistic model. We also observed elevated activity of HNF4G and PPARA in Glyco_High cells, suggesting that glycolytic regulation in HB may differ from that in other tumor types and may involve cooperative liver-lineage-specific transcriptional circuits [18, 19]. This multidimensional regulatory network may provide a potential entry point for future strategies combining Wnt pathway targeting with metabolic vulnerability–directed interventions.

  CellChat analysis predicted increased communication probability through the MDK–NCL axis between Glyco_High tumor cells and neutrophils. MDK is reactivated in multiple malignancies [20] and has been reported to modulate immune responses in colorectal cancer [21] and glioblastoma [22, 23]. More broadly, the secreted lectin-like glycoprotein ZG16B has been reported to regulate tumor-cell behavior, angiogenesis, vascular permeability, myeloid-derived suppressor-cell activity, and macrophage polarization, providing a conceptual precedent for secreted-factor-mediated microenvironmental remodeling [24].In our analysis, Glyco_High tumor cells were predicted to be the principal source of MDK-related signaling, whereas neutrophils represented a major recipient population, suggesting a potential association between the high-glycolysis state and paracrine myeloid-cell communication. Mechanistically, lactate produced by enhanced glycolysis can acidify the tumor microenvironment and upregulate immunomodulatory factors via HIF-1α and NF-κB pathways [25]. Consistent with this concept, HIF-1α was activated in Glyco_High cells in our analysis, and the MDK promoter contains hypoxia-response elements that enable direct transcriptional regulation by HIF-1α [26]. However, all cell–cell communication results reflect predicted ligand–receptor co-expression probabilities, not demonstrated intercellular interactions. Functional validation using co-culture assays, neutralizing antibodies, or MDK/NCL conditional knockdown is required to establish whether this axis operationally remodels neutrophil phenotype in HB.

  Using a dual strategy that integrated hdWGCNA with differential gene expression analysis, we identified ENO1 among 155 candidate genes as a core hub gene associated with glycolytic metabolism. ENO1 encodes α-enolase, a glycolytic enzyme that catalyzes the conversion of 2-phosphoglycerate to phosphoenolpyruvate and represents a key enzymatic component of the pathway. ENO1 is overexpressed in multiple cancers and has been associated with adverse clinical outcomes in several adult malignancies, including hepatocellular carcinoma, colorectal cancer, glioblastoma, breast cancer, and pancreatic cancer [27]. Functional experiments in HepG2 and Huh6 cells showed that ENO1 knockdown not only inhibited proliferation but also reduced lactate production and glucose consumption, consistent with reported roles of ENO1 in driving aerobic glycolysis in multiple tumor types [28, 29]. ENO1 has attracted considerable attention as an anticancer drug target. ENOblock (AP-III-a4), a non-substrate analog inhibitor, suppresses enolase activity by directly binding enolase without competing for substrate binding, and has been shown to inhibit colorectal cancer cell proliferation and tumor growth in vivo [30]. ENOblock can also enhance cisplatin sensitivity in gastric cancer cells by suppressing glycolysis and stemness-associated properties [31]. However, ENO1-directed therapeutic strategies remain preclinical, and no ENO1-targeted agent has entered routine clinical use [32]. Based on DrugReflector-based drug sensitivity prediction [33], BRD-K14653796 was computationally prioritized as the top-ranked candidate compound, and subsequent molecular docking and 100-ns molecular dynamics simulation indicated that the modeled ENO1–BRD-K14653796 complex remained conformationally stable under the specified simulation conditions. These computational findings do not establish direct ENO1 binding, enzymatic inhibition, target specificity, or cellular antitumor activity.

  This study has several limitations. First, all omics analyses relied on public datasets, which may introduce sampling and batch-related heterogeneity. The CNV-high putative malignant population and Glyco_High/Glyco_Low states were inferred from transcriptomic data without matched genomic or metabolomic validation, while the exploratory HB cohort analyses lacked sufficient survival and clinicopathological information for reliable multivariable modeling. Second, the MDK–NCL axis was supported only by computational inference and spatial association, and ENO1 function was evaluated in two cell lines without rescue, patient-derived, or in vivo validation, although two independent shRNAs produced concordant phenotypes. Finally, BRD-K14653796 remains a computationally prioritized candidate compound, and its direct target engagement, enzymatic and cellular activity, ENO1 dependency, pharmacokinetics, and in vivo efficacy and safety require systematic validation.


## Conclusion
  This study characterizes glycolytic heterogeneity in HB at single-cell and spatial resolution and nominates ENO1 as a candidate metabolic vulnerability in CNV-high putative malignant epithelial cells. It also highlights a computationally predicted MDK–NCL-associated tumor–immune communication pattern and provides testable hypotheses for future preclinical validation.
