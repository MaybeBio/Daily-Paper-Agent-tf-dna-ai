# Toddalolactone inhibits the activation of fibroblasts in skin fibrosis by regulating MAOA


## Abstract
  Skin fibrosis is characterized by excessive deposition of extracellular matrix driven by activated fibroblasts, leading to varying degrees of patient discomfort, deformity, disability, and even death. In vitro studies using mouse primary skin fibroblasts (PSFs) and patient-derived primary keloid fibroblasts (KFs) demonstrated that toddalolactone (Tod) significantly inhibited fibroblast proliferation, migration, and activation and suppressed the TGF-β1/Smad and TGF-β1/non-Smad pathways in these cells. In a bleomycin (BLM)-induced mouse model of skin fibrosis, Tod treatment significantly reduced dermal thickness and collagen deposition in mouse skin, decreased skin hydroxyproline content, and inhibited fibroblast activation as well as the TGF-β1/Smad and TGF-β1/non-Smad pathways in vivo. In a keloid xenograft mouse model, Tod treatment significantly reduced the relative weight of keloids in mice. Histopathological analysis revealed that Tod alleviated the degree of keloid fibrosis and collagen aggregation while inhibiting fibroblast activation in keloid tissue. Furthermore, molecular docking identified monoamine oxidase A (MAOA) as a potential target of Tod. Cellular thermal shift assays and MAOA knockdown using siRNA in PSFs and KFs demonstrated that Tod inhibits the TGF-β1 pathway and suppresses fibroblast activation by inhibiting MAOA. Collectively, the findings reveal that Tod regulates the TGF-β1/Smad and TGF-β1/non-Smad pathways through inhibiting MAOA, thereby affecting skin fibroblast activation and alleviating skin fibrosis.


## 1. Introduction
  Skin fibrosis is a hallmark pathological feature observed in various conditions, including keloids, systemic sclerosis, morphea, hypertrophic scars, and other disorders with metabolic, vascular, or genetic origins [1]. Skin fibrosis can generally be categorized into two forms. The first is associated with abnormal cutaneous wound healing, such as in keloids [2]. The second is driven by complex autoimmune reactions, as seen in systemic sclerosis [3]. To date, the etiopathogenesis of fibrotic skin diseases remains incompletely understood, and definitive treatments are lacking [4]. Most studies suggest that these conditions arise from the intricate interplay of genetic, immunologic, and neurovascular factors, among which skin fibroblasts are important effector cells [5].

  Skin fibroblasts are specialized cells responsible for the production and maintenance of anatomically diverse connective tissues that are rich in extracellular matrix (ECM), which is essential for facilitating skin functions [6]. During the complex process of skin fibrosis, fibroblasts are influenced by a variety of factors that are either resident in fibrotic skin tissue or recruited from professional inflammatory cells. These factors endow fibroblasts with the capacity for migration, proliferation, and metabolism, thereby enabling the deposition and resorption of ECM [7]. Among these factors, transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β) is regarded as a key mediator involved in the recruitment and transdifferentiation of cell precursors into myofibroblasts, as well as the activation of fibroblasts [8]. Additionally, activated fibroblasts in skin fibrosis exhibit contractile properties associated with the expression of α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA) [9] and a high capacity for synthesizing and secreting type I collagen (Col1) and fibronectin (Fn) [10]. Therefore, we used TGF-β-activated primary skin fibroblasts (PSFs) and patient-derived keloid fibroblasts (KFs) to evaluate the pharmacological effects of toddalolactone on skin fibrosis, as assessed by TGF-β-related signaling activity and the expression of the fibrotic markers α-SMA, Col1, and Fn.

  Toddalolactone (Tod) is a natural coumarin compound with the chemical formula C16H20O6. It is primarily derived from Toddalia asiatica L [11]., which have been shown to exhibit efficacy in dispersing blood stasis, stopping bleeding, and easing pain according to records in traditional Chinese medical books on Chinese material medica. Some studies have reported that Tod is broadly adopted for treating various chronic diseases such as stroke, rheumatoid arthritis, and oedema [12] and has antiembolism-related, haemostatic and antifibrotic effects [13]. However, no studies have reported the effect of Tod on skin fibrosis. Therefore, we evaluated the therapeutic effect of Tod on skin fibrosis using in vivo and in vitro models and report that Tod can effectively alleviate skin fibrosis. Further exploration of therapeutic targets of Tod revealed that Tod could modulate MAOA. Monoamine oxidase (MAO) is a mitochondrial enzyme that is primarily responsible for catalysing the oxidative deamination of monoamine neurotransmitters and dietary amines [14]. MAO has been distinctly identified as two separate protein isoforms, MAOA and MAOB, which are encoded by different genes [15]. MAOA has been extensively investigated in the context of neuropsychiatric disorders, including aggressive behaviour and psychotic depression [16]. In the present study, we revealed that Tod can mitigate the progression of skin fibrosis by inhibiting MAOA. Thus, Tod may represent a potential therapeutic strategy for the treatment of skin fibrosis.


## 2. Methods and materials

### 2.1. Separation and culture of murine primary dermal fibroblasts
  Primary skin dermal fibroblasts (PSFs) were isolated from the dermis of neonatal mice, cultured in DMEM supplemented with 15% foetal bovine serum (FBS) and incubated at 37 °C in a humidified environment containing 5% CO2.


### 2.2. Separation and culture of keloid fibroblasts
  Keloid tissue samples were excised under sterile conditions, and the epithelial and subcutaneous layers were carefully removed using ophthalmic scissors. The tissue was then diced into 5 mm3 fragments and placed in Dulbecco's modified Eagle medium (DMEM) supplemented with 15% foetal bovine serum (FBS). The tissue fragments were incubated at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO2. When keloid fibroblasts (KFs) approached confluence, they were passaged at a 1:2 dilution ratio.


### 2.3. CCK-8 assay
  Fibroblasts at 90% confluence were seeded into a 96-well plate at a density of approximately 1000 primary cells per well. Each well was inoculated with 100 μL of cell suspension and incubated in a constant-temperature incubator for 24 hours. The culture medium was subsequently aspirated from the wells, and for the drug-treated groups, 100 μL of culture medium containing Tod and 10 ng/mL TGF-β1 was subsequently added to each well for 24 hours. Each experimental group included three replicate wells to minimize error. After 24 hours, 10 μL of CCK-8 solution (1 mg/mL) was added to each well in a light-protected manner, followed by incubation for 4 hours. The absorbance was then measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader.


### 2.4. Wound-healing assay
  Cells were seeded into a 24-well plate, and when the cell density reached an appropriate level, a sterile 10-μL pipette tip was used to create vertical scratches in each well. For primary mouse dermal fibroblasts, 100 μL of a solution containing Tod (0, 10, 20, and 40 μM) and/or 10 ng/mL TGF-β1 was added to each well. For keloid fibroblasts, 100 μL of Tod (0, 10, 20, and 40 μM) was added to each well. Cell migration was observed and photographed at the scratch site using a microscope at 0, 6, 24, and 30 hours. For each time point, three different locations were selected for imaging, and efforts were made to ensure that the same locations were photographed at different time points. Image analysis was subsequently performed using ImageJ software.


### 2.5. Western blot
  Cells were plated in 6-well plates at a density of 1.5 × 105 cells per well. Following the respective treatments, the cells were rinsed with PBS and lysed in 50 μL of lysis buffer. The extracted proteins were denatured by boiling at 100 °C for 3 minutes and subsequently separated by electrophoresis on 10% SDS‒PAGE gels. The resolved proteins were transferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes, which were then blocked for 1 h at room temperature in a solution containing 5% nonfat milk in TBS with 0.1% Tween 20. The membranes were incubated with primary antibodies (Table 1) overnight at 4 °C. After being washed, the membranes were incubated with secondary antibodies for 1 hour at room temperature and visualized using a chemiluminescence detection system. Quantitative densitometric analysis of the blots was performed using ImageJ software.


### 2.6. Quantitative Real-Time PCR (qRT‒PCR)
  To determine the gene expression levels of specific proteins in cellular and tissue samples, total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent and subsequently reverse-transcribed using FastKing gDNA Dispelling RT SuperMix (TIANGEN Biotech, Beijing, China). Quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR) was performed according to the manufacturer's protocols, utilizing UNICON® qPCR SYBR Green Master Mix (Yeasen Biotech, Shanghai, China). The sequences of the primers used are provided in Table 2. In all RT‒qPCR experiments, GAPDH or β-actin was used as the internal reference gene. The relative quantification of gene expression was performed using the comparative CT (−ΔΔCT) method, with normalization to the endogenous control gene (GAPDH/β-Actin).


### 2.7. Animal care and use
  All animal welfare and experimental protocols adhered to the guidelines of the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) of Nankai University (Permit No. SYXK 2019‐0001), and the animal study was reviewed and approved by the Nankai University Animal Welfare Ethics Review Committee (2026-SYDWLL-000857). Daily monitoring of body weight and general condition was performed for all the mice. The mice were euthanized immediately when hind limb paralysis or weakness was observed or when body weight loss exceeded 20% within 72 hours. A total of 25 C57BL/6J wild-type mice and 25 BALB/c nude mice were used in this study. No mice reached the humane endpoint or died prior to the experimental endpoint. All personnel involved in animal handling and experimental procedures received adequate training in laboratory animal science, including the principles of the Three Rs, ethical issues in animal use, and species-specific technical procedures. Competency was assessed prior to independent work with the animals.


### 2.8. Bleomycin-induced skin fibrosis model
  Male C57BL/6J wild-type mice (8–10 weeks, 20–22 g) were selected for the study. The mice were anaesthetized via intraperitoneal injection of sodium pentobarbital, and approximately 2 × 2 cm2 of fur on the dorsal skin was gently removed using an electric shaver. Skin fibrosis was induced by daily subcutaneous injection of 100 µL of 0.5 U bleomycin (Nippon Kayaku, Tokyo, Japan) into the same region of the skin for three consecutive weeks. A total of 25 mice were randomly allocated to 5 groups using a computer-generated random number sequence (Microsoft Excel, RAND function), with 5 mice per group. The groups were as follows: (1) the control group, in which the mice received daily intradermal injections of 100 µL of sterile saline; (2) the model group, in which the mice received daily intradermal injections of 100 µL of bleomycin (BLM) solution; and (3–5) the treatment groups, in which the mice received intradermal injections of 100 µL of BLM mixed with Tod (at concentrations of 10, 20, and 40 µM). BLM and Tod, alone or in combination, were dissolved in sterile saline. After 21 days of continuous injection, the mice were euthanized by intraperitoneal injection of sodium pentobarbital, and the dorsal skin was harvested for analysis of pathological changes in the skin.


### 2.9. Keloid tissue xenograft murine model
  Keloid tissue samples were cut into 3 × 3 × 3 mm fragments (weighing between 0.08 and 0.1 g). The weights of the fragments were recorded, and the samples were stored in sterile complete culture medium for transplantation within 24 hours. For transplantation, 25 female BALB/c nude mice, aged 10 weeks, were randomly allocated to 5 groups using a computer-generated random number sequence (Microsoft Excel, RAND function). The mice were anaesthetized with sodium pentobarbital (0.8 mg per 10 g body weight). A 0.5-mm incision was made on the dorsal skin of the mouse using surgical scissors, and the preweighed keloid tissue was inserted into the incision, followed by closure with absorbable sutures. After 14 days, the transplant tissues were firmly fixed. Once the keloid tissue stabilized, the mice were treated with sterile saline, triamcinolone acetonide (TA) or Tod at different concentrations (10, 20, and 40 µM) directly into the keloid tissue at the transplantation site. TA was dissolved in a solution of 5% DMSO in sterile saline (v/v). Tod was dissolved in sterile saline. The injections were administered daily for two weeks. A control group of mice received intratumoral injections of sterile saline as a blank control. At the end of the two-week treatment period, the mice were euthanized by intraperitoneal injection of sodium pentobarbital. Keloid tissue from the dorsal skin was then excised, weighed, and subjected to further histological and gene expression analyses.


### 2.10. Keloid sample collection, ethics approval, and informed consent
  Keloid samples were obtained from three patients at the Department of Dermatology, Tianjin Academy of Traditional Chinese Medicine Affiliated Hospital, on April 6, 2024. The detailed information is as follows: a 41-year-old female patient with a keloid on the lower abdomen; a 62-year-old male patient with a keloid on the right face; and a 34-year-old male patient with a keloid on the right ear. All the participants in our research signed an informed consent before the study, and all the experimental protocols performed on humans complied with the Declaration of Helsinki; the study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Nankai University (approval No. NKUIRB2021116).


### 2.11. Hydroxyproline (HYP) detection
  Equal amounts (10 mg) of mouse skin from the affected area were placed into ampoules and dried in an oven at 120 °C for 18 hours. A hydroxyproline standard solution was prepared. In labelled centrifuge tubes, 350 µL of H2O and 50 µL of the sample were added separately. For the standard, 400 µL was transferred into a new centrifuge tube. To each tube, 200 µL of chloramine T was added. The tubes were then incubated at room temperature for 20 minutes. Afterwards, 200 µL of perchloric acid was added to each tube, which was subsequently vortexed again and incubated at room temperature for 5 minutes. Finally, 200 µL of p-dimethylaminobenzaldehyde (P-DMAB) was added to each tube, vortexed, and incubated in a water bath at 50 °C for 20 minutes. After the solution in the tubes had cooled, 200 µL was transferred to a 96-well plate, with 2–3 replicates per sample. The absorbance was measured at 577 nm, and the hydroxyproline content in the tissue samples was calculated on the basis of the hydroxyproline standard curve.


### 2.12. Histologic analysis of skin sections
  Skin tissues were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 24 hours prior to being embedded in paraffin. Sections from the preserved tissues were subsequently embedded in paraffin for histological analysis, including haematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining and Masson's trichrome staining. Quantitative analysis of the stained sections was performed using ImageJ software.


### 2.13. Immunohistochemical staining
  Deparaffinized tissue sections were placed in a glass container for antigen retrieval. The sections were boiled for 2 minutes each, after which the retrieval solution was replenished promptly to compensate for evaporation during the process. An immunohistochemistry pen was used to outline the tissue margins. The sections were then blocked according to the manufacturer's instructions. Detection antibody diluted in 0.5% BSA (1:200) was applied to the tissue sections, which were then incubated overnight at 4 °C in a humidified chamber. Following antibody incubation, the sections were then counterstained with haematoxylin and blued in running water. The sections were subsequently dehydrated, cleared, and mounted with neutral gum for fixation.


### 2.14. Molecular docking
  The crystal structure of the target protein monoamine oxidase A (MAOA; ID: 4a7u) was downloaded from a protein database. The molecular structure of Tod was drawn using ChemBioDraw Ultra 14.0 and saved. The drawn molecular structure was then converted into a 3D format using ChemBio3D software. Molecular docking was performed using Autodock Tools-4.2.6, with the Local Search Parameters algorithm selected. The docking result with the highest binding energy was imported into PyMOL for visualization to identify the interaction sites between the drug and the target protein.


### 2.15. Cellular thermal shift assay
  When primary mouse dermal fibroblasts reached approximately 80% confluence, they were treated with 40 µM Tod. The cells were then collected and resuspended in RIPA lysis buffer. The cell suspension was divided into 10 RNA-free tubes and subjected to heat treatment at various temperatures (50, 55, 60, 65, 70, 75, 80, 85, and 90 °C) for 5 minutes each. After heating, the samples were placed in liquid nitrogen to induce lysis. The samples were then removed and allowed to thaw at room temperature before being refrozen in liquid nitrogen. This freeze‒thaw cycle was repeated three times. The lysates were subsequently centrifuged at 20,000 × g for 20 minutes at 4 °C. The cellular debris, which appeared as a white precipitate, was collected at the bottom of the centrifuge tubes, and the supernatant was retained. The protein concentration was determined by following the procedure used in the previous western blot experiment. The samples were then mixed with 5 × loading buffer, boiled at 100 °C for 5 min, and analysed by SDS‒PAGE. Western blotting was performed using an antibody against MAOA.


### 2.16. Cell transfection
  KFs were seeded into six-well plates and cultured for 12 hours or until the cell density reached 80%. Transfection was performed according to the manufacturer's instructions for the TransIT-X2 transfection reagent. First, the transfection complexes were prepared and incubated at room temperature for 15–30 minutes. After incubation, 250 µL of the mixture was added dropwise to different areas of the six-well plate containing serum-free DMEM. The plate was gently rocked to ensure an even distribution. After 24 hours of incubation, the medium was replaced, and the cells were treated with the desired agents for an additional 24 hours. Finally, the cells were collected, and the proteins were harvested for subsequent western blot analysis.


### 2.17. Statistical analysis
  The data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism 8.0 software. Differences among the control, model, and Tod-treated groups were assessed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). Normality and homogeneity of variance were first tested; upon satisfaction of these assumptions, one-way ANOVA was performed, including overall difference testing and multiple comparisons. When the assumption of homogeneity of variance was violated, Brown-Forsythe and Welch ANOVA tests were employed. The efficiency of MAOA knockdown by different siRNAs was evaluated using the t-test, which was conducted when normality and homogeneity of variance assumptions were met; otherwise, nonparametric tests were applied. Results are presented as the means ± standard deviations (SDs), and P < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance. The grayscale intensities of western blot bands and positive signal intensities of immunohistochemistry were quantitatively analyzed using ImageJ software. Epidermal thickness of mouse skin was measured using Image-Pro Plus software. Quantitative analyses of dermal thickness, collagen content, and immunohistochemistry were performed by an investigator who was blinded to the treatment groups.


## 3. Results

### 3.1. Tod inhibited the TGF-β-induced activation of PSFs through the TGF-β1/Smad2/3 and non-Smad signalling pathways
  To elucidate the role of Tod in the activation of fibroblasts and its potential influence on skin fibrosis, we initially established the optimal concentration of Tod for use in proliferating skin fibroblasts (PSFs) via a cell viability assay (Fig 1A). Based on these results, concentrations of 10, 20, and 40 μM Tod were selected for subsequent experiments. We employed a wound healing assay to assess the effect of Tod on the migratory capacity of PSFs (Fig 1B-C). Compared with that in the TGF-β group, the migration of PSFs in the groups treated with Tod for 0, 6, 24, and 30 hours was significantly reduced. The mRNA expression levels of α-SMA in PSFs were subsequently measured following treatment with varying concentrations of Tod (Fig 1D). The results indicated that Tod inhibited α-SMA mRNA expression in a dose-dependent manner. To further determine the effect of Tod on the activation of PSFs, we used western blot analysis to measure the protein expression levels of key skin fibrosis biomarkers, including α-SMA, Fn, and Col1 (Fig 1E). The results demonstrated that Tod significantly decreased the protein expression levels of α-SMA, Fn, and Col1 (Fig 1F). We also explored whether the changes in α-SMA, Fn, and Col1 expression were associated with the activity of the TGF-β1 signalling pathway (Fig 1G). Our experiments revealed that Tod effectively suppressed the protein expression of P-ERK, P-AKT, and P-Smad2/3. These findings suggest that the TGF-β-Smad/non-Smad signalling pathway is likely involved in the development and progression of skin fibrosis [17]. TGF-β1 promoted the activation of PSFs, which was subsequently attenuated by Tod. Collectively, these results indicate that Tod mitigates TGF-β1-induced skin fibrosis by modulating the activity of the TGF-β-Smad/non-Smad signalling pathway.


### 3.2. Tod inhibited the activation of KF by the TGF-β1/Smad2/3 and non-Smad signalling pathways
  The TGF-β-induced activation model of proliferating skin fibroblasts (PSFs) has been extensively used to study skin fibrosis associated with complex autoimmune diseases, such as systemic sclerosis. To further explore the effects of Tod in other skin fibrosis conditions, particularly keloids, which are characterized by abnormal cutaneous wound healing, we conducted in vitro experiments using keloid fibroblasts (KFs).

  First, a cell viability assay was performed to determine the optimal concentrations of Tod for KFs (Fig 2A). Concentrations of 10, 20, and 40 μM were selected for subsequent experiments. A wound healing assay was subsequently used to evaluate the impact of Tod on the migratory capacity of KFs (Fig 2B-C). The results showed that compared with the control treatment, Tod treatment significantly reduced the migration of KFs at 0, 6, and 24 hours. The mRNA expression levels of Fn and Col1 in KFs were subsequently measured following treatment with different concentrations of Tod (Fig 2D). These findings indicated that Tod inhibited the mRNA expression of Fn and Col1 in a dose-dependent manner. Western blot analysis was used to measure the protein expression levels of α-SMA, Fn, and Col1 (Fig 2E). The results demonstrated that Tod significantly decreased the protein expression levels of α-SMA, Fn, and Col1 (Fig 2F). Additionally, we examined whether the observed changes in α-SMA, Fn, and Col1 expression were associated with the activity of the TGF-β1 signalling pathway in KFs. The results revealed that Tod effectively suppressed the protein expression of P-ERK, P-AKT, and P-Smad2/3 (Fig 2G). These findings suggest that Tod attenuates TGF-β1-induced skin fibrosis by modulating the activity of the TGF-β-Smad/non-Smad signalling pathway in KFs.


### 3.3. Tod alleviated BLM-induced skin fibrosis in a murine model by targeting the TGF-β/Smad2/3 and non-Smad signalling pathways
  To further investigate the effects of Tod on skin fibrosis, a BLM-induced skin fibrosis model was utilized. The mice were continuously injected with NaCl, BLM, or BLM combined with Tod at concentrations of 10, 20, and 40 μM for 21 days. H&E and Masson's trichrome staining (Fig 3A) revealed that collagen deposition was mitigated by Tod treatment (Fig 3B), and the thickness of the dermis was significantly reduced in a dose-dependent manner following Tod treatment (Fig 3C). Consistently, Tod significantly decreased the BLM-induced increase in the level of hydroxyproline, an indicator of fibrosis severity in the skin (Fig 3D). Furthermore, immunohistochemical analysis revealed that Tod significantly reduced the expression levels of α-SMA, Col1, and Fn in skin tissues compared with the model group (Fig 3E and 3F). Additionally, RT‒PCR analysis revealed that Tod downregulated the mRNA expression of α-SMA, Col1, and Fn (Fig 3G). Moreover, western blot analysis revealed that Tod dose-dependently decreased the protein levels of Col1, Fn, P-ERK, P-AKT, and P-Smad2/3 in skin sections from the BLM-induced model mice (Fig 3H and 3I). These findings suggest that Tod alleviates BLM-induced skin fibrosis in a murine model by targeting the TGF-β1/Smad2/3 and non-Smad signalling pathways.


### 3.4. Tod attenuated keloids in a patient-derived xenograft murine model
  To assess the efficacy of Tod in a humanized animal model, we developed a keloid tissue xenograft murine model. This model is particularly suitable for studying specific human skin fibrosis, as it contains a substantial number of human cells and preserves the interactions between epidermal and dermal cells. After two weeks of intralesional injections of NaCl, triamcinolone acetonide (TA), or Tod at various concentrations, the transplanted keloid tissues were excised for analysis (Fig 4A). The weights of the Tod-treated tissues were significantly lower than those of the control group (Fig 4B). Histological analysis using H&E and Masson's trichrome staining demonstrated that collagen deposition in skin tissue was attenuated by Tod treatment (Fig 4C and 4E). Immunohistochemical staining revealed that the expression of skin fibrosis biomarkers, including α-SMA, Col 1, and Fn, was significantly inhibited by Tod treatment (Fig 4D–4H). qPCR analysis further revealed that Tod dose-dependently decreased the expression of α-SMA, Col 1, and Fn in keloid xenograft tissues (Fig 4I). Collectively, these findings suggest that Tod significantly promotes the regression of xenografted keloid tissues by reducing the expression of extracellular matrix-related genes.


### 3.5. Tod suppressed the activity of the TGF-β1/Smad2/3 and non-Smad signalling pathways by regulating MAOA in PSFs
  Through the aforementioned results, we demonstrated that Tod can modulate the activation of skin fibroblasts by inhibiting the activation of the TGF-β/Smad and non-Smad signalling pathways, thereby alleviating skin fibrosis. To further elucidate the mechanism of action of Tod, we used molecular docking to evaluate the potential targets of Tod on the basis of the compound structure and identified monoamine oxidase A (MAOA) as a potential target. The binding affinity between Tod and the MAOA protein revealed that Tod and MAOA interact through hydrogen bonds and hydrophobic interactions, with a binding energy coefficient of −7.5, indicating a strong binding affinity between Tod and the target protein. Specifically, Tod exhibited hydrophobic interactions with the amino acid LEU-144 and hydrogen bonds with ASP-11, ALA-55, and CYS-57 (Fig 5A). Therefore, we hypothesize that MAOA is the potential target through which Tod inhibits the activation of fibroblasts. The basis for determining the affinity of a ligand for its protein target is to examine changes in protein stability. Given that the interaction between a ligand and its target protein enhances the protein’s thermal resistance, a cellular thermal shift assay (CETSA) was performed to reexamine the binding between Tod and MAOA. The CETSA results based on PSFs demonstrated that Tod significantly enhanced the thermal stability of MAOA, which was consistent with the molecular docking predictions. (Fig 5B and 5C).

  To investigate whether Tod inhibits the activation of PSFs by regulating MAOA, we used three types of siRNAs to knockdown MAOA expression (Fig 5D) and selected the most effective siRNA, MAOA-1, for subsequent experiments. Western blot analysis revealed that MAOA knockdown significantly reduced the protein levels of α-SMA, Col 1, and Fn in PSFs (Fig 5E and 5F). Furthermore, the results indicated that si-MAOA treatment markedly suppressed the expression of P-AKT, P-Smad2/Smad3, and P-ERK, thereby inhibiting the activation of the TGF-β1/Smad2/3 and non-Smad signalling pathways (Fig 5G and 5H). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that Tod suppresses the activity of the TGF-β1/Smad2/3 and non-Smad signalling pathways in PSFs by regulating MAOA.


### 3.6. Tod suppressed the activity of the TGF-β1/Smad2/3 and non-Smad signalling pathways by regulating MAOA in KFs
  CETSA was also employed to reexamine the binding relationship between Tod and MAOA in KFs. The results confirmed that Tod significantly enhanced the thermal stability of MAOA (Figs 6A and 6B). To further investigate the role of MAOA in Tod-mediated signalling, three types of siRNAs were used to knockdown MAOA expression (Fig 6C), and the most effective siRNA was selected for subsequent experiments. Western blot analysis revealed that MAOA knockdown significantly reduced the protein levels of α-SMA, Col 1, and Fn in KFs (Fig 6D and 6E). Additionally, si-MAOA treatment markedly suppressed the expression of P-AKT, P-Smad2/Smad3, and P-ERK, thereby inhibiting the activation of the TGF-β1/Smad2/3 and non-Smad signalling pathways (Fig 6F and 6G). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that Tod suppresses the activity of the TGF-β1/Smad2/3 and non-Smad signalling pathways in KFs by regulating MAOA.


## 4. Discussion
  Skin fibrosis is the excessive deposition of extracellular matrix in the dermis [18]. The results of the present study demonstrated that Tod alleviates skin fibrosis and inhibits the excessive deposition of extracellular matrix by suppressing the activity of the TGF-β/Smad and non-Smad signalling pathways through the inhibition of MAOA, indicating that MAOA may serve as a viable therapeutic target for the prevention and treatment of skin fibrosis.

  Fibroblasts are highly dynamic cells that play a central role in skin fibrosis [19]. In this study, we found that Tod treatment decreased the expression of α-SMA, Col1, and Fn. Skin fibrosis typically follows microvascular and endothelial damage, which leads to the activation of fibroblasts and their transformation into profibrotic myofibroblasts [20]. α-SMA is a hallmark protein of myofibroblasts, and its increased expression is an important indicator of fibroblast activation and transformation. Upon phenotypic transition, myofibroblasts acquire an enhanced ability to synthesize and accumulate extracellular matrix (ECM) proteins, such as Col1 and Fn, initiating an aberrant wound healing process that contributes to fibrogenesis and ultimately leads to skin fibrosis [21]. The inhibition of the increased expression of α-SMA, Col1, and Fn by Tod suggests that it can modulate the process through which fibroblasts transform into myofibroblasts with enhanced contractility and synthetic capacity.

  Under conditions of skin fibrosis, TGF-β is a pivotal factor in the development of fibrotic diseases [22]. Targeting the TGF-β/Smad and non-Smad signalling pathways represents a promising therapeutic strategy [23]. In the present study, we demonstrated that Tod effectively inhibited the upregulation of P-Smad2/Smad3, P-ERK, and P-AKT in both in vivo and in vitro models of skin fibrosis. These findings suggest that Tod regulates the activity of the TGF-β/Smad and non-Smad signalling pathways in skin fibrosis.

  The results of the molecular docking experiments presented in this study demonstrate that inhibiting MAOA with Tod can mitigate skin fibrosis and that knocking down MAOA in both PSFs and KFs can inhibit the increase in the expression levels of α-SMA, Col1, and Fn; however, the precise pathogenesis underlying this effect remains to be elucidated. MAOA is an enzyme located in the mitochondrial outer membrane and is involved primarily in the degradation of biogenic amines, including serotonin, norepinephrine, and epinephrine [24]. To date, MAOA activity has been implicated in various neuropsychiatric conditions, such as anxiety, panic disorder, impulsivity, autism, and antisocial behaviour in humans [25,26]. However, research on the role of MAOA in skin fibrosis is limited. During the oxidative degradation of catecholamines and serotonin, MAO-A participates in the production of hydrogen peroxide, thereby promoting oxidative stress under pathological conditions. Studies have shown that MAO-A inhibitors ameliorate diabetes-induced cardiac fibrosis in rats by suppressing oxidative stress [27]. Given that oxidative stress is one of the pathogenic mechanisms underlying skin fibrosis [28,29], it is suggested that inhibiting MAO-A-mediated oxidative stress may represent a novel and promising therapeutic strategy for skin fibrosis. Based on these observations, we hypothesize that the efficacy of Tod in alleviating skin fibrosis may be associated with its impact on oxidative stress in skin fibroblasts via MAOA, and further investigations are currently underway.


## 5. Conclusion
  In summary, Tod was shown to effectively suppress the activation of skin fibroblasts and mitigate skin fibrosis by modulating the activity of the TGF-β1/Smad and non-Smad signalling pathways via the regulation of MAOA. Collectively, the findings underscore the therapeutic potential of Tod for skin fibrosis disorders and provide a robust scientific rationale for its clinical application in the prevention and treatment of skin fibrosis.
