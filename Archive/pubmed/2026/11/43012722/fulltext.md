20(S)-Protopanaxadiol (PPD), a major intestinal metabolite of ginsenosides from Panax ginseng, possesses anti-inflammatory properties, yet its direct molecular targets are poorly defined. This study investigated the protective effects of PPD against acute lung injury (ALI) and its potential mechanism. Using LPS-challenged immortalized mouse bone-marrow-derived macrophages (iBMDMs) and ALI mouse models, limited proteolysis-mass spectrometry identified HMGB1 as the primary binding target. PPD blocked inflammasome activation and interrupted HMGB1-TLR4 interaction. Molecular docking, dynamics simulations, and site mutagenesis verified that PPD stably binds the HMGB1 B-box domain. In vivo, PPD mitigated pulmonary damage, alleviated alveolar edema and inflammatory infiltration, lowered proinflammatory cytokines, and restrained HMGB1 secretion. In conclusion, PPD directly targets HMGB1 to block HMGB1-TLR4 inflammatory signaling. These data suggest PPD can serve as a promising functional food constituent for ALI prevention.
