# Identification of Endoplasmic reticulum Homeostasis-associated diagnostic biomarkers for Sarcopenia through integrated Bioinformatics and Experimental Validation


## Abstract
  Sarcopenia is an age-related skeletal muscle disorder characterized by progressive loss of muscle mass and function, yet reliable biomarkers for early diagnosis remain limited. Emerging evidence suggests that disruption of endoplasmic reticulum (ER) homeostasis contributes to skeletal muscle aging, however, the diagnostic relevance of ER stress- and reticulophagy-related alterations in sarcopenia remains poorly understood. In this study, three skeletal muscle transcriptomic datasets (GSE25941, GSE28422, and GSE38718) were integrated following batch-effect correction to identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between sarcopenic and control samples. DEGs were intersected with ER stress and reticulophagy related gene sets to obtain sarcopenia-associated candidate genes. Subsequently, three machine-learning algorithms, including least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO), random forest (RF), and support vector machine (SVM), were applied to identify key hub genes. Functional enrichment analyses, transcription factor (TF)-miRNA regulatory network construction, protein-chemical interaction analyses, external validation, nomogram development, and quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) verification were performed. A total of 236 DEGs were identified, comprising 34 upregulated and 202 downregulated genes. Intersection analysis with 1,772 ER stress and reticulophagy related genes yielded 10 candidate genes, from which five hub genes (ACTC1, NPY, CDKN2B, GADD45G, and GRIA1) were consistently identified by machine-learning analyses. Functional enrichment analysis indicated significant involvement in extracellular matrix organization, cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, mitochondrial translation, and respiratory electron transport pathways. Regulatory network analyses further revealed potential interactions with multiple transcription factors, miRNAs, and chemical compounds. In the independent validation cohort (GSE1428), ACTC1 showed strong individual discriminative performance, NPY and GADD45G showed moderate performance, whereas GRIA1 showed limited discrimination and CDKN2B showed near-chance individual performance, whereas the combined gene signature exhibited improved discriminative ability. A predictive nomogram was established. Moreover, qRT-PCR analysis of human skeletal muscle biopsy specimens confirmed significantly increased expression of ACTC1, NPY, CDKN2B, and GRIA1, together with decreased expression of GADD45G in sarcopenic muscle tissues compared with controls (all p < 0.05). Collectively, our findings identified a five-gene signature associated with ER stress and reticulophagy related molecular networks in sarcopenia. Although these genes are not established core regulators of ER stress or reticulophagy, they exhibited promising diagnostic value and may reflect ER homeostasis-related molecular alterations during skeletal muscle aging, providing novel biomarkers and potential insights into the molecular basis of sarcopenia.


## Introduction
  Sarcopenia is defined as the progressive, age-related loss of skeletal muscle mass and function. It constitutes a substantial public health burden, markedly increasing the risk of falls, frailty and premature death in older populations (Cruz-Jentoft & Sayer, 2019). It is estimated that sarcopenia affects 10–16% of the elderly population globally (Petermann-Rocha et al., 2022). However, the field is currently limited by diagnostic approaches that primarily identify muscle loss after clinically appreciable deterioration has occurred, such as assessments of muscle mass, grip strength, and gait speed (Coletta & Phillips, 2023; Ooi & Welch, 2024). It is also constrained by the lack of widely approved disease-modifying pharmacological therapies specifically indicated for sarcopenia (Bahat & Ozkok, 2024). The urgent and pressing priority of current research is to fully elucidate the molecular mechanisms of sarcopenia and identify specific biomarkers. The pathophysiology of sarcopenia defies simple explanation. Hormonal decline, chronic inflammation, and physical inactivity have been identified as contributing factors (Shur et al., 2021; Marmondi et al., 2025). However, these factors alone do not fully account for the precipitous atrophy observed in aged muscle. A mounting body of evidence has emerged that identifies the disruption of intracellular proteostasis as a convergent mechanism (Gallot & Bohnert, 2021).

  The endoplasmic reticulum (ER) is the main organelle responsible for protein folding and calcium homeostasis. Aging disrupts ER homeostasis by reducing the efficiency of protein quality-control systems, thereby promoting the accumulation of aberrant proteins and chronic activation of ER stress responses (Bohnert, McMillan & Kumar, 2018; Paez, Pitzer & Alway, 2023). This stress exerts a bidirectional destructive effect on the body. On the one hand, it activates catabolic signals and apoptosis pathways, accelerating muscle fiber loss. On the other hand, it induces synthetic metabolic resistance, a persistent state in which aged muscle cannot produce a protein synthesis response to ingested amino acids (Zhang et al., 2018; Tang et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2020). Compounding this insult, age-associated mitochondrial dysfunction establishes positive-feedback loops that amplify ER stress, perpetuating a self-sustaining cycle of organelle failure and proteolytic activation (Aragon, Tipton & Schoenfeld, 2023). Although increasing evidence supports a role for ER stress in skeletal muscle aging, the regulatory mechanisms that maintain ER homeostasis under chronic stress conditions remain poorly characterized.

  Among these mechanisms, reticulophagy (ER-phagy), a selective form of autophagy that mediates the degradation of damaged or excess ER fragments, has emerged as a critical pathway for preserving ER integrity and proteostasis. By eliminating dysfunctional ER components, reticulophagy helps alleviate excessive ER stress and restore cellular homeostasis, however, its contribution to skeletal muscle aging and sarcopenia remains largely unexplored (Chino & Mizushima, 2023; Wilson & McCormick, 2025).

  Current diagnostic practice offers no window into these subcellular events. High-throughput transcriptomics, coupled with machine-learning-based feature selection, now provide a means to identify molecular signatures of ER dysfunction that precede clinical manifestation (Ran et al., 2025; Deng et al., 2026). In this study, we integrated three independent transcriptomic datasets, and intersected differentially expressed genes with a curated compendium of ER-related genes and applied multiple machine-learning algorithms for robust feature selection. The resulting composite model was externally validated and functionally interrogated through enrichment analysis and regulatory network construction, with experimental corroboration by quantitative real-time PCR. These findings deepen our understanding of the molecular mechanisms of sarcopenia and point to potential biomarkers for its diagnosis and management.


## Materials & Methods

### Data standardization and preprocessing
  Gene expression datasets were retrieved from the gene expression omnibus (GEO) (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) database using the keyword “skeletal muscle”, “muscle aging”, “aged”, “elderly”, and “sarcopenia”. Datasets were subsequently screened manually according to the following criteria: (1) human skeletal muscle tissue samples; (2) inclusion of aged, elderly, or sarcopenic subjects and healthy controls; (3) available gene expression matrices; and (4) sufficient sample information.

  Following manual screening, GSE38718 (Liu et al., 2013), GSE25941, and GSE28422 (Raue et al., 2012) were selected as training datasets, while GSE1428 (Giresi et al., 2005) was used as an external validation dataset. Two researchers independently screened and cross-checked all retrieved datasets. The characteristics of the datasets are detailed in Table 1.

  Because the public cohorts were not uniformly classified using contemporary Asian working group for sarcopenia (AWGS) or European working group on sarcopenia in older people (EWGSOP) diagnostic criteria, the case labels followed the phenotype definitions provided in the original studies. Where formal clinical sarcopenia criteria were unavailable, the case group should be interpreted as representing an aging-related muscle loss or sarcopenia-related phenotype rather than clinically adjudicated sarcopenia.

  All datasets were preprocessed and normalized before analysis. We merged GSE38718, GSE25941, and GSE28422 with the R package “inSilicoMerging” (v1.2.0) (Taminau et al., 2012) and adjusted for batch effects using ComBat from the “sva” package (v3.50.0) (Johnson, Li & Rabinovic, 2007). The resulting training set included 44 control samples and 41 samples with sarcopenia-related or aging-related muscle phenotypes, while GSE1428 was held out as the validation set (10 control samples and 12 phenotype samples). From the GeneCards database (https://www.genecards.org/), we retrieved 12,948 protein-coding genes linked to “ER-Stress” and “reticulophagy”. Following removal of duplicate genes, only genes with a Relevance Score > 5 were retained to prioritize genes with stronger database-supported associations and reduce low-confidence entries, resulting in a final set of 1,772 genes. The gene list is provided in Table S1.


### Identification of differentially expressed genes (DEGs)
  Transcriptomic differences between control and sarcopenia groups in the training set were quantified using the “limma” package (v3.52.2) (Ritchie et al., 2015). Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were defined by the criteria: —log2FC— > 0.58 and p.adj < 0.05. Resulting DEGs were visualized as volcano plots and clustered heatmaps using ggplot2 (v3.4.4) (Gustavsson et al., 2022) and ComplexHeatmap package (v2.13.1) (Gu, 2022). The gene list is provided in Table S2.

  BOCHKIS_FOXA2_TARGETS.v2026.1.Hs,

  BUYTAERT_PHOTODYNAMIC_THERAPY_STRESS_DN.v2026.1.Hs,

  FAN_OVARY_CL0_XBP1_SELK_HIGH_STROMAL_CELL.v2026.1.Hs,

  Input comprised a ranked gene list sorted by signed differential expression log2FC. Enrichment significance required p.adj < 0.05, FDR < 0.05, and —NES— > 1.5. Top-ranking pathways were plotted with ggplot2 (v3.4.4).


### Gene set variation analysis (GSVA)
  The assessment of single-sample pathway enrichment was facilitated by the implementation of GSVA (Hänzelmann, Castelo & Guinney, 2013) within the GSVA package (v1.50.0), thereby enabling the identification of sample-specific activity alterations associated with sarcopenia. The reference collection, designated c2.cp.v7.4.symbols.gmt, was retrieved from MSigDB (accessed 10 May 2026). The classification of a pathway as significantly altered was determined by the fulfilment of the following criteria: the —t-score— must exceed 4 and the p.adj < 0.05. The resulting top 10 pathways, both positively and negatively enriched, were then plotted in the form of a bar chart.


### Identification and functional enrichment analysis of Sa-ER RGs
  A Venn overlap analysis was performed using the VennDiagram package (version 1.7.3) (Chen & Boutros, 2011) to identify genes that were present in the DEGs, ER stress and reticulophagy related gene. These intersecting genes were labelled “Sarcopenia-Endoplasmic Reticulum stress and reticulophagy related Genes (Sa-ER RGs)”. To optimise candidate selection and guard against elevated false discovery rates, we assessed their expression in the training cohort using Wilcoxon test (Li et al., 2022).

  Functional and pathway enrichment analyses were performed with clusterProfiler (v4.10.0), covering GO and KEGG categories. Significance was set at adjusted p < 0.05.


### Screening for hub genes using machine learning
  In order to distil a robust diagnostic gene signature for sarcopenia, three distinct machine learning strategies were integrated. Least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression (glmnet v4.1.10) (Engebretsen & Bohlin, 2019) was applied for penalized feature selection under 10-fold cross-validation, with the objective of optimising both predictive performance and interpretability. The random forest (RF) (“randomForest” v4.7.1.2) (Wang et al., 2023) ensemble classifier was deployed in order to circumvent the overfitting of decision trees and enhance the generalisability of the results. Support vector machine (SVM) (“e1071” v1.7.16) was employed with 10-fold cross-validation, capitalising on its suitability for high-dimensional, non-linear classification tasks. Hub genes that achieved consensus across all three analytical frameworks were retained as high-confidence diagnostic candidates. The chromosomal architecture of these samples was subsequently visualised using “Circlize” R package (v0.4.16) (Gu et al., 2014).


### TF-miRNA and hub gene interactome construction
  To analyse the regulatory landscape upstream of the core genes, we used NetworkAnalyst (https://www.networkanalyst.ca/), to infer their putative transcription factors (TFs) and microRNAs (miRNAs). We visualised the resulting TF–miRNA–gene regulatory and protein–chemical interaction networks in Cytoscape (v3.10.4). The CytoNCA plugin (Tang et al., 2015) was used to quantify degree centrality (DC), betweenness centrality (BC), closeness centrality (CC), and eigenvector centrality (EC). All nodes in the TF-miRNA-gene network were included in a single topological screening step. Core nodes were defined using the criteria Degree > 2 and BC, CC, and EC values exceeding their respective medians; the Degree > 2 threshold was used to exclude nodes with limited network connectivity.


### Construction of a gene-gene interaction network of hub genes
  To map the functional interactome of the hub genes, we used GeneMANIA (Franz et al., 2018) (https://genemania.org/, accessed 15 May 2026) to identify biologically relevant network partners. This platform uses multi-omics evidence to extend query-based networks, enabling the construction of an expanded gene interaction map for functional interpretation.


### Construction and validation of a diagnostic model
  The diagnostic efficacy of the hub genes was evaluated at three levels: single-gene performance, composite model construction and independent validation in GSE1428. Discrimination was quantified by the area under the curve (AUC) (95% confidence interval (CI)) from receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis (“pROC” v1.18.0) (Robin et al., 2011), with visualisation using the “ggplot2” package. An AUC greater than 0.7 was considered clinically relevant (Mandrekar, 2010). Calibration was examined through predicted versus observed probability plots (“rms” v6.4.0; “ResourceSelection” v0.3-5), and net benefit was examined through DCA (“rmda” v1.6). A clinical nomogram was also developed (“rms” v6.4.0).


### Clinical samples collection
  Vastus lateralis muscle specimens were collected from patients undergoing hip surgery. The sarcopenia group consisted of 6 patients (mean age, 75.3 ± 11.6 years), whereas the non-sarcopenic control group included 6 patients (mean age, 43.7  ± 12.5 years). Diagnostic classification followed the 2019 AWGS guidelines (Chen et al., 2020). Tissue processing occurred within 15 min of excision: formalin fixation (4%) for histopathology and cryopreservation in liquid nitrogen at −80 °C for molecular analyses. The Hubei Provincial Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine provided ethical oversight (approval HBZY-2024-C102-02, dated January 23, 2025), and all participants provided written informed consent.


### Quantitative Real-Time PCR (qRT-PCR) analysis of hub genes
  The RNA was purified from frozen muscle samples using RNA extraction kit (TSP413, Tsingke Biotech, Beijing, China), with quality control by micro-spectrophotometry. The reverse transcription process was carried out utilizing RNA using the SynScript® III RT SuperMix for qPCR kit, while the quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) amplification was conducted employing ArtiCanATM SYBR qPCR Mix kit (TSE501, Tsingke Biotech, China). GAPDH was employed as the reference gene, and relative expression levels were determined by the 2−ΔΔCT method. The primer sequences are presented in Table 2. The raw data can be found in Table S3.


### Statistical analysis
  Statistical analyses were performed using R (version 4.4.2). Two-tailed t-tests and Wilcoxon rank-sum tests were used for two-group comparisons, depending on the distribution of the data. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with Benjamini–Hochberg correction was applied to multi-group analyses. Pearson’s correlation coefficients were used to assess linear relationships between continuous variables. Statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05.


## Results

### Data standardization and preprocessing
  The workflow of this study was shown in Fig. 1.

  Preliminary quality control revealed heterogeneity among different independent cohorts in the three publicly available skeletal muscle microarray datasets (GSE25941, GSE28422, and GSE38718) in terms of gene coverage and expression dynamics (Fig. 2A).

  Following batch correction, the expression distributions of the three datasets were aligned, with the medians falling on the same level (Fig. 2B). Uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP) visualization demonstrated clear dataset-specific clustering before batch correction (Fig. 2C). After correction, samples from the three datasets were intermingled, with no dataset-driven separation observed, indicating that batch effects were effectively eliminated (Fig. 2D). Density plots showed distinct expression distributions before correction (Fig. 2E), whereas the distributions became comparable after correction, with similar means and variances (Fig. 2F).


### Gene set enrichment and variation analysis of the training set
  GSEA identified three positively enriched pathways: Classic Pathway (NES = 2.128), ECM Glycoproteins (NES = 1.968), and Core Matrisome (NES = 1.962) (Figs. 3A–3C). Three pathways were negatively enriched: Respiratory Electron Transport (NES = −2.609), Mitochondrial Translation (NES = −2.601), and the Citric Acid TCA Cycle and Respiratory Electron Transport (NES = −2.571) (Figs. 3D–3F, Table S4). GSVA revealed that pathways with positive t-scores included metabolic reprogramming in colon cancer, Parkinson’s disease, Huntington’s disease, mitochondrial translation, the electron transport chain, the oxidative phosphorylation system, complex I biogenesis, nonalcoholic fatty liver disease, mitochondrial calcium uptake, the mitochondrial complex I assembly model. Pathways with negative t-scores included FGFR2 ligand binding and activation, C3 and C5 activation, Runx3-regulated CDKN1A transcription, phase 1 fast channel inactivation, zinc homeostasis, complement activation, and the metabolism of ingested SeMet Sec Mesic into H2Se (Fig. 3G). Custom gene set enrichment analysis revealed negative enrichment trends for photodynamic therapy stress response, RAB pathway, and B-cell receptor signaling pathway, whereas EIF2 signaling pathway showed weak positive enrichment. None of these four custom gene sets reached statistical significance (Fig. S1).


### Identification and functional characterization of Sa-ER RGs
  A total of 236 DEGs were identified, comprising 34 upregulated and 202 downregulated genes (Fig. 4A). The expression patterns of the top 25 DEGs in each category are displayed in a heatmap (Fig. 4B). Cross-referencing the 236 DEGs with 1,772 ER stress- and reticulophagy-related genes identified 10 Sa-ER RGs (Fig. 4C). All Sa-ER RGs showed significant differences between the control and sarcopenia groups (p < 0.05) (Fig. 4D). GO enrichment of the Sa-ER RGs revealed significant enrichment in activation of signaling receptor activity, response to external stimulus, regulation of receptor activity, collagen-containing extracellular matrix, extracellular matrix, heparin binding, receptor regulator activity, receptor ligand activity, cytokine activity, and regulation of peptidyl-tyrosine phosphorylation (Fig. 4E). KEGG enrichment of the Sa-ER RGs showed several relevant pathways, such as cytokine–cytokine receptor interaction, viral protein interaction with cytokine and cytokine receptor, pertussis, complement, coagulation cascades, cell adhesion molecules, prion diseases, nicotine addiction, the cAMP signaling pathway, vascular smooth muscle contraction, and amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (Fig. 4F).


### Machine learning algorithm-based feature hub genes selection
  LASSO logistic regression with ten-fold cross-validation was used to optimise the Sa-ER gene cluster, and nine key genes were identified (λ.min = 0.023) (Figs. 5A–5B), The Random Forest algorithm identified six feature genes of relative importance by MeanDecreaseGini > 3.5 (Fig. 5C). The 10-fold cross-validation curve in the SVM analysis showed a minimum error rate of 0.11 with 10 features. This indicates that the optimal feature set corresponds to 10 genes (Fig. 5D). The integration of these three algorithms resulted in the identification of five hub genes, namely neuropeptide Y (NPY), Cyclin Dependent Kinase Inhibitor 2B (CDKN2B), Growth Arrest and DNA Damage-inducible 45 Gamma (GADD45G), glutamate ionotropic receptor AMPA type subunit 1 (GRIA1), and actin alpha cardiac muscle 1 (ACTC1) (Fig. 5E). Chromosomal localization of the hub genes was visualized. Specifically, GRIA1 localized to chromosome 5, NPY to chromosome 7, CDKN2B and GADD45G both mapped to chromosome 9 in close proximity, and ACTC1 localized to chromosome 15. The remaining core genes were distributed across other chromosomal regions (Fig. 5F).


### Integrated TF–miRNA-gene, protein–chemical, and gene–gene functional interaction networks of hub genes
  Topological network analysis was performed using the CytoNCA plugin, and hub genes were screened according to the criteria of Degree > 2 and BC, CC, and EC values exceeding their respective medians. The TF-miRNA-gene network consisted of 18 nodes and 28 edges. Four transcription factors were predicted to be associated with the hub genes, including TGFB induced factor homeobox 1 (TGIF1), interferon regulatory factor 1 (IRF1), Serum Response Factor (SRF), and POU class 2 homeobox 1 (POU2F1), as well as 10 miRNAs (hsa-miR-25, hsa-miR-32, hsa-miR-363, hsa-miR-367, hsa-miR-608, hsa-miR-876-3p, hsa-miR-92a, hsa-miR-92b, hsa-miR-140-5p, and hsa-miR-495) (Fig. 6A). Furthermore, the protein-chemical interaction network identified 17 chemicals with reported database associations with the hub genes, including 4-(5-benzo (1,3) dioxol-5-yl-4-pyridin-2-yl-1H-imidazol-2-yl) benzamide, Benzo(a)pyrene, dorsomorphin, Tretinoin, Valproic Acid, Triclosan, Cadmium Chloride, Doxorubicin, Estradiol, sodium arsenite, Aflatoxin B1, Carbamazepine, Sunitinib, Tobacco Smoke Pollution, Phenylmercuric Acetate, Resveratrol, and Diethylhexyl Phthalate (Fig. 6B). In addition, a gene-gene functional interaction network was constructed. Functional annotation indicated enrichment in plasma membrane signaling receptor complex, ionotropic glutamate receptor, complex/neurotransmitter receptor complex, integral component of plasma membrane, and transmembrane transporter complex categories (Fig. 6C).


### Evaluation of the diagnostic value of hub genes
  The diagnostic accuracy of the five hub genes was individually evaluated in the independent GSE1428 cohort. ROC analysis showed that ACTC1 had an AUC of 0.900 (95% CI [0.704–1.000]), NPY had an AUC of 0.725 (95% CI [0.507–0.943]), GADD45G had an AUC of 0.733 (95% CI [0.511–0.955]), GRIA1 had an AUC of 0.625 (95% CI [0.375–0.875]), and CDKN2B had an AUC of 0.508 (95% CI [0.251–0.766]). Thus, ACTC1 showed strong individual discrimination, NPY and GADD45G showed moderate discrimination, GRIA1 showed limited discrimination, and CDKN2B showed near-chance individual performance (Fig. S2).

  The combined ROC curve, calibration curve, and decision curve analysis provided preliminary evidence regarding discrimination, calibration, and potential net clinical benefit of the multi-gene signature (Figs. 7A–7C). A nomogram was constructed by integrating the scores assigned to each hub gene to quantify the risk score (Fig. 7D).


### Clinical validation of hub gene expression
  We next performed qRT-PCR experiments to validate the expression of hub genes in the muscle samples from patients with sarcopenia. The mRNA expression levels of NPY, CDKN2B, GRIA1, and ACTC1 were significantly upregulated compared with that of the control (p < 0.05), whereas GADD45G expression was significantly decreased (p < 0.05). The qRT-PCR results confirmed the differential expression patterns identified in the integrated transcriptomic datasets (Fig. 7E).


## Discussion
  Given its high prevalence and substantial impact on physical function, quality of life, and mortality in older adults, sarcopenia has become a major focus of aging research and biomarker discovery (Gustafsson & Ulfhake, 2024). Although mitochondrial dysfunction, chronic inflammation, impaired proteostasis, autophagy dysregulation, cellular senescence, and neuromuscular degeneration have been implicated in its pathogenesis, clinically applicable molecular biomarkers remain limited (Veronesi et al., 2024). In recent years, disruption of protein quality control has emerged as an important feature of skeletal muscle aging. ER stress and the unfolded protein response (UPR) have been increasingly recognized as key contributors to skeletal muscle atrophy by regulating protein folding, proteasomal degradation, autophagy, inflammation, and mitochondrial homeostasis (Marafon et al., 2022). Under physiological conditions, UPR activation is adaptive and restores ER homeostasis. However, prolonged or excessive ER stress may become maladaptive, promoting muscle catabolism and functional decline (Joshi et al., 2025).

  Reticulophagy, also termed ER phagy, is a selective autophagic process that removes damaged or excessive ER fragments and thereby contributes to ER quality control. Compared with ER stress and general autophagy, the role of reticulophagy in human sarcopenia remains poorly defined. Nevertheless, ER phagy is biologically relevant because it directly participates in ER turnover, proteostasis maintenance, and cellular adaptation to stress (Reggiori & Molinari, 2022; Mochida & Nakatogawa, 2022). Autophagy impairment has also been linked to aging phenotypes and skeletal muscle repair capacity (Chen et al., 2022). Therefore, screening sarcopenia-associated molecular signatures within ER stress and reticulophagy related gene networks may provide a rational strategy for biomarker discovery.

  In this study, three skeletal muscle transcriptomic datasets were integrated, and 236 DEGs were identified between sarcopenic and control samples. By intersecting these DEGs with ER stress and reticulophagy related genes, ten candidate genes were obtained. Subsequently, three machine learning algorithms identified five hub genes: ACTC1, NPY, CDKN2B, GADD45G, and GRIA1. Similar multi-step bioinformatics strategies have been used to identify disease-associated diagnostic gene signatures in recent studies. Importantly, these five genes are not canonical ER stress regulators, nor are they classical ER phagy receptors. Therefore, they should be interpreted as biomarkers associated with ER stress- and reticulophagy-related molecular networks rather than as direct mechanistic regulators of ER homeostasis or ER-phagy.

  Functional enrichment analyses provided biological context for these hub genes. ECM organization was one of the most prominent enriched processes. Age related skeletal muscle remodeling is accompanied by fibrosis, altered collagen deposition, impaired satellite cell activation, and reduced regenerative capacity. These alterations can compromise force transmission and contribute to progressive muscle dysfunction (Chen et al., 2021; Ge et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025a). The enrichment of ECM related pathways in this study therefore suggests that the identified gene signature may reflect structural remodeling and impaired repair in sarcopenic muscle.

  Cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction was also significantly enriched. Chronic low-grade inflammation, or inflammaging, is a key contributor to sarcopenia by promoting proteolysis, anabolic resistance, and impaired regeneration (Dalle, Rossmeislová & Koppo, 2017). Inflammatory signaling also intersects with ER stress pathways through NF-κB, JNK, and related stress-response cascades, suggesting a potential connection between ER homeostasis and inflammatory remodeling in aging muscle (Draganidis et al., 2021; Sharma et al., 2024). Although our data do not directly demonstrate activation of ER stress signaling, the enrichment of inflammatory processes supports the hypothesis that ER-associated molecular changes may be linked to the inflammatory microenvironment of sarcopenic muscle.

  Mitochondrial translation and respiratory electron transport pathways were also enriched. Mitochondrial dysfunction is a central hallmark of sarcopenia and contributes to reduced ATP production, excessive reactive oxygen species generation, and impaired muscle regeneration (Marzetti et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2025). The ER and mitochondria communicate through mitochondria-associated membranes, which regulate calcium signaling, energy metabolism, and stress responses. Disruption of ER homeostasis can impair mitochondrial function, while mitochondrial dysfunction can further amplify ER stress, forming a vicious cycle that promotes muscle degeneration (Ji et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025b).

  Among the five hub genes, ACTC1 was significantly upregulated in sarcopenic muscle and validated by qRT-PCR. ACTC1 encodes α-cardiac actin, a contractile protein that may be re-expressed during muscle injury, remodeling, and regeneration. Its upregulation may therefore reflect compensatory cytoskeletal remodeling in response to chronic structural stress rather than direct ER stress regulation. This interpretation is consistent with studies showing that muscle aging and atrophy involve substantial changes in contractile, cytoskeletal, and regenerative programs (Fernández-Lázaro et al., 2022; Malatesta & Cisterna, 2025).

  NPY was also significantly upregulated. NPY is a neuropeptide involved in energy balance, stress adaptation, appetite regulation, and neuroendocrine signaling. Sarcopenia is increasingly regarded as a systemic disorder involving metabolic, endocrine, neural, and inflammatory interactions rather than an isolated muscle disease (Sousa et al., 2023; Damanti et al., 2025). Thus, elevated NPY may reflect altered metabolic and neuroendocrine adaptation in aging muscle. However, direct evidence linking NPY to ER stress or reticulophagy in sarcopenia remains lacking, and this association should be interpreted cautiously.

  CDKN2B may be particularly relevant to aging-related muscle degeneration. CDKN2B encodes p15INK4B, a cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor involved in cell-cycle arrest and cellular senescence. Cellular senescence contributes to sarcopenia through impaired regenerative capacity, accumulation of dysfunctional satellite cells, and secretion of senescence-associated inflammatory factors (Xia et al., 2021). Persistent ER stress can also promote senescence-related signaling through stress-response pathways. Therefore, CDKN2B upregulation may indicate enhanced senescence activity in sarcopenic muscle and may represent a potential link between cellular stress responses and muscle aging. However, CDKN2B showed near-chance individual discrimination in the external validation cohort (AUC = 0.508); therefore, its relevance in the present study should be interpreted primarily as a biological association and as part of the multigene framework rather than as a standalone diagnostic marker.

  In contrast, GADD45G was significantly downregulated in sarcopenic samples. GADD45G participates in DNA damage response, cellular stress adaptation, growth arrest, and apoptosis regulation. Reduced GADD45G expression may indicate impaired stress-response capacity in aging skeletal muscle. Since proteostasis failure and reduced cellular resilience are key features of skeletal muscle aging, GADD45G downregulation may reflect diminished adaptation to chronic oxidative, metabolic, and proteotoxic stress (Xu & Liu, 2025).

  GRIA1 was another upregulated hub gene. GRIA1 encodes an AMPA type glutamate receptor subunit mainly involved in excitatory neurotransmission. Increasing evidence suggests that neuromuscular junction degeneration, denervation, and impaired neural-muscle communication are important contributors to sarcopenia (Chen et al., 2024). Therefore, GRIA1 upregulation may reflect adaptive or maladaptive changes in neuromuscular signaling during skeletal muscle aging, although its relationship with ER homeostasis remains unclear.

  The five-gene signature showed heterogeneous individual diagnostic performance. ACTC1 demonstrated strong discrimination, NPY and GADD45G showed moderate discrimination, whereas GRIA1 and particularly CDKN2B showed limited individual performance. The multigene framework may nevertheless capture complementary biological information across interconnected processes. The nomogram provides a preliminary framework for individualized prediction, and qRT-PCR validation in clinical muscle biopsies supports the direction of the transcriptomic findings; however, further validation in larger cohorts is required.

  Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this study was mainly based on retrospective public transcriptomic datasets, and the sample size remained limited. The public cohorts were not uniformly defined using contemporary clinical sarcopenia criteria, and aging-related muscle phenotypes may not be fully equivalent to clinically diagnosed sarcopenia. Second, the qRT-PCR validation cohort was small (n = 6 per group), and the sarcopenia and control groups differed substantially in age (75.3 ± 11.6 vs. 43.7 ± 12.5 years), which represents a potential confounder. In addition, skeletal-muscle transcriptomic profiles may vary according to sarcopenia subtype, comorbidities, and other patient-level driving factors. Third, external validation was performed in GSE1428 on the GPL96 platform, whereas the training datasets were generated on GPL570. Despite platform-specific preprocessing and gene-symbol matching, residual cross-platform effects cannot be excluded. Fourth, the GeneCards Relevance Score > 5 threshold was used as a pragmatic filtering criterion without a formal sensitivity analysis across alternative cutoffs, and this choice may influence the composition of the candidate gene set. Fifth, this study demonstrates associations rather than causality. Whether ACTC1, NPY, CDKN2B, GADD45G, and GRIA1 directly contribute to muscle degeneration or merely represent downstream consequences requires experimental validation. Sixth, none of the five genes are currently recognized as canonical ER stress or ER phagy regulators. Therefore, their mechanistic relationship with ER homeostasis must be tested in cellular and animal models. Finally, transcriptomic changes do not necessarily reflect protein abundance or functional activity, and future studies integrating proteomics, single-cell transcriptomics, spatial transcriptomics, and functional assays are needed.

  In conclusion, this study identified and validated five hub genes associated with ER stress and reticulophagy related molecular networks in sarcopenia. Although these genes are not classical ER stress or ER phagy regulators, they may reflect broader ER homeostasis-related alterations during skeletal muscle aging. The five gene signature showed promising diagnostic value and may provide new candidate biomarkers for sarcopenia. These findings offer a foundation for future mechanistic studies investigating the relationship among ER stress, reticulophagy, mitochondrial dysfunction, inflammation, cellular senescence, neuromuscular degeneration, and age-related muscle loss.


## Conclusion
  By integrating multiple transcriptomic datasets, machine-learning approaches, and clinical validation, we identified five hub genes associated with ER stress and reticulophagy related molecular networks in sarcopenia. The resulting gene signature demonstrated preliminary diagnostic potential, although the individual contribution of each gene varied substantially in external validation. While the identified genes are not canonical regulators of ER stress or ER phagy, they may serve as indicators of ER homeostasis related molecular disturbances in aging skeletal muscle. These findings expand our understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying sarcopenia and provide candidate biomarkers for further validation and a basis for future mechanistic investigation.
