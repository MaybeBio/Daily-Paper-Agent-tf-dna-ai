# Identification of COL5A1 and TGFBI as key basement membrane genes and their molecular mechanisms in gestational diabetes mellitus


## Abstract
  Gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) is a common complication during pregnancy, but the role of the basement membrane (BM) in GDM is not well understood. This study aims to investigate BM-related genes in GDM to provide new insights for diagnosis and treatment. Differentially expressed genes were identified from the GSE203346 dataset in the Gene Expression Omnibus and intersected with BM-related genes to identify BM-related differential genes. Machine learning and gene expression validation were used to identify key genes, which were further validated using an artificial neural network. Additional analyses included gene set enrichment analysis, immunoprecipitation, drug prediction, gene localization, and the construction of lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA and transcription factor-mRNA regulatory networks to explore underlying mechanisms. Among 801 differentially expressed genes, 24 BM-related differential genes were identified. COL5A1, TGFBI, AGRN, TNC, and ITGB6 were identified as candidate genes, with COL5A1 and TGFBI showing consistent low expression across datasets and being designated as key genes. The artificial neural network demonstrated that these key genes effectively distinguished GDM from control samples. Gene set enrichment analysis revealed the involvement of these genes in pathways such as systemic lupus erythematosus and cytokine–cytokine receptor interaction. TGFBI showed a significant positive correlation with CD4+ memory T cells, common lymphoid progenitors, hematopoietic stem cells, and smooth muscle, while COL5A1 was positively correlated with common lymphoid progenitors and smooth muscle. Six drugs were identified as interacting with both key genes. Our study suggests that COL5A1 and TGFBI offer the possibility of personalized treatment strategies for GDM in the future.


## 1. Introduction
  Gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) is a transient form of diabetes caused by insulin resistance and induced by insulin resistance and pancreatic β-cell dysfunction during pregnancy, and is one of the most common obstetric complications due to pregnancy.[1] It has been identified as one of the major barriers to improving the health of mothers and children. Approximately 9% to 25% of pregnancies worldwide are impacted by the acute, long-term, and transgenerational health complications of GDM.[1] In addition, patients with GDM are at increased risk of preeclampsia, hyperamniotic fluid, operative delivery and tears of the birth canal, while long-term risks of the birth canal, while short-term risks for the offspring include shoulder dystocia, while short-term risks for offspring include shoulder dystocia, macrosomia, neonatal hypoglycemia, and jaundice. Although GDM usually resolves after delivery, it can have long-term effects on mother and baby, an increased risk of developing future hyperglycemia, diabetes, obesity, and cardiovascular and circulatory diseases.[2,3] Treatment of GDM includes life intervention, monitoring of blood glucose, and medication. Lifestyle intervention may be sufficient to achieve glycemic targets in up to blood glucose targets in as many as 70% to 85% of women with ADA criteria.[4] When blood glucose targets are not achieved with a lifestyle intervention, the addition of glucose-lowering therapy is warranted. Relatively limited drugs are currently used for the treatment of GDM, including insulin, metformin, and glibenclamide.[5] However, as oral antidiabetics have side effects and safety issues, managing and treating remain challenging to manage and treat. As a result, GDM poses a considerable threat to maternal and child health globally. It is necessary to study its pathogenesis, diagnosis, and treatment.

  Basement membrane (BM) is a thin, dense layers of extracellular matrix which enclose most tissues,[6] and is very complex containing many proteins. These proteins provide essential signaling as well as support for mechanical and barrier systems to the body’s tissues during development and normal function, and also have a unique structure to perform the mechanical functions of shaping organs, connecting tissues, and filtering blood.[6] Studies have shown that BM remodeling regulates the growth and morphological changes of mouse embryos. The ectoderm and the extra-embryonic mesoderm of the mouse embryo are encapsulated by the BM. Signaling between the BM and the tissues is critical for cell polarity and subsequent modulation of morphogenesis.[7] In addition, BM accumulation is a hallmark of diabetic microvascular diseases.[8] Another cohort study has suggested a statistically significant expression of BM-specific perlecan sulfate proteoglycan core protein in pregnant women and controls with GDM.[9] In summary, BM plays a certain role in diabetes-related complications and pregnancy, but its mechanism of action in GDM remains unknown. Therefore, this study used bioinformatics methods to further investigate the potential mechanism of basement membrane-related genes (BMRGs) in GDM.

  Bioinformatics methods were used in this study to analyze GDM data in Gene Expression Omnibus database and BMRGs, screened out key genes, revealed their biological functions and regulatory mechanisms, and provided new targets for the treatment of GDM.


## 2. Materials and methods

### 2.1. Data collection
  The gene expression profile data sets, containing GSE203346-GPL24676 and GSE154414-GPL20301, were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/). In the training set (GSE203346), 19 healthy pregnant women and 18 gestational diabetes pregnant women’s placental tissues were analyzed. In the validation cohort (GSE154414), the placental tissues from 4 healthy pregnant women and 4 women with gestational diabetes were included. A total of 222 BMRGs were obtained from basement membraneBASE (https://bmbase.manchester.ac.uk/).


### 2.2. Difference expression analysis
  In GSE203346, differential expression analysis of placental tissue samples was executed via DESeq2 package (v 1.38.0)[10] to obtain differentially expressed genes (DEGs; |log2 FC| > 0.5, P < .05). Then, volcano plot and heat map were produced using the ggplot2 (v 3.4.3)[11] and pheatmap packages (v 1.0.12)[12] to demonstrate the results of differential expression analysis.


### 2.3. Identification and functional annotation of BMRGs
  BMRGs were obtained by intersecting DEGs and BMRGs. In order to determine the biological functions of BMRGs, Gene Ontology (GO) and the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses were executed using the clusterProfiler package (P < .05; v 4.7.1.003).[13] In addition, a protein–protein interaction network was established using the STRING (https://string-db.org; confidence = 0.4). Visualization was performed using Cytoscape software (v 3.8.2).[14]


### 2.4. Identification of key genes
  During the gene selection phase, a suite of 4 machine learning techniques was utilized, which included eXtreme gradient boosting, support vector machine, general linear model, and random forest. Four diagnostic models were built with the caret package (v 6.0-93)[15] and analyzed using the DALEX package (v 2.4.3)[16] to plot residual distributions, boxplots, receiver operating characteristics (ROC) curves, and assess variable importance. The model with the highest area under the ROC curve (AUC) was selected as the best predictive model. Subsequently, based on the optimal predictive model, the top 5 genes in terms of gene expression were selected as candidate genes. Then, the following candidate genes were displayed in GSE203346 and GSE154414 using the ggplot2 package (v 3.4.3).[11] Then the genes that exhibited consistent expression trends and significant differences across GSE203346 and GSE154414 were considered as key genes (P < .05).


### 2.5. Construction of artificial neural networks
  The neuralnet package (v 1.44.2)[17] was used to construct an artificial neural network model. First, data were normalized using the min to max method to give the range [0,1]. Then, artificial neural networks were built in GSE203346 and GSE154414 based on key genes to determine the weights of these genes. The ability to distinguish between the GDM and the control group of the artificial neural networks was subsequently evaluated using ROC curves.


### 2.6. Gene set enrichment analysis
  For the investigation of the significantly enriched signaling pathways of key genes, based on the gene set: c2.cp.kegg.v7.4.symbols.gmt, the genes were sorted based on the relationship coefficients between the key genes and the rest of the genes. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was then performed according to the sorting results. Subsequently, the enrichplot package (v 1.18.3)[18] was used to visualize the top 5 most significant functions/pathways enriched for each key gene (P < .05).


### 2.7. Immune infiltration analysis
  To explore immune cell infiltration, the eXtreme component-wise linear elicitation with logit-normalization algorithm was used to calculate 64 immune cell infiltrations in GSE203346. The Wilcoxon test was used to identify immune cells with differences between the GDM and the control group. Then, the correlations between key genes and differential immune cells were analyzed using Spearman analysis, and the corrplot package (v 0.92)[19] was used to create a correlation heat map.


### 2.8. Construction of regulatory networks and drug forecasting
  The positions of key genes on the human chromosomes were made clear through a visualization analysis using the RCircos package (v 1.2.2).[20] The miRNAs related to these key genes were then predicted by the mirdb database (http://mirdb.org/). Subsequently, the lncRNAs were identified through the database of the starbase (http://starbase.sysu.edu.cn/). A lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA interaction network was ultimately established, reflecting the interactions between genes, miRNAs, and lncRNAs. In addition to this, transcription factor (TF) targeted key genes were predicted with the aid of the NetworkAnalyst database (https://www.networkanalyst.ca/). The interactions between these TFs and mRNAs were visually represented in a network constructed using Cytoscape (v 3.8.2).[14] Moreover, targeted drugs that could potentially act on key genes were predicted using the DsigDB database (https://dsigdb.tanlab.org/DSigDBv1.0/). Subsequently, it was determined through ClinicalTrials.gov (https://clinicaltrials.gov) whether these drugs had previously been the subject of studies or were under investigation in clinical trials.


### 2.9. Expression verification of key genes
  The tissue samples were obtained from the 5 GDM patients in Fuqing City Hospital, Fujian Province. The tissue samples obtained from 5 healthy individuals were utilized as control samples. The tissue samples were acquired from the samples to perform reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR). Total RNA was extracted using TRIZol (Chronchemicals), mRNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA, and the RT-qPCR reactions were performed by using SureScript-First-strand-cDNA-synthesis-kit (Servicebio). The primer sequences for PCR are shown in Table 1. Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase was used as an internal reference gene. The 2−ΔΔCt method was utilized to calculate the expression of biomarkers.[21] This study was approved by the Fuqing City Hospital ethical review committee.


### 2.10. Statistical analysis
  All data were analyzed using R software (v 4.2.2). The Wilcoxon test was utilized to compare differences between groups (P < .05).


## 3. Results

### 3.1. A total of 24 BMRGs were obtained
  In GSE203346, 801 DEGs were identified between the GDM and the control group, of which 361 were upregulated and 440 were downregulated (Fig. 1A, B). Taking the intersection of DEGs and BMRGs, 24 BMRGs were obtained (Fig. 1C). To understand the molecular function and mechanism, we analyzed the 24 BMRGs for GO and KEGG enrichment. They were mainly enriched in GO enrichment analysis in extracellular matrix organization, extracellular structure organization, collagenous extracellular matrix, BM extracellular matrix structural component, integrin binding, glycosaminoglycan binding, and other functional entries (Fig. 1D). The pathways that were predominantly by the KEGG enrichment analysis included valine, leucine and isoleucine catabolism, carbon metabolism, oxidative phosphorylation, fatty acid biosynthesis, as well as nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (Fig. 1E). These analyses provided a significant foundation for the functional significance of BMRGs in the pathogenesis and progression of GDM. Through the construction of a protein–protein interaction network, the interaction network of 24 BMRGs was obtained, which had a total of 24 nodes and 43 edges. These included the following relationship pairs: MMP2-TNC, VTN-AGRN, ADAMTS4-FMOD, etc (Fig. 1F, G).


### 3.2. COL5A1 and TGFBI were identified as key genes
  Upon evaluating 4 distinct models, it was determined that the general linear model had the lowest residual values and boasted the highest AUC score, which led to its recognition as the most effective predictive model. As a result, a list of 5 candidate genes was identified, which included COL5A1, TGFBI, AGRN, TNC, and ITGB6 (Fig. 2A–D).

  In addition, tGSE203346 was found to significantly downregulate the expression of these candidate genes (Fig. 2E). However, in GSE154414, only COL5A1 and TGFBI were observed to be significantly downregulated (Fig. 2F), leading to their consideration as key genes.


### 3.3. Artificial neural networks and GSEA
  An artificial neural network was constructed based on the key genes (COL5A1 and TGFBI) to determine the weights of these key genes (Fig. 3A, B). Subsequently, the ROC results indicated that the artificial neural networks established from GSE203346 (AUC = 0.8918129) and GSE154414 (AUC = 0.875) demonstrated good discriminatory effectiveness between the disease and control groups (Fig. 3C, D). And the results of GSEA revealed that COL5A1 and TGFBI were significantly enriched for 5 pathways, including systemic lupus erythematosus, starch and sucrose metabolism, pentose and glucuronate interconversions, ascorbate and aldarate metabolism, and cytokine–cytokine receptor interaction (Fig. 3E, F). These analyses offered a key insight into COL5A1 and TGFBI in the pathogenesis of GDM.


### 3.4. Immune infiltration profiling and drug prediction
  The immune cell infiltration abundance in GSE203346 was demonstrated in Figure 4A. There were 6 types of immune cells with obvious differences between GDM and control groups, respectively, including CD4+ memory T-cells, common lymphoid progenitor (CLP), hematopoietic stem cell, platelets, smooth muscle, and Th1 cells (Fig. 4B). Correlation analysis showed that TGFBI was found to have a significantly positive correlation with CD4+ memory T-cells, CLP, hematopoietic stem cell, and smooth muscle. Meanwhile, COL5A1 was observed to have a significantly positive correlation with CLP and smooth muscle (Fig. 4C). Beyond that, through the analysis of COL5A1 and TGFBI, several potential drugs were identified, including cyclosporin, raloxifene, mebendazole, VANADIUM, and puromycin, among others. Among them, these 6 drugs (trichostatin, estradiol, arsenite, potassium, retinoic, and valproic) were found to have interactive relationships with both genes (Fig. 4D). As an illustration with arsenite, the study status was noted as recruiting, with a literature reference on “Risk Analysis of cardiotoxic drug use due to sodium arsenite chloride injection.” The associated conditions were adverse reaction, cardiomyopathy, and evaluation study, using sodium arsenite chloride injection. The study was located at the First Affiliated Hospital of Shandong and Qianfoshan Hospital of Shandong Province in Jinan, Shandong, China (Fig. 4E). Other drugs that interacted with both genes had their research information or clinical trial details presented in Figure 4F–J.


### 3.5. Potential regulatory mechanisms of key genes
  Gene localization analysis revealed that TGFBI was positioned on chromosome 5 and COL5A1 on chromosome 9, with both key genes exhibiting significant expression levels on their respective chromosomes (Fig. 5A, B). Furthermore, a total of 8 miRNA (hsa-miR-126-5p, hsa-miR-21-5p, hsa-miR-29a-3p, hsa-miR-29b-3p, hsa-miR-29c-3p, hsa-miR-582-5p, hsa-miR-590-5p, and hsa-miR-9-5p) and 42 lncRNA (TUG1, MIR497HG, AC012146.1, NEAT1, etc) were obtained. The lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA regulatory network was depicted in Figure 5C, revealing several regulatory relationships such as COL5A1 – hsa-miR-29b-3p-H19, COL5A1 – hsa-miR-29a-3p-AL137129.1, TGFBI – hsa-miR-590-5p-XIST, TGFBI – hsa-miR-9-5p-NEAT1. Then, 23 target TFs were predicted, and a TF-mRNA network was constructed. These included the following relationship pairs: COL5A1 – KLF8, COL5A1 – MAZ, TGFBI – FOS, TGFBI – EZH2, etc (Fig. 5D).


### 3.6. The expression levels of the key genes
  The RT-qPCR results indicated that despite the significant difference only in TGFBI between the groups (P = .0072), both of them showed consistent results, which were that COL5A1 and TGFBI were underexpressed in the GDM group (Fig. 6).


## 4. Discussion
  As one of the most common complications of pregnancy, the biological mechanism of GDM remains to be clearly elucidated. The BM is a thin, planar, organized, extracellular matrix that lies at the base of epithelia and surrounds most organs. It is well known that substances produced by the placenta during pregnancy influence maternal metabolism. Research has shown that BM may be a key part of the development and progression of GDM.[22–27] The purpose of this project is to identify BMRGs in GDM by bioinformatics means and provide directions for novel therapeutic drug targets for the treatment of GDM. We identified 801 DEGs between the GDM and the control group from public databases.

  With the results of the studies, 2 genes, COL5A1 and TGFBI, were identified as key genes of GDM. The COL5A1 cDNA comprises 66 exons. These are distributed over more than 150 kb of genomic DNA. COL5A1 codes for the alpha 1 chain of collagen V protein and is positively correlated with the expansion of adipose tissue.[28] The current reports on this gene are mainly in tumors and arthritis. Several studies have reported the role of COL5A1 in cancer. A comprehensive bioinformatics analysis revealed the dysregulation of COL5A1 in various cancers and its association with cancer prognosis and chemotherapy drug sensitivity.[29] The hypoxia-related gene COL5A1 is expressed in the other results. Based on PCR, COL5A1 has been identified as a potential diagnostic marker and drug target for the detection/management of osteoarthritis.[30] A pig model test suggested the differential expression of gene COL5A1 methylation, which affects the metabolism of piglets.[31]

  TGFBI is expressed in the heart, blood vessels, gut, eyes, and other tissues.[32] TGFBI has been implicated in human genetic studies as a risk factor for both type 1 and type 2 diabetes. An interesting study of the TGFBI gene knockout (KO) mice. In vitro, the survival and function of KO pancreatic islets were impaired. In vivo, KO mice were more susceptible to low-dose streptozotocin-induced diabetes.[33] There are several other placental genes associated with GDM. A study of placental gene profiling in patients with GDM identified TGFB1 and COL1A1 as genes involved in GDM through the TGF-β signaling pathway and extracellular matrix organization.[34] We have found a significant downregulation of placental TGFBI in GDM patients, which is consistent with the performance of this gene in diabetic patients.

  Our study revealing several regulatory relationships, such as COL5A1 – hsa-miR-29b-3p-H19, COL5A1 – hsa-miR-29a-3p-AL137129.1, TGFBI – hsa-miR-590-5p-XIST, TGFBI – hsa-miR-9-5p-NEAT1. Then, 23 target TFs were predicted, and a TF-mRNA network was constructed. These included the following relationship pairs: COL5A1 – KLF8, COL5A1 – MAZ, TGFBI – FOS, TGFBI – EZH2. It would be desirable to obtain more information on the lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA regulatory axes associated with disease to provide more clues and evidence for the molecular mechanisms of disease.[35] Also, the onset is associated with changes in the expression levels of a large number of lncRNAs.[36]

  A study has confirmed the difference in miRNAs between diabetic and nondiabetic groups. Serum levels of miR-16-5p and miR-142-3p in patients, and the expression of miR-144-3p was significantly upregulated.[37] Li et al have suggested that lncRNA SNHG17 is significantly downregulated in GDM, which was valuable for early diagnosis of GDM.[38] Guangqin et al also confirmed the upregulation of lncRNA SOX20T expression. Upregulation of SOX2OT can predict GDM and its adverse events.[39] Zheng et al constructed networks of TF hub genes and miRNA hub genes, respectively, and the intersection of the networks revealed the important roles of certain miRNAs in gene expression.[40] In view of the results of previous studies, we found that the lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA network influences the occurrence of GDM. We did further research and found that 8 miRNA (hsa-miR-126-5p, hsa-miR-21-5p, hsa-miR-29a-3p, hsa-miR-29b-3p, hsa-miR-29c-3p, hsa-miR-582-5p, hsa-miR-590-5p, and hsa-miR-9-5p) and 42 lncRNA (TUG1, MIR497HG, AC012146.1, NEAT1, etc) were obtained. The miR-29b-3p was found to be involved in the regulation of various biological processes in tumors, including tumor cell proliferation, metastasis, and inhibition of apoptosis.[41] NO reports of TGFBI – hsa-miR-590-5p-XIST and TGFBI – hsa-miR-9-5p-NEAT1 were found. These findings can provide new directions for future research on GDM.

  To provide directions for novel therapeutic drug targets for the treatment of GDM, in the study, 6 targeted drugs (trichostatin, estradiol, arsenite, potassium, retinoic, and valproic) that act simultaneously on 2 key genes (COL5A1, TGFBI) were predicted.

  Trichostatin A has been shown to have a protective effect on the central nervous system in Alzheimer disease and hypoxic-ischemic brain injury.[42,43] An animal experiment identified the key pathways and key hub proteins that mediate the effects of Trichostatin A on the hippocampus of type II diabetic mice, including the longevity regulatory pathway, peroxisomes, ribosomes, protein processing in the endoplasmic reticulum, the insulin signaling pathway, CAT, and PRKAA2.[44] Estradiol is a primary female sex hormone that plays a central role in female reproduction and is mainly secreted by the placenta during pregnancy. As shown in an experimental study of the GDM rats, E2 levels are elevated in the placenta of GDM rats, which may have an effect against uterine remodeling and abnormal placental angiogenesis caused by GDM.[45] Arsenic, a metalloid that occurs naturally in the earth’s crust, causes a wide range of cancers, including skin, bladder, lung, and liver cancer.[46] Exposure to arsenic elevates the risk of diabetes.[47] Acid is a potent derivative of vitamin A and plays a key role in body development and organogenesis by controlling cell proliferation and differentiation. In diabetes mellitus, RA and activation of the vitamin A-related receptor improved blood glucose levels, and ameliorated the syndrome of diabetes mellitus.[48]


## 5. Conclusions
  In conclusion, this study identified BM-related genes in GDM, but a large number of experimental mechanisms and clinical application studies are still needed for verification. We look forward to new discoveries and will continue to investigate the role of these genes.
