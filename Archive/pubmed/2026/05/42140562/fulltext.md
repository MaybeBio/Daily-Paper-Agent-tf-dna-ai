Glutaminase catalyzes the hydrolysis of gamma-glutamyl groups to produce glutamic acid and ammonia, exhibiting broad-spectrum applications in food processing, fine chemicals, and pharmaceutical industries. However, its limited production efficiency constrains its industrial application. In this study, a small mutant library was constructed by rational protein design. Mutant strain HZ-12/pHY-glu3 (S467H) displayed a 100% increase in glutaminase activity. The interaction force analysis and molecular dynamics simulation revealed the mechanisms by which interaction force enhancement, binding pocket size increase, and binding conformation tightening enhanced glutaminase activity. Further, glutaminase activity-related transcription factors and respiratory chain-related genes were identified in the host strain Bacillus amyloliquefaciens HZ-12. The deletion of transcription factors genes (ccpC, phoP, nsrR, and glcR) and respiratory chain-related genes (ythAB, cydCD) significantly improved glutaminase activity. Notably, engineered strain HZ-12DeltaythAB/pHY-glu3 exhibited the highest glutaminase activity (1.08 U/mL), which was 575% higher than that of control strain HZ-12/pHY-glu3(S467H). Overall, this study developed a strategy for enhancing glutaminase activity by combining protein engineering and key gene deletion, offering valuable gene resources and engineered strains for glutaminase production.
