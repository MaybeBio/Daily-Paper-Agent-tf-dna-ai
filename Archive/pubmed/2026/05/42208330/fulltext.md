BACKGROUND: The global incidence of squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) is on the rise. Previous studies have confirmed that the TWEAK/cIAP1/NF-kappaB axis plays a crucial role in SCC progression. Acteoside (ACT), a phenylethanoid glycoside with established antitumor properties, has not been fully elucidated regarding its specific effects and underlying mechanisms in SCC. METHODS: KEGG/GO analyses identified potential targets and enrichment profiles of ACT against SCC. Molecular docking and 100-ns molecular dynamics simulations evaluated ACT's binding potential to key proteins of the TWEAK/cIAP1/NF-kappaB axis. In vitro, CCK-8, scratch assay, flow cytometry, and NF-kappaB nuclear translocation assay assessed ACT's effects on A431/SCC9 cell proliferation, migration, apoptosis, and NF-kappaB activation. Additionally, LPS-stimulated A431 and SCC9 cells were used to evaluate the effect of ACT on the activated TWEAK/cIAP1/NF-kappaB axis. In vivo, a BALB/c nude mouse xenograft model evaluated ACT's antitumor efficacy; IHC, IF, WB, and qPCR analyzed TWEAK/cIAP1/NF-kappaB axis proteins and mRNA in tumor tissues, while ELISA measured serum IL-6, IL-8, and VEGF levels. RESULTS: ACT targets were enriched in cancer-related and NF-kappaB signaling pathways. Molecular docking and dynamics simulations suggested stable interactions between ACT and TWEAK, FN14, cIAP1, RIP1, and NF-kappaB. In vitro, ACT concentration-dependently inhibited proliferation and migration, promoted apoptosis, suppressed LPS-induced NF-kappaB nuclear translocation, and downregulated TWEAK, cIAP1, NF-kappaB, and p-NF-kappaB. Notably, ACT significantly reversed LPS-induced upregulation of TWEAK, FN14, cIAP1, NF-kappaB, and p-NF-kappaB, demonstrating functional antagonism of the activated signaling axis. In vivo, ACT significantly inhibited tumor growth, reduced suspected pulmonary metastases, downregulated TWEAK/cIAP1/NF-kappaB axis proteins and mRNA in tumors, and decreased serum IL-6, IL-8, and VEGF levels. CONCLUSION: ACT modulates the TWEAK/cIAP1/NF-kappaB signaling axis, downregulates the expression of key pathway proteins at both the protein and mRNA levels, antagonizes LPS-induced pathway activation, and reduces the secretion of IL-6, IL-8, and VEGF, thereby inhibiting SCC growth and progression.
