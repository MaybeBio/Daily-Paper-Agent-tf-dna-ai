# DNA methylation biomarkers associated with early gastric cardia carcinogenesis

## Background
  Gastric cancer (GC) is one of the most prevalent cancers worldwide, ranking fifth in both global incidence and cancer related mortality [1]. Anatomically, GC can be generally classified into two subtypes: proximal gastric cancer (gastric cardia cancer, GCC) and distal gastric cancer (non-cardia gastric cancer, NCGC), which exhibit distinct clinical features. Compared with NCGC, GCC is more frequently diagnosed at an advanced stage [2], reflecting differences in tumor biology and progression patterns [3]. Moreover, most GCC cases present at advanced stages [4], where surgical resection and systemic therapies often yield poor outcomes, highlighting the need for more effective early detection strategies. Therefore, improving the prognosis of GCC requires the development of more effective molecular tools for identifying early gastric cardia lesions.

  DNA methylation, a key epigenetic modification, primarily occurs at cytosine-phosphate-guanosine dinucleotide (CpG) sites, where cytosine is converted to 5-methylcytosine (5mC). This process plays an important role in cancer development [5]. Notably, aberrant DNA methylation patterns are among the earliest and most frequent molecular alterations in carcinogenesis [6], making them highly promising candidates for early detection [7, 8]. Our previous studies [9–11] have established genome-wide DNA methylation profiles of GCC and its precancerous lesions, providing a foundation for biomarker discovery. However, translating these findings into clinically applicable biomarkers has been challenging, primarily due to insufficient validation across the full spectrum of GCC progression, from precancerous lesions to invasive cancer.

  Building upon our prior work, this study aimed to systematically integrate existing methylation datasets and perform comprehensive screening and validation in multi-stage samples to identify specific DNA methylation markers associated with early gastric cardia carcinogenesis and precancerous gastric cardia lesions. The identification of such markers could not only improve molecular characterization of early lesions but also provide valuable insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying GCC development, thereby facilitating the development of targeted prevention and intervention strategies.


## Methods

### Data source
  In this study, we performed an integrative reanalysis of three datasets, which we have previously published [9–11]. Ultimately, we integrated DNA methylation data from 69 gastric cardia samples of 60 subjects. These 69 samples included 22 normal gastric cardia mucosa (Normal), 32 intestinal metaplasia (IM), 7 intraepithelial neoplasia (IEN), and 8 gastric cardia cancer (GCC).


### DNA methylation data reanalysis
  We processed the combined 850 K methylation data using the “ChAMP” (version 2.28.0) R package. A total of 740,141 qualified CpG sites from 69 gastric cardia samples were obtained, and β values were calculated to represent the DNA methylation levels. The BMIQ method was then used to normalize the β value matrix, and Combat was applied to correct the batch effect caused by the BeadChip slide and array. The adjusted-P values were computed using the Benjamini-Hochberg method. For differential DNA methylation analysis, a CpG site was defined as differentially methylated probe (DMP) if the absolute difference in mean β value (|Δβ|, IM/IEN/GCC vs. Normal) was > 0.15, with P value < 0.01. Comparisons were performed for different lesion types vs. normal (IM vs. Normal; IEN vs. Normal; GCC vs. Normal). Volcano plots were generated using the “ggplot2” package to visualize the DMPs.


### Identification of key DMPs with diagnostic capability for precancerous gastric cardia lesions
  Only DMPs located in the promoter (TSS1500, TSS200, 5’UTR, and 1st Exon) and CpG island regions were selected for further analysis. For the DMPs identified in different comparisons, some DMPs occurred in different histopathologic lesions during gastric cardia carcinogenesis. We named these DMPs “carcinogenic-related DMPs”. These DMPs were identified following visualization using a Venn diagram.

  Next, we combined Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) and Random Forest (RF) machine learning algorithms to explore candidate diagnostic biomarkers of precancerous gastric cardia lesions. In brief, 89 DMPs shared by IM, IEN, and GCC were selected as input for LASSO and RF analysis. LASSO analysis and RF algorithm were performed using the “glmnet” and “randomForest” packages, respectively. The LASSO regression identified DMPs with non-zero coefficients as potential biomarkers, and the RF algorithm selected the top 10 DMPs by ranking their importance based on the mean decrease in accuracy values. To ensure reliability and stability, the intersection of DMPs selected by both machine learning algorithms was defined as diagnostic candidates for PGCLs and GCC. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were plotted using the “pROC” package and area under curve (AUC) values were calculated to examine the diagnostic value of each biomarker, and then visualization was performed using the “ggplot2” package. A nomogram was constructed based on the diagnostic candidates.


### Immunohistochemistry
  Paraffin-embedded gastric cardia tissues were sectioned into 4-µm thickness, and placed on adhesion glass slides, followed by baking at 62°C for 2 h. Slides were then deparaffinized in xylene (10 min ×3) and rehydrated with descending concentrations of ethanol (100%, 5 min; 95%, 5 min; 85%, 5 min; 75%, 5 min) and rinsed with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). Then, antigen retrieval was performed by heating the slides for 3 min in a pressure cooker. Slides were washed with PBS (3 min ×3), and the endogenous peroxidase activity was quenched with 3% hydrogen peroxide for 10 min at room temperature, followed by three PBS washes (3 min each). Slides were incubated with EDNRB primary antibody (Cat# 31191; Signalway Antibody LLC, MD, USA; dilution 1:500) overnight at 4°C. Then the slides were thoroughly washed in PBS (3 min ×3) and incubated with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-labeled goat anti-mouse/rabbit secondary antibody (Maixin Biotechnologies, China) for 30 min at 37°C. After an additional three washes in PBS (3 min each), staining was visualized using the 3,3’-diaminobenzidine and counterstained with hematoxylin for 1 min. Finally, slides were dehydrated with series ethanol (75%, 5 min; 85%, 5 min; 95%, 5 min; 100%, 5 min ×2) and cleared in xylene (5 min ×3).

  IHC scoring was performed by two independent pathologists who evaluated each sample according to a standardized scoring system and scale. In cases of discrepancy, the average score was taken. The semi-quantitative evaluation was based on staining intensity (weak: 1; moderate: 2; strong: 3) and the abundance of positive cells (≤ 5%: 0; 6%–25%: 1; 26%–50%: 2; 51%–75%: 3; ≥ 76%: 4). The final staining score was obtained by multiplying the staining intensity with the abundance of positive cells, yielding the final data [12].


### Statistical analyses
  All bioinformatics and statistical analyses were processed using R (version 4.2.2). Statistical significance was defined as a two-tailed P-value < 0.05. The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used for comparisons between two groups, unless otherwise specified. ROC curve was plotted and the corresponding AUC was calculated using “pROC” [13]. Heatmaps were generated using “pheatmap” package. Results with P values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant (*P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001, ****P < 0.0001).


## Results

### Delineation of DNA methylation profiles in different histopathologic stages during gastric cardia carcinogenesis
  By integrating the DNA methylation datasets of 69 gastric cardia samples we previously published, we formulated a combined 850 K methylation dataset comprising 22 normal gastric cardia mucosa, 32 IM, 7 IEN, and 8 GCC tissues (Fig. 1A). The basic characteristics of the study subjects included in the genome-wide DNA methylation profiling are summarized in Supplementary Table S1. After quality control and batch effect removal , methylation data for a total of 740,141 CpG sites were kept for downstream analyses (Fig. 1B). The tissue samples formed mostly discernible clusters where GCC and IEN could be clearly resolved from the normal gastric cardia. However, IM tissues appeared to be heterogeneous based on global DNA methylation (Fig. 1C). We next generated density plots comparing the average β values of CpG sites between normal tissue and each disease group (IM, IEN, and GCC). The comparison between normal and IM tissues revealed a highly similar methylation pattern, with a Pearson correlation coefficient of 0.988 (P < 2.2e-16). In contrast, the methylation profiles of GCC and IEN tissues showed substantially greater deviation from normal tissue, with correlation coefficients of 0.972 and 0.913, respectively (P < 2.2e-16 for both; Fig. 1D). The progressive decrease in correlation coefficients from IM to IEN to GCC reflects an increasing degree of methylation alterations relative to normal tissue, with GCC displaying the most pronounced epigenetic remodeling.

  Differential methylation analyses revealed a gradually increasing number of DMPs in IM (n = 9,405), IEN (n = 65,952), and GCC (n = 211,440) compared with normal gastric cardia mucosa (Fig. 1E-G; Supplementary Table S2-S4). Notably, PGCL group (IM [hyper-DMPs = 79.3%] and IEN [hyper-DMPs = 86.3%]) exhibited a greater proportion of hyper-DMPs, whereas hypo-DMPs were more abundant in GCC (hypo-DMPs = 87.7%) samples (Fig. 1H). These findings suggest that aberrant hypermethylation occurs early in precancerous lesions, providing insights into the possible role of hyper-DMPs in the diagnosis of PGCLs.

### Genomic distribution of DMPs and selection of candidate biomarkers for lesion stratification
  We next compared the distribution pattern of the DMPs identified in each lesion group, and found that both hypermethylated and hypomethylated DMPs showed significant deviation from the expected array distribution, particularly in IGR (intergenic regions) and Opensea regions. Strikingly, across all three lesion groups (IM, IEN, and GCC), hypermethylated DMPs exhibited conspicuous enrichment in promoter-associated regions and CpG islands (CGI) (Fig. 2A-F, P < 0.001). This concordant pattern of hypermethylation clustering in promoter and CGI regions across different lesion stages implies that these DNA methylation changes may represent key regulatory switches with both biological significance and diagnostic potential.

  To identify candidate diagnostic DMPs worthy of biological and clinical validation, we prioritized potential diagnostic DMPs with a stringent multi-step filtering strategy (Fig. 2G): DMPs were initially selected from common sites identified across all three lesion groups compared to normal tissue, refined to those located in promoter-associated CGI regions—thereby enriching for functionally consequential methylation events—and further filtered to include only those genes represented by at least 10 DMPs. This approach yielded a total of 89 candidate DMPs. Unsupervised hierarchical clustering analysis of methylation levels for these 89 candidate DMPs across all samples demonstrated robust discriminatory power (Fig. 2H; Supplementary Table S5). The heatmap revealed distinct methylation signatures that effectively separated normal tissues from pathological samples, including PGCLs (IM and IEN) and GCC, with most normal samples clustering distinctly from lesion samples.

### Identification of diagnostic methylation biomarkers for precancerous gastric cardia lesions
  To identify robust diagnostic biomarkers for precancerous gastric cardia lesions (PGCLs: IM and IEN) and GCC, we applied two independent machine learning algorithms—LASSO regression and RF algorithm—to refine and prioritize diagnostic candidates based on methylation levels of 89 DMPs shared across IM, IEN, and GCC. For LASSO regression, the lambda.min value (0.068) was selected as the optimal lambda via a 10-fold cross-validation, and plots of the regression coefficients were generated (Fig. 3A-B). This process identified four characteristic methylation markers, including SALL1_cg05151154, SALL1_cg09016242, EDNRB_cg04390523, and EYA4_cg20602026 (Fig. 3C). Meanwhile, RF algorithm ranked 10 features based on MeanDecreaseAccuracy importance score (Fig. 3D-E). Notably, two diagnostic candidates—EDNRB_cg04390523 and SALL1_cg09016242—were consistently identified in both algorithms (LASSO: 4, RF: 10; Fig. 3F). Their convergence across differential methylation and machine learning analyses highlights these two candidates as robust candidates with both diagnostic value and biological significance. Furthermore, a nomogram incorporating these two DMPs was generated, yielding an AUC of 0.947 (95% confidence interval (CI), 0.897–0.997) for predicting PGCL (IM and IEN) and GCC (Fig. 3G-H). Both of the selected DMPs exhibited significantly higher methylation levels in both PGCL and GCC compared with normal gastric cardia mucosa, with methylation levels increasing significantly with lesion severity (P for trend = 2.77e-12; Fig. 3I-J).

### EDNRB expression is significantly reduced in PGCLs
  Given that these diagnostic candidates are located in gene promoters, we next examined EDNRB protein expression by immunohistochemistry (IHC) in a total of 212 gastric cardia tissues (Normal = 85; IM = 90; IEN = 37). Nuclei of normal gastric cardia glandular cells were positive for EDNRB (Fig. 4A). As expected, we noticed that nuclear immunostaining for EDNRB was rarely detected in IM or IEN tissues (Fig. 4A). Semiquantitative analysis of immunostaining scores revealed a stepwise decrease in EDNRB expression from normal gastric cardia epithelium through IM to IEN (Fig. 4B), thereby indicating the participation of DNA methylation changes during gastric cardia carcinogenesis (Fig. 4C).

## Discussion
  The present study systematically delineates the DNA methylation landscape across the histopathologic continuum of gastric cardia carcinogenesis and identifies candidate methylation biomarkers associated with early pathologic transformation in the gastric cardia. By integrating methylation datasets spanning normal mucosa, IM, IEN, and GCC, we observed progressive epigenetic dysregulation during lesion development and identified two promoter-associated CpG sites, EDNRB_cg04390523 and SALL1_cg09016242, as candidate biomarkers with discriminatory value in the integrated tissue dataset.

  Aberrant DNA methylation is among the earliest molecular alterations in tumorigenesis [14, 15] and has attracted substantial interest as a source of diagnostic and mechanistic biomarkers in cancer [16]. In this study, the stepwise accumulation of DMPs from IM (9,405) to IEN (65,952), and GCC (211,440) aligns with our previous findings in gastric cardia IM and IEN samples [9], reinforcing the notion that DNA methylation alterations are involved early in gastric cardia carcinogenesis. Notably, the predominance of hyper-DMPs in PGCLs (79.3%–86.3%) versus hypo-DMPs in GCC (87.7%) implies that distinct epigenetic mechanisms may operate at different stages of lesion progression: promoter hypermethylation may contribute to early transcriptional silencing of tumor suppressor genes, whereas more widespread hypomethylation in GCC may reflect later genomic instability [17].

  The consistent enrichment of hyper-DMPs in promoter-associated CGIs across all lesion stages highlights the functional relevance of these regions, as previous studies have also shown extensive DNA methylation changes in promoter CGIs in gastric cardia IM and gastric IM [11, 18]. CGI hypermethylation is a well-established mechanism for transcriptional silencing in cancer [19, 20], and our immunohistochemical validation of EDNRB (endothelin receptor type B) downregulation in PGCLs is consistent with this paradigm. Focusing on shared DMPs located in promoter-associated CGIs across PGCLs and GCC, the convergence of EDNRB_cg04390523 and SALL1_cg09016242 in both LASSO and RF analyses underscores their potential relevance as tissue-based methylation biomarkers, with the nomogram achieving an AUC of 0.947 in the integrated dataset. These findings align with prior studies implicating promoter hypermethylation of EDNRB in head and neck carcinoma [21], prostate cancer [22], cervical cancer [23], and esophageal squamous cell carcinoma [24]. Moreover, aberrant promoter methylation of SALL1 has been reported in breast cancer [25]. In fact, both in vivo and in vitro investigations have suggested that enhanced EDNRB [26] or SALL1 [27, 28] expression may suppress tumor growth in certain cancer types. Collectively, these findings support their potential utility as candidate biomarkers and suggest possible biological relevance during gastric cardia carcinogenesis.

  Among the two candidate markers, EDNRB was further supported by immunohistochemical validation. We observed a progressive reduction in EDNRB protein expression from normal gastric cardia mucosa to IM and IEN, consistent with the promoter hypermethylation pattern identified in the methylation analysis. Given that reduced EDNRB expression has also been associated with adverse biological behavior in several malignancies [21–24, 26], our findings raise the possibility that epigenetic silencing of EDNRB may participate in early neoplastic transformation in the gastric cardia mucosa. However, because no functional experiments were performed in the present study, the mechanistic role of EDNRB in gastric cardia carcinogenesis remains to be further clarified.

  However, several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, this was a retrospective, cross-sectional study and lacked longitudinal follow-up validation; therefore, we could not stratify IM lesions according to subsequent neoplastic progression. Second, although the integrated analysis showed good discriminatory performance for GCC and its precancerous lesions, the number of samples used to represent each stage was limited, especially for IEN and GCC. Further validation in larger independent cohorts is warranted to confirm these findings. Third, only EDNRB, but not SALL1, was validated at the protein level. Finally, clinicopathologic variables such as Helicobacter pylori and Epstein-Barr virus status were not systematically available for all samples included in the integrated analysis, precluding further stratified analyses.


## Conclusions
  Our integrative analysis reveals that gastric cardia carcinogenesis is characterized by stage-specific epigenetic remodeling, with promoter CGI hypermethylation emerging as an early and recurrent event. The identified biomarkers, particularly EDNRB and SALL1, represent promising tissue-based candidate methylation biomarkers associated with early gastric cardia carcinogenesis and merit further validation in larger, longitudinal, and independent cohorts (Fig. 4C).
