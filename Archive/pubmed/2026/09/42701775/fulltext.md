# Bioinformatics Screening and Preliminary Experimental Verification of Integrated Stress Response-Associated MYC and BAG3 in Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease


## Background
  Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) has high morbidity and mortality, and integrated stress response (ISR) participates in its progression. Traditional COPD diagnosis relies mainly on GOLD criteria, while ISR-based auxiliary molecular markers are still lacking. This study aimed to comprehensively analyze and identify ISR-related biomarkers in COPD, and to uncover their underlying molecular mechanisms.


## Methods
  COPD data were obtained from the GEO database. Biomarkers were identified and validated through a comprehensive approach, including differential expression analysis, machine learning algorithms, expression level verification, and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. Moreover, a nomogram was applied to construct an ISR-related predictive model for COPD. Enrichment analysis, immune infiltration analysis, molecular regulatory network, and drug prediction were performed to further confirm the regulatory roles of biomarkers. Furthermore, the expression patterns of biomarkers in core cells were investigated using dataset GSE173896. Furthermore, Western blotting was employed to detect the protein expression levels of biomarkers in clinical samples.


## Results
  Overall, 2 biomarkers (MYC and BAG3) were identified as significantly associated with ISR in COPD. A predictive nomogram constructed based on MYC and BAG3 demonstrated excellent diagnostic performance, with an area under the curve (AUC) value of 0.799. The biomarkers were significantly enriched in the MAPK signaling pathway and cell cycle. Notably, biomarkers were positively correlated with effector memory CD4 T cells and eosinophils. Subsequently, 36 microRNAs (miRNAs) (like hsa-miR-4699-3p), 260 transcription factors (TFs) (like SNAI2, ZBTB43, and ZNF395) and 9 drugs (like staurosporine and N-acetyl-L-cysteine) were associated with the biomarkers. Moreover, significant changes in MYC and BAG3 expression were observed during T cell differentiation. Clinically, MYC and BAG3 were significantly upregulated in COPD tissues, with protein levels increased by 58.4% and 252.2% respectively.


## Conclusion
  This study identified and preliminarily verified ISR-associated MYC and BAG3, offering novel molecular references to complement conventional COPD clinical assessment.


## Introduction
  Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a progressive respiratory disorder marked by irreversible airflow limitation and sustained chronic inflammation, typically involving destruction of the pulmonary parenchyma (emphysema) and remodeling of the small airways. These structural changes result in impaired gas exchange and a progressive, irreversible decline in lung function. COPD carries high morbidity and disability rates, and is currently the third leading cause of death worldwide.1–3 Its pathogenesis involves multifactorial interactions among oxidative stress, protease–antiprotease imbalance, and cellular senescence.4 Current pharmacotherapies for COPD have significant limitations in disease management. Bronchodilators such as salbutamol, salmeterol, and ipratropium relieve symptoms but cannot reverse lung-function decline or halt disease progression. Inhaled corticosteroids (ICS) provide only modest anti-inflammatory benefit, with clear efficacy restricted to patients with elevated blood eosinophils; for the majority of COPD patients, ICS confer no meaningful gain.5 In end-stage disease, lung-volume-reduction surgery or transplantation may be beneficial, yet these options remain inaccessible to most patients because of stringent selection criteria, scarce donor organs, and prohibitive costs.6 Existing clinical evaluation mainly relies on traditional GOLD staging standards, which focus on functional indicators and lack effective molecular auxiliary indicators.7 Reliable molecular predictors can effectively supplement clinical staging and offer theoretical reference for individualized intervention strategies for COPD patients. Consequently, there is an urgent need to identify novel biomarkers and therapeutic targets to overcome current therapeutic bottlenecks.

  The integrated stress response (ISR) is an evolutionarily conserved signaling network that enables cells to cope with endoplasmic reticulum stress, oxidative stress, and other cellular insults. By phosphorylating eIF2α, the ISR suppresses global protein translation while selectively activating stress-responsive genes to restore homeostasis or trigger apoptosis.8 Recent studies reveal that COPD lungs exhibit marked up-regulation of ER-stress markers such as GRP78 and CHOP, driving alveolar epithelial cell death and amplifying inflammatory cascades.9 Excessive ISR activation promotes alveolar epithelial apoptosis and airway remodeling, positioning the ISR as a potential therapeutic target.10 In animal models, pharmacological inhibition of ISR components (eg, the PERK-eIF2α-ATF4 axis) attenuates emphysema and preserves lung function.11 Furthermore, ISR-related genes (ISRGs), including MYC and BAG3, may be closely associated with pulmonary inflammatory injury and cellular stress responses.12,13 Collectively, these findings indicated that ISRGs could serve as central drivers of COPD progression.

  Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) enables the dissection of transcriptomic heterogeneity at single-cell resolution, uncovering rare cell subsets that are masked by bulk sequencing.14 In COPD, scRNA-seq has demonstrated pronounced ISR activation in club cells and macrophages,15 detected early pre-activation of ISR genes such as XBP1 and ATF6 upon smoking exposure,16 and revealed that ISR-high macrophages foster fibroblast activation via TGF-β signaling, thereby driving small-airway fibrosis.17 Integrating bulk RNA-seq with scRNA-seq, therefore, enables the construction of a comprehensive map from molecular signatures to cellular ecosystems. In terms of methodological selection, we combined LASSO regression and SVM-RFE algorithms. This combined strategy can simultaneously realize dimensionality reduction screening and accuracy optimization of candidate genes, effectively eliminate redundant interfering genes, and achieve more stable and reliable core biomarker screening results compared with single machine learning methods.

  In our study, we integrated differential expression analysis (edgeR), machine learning algorithms (LASSO + SVM), and single-cell trajectory inference to establish the clinical predictive model based on ISR gene signatures (MYC and BAG3) in COPD. Gene-set enrichment analysis (GSEA) revealed significant enrichment of gene sets associated with the MAPK signaling pathway and cell cycle. Immune-infiltration analyses linked these genes to effector memory CD4 T cells and eosinophils. CellChat analysis demonstrated that the T-cell-associated MIF-(CD74+CXCR4) and ANXA1-FPR1 signaling axis plays a crucial role. This potential regulatory mechanism helps partly interpret the pathological features of COPD from the bioinformatic perspective.


## Materials and Methods

### Data Selection
  The transcriptome data from RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) datasets (GSE103174 and GSE106986) and the single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) dataset GSE173896, all of which were linked to COPD, were retrieved from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). Specifically, for GSE103174 (platform: GPL13667, training set), 14 COPD non-smoking lung tissue samples and 10 non-smoking control lung tissue samples were selected. For GSE106986 (platform: GPL13497, validation set), 8 COPD non-smoking lung tissue samples and 5 non-smoking control lung tissue samples were selected. Meanwhile, for the scRNA-seq dataset GSE173896 (platform: GPL20795), 5 COPD lung tissue samples and 3 normal controls were selected. All control lung tissues were collected from non-smoking individuals without COPD. Moreover, 534 integrated stress response-related genes (ISRRGs) (Table S1) were obtained from the literature.18


### Differential Expression Analysis
  The limma package (v 3.56.2)19 was employed to determine bulk-differentially expressed genes (bulk-DEGs) between COPD and control samples in GSE103174 (|log2 fold change (FC)| > 0.5 and p < 0.05). Afterwards, the EnhancedVolcano package (v 1.18.0)20 and the ComplexHeatmap package (v 2.16.0)21 were employed to visualize the markers and expression patterns of bulk-DEGs.


### Functional Enrichment Analysis and Protein-Protein Interaction (PPI) Network Construction
  To elucidate the biological functions of bulk-DEGs in COPD, the clusterProfiler package (v 4.15.0.003)22 was applied to perform Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses (p < 0.05). The overlap between ISRRGs and bulk-DEGs was analyzed to determine differentially expressed integrated stress response-related genes (DE-ISRRGs) using the VennDiagram package (v 1.7.3).23 Subsequently, the Metascape database (http://metascape.org) was utilized to characterize the signaling pathways and biological functions associated with the DE-ISRRGs (Min overlap ≥ 3 and p ≤ 0.01).

  Furthermore, the DE-ISRRGs were imported into the Search Tool for the Retrieval of Interacting Genes/Proteins (STRING) database (https://cn.string-db.org/), and a PPI network was created (Interaction scores > 0.4) to explore their interaction relationships using the Cytoscape software (v 3.10.2).24


### Machine Learning Algorithms
  From all samples of the GSE103174 dataset, 2 machine learning algorithms, namely least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) and support vector machine-recursive feature elimination (SVM-RFE), were employed to select characteristic genes associated with COPD based on the DE-ISRRGs. LASSO has achieved notable attention due to its advantages in feature selection, dimensionality reduction, enhancing model interpretability, resolving multicollinearity issues, and improving model generalization.25 The glmnet package (v 4.1–8)26 was employed to determine the LASSO-characteristic genes, and the optimal lambda value was ascertained through 10-fold cross-validation. The SVM-RFE algorithm was conducted via the caret package (v 6.0–94).27 Model performance was evaluated via 5-fold cross-validation. The SVM-RFE-characteristic genes that yielded the highest cross-validation accuracy was ultimately determined. The key hyperparameters of the radial basis function (RBF) kernel, namely sigma (kernel width) and C (penalty coefficient), were automatically optimized through grid search based on datasets during model training. The model was constructed for binary classification to distinguish COPD patients from control samples, and classification accuracy was adopted as the evaluation metric. Using the ggvenn package (v 0.1.10),28 the overlap between LASSO characteristic genes and SVM-RFE characteristic genes was analyzed to obtain characteristic genes.


### Identification and Diagnostic Value of Biomarkers
  The expression of characteristic genes was assessed in both the GSE103174 and GSE106986 datasets. Characteristic genes exhibiting consistent expression patterns in these 2 datasets and notable disparities between COPD and control groups were designated as candidate biomarkers (Wilcoxon test, p < 0.05). To probe the diagnostic capacity of candidate biomarkers, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were created through the pROC package (v 1.18.5)29 in the GSE103174 and GSE106986 datasets. Biomarkers with the area under the curve (AUC) values greater than 0.7 indicated good diagnostic capability for COPD.


### Creation and Evaluation of a Nomogram
  To further appraise the overall predictive capability of biomarkers for COPD, a nomogram was established via the rms package (v 6.8-1)30 in all samples of GSE103174. The total points were the sum of the points of each biomarker. The greater the total points, the greater the probability of COPD occurrence. Subsequently, calibration curves were plotted to exhibit the predictive ability of the nomogram via the rms package (v 6.8-1). When the p-value of the Hosmer-Lemeshow (HL) test exceeded 0.05, it suggested that no substantial discrepancy existed between the predicted values and the actual values. An ROC curve was generated via the pROC package (v 1.18.5), and the AUC value was greater than 0.7, implying its favorable predictive ability.


### Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA)
  In all samples from GSE103174, the Spearman correlation coefficient (cor) values between each biomarker and all other genes were calculated via the Spearman correlation analysis from the psych package (v 2.3.6).31 Then, the cor values were sorted in descending order to generate a list of related genes corresponding to each biomarker. GSEA was performed through the clusterProfiler package (v 4.15.0.003) to further determine the possible biological functions of the biomarkers (p < 0.05, normalized enrichment score (NES) > 1). Notably, GSEA used “c2.cp.kegg.v7.4.symbols.gmt” as the KEGG background gene set from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB) (http://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/downloads.jsp).


### Immune Microenvironment Analysis
  The significant changes in immune cells might mirror the regulatory imbalance of the immune system in the context of COPD, indicating their clinical and pathological significance for diagnosis and treatment efficacy.32 In GSE103174, the infiltration proportions of 28 immune cells33 were obtained via the ssGSEA algorithm from the GSVA package (v 1.42.0),34 and differential immune cells (DICs) between COPD and control samples were identified (Wilcoxon test, p < 0.05). Subsequently, Spearman analyses were conducted among DICs, among biomarkers, and between DICs and biomarkers to explore their relationships via the psych package (v 2.3.6) (|cor| > 0.3 and p < 0.05).


### Molecular Regulatory Network
  MicroRNAs (miRNAs), as important non-coding regulatory factors, exert their biological functions by participating in the regulation of the translation process of their downstream genes. To elucidate the molecular regulatory mechanisms of biomarkers in COPD, relevant miRNAs targeting the biomarkers were identified through the miRWalk database (http://mirwalk.umm.uni-heidelberg.de/). Transcription factors (TFs) are proteins that bind to specific DNA sequences and regulate gene expression. TFs that regulate the biomarkers were predicted via the ChEA3 database (https://maayanlab.cloud/chea3/). Potential therapeutic drugs that might act on the biomarkers were explored via the Drug Signatures Database (DSigDB) (https://www.dgidb.org/). Finally, biomarker-miRNA, biomarker-TF, and biomarker-drug networks were displayed using Cytoscape software (v 3.10.2).


### The scRNA-Seq Data Processing
  In GSE173896, scRNA-seq data were filtered via the subset function from the Seurat package (v 5.0.1)35 to select high-quality cells (nFeature_RNA between 200 and 5,000, nCount_RNA between 200 and 90,000, genes covered in at least 3 cells, and mitochondrial proportion less than 5%). Subsequently, filtered data were normalized via the NormalizeData function. The FindVariableFeatures function was used to extract the top 2,000 highly variable genes (HVGs). Data scaling was performed using the ScaleData function, and principal component analysis (PCA) results were analyzed using the JackStrawPlot and RunPCA functions to determine the top significant principal components (PCs) (p < 0.05). Uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP) was applied for cell clustering via the FindNeighbors and FindClusters functions (resolution = 1). Subsequently, cells were annotated as different types according to marker genes using the FindAllMarkers function, the singleR package (v 2.4.0),36 and published literature.37 Meanwhile, plots were generated to visualize the expression profiles of specifically highly expressed genes and marker genes. In addition, the ReactomeGSA package (v 1.16.1)38 was utilized to understand the biological pathways in which the annotated cells were involved.

  Moreover, a histogram was created to display the proportions of various annotated cells across all samples. The distribution of biomarkers in all annotated cells was examined between the COPD and control groups. The expression disparities of biomarkers between these 2 groups in the annotated cells were analyzed. Annotated cells with significant differences (p < 0.05) were screened out as core cells, which have been reported in the literature to significantly influence the COPD process.


### Features of Core Cells
  In all samples of GSE173896, the Seurat package (v 5.0.1) was employed to analyze the cell cycle of annotated cells, covering the G1 phase, G2.M phase, and S phase. Core cells underwent a secondary round of dimensionality reduction and clustering (resolution = 0.5) following the method described in The scRNA-Seq Data Processing. Subsequently, the core cells were annotated into different core cell subtypes using the literature.39 Differential analysis was first performed on the subtypes of core cells. The FindMarkers function was utilized (min.pct = 0.25, logfc.threshold = 0.5, adj.p < 0.05) to identify scRNA seq-DEGs. Subsequently, the clusterProfiler package (v 4.15.0.003) was applied to perform GO and KEGG enrichment analyses to elucidate the biological functions of core cells in COPD (adj. p < 0.05). Considering the critical role of TFs in gene expression regulation, the SCENIC algorithm40 was used to evaluate the TF activity of core cell subtypes. The CellChat package (v 1.6.1)41 was utilized to analyze the communication among the annotated cells in COPD and control samples. The aggregated ligand-receptor-mediated cell-interaction network and the signals of the different annotated cells were visualized. The monocle3 package (v 2.14.0)42 was used for pseudo-time analysis to explore the states and subtypes of core cells. Variations in the expression patterns of biomarkers were analyzed during core cell differentiation.


### Clinical Specimen Collection
  The Medical Ethics Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Army Medical University, PLA, approved this study (approval No. 2025-YD387-01), and all participants provided written informed consent. Six consecutive COPD patients who met the GOLD diagnostic criteria between November and December 2025 were enrolled, along with six control samples examined during the same period as controls, ensuring comparable demographic data between the two groups-specifically matching sex and restricting age differences to within five years. Statistical analysis confirmed no significant differences in age or sex between groups. Inclusion criteria for the COPD group strictly followed the 2024 Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease (GOLD) diagnostic standards. Exclusion criteria were concomitant malignancy, autoimmune disease, or systemic corticosteroid use within the past three months. Inclusion criteria for control subjects: All enrolled participants had clinical indications for bronchoscopy examination and were confirmed to receive bronchoscopy by associate chief physicians or senior respiratory physicians. Subjects with COPD were strictly excluded with preoperative pulmonary function tests confirming FEV1/FVC > 0.7. These control individuals mainly suffered from pulmonary shadow, pulmonary tuberculosis, pneumonia, pulmonary infection and early lung adenocarcinoma, without chronic obstructive pulmonary disease. Bronchoscopic biopsy specimens of lung tissue (2–3 mm3) were collected and stored at −80 °C for further analysis.


### Western Blot Analysis
  Lung tissue cell lysates were prepared in RIPA buffer. Proteins were embedded in a 10% SDS-polyacrylamide gel and electrophoretically transferred to PVDF membranes. The membranes were blocked with 5% skim milk for 2 hours, followed by overnight incubation at 4°C with primary antibodies against antibodies against BAG3, MYC and GAPDH (all from Cell Signaling Technology; catalog No. 23842, No. 13987, No. 2118; 1:1000 dilution). After washing three times with TBST, membranes were incubated with the corresponding HRP-labeled secondary antibody at room temperature for 2 hours. Immunoreactive bands were visualized with ECL and quantified using Image-Lab software, with results normalized to GAPDH as an internal reference.


### Statistical Analysis
  All data were processed through R language software (v 4.3.3). Comparisons were analyzed through the Wilcoxon test, and p < 0.05 denoted statistical significance. The detailed sample sizes involved in this study were as follows: training set GSE103174 contained 14 COPD non-smoking samples and 10 non-smoking normal controls; validation set GSE106986 included 8 COPD samples and 5 normal controls; scRNA-seq dataset GSE173896 consisted of 5 COPD samples and 3 normal controls; independent clinical specimens used for Western blot verification included 6 paired COPD and control lung tissues.

## Discussion
  COPD, the third leading cause of death worldwide, is characterized by persistent airflow limitation, chronic inflammation, and emphysema.46 Identifying novel targets to enable precision diagnosis and treatment is therefore a major focus of current COPD research. OS within the ISR disrupts the oxidant-antioxidant balance, leading to the accumulation of reactive ROS that damage DNA, lipids, and proteins and trigger apoptosis. Simultaneously, ROS amplify pro-inflammatory mediators, recruit neutrophils and macrophages, and establish a vicious OS-inflammation cycle.47 Chronic inflammation involving the airways, lung parenchyma, and pulmonary vasculature ultimately produces the characteristic pathological changes of COPD. By integrating bulk RNA-seq and single-cell RNA-seq (scRNA-seq), the present study identified the ISR core markers MYC and BAG3 as highly expressed in T cells, offering potential mechanistic clues and preliminary candidate molecules for further exploring targeted intervention strategies of COPD.48 The current sample size of this study was limited, and the verification scale was insufficient. The two core genes identified in this work could only be regarded as potential research targets related to COPD mechanism, and could not be directly defined as mature clinical diagnostic biomarkers with popularization and application value. More large-sample multi-center cohort verification was required before clinical application.

  In recent years, numerous bioinformatics analyses have explored stress-related molecular alterations in COPD. However, most published studies have only focused on a single subtype of stress response, such as oxidative stress, endoplasmic reticulum stress or inflammatory stress, instead of targeting the entire integrated stress response network. For instance, some studies identified four pivotal endoplasmic reticulum stress-related genes including STC2, APAF1, BAX and PTPN1 that exerted vital roles in COPD via bioinformatic analysis and clinical validation.49 Other investigations verified Hsp70 and HSPA1A as potential biomarkers associated with oxidative stress in COPD.48 Additionally, TNFAIP3 was reported to serve as a dual regulator of inflammation and oxidative stress in COPD.50 Differently, the present study specifically focused on the complete integrated stress response system and aimed to screen core functional genes across the whole ISR axis, which could compensate for the limitations of previous bioinformatic researches concentrating merely on single stress patterns.


### ISR-Related Biomarkers for COPD
  MYC (v-myc myelocytomatosis viral oncogene homolog) is a pivotal proto-oncogene belonging to the transcription factor family. The MYC protein binds DNA to regulate downstream gene expression, promoting cell-cycle progression—for example, by activating cyclin genes—and driving cellular proliferation. Research on MYC has predominantly focused on cancer, where it is frequently mutated in small-cell lung cancer (SCLC).51 In diverse biological contexts, MYC can modulate the cell cycle by antagonizing p21.52 Additionally, RB1 inhibition has been shown to prevent apoptosis in TP53-deficient melanoma cells otherwise induced by MYC suppression.53 While MYC has often been described as a downstream effector in COPD literature, direct evidence supporting its role as a core biomarker has been scarce. Our findings reveal that MYC up-regulation aligns with ISR activation and may contribute to the chronic inflammatory processes underlying COPD.

  BAG3 (Bcl-2-associated athanogene 3) is an essential regulatory gene located on chromosome 16q22.1 and a member of the BAG (Bcl-2-associated athanogene) family. Acting as a co-chaperone of heat-shock protein 70 (HSP70), BAG3 shares the anti-apoptotic properties characteristic of BAG-family proteins.54,55 Under diverse stress stimuli, BAG3 facilitates selective autophagy and sustains adaptive responses that preserve cellular homeostasis. Extensively studied in neurodegenerative diseases, cardiomyopathies, and lung cancer, BAG3 has been shown to promote apoptosis resistance in non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) via interactions with Bcl-2 family members.56 In COPD, chronic exposure to cigarette smoke disrupts the protease–anti-protease balance (eg, functional loss of α1-antitrypsin), generating oxidized or misfolded proteins that overwhelm the ubiquitin-proteasome system (UPS). In this context, the BAG3-mediated autophagy pathway is compensatorily up-regulated: BAG3 orchestrates the formation of protein aggregates and their lysosomal degradation, averting toxic protein accumulation.57 BAG3 is differentially expressed in COPD lung tissue and inversely correlates with lung function, establishing it as a COPD-associated gene with potential predictive value for disease development.58

  In summary, MYC drives a proliferation–apoptosis imbalance, while BAG3 orchestrates the autophagy–apoptosis switch; together they form a “proliferation-autophagy” synergistic axis in COPD. Our nomogram model, constructed with MYC and BAG3, demonstrated excellent performance in predicting COPD onset. Combined inhibition of MYC (via degraders) and enhancement of BAG3-mediated selective autophagy may restore ISR homeostasis, providing novel theoretical hints for subsequent mechanism research and therapeutic exploration.


### Functional Enrichment Analyses in COPD
  Functional enrichment analysis in this study revealed that the ISR-associated DEGs we identified are significantly enriched in three core signaling pathways-JAK-STAT, NF-κB, and IL-17-offering new insights into the molecular pathogenesis of COPD. The JAK-STAT pathway plays a pivotal role in sustaining chronic inflammation in COPD. Upon cigarette-smoke exposure, activation of this pathway triggers macrophages to release abundant pro-inflammatory mediators such as TNF-α and IL-8, which not only recruit neutrophils to the airways but also directly promote alveolar-wall destruction.59 More importantly, STAT3-mediated differentiation of Th17 cells amplifies IL-17-driven inflammatory responses, creating a vicious “inflammation-immune dysregulation” cycle.60 The NF-κB pathway serves as a crucial bridge between oxidative stress and inflammation. In COPD patients, persistent oxidative stress activates NF-κB, up-regulating matrix metalloproteinases to accelerate lung-tissue degradation while simultaneously suppressing the Nrf2 antioxidant system, thereby exacerbating cellular oxidative damage.61,62 This dual role explains the profound imbalance between oxidants and antioxidants in COPD. The IL-17 signaling axis is decisive in airway remodeling: IL-17 not only promotes inflammatory-cell infiltration via MAPK activation but also induces TGF-β1 and MMP-9 expression, driving fibroblast proliferation and collagen deposition that ultimately lead to small-airway fibrosis and airflow limitation.63 The interplay among these three pathways constitutes a central network underlying COPD pathogenesis, providing a theoretical foundation for targeted therapeutic strategies.

  GSEA further demonstrated that our identified biomarkers are significantly enriched in the p53 and MAPK signaling pathways, which, together with the previously described inflammatory network, form a comprehensive molecular atlas of COPD pathogenesis. The p53 pathway exerts dual, seemingly paradoxical roles during COPD progression. Chronic cigarette-smoke exposure induces sustained p53 activation, driving alveolar epithelial cells into premature senescence and triggering the senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP) -including IL-6 and other cytokines-that exacerbates local inflammatory microenvironments.64 Simultaneously, p53 suppresses the Nrf2 antioxidant program, thereby crippling cellular antioxidant capacity and establishing a vicious cycle of oxidative damage.65 Notably, accumulation of p53 mutations in COPD lung tissue markedly increases the risk of malignant transformation, providing a molecular explanation for the COPD-to-lung-cancer continuum.66

  The MAPK signaling cascade finely orchestrates COPD pathology through its sub-pathways. p38 MAPK phosphorylates NF-κB and stabilizes HIF-1α, collaboratively up-regulating IL-8 and MMP-12 to accelerate tissue destruction.66 The JNK arm mediates TGF-β1-driven transdifferentiation of fibroblasts into myofibroblasts, promoting collagen deposition and airway fibrosis.67 Importantly, cigarette smoke activates the ASK1-MAPK axis, establishing a “ROS-MAPK” positive-feedback loop that perpetuates oxidative stress.64 Collectively, our data indicate that DE-ISRRGs coordinate these pivotal pathways via direct modulation of pathway components, epigenetic control, and intercellular communication, thereby laying a bioinformatic foundation for screening potential intervention targets related to these pathways.


### Immune-Infiltration Analysis in COPD
  To further dissect the immune microenvironment of COPD, our immune infiltration analysis identified two immune populations with significantly altered abundance between COPD and control samples: effector memory CD4 T cells (Tem) and eosinophils. Effector memory CD4 T cells are pivotal players in COPD pathophysiology. Previous studies have shown that activation markers such as CD28 are up-regulated on Tem cells in COPD patients. These cells likely orchestrate antigen-dependent inflammatory circuits by polarizing adaptive immunity, thereby sustaining chronic inflammation and disease progression. Moreover, the positive correlation between Tem cells and eosinophils suggests that Tem cells may crosstalk with other immune subsets to jointly shape the COPD immune landscape. Eosinophilic COPD has been recognized as a clinically relevant phenotype defined by elevated blood eosinophil counts (BEC). Patients within this subgroup exhibit superior responses to maintenance inhaled corticosteroid (ICS) therapy. In COPD lungs, increased eosinophil infiltration and activation release inflammatory mediators-including cytokines and granule proteins-that exacerbate airway inflammation and tissue damage, thereby accelerating disease progression.68,69 Our immune-focused analyses further indicate that the ISR may modulate the activity and function of these key immune cells (Tem cells and eosinophils), thereby reprogramming the COPD immune microenvironment and influencing both disease trajectory and therapeutic responsiveness. In summary, effector memory CD4 T cells and eosinophils are critical components of the COPD immune milieu, and their dysregulated function is intimately linked to disease progression and treatment outcomes. A deeper understanding of these interactions may deepen our understanding of immune disturbance characteristics in COPD and lay a preliminary basis for subsequent related immunological research.


### Integrated Regulatory Factor Network in COPD
  Drawing on our signature biomarkers, we constructed an integrated regulatory network encompassing 36 miRNAs, 260 transcription factors, and nine therapeutic compounds.

  At the miRNA level, hsa-miR-4699-3p stabilizes HIF-1α by suppressing PHD2, thereby promoting the polarization of macrophages toward a pro-inflammatory M1 phenotype under hypoxia and exacerbating airway inflammation and emphysematous destruction.70,71 In parallel, this miRNA directly targets SOCS3 to relieve JAK/STAT pathway inhibition, amplifying the release of TNF-α, IL-6, and other pro-inflammatory mediators.72

  Among transcription factors, SNAI2-master regulator of epithelial–mesenchymal transition—disrupts the airway epithelial barrier via E-cadherin repression and promotes fibroblast activation, driving small-airway fibrosis.71 ZNF395, an interferon-stimulated gene activator and direct HIF-1α transcriptional target, intensifies COPD inflammation through a dual mechanism involving hypoxia-driven inflammatory signaling and dysregulated antiviral immunity.70,73 ZBTB43 attenuates antioxidant defenses by repressing NRF2 and related genes, creating a vicious cycle with smoke-induced oxidative injury.74

  Therapeutically, although the broad-spectrum kinase inhibition of staurosporine limits direct clinical use, its derivative PKC412 selectively inhibits PKC-θ to attenuate Th17 differentiation and autoimmune responses.72 Moreover, staurosporine-mediated NF-κB suppression reduces IL-8-driven neutrophil infiltration, providing a rationale for targeting neutrophilic COPD.

  By integrating the miRNA-TF-drug interaction network, we establish the ISR as a central regulator of the COPD immune microenvironment. This framework offers novel molecular targets and a theoretical basis for precision medicine in COPD. This exploratory model possesses certain reference value, and its clinical practicality needs larger sample verification.


### Core Cells in COPD
  In our study, T cells emerge as the central immune population shaping COPD progression; their abundance, subset composition and functional state correlate tightly with disease severity and pulmonary pathology.75,76 Within the COPD immune micro-environment, both CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T cells accumulate markedly in lung tissue, and their density inversely correlates with FEV1. These cells shuttle between airway mucosa and regional lymph nodes via the CCR5/CXCR3 axis, establishing a self-perpetuating “inflammation–immunity cycle” that sustains local inflammation.75

  Mechanistically, CD8⁺ T cells directly lyse airway epithelial cells through the perforin-granzyme pathway, whereas CD4⁺ T cells-especially the Th17 subset-secrete IL-17A to recruit neutrophils, disrupt the protease–antiprotease balance and promote alveolar wall destruction. Concurrently, T-cell-derived IFN-γ drives macrophage polarization toward the M1 phenotype, amplifying oxidative stress.76,77

  Our biomarker MYC critically regulates T-cell function: by up-regulating key glycolytic enzymes (eg, HK2, LDHA), MYC fuels aberrant T-cell activation. Under COPD-associated hypoxia, the HIF-1α–MYC axis becomes hyperactive, enhancing glycolysis in CD8⁺ T cells and pushing them toward terminal effector differentiation accompanied by PD-1 up-regulation and functional exhaustion.78 As COPD advances, MYC expression rises progressively from naïve to effector T cells, while FOXP3 expression declines, indicating that the Th17/Treg imbalance is governed by MYC-driven metabolic-epigenetic crosstalk.79

  The p38 MAPK pathway, a central signaling axis in inflammation and stress responses, is essential for T-cell effector function. It senses inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, TNF-α) and oxidative stress, then phosphorylates transcription factors such as ATF2 and CREB to precisely control the expression of T-cell differentiation genes (eg, RORγt, T-bet) and inflammatory mediators (eg, IL-17, IFN-γ).80 Collectively, this intricate regulatory network illustrates how metabolic reprogramming, functional differentiation and signal transduction in T cells cooperate to drive COPD pathogenesis.

  Nevertheless, several limitations should be acknowledged. Firstly, the overall sample size in public datasets and clinical specimens was relatively small, leading to limited statistical power. The constructed machine learning models had potential overfitting risks, and the stability of AUC and P-value results was restricted. Secondly, we only performed preliminary Western blot verification at the protein level, lacking sufficient qPCR, IHC and comprehensive functional experiments to confirm biological functions. Thirdly, although all enrolled samples were limited to non-smoking populations to eliminate smoking-related confounding factors, incomplete clinical metadata failed to support multivariate adjustment for age, gender and other clinical covariates. Fourthly, our single-cell analysis only covered partial cellular subsets, and the research findings cannot be directly applied to clinical practice.

  Future studies should (1) validate the clinical utility of MYC and BAG3 in larger, multicenter cohorts spanning diverse COPD phenotypes; (2) supplement qPCR and IHC experiments to verify gene expression and tissue localization; (3) leverage CRISPR gene editing and organoid models to dissect the mechanistic roles of MYC/BAG3; and (4) conduct pre-clinical pharmacological evaluations of small-molecule inhibitors targeting these molecules to pave the way for subsequent clinical trials. In addition, we will collect complete clinical information to realize multivariate covariate correction, and conduct in-depth functional experiments to clarify the exact mechanisms of MYC and BAG3, so as to promote the clinical translational value of relevant research conclusions. Advancing these lines of research will bridge the bench-to-bedside divide and ultimately improve clinical outcomes for patients with COPD.


## Conclusion
  By integrating large-scale transcriptomic and single-cell datasets, we have systematically identified MYC and BAG3 as potential biomarkers of the ISR in COPD. We further delineate how these two molecules orchestrate oxidative-stress amplification, inflammatory-signal dysregulation, and immune-microenvironment remodeling through their coordinated regulation of JAK-STAT, NF-κB, IL-17, p53, and MAPK pathways. This exploratory study provides novel mechanistic insights and preliminary candidate molecular clues for in-depth exploration of ISR mechanisms in COPD, which lays a theoretical foundation for subsequent confirmatory experiments and clinical validation research.
