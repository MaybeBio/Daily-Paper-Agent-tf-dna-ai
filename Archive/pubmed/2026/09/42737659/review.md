## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, results, discussion, materials and methods, conclusions) as provided. Supplementary figures and tables are referenced but not supplied.
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, experimental design, data interpretation, and claims as presented in the provided text. No assessment of editorial quality or journal fit beyond Nature-style criteria.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors characterize four σ32-dependent promoters (PdnaK, PgrpE, PibpA, PclpB) for heat-inducible expression of FAST-PETase-sfGFP in E. coli, identify PdnaK as the strongest performer, dissect its three promoter elements (P1, P2, P3), and use molecular docking to provide structural context for observed differences.
- **Visible evidence base** Main text figures (Figures 1–4), supplementary figures (S1–S5) and tables (S1) referenced but not provided. Statistical methods described in Section 4.8.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Supplementary figures and tables (S1–S5, Table S1) are not available. Raw data, plasmid maps, and full promoter sequences are not provided. This limits verification of several quantitative claims and the docking analysis.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript presents a systematic and practically oriented comparison of σ32-dependent promoters for heat-inducible recombinant protein expression. The experimental design is logical, and the use of FAST-PETase-sfGFP as a model is appropriate. The identification of PdnaK as the strongest promoter and the dissection of its elements are useful contributions. However, several technical concerns, particularly around the docking methodology, statistical reporting, and missing supplementary data, prevent full confidence in the conclusions as stated.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in synthetic biology, metabolic engineering, and bioprocess development who require inducible expression systems without chemical inducers. Those working on PET-degrading enzymes and their scalable production would also find the work relevant. The promoter element dissection may interest those studying σ32-dependent transcription regulation.
- **Major strengths** 1) Systematic comparison of four native σ32-dependent promoters under identical conditions. 2) Use of a real-time fluorescent reporter for promoter activity. 3) Dissection of PdnaK into its three elements, providing insight into promoter architecture. 4) Integration of molecular docking to offer a structural hypothesis. 5) Practical relevance for inducer-free recombinant protein production.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Claim pointer** "Molecular docking analysis further supported the experimental observations by showing qualitative agreement between predicted σ32–DNA interactions and promoter performance."
  - **Evidence pointer** Section 2.4, Figure 4, Figure S5
  - **Concern** The docking analysis is presented as supporting the experimental results, but the methodology has significant limitations. The authors use HADDOCK with idealized B-form DNA models generated by ChimeraX, which do not account for sequence-dependent DNA curvature or flexibility. The active residues are defined a priori, and the docking scores are used as a proxy for binding affinity without experimental validation. The claim of "qualitative agreement" is based on a single metric (HADDOCK score) and does not include error estimates or statistical comparison between the three promoter elements.
  - **Why it matters** The docking results are used to provide a structural explanation for the observed promoter activities. If the docking methodology is not robust, this interpretation is weakened, and the claim of agreement between computation and experiment becomes unsupported.
  - **Resolution test** Provide a more rigorous docking analysis, including multiple independent runs, explicit treatment of DNA flexibility, and a statistical comparison of docking scores. Alternatively, validate the predicted interactions with experimental methods such as electrophoretic mobility shift assays or DNase I footprinting. If docking is retained, clearly state its limitations and avoid overinterpreting qualitative trends.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Claim pointer** "PdnaK exhibited the strongest heat-inducible expression and was dissected to examine the autonomous and combinatorial behavior of its promoter-derived elements."
  - **Evidence pointer** Section 2.2, Figure 2, Section 2.3, Figure 3
  - **Concern** The comparison of promoter performance relies on GFP fluorescence normalized to OD600. However, the authors do not report whether the fusion protein (FAST-PETase-sfGFP) is stable and properly folded across all constructs. Differences in mRNA stability, translation efficiency, or protein degradation could confound the fluorescence readout. Additionally, the authors do not report mRNA levels or protein yields, so the fluorescence data may not accurately reflect promoter activity.
  - **Why it matters** The central conclusion that PdnaK is the strongest promoter depends on the accuracy of the fluorescence readout. If the readout is confounded by post-transcriptional effects, the ranking of promoters could be incorrect.
  - **Resolution test** Include measurements of mRNA levels (e.g., RT-qPCR) or protein yields (e.g., Western blot quantification) for at least the key constructs. Alternatively, demonstrate that the fusion protein is stable and that fluorescence correlates with promoter activity across constructs.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that docking results "supported" the experimental observations is not fully supported given the methodological limitations described above.
- The claim that promoter-element composition had no effect on cell growth cannot be evaluated without the growth data.
- The ranking of promoters based on fluorescence alone may be confounded by post-transcriptional effects, which are not addressed.
- The general applicability of the findings to other recombinant proteins is speculative and not tested in this study.

## Assessment against Nature-style criteria
- **Originality** Moderate. The work is a systematic comparison of known promoters rather than a fundamentally new discovery. The element dissection of PdnaK is a useful contribution but not highly novel.
- **Scientific importance** Moderate. The findings are practically useful for those developing heat-inducible expression systems, but the impact is limited to a specific application area.
- **Interdisciplinary readership** Limited. The work is primarily of interest to synthetic biologists and bioprocess engineers. It is unlikely to attract a broad interdisciplinary audience.
- **Technical soundness** Partially sound. The experimental design is reasonable, but the docking analysis and lack of mRNA/protein-level data weaken the conclusions.
- **Readability for nonspecialists** Acceptable. The manuscript is clearly written, but the docking section may be difficult for nonspecialists to follow.

## Recommendation posture
**Currently not established from the provided evidence.** The manuscript addresses a relevant question and provides useful comparative data, but the technical concerns regarding the fluorescence readout, docking methodology, and missing data prevent full confidence in the conclusions. The authors should address these issues and provide the missing supplementary data before the claims can be considered established.
