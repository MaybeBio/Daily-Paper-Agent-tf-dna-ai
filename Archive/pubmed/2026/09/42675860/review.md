## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence as presented in the abstract; no figures, tables, methods, or supplementary materials were provided
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report a systematic screen of 23 Cas12f orthologs, identification of two active nucleases (PspCas12f1 and TcCas12f1) in human cells, sgRNA scaffold optimization, and a combined structure-guided and protein language model-assisted engineering strategy that yielded the variant enPspCas12f1 with editing efficiencies comparable to SpCas9 and high specificity
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no experimental details, quantitative data, or methodological descriptions are available
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, all figures and tables, methods section, supplementary data, sequence information, and statistical analyses

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract describes a potentially valuable engineering effort for compact CRISPR-Cas12f editors, with a plausible integrative design strategy. However, the abstract alone provides insufficient quantitative evidence to evaluate the strength of the claims. The central assertions regarding editing efficiency parity with SpCas9 and high specificity cannot be assessed without the underlying data. The work addresses a relevant problem in genome editing, but the current evidence base is too limited to establish the case.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in genome editing, gene therapy, and protein engineering would be interested. The compact size of Cas12f editors is relevant for adeno-associated virus delivery, and the described engineering strategy may be applicable to other CRISPR systems. Clinicians and translational scientists working on therapeutic genome editing would also find the efficiency and specificity claims relevant.
- **Major strengths** The systematic screening of 23 orthologs is a reasonable approach to identify new editors. The combination of structure-guided design with protein language model filtering is a contemporary and potentially generalizable strategy. The identification of specific mutations (Q100R and E293R) provides concrete engineering outcomes. The compact size of the final variant is a practical advantage for delivery.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
  - **Claim pointer** enPspCas12f1 achieved genome-editing efficiencies comparable to SpCas9 across multiple endogenous loci while maintaining high specificity
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided
  - **Concern** The claim of efficiency parity with SpCas9 is a strong quantitative assertion, but the abstract provides no numerical data, no number of loci tested, no cell types, no delivery methods, and no statistical comparisons. Specificity is mentioned but no off-target analysis is described.
  - **Why it matters** Efficiency and specificity are the two most important performance metrics for a genome editor intended for therapeutic use. Without quantitative evidence, the claim of parity with SpCas9 cannot be evaluated, and the specificity claim is unverifiable.
  - **Resolution test** Provide editing efficiency percentages with standard deviations for enPspCas12f1 and SpCas9 at each tested locus, specify the number of biological replicates, and include off-target assessment data such as targeted amplicon sequencing or whole-genome analysis with defined thresholds.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Methodological transparency
  - **Claim pointer** Candidate mutations predicted by SaProt were further screened based on structural proximity to the DNA-binding interface and electrostatic compatibility
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided
  - **Concern** The abstract does not describe how many candidates were generated by SaProt, what filtering thresholds were applied for structural proximity and electrostatic compatibility, how many candidates were tested experimentally, or how the final two mutations were selected. The rationale for choosing Q100R and E293R over other candidates is not stated.
  - **Why it matters** The engineering strategy is a central contribution of the work. Without details on the filtering criteria and the number of candidates tested, the reader cannot judge whether the approach is systematic or whether the final choice was well justified. Reproducibility requires this information.
  - **Resolution test** Describe the SaProt prediction pipeline, the number of candidate mutations generated, the structural and electrostatic filters applied with specific thresholds, the number of variants experimentally tested, and the selection criteria for Q100R and E293R.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Scope of validation
  - **Claim pointer** We systematically screened 23 Cas12f orthologs and identified two active nucleases, PspCas12f1 and TcCas12f1, capable of genome editing in human cells
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided
  - **Concern** The abstract states that 23 orthologs were screened but does not specify the screening method, the readout used to define activity, the number of loci or cell types tested, or the criteria by which PspCas12f1 and TcCas12f1 were deemed active. The activity of TcCas12f1 is not further discussed in the abstract, leaving its role unclear.
  - **Why it matters** The screening result is the foundation for the rest of the study. If the screening criteria are not defined, the reader cannot assess whether the identification of the two active nucleases was robust or whether other orthologs might have been missed. The fate of TcCas12f1 is also unclear.
  - **Resolution test** Describe the screening assay, the activity threshold used, the number of loci and cell types tested, and clarify whether TcCas12f1 was characterized further or only used as a comparator.

  - **Concern ID** R1-m3

- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1, R1-M2, R1-M3. The abstract lacks quantitative data, methodological detail, and validation scope necessary to support the central claims.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: the combination of protein language model filtering with structure-guided design is a contemporary approach, though the abstract does not establish whether this combination is genuinely novel or an incremental application of existing tools. Scientific importance: compact CRISPR editors are relevant for therapeutic delivery, and improved variants would be valuable, but the importance cannot be fully assessed without efficiency and specificity data. Interdisciplinary readership: the work would appeal to genome editing, protein engineering, and gene therapy communities, but the abstract is written in a way that is accessible to specialists rather than a broad audience. Technical soundness: cannot be evaluated from the abstract alone; the absence of any quantitative results or methodological detail prevents assessment. Readability for nonspecialists: the abstract is concise and generally clear, but terms such as "protein language model" and "structural proximity" are used without explanation, which may limit accessibility.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The abstract describes a plausible and potentially useful engineering effort, but the absence of quantitative data, methodological detail, and validation scope means the central claims cannot be verified. A supportive assessment would require the full manuscript with complete experimental results.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that enPspCas12f1 achieves editing efficiencies comparable to SpCas9 is unsupported by any quantitative data in the abstract.
- The claim of high specificity is unsupported by any off-target analysis or quantitative definition.
- The claim that the integrative strategy is effective is a general conclusion that cannot be evaluated without details on the number of candidates tested and the success rate.
- The identification of PspCas12f1 and TcCas12f1 as active nucleases is stated without supporting screening data.
- The improvement in basal activity from sgRNA scaffold optimization is stated without quantitative evidence.
- The generalizability of the engineering strategy to other compact CRISPR nucleases is speculative and not supported by data in the abstract.
