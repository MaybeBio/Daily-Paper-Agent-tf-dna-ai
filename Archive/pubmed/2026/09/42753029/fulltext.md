# Fused Azine Derivatives Mitigate Long-Term Paracetamol-Induced Liver Fibrosis in Mice: Role of JNK1/ASK1 and Nrf2/HO-1 Signaling Pathways

## Introduction
  The liver is an extremely complex organ that regulates the immune system, hormones, and digestion. Because of its roles in metabolism, detoxification, and immune response, the liver plays a significant role in maintaining physiological homeostasis [1]. Numerous risk factors can impair liver function, leading to acute or chronic injury that may progress to end-stage liver disease. These factors include viruses, alcohol, drugs, metabolites, toxins, and other pathogenic agents [2]. Liver diseases account for 4% of all mortality (1 out of every 25 deaths worldwide) and cause two million deaths every year. Males account for over two-thirds of liver-related deaths. Hepatocellular carcinoma, cirrhosis, and complications of liver fibrosis account for the majority of deaths [3].

  Liver fibrosis is a serious injury that occurs due to massive buildup of extracellular matrix proteins (ECM) as a result of persistent liver inflammation and cell death. Risk factors of liver fibrosis include: hepatitis virus, chemical agents, drugs, alcohol, type 2 diabetes mellitus, as well as obesity. In Liver fibrosis, synthesis and breakdown of ECM components are out of balance. When liver fibrosis is sustained, ECMs accumulate, which disrupts the normal hepatic architecture through the formation of fibrous scars, followed by low blood flow in the liver and deteriorated liver functions, which eventually necessitates liver transplantation [4].

  Drug-induced liver injury (DILI) severity varies from mild injury, which can be improved after stopping the drug, to severe liver injury that can be fatal. Examples of these drugs include: anesthetics, non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, antimicrobial drugs, antibiotics, oral hypoglycemics, lipid-lowering agents, and analgesics [5]. Paracetamol (APAP) is a common analgesic/antipyretic drug used all over the world. As it is reported to be safe in therapeutic doses, it is prescribed as an over-the-counter medication (OTC) in many countries. Patients with osteoarthritis, lower back pain, and migraines typically use it as a first-line, non-addictive analgesic medication to manage their pain and symptoms for a variety of short- and long-term durations [6]. The administration of APAP typically lasts between one week and two years, with a daily dosage ranging from two to four grams. However, because APAP is widely available, patients may be exposed to paracetamol poisoning by accident, which leads to serious hepatic injury. The pathogenic mechanisms of APAP-induced liver injury are complicated and include crosstalk between oxidant stress, inflammation, mitochondrial malfunction, and endoplasmic reticulum stress [7].

  CYP2E1, the most prevalent CYP isoform in the human liver and essential for the metabolism of several xenobiotics, has been confirmed as a crucial enzyme responsible for the generation of an extremely active metabolite, N-acetyl-p-benzoquinone-imine (NAPQI), and the liver injury development. The activity and the content of CYP2E1 are variable with stimulation such as alcohol and drugs [8]. When CYP2E1 breaks down APAP, NAPQI is produced, rapidly combines with glutathione (GSH), an endogenous antioxidant, and depletes GSH, starting the pathogenic mechanism of APAP. Afterwards, NAPQI quickly builds up and combines with other proteins in the cell to create cytotoxic arylated proteins, which lead to oxidative stress and necrosis of the hepatocytes [9]. The transcription of certain inflammatory cytokines, including transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β), interleukins (ILs), and tumor-necrosis factor α (TNF-α), is increased as a result of excessive oxidative stress [10, 11].

  Furthermore, NAPQI-induced mitochondrial oxidant stress triggers apoptosis signal-regulating kinase-1 and the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathway, leading to activation of c-Jun N-terminal kinase (JNK) in the cytoplasm. Usually, ASK-1 and thioredoxin (Trx) form an inactive complex. Excessive ROS generation and inflammatory stimuli, such as TNF-α, also cause liver damage by activating ASK-1, which in turn stimulates JNK. P-JNK then travels to the mitochondria, disrupts the potential of the membrane of the mitochondria, which leads to the opening of the permeability transition pore. Eventually, this results in mitochondrial malfunction and the release of apoptotic agents [12, 13]. Nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (Nrf2) is an oxidative stress-mediated transcription factor that regulates the expression of a variety of downstream signals, including antioxidant enzymes. A previous study demonstrated that Nrf2 is critical for preventing APAP-induced liver fibrosis in mice, and activating Nrf2 may be an ideal strategy for the treatment of APAP-induced liver fibrosis [14].

  For the treatment of illnesses as well as for maintaining general health and well-being, medicinal chemistry is vital to human healthcare. Fused azine derivatives have an important role as their pharmacological activities are diverse. Also, they are commonly utilized as scaffolds in the design of a number of bioactive molecules having acetylcholinesterase inhibitory, antibacterial, anticancer, anti-inflammatory, and hepatoprotective characteristics. The significance of this group is further shown by the finding that several fused azine derivatives produced in our labs can lower the increased measures of hepatic function assessments, such as ALT and AST, in tested animals. Figure 1 illustrates how the structure of these compounds was influenced by tacrine and acetylcholinesterase inhibitors (AchEIs) [15]. Previous studies have been conducted in our laboratory and showed that tacrine derivative compounds have a hepatoprotective effect against CCL4-induced liver damage [16, 17].Fig. 1Rationale for the synthesis of the target compounds

  These studies revealed the importance of a chloro substituent at the 2-position in amplifying the biological activity of these derivatives. Based on the above-mentioned finding, we deemed it interesting to synthesize a 2 sets of 4-phenyl-2-substituted tetrahydroquinolines; one having a p-chloro substitution at the 4-phenyl ring and the other lacking the chloro substitution to investigate whether the introduction of a chloro substituent to the phenyl ring at position 4 of the tetrahydroquinoline skeleton would have the same positive influence as its presence at the 2-position of the pyridyl ring. Furthermore, in our previous studies, replacement of the 2-chloro substituent with an amino group was well tolerated; accordingly, the introduction of a hydroxy group at the 2-position was investigated as a bio-isostere of the amino group. Such groups would potentially form hydrogen bonds with the receptors involved improving their binding. Finally, the replacement of the cyano group at the 3-position with its non-classical bio-isosteric ester group was done.

  Numerous experimental investigations have shown that silymarin functions as an antioxidant by lowering lipid peroxidation and free radical generation. Through its scavenger activity, it lowers the levels of superoxide and peroxynitrite and prevents toxins from attaching to the receptors on the hepatocyte cell membrane. Silymarin also reduces CYP2E1 expression and activity [18].

  Our investigations aimed to assess the hepatoprotective effect of newly synthesized fused azine derivatives to ameliorate APAP-induced liver fibrosis in mice, using silymarin as a reference drug.


## Materials and Methods

### Compounds
  Normal saline 0.9%, Paracetamol (APAP) (dissolved in normal saline 0.9%), and carboxy methyl cellulose sodium (CMC 0.5%) were obtained from El Nasr Pharmaceutical Company (Egypt), Silymarin, purchased from PHARCO-pharmaceuticals Inc. (Egypt) (suspended in CMC 0.5%).


#### Fused Azine Derivatives (1a, 1b, 2a and 2b) Synthesis
  Every chemical utilized in the synthesis was purchased from a commercial source. They were dried and purified using conventional methods if needed. The melting points were determined using the Thomas-Hoover melting point apparatus. Nicolet-800 FT-IR infrared spectrophotometer was used to measure the infrared spectra (IR) of KBr discs of synthesized compounds. A Bruker 300 ultra shield spectrometer was used to determine the NMR spectrum. The internal standard was tetramethyl silane (TMS), and the solvent was DMSO-d6 (chemical shifts are provided in δ/ppm). The results of microanalyses (a purity test) conducted at El Azhar University’s regional Center for Mycology and Biotechnology fell around ± 0.4% of the theoretical values. The compounds’ purity was confirmed, and the reactions were followed using thin-layer chromatography (TLC) on aluminum sheets that had been precoated with silica gel (Type 60 GF254; Merck; Germany). The reported approach was followed in the synthesis of the targeted chemicals [15].


##### Methyl 5,6,7,8-Tetrahydro-2-Hydroxy-4-Phenylquinolin-3-Carboxylates (1a)
  Equimolar amounts of the appropriate benzaldehyde (45 mmol, 4.77 g), cyclohexanone (45 mmol, 4.41 g), dimethyl malonate (45 mmol, 7.2 g), and ammonium acetate (90 mmol, 6.9 g) were heated under reflux in 25 mL ethanol for 3 h. Most of the solvent was removed by evaporation under vacuum, and the residue was left to stand for 24 h to separate yellow crystals. These were filtered, washed with ethanol, dried, and crystallized from ethanol. Yield 45.9%, m.p. 167–168 °C. 1H-NMR (300 MHz, DMSO-d6): δ = 1.63–1.68 (m, 2H, C6-H2), 1.76–1.81 (m, 2H, C7-H2), 2.61–2.66 (m, 2H, C5-H2), 2.77–2.82 (m, 2H, C8-H2), 3.59 (s, 3H, OCH3), 7.21 (s, 1/4H, OH, D2O-exchangeable), 7.32–7.45 (m, 5H, phenyl-H), 7.81 (s, 3/4H, NH, D2O-exchangeable); 13C- NMR (75 MHz, DMSO-d6): δ = 22.4, 27.3, 31.2, 38.4 (cyclohexyl-C), 56.1 (OCH3), 119.4, 121.0, 126.4, 128.7, 132.6, 133.8 152.1, 157.0, 161.8 (Ar–C), 164.3 (ester C = O) [15].

Most of the solvent was removed by evaporation under vacuum, and the residue was left to stand for 24 h to separate yellow crystals. These were filtered, washed with ethanol, dried, and crystallized from ethanol. Yield 42.3%, m.p. 171 °C. 1H NMR (300 MHz, DMSO-d6): δ = 1.64–1.68 (m, 2H, C6-H2), 1.80–1.84 (m, 2H, C7-H2), 2.62–2.67 (m, 2H, C5-H2), 2.76–2.81 (m, 2H, C8-H2), 3.32 (s, 3H, OCH3), 7.43 (s, 1/4H, OH, D2O-exchangeable), 7.51,7.59 (2d, J = 8.0 Hz, each 2H, chlorophenyl-H) and 7.79 (s, 3/4H, NH, D2O-exchangeable); 13C NMR (75 MHz, DMSO-d6): δ = 25.37, 26.29, 31.12, 38.22 (cyclohexyl-C), 56.5 (OCH3), 119.2, 120.6, 127.1, 128.6, 132.5, 132.9, 152.8, 157.0, 165.3 (Ar–C), 165.8 (ester C = O) [15].


##### Chloro-5,6,7,8-Tetrahydro-4-Phenylquinoline-3-Carbonitrile (2a)
  A solution of 5,6,7,8-Tetrahydro-2-hydroxy-4-phenylquinoline-3-carbonitrile (20 mmol, 5.0 gm) in 15 mL phosphorus oxychloride was heated under reflux for 20 h in the presence of a catalytic amount of phosphorus pentachloride. The reaction mixture was cooled and poured onto crushed ice to give a yellowish-orange precipitate, which was filtered, dried, and crystallized from ethanol. Yield 93.3%, m.p. 201–202 °C. 1H-NMR (300 MHz, DMSO-d6): δ = 1.62–1.65 (m, 2H, C6-H2), 1.73–1.76 (m, 2H, C7-H2), 2.32–2.41 (m, 2H, C5-H2), 2.8–2.87 (m, 2H, C8-H2), and 7.23–7.56 (m, 5H, phenyl-H); 13C NMR (75 MHz, DMSO-d6): δ = 22.5, 26.8, 30.9, 35.3 (cyclohexyl-C), 112.2 (CN), 105.6, 128.6, 130.6, 132.8, 134.9, 135.9, 151.5, 152.9, 160.6 (Ar–C) [15].

### Experimental Design
  72 male mice (20–25 g, 6–8 weeks old) were kept under standard conditions for humidity, temperature, light cycle, and unlimited access to food and water. The Animal Care and Use Committee of Alexandria University (ALEXU-IACUC Member of ICLAS) approved this study (approval code AU 06–2024-8–28-1–245), which was performed in compliance with ARRIVE guidelines.

  Animals were divided into 12 groups (6 mice/group) as follows:Group (1): Control (normal saline 0.9%) po daily for 45 days.Group (2): APAP (300mg/kg, dissolved in normal saline 0.9%) po daily for 45 days according to a previous report. [14]Group (3): Silymarin (100mg/kg, suspended in CMC 0.5%) po [16, 17] + APAP (300mg/kg) po daily for 45 days.Groups (4–7): (1a, 1b, 2a, and 2b) drugs (25 mg/kg, suspended in CMC 0.5%) po [16, 17] + APAP (300 mg/kg) po daily for 45 days.Groups (8): (CMC 0.5%) po daily for 45 days.Groups (9–12): The safety profile groups, administered (1a, 1b, 2a, and 2b) compounds respectively (25 mg/kg, suspended in CMC 0.5%, po daily for 45 days).

  The dose of newly synthesized compounds was chosen based on a previous report by Goh et al., who examined tacrine and other cholinesterase inhibitors’ pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic characteristics. The dose of tacrine for therapeutic purposes, which preserved the cholinergic-mediated behaviors of animals, served as the basis for the newly synthesized compounds’ dosage (25 mg/kg/day, po) [19]. 24 h after the last dose, animals were anesthetized using xylazine (10 mg/kg) and ketamine (75 mg/kg), then underwent euthanasia via cervical dislocation. For biochemical examinations, blood samples were obtained through the retro-orbital bleeding technique by penetrating the retro-orbital sinus with a sterile hematocrit capillary tube [20], centrifuged to extract serum, and stored at −80 °C. Harvested livers were cut into portions. One part of the liver tissue was immersed in 10% neutral-buffered formalin and analyzed histologically and immunohistochemically (IHC), while another part was homogenized in PBS (phosphate-buffered saline) and centrifuged to further analyze the supernatant. Liver tissues and homogenates were kept at −80 °C.


### Biochemical Analysis
  Serum alanine transaminase (ALT), aspartate transaminase (AST), and alkaline phosphatase (ALP) activities, together with albumin and total bilirubin (TBIL) levels, were assessed in addition to kidney function tests (creatinine and urea). All diagnostic kits were purchased from Bio-Diagnostics in Egypt, used, and stored based on the manufacturer’s guidelines.

  Liver oxidative stress markers, including Malondialdehyde (MDA) (Catalog number: NBP3-24519, NOVUS BIOLOGICALS, USA) and nitric oxide (NO) (Catalog number: BA0007, Assay Genie, Ireland) levels, were measured. Also, liver antioxidant markers, superoxide dismutase (SOD) activity (Catalog number: MA-SOD, Ray Biotech, USA), and reduced glutathione (GSH) level (Catalog number: E-BC-K030-M, Elabscience, USA), were measured using a colorimetric method in accordance with instructions provided by the manufacturer.

  To determine the safety profile of our newly synthesized compounds (animal groups 9–12), serum ALT, AST, ALP, albumin, total bilirubin, urea, creatinine, and serum lipid profile (triglycerides and total cholesterol) levels were evaluated. The levels of liver oxidative stress markers (MDA and NO) and liver antioxidant markers (SOD and GSH) were assessed to provide additional insight into our compound’s safety profile.

### Histopathological Examination
  After being dissected, liver tissue samples from mice were washed using normal saline. They were fixed for at least 24 h in 10% phosphate-buffered formalin (pH 7.4), and then the samples were embedded in paraffin blocks, cut into 5-μm slices, placed on slides, deparaffinated with xylene, and then rehydrated with ethanol. One section was stained with Mayer’s hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) stain to evaluate activity, whereas the other section was stained with Masson’s trichrome stain to determine fibrosis. After the slides were blindly seen under a light microscope (Leica, DM500) at magnifications of × 100 and × 400, they were photographed using a digital camera (EC3, Leica, Germany) [21]. Fibrosis and activity were evaluated by the METAVIR scoring system. The number of inflammatory foci (lobular necrosis) and the extent of portal inflammation (piecemeal necrosis) were assessed to produce a final histological activity score (A0 = none, A1 = mild, A2 = moderate, and A3 = severe) [22]. Then, slices stained with Masson’s trichrome were examined for fibrosis. The fibrosis was graded using a four-tiered system: F0 no fibrosis, F1 mild/moderate fibrosis, F2 significant fibrosis, F3 advanced fibrosis, and F4 cirrhosis [23]. In addition to histopathological analysis, morphometric analysis offers valuable insights into the structural alterations in liver tissue through quantitative measurement of liver tissue components, such as the extent of fibrosis. Also, by applying morphometric techniques, the severity of liver damage can be assessed more precisely, in addition to tracking disease progression over time. For instance, morphometric studies may reveal an increased area of fibrosis [24].


### Immunohistochemical Staining of α-SMA and Caspase-3
  Liver tissues were fixed in 10% neutral-buffered formalin, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned into 4-µm-thick slices. Tissue sections were deparaffinized in xylene and rehydrated through a graded ethanol series (100%, 95%, and 75%). Heat-induced epitope retrieval (HIER) was performed by heating the sections in Cell Marque Trilogy™ solution using a pressure cooker. Following antigen retrieval, the sections were washed five times with distilled water and phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). Endogenous peroxidase activity was quenched with 3% hydrogen peroxide. The sections were then incubated with primary antibodies diluted in PBS, including anti-α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA) rabbit polyclonal antibody (1:1000; Cat. No. GB111364, Servicebio) and active caspase-3 rabbit polyclonal antibody (1:1000; Cat. No. GB11532, Servicebio), in a humidified chamber (Wet Box, SIB-20U) at 37 °C for 60 min. Following PBS washing, the sections were incubated with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG (H + L) secondary antibody (1:5000; Cat. No. GB23303, Servicebio) at 37 °C for 30 min. Following additional PBS washes, a freshly prepared 3,3′-diaminobenzidine (DAB) working solution, prepared by mixing DAB Chromogen Solution (Reagent B1) and DAB Buffer Solution (Reagent B2) at a 1:1 ratio according to the manufacturer’s instructions, was applied to the sections. Then the sections were incubated until a brown reaction product developed under microscopic observation. Subsequently, the sections were rinsed with distilled water, counterstained with hematoxylin, dehydrated through graded ethanol and xylene, mounted with a permanent mounting medium, and coverslipped. The stained sections were examined under a light microscope.


### Data Analysis
  GraphPad Prism (version 8.0.2) was utilized to perform statistical analysis. After the one-way ANOVA test was completed for the comparison of several groups, the Tukey–Kramer post-hoc test was used to compare multiple groups. The results were shown as mean ± SEM. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.


### Docking Study
  The Molecular Operating Environment (MOE Dock 2015) program (Chemical Computing Group, Montreal, QC) used the MMFF94X force field to conduct computer-assisted simulation docking experiments.


#### Docking Protocol
  Crystal structures of CYP2E1, JNK1, and Ask1 were obtained from the Protein Data Bank (PDB ID: 3GPH, 2NO3, and 5VIO, respectively) and used in the in-silico docking simulations. The builder molecule was used to construct the ligand molecules, followed by energy minimization. Ligands were docked within the active site using the MOE Dock. The lowest energy conformation was selected, followed by recording the ligand interactions, such as hydrogen bonding, arene-H, and arene-arene interactions, in addition to other hydrophobic interactions with the receptors.


### In Silico ADME Prediction
  The pharmacokinetic properties of the synthesized compounds (1a, 1b, 2a, and 2b) were predicted using the SwissADME online tool. The SMILES of each compound were submitted to evaluate key parameters including gastrointestinal (GI) absorption, blood–brain barrier (BBB) permeability, lipophilicity (LogP), and drug-likeness according to Lipinski’s Rule of Five. Additionally, potential interactions with cytochrome P450 enzymes were assessed.

### Effect of Compounds (1a, 1b, 2a and 2b) on Hepatic Protein Biomarkers Levels Measured by ELISA

#### Compounds (1a, 1b, 2a and 2b) Decreased the Hepatic Level of CYP2E1 and Alleviated the Hepatic APAP-Induced Inflammatory Cytokines
  The activity of the cytochrome-P450 (CYP2E1) enzyme was increased in mice administered APAP. Moreover, it was reduced in the treated groups, as illustrated in Fig. 3a. ## Discussion
  Liver fibrosis is the abnormal buildup of fibrillar collagen and extracellular matrix in the hepatocytes. Activation of myofibroblasts and hepatic stellate cells is a crucial step in fibrosis [25]. Previous studies showed that the administration of paracetamol in mice caused liver fibrosis [26, 27]. Our investigation aimed to evaluate the hepatoprotective potential of newly synthesized fused azine derivative compounds (1a, 1b, 2a, and 2b) against the long-term APAP-induced hepatotoxicity in mice.

  Mice that received 300 mg/kg of APAP experienced severe liver damage, indicated by the release of liver enzymes (ALT, AST, and ALP) in the blood circulation, and a notable increase in their activities compared with the control group, in accordance with previous studies [28–30]. Additionally, mice administered paracetamol showed impaired hepatic excretion of bilirubin and had greater levels of total bilirubin, the main breakdown product of hemoglobin, compared with the control group, similar to previous reports [30–32]. Most serum proteins are primarily synthesized in the liver, particularly those produced by parenchymal cells, such as fibrinogen, albumin, coagulation factors, and mostly alpha and beta globulins [33]. When paracetamol was administered, serum albumin levels decreased, which indicated a reduction in total protein levels similar to prior studies [28, 30]. Our study showed that APAP administration caused elevation of urea and creatinine levels compared with control, which is similar to earlier findings [30, 34]. Concomitant administration of our newly synthesized compounds (1a, 1b, 2a, and 2b) with paracetamol led to a lowering of the high levels of total bilirubin, ALT, AST, and ALP, while normal levels of albumin were restored , reflecting the hepatoprotective effect of these compounds. Also, our compounds ameliorated kidney functions by restoring the normal levels of urea and creatinine.

  MDA, the primary byproduct of membrane lipid peroxidation produced when polyunsaturated fatty acids are degraded, is regarded as an essential biomarker of oxidative stress [35, 36]. Additionally, nitric oxide (NO) is a sign of oxidative stress [37]. As CYP2E1 metabolizes APAP into NAPQI, increased CYP2E1 activity may enhance NAPQI formation, thereby increasing susceptibility to APAP-induced hepatotoxicity and oxidative stress [38–40]. This explains why MDA and NO levels were elevated in our APAP group, which is consistent with previous studies [41, 42]. In many hepatic disorders, CYP2E1 inhibition significantly reduces oxidative stress and liver damage [40]. Additionally, the APAP group had decreased levels of SOD and GSH, which are considered antioxidants that can protect liver cells from oxygen-free radicals, [43] In accordance with previous studies [9, 29, 42]. Our results showed that MDA and NO levels were significantly decreased in all compound-treated groups, while SOD activity and GSH levels were raised compared with the APAP group.

  In the present study, the APAP group showed a marked increase in CYP2E1 levels, as stated in previous reports [44, 45]. Notably, CYP2E1 knockout mice exhibit marked resistance to APAP toxicity, supporting the critical role of this enzyme in the bioactivation of APAP to the reactive metabolite NAPQI [45]. The decreased CYP2E1 expression in the treated groups compared with the paracetamol group may reflect the ability of the tested compounds to downregulate CYP2E1 protein expression. This interpretation is consistent with the chronic nature of our study, in which modulation of enzyme expression represents a key regulatory mechanism in mitigating APAP-induced hepatotoxicity, as supported by previous studies [46, 47]. In support of this hypothesis, molecular docking analysis revealed favorable binding interactions of compounds (1b and 2a) with the CYP2E1 active site, indicating a potential structural basis for their modulatory effects and a potential interaction. Also, the observed CYP interaction profile suggested by ADME findings showed that the protective effects of the tested compounds may be associated with modulation of hepatic CYP activity. In this context, the predicted absence of CYP3A4 inhibition among all tested compounds is advantageous, as CYP3A4 is the principal enzyme involved in drug metabolism. Therefore, this finding may suggest a lower risk of major metabolic drug–drug interactions as it reduces the likelihood of broad metabolic suppression, while the selective CYP inhibition pattern may contribute to a targeted modulation of oxidative stress-related metabolic pathways.

  Worth noting, excessive oxidative stress causes transcription of various inflammatory cytokines, including interleukins (ILs), tumor-necrosis factor α (TNF-α), and transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β) [11]. Quiescent hepatic stellate cells (HSCs) are activated and transdifferentiated to a myofibroblast (MFB) phenotype by TGF-β. MFBs are thought to be the primary source of extracellular matrix protein (ECM) and are distinguished by the expression of α-SMA accumulation, which is considered one of the most common markers of HSCs and plays a significant role in liver fibrogenesis [48–50]. Accordingly, liver fibrosis develops, and scarring eventually occurs [51].

  In our study, the APAP group showed increased levels of TGF-β, TNF-α, and JNK1, and decreased IL10 levels, consistent with previous reports [7, 52–54]. Also, our compounds (1a, 1b, 2a, and 2b) showed a significant reduction in levels of TNF-α, TGF-β, and JNK1; in addition, levels of IL-10 were raised in comparison with the paracetamol group, supporting the potential anti-inflammatory effects of our compounds. In addition, the paracetamol group showed intense brown staining in the fibrotic septa, indicating significant α-SMA immune expression and supporting the presence of liver fibrosis, similar to a previous report [55]. The antifibrotic activity of our compounds was demonstrated by the considerable reduction in α-SMA expression in the treated groups (2a, 1b, 1a, and 2b), respectively.

  TNFα is crucial in liver inflammation and fibrosis. It triggers NF-KB, which is the motivating force of inflammation, and activates TGF-β, the main fibrogenic marker. Furthermore, NF-KB promotes HSCs differentiation, which in turn stimulates activated myofibroblast survival and production [56]. Furthermore, TGF-β has been reported to activate the JNK signaling pathway [57]. Contrarily, interleukin 10 (IL-10), an anti-inflammatory cytokine, plays a crucial role in preventing inflammation, lowering liver damage, directly decreasing collagen synthesis, raising collagenase, and lowering TNFα levels to avoid hepatic fibrosis [58, 59].

  According to our research, the paracetamol group showed higher levels of ASK1 and Bax compared with the control group, as in an earlier study [60]. Our compounds (1a, 1b, 2a, and 2b) significantly declined ASK1 and Bax levels in comparison with paracetamol. In addition, the paracetamol group exhibited intense brown staining of caspase-3, indicating that APAP administration resulted in increased apoptosis, as shown in a previous study [7]. However, the treated groups (1b, 1a, and 2b) had significantly lower levels of caspase-3 expression compared with the diseased group. Compound 2a exhibited the most pronounced anti-apoptotic effect among the derivatives investigated.

  Apoptosis signal-regulating kinase-1 (ASK-1) was triggered by excessive oxidative stress and other inflammatory stimuli, such as TNF-α [61]. ROS oxidizes thioredoxin (TRX), causing the ASK1–TRX complex to dissociate, and then ASK1 is activated. Ultimately leading to cytoplasmic phosphorylation of JNK, activation of the JNK pathway, and necrosis [62]. P-JNK enters the mitochondria and opens the permeability transition pore, causing the potential of the mitochondrial membrane to be lost [13]. Continuous activation of the JNK signaling pathway results in the constant release of ROS, which, in turn, causes Bax activation and translocation to mitochondria. Sequentially, the mitochondrial permeability transition (MPT) pore opens, causing the membrane potential to collapse and ATP to be depleted. MPT promotes the release of mitochondrial intermembrane proteins, such as apoptosis-inducing factor, that cause liver damage [12]. Apoptosis is a tightly controlled process in which bcl-2, Bax, and Bak serve as crucial regulatory molecules while caspase-3, −7, and −9 act as executioners [63]. Fibrosis and apoptosis are directly related to one another. Also, TGF-β is secreted by macrophages, which clear apoptotic bodies through phagocytosis in tissues, activating HSCs, leading to liver fibrosis [64, 65].

  Oxidative stress and inflammation crosstalk mediate the pathogenic pathways of APAP-induced liver damage [7]. Excessive oxidative stress triggers a variety of compensatory signaling pathways to reverse APAP hepatotoxicity, such as the nuclear factor erythroid 2–related factor 2 (Nrf2) signaling pathway. In response to NAPQI-induced oxidative stress, Nrf2 is activated; as a result, the transcription of antioxidant enzymes is increased, such as quinone oxidoreductase 1 (NQO-1), heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1), and microsomal epoxide hydrolase, in addition to NADPH. These antioxidant and detoxification enzymes enhance cellular defense mechanisms, thereby limiting NAPQI-induced oxidative damage and protecting against APAP-induced hepatotoxicity [12, 66]. Worth noting, while APAP treatment activates JNK, NAPQI induces the activation and nuclear accumulation of Nrf2. P‐JNK phosphorylates the Nrf2 domain, triggering its degradation. As a result, the Nrf2‐directed transcriptional program is downregulated, and, consequently, the detoxification and cytoprotection system is impaired, contributing to APAP hepatotoxicity [67–69].

  In our study, the APAP group showed lower levels of Nrf2 and HO-1 compared with the control group, as in previous studies [70–72]. Our compounds (1a, 1b, 2a, and 2b) displayed a remarkable increase in Nrf2 and HO-1 levels when compared with the paracetamol group, supporting their potential antioxidant and hepatoprotective effects. A previous study showed that liver fibrosis induced by long-term ingestion of APAP could be attenuated by reducing liver oxidative stress injury, hepatic collagen deposition, and HSCs activation via activating the Nrf2 antioxidant signaling pathway [14].

  Furthermore, our fused azine derivatives effectively restored the paracetamol-induced deterioration of the hepatic tissues, supporting the hepatoprotective effect of (1a, 1b, 2a, and 2b) compounds through histological evaluation utilizing H&E and Masson’s Trichrome stains. The liver tissues of the paracetamol group exhibited hepatic hemorrhage, portal inflammation with mononuclear cell infiltration, multifocal necrosis, and widespread fibrous connective tissue proliferation in the hepatic lobules and portal areas, as shown in previous studies [29, 73]. All the treatment groups showed varying levels of protection against the harmful effects of paracetamol through improving activity scores and fibrosis scores. The (2a) group exhibited the greatest protective effect on the liver sections. These findings were consistent with the biochemical and molecular results, further supporting the potential hepatoprotective effects of the investigated compounds.

  To evaluate the safety profile of the synthesized compounds, serum liver and kidney function markers, lipid profile, oxidative stress, and antioxidant markers were assessed. No significant differences were observed between the CMC and treatment groups. Furthermore, histopathological examination of liver tissues using H&E and Masson’s trichrome staining revealed normal hepatic architecture with no evidence of inflammation or fibrotic changes.

  Our docking studies revealed that compounds having a 1-chloro substituent in their structure, such as compounds 1b and 2a, regardless of the position of the chloro atom in their structure, showed superior binding patterns to all three receptors (CYP2E1, JNK-1, and ASK-1) superior over both chloro-free compounds (1a) and the dihalogenated compound (2b). Results indicated compound 2a to be superior in most of the biological testing that was conducted, indicating that the presence of the 2-chloro and 3-cyano groups remained superior to any bio-isosteric replacements. Furthermore, it was clear that the introduction of the p-chloro substituent to the 4-phenyl ring was of no value and not comparable to the effect of 2-chloro substitution at the pyridyl ring. Notably, compound 2a emerged as the most favorable candidate, exhibiting a reduced risk of CYP-mediated drug–drug interactions. Our docking outcomes were compatible with the experimental measurements obtained.

  Despite the well-established roles of the Nrf2/HO-1 and ASK1/JNK signaling pathways in liver injury, the precise molecular interplay between these pathways has not yet been fully elucidated. Although our findings suggest concurrent modulation of these pathways, additional mechanistic investigations are warranted to clarify their potential crosstalk. In addition, further studies, including CYP2E1 kinetic and pharmacokinetic analyses, are also warranted to better define the underlying mechanisms and their potential clinical implications.


## Conclusion
  Taken together, the present findings suggest that the newly synthesized fused azine derivatives, particularly compound 2a, exert promising hepatoprotective effects against long-term APAP-induced liver fibrosis. They improved hepatic and renal function, attenuated oxidative stress, inflammation, apoptosis, and fibrogenesis, accompanied by modulation of CYP2E1, ASK1/JNK1, and Nrf2/HO-1 signaling. These findings support the potential contribution of these pathways to the hepatoprotective activity of the synthesized derivatives. In addition, molecular docking analyses were consistent with the experimental observations and supported the predicted interactions of the synthesized compounds with the investigated molecular targets, while in silico ADME profiling suggested a favorable pharmacokinetic profile and a low potential for CYP-mediated drug–drug interactions, particularly for compound 2a. Nevertheless, additional preclinical and clinical studies are warranted to confirm these findings and evaluate the therapeutic potential of these compounds in liver fibrosis.
