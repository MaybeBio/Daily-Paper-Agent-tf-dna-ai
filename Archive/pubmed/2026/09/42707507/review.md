## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, supplementary figure legends referenced but not provided)
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific and technical evaluation of the manuscript as submitted to Bioinformatics Advances; assessment is based solely on the provided text
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors present ESM-2-based transformer classifiers with LoRA fine-tuning for predicting DNA-binding and RNA-binding proteins from human protein sequences, trained exclusively on experimentally validated datasets. They report AUROC values of 0.84 (DBP) and 0.92 (RBP), MCC values of 0.40 and 0.46, and demonstrate via value-aware attention (VAT) scores that model attention localizes to annotated nucleic acid-binding domains. The authors claim this establishes an interpretable and scalable framework for proteome-wide characterization of protein-nucleic acid interactions.
- **Visible evidence base** Main text with Methods (Sections 2.1-2.7), Results (Sections 3.1-3.4), Discussion (Section 4), Table 1 referenced, Figures 1-4 referenced (main figures and supplementary figures not provided in the submitted material)
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** All figures and tables (Fig. 1A-B, Fig. 2A-B, Fig. 3A-C, Fig. 4A-D, Table 1, supplementary figures), exact MCC formula (equation appears corrupted in text), detailed hyperparameter settings, dataset composition details, baseline model configurations, and code repository contents

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript addresses a relevant problem in computational biology, namely the prediction of nucleic acid-binding proteins with interpretability. The use of experimentally validated datasets is a strength relative to prior work relying on inferred labels. However, the manuscript as presented has several critical gaps. The performance metrics are modest, particularly the MCC of 0.40 for DBP prediction, and the comparison to baselines is incomplete. The interpretability analysis, while conceptually sound, lacks quantitative validation against independent benchmarks. The negative class construction is problematic, as the authors acknowledge that "non-binding" proteins may include uncharacterized binders. The manuscript would benefit from clearer reporting of experimental details and a more rigorous evaluation of the interpretability claims. The writing is generally clear but some methodological details are underspecified.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Computational biologists working on protein function prediction, particularly those developing or applying protein language models. Researchers studying DNA- and RNA-binding proteins, especially in human contexts, may find the interpretability framework useful for generating hypotheses about functional regions. The work may also interest method developers in the area of attention-based model interpretation for biological sequences.
- **Major strengths** (1) Use of experimentally validated datasets rather than computationally inferred labels, which addresses a known limitation in the field. (2) The interpretability analysis via VAT scores provides a mechanism for linking model predictions to biological function, which is valuable for hypothesis generation. (3) The comparison of fine-tuned versus untuned model attention patterns is a useful control that helps establish that the observed patterns are not artifacts of fine-tuning. (4) The framework is presented as broadly applicable to other protein function prediction tasks.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that the models achieve "robust predictive performance" with AUROC of 0.84 (DBP) and 0.92 (RBP), and MCC of 0.40 and 0.46, respectively.
  - **Evidence pointer** Section 3.2, Figure 2B, Table 1
  - **Concern** The reported MCC values are modest, particularly for the DBP model (0.40). The manuscript does not report precision, recall, F1-score, or specificity values in the main text, despite stating these were computed. Without these metrics, it is difficult to assess whether the models are practically useful. Furthermore, the threshold optimization procedure (maximizing MCC) may lead to optimistic estimates when evaluated on the same data used for threshold selection, even within cross-validation. The authors do not clarify whether thresholds were selected within each training fold or on the full dataset.
  - **Why it matters** The core claim of the manuscript is that these models provide accurate prediction. If the performance is only modest and the evaluation procedure is not fully described, the practical utility of the framework is not established.
  - **Resolution test** Report all computed metrics (precision, recall, F1, specificity) with confidence intervals across folds. Clarify the threshold selection procedure, specifically whether thresholds were chosen within each training fold. Provide calibration plots or reliability curves. Consider reporting performance at a fixed operating point (e.g., 0.5) in addition to the optimized threshold.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that "proteins predicted as nucleic acid-binding were enriched for known binding domains" and that VAT scores are "significantly higher in the positive predicted groups than in the negative groups" (P = .011 and P = .017).
  - **Evidence pointer** Section 3.3, Figure 3A-C
  - **Concern** The enrichment analysis compares predicted positives against predicted negatives, which is circular in the sense that the model's own predictions define the groups. This does not validate that the model identifies true biological signal; it only shows that the model's predictions correlate with domain annotations. A more rigorous approach would compare predicted positives against experimentally validated negatives, or at minimum, validate predictions against an independent dataset. Additionally, the Mann-Whitney U test is applied to VAT scores within the top 10 IPR domains, but the selection of these domains is not described. Were these domains selected based on the enrichment analysis, creating a selection bias?
  - **Why it matters** The interpretability claim is central to the manuscript. If the enrichment analysis is circular, the biological relevance of the attention patterns is not established.
  - **Resolution test** Validate predictions against an independent experimentally derived dataset. Compare VAT scores in predicted positives against a set of proteins experimentally confirmed as non-binding. Describe the domain selection procedure and correct for multiple testing. Consider using a permutation-based null model for the enrichment analysis.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Claim pointer** The authors state that "the untrained model displayed a similar preference for annotated DNA- or RNA-binding domains prior to optimization" and that "these domain-level features are intrinsic to the pretrained sequence representations."
  - **Evidence pointer** Section 3.4, Figure 4 and supplementary Figure 4
  - **Concern** This is a potentially important observation, but the comparison between fine-tuned and untuned models is not quantified. The authors state that peak coordinates "closely matched" but do not provide a quantitative measure of similarity. Moreover, if the untuned model already shows the same attention patterns, the value added by fine-tuning for interpretability is unclear. The authors should quantify the similarity (e.g., correlation of VAT profiles, overlap of peak regions) and discuss what fine-tuning contributes beyond the pretrained representations.
  - **Why it matters** The claim that fine-tuning preserves or enhances interpretability is not supported without a quantitative comparison. If the untuned model already provides the same interpretability, the contribution of the fine-tuning step is diminished.
  - **Resolution test** Provide quantitative comparison of VAT profiles between fine-tuned and untuned models (e.g., Pearson correlation, peak overlap coefficient). Report the fraction of peaks that fall within annotated domains for both models. Discuss the specific contribution of fine-tuning to interpretability.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M4
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Claim pointer** The authors state that "all positive annotations were derived exclusively from experimental evidence" and that the negative class comprises "proteins without experimental evidence" of binding.
  - **Evidence pointer** Section 2.1, Section 4 (Discussion)
  - **Concern** The negative class construction is a significant concern. Proteins without experimental evidence of binding are not confirmed non-binders; they may simply be uncharacterized. The authors acknowledge this in the Discussion but do not address it in the evaluation. The use of such "silver standard" negatives can lead to inflated performance estimates if some negatives are actually true binders. The authors mention that "a subset of proteins classified as false positives by the model was independently annotated as DNA- or RNA-binding in UniProt," which confirms this concern. The manuscript should quantify the potential impact of this issue on the reported metrics.
  - **Why it matters** The reliability of the performance estimates depends on the quality of the negative set. If a substantial fraction of negatives are true binders, the reported AUROC and MCC values may be underestimated, and the model may be better than reported. Conversely, if the negative set is biased toward easy negatives, performance may be overestimated.
  - **Resolution test** Estimate the proportion of potential false negatives in the negative set using independent annotations. Perform sensitivity analysis by removing proteins with any annotation suggestive of binding from the negative set and re-evaluating. Report performance on a curated negative set of proteins with strong evidence of non-binding, if such a set can be constructed.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M5
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Scientific importance
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that their approach "addresses the principal weakness of feature-based methods" and that the framework is "broadly applicable to other protein function prediction tasks."
  - **Evidence pointer** Section 1 (Introduction), Section 4 (Discussion)
  - **Concern** The novelty of the approach is not fully established. The authors mention prior PLM-based methods for nucleic acid-binding protein prediction (e.g., ESM-DBP, TransBind, DRBP-EDP) but do not clearly articulate what is new about their contribution beyond the use of experimentally validated data. The use of VAT scores for interpretability has been described previously (Guo et al. 2024), and the authors do not explain what is novel about their application. The claim of broad applicability is not supported by experiments on other tasks.
  - **Why it matters** For a methods paper, the novelty of the contribution must be clearly articulated. If the approach is largely a reapplication of existing methods with a different dataset, the scientific contribution is limited.
  - **Resolution test** Clearly state the novel contributions relative to prior work. If the contribution is the use of experimentally validated data, demonstrate the impact of this choice on performance and interpretability. If the contribution is the interpretability framework, provide a more detailed analysis of what VAT scores reveal beyond existing attention-based methods. Consider adding experiments on at least one additional protein function prediction task to support the claim of broad applicability.

- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. ## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that the models "reveal biologically meaningful sequence determinants of binding" is not fully supported, as the enrichment analysis is circular and the comparison with untuned models is not quantified.
- The claim that the framework is "broadly applicable to other protein function prediction tasks" is unsupported, as no experiments on other tasks are presented.
- The claim that the use of experimentally validated data "reduces the risk of false positives" is plausible but not directly demonstrated.
- The claim that the models "outperform existing predictors" is based on comparisons that are not fully described in the provided text; the baseline configurations and evaluation details are not provided.
- The claim that VAT scores "highlight the sequence segments most influential for individual predictions" is not validated against any ground truth or independent benchmark.
- The statement that "the untrained model displayed a similar preference for annotated DNA- or RNA-binding domains" is not supported by quantitative data in the provided text.
