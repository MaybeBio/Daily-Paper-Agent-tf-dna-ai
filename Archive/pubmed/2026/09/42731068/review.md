## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence presented in the abstract; no access to full manuscript, figures, tables, or supplementary materials
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report two siblings from a consanguineous Kurdish family with severe short stature, biochemical features of growth hormone deficiency, microcephaly, developmental delay, and a distinctive facial phenotype. Whole-exome sequencing identified a homozygous ZSWIM6 variant (c.3119G>A, p.Arg1040His). Functional assays in HEK293 cells using dual-luciferase reporters for HECW2 and ZIC2 promoters, plus AlphaFold structural modelling, were used to compare this variant with two previously described heterozygous variants (p.Arg1163Trp and p.Arg913Ter). The authors conclude that ZSWIM6 is a transcriptional regulator and that allele-specific effects on HECW2 and ZIC2 activation explain clinical heterogeneity.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no raw sequencing data, functional assay details, statistical analyses, structural modelling outputs, or clinical measurements are provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, methods section, figures and tables, supplementary data, clinical phenotyping details, segregation data, variant curation evidence, and statistical reporting

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a potentially novel finding, namely the first homozygous ZSWIM6 variant associated with a syndromic short stature phenotype. The proposed genotype-phenotype correlation, supported by in vitro transcriptional assays and structural modelling, is intellectually interesting. However, the evidence base visible in the abstract is insufficient to establish the core claims. Key limitations include the absence of functional validation in relevant cell types, lack of statistical detail, no demonstration of specificity for the proposed promoter targets, and no evidence that the in vitro effects translate to the clinical phenotype. The claim of a "novel phenotype" requires more rigorous clinical delineation and comparison with existing ZSWIM6-related disorders. The abstract is well written and the hypothesis is clear, but the scientific case is not yet established from the provided material.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Clinical geneticists and pediatric endocrinologists would be interested in the expansion of the ZSWIM6-associated phenotypic spectrum, particularly the link to growth hormone deficiency. Molecular biologists studying transcriptional regulation and developmental disorders would find the functional dissection of ZSWIM6 variants relevant. The work may also appeal to researchers using in silico structural predictions to interpret variant effects.
- **Major strengths** The identification of a homozygous variant in a gene previously associated only with heterozygous de novo variants is a notable observation. The use of functional assays to compare three distinct variants in a single study is a commendable approach. The integration of clinical, genetic, and functional data, albeit at a preliminary level, provides a coherent narrative.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Functional evidence  
- **Claim pointer** "Functional assays confirmed ZSWIM6 is a transcriptional regulator of the HECW2 and ZIC2 promoters."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, Methods and Results sections; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract states that dual-luciferase reporter assays were performed in HEK293 cells, but no data are shown. There is no mention of experimental replicates, positive or negative controls, dose-response experiments, or statistical analysis. The claim that ZSWIM6 regulates these promoters is presented as definitive, but the evidence is not visible.  
- **Why it matters** The entire genotype-phenotype correlation rests on the assumption that the observed transcriptional effects are real and specific. Without robust experimental evidence, the functional conclusions are unsupported.  
- **Resolution test** Provide full reporter assay data, including at least three independent experiments, appropriate controls (e.g., empty vector, mutant promoter constructs), and statistical tests. Show that the effects are specific to ZSWIM6 and not due to off-target effects.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Clinical validity  
- **Claim pointer** "We report the first-ever homozygous ZSWIM6 variant, causing a novel phenotype of severe syndromic short stature with biochemical features consistent with GH deficiency and developmental delay."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, Introduction and Conclusion; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract does not provide details on the clinical workup, such as growth hormone stimulation test results, IGF-1 levels, bone age, or exclusion of other causes of short stature. The term "biochemical features of GH deficiency" is vague. Additionally, no information is given on whether the variant segregated with the phenotype in the family or whether other genetic causes were excluded.  
- **Why it matters** Establishing a causal link between a novel variant and a phenotype requires rigorous clinical characterization and genetic evidence. Without these, the claim of a "novel phenotype" is premature.  
- **Resolution test** Include detailed clinical data, segregation analysis of the variant in all available family members, and evidence that other known causes of short stature and developmental delay were excluded.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Variant interpretation  
- **Claim pointer** "The p.Arg1040His variant reduced ZIC2 activation (loss-of-function), p.Arg1163Trp enhanced ZIC2 activation (gain-of-function), and p.Arg913Ter specifically increased HECW2 activation (derepression)."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, Results; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract assigns distinct functional consequences to each variant, but the magnitude of these effects, the baseline activity levels, and the statistical significance are not reported. The term "derepression" implies a specific mechanism that is not explained. Furthermore, the relevance of these in vitro effects to the in vivo phenotype is not established.  
- **Why it matters** The proposed allele-specific effects are central to the genotype-phenotype correlation. Without quantitative data and mechanistic insight, these conclusions are speculative.  
- **Resolution test** Provide quantitative data for each variant's effect on both promoters, including fold-change relative to wild-type, statistical significance, and a clear explanation of the proposed mechanism for each effect.

- **Concern ID** R1-M4  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Structural modelling  
- **Claim pointer** "The 3D structural modelling predicted that p.Arg1040His and p.Arg1163Trp alter local surface charge without disrupting the overall fold, while p.Arg913Ter truncates ZSWIM6, potentially affecting the DNA binding interactions."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, Methods and Results; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract mentions AlphaFold predictions but provides no details on the confidence of the models, the specific residues affected, or how the predictions were validated. The statement that p.Arg913Ter "potentially" affects DNA binding is speculative and not supported by experimental evidence.  
- **Why it matters** Structural predictions are only as reliable as their validation. Without experimental confirmation (e.g., DNA binding assays, protein stability measurements), these predictions add little to the functional claims.  
- **Resolution test** Show the AlphaFold models with confidence scores, highlight the affected residues, and provide experimental validation of the predicted effects on DNA binding or protein function.

- **Concern ID** R1-M5  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Generalizability  
- **Claim pointer** "Our findings demonstrate that ZSWIM6 is a key transcriptional regulator and that allele-specific disturbances drive the marked clinical heterogeneity of ZSWIM6-related disorders."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, Conclusion; location not provided  
- **Concern** The conclusion that allele-specific disturbances drive clinical heterogeneity is based on a comparison of only three variants, two of which are from unrelated conditions. The abstract does not discuss potential confounding factors, such as genetic background, environmental influences, or other modifying variants.  
- **Why it matters** Overgeneralizing from a small number of variants could mislead the field. The claim of "key transcriptional regulator" requires broader evidence, such as ChIP-seq or transcriptomic data.  
- **Resolution test** Acknowledge the limitations of the study and provide additional evidence, such as transcriptomic analysis or validation in patient-derived cells, to support the general claim.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that ZSWIM6 is a "key transcriptional regulator" is unsupported by the abstract alone, as only two promoter targets were tested in a single cell line.
- The assertion that the homozygous variant "causes" the phenotype is not supported without segregation data and exclusion of other genetic causes.
- The functional distinction between loss-of-function, gain-of-function, and derepression is not quantitatively supported in the abstract.
- The structural predictions are presented as definitive but lack validation and confidence metrics.
- The "novel phenotype" claim requires more detailed clinical comparison with existing ZSWIM6-related disorders to be substantiated.
