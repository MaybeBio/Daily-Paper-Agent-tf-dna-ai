# Single-cell and machine learning identify a trihydroxybutyrylation-related prognostic signature in esophageal cancer


## Background
  Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) is a highly aggressive malignancy with a poor prognosis. This study identifies malignant epithelial subtypes and establishes prognostic biomarkers through single-cell transcriptomics and integrative machine learning approaches.


## Methods
  We re-analyzed the single-cell RNA sequencing data (GSE188900) and identified malignant epithelial cells using inferCNV. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) among malignant epithelial subtypes were identified and intersected with lysine β-hydroxybutyrylation (Kbhb)-related genes. Candidate genes were screened using Cox, least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO), and extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost) to construct a random survival forest (RSF) prognostic model. Model performance was validated in GSE53625, TCGA-ESCC, and GSE53624 cohorts. Finally, the expression of prognostic genes was detected by quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR).


## Results
  Single-cell RNA sequencing identified three distinct malignant epithelial cells in ESCC. Among them, the Malignant2 subtype exhibited stemness-related characteristics, initiated tumor differentiation, and was potentially regulated by the transcription factor (TF) SOX11. There were 659 genes identified by intersecting Malignant2-related DEGs with Kbhb-related genes. Based on these genes, a seven-gene prognostic model was constructed, and a corresponding risk score was calculated. Correlation analysis showed that the risk score was positively associated with tumor stage. qPCR results confirmed that the expression patterns of the prognostic genes were consistent with the computational analysis. The enrichment of Kbhb-related genes in the Malignant2 subtype suggests a potential link between metabolic reprogramming and epigenetic regulation in ESCC progression.


## Conclusions
  We identified distinct malignant epithelial subtypes in ESCC and developed a seven-gene prognostic signature based on Kbhb-related genes. This model demonstrated robust predictive performance and was significantly associated with tumor stages.


## Introduction
  Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) is the most frequent histological subtype of esophageal carcinoma (ESCA) (1), one of the most prevalent malignant tumors of the digestive system (1). ESCC is particularly prevalent in high-incidence regions such as East Asia (2). Numerous risk factors, including chronic tobacco use, poor eating habits, nutritional deficiencies, and genetic susceptibility, might contribute to the development of ESCC (3,4). Epidemiological studies have shown that ESCA ranks among the leading cancers in terms of both incidence and mortality globally, and the disease burden of ESCC is especially severe in China (5,6). Despite advances in endoscopic screening and treatment (7,8), the overall prognosis of ESCC remains unsatisfactory (9), primarily because the absence of early diagnostic tools often results in late-stage identification, leading to rapid progression (10,11). Therefore, there is an urgent need for novel biomarkers and therapeutic targets to refine diagnosis and improve the survival of patients with ESCC.

  Post-translational modifications (PTMs), such as phosphorylation, acetylation, represent a sophisticated layer of protein regulation that orchestrates protein structure and function (12,13). By precisely modulating key biological processes, PTMs maintain cellular homeostasis (14). In recent years, the dysregulation of PTM patterns has been recognized as a hallmark of oncogenesis and tumor progression (15,16). Lysine β-hydroxybutyrylation (Kbhb) is a newly identified type of PTM that was first discovered on histones (17,18). This modification can modulate chromatin architecture and transcriptional activity, thereby influencing diverse cellular processes (19). Although research into the oncogenic role of Kbhb is in its infancy, burgeoning evidence suggests that its aberrant expression is closely linked to tumor proliferation, invasion, and chemoresistance across various malignancies (20,21). However, the specific contribution of Kbhb to the pathogenesis of ESCC remains poorly understood. Therefore, investigating the role of Kbhb in ESCC is essential to identifying potential diagnostic markers and therapeutic targets, thereby improving patient outcomes.

  In the present study, we integrated single-cell transcriptomic data with bulk RNA sequencing data to systematically elucidate the intratumoral heterogeneity of ESCC. Using the single cell transcriptomic data, we identified 3 distinct malignant epithelial subtypes and explored the potential regulatory networks. Besides, the incorporation of large-scale bulk RNA sequencing data facilitated the identification of a robust Kbhb-related gene signature, offering novel insights into the metabolic and functional reprogramming of tumor cells. Furthermore, we constructed a Kbhb-based prognostic signature to evaluate its correlation with patient survival, immune infiltration, and tumor mutational burden (TMB). This integrative framework not only refines clinical risk stratification but also identifies potential therapeutic vulnerabilities for personalized management of ESCC (Figure 1). We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2026-0900/rc).


## Methods

### Data source
  We retrieved the ESCC single-cell RNA sequencing dataset (GSE188900) from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (Table 1). Bulk transcriptome datasets of ESCC were collected from both The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA, https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/) and the GEO database, including the training cohort GSE53625, as well as two external validation cohorts, TCGA and GSE53624. To ensure the integrity and reliability of survival analyses, only patients with complete clinical survival information were retained, and those with an overall survival (OS) time of less than 30 days were excluded. Furthermore, 1,493 genes linked to Kbhb were extracted from previously published works (22,23) (available online: https://cdn.amegroups.cn/static/public/jtd-2026-0900-1.xlsx). The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments.


### Single-cell RNA sequencing data preprocessing
  The number of discovered genes per cell (nFeature), the number of transcripts per cell (nCount), and the percentage of mitochondrial transcripts (percent.mt) were used to perform quality control on the single-cell RNA sequencing dataset GSE188900. Cells that did not satisfy quality standards were not included in further examinations. The NormalizeData function in the Seurat R package was applied to normalize the retained data. To guarantee a mean of zero and a standard deviation of one, highly variable genes (HVGs) were first discovered with the FindVariableFeatures function and then scaled by the ScaleData function. Batch effects were adjusted via the RunHarmony function to lessen technical variance across samples.


### Single-cell clustering and cell type annotation
  Principal component analysis (PCA) and nonlinear techniques such as uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP) and t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE) were applied to reduce the dimensionality of the single-cell RNA sequencing dataset GSE188900. Clustering was conducted with the FindNeighbors and FindClusters functions implemented in the Seurat R package. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in each cluster were identified using the FindAllMarkers function, which compared one cell population against all others to detect significantly upregulated or downregulated genes. Cell type annotation was subsequently performed by referencing previously published canonical marker genes reported in well-established studies (24,25).


### Identification and characterization of tumor cell subtypes
  Copy number variations (CNVs) in ESCC tumor cells were inferred using the inferCNV tool, with normal squamous epithelial cells (SECs) serving as a reference. Based on the inferred CNV expression matrix, k-means clustering was applied to identify subpopulations with distinct CNV profiles, and a CNV scoring system was established to quantify genomic variation levels. Tumor cell populations identified by CNV analysis were subsequently extracted for further dimensionality reduction, clustering, and cell type annotation. To assess proliferative activity, cell cycle scores of each subpopulation were calculated using the scran R package. The enrichment of Kbhb-related gene sets in individual cells was quantified with the AUCell method to identify tumor cell subtypes with high Kbhb activity. Gene set scoring was also carried out using the AddModuleScore function included in the Seurat package in order to confirm the validity of these results.


### Pseudo-timing analysis, cell communication, and transcription factor (TF) analysis
  The differentiation trajectories of tumor cell subtypes were inferred using three complementary algorithms: CytoTRACE2, Slingshot, and Monocle, thereby providing a comprehensive view of their developmental progression. Subsequently, cell-cell communication networks were analyzed and visualized with the CellChat R package to explore interactions among key cell populations. Simultaneously, Single-Cell Regulatory Network Inference and Clustering was used to infer TF regulatory activity and possible target interactions from single-cell RNA sequencing data. Finally, differences in TF activity among tumor cell subtypes were systematically evaluated, and the top 10 highly active TFs in each subtype were visualized, thereby revealing their potential roles in subtype differentiation and functional states.


### Differential gene expression and functional enrichment analysis
  The RunDEtest function from the single-cell pipeline (SCP) R package was utilized to evaluate the differences in gene expression across tumor cell subtypes. Genes with |average-log2 fold change (FC)| >0.25 and an adjusted P value <0.05 were defined as DEGs. The VolcanoPlot program was applied to illustrate the DEG distribution. The detected DEGs were then integrated with gene sets from the Gene Ontology (GO) database to conduct functional enrichment analysis.


### Identification of Kbhb-related prognostic genes
  DEGs between the key tumor cell subtype and other subtypes were intersected with Kbhb-related genes to obtain tumor-associated Kbhb genes. Through univariate Cox regression analysis, prognostic genes that were substantially linked to the survival of ESCC patients were initially identified. Following that, prognostic genes with high predictive relevance were chosen using two machine learning algorithms: extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost) and least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression.


### Construction and evaluation of prognostic models
  Based on the chosen prognostic genes, a random survival forest (RSF) model for ESCC patients was built using the randomForestSRC R package’s rfsrc function. The model’s performance was initially evaluated using time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and the area under the curve (AUC), computed with the timeROC R package. The differences in survival between high- and low-risk groups were then assessed using Kaplan-Meier (KM) survival analysis, and the log-rank test was used to ascertain statistical significance. The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used to further investigate the relationships between the predictive model and clinical characteristics. Calibration curves were generated to assess the consistency between predicted probabilities and observed outcomes, ensuring model reliability. Finally, model interpretability was investigated using the explain function from the DALEX R package, with SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) values calculated via predict-parts for each patient. The global importance of prognostic genes was ranked based on the absolute mean SHAP values, and multidimensional visualizations, including bar plots for global importance and beeswarm plots for expression-SHAP distributions, were generated to illustrate their contribution to model predictions. Spearman correlation analysis was performed to evaluate the expression relationships between the seven prognostic genes and 11 core Kbhb regulatory factors (writers, erasers, a reader, and metabolic enzymes) in ESCC tumor samples. Statistical significance was adjusted using the false discovery rate (FDR) method, and results were visualized via a heatmap.


### Immune infiltration, anti-cancer immune circulation, and immune checkpoint analysis
  The immune infiltration of ESCC patients with different risk levels was investigated using three complementary algorithms: Microenvironment Cell Populations (MCP)-counter, single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA), and ESTIMATE. In addition, the activity scores of the seven steps of the tumor-immune cycle for each patient were determined using the tumor immunophenotype (TIP) platform, and differences between high- and low-risk groups were assessed with the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was applied to evaluate the expression levels of immune checkpoint molecules in high- and low-risk patients in order to better investigate the connection between the risk score model and the immunological landscape. Furthermore, relationships between immune checkpoint genes and prognostic genes were assessed with Spearman correlation analysis.


### Somatic mutation and drug sensitivity analysis
  Somatic mutation data for each ESCC sample were obtained from TCGA and visualized using waterfall plots generated with the oncoplot function in the maftools R package. Through the pRRophetic R software to estimate half-maximal inhibitory concentrations (IC50) for certain medications, drug sensitivity for ESCC patients was predicted based on the Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer (GDSC) database. Differences in predicted IC50 values between high- and low-risk groups were further evaluated using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, thereby providing insights into potential variations in drug response among patients with different risk levels.


### Expression validation and pan-cancer analysis of prognostic genes
  The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was adopted to examine the expression levels of the chosen prognostic genes, and their relationships with patient survival were also assessed. Based on a pan-cancer study, the expression patterns of these prognostic genes were examined across various cancer types. Specifically, the Gene Set Cancer Analysis (GSCA) database (http://bioinfo.life.hust.edu.cn/GSCA) was utilized to explore gene expression profiles and potential shared mechanisms of the prognostic genes across different malignancies.


### Quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) analysis
  Total RNA was extracted from tissue samples using TRIzol reagent (Tiangen, China), followed by phase separation with chloroform and RNA precipitation with isopropanol. The concentration and purity of RNA were determined using a SYNERGY/HTX microplate reader (BioTek, USA). Genomic DNA contamination was eliminated using the gDNA Eraser Mix (Vazyme, China), and first-strand cDNA was synthesized with the HiScript III RT SuperMix for qPCR (Vazyme, China). PCR was performed using SYBR Green Master Mix (Vazyme, China) on a Real-Time PCR Detection System (Bio-Rad, USA) with gene-specific primers. The primer sequences are shown in Table 2.


### Statistical analysis
  R software (v 4.2.2) served for statistical analysis, while Cytoscape (v 3.9.1) was applied to create network visualizations. To find differences between two groups, the Wilcoxon test was employed, and unless otherwise noted, a P<0.05 was deemed substantially different. For comparing continuous variables between groups, we used the Student’s t-test for normally distributed data and the Mann-Whitney U-test (also known as the Wilcoxon test) for non-normally distributed data. A two-tailed P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant unless otherwise stated.


## Results

### Identification of malignant SECs by single-cell sequencing data
  Following quality control of the GSE188900 dataset, a total of 51,111 high-quality cells were retained (Figure 2A). After data normalization and PCA, the first 32 principal components (PCs) were selected for further dimensionality reduction (Figure S1A,S1B). After batch effect correction, cells from different samples demonstrated a homogeneous distribution (Figure 2B). At a resolution of 0.3, 19 clusters were identified and subsequently annotated into 11 major cell types using established marker genes, namely macrophages, SECs, fibroblast, T cells, B cells, endothelial, smooth muscle cells (SMCs), SECs, plasma, mast cells, plasmacytoid dendritic cell (pDC) (Figure 2C, Figure S1C,S1D). Notably, tumor samples exhibited a significantly higher cell density compared to normal controls, with SECs being the predominant population (Figure 2D). To further characterize malignant cells, SECs from tumor samples were compared with those from normal controls, and CNVs were inferred using inferCNV (Figure 2E). CNV scores were then calculated from the inferCNV-based expression matrix. SECs were subdivided into six clusters, and CNV scores were visualized across subpopulations (Figure 2F). Clusters 1, 2, 4, and 6 exhibited elevated CNV scores and were exclusively distributed in tumor samples, thereby being defined as malignant epithelial cells, comprising a total of 5,047 cells (Figure 2G).


### Identification and functional characterization of malignant SEC subtypes in ESCC
  To resolve the intra-tumoral heterogeneity of the malignant compartment, malignant epithelial cells were re-normalized, followed by the identification of the top 2,000 HVGs. Initially, four clusters were identified (Figure 3A). Cluster 3, characterized by abnormally high unique molecular identifier (UMI) counts and multi-lineage markers (suggestive of doublets), was excluded from subsequent analysis. The remaining cells were re-clustered into three distinct malignant subtypes: Malignant1, Malignant2, and Malignant3 (Figure 3B,3C). The top marker genes visualized in a heatmap (Figure 3D). Further quantitative analysis confirmed that Malignant1 and Malignant3 possessed higher transcriptional activity (higher nCount-RNA and nFeature-RNA) (Figure 3E,3F). Besides, cell cycle profiling revealed distinct proliferative states among the subtypes: Malignant1 and Malignant3 was characterized by a high proportion of cells in the S and G1 phase, indicating active DNA replication, while Malignant2 showed an enrichment of cells in the G2/M phase, suggesting active mitotic progression (Figure 3G-3K). Notably, Malignant2 exhibited significantly elevated CNV levels, suggesting a state of higher genomic instability and rapid cell division (Figure 3K).


### Intercellular crosstalk and regulon analysis identify SOX11 as a potential regulator of proliferative malignant cells
  To explore the microenvironmental interactions of the identified subtypes, we performed cell-cell communication analysis. Robust signaling crosstalk was observed between fibroblasts, SECs, and three malignant subtypes, where Malignant2 exhibited intensive interactions with Malignant1, Malignant3, and fibroblasts, acting as sender of signaling molecules (Figure 4A,4B). Besides, differential expression analysis (DEA) was then conducted to delineate the differential expressed genes of each subtype compared to other malignant subtypes (Figure 4C). Functional enrichment analysis revealed distinct biological profiles. Malignant1 was primarily associated with immune response and signaling regulation. Malignant2 was significantly enriched in cell cycle and mitotic pathways, confirming its status as a highly proliferative subpopulation. Malignant3 focused on mitochondrial and metabolic functions (Figure 4D). These findings were further corroborated by GSEA, which highlighted T cell-mediated immunity in Malignant1, chromosome segregation in Malignant2, and collagen metabolic processes in Malignant3 (Figure 4E).

  To uncover the underlying regulatory framework, we performed TF analysis (SCENIC). We identified the top 15 differentially active regulons across the tumor subtypes (Figure 4F,4G). Notably, the SOX11 regulons exhibited markedly higher activity in Malignant2. Consistent with the regulon activity, SOX11 mRNA expression was specifically upregulated in the Malignant2 subpopulation compared to Malignant1 and Malignant3 (Figure 4H). Collectively, these results suggest that SOX11 may function as a key master regulator driving the proliferative phenotype of malignant cells in ESCC.


### Malignant2 acts as a progenitor-like hub with enhanced stemness and differentiation potential
  To determine the hierarchical relationship among the malignant subtypes, we first assessed their developmental potential using CytoTRACE. Malignant2 exhibited the highest CytoTRACE score, suggesting a less differentiated, progenitor-like state compared to Malignant1 and Malignant3 (Figure 5A,5B). Pseudotime trajectory analysis via Monocle2 further elucidated the evolutionary dynamics. The results revealed a continuous developmental path originating from the Malignant2 subpopulation, transitioning through Malignant1 and Malignant3 states (Figure 5C,5D). This evolutionary progression was further supported by hierarchical clustering and cell density distributions along the pseudotime axis (Figure 5E).

  To validate this differentiation axis, we independently reconstructed the trajectory using the Slingshot algorithm. Consistently, Slingshot result confirmed that differentiation initiated from Malignant2 and progressed toward Malignant3 (Figure 5F). Module clustering along this trajectory identified four distinct gene expression waves (C1-C4). Functional enrichment analysis indicated that these modules were sequentially activated, with early-stage genes (C1-C2) enriched in vesicle transport and purine metabolism, while late-stage genes (C3-C4) were associated with cytoskeletal remodeling and developmental programs (Figure 5G). Furthermore, AUCell analysis with Kbhb-related genes revealed that Malignant2 was enriched in Kbhb-related gene set, suggesting that specific epigenetic or metabolic modifications may underpin its stemness advantage (Figure 5G-5I).


### Construction and validation of a Kbhb-related prognostic signature
  To translate the molecular characteristics of the Malignant2 subpopulation into clinical utility, we intersected the DEGs of Malignant2 with Kbhb-related genes, leading to 659 tumor-associated Kbhb genes (Figure 6A). This gene intersection directly bridges Malignant2 stemness with Kbhb metabolism. This molecular overlap demonstrates that Kbhb-related metabolic and epigenetic machinery is intrinsically active within this stem-like subpopulation. To narrow down the prognostic genes, we employed an integrative machine learning approach. First, univariate Cox regression identified 15 candidates significantly associated with OS (Figure 6B). Subsequently, Lasso-Cox regression was applied to mitigate over-fitting, retaining 11 key genes (Figure 6C). Simultaneously, an XGBoost classifier was utilized to rank these 15 candidates by feature importance (Figure 6D). The intersection of the Lasso-selected genes and the top 10 XGBoost-ranked features resulted in a core 7-gene prognostic signature, comprising HMGB1, CTTN, PSIP1, CSRP1, MAPRE3, SNRNP27, and NDUFAF4 (Figure 6E). Expression correlation analysis revealed widespread and significant associations between the seven prognostic genes and 11 core Kbhb regulatory factors in ESCC (Figure S2).

  Based on this signature, a RSF model was constructed to calculate risk scores for ESCC patients. The model demonstrated exceptional predictive performance. In the training cohort (GSE53625), time-dependent ROC analysis yielded AUC values of 0.882, 0.853, and 0. 859 for 2-, 3-, and 4-year OS, respectively (Figure 6F). KM curves revealed that patients in the high-risk group had significantly poorer outcomes than those in the low-risk group (Figure 6G). The risk distribution plot confirmed a higher mortality rate among high-risk patients (Figure 6H, Figure S2E). The robustness of the model was further validated in an independent cohort GSE53624 and TCGA-ESCC (Figure 6I-6K, Figure S3A-S3F).


### Evaluation and comparison of the prognostic signature with traditional clinical staging
  To rigorously evaluate the clinical application value and predictive accuracy of our established risk model, we performed time-dependent ROC analysis at 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival horizons across three independent cohorts, comparing the performance of the risk score, TN staging, and a combined model. In the GSE53625 cohort (Figure 7A-7C) and the GSE53624 cohort (Figure 7D-7F), the combined model integrated with both the risk score and TN staging exhibited the highest predictive efficacy, yielding AUC values that consistently outperformed traditional TN staging and the risk model alone. Notably, the risk model alone also demonstrated superior or comparable predictive capacity compared to conventional TN staging across these time points. Intriguingly, in the external TCGA validation cohort (Figure 7G-7I), the risk model alone demonstrated the most robust generalization performance, achieving higher AUC values at 1-, 3-, and 5-year intervals than both the combined model and the traditional TN staging system. Taken together, these results indicate that our Kbhb-related gene signature possesses stable, superior, and reliable predictive capability across different datasets, offering substantial complementary value to traditional clinical staging systems.


### Prognostic genes are associated with age, stage, and survival status
  To enhance the interpretability of the prognostic model, we employed SHAP to quantify the contribution of each gene. The mean absolute SHAP values identified CTTN, CSRP1, and MAPRE3 as the top three predictors driving the model’s performance (Figure 8A). SHAP summary plots further revealed the direction of these effects: high expression of PSIP1, CSRP1, CTTN, NDUFAF4, HMGB1, and SNRNP27 was positively correlated with higher SHAP values, whereas elevated MAPRE3 expression was associated with negative SHAP values (Figure 8B). We further investigated the distribution of risk scores across various clinical categories. Patients aged >60 years exhibited significantly higher risk scores compared to younger patients (Figure 8C). In terms of disease progression, risk scores significantly increased with advancing clinical stage (Figure 8D), T stage (Figure 8E), and N stage (Figure 8F), suggesting that the Kbhb-related signature reflects tumor burden and metastatic potential. While no significant difference was observed between genders (Figure 8G), patients who deceased during follow-up displayed markedly higher risk scores than those who remained alive (Figure 8H). To further validate the reliability of the prognostic signature, we performed KM survival analysis. In TCGA-ESCC dataset, high expression of PSIP1, CSRP1, CTTN, NDUFAF4, SNRNP27, and HMGB1 was significantly associated with poor OS, whereas high MAPRE3 expression is associated with prolonged survival (Figure 8I).


### Expression validation and prognostic significance of seven ESCC-related genes
  Expression analysis of the seven prognostic genes revealed consistent patterns across the training cohort GSE53625 and external validation cohorts GSE53624, with HMGB1, CTTN, and SNRNP27 significantly upregulated in tumor samples (Figure 9A-9C). To further verify these findings, qPCR analysis was performed to compare gene expression between normal and ESCA tissues. The results showed a highly consistent trend, in which HMGB1, CTTN, SNRNP27, and MAPRE3 were markedly upregulated in ESCA tissues, while CSRP1 was significantly downregulated. In contrast, PSIP1 and NDUFAF4 displayed no significant expression changes (Figure 9C).


## Discussion
  With a significant worldwide incidence and death rate, ESCC is a very aggressive cancer whose genesis and spread are fueled by intricate molecular processes (26). Kbhb modification is a newly identified PTM, plays a crucial role in epigenetic regulation and tumor biology (27). However, its specific function in ESCC remains largely unexplored (28). In this study, by integrating single-cell transcriptomic and bulk RNA sequencing data, we identified a Kbhb-related prognostic signature and constructed a RSF prognostic model validated across multiple independent cohorts. Further analyses indicated that these prognostic genes were closely associated with patient age, clinical stage, survival status. Collectively, these findings highlight the biological significance of Kbhb-related genes in ESCC and underscore their potential as biomarkers and targets for precision oncology.

  In this study, malignant SECs characterized by significant CNVs were identified through single-cell sequencing and further classified into three distinct subtypes. Notably, close intercellular interactions were observed between malignant epithelial subpopulations, fibroblasts, and SMCs, suggesting intricate communication within the tumor microenvironment. Previous studies have demonstrated that vascular SMCs can be activated and migrate following the injury process, potentially mediated by FAT1, which may play a role in the initiation and progression of ESCC (29). Furthermore, cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) interact closely with tumor cells in ESCC, contributing to chemoradiotherapy resistance, enhancing tumor cell migration, invasion, and immune evasion, and promoting collective epithelial cancer cell invasion through extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling (30). Another study reported that the progressive loss of ANXA1 expression in SECs during ESCC development can induce the conversion of fibroblasts into CAFs, thereby facilitating tumor progression (31). Consistently, single-cell transcriptomic analyses have revealed marked differences and pronounced heterogeneity between SECs in ESCC and normal tissues. Among fibroblast populations, the myofibroblast subcluster was found to be significantly enriched, suggesting its potential involvement in tumor progression. Moreover, differential genes and marker profiles identified in certain epithelial and fibroblast subpopulations may possess prognostic relevance (25). Taken together, these findings indicate that the complex crosstalk among malignant SECs, fibroblasts, and SMCs may collectively drive the initiation and progression of ESCC.

  Our study revealed marked functional and differentiation heterogeneity among the three malignant SEC subtypes. Among them, Malignant2 was positioned at the origin of the differentiation trajectory, exhibiting high proliferative potential and strong stemness features, with SOX11 identified as a key regulatory factor. Trajectory-based gene module analysis further indicated enrichment in pathways related to vesicle transport, purine metabolism, and cardiac muscle development, with Malignant2 showing the most pronounced activity, suggesting its pivotal role in tumor progression. Crucially, the significant overlap between Malignant2 marker genes and the Kbhb gene set directly links tumor stemness to metabolic reprogramming. This molecular coupling suggests that Kbhb modification acts as a key epigenetic driver sustaining the stem-like state and metabolic plasticity of ESCC cells. Previous studies have demonstrated that inhibition of purine metabolism, such as through serine deprivation, can markedly suppress ESCC cell proliferation by disrupting nucleotide synthesis and NADPH production, thereby activating AMPK, inhibiting mTORC1, and inducing cell-cycle arrest (32). Moreover, SOX11 and SOX12 are frequently overexpressed in various cancers and are associated with cell-cycle regulation, mitosis, and immune-related pathways (33). Aberrant SOX11 expression has also been linked to poor survival, advanced tumor stage, recurrence, and stemness characteristics, while its genetic alterations correlate with immune infiltration and cancer progression (34). Taken together, these findings suggest that SOX11 may serve as a central regulator of malignant cell proliferation and differentiation in ESCC, potentially bridging metabolic reprogramming, stemness maintenance, and tumor progression. Nevertheless, its precise role and regulatory mechanisms require further experimental validation.

  Based on the DEGs between Malignant2 and the other subtypes, a seven-gene RSF prognostic model was established, comprising HMGB1, CTTN, PSIP1, CSRP1, MAPRE3, SNRNP27, and NDUFAF4. The model exhibited robust performance in both the training and validation cohorts and effectively discriminated between patients with high and low survival risks. Further analyses revealed that the expression levels of several prognostic genes were significantly correlated with age, clinical stage, and survival outcomes, with a general upregulation of these genes in the high-risk group, accompanied by markedly reduced survival rates. Among these genes, HMGB1 has been extensively investigated in ESCC and appears to play a pivotal role in therapeutic resistance and tumor progression. Elevated HMGB1 expression enhances radioresistance by activating the PI3K/AKT/ATM signaling pathway, whereas inhibition of HMGB1 or blockade of this pathway induces apoptosis, arrests cells at the G0/G1 phase, and suppresses proliferation and invasion (35). Consistently, high HMGB1 expression also promotes cell proliferation and migration while attenuating radiotherapy-induced G0/G1 arrest, resulting in poorer patient prognosis (36). Moreover, PTMA, another upregulated gene, has been shown to interact with HMGB1 to modulate mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation, thereby affecting tumor-cell proliferation and apoptosis and promoting ROS accumulation, which collectively drive ESCC malignancy (37). These findings highlight HMGB1 as a central determinant of ESCC prognosis and progression, likely mediating metabolic reprogramming and treatment resistance. Nevertheless, the biological functions and regulatory mechanisms of the other prognostic genes remain largely unexplored and warrant further investigation.

  However, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the single-cell transcriptomic data were obtained from public databases with a relatively small sample size, and incomplete clinical information for certain patients may have affected the generalizability of the results. Second, while the mRNA expression trends of key genes were preliminarily validated by qPCR, this study lacks in-depth cell functional experiments and clinical tissue verification. Consequently, the causal roles of these candidate genes in regulating ESCC malignant proliferation, stemness, and metabolic phenotypes, as well as the direct clinical association between their expression and actual protein Kbhb modification levels, remain to be fully elucidated. Finally, the predictive findings regarding drug sensitivity should be confirmed by additional preclinical and clinical studies to ensure the reliability and translational potential of the proposed model. This study provides novel insights into the cellular heterogeneity and molecular regulation of malignant epithelial cells in ESCC and offers a theoretical basis for personalized prognosis assessment and therapeutic optimization.


## Conclusions
  This study systematically characterized the heterogeneity of malignant SECs in ESCC using single-cell sequencing, identifying three distinct malignant subtypes with divergent proliferative and metabolic states. Malignant2 emerged as a highly proliferative population displaying stemness features positioned at the origin of differentiation trajectories. Integrating these insights, we developed a correlative seven-gene RSF prognostic model based on Kbhb-associated genes, which demonstrated robust predictive accuracy across multiple cohorts. Although this signature reflects statistical and prognostic correlations rather than verified causal mechanisms of Kbhb-mediated epigenetic reprogramming, it offers a reliable framework for stratifying patient survival outcomes, immune infiltration profiles, and potential drug responses. These findings provide a valuable statistical baseline for understanding ESCC heterogeneity and highlight potential prognostic biomarkers for improving patient stratification.
