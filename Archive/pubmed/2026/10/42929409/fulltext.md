Ferroptosis is an iron-dependent lipid-peroxidation program with few mechanism-based suppression strategies. Hydrogen sulfide (H(2)S), an endogenous gasotransmitter, may restrain ferroptosis, but the structural route linking H(2)S-responsive Keap1 chemistry to Nrf2 stabilization is unresolved. We combined network pharmacology, Keap1-Cul3 structural modeling, three independent 100-ns molecular dynamics (MD) simulations per state, exploratory molecular mechanics generalized Born surface area (MMGBSA) energy estimation, biochemical analysis of purified wild-type and Cys151-->Ser (C151S) Keap1, and cell-based western blotting and quantitative PCR. Trajectories were assessed by root-mean-square deviation (RMSD), radius of gyration (Rg), and root-mean-square fluctuation (RMSF). Network analysis prioritized NFE2L2, HMOX1, AKT1 and SIRT1. Across replicates, the persulfidated state showed higher Cys151, Cul3-binding-region, and Cul3 RMSD with unchanged Rg, while Bric-a-brac/Tramtrack/Broad-complex (BTB) self-RMSD was lower than wild type, indicating altered BTB-Cul3 docking, not BTB destabilization. Interface contacts expanded from 20 to 45 residues, and the Glu192-Lys68 salt bridge was lost. In purified protein, NaHS generated a Cys151-dependent persulfide without altering secondary structure, thermal stability, or BTB dimerization, reduced Cul3 affinity (dissociation constant: 0.9 +/- 0.2-2.4 +/- 0.5 muM) and lowered reconstituted Nrf2 ubiquitination to 40% of control; both effects were largely lost in C151S. In HepG2 cells, NaHS increased Nrf2, HMOX1, and phospho-Akt/Akt and restored GPX4 under erastin-induced stress. Persulfidation of Keap1 Cys151 weakens productive Cul3 engagement and reduces Nrf2 ubiquitination in vitro, with simulations indicating associated remodeling of the BTB-Cul3 interface, supporting H(2)S-responsive Keap1 modification as a contributor to Nrf2 stabilization and Akt/Nrf2/HMOX1-GPX4 anti-ferroptotic defense.
