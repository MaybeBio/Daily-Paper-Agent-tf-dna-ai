BACKGROUND & AIMS: The liver is a vital metabolic organ with an extraordinary capacity for regeneration, making it a key focus for understanding tissue repair and developing therapeutic strategies for liver diseases. However, ethical limitations restrict liver regeneration research largely to rodents, leaving critical gaps in our understanding of primate-specific mechanisms. METHODS: We performed partial hepatectomy (PHx) using a novel laparoscopically assisted hepatic wedge resection (LAHWR) approach in two cynomolgus monkeys (Macaca fascicularis), one for mechanistic exploration and one for validation. Afterwards, we performed sequential sampling and applied integrative multi-omics approaches, including snRNA-Seq, snATAC-Seq, and spatial transcriptomics (Stereo-Seq), to systematically map cellular and molecular dynamics of primate liver regeneration. RESULTS: We successfully established two non-human primate models to study the spatiotemporal dynamics of liver regeneration following PHx. We generate a high-resolution map of cellular and molecular dynamics of primate liver regeneration. Our analysis identified a distinct hepatocyte subcluster with fetal liver-like transcriptional features and high regenerative activity emerging in the early stages post-PHx (18.83% in Day 3 vs. 0.48% in Day 0), which predominantly localizes in midlobular zone 2 (P < 0.0001). This subcluster was characterized by elevated expression of proliferation-associated genes and was regulated by key transcription factor POU2F1. Cross-species comparisons revealed that POU2F1 is a primate-specific regulator of liver regeneration, showing significant upregulation in primates (P < 0.01), but not in rodents. Importantly, POU2F1 expression was markedly upregulated in cirrhotic patients (2 for liver samples). CONCLUSION: Proliferative hepatocytes localize primarily to midlobular zone 2 during primate hepatectomy regeneration process. POU2F1 is a primate-specific regulator of liver regeneration, implying novel regulatory mechanisms and emphasizing species-specific features. IMPACT AND IMPLICATIONS: The specific mechanisms of liver regeneration in primates remain poorly understood. The hepatocyte population expressing POU2F1 is a hepatocyte cluster with high proliferative capacity, induced after PHx, mainly located in zone 2 of liver lobule. Comparative studies of liver regeneration across species indicate that POU2F1 is a specific regulator in the liver regeneration process of primates. The therapeutic targeting of POU2F1+ hepatocytes is of potential interest in human liver regeneration.
