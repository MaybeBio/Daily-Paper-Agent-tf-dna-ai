### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim a spontaneous process supported mainly by hydrogen bonding and van der Waals interactions.  
- **Evidence pointer** Thermodynamic analysis section (location not provided)  
- **Concern** The abstract states that thermodynamic characteristics revealed a spontaneous process, but no numerical values for enthalpy change, entropy change, or Gibbs free energy are provided. The claim that hydrogen bonding and van der Waals interactions dominate is a mechanistic inference that requires quantitative thermodynamic data (e.g., negative enthalpy and entropy values) to be credible.  
- **Why it matters** Without quantitative thermodynamic parameters, the reader cannot assess the magnitude or reliability of the interaction forces. The distinction between hydrogen bonding/van der Waals interactions and hydrophobic or electrostatic contributions is central to understanding the binding mechanism.  
- **Resolution test** Provide the values of enthalpy change, entropy change, and Gibbs free energy, along with the temperature range studied and the method of calculation. Show that the signs and magnitudes are consistent with the claimed dominant forces.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that molecular docking results and MEP analysis support groove binding and that the nitro group creates electrophilic and nucleophilic zones that facilitate groove interaction while reducing electron density for pi-pi stacking.  
- **Evidence pointer** Molecular docking and MEP mapping sections (location not provided)  
- **Concern** No docking scores, binding poses, or interaction residues are reported. The MEP analysis is described only qualitatively, with no quantitative electrostatic potential values or maps shown. The claim that the nitro group "really changes" electron distribution is informal and unsupported by numerical data.  
- **Why it matters** The central mechanistic conclusion rests on these computational results. Without quantitative docking data and MEP values, the reader cannot evaluate whether the proposed groove binding mode and the electronic effect of the nitro group are convincingly demonstrated.  
- **Resolution test** Report docking scores (e.g., binding free energy estimates), the specific DNA groove targeted, and key interacting atoms. Provide MEP maps with quantitative electrostatic potential ranges and show how the nitro substituent alters the electron density distribution relative to a non-substituted analogue.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim a binding constant of approximately 1.7 x 10^5 M^-1 and approximately 1:1 binding stoichiometry.  
- **Evidence pointer** UV-Vis absorption and fluorescence quenching sections (location not provided)  
- **Concern** The binding constant is reported without context regarding the method of determination, the concentration range, or the fitting model. The stoichiometry claim is stated without supporting data, such as a Job plot or a Benesi-Hildebrand analysis.

- **Resolution test** Describe the method used to calculate the binding constant, including the equation, concentration ranges, and any fitting parameters. Provide a Job plot or equivalent evidence for the 1:1 stoichiometry.

- **Concern ID** R1-M4  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** No  
- **Axis** Internal consistency  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that the absence of significant viscosity change and negligible DNA-cleavage activity suggest groove binding rather than intercalation.  
- **Evidence pointer** Viscosity measurements and agarose gel electrophoresis sections (location not provided)  
- **Concern** The abstract does not report the actual viscosity data or the extent of cleavage activity. - **Resolution Test** Provide relative viscosity values as a function of complex concentration, and show gel images with appropriate controls to demonstrate negligible cleavage.
