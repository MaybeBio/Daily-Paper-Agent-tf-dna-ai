## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text including abstract, main text, methods, and supplementary figure/table references
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, experimental design, data interpretation, computational methodology, and claims as presented in the supplied material
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report that introducing a third cysteine at different positions in an engineered BetI L79C/Q81C scaffold produces position-dependent changes in whole-cell sfGFP reporter output upon As(III) exposure, including opposite response directions. MD simulations using predefined dithiol- and trithiol-coordination models yield distinct structural dynamics that provide testable hypotheses for the observed phenotypes.
- **Visible evidence base** Whole-cell reporter assays (Figure c, Figure a-c), MD simulation analyses (Figure a,b, Figure a,b, Supplementary Figures S1-S3, Table S3), statistical analyses as described, and control experiments (pAC void, BetII22T reference)
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Figures and tables are referenced but not provided in the supplied text; Supplementary Tables S1, S2, S4 and Supplementary Figures S1-S3 are referenced but not available; raw numerical data for reporter assays and MD simulations are not provided; experimental replicates and error bars are described but not shown

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript presents a systematic experimental survey of cysteine position effects on As(III)-dependent reporter output in an engineered BetI scaffold, complemented by MD simulations that offer plausible structural rationales. The work is clearly presented and the authors are appropriately cautious in limiting their claims to the reporter phenotype level. However, the absence of direct biochemical validation of As(III) binding or BetI-DNA interactions, combined with the lack of visible figures and raw data, limits the depth of mechanistic insight. The study is a useful contribution to the synthetic biology design space but does not yet establish a general principle or a validated mechanism.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in synthetic biology and whole-cell biosensor engineering, particularly those working on metalloregulatory transcription factors and multi-input logic gates. The demonstration of position-dependent response inversion could inform future design strategies for tunable transcriptional regulators. The MD simulation approach may also interest computational biologists studying allosteric regulation in TetR-family repressors.
- **Major strengths** 1) Systematic positional scanning of cysteine insertion sites provides a clear empirical map of how mutation position affects reporter output direction and magnitude. 2) The authors explicitly acknowledge the limitations of their study and do not overclaim mechanistic insight. 3) The inclusion of control experiments (pAC void, BetII22T reference, OD600 normalization) strengthens the interpretation that observed effects are BetI-dependent. 4) The MD simulation framework, while based on predefined coordination models, offers a transparent and testable structural hypothesis.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency for mechanistic claims
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript states that MD simulations "provide an explanation for the response inversion to As(III) observed in BetIcys79,81,89 and BetIcys79,81,86" and that the simulations "yielded distinct fluctuation patterns in the DNA-binding domain, providing testable structural hypotheses for the observed reporter phenotypes."
  - **Evidence pointer** Results and Discussion, "Structural Dynamics of Predefined As(III) Coordination Models" section; Figure a,b; Figure a,b; Supplementary Figures S2, S3
  - **Concern** The MD simulations are based on predefined coordination models (dithiol for BetIcys79,81,89 and trithiol for BetIcys79,81,86) that were assumed rather than experimentally validated. The authors acknowledge this limitation, but the interpretive weight placed on the simulation results in the Discussion is substantial. The simulations show differences in fluctuation patterns, but there is no direct link established between these fluctuations and actual DNA-binding affinity changes. The claim that the simulations "provide an explanation" for the observed reporter phenotypes is therefore overstated; at best, they offer a plausible but unvalidated hypothesis.
  - **Why it matters** The central mechanistic narrative of the manuscript rests on the MD simulation results. Without experimental validation of As(III) binding stoichiometry, coordination geometry, or BetI-DNA binding changes, the simulations cannot distinguish between competing explanations for the reporter phenotypes (e.g., altered protein stability, changes in dimerization, or indirect cellular effects). The reader cannot assess whether the simulation results are robust to alternative coordination models or force field choices.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should either (a) provide direct experimental evidence of As(III) binding to the engineered cysteine sites (e.g., via mass spectrometry, X-ray crystallography, or a suitable in-cell assay), (b) demonstrate As(III)-dependent changes in BetI-DNA binding (e.g., via EMSA or a reporter assay with a controlled DNA-binding domain), or (c) substantially temper the interpretive language so that the simulations are presented purely as speculative hypotheses without claiming to "explain" the experimental observations.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Reproducibility and data availability
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript reports quantitative reporter assay results (e.g., "8.3-fold increase" for BetIcys79,81,89) and MD simulation analyses (RMSF, PCA, standard deviations of 2.5 and 1.9 nm) but the actual figures and raw data are not provided in the supplied material.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results and Discussion sections; Figure c; Figure a-c; Figure a,b; Figure a,b; Supplementary Figures S1-S3; Supplementary Tables S1, S2
  - **Concern** The manuscript text references multiple figures and supplementary items that are essential for evaluating the claims, but none of these are visible in the supplied material. The reader cannot verify the magnitude of reported effects, assess the quality of the data (e.g., error bars, replicate numbers, statistical test appropriateness), or evaluate the MD simulation convergence and sampling quality. The statistical analysis section describes Welch's t tests but does not specify the number of biological or technical replicates, nor whether multiple testing corrections were applied.
  - **Why it matters** Reproducibility is a core requirement for any scientific claim. Without access to the figures and raw data, the reported findings cannot be independently assessed. The lack of detail on replicate numbers and statistical power is particularly concerning for the claims of "significant" differences between variants.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should provide the full figures and supplementary materials, including raw data tables, replicate numbers, and statistical details. If the manuscript is under review, the reviewers should have access to all figures and supplementary items. The statistical methods should be described with sufficient detail to allow replication.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Generalizability of the design principle
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript concludes that "cysteine placement can tune the direction of As(III)-dependent whole-cell sfGFP output in an engineered BetI regulatory system and thereby expand the design space for multi-input transcriptional devices."
  - **Evidence pointer** Results and Discussion, "Position-Dependent As(III) Responsiveness Induced by Cysteine Substitution in BetI" and "Position-Dependent Logic-Like Responses to Choline and As(III) in BetI Mutants"; Figure c; Figure a-c
  - **Concern** The study examines a limited set of cysteine substitution positions (16 positions on α4, the turn region, α5, and one on α7) in a single scaffold (BetI L79C/Q81C). While the observed position-dependent effects are interesting, the manuscript does not provide evidence that this design principle generalizes to other TetR-family repressors or to other metal ions. The claim that this "expands the design space" is supported, but the broader implication that this constitutes a general design strategy is not established.
  - **Why it matters** The stated motivation includes "general design principles for such systems have not yet been fully established." If the authors intend to contribute to such principles, a single scaffold with a limited set of mutations is insufficient. The field would benefit from a clearer statement of what is scaffold-specific versus what might be transferable.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should either (a) test the same cysteine-scanning approach in at least one additional TetR-family repressor or a related scaffold, (b) provide a computational or structural rationale for why the observed position-dependence might be general, or (c) explicitly limit their claims to the BetI scaffold and avoid implying generalizability.
- **Minor Comments**
  - **Concern ID** R1-m1
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Clarity of terminology
  - **Affected element** Use of "logic-like" and "operational" descriptors
  - **Evidence pointer** Results and Discussion, "Position-Dependent Logic-Like Responses to Choline and As(III) in BetI Mutants"; Supplementary Tables S1, S2
  - **Issue** The terms "logic-like," "operational truth-table-style," and "operational classifications" are used to describe the reporter output patterns. While the authors are appropriately cautious, the terminology is somewhat awkward and could be simplified. The distinction between "logic" at the biochemical level versus the reporter level is important but could be stated more directly.
  - **Required correction** Consider using clearer phrasing such as "reporter-level logic" or "apparent logic" throughout, and define these terms explicitly at first use.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m2
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Completeness of methods
  - **Affected element** MD simulation details
  - **Evidence pointer** Materials and Methods, "MD Simulations"
  - **Issue** The methods describe the simulation protocol but do not specify the number of independent simulations performed, the criteria for convergence, or how the "representative structures" for As(III) coordination were selected beyond "low RMSD values." The statement that "frames exhibiting low RMSD values were identified" is vague.
  - **Required correction** Provide quantitative criteria for RMSD thresholds, the number of frames or clusters considered, and the number of independent simulations. State how many replicate simulations were run and how statistical uncertainties in the RMSF and PCA results were estimated.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m3
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Presentation of statistical results
  - **Affected element** Statistical analysis section
  - **Evidence pointer** Materials and Methods, "Statistical Analysis"
  - **Issue** The methods state that a two-tailed Welch's t test was used but do not specify the number of replicates, whether the data met the assumptions of the test, or whether any corrections for multiple comparisons were applied. The phrase "adjusted p value" is mentioned but the adjustment method is not described.
  - **Required correction** Specify the number of biological and technical replicates for each experiment, state whether data were tested for normality, and describe any multiple testing correction method used.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m4
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Discussion of alternative interpretations
  - **Affected element** Mechanistic interpretation of BetIcys79,81,86
  - **Evidence pointer** Results and Discussion, "Position-Dependent As(III) Responsiveness Induced by Cysteine Substitution in BetI"
  - **Issue** The authors note that the decreased reporter output in BetIcys79,81,86 "is consistent with increased net transcriptional repression at the reporter level" but do not discuss alternative explanations, such as reduced protein stability leading to lower effective repressor concentration, or indirect effects on cell physiology.
  - **Required correction** Add a brief discussion of alternative interpretations and why the reporter-level observation cannot distinguish between them, which the authors partially do in the "Mechanistic Limitations" section but could be integrated earlier.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** 1) The MD simulation results are presented as explanatory but are based on assumed coordination geometries without experimental validation. 2) The statistical methods are described without sufficient detail on replicates and multiple testing corrections. 3) The manuscript relies heavily on figures and supplementary materials that are not visible in the supplied text, preventing independent verification of the reported effects.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: The positional scanning approach to engineer As(III) responsiveness in a TetR-family repressor is a reasonable extension of prior work by the same group, but the concept of position-dependent allosteric modulation is not entirely new. The work is incremental rather than paradigm-shifting. Scientific importance: The demonstration of response direction inversion is potentially useful for synthetic biology applications, but the lack of mechanistic validation limits its broader impact. Interdisciplinary readership: The work bridges synthetic biology, protein engineering, and computational biophysics, but the depth in each area is limited. Technical soundness: The experimental design is generally sound, but the MD simulation framework is based on unvalidated assumptions, and the statistical reporting is incomplete. Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is clearly written and accessible, with appropriate caveats about the scope of the claims.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The core experimental observation of position-dependent response inversion is interesting and potentially useful, but the manuscript currently overinterprets the MD simulation results and lacks sufficient experimental validation of the underlying mechanism. The authors should either provide direct biochemical evidence of As(III) binding and its effects on BetI-DNA interactions, or substantially revise the interpretive language to clearly separate the experimentally supported reporter-level findings from the speculative structural hypotheses. Access to the full figures and raw data is essential for a complete evaluation.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that MD simulations "provide an explanation" for the observed reporter phenotypes is unsupported because the coordination models are assumed, not experimentally validated, and no direct link between simulated fluctuations and DNA-binding affinity is established.
- The claim that the observed position-dependent effects "expand the design space for multi-input transcriptional devices" is supported at the level of a proof-of-concept but is not validated as a general design principle.
- The operational "logic-like" descriptions (OR-like, As(III)-inhibited) are appropriately framed as reporter-level classifications, but the manuscript does not provide evidence that these patterns reflect true biochemical logic at the level of BetI-DNA binding.
- The MD simulation results (RMSF, PCA, standard deviations) are reported without visible figures or convergence metrics, making them unverifiable from the supplied material.
