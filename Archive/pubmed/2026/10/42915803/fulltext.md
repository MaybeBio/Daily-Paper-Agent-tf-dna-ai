Insect-derived peptides have attracted increasing attention due to their potential health-promoting properties. This study investigated peptides released from Galleria mellonella (W) and Alphitobius diaperinus (B) proteins following a modified static INFOGEST protocol. Digestion-derived peptides were identified by LC-MS/MS and evaluated for anti-inflammatory activity using in vitro assays, bioinformatic prediction, and molecular docking, focusing on IKK-beta as a key regulator of the NF-kappaB pathway. At 500 mug protein/mL, W-I2h and B-I2h reduced NF-kappaB reporter activity by 28.7% and 53.5%, respectively, and decreased nitric oxide production by 31%. Digestates also reduced IL-6, TNF-alpha, IFN-gamma, and IL-1beta secretion at selected concentrations and decreased IL-6 and IL-1beta mRNA expression. Conversely, 25 mug protein/mL increased IL-1beta secretion by 109.4% and 102.1%, respectively. Increased HO-1 and gammaGCS transcripts suggested modulation of Nrf2-responsive gene expression, although pathway activation was not confirmed. These findings demonstrate concentration-dependent immunomodulatory effects and identify candidate peptides for future validation.
