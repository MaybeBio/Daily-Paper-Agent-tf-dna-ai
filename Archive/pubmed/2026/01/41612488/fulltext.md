# Identification of the biomarkers associated with lipid metabolism and endoplasmic reticulum stress in pediatric sepsis through bioinformatics analysis and quantitative reverse transcriptase PCR (qRT-PCR) assay


## Background
  Sepsis constitutes a critical clinical condition characterized by organ failure and systemic inflammation, especially affecting children. Lipid metabolism and endoplasmic reticulum stress are involved in various diseases, yet their roles in pediatric sepsis remain unclear. This study sought to discover and validate biomarkers associated with lipid metabolism and endoplasmic reticulum stress in pediatric sepsis.


## Methods
  Differentially expressed genes were identified from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. Lipid metabolism-related genes (LRGs) and endoplasmic reticulum stress-related genes (ERGs) were sourced from literature. After weighted gene co-expression network analysis and intersections, candidate genes were screened by machine learning and validated. A nomogram was built, followed by various bioinformatics analyses and quantitative reverse transcriptase PCR (qRT-PCR) confirmation.


## Results
  There were 12 candidate genes, and 4 of them (ACER3, DGAT2, GBA, and TSPO) were screened as biomarkers. A nomogram integrating these four biomarkers showed excellent predictive ability. Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA) and Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) revealed their involvement in pediatric sepsis-related pathways. They correlated positively with stimulated dendritic cells and negatively with stimulated B cells. The molecular regulatory network included multiple RNAs and transcription factors. 2,3,7,8-Tetrachlorodibenzo-p-dioxin (TCDD) bound strongly to the biomarkers. qRT-PCR confirmed their high expression in pediatric sepsis patients’ blood (all P < 0.05).


## Conclusions
  A total of 4 biomarkers (ACER3, DGAT2, GBA, and TSPO) related to lipid metabolism and endoplasmic reticulum stress in pediatric sepsis were identified. These biomarkers offered fresh insights into the mechanisms and therapeutic approaches of pediatric sepsis.

## Introduction
  Sepsis represents a critical condition characterized by organ failure resulting from an abnormal and disordered immune reaction to infectious agents [1]. The annual incidence of pediatric sepsis is approximately 22 per 10,000 children, with severe sepsis and septic shock accounting for 8–10% of pediatric ICU admission cases [2]. The etiological factors of pediatric sepsis involve multifactorial interactions encompassing host-related determinants, pathogen-specific characteristics, environmental influences, and iatrogenic interventions [3]. Current mainstream therapeutic approaches for pediatric sepsis primarily focus on infection control, organ function support, and immunomodulation. However, due to rapid disease progression, delayed diagnosis and treatment, uneven distribution of healthcare resources, emerging resistance to broad-spectrum antibiotics, and challenges in organ function management, sepsis continues to claim over 3 million pediatric lives annually. Survivors face a substantial risk of long-term functional impairments, including diminished quality of life, dependency on prolonged respiratory or nutritional support, reliance on assistive technologies, and recurrent severe infections [3]. In-depth research into the pathogenesis, age-specific and immune characteristics, and molecular mechanisms of sepsis is critical to reducing global pediatric mortality, alleviating healthcare resource strain and family economic burdens, advancing precision medicine and translational research, and enhancing children’s health and quality of survival. Investigations into the molecular mechanisms of pediatric sepsis elucidate the intrinsic nature of disease progression and provide essential theoretical foundations for clinical diagnosis, therapeutic interventions, and prognostic evaluation.

  Lipid metabolism serves as the central hub for cellular energy and structural integrity while playing a pivotal role in signal transduction, immune regulation, and inter-organ interactions [4]. Its dysregulation is a hallmark of inflammation and metabolic disorders [5]. Research shows that the endoplasmic reticulum partially regulates lipid anabolism, catabolism, and distribution. Lipid metabolic reprogramming and endoplasmic reticulum stress coordinate with additional cellular processes to promote disease progression [6, 7]. Endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress and lipid metabolism jointly regulate inflammation, immunity, and organ function in sepsis through direct and indirect interactions [8]. In sepsis, dysregulated lipid metabolism (e.g., free fatty acids, triglycerides, and lipoproteins) exacerbates the inflammatory response and contributes to organ damage, such as liver injury and myocardial dysfunction [9]. The body enters a hypermetabolic state, reprogramming lipid metabolism to meet energy demands. However, excessive lipolysis and fatty acid accumulation can lead to lipotoxicity, damaging cells and tissues [10–12]. During sepsis, oxidative stress and excessive lipids impair ER function, activating ER stress and the unfolded protein response (UPR). Although the UPR functions to reestablish protein balance, prolonged ER stress triggers cellular death and structural deterioration [13, 14]. Moreover, ER stress fundamentally shapes lipid homeostasis by orchestrating the genetic machinery involved in fat molecule formation and catabolism. The dysregulated lipid metabolites (e.g., free fatty acids, triglycerides) and ER stress together activate inflammatory signaling pathways (e.g., NF-κB, JNK, and TLR4), intensifying the inflammatory response [15]. Simultaneously, both factors synergistically modulate immune cell function, affecting the immune response in sepsis and forming a positive feedback loop of inflammation that drives the systemic inflammatory response in sepsis [16]. Crucially, recent high-throughput metabolomics studies have provided direct evidence that lipid metabolism disorder is a core pathological feature of sepsis. For instance, Pandey et al. (2021) confirmed significant aberrations in serum lipid metabolites—specifically free fatty acids, ceramides, and triglycerides—in septic patients [17]. Further lipidomics investigations suggest that this sepsis-induced metabolic reprogramming extends beyond energy deficits; it actively exacerbates systemic inflammation by modulating key signaling pathways, such as NF-κB [18]. However, the specific molecular mechanisms underlying their interactions remain unclear. This highlights the need for further in-depth research into the relationship between lipid metabolism, ER stress, and sepsis, which holds significant scientific importance for unraveling the pathogenesis of sepsis.

  Guided by the metabolomic evidence revealing distinct lipid and ER stress signatures in sepsis, this study focuses on dissecting these pathways at the transcriptomic level. We utilize multiple bioinformatics analysis methods to detect and identify biomarkers associated with lipid metabolism and endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress in pediatric sepsis. The aim seeks to examine the potential value of these biomarkers in disease diagnosis and prognosis assessment, to deeply analyze their involvement in biological functions, immune characteristics, and potential molecular regulatory mechanisms, and to investigate their contribution to the development and advancement of sepsis. Additionally, the study seeks to identify potential drug intervention targets. Finally, the identified biomarkers will be validated through qRT-PCR experiments. This research aims to establish fresh conceptual support and potential therapeutic targets for treating pediatric sepsis, offering novel approaches and methods to improve the prognosis of pediatric sepsis patients.


## Materials and methods

### Public data download
  The GSE13904 and GSE145227 datasets were obtained through the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). The GSE13904 dataset, sequenced by the GPL570 platform, consisted of 32 peripheral blood samples from pediatric sepsis patients and 18 normal control samples derived from whole peripheral blood RNA (only day 1 samples of disease controls were included) and served as the training set. The GSE145227 dataset, sequenced by the GPL23178 platform, contained 10 peripheral blood samples from pediatric sepsis patients and 12 peripheral blood samples from normal controls derived from peripheral blood mononuclear cell (MNC) RNA and was used as the validation set. Both GSE13904 and GSE145227 were preprocessed prior to download using robust multi-array average (RMA)-based workflows, including background correction and quantile normalization. As consistent preprocessing strategies were applied, no statistically significant batch effects were observed, supporting their suitability for integrated analysis. Additionally, a total of 776 lipid metabolism-related genes (LRGs) were retrieved from the literature [19] (Table S1). A total of 1406 endoplasmic reticulum stress-related genes (ERGs) were obtained from the literature [20] (Table S2).


### Differential expression analysis
  Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were screened by the R package “limma” (v 3.62.1) [21] based on the training set. The filtering criteria were set as |log2FoldChange (FC)|> 0.5 and adjusted P < 0.05. R package “ggplot2” (v 3.5.1) [22] was utilized for a volcano plot for the results, where the top 10 highly expressed and top 10 low-expressed DEGs were labeled, sorted by the log2FC values. R package “ComplexHeatmap” (v 2.12.1) [23] was used to draw an expression heatmap according to the |log2FC| value of the top 10 high-expressed and top 10 low-expressed DEGs.


### Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA)
  WGCNA was conducted with the R package “WGCNA” (v 1.72) [24] on the training set. First, genes with median absolute deviation (MAD) values in the bottom 50% were removed. Then, the goodSamplesGenes function in the R package “WGCNA” (v 1.72) was employed to eliminate inappropriate genes and samples, ensuring the quality of the data for subsequent analysis. The hclust function in the “WGCNA” package (v 1.72) was used for sample clustering, and outlier samples were removed. The soft threshold (power) was established by ensuring that the scale-free R2 value exceeded 0.85 and the mean connectivity tended to 0. The dynamic tree-cutting algorithm was employed to identify modules, with the minimum module size set at 50 genes and the module merging threshold set at 0.2. Based on the R package “psych” (v 2.3.6) [25], Pearson correlation analysis between Module Eigengene scores and sepsis traits was performed, with |correlation coefficient (cor)|> 0.3 and P < 0.05. The module displaying the maximum absolute correlation coefficient (|cor|) was selected as the primary module. Genes within this module were regarded as key module genes and were highly likely to play significant roles in the biological processes associated with sepsis.


### Candidate gene identification
  The R package “ggvenn” (v 1.2.2) [26] was utilized to discover the intersection genes among the DEGs, genes from the key module identified in WGCNA, 776 LRGs, and 1406 ERGs. These intersection genes were defined as candidate genes, and the results were presented in a Venn diagram.


### Enrichment analysis
  R package “clusterProfiler” (v 4.2.2) [27] was applied for enrichment analysis, including Gene Ontology (GO) analysis and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis on the candidate genes. The threshold was set at adjusted P < 0.05 in enrichment analysis. GO analysis was employed in three aspects: biological process (BP), cellular component (CC), and molecular function (MF). The top 5 GO terms in the BP, CC, and MF categories were all displayed according to enriched gene number using the R package “ggplot2” (v 3.5.1). For KEGG pathway analysis, the top 10 enriched pathways were also presented according to P value using the R package “ggplot2” (v 3.5.1). GO and KEGG enrichment analyses were performed using raw P values.


### Protein–protein interaction (PPI) network construction
  The protein–protein interaction (PPI) network for candidate genes was constructed using the STRING database (https://string-db.org). For this network analysis, confidence parameters were established at 0.15 and "Homo sapiens" was designated as the target species. Subsequently, the PPI network visualization was generated through Cytoscape software (v 3.8.2) [28].


### Biomarker screening
  The R package “glmnet” (v 4.1–4) [29] was applied for Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression analysis. The optimal lambda value was determined at the minimum value of lambda where the regression coefficient was not zero through fivefold cross-validation when the model achieved the minimum residual sum of squares. LASSO feature genes were selected based on this analysis. R package “Caret” (v 6.0–93) [30] was applied for Support Vector Machine Recursive Feature Elimination (SVM-RFE) analysis on the candidate genes in the training set. The process involved iteratively removing the least important features and retraining the model. The model with the highest prediction accuracy and the smallest number of features was selected, and the corresponding genes were SVM-RFE feature genes. The R package “ggvenn” (v 1.2.2) was applied for the intersection of genes from two feature genes. These intersection genes were defined as candidate biomarkers, and the results were presented in a Venn diagram. The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was performed to assess expression differences of the candidate biomarkers between disease (pediatric sepsis) samples and normal samples. The threshold was selected as P < 0.05. The R package “pROC” (v 1.18.5) [31] was used to plot the ROC curve and calculate the area under the curve (AUC). The AUC > 0.7 was considered to indicate that the candidate biomarkers exhibited better predictive values for pediatric sepsis. The candidate biomarkers with significant differential expression (P < 0.05), consistent expression trends, and AUC > 0.7 were selected as biomarkers.


### Nomogram model construction and evaluation
  In the training set, the R package “rms” (v 6.5-0.5) [32] was applied for the nomogram model constructed using the abundance values of the biomarkers. R package “rms” (v 6.9.0) was also used to draw a calibration curve to evaluate the prediction ability of the nomogram model. The Hosmer–Lemeshow test (HLT) was applied as a model-fitting index, with a P > 0.05 indicating a good fit. R package “rmda” (v 1.6) [33] was used to draw a decision curve analysis (DCA) curve to compare the prediction performance of the nomogram model with single-gene prediction. If the net benefit in the decision curve was greater than that of the net benefit of a single-gene, it indicated that the nomogram model exhibited good predictive performance for the disease. R package “pROC” (v 1.18.0) was applied for ROC curve. AUC > 0.7 was considered to have high-accuracy prediction ability.


### Chromosome localization, sub-cellular localization, and protein expression analyses
  R package “RCircos” (v 1.2.2) [34] was applied for the distribution of biomarkers on human chromosomes, showing their chromosomal positions.

  The Genecards database (http://www.genecards.org/) was used to obtain sub-cellular distribution information for biomarkers.

  The Human Protein Atlas (HPA) (https://www.proteinatlas.org/) was applied to the protein expression of biomarkers in different tissues.


### Gene function similarity and gene correlations analyses
  R package “GOSemSim” (v 2.22.0) [35] was used to calculate the function similarity scores of the biomarkers based on the training set. A function similarity score > 0.3 was considered to indicate high function similarity among the biomarkers.

  The “cor” function in the R package “Stats” (v 4.2.2) [36] was applied for Spearman correlation analysis, with |cor|> = 0.3 and P < 0.05.


### Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA)
  Based on the expression levels of the biomarkers, the training set samples were divided into high- and low-expression groups. R package “GSVA” (v 1.42.0) [37] was used to calculate the expression data for different gene sets in each sample based on the training set. Then, differential expression analysis was performed between the two groups. Background gene set referred to c2.all.v2023.2.Hs.symbols.gmt from the Molecular Signatures Database (MsigDB) (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/msigdb/index.jsp). Pathways with t > 0 and P < 0.05 were considered upregulated, while those with t < 0 and P < 0.05 were considered downregulated. R package “ggplot2” (v 3.5.1) was employed to visualize the top 5 enriched pathways, sorted by t value descending for each biomarker.


### Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA)
  R package “psych” (v 2.2.9) [25] was applied for correlation. The results were sorted, and GSEA was performed based on the background gene set (c2.cp.kegg.v7.4.symbols.gmt). The threshold was |normalized enrichment score (NES)|> 1 and P < 0.05. R package “enrichplot” (v 1.18.3) [38] was used to visualize the 5 randomized pathways.


### Immune infiltration analysis
  The single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) algorithm was utilized for 28 immune cell abundances in disease (pediatric sepsis) and control samples of the training set. The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was applied for differences in immune-infiltrating cells, with a P < 0.05. These cells with differences were identified as differential immune cells. The R package “psych” (v 2.3.6) was applied for Spearman correlation analysis. Then, the R package “corrplot” (v 0.92) [38] was used to draw a heatmap to show the correlations.


### Molecular regulatory network construction
  The NetworkAnalyst database (https://www.networkanalyst.ca/) was used to find the transcription factors (TFs) that target the biomarkers. Cytoscape software (v 3.8.2) was utilized to construct the TF–mRNA network.

  The mirdb database (http://www.mirdb.org/), based on the R package “multiMiR” (v 1.18.0) [39], was applied to identify the microRNAs (miRNAs) that target the biomarkers. Subsequently, the predicted miRNAs were used to predict the long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs).


### The correlation analysis between biomarkers and inflammatory factors, and the immune factor
  The R package “GSVA” (v 1.42.0) was employed in the training set for ssGSEA analysis based on the hallmark inflammatory response pathway. The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was conducted to assess the ssGSEA scores of inflammatory factors related to the hallmark inflammatory response pathway, with a P < 0.05 indicating a significant difference. R package “corrplot” (v 0.92) was utilized for Spearman correlation analysis, with a threshold of |cor|> 0.3 and P < 0.05. R package “ggplot2” (v 3.5.1) was used to draw a histogram that showed the correlations between the biomarkers and the top 10 inflammatory factors sorted by cor value.

  Data on chemokine receptors, chemokines, immune inhibitors, major histocompatibility complexes, and immune stimulants were found through a comprehensive resource platform of tumor–immune system interactions (TISIDB) (http://cis.hku.hk/TISIDB/) database in the training set. R package “corrplot” (v 0.92) was employed for Spearman correlation analysis between immune factors (including chemokines, immune inhibitors, immune stimulants, chemokine receptors, and major histocompatibility complexes) and biomarkers, with a threshold of |cor|> 0.3 and P < 0.05. R package “pheatmap” (v 1.0.12) [23] was used to draw a heatmap to show the results.


### Drug prediction and molecular docking analysis
  The Drug Signatures Database (DsigDB) (https://dsigdb.tanlab.org/DSigDBv1.0/) served to identify the targeted drugs that might act on the biomarkers. The number of predicted targeted drugs for each biomarker was counted, and the drug–biomarker interactions were illustrated through Cytoscape software (v 3.8.2).

  Three-dimensional (3D) structures of the active ingredients of the targeted drug (SDF format) were acquired from the Public Chemical Database (PubChem) database (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/). The 3D structures (PDB format) for biomarkers were obtained from the Protein Data Bank (PDB) Database (https://www.rcsb.org/). "CB-DOCK (https://cadd.labshare.cn/cb-dock/php/blinddock.php) was implemented in molecular docking. PyMOL (v 3.1.1) [40] was used to visualize the docking results. A binding energy < − 5 kcal/mol implied a strong binding affinity.


### Quantitative reverse transcriptase PCR (qRT-PCR) assay
  A total of 7 peripheral blood samples were obtained from pediatric sepsis patients, and another 7 were collected from normal individuals at Children’s Hospital of Chongqing Medical University. The Institutional Review Board of the Children’s Hospital of Chongqing Medical University approved the research project, and every participant provided their informed consent. TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA) was utilized to extract total RNA from these samples. Following RNA extraction, cDNA synthesis was performed with PrimeScript RT Reagent Kit (TaKaRa, Japan). A StepOnePlus Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems, USA) was employed for RT-qPCR analysis. The 2−ΔΔCt methodology was applied to calculate comparative expression values of four biomarkers (ACER3, DGAT2, GBA, and TSPO), with GAPDH functioning as the internal reference gene. The sequence information for all primers can be found in Table S3.


### Statistical analysis
  The R programming language (v 4.3.1) facilitated all computational analyses. The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was applied for comparative gene expression evaluation and immune infiltration measurement. RT-qPCR experimental data underwent assessment via a Student’s t-test. Results yielding P-values below 0.05 were considered statistically meaningful.

### ACER3, DGAT2, GBA, and TSPO were identified as biomarkers through machine learning, expression validation, and ROC curve analysis
  Through LASSO and SVM-RFE machine-learning analyses on the 12 candidate genes, a total of 6 feature genes (CYP1B, ACER3, DGAT2, TSPO, GBA, and GLA) were obtained from LASSO analysis and 5 feature genes (DGAT2, TSPO, ACER3, GBA, and ALOX5) from SVM-RFE analysis (Fig. 3a-d). By taking the intersection of these two feature genes, a total of 4 candidate biomarkers (ACER3, DGAT2, GBA, and TSPO) were identified. The gene intersection was presented in a Venn diagram, clearly showing the common genes (Supplementary Figure S1a). The 4 candidate biomarkers (ACER3, DGAT2, GBA, and TSPO) were highly expressed in the pediatric sepsis groups (all P < 0.05) (Fig. 3f, g). This consistent high-expression pattern in different datasets suggested the reliability of these candidate biomarkers in relation to pediatric sepsis. The ROC curve analysis of the 4 candidate biomarkers demonstrated good predictive values. In the training set, the AUC values for ACER3, DGAT2, GBA, and TSPO all were greater than 0.9 (Fig. 3g–j). In the validation set, the AUC values were all greater than 0.8, respectively (Supplementary Figure S1b–e). Since all AUC values were greater than 0.7, these candidate biomarkers have a certain ability to predict pediatric sepsis. This indicated that the identified biomarkers could potentially be used to predict pediatric sepsis. ### ACER3, DGAT2, GBA, and TSPO were mainly enriched in various pathways related to pediatric sepsis progression
  GSVA and GSEA were performed on the 4 biomarkers. ## Discussion
  Pediatric sepsis is a critical and severe condition that poses a significant threat to the health and lives of children, characterized by complex pathogenesis and a high mortality rate [41]. Studies have found that abnormal lipid metabolism may exacerbate the pathological progression of sepsis by affecting immune cell functions (e.g., T lymphocyte differentiation) [42, 43]. Additionally, endoplasmic reticulum stress (ERS) not only directly contributes to sepsis-associated encephalopathy but may also lead to multiple organ dysfunction by disrupting hormone synthesis and immune cell functions [42, 44]. Furthermore, ERS activates the unfolded protein response (UPR) signaling pathway, regulating the expression of key enzymes such as sterol regulatory element-binding proteins (SREBPs) and sterol O-acyltransferases (SOATs), thereby influencing lipid synthesis and metabolism [45]. Consequently, both ERS and dysregulated lipid metabolism represent critical nodes in the pathophysiology of pediatric sepsis, where their interaction may form a vicious cycle of “stress–metabolism–immunity”. Further exploration of precise intervention strategies is needed to identify novel therapeutic targets for improving clinical outcomes in pediatric patients. This study aims to explore biomarkers associated with lipid metabolism and endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress and their underlying molecular mechanisms through bioinformatics analysis. The research obtained data from pediatric sepsis patients and normal controls from relevant databases. Through a series of bioinformatics analysis workflows, including differential gene screening, WGCNA analysis, functional enrichment analysis, and machine learning algorithm screening, four biomarkers (ACER3, DGAT2, GBA, and TSPO) were ultimately identified. In-depth investigations were conducted on their multidimensional characteristics, functions, and regulatory networks.

  ACER3 is a seven-transmembrane domain protein belonging to the alkaline ceramidase family. It has been implicated in the pathogenesis of hepatocellular carcinoma and acute myeloid leukemia, where its downregulation can suppress cell proliferation [46, 47]. Additionally, ACER3 deficiency causes early-onset progressive leukodystrophy in children—a rare disease currently lacking effective treatments [48]. Studies suggest that ACER3 regulates ceramide levels through hydrolysis, potentially influencing immune cell functions such as T lymphocyte differentiation and macrophage polarization [47]. Metabolomics studies have demonstrated that ceramide, the substrate of ACER3, is significantly elevated in sepsis patients, and this ceramide accumulation can induce inflammatory storms and cytotoxicity [17]. Dysregulated lipid metabolism, particularly ceramide imbalance (e.g., ceramide accumulation), may trigger uncontrolled inflammation or immunosuppression, which aligns with sepsis pathology [42]. This study observed ACER3 upregulation in pediatric sepsis samples, suggesting its potential role in modulating ceramide levels to impact disease progression. Specifically, the present study found that ACER3 is highly expressed in sepsis, indicating that it may be involved in lipid metabolic reprogramming by regulating ceramide hydrolysis, thereby influencing sepsis progression. However, the underlying mechanisms and therapeutic potential require further validation.

  DGAT2, located on chromosome 11, is involved in oxidative stress and the TGF-β1 signaling pathway, serving as a key regulator of lipid metabolism and providing energy for cell proliferation and growth [4]. DGAT2-mediated triglyceride (TG) synthesis may influence inflammatory signaling pathways (e.g., NF-κB, TNF-α) [5], thereby affecting the progression of sepsis. As the enzyme catalyzing the final step of TG synthesis, changes in DGAT2 activity may impact energy metabolism. For instance, abnormal hepatic lipid metabolism due to DGAT2 dysregulation could exacerbate sepsis-associated liver injury or multiple organ dysfunction syndrome (MODS) [49]. As a key lipogenic enzyme, high DGAT2 expression may promote triglyceride accumulation [50], thereby worsening sepsis-induced hepatic injury and systemic inflammation, consistent with the disease’s pathology [17], which is consistent with the high DGAT2 expression observed in the present study. Inhibition of DGAT2 may attenuate inflammatory responses and improve sepsis outcomes [49], though the precise mechanisms require further investigation.

  The mitochondrial translocator protein (TSPO), a mitochondrial outer membrane protein, is a recognized diagnostic biomarker upregulated in sepsis. Its overexpression precipitates mitochondrial dysfunction, ROS generation, and impaired autophagy, thereby amplifying the systemic inflammatory response [51]. Crucially, recent lipidomics studies have linked TSPO-mediated mitochondrial dysfunction to lipid metabolic reprogramming, characterized by the accumulation of triglycerides and toxic sphingolipids (e.g., ceramides) that correlate with myocardial injury severity [52]. Therapeutic targeting of TSPO with NADH has shown promise in reversing these defects. Metabolomic analyses confirm that NADH not only restores mitochondrial autophagy, but also normalizes the lipid profile by suppressing pro-inflammatory saturated fatty acids and upregulating anti-inflammatory mediators like lipoxin A4 [52]. Collectively, these findings highlight the NADH–TSPO axis as a critical therapeutic target for mitigating the metabolic and inflammatory sequelae of sepsis.

  GBA encodes glucocerebrosidase, and its increased transcription has been associated with impaired sphingolipid degradation and lysosomal lipid accumulation in sepsis macrophages. Such sphingolipid accumulation disrupts macrophage phagocytosis and antigen presentation, contributes to mucosal barrier dysfunction, and is linked to broader cellular impairments involving lysosomal autophagy [53], endoplasmic reticulum stress, and mitochondrial dysfunction, thereby promoting sepsis progression [54].

  Drawing on current literature and our analytical findings of this study, we explored the collective significance of ACER3, DGAT2, GBA, and TSPO in pediatric sepsis. While ACER3 and DGAT2 modulate lipid metabolism, and GBA governs lysosomal degradation, our analysis reveals that these biomarkers function synergistically to drive septic pathology. Mechanistically, the upregulation of ACER3 and DGAT2 drives ceramide and triglyceride accumulation, respectively, which—compounded by GBA-mediated impairments in lipid degradation—triggers severe lipotoxicity. This converges with TSPO-mediated mitochondrial ROS generation to amplify ER stress. In the pediatric context, this metabolic–inflammatory cascade exploits specific vulnerabilities—namely, immune immaturity and limited metabolic reserves—thereby accelerating multiple organ dysfunction syndrome (MODS). Clinically, these molecular disruptions map to distinct phenotypes: ACER3 predicts septic shock, DGAT2 correlates with liver injury, TSPO is linked to heart failure, and suppressed GBA prolongs hospitalization. Consequently, given their coordinated fluctuation under disease conditions, we propose that the combined detection of these biomarkers offers superior diagnostic specificity and prognostic accuracy compared to individual assessments (Fig. 12). TCDD was prioritized as a candidate compound due to its well-established interaction with the aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR) [55], which plays a critical role in regulating inflammatory signaling and lipid metabolism in sepsis [56, 57]. This AhR-centered mechanism is highly consistent with the lipid metabolic dysregulation and endoplasmic reticulum stress pathways identified in the present study.Fig. 12Proposed mechanistic model linking lipid metabolism, endoplasmic reticulum stress, and immune dysfunction in pediatric sepsis

  Sepsis is driven by maladaptive metabolic reprogramming in which lipid dysregulation and ER stress fuel immune dysfunction and organ injury, and the upregulation of ACER3, DGAT2, GBA, and TSPO captures this transcriptomic–metabolic stress state. Integrating these transcriptional biomarkers with lipid metabolite readouts enables early risk stratification and provides a mechanistic framework for precision interventions targeting lipid metabolism and ER stress in pediatric sepsis.

  Through GSEA enrichment, this study identified four biological pathways commonly associated with the biomarkers, including the ribosome, FcγR-mediated phagocytosis, and Leishmania infection. The ribosome serves as the core machinery for protein synthesis, with its rRNA catalytic function and dynamic interactions with tRNA being key research focuses [58]. Current studies have found that sepsis significantly suppresses muscle protein synthesis (MPS) in neonatal pigs, an effect closely linked to reduced ribosomal functional activity and translational efficiency [59]. In contrast, the physiological decline in MPS during development is primarily attributed to a decrease in ribosomal abundance. Additionally, research has shown that sepsis patients exhibit increased insulin-responsive aminopeptidase (IRAP) expression in platelets. IRAP can drive ribosomal degradation to supply energy for platelet activation, thereby influencing thrombus formation in sepsis [60]. Based on these findings, it is hypothesized that under septic conditions, systemic inflammatory responses and organ dysfunction interfere with the rRNA catalytic activity of ribosomes and their dynamic interactions with tRNA, leading to a significant decline in protein synthesis efficiency. Ribosome-related processes are essential for synthesizing proteins required for immune responses, while FcγR-mediated phagocytosis is crucial for pathogen clearance. The involvement in the Leishmania infection pathway may suggest a shared immune-evasion mechanism related to sepsis [61].

  In this study, the chemokines CCL5, CCL4, and CCL28 were all negatively correlated with the four identified biomarkers. Previous reports indicate that in a mouse model of sepsis-associated acute kidney injury (AKI), CCL5 + macrophages significantly amplify the inflammatory response by activating the IL6–JAK–STAT3 and TGF-β signaling pathways. Additionally, they recruit neutrophils via the CCL5-CCR1 ligand–receptor pair, exacerbating renal damage [62]. CCL4 has been identified as a potential biomarker for pediatric sepsis and septic shock, possibly influencing immune dysregulation in sepsis by modulating monocyte chemotaxis and cytokine secretion (e.g., TNF-α, IL-6). Its expression levels correlate with the severity of inflammation and organ injury [63].

  Although CCL28 has not been specifically studied in pediatric sepsis, it is known to recruit CCR10 + IgA plasmablasts and T cells to mucosal tissues (e.g., gut, respiratory tract), enhancing local immune defense. It is hypothesized that CCL28 may help maintain mucosal barrier integrity, thereby reducing the risk of secondary infections [64]. Based on these findings, the negative correlations between CCL5, CCL4, and CCL28 and the four biomarkers may reflect a compensatory anti-inflammatory response in the body.

  This study identified key biomarkers associated with lipid metabolism and endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress in pediatric sepsis (e.g., ACER3, DGAT2, GBA, and TSPO), through integrative bioinformatics analysis, and validated their aberrant expression by qRT-PCR. These genes were found to be centrally involved in lipid metabolic regulation and cellular stress responses, particularly sphingolipid metabolism, triglyceride synthesis, mitochondrial function, and oxidative stress. Collectively, our findings define an integrated transcription–metabolism–immunity axis, in which transcriptional activation of these biomarkers promotes lipotoxic metabolite accumulation and sustained ER stress, leading to mitochondrial dysfunction, excessive inflammatory signaling, and immune imbalance characterized by neutrophil overactivation and suppression of adaptive immune responses. This molecular signature reflects a systemic stress-adaptive state underlying pediatric sepsis and provides a mechanistic basis for early risk stratification and the development of metabolism- and ER stress-oriented therapeutic strategies. However, we acknowledge that these conclusions are currently based on transcriptomic and gene expression data; the study is limited by the absence of proteomic and metabolomic validation to confirm downstream lipid flux, as well as constraints regarding sample source homogeneity and insufficient clinical heterogeneity. Additionally, the limited sample size (n = 7/group) compromises statistical power and generalizability, despite achieving P < 0.05. Moreover, the lack of effect size analysis restricts our ability to quantify the true biological magnitude of the observed expression changes. Future research should focus on: (1) expanding multicenter clinical cohorts to integrate data from diverse geographic regions, pathogen types, and organ injury severities, thereby establishing a stratified biomarker system; (2) implementing a multi-omics approach—utilizing UPLC–MS/MS and protein assays—to map the complete “gene–protein–metabolite” axis and verify the functional impact on lipid metabolites (e.g., ceramides, triglycerides); (3) developing cross-species validation models, including LPS-induced endothelial cell pyroptosis models and CLP-induced sepsis juvenile rat models, to elucidate the dynamic regulatory mechanisms of candidate genes in ER stress–mitochondrial dysfunction cascades using CRISPR-based gene editing; (4) creating a rapid diagnostic kit based on the 4-gene panel, combined with single-cell sequencing to resolve spatiotemporal expression patterns of target genes in immune subsets such as M0 macrophages, thereby providing a theoretical foundation for precise therapeutic interventions.


## Conclusions
  This study aims to discover and validate biomarkers related to lipid metabolism and endoplasmic reticulum stress in pediatric sepsis. The study analyzed the transcriptomic data from the GEO database to screen for differentially expressed genes (DEGs). By integrating lipid metabolism-related genes (LRGs), endoplasmic reticulum stress-related genes (ERGs), and the key module genes obtained through weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA), four biomarkers, namely ACER3, DGAT2, GBA, and TSPO, were selected via machine learning methods (LASSO and SVM-RFE). The results showed that these biomarkers were highly expressed in pediatric sepsis patients and had good predictive ability (AUC > 0.7). The constructed nomogram model had excellent predictive performance. They were involved in sepsis-related pathways and were associated with immune cells such as dendritic cells (positively correlated) and B cells (negatively correlated). There was a complex molecular regulatory network including transcription factors, miRNAs, and lncRNAs, and they had a strong binding affinity with tetrachlorodibenzo-p-dioxin. The high expression was also verified by qRT-PCR. This study provides new insights into the pathogenesis and treatment of pediatric sepsis.
