# Integrated bioinformatic identification and translational validation of key biomarkers and therapeutic candidates for preeclampsia-related acute kidney injury


## Objective
  This study aimed to identify and validate key biomarkers for PE-AKI and to explore potential therapeutic candidates through an integrated bioinformatic and experimental approach.


## Methods
  Data was obtained from transcriptome sequencing of 12 mouse tissue samples and 8 human cell samples. Differential expression analysis identified differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in mouse and human PE kidney injury. The intersection of DEGs was used to identify candidate genes. Key genes were further identified through protein–protein interaction (PPI) networks, followed by subcellular localization, functional enrichment, molecular regulatory network construction, drug prediction, and molecular docking. The expression of key genes was validated in LPS-induced PE mouse models and blood samples from PE and PE-AKI patients.


## Results
  IL-6, CXCL2, and CEBPB were identified as key genes. They were significantly upregulated in both experimental and clinical PE-AKI samples and were co-enriched in pivotal inflammatory pathways, including interferon-gamma/alpha response and TNF-α signaling via NF-κB. Immune infiltration analysis revealed significant alterations in 28 immune cell types in the PE-AKI model. A comprehensive molecular regulatory network, encompassing 35 transcription factors and 93 miRNAs, was constructed. Drug prediction and molecular docking identified deptropine and clotrimazole as high-affinity candidates capable of stably binding all three key genes. Clinically, CXCL2 and CEBPB demonstrated promising diagnostic value for PE-AKI, with AUCs of 0.770 and 0.790, respectively.


## Conclusion
  IL-6, CXCL2, and CEBPB are identified as key mediators in PE-AKI. Among them, CXCL2 and CEBPB show particular promise as diagnostic biomarkers, a finding that warrants further validation in larger, multi-center cohorts to confirm their utility in stratifying disease severity.

## What does this study add to the clinical work
  This study identifies CXCL2 and CEBPB as promising diagnostic biomarkers for PE-AKI, potentially enabling earlier detection and improved risk stratification. Furthermore, deptropine and clotrimazole are highlighted as repurposable therapeutic candidates, suggesting novel avenues for targeted intervention in PE-AKI management.


## Introduction
  Renal injury is characterized by acute or chronic deterioration of kidney function resulting from diverse pathological factors [1] (e.g., ischemia, nephrotoxic agents, immune dysregulation). Its pathological hallmarks include decreased glomerular filtration rate (GFR), tubular necrosis, and interstitial fibrosis [2]. Preeclampsia (PE), a life-threatening gestational disorder, manifests as hypertension and proteinuria emerging after 20 weeks of gestation, potentially escalating to multi-organ failure [3, 4]. Renal involvement in PE results in PE-related acute kidney injury (PE-AKI), characterized by elevated creatinine, significant GFR decline, progressive proteinuria, and distinct histopathological lesions including glomerular endothelial swelling and thrombotic microangiopathy [5]. PE-AKI not only reflects disease severity, but is also independently associated with adverse outcomes, including preterm delivery, fetal growth restriction, and maternal renal failure, posing a major perinatal healthcare burden [5]. Epidemiological studies reveal that 2–8% of global pregnancies are complicated by PE, with 15–30% of these patients developing acute kidney injury—particularly in resource-limited regions where PE-AKI accounts for 10–15% of maternal mortality [6].

  While mechanistic research has advanced our understanding of PE pathogenesis (e.g., placental ischemia-induced oxidative stress, soluble fms-like tyrosine kinase-1 (sFlt-1)/placental growth factor (PlGF) ratio imbalance, systemic endothelial dysfunction), the kidney-specific molecular mechanisms underlying PE-AKI remain elusive. Current management focuses on blood pressure control (e.g., labetalol), seizure prophylaxis (magnesium sulfate), and timely delivery, but lacks targeted renal-protective therapies [7]. Emerging strategies—including angiogenic factor modulation (sFlt-1 neutralizing antibodies), complement inhibition (anti-C5 monoclonal antibodies), and mitochondrial regulators (coenzyme Q10)—show preclinical promise but require rigorous clinical validation [8]. Notably, most existing treatments address systemic symptoms without targeting kidney-specific injury pathways in PE-AKI, and some carry potential fetal risks. This therapeutic gap underscores the urgent need to elucidate PE-AKI’s molecular mechanisms and identify key regulatory genes for targeted intervention.

  RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) has emerged as a cornerstone technology for deciphering gene expression regulation. This high-throughput approach, utilizing platforms such as Illumina short-read, PacBio, and Nanopore long-read sequencing, enables comprehensive characterization of transcriptome-wide changes—including transcript abundance, alternative splicing patterns, novel transcripts, and non-coding RNA profiles—under specific physiological or pathological conditions [9, 10]. In nephrology research, RNA-seq has become instrumental in elucidating molecular signatures of acute and chronic kidney diseases [11]. Key findings include: In acute kidney injury (AKI), ischemia–reperfusion models reveal tubular epithelial cell markers (e.g., Havcr1, Lcn2) for early injury detection, with dysregulated TNF-α/NF-κB and Hippo-YAP signaling driving inflammatory and fibrotic responses [12, 13]. Chronic kidney disease (CKD) studies integrating bulk and single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) have identified podocyte-specific gene inactivation (NPHS1, WT1) and fibroblast phenotypic switching as key mediators of interstitial fibrosis [14]. Notably, while these advances have uncovered shared molecular mechanisms of renal injury, significant knowledge gaps remain regarding PE-AKI. The unique pathophysiology of PE-AKI characterized by a placental anti-angiogenic factor (sFlt-1) release, pregnancy-specific immune adaptation, and renal endothelial dysfunction [15], likely drives distinct transcriptional networks that cannot be extrapolated from non-pregnancy renal injury models.

  This study pioneered the comparative transcriptomic analysis of LPS-induced preeclampsia-like kidney injury in both murine models and human renal tubular epithelial cells using RNA sequencing. Furthermore, by integrating machine learning algorithms, it aimed to screen key genes with high diagnostic efficacy, which was expected to overcome the current limitations of relying on a single indicator, achieving early warning and risk stratification for PE-AKI, thereby improving the precision and safety of renal protection during pregnancy.


## Methods

### Sample collection and sequencing

#### Establishment of LPS-induced PE-like mouse model
  All animal experiments were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Gansu Provincial Maternity and Child Health Care Hospital. Female ICR mice (8 weeks old, 35–40 g body weight) were purchased from Shanghai Sippr-BK Laboratory Animal Co. Ltd. and randomly divided into two groups: control group (Con, n = 15): received daily intraperitoneal (i.p.) injections of saline (volume-matched to LPS) from embryonic day (E) 7.5 to E17.5. LPS-treated group (n = 15): administered 20 mg/kg LPS [16] (Sigma-Aldrich, USA) daily via i.p. injection during E7.5–E17.5.All mice were euthanized at E18.5 (pre-delivery). Blood, urine, and tissues (placenta and kidneys) were collected for further analysis. Kidney samples were processed for transcriptomic sequencing and validation. The LPS-induced model was selected for this study due to its well-characterized ability to elicit a robust systemic inflammatory response, a cornerstone of PE pathophysiology [17]. This model efficiently recapitulates several key clinical features of PE, including the rapid onset of hypertension, proteinuria, and glomerular endotheliosis [16], which are highly relevant to the study of PE-AKI.


#### Blood pressure monitoring and proteinuria assessment
  Blood pressure parameters (systolic [SBP], mean, and diastolic [DBP]) were measured daily between 8:00 and 11:00 AM using tail-cuff plethysmography (Softron, Japan), following a 5-day acclimation period to the procedure prior to pregnancy. At E18.5, urine was collected from individually housed dams maintained in metabolic cages under fasting conditions. Urinary albumin levels were quantified using a commercial assay kit, with creatinine concentrations determined in parallel. The albumin-to-creatinine ratio (ACR) was calculated as a standardized index of proteinuria, as previously described.


#### Cell culture and treatment
  The HK-2 human renal tubular epithelial cell line was obtained from Zhong Qiao Xin Zhou Biotechnology (Shanghai, China) and maintained in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium/F-12 (DMEM/F-12; Gibco, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum. Cells were cultured at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO2. For experiments, cells were treated with 10 μg/mL LPS[16, 18] for 24 h prior to collection for transcriptomic sequencing analysis.


### RNA isolation and sequencing protocol
  Total RNA was extracted from renal tissues of both control and LPS-treated groups (n = 6 mice per group) and HK-2 human renal tubular epithelial cells (n = 4 replicates per group) using TRIzol reagent (Thermo Fisher, 15,596,018), with subsequent quality control performed using a NanoDrop ND-1000 spectrophotometer for concentration/purity assessment and a Bioanalyzer 2100 system for RNA integrity evaluation, where only samples meeting the stringent criteria of > 50 ng/μL concentration, RNA integrity number (RIN) > 7.0, and total RNA quantity > 1 μg were processed for downstream applications. Sequencing libraries were then prepared from 1 μg of qualified total RNA using the TruSeq RNA LT Sample Prep Kit v2 (Illumina) through a standardized workflow comprising poly(A) selection with oligo-dT magnetic beads, RNA fragmentation (200–300 bp), cDNA synthesis with SuperScript II reverse transcriptase, and adapter ligation, followed by 8-cycle PCR amplification to generate strand-specific libraries with 300 ± 50 bp insert sizes, which were subsequently subjected to paired-end sequencing (PE150) on an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform following the manufacturer’s standardized protocols.


### Differential expression analysis
  To assess sample quality and reproducibility, and to explore whether preeclampsia kidney injury mouse samples could be distinguished from control mouse samples, as well as whether human kidney injury cell samples could be differentiated from control cell samples, principal component analysis (PCA) analysis of the data was conducted. Pearson correlation analysis and PCA were, respectively, performed on the RNA-sequencing data of six mice with preeclampsia acute kidney injury (PE-AKI Illness) and six control samples to filter out the outlier samples. The RNA-sequencing data of four individuals with kidney injury and four healthy control cell samples were used to perform Pearson correlation analysis and principal component analysis (PCA) independently. Subsequently, for the PE-AKI and control of mouse samples, the “DESeq2” package (v 1.42.0) [19] was applied to detect differentially expressed genes 1 (DEGs1), where the genes met the conditions of |log2 fold change (FC)|> 0.5 and p < 0.05. Likewise, for the kidney injury and control of human renal tubule epithelial cell samples, the “DESeq2” package (v 1.42.0) was used to detect differentially expressed genes 2 (DEGs2) under the same thresholds of |log2 fold change (FC)|> 0.5 and p < 0.05 (false discovery rate (FDR) correction was carried out during the analysis). The “ggplot2” package (v 3.5.1) [20] was utilized to create individual volcano plots for DEG1 and DEG2. After that, for each plot among these, the five genes that were most prominently upregulated and the five genes that were most prominently downregulated were indicated, specifically from DEG1 and DEG2, respectively, were marked clearly. With the aim of identifying candidate genes, the “ggvenn” package (v 0.1.9) [20] was utilized for calculating the overlapping part of DEGs1 and DEGs2.


### Functional enrichment analysis of candidate genes and identification of key genes
  We conducted functional enrichment analysis to delve into the biological functions and signaling pathways of candidate genes associated with the pathogenesis of kidney injury. The “clusterProfiler” package (v 4.15.0.003) [20] and “org.Hs.eg.db” (v 3.16.0) [21] were employed to conduct Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) functional enrichment analyses on the candidate genes. Significance was determined with a threshold of p < 0.05. Next, to study the network interactions of candidate genes at the protein level, the protein interaction relationships among candidate genes were determined by making use of the STRING database (https://cn.string-db.org/) (interaction score ≥ 0.15). With the isolated nodes eliminated, the Cytoscape software (v 3.9.1) [22] was resorted to generate a protein–protein interaction (PPI) network. In an attempt to pinpoint the key genes, two algorithms (MCC and EPC) in the CytoHubba plugin of the Cytoscape software (v 3.10.2) were employed to evaluate the importance of each candidate gene. The top five candidate genes from each algorithm were selected and visualized, and their intersection was determined as the key genes using the “VennDiagram” package (v 1.4.0) [23]. In addition, the expression levels of key genes in mouse PE-AKI and control samples, as well as human AKI and control samples, were, respectively, calculated using the Wilcox. Test (p < 0.05).


### Localization and correlation analysis of key genes
  In an effort to explore the potential function of the key genes, GeneCards (https://www.genecards.org/) was used for subcellular localization analysis to determine the subcellular regions associated with the key genes. In addition, to obtain the correlations among key genes, the “psych” package (v 2.2.9) [24] was employed to perform a Spearman correlation analysis on key genes, with the analysis relying on the mouse self-sequencing data from PE-AKI (Illness) and control samples. The analysis was carried out according to the standards of |cor|> 0.3 and p < 0.05. Furthermore, the “GOSemSim” package [25] was employed to compute the functional similarity between key genes, where a score > 0.4 was considered indicative of functional similarity.


### Gene enrichment analysis (GSEA), gene co-expression network, and immune infiltration analysis
  In an attempt to identify the biological pathways through which key genes function in PE-AKI, we conducted enrichment analysis of key genes based on mouse transcriptome data. To obtain the biological pathways through which key genes function in the Illness of mice, the Spearman correlation coefficient between each key gene and other genes was first computed using the cor function of “stats” package (v 4.2.2) [26]. Then the key genes and other genes were ranked in descending order based on their correlation coefficients to illustrate the correlations between them. Subsequently, the c2.kegg.v7.4.symb dataset from the MSigDB (http://www.broadinstitute.org/msigdb) was taken as the background gene set, and the species was selected as mice. Finally, the “clusterProfiler” (v 4.7.1.003) [27] was used for GSEA pathway enrichment analysis of key genes (normalized enrichment score (NES)|> 1, FDR < 0.25, p < 0.05). Furthermore, to find other genes linked to the functions of key genes, the GeneMANIA database (https://genemania.org/) was applied to estimate the genes linked to the functions of key genes and the functions in which they are involved. Then the GeneMANIA network was set up. The relative abundance of cell infiltration in mouse PE-AKI and control samples was quantified using the ssGSEA algorithm in the “GSVA” package (v 1.48.2) [28], and the reference gene set of mouse cells was obtained from a published study [29]. The infiltration status of 36 types of immune cells in mouse PE-AKI and control samples was further evaluated. Differential immune cells between the mouse PE-AKI group and the control group were calculated using the Wilcoxon test (p < 0.05).


### Molecular regulatory network analysis
  To investigate the regulatory function of key genes in renal injury, the Transcriptional Regulatory Relationships Unraveled by Sentence-based Text mining (TRRUST) database (https://www.grnpedia.org/trrust/) was first utilized to predict the transcription factors (TFs) that interact with these key genes. Subsequently, the microRNA Database (miRDB) database (http://www.mirdb.org/) was resorted for forecasting the miRNAs interacting with key genes, and to visualize the TF-mRNA and miRNA-mRNA network by cytoscape software (v 3.9.1) [30].


### Drug prediction and molecular docking analysis
  In an attempt to explore potential drugs for AKI treatment, the potential targeted therapeutic drugs for key genes were predicted using the “enrichR” package (v 3.4) [31] and the Drug SIGnatures DataBase (DSigDB, http://dsigdb.tanlab.org/DSiqDBv1.0/), and drugs with an Adjusted p value greater than 0.05 were screened. Finally, Cytoscape software (v 3.9.1) was utilized to construct a network depicting the interactions between the drugs and the key genes. Subsequently, the top two drugs ranked by combined score and the key genes were selected for molecular docking. First, the drugs’ 3D structures active ingredients were obtained from PubChem (https://pubchem.ncbi. nlm.nih.gov/). Then the target protein’s 3D structure (key genes) was retrieved from the Protein Data Bank (PDB) (http://www.rcsb.org). Subsequently, molecular docking was performed using the AutoDock software (v 4.2.6) [32].


### Human subject
  Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to study initiation. All research procedures were conducted in accordance with the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Gansu Provincial Maternity and Child Health Care Hospital. Between May 2024 and January 2025, we prospectively enrolled 30 pregnant women from the Department of Obstetrics, including 10 patients with preeclampsia (PE), 10 with PE-related acute kidney injury (PE-AKI), and 10 normotensive pregnancies (NP) with uncomplicated singleton pregnancies. Participants were selected based on the following criteria: 1. diagnosis of PE according to Chinese guidelines for hypertensive disorders in pregnancy (2015/2020 editions) [33, 34], preeclampsia was diagnosed after the 20th week of pregnancy when blood pressure  ≥ 140 mmHg systolic and/or ≥ 90 mmHg diastolic at least in two measurements made 4 h apart accompanied by one or more of the following: (1). proteinuria, (2). maternal organ dysfunction including: renal insufficiency (serum creatinine concentrations ≥ 97 μmol/L; 1.1 mg/dL), impaired liver function (ALT or AST ≥ 70 U/L), pulmonary edema, microvascular disease, thrombocytopenia, impaired liver function, and peripheral severe organ involvement (visual impairment and headache); 2. the diagnosis of AKI [35], defined by the Kidney Disease Improving Global Outcomes clinical practice guideline as an increase in serum creatinine levels of 0.3 mg/dL (26.5 μmol/L) within 48 h, or 1.5 times from baseline within 48 h within 7 days, or an accumulated 6-h urine volume of 0.5 mL/kg/h.; 3. nulliparity; 4. singleton gestation; and 5.comprehensive laboratory data. Exclusion criteria included multiple gestation, pre-existing chronic diseases (cardiovascular, renal, or endocrine disorders), and other pregnancy complications (placenta previa, placental abruption, or fetal anomalies). Clinical parameters including maternal age, body mass index, gestational age, parity, blood pressure measurements, 24-h urinary protein, and serum markers (creatinine, uric acid, and blood urea nitrogen) were systematically collected. Following an overnight fast, peripheral blood samples were obtained via antecubital venipuncture in the morning, from which peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were immediately isolated for subsequent RNA extraction and analysis.


### Reverse transcription-quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR)
  Renal tissue specimens and whole blood samples (n = 5 pairs) were obtained from LPS-induced preeclampsia-like pregnant mice. Concurrently, peripheral blood samples were collected from three human cohorts: (1) PE patients, (2) PE-AKI cases, and (3) normotensive pregnancy (NP). These samples were then used for RT-qPCR analysis. This research obtained ethical approval from the Gansu Provincial Maternity and Child Care Hospital Ethics Committee. Total RNA was isolated from these samples in line with the manufacturer’s protocol for TRIzol (Ambion, Austin, USA). Then in accordance with the provided protocol, the extracted RNA’s reverse-transcription into cDNA was performed with the SureScript First-Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Servicebio, Wuhan, China). Subsequently, RT-qPCR’s implementation was achieved with the 2 × Universal Blue SYBR Green qPCR Master Mix (Servicebio, Wuhan, China). The primer sequences were as follows: mouse IL-6,5-TAGTCCTTCCTACCCCAATTTCC-3 (forward) and 5-TTGGTCCTTAGCCACTCCTTC-3 (reverse), mouse CXCL2, 5-CCAACCACCAGGCTACAGG-3 (forward) and 5-GTGCCTATGACTTCTTTTCCACCA-3 (reverse), mouse CEBPB, 5-CCGGAAGTAGCCGTTGATAA-3 (forward) and 5-GCTCGGCTGCTTGGAC-3 (reverse) mouse GAPDH, 5-CTCTTCCACCTTCGATGCCG-3 (forward) and 5-TTATGGGGGTCTGGGATGGA-3 (reverse), human IL-6, 5-CTCAATATTAGAGTCTCAACCCCCA-3 (forward) and 5-;G 5-;GTGGGGCGGCTACATCTTT-3 (reverse), human CXCL2, 5-CCGGTCGCCCAGTGCTT-3 (forward) and 5-TGGCATATTCTTGCCTTTGAAG-3 (reverse), human CEBPB, 5-CGACTTCAGCGCCTACATTG-3 (forward) and 5-CAGCTTGAGCAAGTAGCCG-3 (reverse), human GAPDH, 5-GGAGCGAGATCCCTCCAAAAT-3 (forward) and 5-GGCTGTTGTCATACTTCTCATGG-3 (reverse). GAPDH served as the internal reference gene and the key genes’ expression levels were calculated by the 2 − ΔΔCt method. Eventually, the PCR results were examined and presented visually with the help of GraphPad (v v 5.0.0) [36].


### Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) and periodic acid-Schiff (PAS) staining
  Placental and kidney tissues were harvested from the mice on E18.5, fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde overnight at 4 ℃ and embedded in paraffin wax. Paraffin sections at a 5-mmthickness were routinely dewaxed and rehydrated, stained with H&E using the standard techniques and analyzed. Histological quantification of the number of calcifications per field was performed under 10 × magnification in a random field (18 placentas for each group). The statistical analysis was based on the results of six animals from each group and three placentas from each animal. For PAS staining, renal injury was assessed by quantifying the glomeruli that showed characteristic features of damage, which mainly included a decreased Bowman’s space and occlusion of the capillary loop spaces. Histological quantification of renal damage per field was performed under 10 × magnification in a random field (15 glomeruli for each field and 6 animals/kidneys for each group). The glomeruli in each field were given a score based on the amount of capillary space evident within Bowman’s capsule, as described previously [7]. The highest score of 5 was awarded to a glomeruli patient with a normal amount of interstitial space within Bowman’s capsule. A score of 1 was assigned to glomeruli that showed a complete loss of capillary space, and an intermediate score of 3 was assigned to glomeruli that displayed reduced, but not completely erased, capillary spaces. The scores for each field were divided by the number of glomeruli to obtain an average score per glomerulus for each field.


### Statistical analysis
  The R programming language (v 4.2.2) was employed to conduct bioinformatics analyses. For two groups of data with non-normal distribution (e.g., differential gene expression), the Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used; for two groups of qPCR data that conformed to normal distribution and had homogeneous variance, the t test was used; for multiple groups of clinical data (expressed as mean ± standard deviation), ANOVA was used. If p < 0.05, it was considered that there was a statistically significant difference. The receiver operating curve (ROC) was used to assess the efficacy of the model, and the area under the curve (AUC) was used to assess the efficacy of the model.


## Results

### Identification and enrichment of candidate genes
  Through mouse samples’ study, a total of 6006 differentially expressed genes 1 (DEGs1) were detected between the PE-AKI (Illness) and control groups. Among these, 2882 genes showed upregulation and 3124 genes showed downregulation in the PE-AKI (Illness) group (Fig. 1A, B). However, in the human samples’ analysis, only 156 differentially expressed genes 2 (DEGs2) were detected between the AKI and control groups. Of these, 106 genes were upregulated and 50 genes were downregulated in the AKI group (Fig. 1C, D). Subsequently, the “ggvenn” package was employed to identify the intersection of DEGs1 and DEGs2, resulting in the identification of 72 candidate genes (Fig. 1E). In addition, the candidate genes were significantly enriched in a total of 650 Gene Ontology (GO), including 587 biological processes (BPs) such as response to lipopolysaccharide, 9 cellular components (CCs) such as the rough endoplasmic reticulum and chloride channel complex, and 54 molecular functions (MFs) such as cytokine activity and cytokine receptor binding (p < 0.05) (Fig. 1F, Additional File 1). Besides, KEGG analysis identified a total of 23 enriched functional pathways, including tuberculosis, NOD-like receptor signaling pathway, and others (p < 0.05) (Fig. 1G, Additional File 2). These findings indicated that IL-6, CXCL2, and CEBPB were all co-enriched in five pathways, including interferon-gamma response, interferon-alpha response, and so on. This suggested that these pathways were likely to be crucial in the PE-AKI (Illness) and AKI’s physiological processes. ## Discussion
  The kidneys are among the earliest and most severely affected organs in PE, with renal injury representing a critical complication of systemic immune-inflammatory dysregulation that can lead to variable degrees of renal dysfunction [16]. Early diagnosis is crucial for improving clinical outcomes and preventing long-term chronic kidney disease [37]. Our study employed an integrated approach combining transcriptomic profiling of LPS-induced PE-like mouse models (n = 12) and LPS-treated human renal tubular epithelial cells (n = 8) with bioinformatics analysis to elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying PE-AKI. Through differential gene expression analysis and protein–protein interaction network screening, we identified three key hub genes—IL-6, CXCL2, and CEBPB—that were significantly enriched in immune-inflammatory pathways, including IFN-γ/α response, TNF-α/NF-κB signaling, and the IL-6-JAK-STAT3 axis. These findings suggest that inflammatory-immune crosstalk, mediated by these hub genes, plays a central role in driving the progression of PE-AKI. Our study delivered fresh perspectives on the molecular underpinnings of the PE-AKI’s development and identified potential biomarkers and therapeutic targets that could be instrumental for preemptive medical interventions.

  Interleukin-6 (IL-6), encoded by the IL6 gene, is a pleiotropic glycoprotein cytokine that plays a pivotal role in initiating and regulating immune responses [38]. It promotes B-cell differentiation into plasma cells, thereby enhancing antibody production and humoral immunity, while also modulating T-cell activation, proliferation, and differentiation to regulate cellular immune responses [39]. Notably, IL-6 serves as the primary inducer of hepatic acute-phase protein synthesis (e.g., C-reactive protein, CRP) during infection or tissue injury, coordinating systemic inflammatory defenses. In the kidney, IL-6 mediates local pathological effects through trans-signaling via soluble IL-6 receptor (sIL-6R), driving Th17 cell differentiation and collagen deposition [40]. Of clinical relevance, elevated IL-6 levels in preeclamptic placentas disrupt the trophoblast–endothelial invasion barrier, a process potentially propagated to the kidneys through circulating exosomes, leading to remote renal injury [41]. This dual role—both in systemic inflammation and organ-specific damage—highlights IL-6 as a key mediator linking placental dysfunction to renal pathology in PE.

  CXCL2 (C-X-C motif chemokine ligand 2), a member of the CXC chemokine subfamily, functions as a key mediator of neutrophil recruitment through specific binding to the CXCR2 receptor. In the hypoxic-ischemic renal microenvironment, tubular epithelial cell-derived CXCL2 promotes neutrophil extracellular trap (NET) formation [42]. The subsequent release of cytotoxic NET components, including histone H3 and myeloperoxidase (MPO), directly damages podocyte slit diaphragm integrity, exacerbating proteinuria [43]. Notably, CXCL2 also activates the PI3K/Akt pathway to induce endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EndMT) in glomerular endothelial cells [44]. This pathological remodeling process shares molecular homology with the aberrant placental vascular changes observed in PE [45], suggesting that CXCL2 may represent a common mechanistic node linking renal and placental dysfunction in this disorder. Such molecular convergence raises the intriguing possibility of developing unified therapeutic strategies targeting the CXCL2-CXCR2 axis.

  The transcription factor CCAAT/enhancer-binding protein beta (CEBPB), a member of the C/EBP family, serves as a critical regulator of cellular proliferation, differentiation, and inflammatory responses. Acting downstream of NF-κB signaling, CEBPB forms transcriptionally active heterodimers with p65 upon LPS stimulation, directly activating IL-6 and CXCL2 expression [46]. In macrophages, CEBPB drives M1 polarization and TNF-α production, while in adipocytes, it modulates lipid metabolism through PPARγ-dependent pathways [47]. Clinical samples demonstrated progressive upregulation of CEBPB expression with disease severity, mirroring the trajectory of renal dysfunction in preeclampsia. This pattern supports the proposed “inflammation-insulin resistance” vicious cycle in PE-AKI, wherein placental ischemia-derived free fatty acids activate renal CEBPB, creating a feedforward loop that perpetuates both local and systemic pathology.

  The results of GSEA indicated that the key genes IL6, CXCL2, and CEBP were significantly enriched in the relevant key pathways of interferon (IFN)-γ/α response, TNF-α/NF-κB signaling, IL-6-JAK-STAT3 signaling, and inflammatory response. These findings provided valuable insights into the potential mechanisms by which these genes might contribute to the pathogenesis of PE-AKI. Enrichment of genes in the IFN-γ/α pathway suggested their involvement in immune responses against viral infections [48]. In the context of PE-AKI, this may indicate that excessive immune activation contributes to endothelial dysfunction and renal damage. IFN production can trigger the JAK-STAT signaling pathway, which regulate inflammation and immune responses [49], potentially exacerbating kidney injury in PE. The TNF-α/NF-κB signaling pathway is a key mediator of inflammation and immune activation [50, 51]. TNF-α, a proinflammatory cytokine, induces the expression of cell adhesion molecules and chemokines, promoting leukocyte infiltration and tissue inflammation [52]. In PE-AKI, elevated TNF-α levels may activate NF-κB transcription factors, leading to the upregulation of inflammatory genes and subsequent endothelial dysfunction and renal injury [53].

  The IL-6-JAK-STAT3 pathway serves as a central regulator of inflammatory and immune responses [54]. Its enrichment in PE-AKI suggests a potentially significant role in the inflammatory cascade leading to renal damage. Activation of IL-6-mediated JAK-STAT3 signaling may upregulate genes involved in cell survival, proliferation, and inflammation [55, 56], potentially contributing to the pathogenesis of PE-AKI. Notably, a positive feedback loop exists between the IL-6-JAK-STAT3 and TNF-α/NF-κB pathways: TNF-α activates NF-κB via IκB kinase (IKK)-mediated phosphorylation, upregulating CEBPB expression, while CEBPβ enhances IL6 transcription, and IL-6 further amplifies NF-κB activity through STAT3 phosphorylation [57]. This feedforward amplification loop provides a mechanistic explanation for the persistent systemic inflammation observed in PE patients, even after pathogen-associated molecular patterns (e.g., LPS) are cleared. Inflammatory response pathways represent coordinated biological processes involving immune system activation in response to harmful stimuli [58]. In PE-AKI, excessive inflammatory activation may trigger the release of multiple mediators, including IL-6, TNF-α, and CXCL2 [59, 60], potentially contributing to endothelial dysfunction and renal damage. The enrichment of these genes in inflammatory pathways suggests their potential role as key drivers of PE-AKI pathogenesis. GSEA analysis highlights the involvement of IL6, CXCL2, and CEBP in inflammatory and immune response pathways, which may represent promising therapeutic targets for mitigating renal injury in PE. Further investigation is required to elucidate the precise mechanisms of these genes and pathways in PE-AKI development and to develop targeted therapeutic strategies. These mechanistic insights support three promising therapeutic strategies: (1) IL-6R blockade with tocilizumab (demonstrated renal protection in pregnancy models) [61], (2) CXCL2/CXCR2 axis inhibition using reparixin (reduces renal neutrophil infiltration [62]; and (3) CEBPβ modulation via AMPK-activating AICAR to disrupt the inflammation-metabolism cycle [63]. These findings collectively bridge molecular pathways with clinical manifestations while outlining targeted intervention approaches for PE-AKI.

  Furthermore, drug prediction analysis identified deptropine and clotrimazole as two promising candidates for PE-AKI treatment by targeting the key genes. Deptropine, a classical histamine H1 receptor antagonist used for asthma symptom relief [64], exhibited molecular docking binding energies of − 7.7 kcal/mol with IL-6, 7.2 kcal/mol with CEBPB, and − 6.4 kcal/mol with CXCL2. Binding energies ≤  − 5.0 kcal/mol suggest stable interactions between the drug and these targets. Given that GSEA enrichment analysis revealed significant involvement of IL-6, CEBPB, and CXCL2 in interferon-gamma and interferon-alpha response pathways, deptropine may attenuate PE-AKI progression by functionally inhibiting these molecules.

  Although clotrimazole is primarily known as an antifungal agent [65], it demonstrated binding energies of − 6.3 kcal/mol with IL-6, − 6.1 kcal/mol with CEBPB, and − 5.6 kcal/mol with CXCL2. The stable binding conformations suggest potential interference with IL-6-triggered inflammatory cascades, suppression of CEBPB transcriptional activity, and inhibition of the CXCL2-CXCR2 axis-mediated proinflammatory effects [66, 67]. Such multi-target engagement could reduce renal inflammatory infiltration and endothelial injury, ultimately improving renal function in PE-AKI. The drug repurposing potential of both deptropine and clotrimazole offers a novel therapeutic strategy for PE-AKI and establishes a theoretical foundation for further validation through in vitro and in vivo studies.

  Experimental validation in both LPS-induced PE-like mouse models and clinical samples from PE patients with renal injury confirmed consistent upregulation of IL-6, CXCL2, and CEBPB expression. While all three biomarkers were elevated in PE patients versus controls, only CXCL2 and CEBPB showed statistically significant increases in PE-AKI cases (p < 0.05), with IL-6 demonstrating comparable levels between PE and PE-AKI groups. This finding may be due to a type II error caused by insufficient sample size. IL-6 is a potent but highly dynamic inflammatory cytokine with considerable inter-individual variability. A larger cohort might be necessary to detect a more subtle, yet biologically relevant, incremental rise in IL-6 levels associated with the progression from PE to PE-AKI. Consequently, our data robustly establish IL-6 as a general inflammatory marker in PE but preclude definitive conclusions regarding its utility in stratifying renal injury severity within this pilot study. Importantly, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis revealed strong diagnostic performance for both CXCL2 (AUC = 0.770, 95% CI 0.55–0.91) and CEBPB (AUC = 0.790, 95% CI 0.58–0.92), establishing them as clinically relevant biomarkers for PE-associated renal injury. To our knowledge, this represents the first demonstration of CXCL2 and CEBPB as predictive molecular targets for PE-related renal complications, offering potential utility for both disease diagnosis and severity stratification. However, the promising diagnostic performance (AUC) of CXCL2 and CEBPB must be interpreted with caution. The wide confidence intervals in our ROC analysis underscore the preliminary nature of these findings and highlight the imperative for validation in larger, independent clinical cohorts before any consideration of clinical translation. Future multi-center studies with substantial sample sizes are essential to obtain more precise estimates of their diagnostic accuracy and to definitively establish their utility in disease diagnosis and severity stratification.

  Although we have achieved some results, this research still has certain limitations. First, the relatively small patient cohort may limit the statistical power of our findings, particularly regarding IL-6’s non-significant differential expression between PE and severe PE groups. Future large-scale, multi-center prospective studies are needed to validate the clinical significance of IL-6, CXCL2, and CEBPB in PE-AKI. Second, while we identified these biomarkers’ association with PE-AKI, their precise roles in immune-inflammatory pathways remain to be mechanistically elucidated through functional studies. Third, our study relies on the LPS-induced PE-like mouse model. While this model effectively induces a strong systemic inflammatory phenotype—including hypertension, proteinuria, and renal inflammation—that is highly pertinent to the immune-driven mechanisms we sought to investigate for IL-6, CXCL2, and CEBPB, it is important to acknowledge its limitations in fully recapitulating the human PE-AKI pathophysiology. The LPS model primarily triggers inflammation via Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4) activation, which may overemphasize the role of pathogen-like triggers compared to the more complex, chronic placental ischemia believed to initiate most human PE cases [68].

  In contrast, the reduced uterine perfusion pressure (RUPP) model, which induces placental ischemia, might yield a transcriptional profile with a greater emphasis on hypoxia-inducible factors (HIFs), anti-angiogenic factors (e.g., sFlt-1), and adaptive immune responses. Consequently, the relative contribution and regulatory networks of the key genes we identified might differ in a RUPP setting. For instance, the upregulation of IL-6 and CXCL2 we observed is likely a direct consequence of LPS/TLR4/NF-κB signaling, whereas in the RUPP model, their expression might be secondary to ischemic placental release of factors like sFlt-1 and TNF-α [69]. Nonetheless, the convergence of both models on a final common pathway of systemic inflammation and endothelial dysfunction strengthens the relevance of our findings [70]. The consistent upregulation of our key genes in human PE and PE-AKI blood samples further validates their clinical significance, suggesting that irrespective of the initial insult (infection/inflammation or ischemia), these inflammatory mediators are critically involved in the progression to renal injury. Future studies directly comparing transcriptomic profiles from LPS and RUPP models will be invaluable to delineate model-specific and core shared pathways in PE-AKI.

  Through an integrated bioinformatics approach, we identified three key genes (IL6, CEBPB, and CXCL2) associated with PE-AKI Comprehensive analyses—including subcellular localization, functional enrichment, molecular regulatory network, drug prediction, molecular docking, and clinical validation—established their potential involvement in PE-AKI pathogenesis. Importantly, targeting these genes might offer novel diagnostic and therapeutic opportunities for PE-AKI management.


## Conclusion
  In this study, we integrated bioinformatics analysis and experimental validation to identify IL-6, CEBPB, and Cxcl2 as key genes involved in PE-AKI. Through comprehensive analyses—including subcellular localization, co-expression networks, functional enrichment, GeneMANIA interaction networks, molecular regulatory pathways, and drug prediction with molecular docking—we elucidated their potential roles in PE-AKI pathogenesis. These findings provide novel insights into diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets for PE-AKI. However, this study has some limitations. First, further experimental validation, such as in vivo animal models, immunohistochemistry, and functional assays, is required to confirm the mechanistic roles of these genes in PE-AKI. Second, the clinical relevance of these biomarkers needs verification in larger patient cohorts. Despite these limitations, our study lays a foundation for future research on PE-AKI. We will continue to investigate these key genes and explore their translational potential in improving the diagnosis and treatment of PE-AKI. This work highlights the importance of multi-omics approaches in obstetrical research and underscores the need for deeper mechanistic studies to combat pregnancy-related kidney complications.
