# Exploring the key molecular mechanisms and immune microenvironment of oxidative stress-related pathways in pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor combining scRNA-seq and bulk RNA


## Background
  Pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor (pNET) is a heterogeneous tumor originating from pancreatic endocrine cells. Emerging evidence suggests that oxidative stress plays a crucial role in pNET pathogenesis, yet the precise molecular mechanisms and their interplay with the tumor microenvironment remain unclear. This study aims to systematically elucidate how oxidative stress-related pathways drive pNET progression through an integrated multi-omics approach.


## Methods
  We designed a three-tier analytical strategy to address interconnected scientific questions. First, to identify which oxidative stress-related genes are dysregulated in pNET, we performed differential expression analysis and weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) on the GSE73338 dataset (63 pNET samples, 5 controls), intersecting the. results with oxidative stress gene sets to obtain 71 candidate genes. Second, to understand the functional implications of these genes, we conducted GO/KEGG enrichment analysis and constructed protein-protein interaction (PPI) networks, from which we identified BCL2L1 and PHGDH as key hub genes using three independent algorithms. We then assessed their diagnostic value through ROC analysis and built a prognostic nomogram model. Third, to explore how these key genes influence the tumor microenvironment, we performed immune infiltration analysis using CIBERSORTx. Fourth, to reveal upstream regulatory mechanisms, we constructed ceRNA networks and predicted transcription factors. Fifth, to identify potential therapeutic interventions, we conducted drug prediction and molecular docking analyses. Finally, to validate our findings at cellular resolution and understand cellular heterogeneity, we analyzed single-cell RNA sequencing data from GSE256136 (20 samples), identifying cell types, quantifying cell-cell communications, and confirming key gene expression patterns across different cell populations.


## Results
  Our systematic analysis revealed that oxidative stress-related genes in pNET were significantly enriched in the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, cysteine and methionine metabolism, and HIF-1 signaling pathway. BCL2L1 and PHGDH emerged as central regulators with excellent diagnostic performance (AUC > 0.9). Immune infiltration analysis demonstrated significant alterations in activated dendritic cells, memory B cells, and resting NK cells, which correlated strongly with BCL2L1 and PHGDH expression, suggesting these genes link oxidative stress to immune dysfunction. The ceRNA network centered on KCNQ1OT1 and hsa-miR-15a-5p revealed multi-layered transcriptional and post-transcriptional regulation. Drug prediction identified sertindole and cabozantinib as promising therapeutic candidates. Single-cell analysis identified 11 cell types and confirmed that endocrine cells are the primary site of BCL2L1 and PHGDH dysregulation, with extensive crosstalk between endocrine cells and T cells potentially mediating immune evasion.


## Conclusion
  Through integrated multi-omics analysis, we established that oxidative stress pathways may drive pNET progression through a coordinated mechanism involving metabolic reprogramming (via BCL2L1 and PHGDH downregulation), immune microenvironment remodeling (through altered dendritic cell and NK cell function), and complex regulatory networks. BCL2L1 and PHGDH represent potential diagnostic biomarkers and candidate therapeutic targets that require experimental validation, providing new directions for precision medicine in pNET.


## Introduction
  Pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor (pNET) is a heterogeneous group of tumors originating from pancreatic endocrine cells. Although they account for a relatively low incidence of 1% to 2% of all pancreatic tumors, the diagnostic rate of pNET has increased in recent years due to advancements in imaging technologies and the development of early screening methods [1]. The pNET exhibits significant biological heterogeneity, which can manifest as non-invasive, slow-growing tumors or locally invasive masses. Clinically, these tumors may present as functional or non-functional tumors, with the latter being more common [2, 3]. Although pNET tends to grow slowly, once metastasis occurs, particularly to the liver, the prognosis is usually poor, with the median survival time ranging from 2 to 5 years [4]. Currently, surgery remains the most effective treatment for pNET, but due to the non-specific early symptoms and the high heterogeneity of the disease, many patients are diagnosed at advanced stages, often with widespread metastasis, which limits the effectiveness of treatment [5, 6]. Therefore, a deeper understanding of the mechanisms underlying the occurrence and metastasis of pNET is crucial for improving early diagnosis rates and therapeutic outcomes.

  Oxidative stress refers to the excessive production of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and reactive nitrogen species (RNS) within cells, exceeding the capacity of the antioxidant system to neutralize them, leading to oxidative damage to macromolecules such as lipids, proteins, and DNA [7]. Chronic oxidative stress not only causes cellular dysfunction and inflammatory responses but can also promote tumorigenesis and tumor progression through mechanisms such as gene mutations, uncontrolled cell proliferation, and inhibition of apoptosis [8, 9]. In recent years, numerous studies have indicated a close relationship between oxidative stress pathways and the occurrence and development of pNET. It has been demonstrated that oxidative stress plays a key role not only in pancreatic cancer but also in significant alterations in pNET [10]. Specifically, oxidative stress contributes to the early occurrence and metastasis of pNET by promoting DNA damage, activating carcinogenic signaling pathways (such as NF-κB signaling pathways, MAPK signaling pathways, etc.), and altering cellular metabolism [11–13]. Additionally, oxidative stress can modulate immune cells, endothelial cells, and other components within the tumor microenvironment, influencing the immune evasion mechanisms of pNET [14, 15]. Recently, emerging evidence has demonstrated that exosomes and long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) play crucial regulatory roles in oxidative stress-mediated tumorigenesis and immune modulation. Exosomes, as important mediators of intercellular communication, can transfer oxidative stress-related molecules and regulatory RNAs between tumor cells and immune cells. In pancreatic tumors, tumor-derived exosomes have been shown to induce immunosuppression by triggering apoptosis in T lymphocytes through activation of the p38 MAPK-mediated endoplasmic reticulum stress pathway, thereby reshaping the tumor microenvironment and influencing therapeutic responses [16]. Furthermore, lncRNAs have been shown to regulate oxidative stress pathways and tumor-immune interactions through complex regulatory networks. Studies have demonstrated that oxidative stress-related lncRNAs can alter the tumor microenvironment by modulating immune cell infiltration, affecting pancreatic cancer progression and providing potential prognostic biomarkers [17]. Additionally, lncRNAs participate in tumor-stroma crosstalk and reprogramming of the pancreatic tumors immune microenvironment, influencing immune escape mechanisms and offering novel insights for pancreatic tumors diagnosis and immunotherapy strategies [18]. These findings underscore the importance of investigating the intricate interplay between oxidative stress, immune regulation, and molecular networks in pNET. Therefore, continued exploration of the role of oxidative stress in pNET will not only deepen our understanding of tumorigenesis but also provide potential therapeutic strategies for clinical use.

  The heterogeneity of pNET cells is reflected not only at the molecular level in terms of gene expression profiles but also in the metabolic characteristics, proliferative capacity, and responses to treatment. Studies have shown that the heterogeneity of pNET directly affects their prognosis and therapeutic efficacy [19, 20]. This makes traditional single-target therapies often ineffective, highlighting the importance of precision medicine in the treatment of this disease. On the other hand, the immune microenvironment of pNET plays a critical role in its progression. Research has shown that the immune microenvironment of pNET typically exhibits immunosuppressive characteristics, such as reduced infiltration of CD8+ T cells and increased infiltration of macrophages. These immune cells promote tumorigenesis and progression through various mechanisms, including cytokine secretion, modulation of tumor cell proliferation and apoptosis, and facilitating tumor cell migration and metastasis [21]. Therefore, studying the types of immune cells in the tumor microenvironment and their mechanisms of action not only helps to reveal the tumor’s immune evasion strategies but also provides a theoretical basis for the application of immunotherapy.

  With the rapid development of high-throughput sequencing technologies, especially the widespread use of single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) [22, 23], research into the molecular mechanisms of pNET has entered a new era. The scRNA-seq enables high-resolution analysis of the heterogeneity of both tumor cells and immune cells, helping researchers explore the interactions between different cell types in the tumor microenvironment and their roles in disease progression. For instance, based on single-cell RNA-seq data, researchers have found that tumor cell populations in pNETs exhibit diverse transcriptional features, and the immune cells in the tumor microenvironment show significant subgroup differentiation [24]. This technology provides a powerful tool for the precise diagnosis, personalized treatment, and exploration of immunotherapy in pNET. Moreover, integrating multi-omics data (such as transcriptomics, scRNA-seq, and proteomics) can comprehensively analyze the complexity of pNET from multiple perspectives [25]. This approach not only aids in the discovery of potential biomarkers but also reveals possible therapeutic targets for the tumor. It provides a solid foundation for developing personalized treatment plans and advancing precision medicine in the treatment of pNET.

  To systematically investigate oxidative stress mechanisms in pNET, we designed a hierarchical multi-omics framework addressing four sequential questions through progressively refined analyses: Our initial focus was identifying which oxidative stress-related genes are dysregulated. We analyzed bulk RNA-seq data (GSE73338) using differential expression analysis and WGCNA to identify candidate genes, followed by GO/KEGG enrichment to reveal their biological functions. Building upon this gene catalog, we then determined which genes serve as central regulators with clinical utility. We applied PPI network topology analysis to identify hub genes, then validated their diagnostic performance and prognostic value. With key genes identified, our investigation expanded to understand how they interact with the microenvironment. We performed immune infiltration analysis (CIBERSORTx) to reveal correlations with immune cells, constructed ceRNA networks and predicted transcription factors to elucidate regulatory mechanisms, and conducted drug prediction to identify therapeutic compounds. To validate these findings at cellular resolution, we analyzed single-cell RNA-seq data (GSE256136), mapping key gene expression across cell types, characterizing cellular heterogeneity, and revealing cell-cell communications.


## Methods

### Data download and preprocessing
  The transcriptomic dataset GSE73338 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE73338), related to pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor (pNET), was downloaded from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geoprofiles/). This dataset, based on the GPL20945 platform, includes transcriptomic data from 63 pNET patient pancreatic tissue samples as pNET group and 5 normal control (NC) samples as NC group. The data was preprocessed, including background correction, gene name conversion, and data normalization.

  The single-cell RNA sequencing dataset GSE256136 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi? acc=GSE256136) was also obtained from GEO. This dataset, based on the GPL24676 platform, contains 20 samples: 3 normal control samples (GSM8086245, GSM8086246, GSM8086247), 15 pNET core tissue samples (GSM8086248 ~ GSM8086262), and 2 liver metastatic samples (GSM8086263, GSM8086264). The preprocessing of single-cell data was performed using 10x Genomics Cell Ranger software for alignment, quantification, and cell identification.

  Additionally, to explore the involvement of oxidative stress in the molecular mechanisms of pNET, oxidative stress-related genes with a relevance score > 7 were retrieved from the GeneCards database (https://www.genecards.org), totaling 2054 genes.


### Differential expression analysis of transcriptomic data
  Using the GSE73338 dataset, differential expression analysis was performed with the edgeR package, setting a |log2 fold change (FC)| > 1 and false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.01 as thresholds to identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between pNET and NC groups. Volcano plot and heatmap were generated for visualizing DEGs.


### Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA)
  To identify co-expressed gene modules and hub genes associated with pNET, WGCNA was performed on the GSE73338 training dataset. The soft threshold power (β) was determined using the pickSoftThreshold function based on the scale-free fit index and average connectivity. The optimal soft threshold was used to construct an adjacency matrix, which was then transformed into a topological overlap matrix (TOM) to measure the similarity of gene co-expression. Hierarchical clustering was performed on the TOM-based dissimilarity matrix, and gene modules were identified using the dynamic tree cutting algorithm. The minimum module size was set to 30, the deep split was set to 3 and the cut height was set to 0.25. Module eigengenes (MEs) were calculated as the first principal component for each module, representing the overall expression profile. Pearson correlation analysis was used to associate MEs with clinical features (pNET group vs. NC group). Modules significantly correlated with pNET (P < 0.05) were prioritized for further investigation. Genes with high gene significance (GS) and module membership (MM) values (|GS| > 0.6 and |MM| > 0.6) were considered hub genes. The DEGs, hub genes identified by WGCNA, and oxidative stress-related genes were compared to identify candidate genes associated with oxidative stress and pNET development for further functional analysis and validation.


### Enrichment analysis
  To explore the biological functions associated with oxidative stress and pNET development, we performed functional enrichment analysis of the candidate genes. DAVID database (https://david.ncifcrf.gov) was used for Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis. The GO annotations included biological processes (BPs), cellular components (CCs), and molecular functions (MFs). Fisher’s exact test was applied to identify significantly enriched KEGG pathways and GO terms (P < 0.05), revealing the biological functions and pathways involved in pNET. Additionally, gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was conducted using the “gsea” package in R software to identify the major signaling pathways that differ between pNET and NC groups.


### Protein-protein interaction (PPI) network analysis, key gene screening, and diagnostic value
  The STRING database (https://cn.string-db.org/) was used to perform PPI analysis for the identified candidate genes. After constructing the PPI network, Cytoscape software (version 3.8.0) was used for network visualization and topological analysis. To further identify key nodes in the PPI network, we utilized Cytoscape’s CytoHubba plugin, which offers various node importance evaluation algorithms, such as Degree, Maximal Clique Centrality (MCC), Maximum Neighborhood Component (MNC), and ClusteringCoefficient. We applied MCC, MNC, and ClusteringCoefficient algorithms and selected the top 20 genes from each algorithm. The intersection of these genes was extracted to obtain key genes.

  To validate the expression of key genes, the Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used to assess the differences in expression between the pNET and NC groups. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were generated to evaluate the ability of key genes to distinguish between the two groups.

  Finally, a risk prediction model (nomogram) based on key gene expression levels was constructed. The model’s accuracy and clinical application value were assessed using ROC curves and Decision Curve Analysis (DCA).


### Immune infiltration analysis
  CIBERSORTx, a machine learning algorithm based on gene expression profiles, is used to estimate the relative abundance of 22 immune cell subtypes in each pNET and control tissue sample. The immune cell types and their relative proportions were analyzed using the CIBERSORTx database (https://cibersortx.stanford.edu/). Wilcoxon rank-sum tests were performed to compare immune cell infiltration differences between pNET and NC groups. Pearson correlation analysis was used to assess the relationship between immune cells as well as between immune cells and key genes.


### Construction of the competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) network and prediction of transcription factors (TFs)
  To further explore the upstream regulatory mechanisms of key genes, we constructed a ceRNA regulatory network. First, we predicted the miRNAs interacting with key genes using the miRWalk database (http://mirwalk.umm.uni-heidelberg.de/). Then, we identified the upstream regulatory lncRNAs of these miRNAs through the starBase database (http://starbase.sysu.edu.cn/). Based on the predicted miRNA-mRNA and lncRNA-miRNA interactions, we constructed the ceRNA regulatory network. The ceRNA network was visualized using Cytoscape software, which intuitively revealed the potential regulatory mechanisms of key genes.

  In addition, to explore the TFs involved in the regulation of key gene expression, we uploaded the key genes to the miRNet database (https://www.mirnet.ca/upload/GeneUploadView.xhtml) and performed TF prediction using the JASPAR database. Cytoscape software was used to visualize the TF-key gene regulatory network, providing an in-depth analysis of the transcriptional regulatory mechanisms.


### Drug prediction and molecular Docking analysis
  The Connectivity Map (CMap) database (https://clue.io) is a platform that can predict potential small molecules capable of reversing or inducing gene expression associated with specific biological states. In this study, we uploaded lists of upregulated and downregulated key genes to the CMap platform for gene set enrichment analysis. The results were ranked based on connectivity scores, where small molecules with negative enrichment scores indicate the potential to reverse the expression direction of key genes, which were considered as candidate small molecules.

  To validate the reliability of the small molecule drug predictions and explore the biological mechanisms by which these candidate drugs may improve tumor, we used AutoDock Vina software to analyze the interaction strength and modes between small molecules and key targets. Specifically, we first downloaded the 3D structures of the candidate small molecules from the PubChem database and preprocessed the small molecules and target proteins using AutoDockTools (version 1.5.7), including adding hydrogens and calculating Gasteiger charges. Then, based on the structure of the target protein, docking regions were defined, and docking parameters were set. Molecular docking was performed using AutoDock Vina (version 1.2.0) to calculate the binding free energy between each small molecule and target protein, and the binding modes, including hydrogen bonds and hydrophobic interactions, were analyzed. The binding affinity values were expressed as binding free energy in kcal/mol, with more negative values indicating stronger binding affinity. A binding energy threshold of < −7.0 kcal/mol was applied to identify potentially favorable drug-target interactions, as this cutoff is widely accepted in computational drug screening studies to indicate moderate to strong binding affinity. For each key target, the “ligand-receptor” complex with the highest binding energy was selected for visualization to intuitively display the interaction between the small molecule drug and the target protein, providing more information for understanding the mechanism of action.


### Cell heterogeneity analysis and validation of key genes
  In this study, the Seurat software package (version 5.1.0) was used to analyze the quality-controlled single-cell sequencing data to investigate cell heterogeneity in pNET and validate key genes at the single-cell level. The GSE256136 dataset was downloaded from the GEO database in the form of processed count matrices aligned to the human reference genome GRCh38 (hg38), comprising 20 samples from pNET and normal control (NC) groups. To account for batch effects across samples, Seurat’s reciprocal PCA (RPCA) integration method was applied using FindIntegrationAnchors and IntegrateData functions with default parameters (dims = 1:30). Quality control retained high-quality cells with nFeature_RNA > 200, nCount_RNA < 50,000, and percent.mt < 40%. The 40% mitochondrial threshold was selected based on the high metabolic activity characteristic of pancreatic neuroendocrine cells, validated by examining mitochondrial percentage distributions to ensure biologically relevant populations were retained.

  Gene expression normalization was performed using the “LogNormalize” method, followed by identification of highly variable genes (HVGs) and data scaling via the “ScaleData” function. Principal component analysis (PCA) was conducted, and the PCs were selected based on elbow plot analysis showing diminishing variance returns beyond this point, further validated by examining gene loadings for biological relevance. Clustering was performed using “FindNeighbors” and “FindClusters” functions with the shared nearest neighbor (SNN) graph and Louvain algorithm at a resolution of 0.8, selected after testing values from 0.4 to 1.2 to balance cell type distinction and avoid over-fragmentation. Cell type annotation was performed using known marker genes, and “RunUMAP” was applied for dimensionality reduction visualization.

  Cell proportion analysis was conducted to compare the relative abundance of each cell type between pNET and NC groups. Cell-cell communication analysis was performed using the “CellChat” tool to construct interaction networks, calculate interaction frequencies and strengths, and identify key cell types involved in intercellular communication patterns.

  Based on the cell heterogeneity analysis, we further validated the expression patterns of key genes within the single-cell dataset. UMAP clustering plots and violin plots were used to show the spatial expression distribution and expression levels of these genes across all single-cell samples and within different cell types. Additionally, we compared the overall distribution and expression differences of the key genes between the pNET and NC groups.


## Results

### Differential expression analysis of transcriptome data
  Differential expression analysis of transcriptomic data was performed based on the criteria of FDR < 0.01 and |log2FC| > 1. The results showed that compared to the NC group, a total of 1965 DEGs were significantly differentially expressed in the pNET group, including 1453 upregulated genes and 512 downregulated genes (Fig. 1A, B).

### WGCNA
  The pickSoftThreshold function was used to test different soft threshold values (β), and the optimal soft threshold (β = 7) was selected to construct an adjacency matrix (Fig. 2A). The matrix was then converted into a topological overlap matrix (TOM) to reflect the gene co-expression similarity. Based on hierarchical clustering and dynamic tree cutting algorithms, a total of 32 gene modules were identified (Fig. 2B). The connectivity of MEs was then analyzed, showing that the distances between modules were greater than 0.25 (Fig. 2C), indicating good independence between each module. The correlation between the MEs and clinical features (pNET group vs. NC group) was calculated, and three modules—black, turquoise, and blue—were found to be significantly correlated with the pNET group (Fig. 2D). A total of 640 hub genes were identified from these modules (|MM| > 0.6, |GS| > 0.6). By intersecting DEGs, hub genes from WGCNA, and oxidative stress-related gene sets from databases, we identified 71 common genes (Fig. 2E). These genes are believed to be closely related to the development of oxidative stress and the occurrence of pNET.

### PPI network and identification of key gene
  To further investigate the potential regulatory interactions of key genes at the protein level, we performed PPI network analysis on the candidate genes using the STRING database (https://cn.string-db.org/). The PPI network contained 71 nodes and 627 edges, indicating strong regulatory interactions among these genes, suggesting that they may play a synergistic role in the progression of pNET (Fig. 4). Moreover, using the built-in algorithms in Cytoscape software (MCC, MNC, and ClusteringCoefficient), we ranked the top 20 genes from each algorithm (Fig. 5A). We then intersected the top 20 genes from each algorithm, identifying two key genes: BCL2L1 and PHGDH (Fig. 5B). Notably, these two genes exhibited higher connectivity and centrality within the PPI network, indicating that they serve as hub nodes in the protein interaction network and play a crucial role in maintaining network stability and function. Furthermore, compared to the NC group, both genes were significantly downregulated in the pNET group (P < 0.05) (Fig. 5C). ROC curve analysis revealed that BCL2L1 and PHGDH both had an area under the curve (AUC) greater than 0.9, suggesting excellent diagnostic ability for distinguishing between the NC and pNET groups (Fig. 5D).

  Additionally, a nomogram risk prediction model was constructed based on these two key genes to evaluate the risk of developing pNET. The results showed that the low expression of both genes was associated with an increased risk of pNET, with PHGDH contributing most significantly to the model (Fig. 6A). The model’s accuracy was further assessed using ROC curve analysis and DCA. ROC curve analysis showed that the model exhibited high sensitivity and specificity, with an AUC value of 0.997 (Fig. 6B). DCA results indicated that the net benefit of using this model for prediction was superior to both the extreme cases of predicting all patients or no patients, confirming its potential clinical application value (Fig. 6C).

### Immune infiltration analysis
  The CIBERSORT method was employed to investigate the immune cell infiltration in samples from the NC group and pNET group. A total of 22 immune cell types were identified in both NC and pNET samples. The bar chart (Fig. 7A) shows the types and quantities of immune cells infiltrating each sample. Subsequently, the correlation between these immune cell populations was assessed, revealing the strongest negative correlation between M0 macrophages and M2 macrophages (r = −0.65, P < 0.001), and the strongest positive correlation between resting dendritic cells and M1 macrophages (r = 0.41, P < 0.05) (Fig. 7B).

  Next, we compared the infiltration differences of each immune cell subgroup between the NC and pNET groups. The results showed that the infiltration of T cells gamma and resting dendritic cells were significantly upregulated in pNET samples (P < 0.05), while the infiltration of memory B cells and resting NK cells was significantly downregulated in pNET samples (P < 0.05) (Fig. 7C). Furthermore, we further explored the correlation between the expression of two key genes and the infiltration of each immune cell subgroup. The results showed that the expression of these two key genes was significantly correlated with the infiltration levels of naive B cells, memory B cells, CD4 naive T cells, regulatory T cells (Tregs), resting NK cells, activated NK cells, M1 macrophages, and activated dendritic cells (P < 0.05). Specifically, PHGDH and BCL2L1 were both significantly positively correlated with resting NK cells and M1 macrophages (P < 0.05), while significantly negatively correlated with activated dendritic cells and activated NK cells (P < 0.05). Only PHGDH was significantly positively correlated with memory B cells and regulatory T cells (Tregs) (P < 0.05), while significantly negatively correlated with naive B cells and CD4 naive T cells (P < 0.05) (Fig. 7D).

  In summary, dendritic cells activated, memory B cells, and resting NK cells not only showed significant differences between the pNET and NC groups, but also had significant correlations with the expression of key genes. These findings suggest that key genes may play an important role in the development and progression of pNET by regulating immune cells such as activated dendritic cells, memory B cells, and resting NK cells, though causal relationships require experimental validation.

### CeRNA network construction and TF prediction
  The ceRNA network consists of 268 nodes (2 key mRNAs, 60 microRNAs (miRNAs), and 206 long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs)) and 2206 interaction relationships. We calculated the network topology parameters of the ceRNA network, with KCNQ1OT1 and NEAT1 being the lncRNAs with the highest degree values in the ceRNA network topology. The hsa-miR-15a-5p, hsa-miR-16-5p, and hsa-miR-15b-5p were the miRNAs with the highest degree values in the network. PHGDH and BCL2L1 were the mRNAs with the highest degree values, and there was mutual regulatory interaction between them (Fig. 8A), suggesting that these ceRNA networks may play a role in the pathogenesis of pNET. Furthermore, we performed TF analysis on these two key genes and found that TFs such as TAF7, SSRP1, KLF9, CREB3L1, CHD1, ZFP2, and TFAP4 interact with the key genes PHGDH and BCL2L1 (Fig. 8B).

### Drug prediction
  Based on the CMap database, we identified 10 small molecules that best reverse the transcriptional changes associated with pNET, including roflumilast, cabozantinib, gossypol, rhodomyrtoxin-b, erlotinib, dacarbazine, bromocriptine, suloctidil, sertindole, and oxiperomide. These drugs may potentially improve disease progression.

  To verify the reliability of the drug targets, molecular docking simulations were performed to predict the binding ability of the candidate drugs with the proteins encoded by the key genes. Using BCL2L1 and PHGDH as target proteins, docking analyses were conducted for the candidate drugs. The molecular docking results revealed that most candidate drugs exhibited moderate to strong binding affinity to at least one target protein (Table 1). These results suggest that these small molecules, especially sertindole and cabozantinib, showing potential binding affinity that requires experimental validation.

### Validation of key gene expression at the cellular level
  To further validate the expression patterns of two key genes based on single-cell sequencing data, UMAP clustering plots were used to display the expression distribution of BCL2L1 and PHGDH in all single-cell samples (Fig. 13A), followed by quantitative analysis using violin plots. The results show that BCL2L1 and PHGDH were expressed in endocrine cells, fibroblasts, ductal cells, and acinar cells (Fig. 13B). We compared the expression differences of these key genes at the overall cell level between the NC and pNET groups, and the results revealed that the expression levels of BCL2L1 and PHGDH were significantly lower in the pNET group than in the NC group (P < 0.05), consistent with the trend observed in our training dataset (GSE73338) (Fig. 13C, D).

  Furthermore, we explored the expression differences of BCL2L1 and PHGDH across different cell types. The results (Fig. 14) showed significant differences in the expression patterns of these two key genes in various cell types. Specifically, the expression of both genes in acinar cell, ductal cell, and plasma cell was differentially downregulated in the pNET group (P < 0.05). Furthermore, the expression of PHGDH was differentially significantly upregulated in the NC group in both macrophage and endothelial cell (P < 0.05). In conclusion, this study provides further validation of the expression characteristics of BCL2L1 and PHGDH in pNET at the single-cell level, suggesting that these genes may play a key role in specific cell types and provide new directions for subsequent functional studies and targeted therapies.

## Discussion
  pNET is a rare neoplasm originating from pancreatic endocrine cells, characterized by high heterogeneity [2]. In recent years, with the deepening understanding of pNET biological behavior, increasing attention has been focused on the crucial role of oxidative stress in pNET development. Studies have shown that oxidative stress participates in tumor development by affecting multiple biological processes including cellular metabolism, DNA damage repair, and inflammatory responses, though its specific molecular mechanisms in pNET remain unclear [8, 24]. Therefore, investigating the molecular regulatory networks and immune microenvironment of oxidative stress in pNET is vital for elucidating pathogenesis and identifying potential diagnostic markers and therapeutic targets.

  Based on differential expression analysis and WGCNA, we identified 71 candidate genes mainly enriched in PI3K-Akt and HIF-1 pathways, with two key genes (BCL2L1 and PHGDH) screened through PPI network analysis. Immune infiltration analysis revealed the role of immune cells in the pNET microenvironment. Additionally, we constructed ceRNA and transcription factor regulatory networks and predicted potential drugs including sertindole and cabozantinib. Single-cell analysis identified 11 cell types and validated the expression patterns of key genes. Overall, this study reveals the role of oxidative stress in pNET, providing clues for subsequent treatment.

  Our findings showed significant enrichment in PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, cysteine and methionine metabolism, and HIF-1 signaling pathway. Studies have found that abnormal activation of PI3K/Akt pathway can promote cancer invasion and metastasis through mechanisms involving upregulation of MMP-2 expression and promotion of EMT process [26, 27]. The cysteine and methionine metabolism pathway is closely related to tumor redox homeostasis, and its dysregulation can alter oxidative stress levels, affecting cancer progression and serving as druggable targets [28, 29]. The HIF-1 signaling pathway, abnormally activated in hypoxic microenvironments, mediates pNET malignant progression by regulating metabolic reprogramming and angiogenesis [30, 31]. These findings validate and expand the relationship between these pathways and pNET progression, providing important insights into the molecular mechanisms of oxidative stress in pNET.

  BCL2L1 and PHGDH were identified as key genes in this study. BCL2L1, a member of the BCL2 family of apoptosis regulatory proteins, maintains mitochondrial homeostasis and inhibits cell apoptosis [32], with its family members mediating tumor chemoresistance through Caspases inhibition [33]. PHGDH, the rate-limiting enzyme in serine biosynthesis, drives tumor metabolic reprogramming and is upregulated in various cancers [34], though low PHGDH expression paradoxically promotes metastasis in some contexts [35]. We found both genes were significantly downregulated in pNET tissues and demonstrated excellent diagnostic and prognostic performance (AUC > 0.9), indicating their potential as clinical biomarkers.

  The significance of these genes lies in their central roles in oxidative stress management. Based on established literature and our expression data, we hypothesize that BCL2L1 downregulation disrupts mitochondrial ROS homeostasis in pNET. To survive this oxidative stress, pNET cells activate compensatory mechanisms including PI3K-Akt signaling and glycolytic reprogramming, which may explain the chemoresistance observed in BCL2 family studies [33].

  PHGDH directly supports antioxidant defenses through serine biosynthesis, which provides: (1) cysteine for glutathione synthesis—the primary cellular antioxidant, and (2) NADPH via one-carbon metabolism—the reducing power for antioxidant enzyme systems. PHGDH downregulation compromises these defenses, forcing cells to scavenge extracellular serine and activate alternative pathways that may enhance metastatic potential [35].

  The co-downregulation of both genes creates a unique vulnerability: pNET cells simultaneously lose mitochondrial ROS buffering (BCL2L1) and antioxidant biosynthesis (PHGDH), existing in a precarious redox state dependent on residual compensatory mechanisms. This “double-hit” to oxidative stress defenses represents a therapeutic opportunity where oxidative stress-inducing agents could selectively eliminate pNET cells while sparing normal tissues. In conclusion, BCL2L1 and PHGDH serve as both biomarkers reflecting oxidative stress adaptation in pNET and potential therapeutic targets exploiting this metabolic vulnerability.

  The immune microenvironment is closely related to tumor progression. We found significant enrichment of immune cell subsets including activated dendritic cells, memory B cells, and resting NK cells in the pNET microenvironment, which showed significant correlation with key genes. Dendritic cells are important antigen-presenting cells that can induce effector T cell responses and exert anti-tumor immune effects when activated [36]. The upregulation of dendritic cells in pNET samples observed in our study is consistent with literature reports, indicating their activation and anti-tumor role during disease progression. Memory B cells can secrete antibodies to participate in humoral immune responses [37], but their infiltration level’s relationship with pNET prognosis remains unclear and warrants further investigation. Resting NK cells are important anti-tumor effector cells, but their killing function is often suppressed in the tumor microenvironment [38]. We found that BCL2L1 and PHGDH showed significant positive correlation with resting NK cells but negative correlation with activated dendritic cells. Combined with the trend of low expression of key genes in pNET, it is speculated that the downregulation of their expression may participate in the immune response and disease process by inhibiting the resting NK cells and enhancing the antigen presentation function of activated dendritic cells. This study reveals the immune composition characteristics of the pNET microenvironment at both global and local levels, providing theoretical basis and potential markers for subsequent immunotherapy.

  ceRNA and transcriptional regulatory networks are important mechanisms of gene expression regulation. We constructed a lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA network centered on KCNQ1OT1 and NEAT1. Studies have shown that both KCNQ1OT1 and NEAT1 have dual oncogenic or tumor-suppressive roles and can regulate downstream gene expression by competitively binding multiple miRNAs in various tumors including pancreatic cancer, colorectal cancer, and non-small cell lung cancer [39–41]. Additionally, hsa-miR-15a-5p and hsa-mir-15b-5p are important cancer-related miRNAs that can target and inhibit the expression of multiple oncogenes, exerting either suppressive or promoting effects on tumor proliferation and invasion [42–44]. These lncRNAs and miRNAs likely regulate BCL2L1 and PHGDH expression through competitive binding, thereby affecting pNET progression. TFs are key regulators of gene transcription. We predicted several TFs including TAF7 and SSRP1 that may regulate key gene expression. TAF7 can recruit RNA polymerase II to promote transcription of cell cycle-related genes [45]. SSRP1 is an important component of the FACT complex, playing key roles in gene transcription, DNA replication, and chromatin remodeling [46]. These TFs may directly regulate transcription by binding to BCL2L1 and PHGDH promoter regions. Overall, the construction of ceRNA and transcriptional regulatory networks reveals the complexity of key gene expression regulation, providing new insights into their mechanisms in pNET progression.

  Targeting key genes and their related pathways is an important strategy for tumor treatment. Through CMap database prediction analysis, we found that small molecule drugs including sertindole and cabozantinib may exert anti-pNET effects by reversing key gene expression changes. Molecular docking analysis further validated their high-affinity binding with BCL2L1 and PHGDH. Sertindole, an atypical antipsychotic drug, has both dopamine receptor antagonist activity and tumor cell apoptosis-inducing properties [47]. Cabozantinib is a multi-target tyrosine kinase inhibitor that can inhibit VEGFR2, MET, AXL and other signaling pathways, and has been applied in the treatment of solid tumors such as thyroid cancer and neuroendocrine tumor [48, 49]. These drugs likely exert anti-pNET effects by directly binding to BCL2L1 and PHGDH and inhibiting their downstream signaling pathways. Moreover, given the close relationship between key gene-involved pathways like PI3K/Akt and HIF-1 with pNET development, combining inhibitors targeting these pathways may provide new strategies for pNET treatment. In summary, this study provides important clues for subsequent drug development targeting key genes and their related pathways.

  Tumor heterogeneity is a key factor affecting diagnosis and treatment outcomes. Through single-cell sequencing, we revealed the cellular compositional heterogeneity of pNET and identified 11 major cell types. Among them, endocrine cells showed significantly increased proportion in pNET samples, suggesting their potential as the cells of origin. Previous studies have shown that pNET arises from malignant transformation of pancreatic endocrine cells, whose hormone secretion can cause various clinical symptoms [50]. Additionally, endocrine cells exhibit extensive signal communication with various cell types, playing a central regulatory role in the pNET microenvironment [51]. T cells, as important immune cells, showed enhanced signal communication with endocrine cells, suggesting potential immune responses between them. Studies have shown that T cell infiltration levels in pNET tissues correlate with prognosis [52].but the proportion of functional T cells decreases with suppressed immune function. We speculate that endocrine cells may suppress T cell anti-tumor immunity through mechanisms such as secreting immunosuppressive factors, though specific mechanisms require further exploration. In summary, the interaction between endocrine cells and immune cells like T cells likely affects pNET malignant progression, and in-depth analysis of their signal communication mechanisms will help elucidate pNET’s immune regulatory mechanisms.

  Integrative analysis of multi-omics data is an effective strategy for comprehensively analyzing key gene functions. This study validated the expression patterns of BCL2L1 and PHGDH at both global and local levels, finding consistent expression changes at transcriptomic and single-cell levels, with significant differences among cell types. Previous studies have shown that BCL2L1 and PHGDH are expressed in pancreatic exocrine cells (acinar and ductal cells) and endocrine cells [53], which is consistent with the findings of our study. Additionally, we found BCL2L1 upregulation in fibroblasts and smooth muscle cells, while PHGDH was downregulated in macrophages. These results suggest that BCL2L1 and PHGDH may play important roles in tumor microenvironment remodeling by regulating tumor stromal cell activity. Overall, multi-omics data integration analysis further corroborates the key roles of BCL2L1 and PHGDH in pNET development and reveals new mechanisms of their regulation of pNET progression from a spatial resolution perspective.

  In conclusion, this study employed multi-omics data integration analysis strategy to reveal the important role of oxidative stress pathways in pNET development at both transcriptomic and single-cell levels. On one hand, we identified BCL2L1 and PHGDH as potential key genes for pNET diagnosis and prognosis, constructing a risk assessment model with good predictive performance that can guide pNET diagnosis and prognostic evaluation. On the other hand, we revealed the cellular composition and immune landscape characteristics of the pNET microenvironment, discovering the importance of regulatory networks between key genes, immune cells and endocrine cells in pNET progression, providing new insights for subsequent mechanism analysis and immunotherapy. Additionally, we predicted small molecule drugs including sertindole and cabozantinib that may have therapeutic potential for pNET, providing important clues for subsequent drug development.

  Nevertheless, this study has several important limitations that should be acknowledged. First, our findings are based entirely on computational analyses of publicly available datasets without wet-lab experimental validation. While we employed rigorous bioinformatics approaches and validated our results across independent datasets at both bulk and single-cell resolution, the functional roles of BCL2L1 and PHGDH in pNET pathogenesis require experimental confirmation. However, it is important to contextualize this limitation within the unique challenges of rare tumor research. pNET accounts for only 1–2% of all pancreatic tumors, making large-scale prospective studies and comprehensive experimental validation logistically challenging, time-intensive, and resource-prohibitive. Fresh surgical specimens with matched normal pancreatic tissue are particularly difficult to obtain due to the tumor’s rarity, anatomical location, and surgical considerations. In this context, our integrative bioinformatics analysis of publicly available datasets provides a cost-effective and time-efficient strategy to: (1) identify potential biomarkers and therapeutic targets from existing data, (2) prioritize candidates for subsequent focused experimental validation based on rigorous computational evidence, (3) generate mechanistic hypotheses that can guide experimental design, and (4) reveal systems-level patterns that may be missed in reductionist experimental approaches. The cross-validation of our findings across two independent datasets—bulk RNA-seq (GSE73338) and single-cell RNA-seq (GSE256136)—representing different patient cohorts, sequencing platforms, and analytical resolutions, substantially strengthens confidence in the robustness of identified patterns. Furthermore, our findings align with established biological knowledge of BCL2L1’s role in mitochondrial homeostasis and PHGDH’s function in serine biosynthesis and antioxidant defense, providing additional biological plausibility.

  Second, due to the limited sample size and sequencing depth of available single-cell datasets, some low-abundance immune cell subsets may have been overlooked, potentially limiting our comprehensive characterization of immune microenvironment dynamics during pNET progression. Third, the ceRNA network and transcription factor predictions require experimental validation through techniques such as RNA immunoprecipitation, luciferase reporter assays, and chromatin immunoprecipitation to confirm direct regulatory interactions. Fourth, the molecular docking predictions, while providing structural insights into drug-target interactions, require biochemical binding assays and cellular functional studies to validate binding affinities and therapeutic potential. In summary, pNET development involves complex regulatory networks of multiple factors, and elucidating its molecular mechanisms requires deep integration of multi-omics data and interdisciplinary research. This study broadens research perspectives in the pNET field, but more follow-up work is needed to validate results and apply them to clinical practice.
