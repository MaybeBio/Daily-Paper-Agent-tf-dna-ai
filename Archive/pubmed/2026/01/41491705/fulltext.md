# Yangjingzhongyu Decoction regulates primordial follicle initiation via LncRNA-Smad1/AMPK dual pathways in diminished ovarian reserve


## Background
  Targeting the dysregulated initiation of primordial follicles represents a potential therapeutic breakthrough in managing pathological diminished ovarian reserve (DOR). Previous research has demonstrated that Yangjingzhongyu Decoction (YJZYD) promotes the transition from primordial to primary follicles by post-transcriptionally regulating Smad1 protein via the ovary-specific long non-coding RNA (LncRNA) LTCONS-00011173. This effect may be further supported by YJZYD’s ability to ameliorate oxidative stress in the follicular developmental microenvironment through modulation of the AMPK pathway. This study aims to integrate these two mechanistic axes to elucidate the holistic mechanism through which YJZYD modulates the primordial follicle microenvironment.


## Objective
  To investigate YJZYD’s regulatory mechanism on the primordial follicle initiating microenvironment in DOR rats.


## Results
  In DOR model rats, primordial and growing follicles significantly decreased (p < 0.05), accompanied by reduced E2 and AMH levels (p < 0.05) and elevated FSH (p < 0.05). Treatment with YJZYD-medicated serum increased counts of primordial/growing follicle counts (p < 0.05), with no significant difference between the high-dose group and the control group (p > 0.05). Additionally, YJZYD reduced FSH, increased E2 and AMH (p < 0.05). It also downregulated LTCONS-00011173 and upregulated Smad1 (p < 0.05). Furthermore, YJZYD elevated SOD activity and ATP content (p < 0.05) and reduced MDA levels (p < 0.05). Moreover, it suppressed PTEN expression, dose-dependently regulated MST and AMH (p < 0.05), and enhanced GDF9 expression at low dose (p < 0.05). Molecular docking identified Apigenin and Curcumin as key monomers targeting AMPK/SIRT1/MAPK pathway. Experimental validation of Apigenin replicated YJZYD’s effects on follicle numbers and hormone levels (p < 0.01).


## Conclusion
  YJZYD alleviates primordial follicle initiation inhibition by targeting the LTCONS-00011173/Smad1 axis, while concurrently improving the follicular microenvironment through AMPK/SIRT1/PGC-1α mediated oxidative stress repair and energy metabolism enhancement. This dual-pathway mechanism restores follicular factor balance and ovarian reserve function in DOR.

## Introduction
  China’s birth rate declined to 6.77 per thousand in recent years, largely due to delayed childbearing among younger generations, which shortens reproductive windows and increases infertility risks. Globally, 10%-15% of couples experience infertility, with China reporting higher rates of 15%-18% [1–3]. Pathological diminished ovarian reserve (DOR) contributes to 10–35% of infertility cases and directly impacts assisted reproduction outcomes [4, 5]. The 2022 Chinese DOR consensus highlights its rising incidence [6], where early follicle depletion reduces fertility potential [7, 8].

  Primordial follicle activation requires precise regulation, and oxidative stress imbalance accelerates ovarian aging. While low reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels maintain primordial follicle dormancy, excessive ROS accumulation preferentially damages primordial follicles over growing ones [9]. Our previous exploratory studies conducted around this experiment showed that curcumin (a key component of this study) can inhibit the phosphorylation of Smad1 protein through long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) LTCONS-00011173, thereby affecting the proliferation and apoptosis of ovarian tissues in 3-day-old rats [10]. However, the lncRNA-Smad1 mechanistic relationship remains uncharacterized, and the antioxidant role of Yangjingzhongyu Decoction (YJZYD)—curcumin’s source formula—requires validation.

  YJZYD, a 300-year-old infertility prescription, clinically improves endometrial receptivity and oocyte yield in DOR patients undergoing ART [11–13]. It reduces ovarian oxidative stress by enhancing superoxide dismutase (SOD) activity [14]. Animal studies confirm YJZYD upregulates silent information regulator 1 (SIRT1) and peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma coactivator 1-alpha‌ (PGC-1α) [15],, and modulates primordial follicle initiation via LTCONS-00011173-mediated transforming growth factor (TGF-β)1/Smad1 signaling [16]. Since PGC-1α activation downstream of Smad1 may drive antioxidant effects, we propose YJZYD alleviates LTCONS-00011173-induced Smad1 suppression, thereby enhancing mitochondrial biogenesis and optimizing primordial follicle initiation (Fig. 1).

## Materials and methods

### Animals
  Female Sprague-Dawley (SD) rats at two developmental stages were utilized: neonatal rats (3 days old; body weight 9 ± 2 g) used for establishing in vitro culture of isolated ovarian tissues and preparing diminished ovarian reserve (DOR) models and adults (7–8 weeks old; 250 ± 10 g) for serum collection. Animals were purchased from SPF (Suzhou) Biotechnology Co.,Ltd. (Production License: SCXK (Zhejiang) 2019-0002). Rats were housed under controlled conditions: 25 ± 2 °C, 45–65% relative humidity, and 12-hour light/dark cycles with ad libitum access to standard chow and water.

  All experimental procedures strictly adhered to the NIH Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals and were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Fujian Medical University (Approval No.: IACUC FJMU 2023 − 0214).

### Liquid chromatography coupled with tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS)
  Approximately 200 mg (± 1%) of YJZYD dry powder was accurately weighed into a 2 mL EP tube. Then, 0.6 mL of a methanolic solution containing 4 ppm 2-chlorophenylalanine (pre-cooled to − 20 °C) was added. The mixture was vortexed for 30 s, followed by the addition of 100 mg glass beads. It was then homogenized in a high-throughput grinder at 50 Hz for 60 s, sonicated at room temperature for 15 min, and centrifuged at 12,000 rpm and 25 °C for 10 min. A 300 µL aliquot of the supernatant was passed through a 0.22 μm membrane filter, and the filtrate was transferred into an LC vial for analysis.

  A quality control (QC) sample was prepared by pooling 20 µL from each individual sample. LC-MS analysis was performed using a Thermo Ultimate 3000 system coupled to a Thermo Q Exactive mass spectrometer. Chromatographic separation was achieved on an ACQUITY UPLC® HSS T3 column (1.8 μm, 2.1 × 150 mm) maintained at 40 °C. The autosampler temperature was set at 8 °C, and the injection volume was 2 µL. A flow rate of 0.25 mL/min was used with the following mobile phases:

  Positive mode: 0.1% formic acid in water (C) and 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile (D).

  Negative mode: 5 mM ammonium formate in water (A) and acetonitrile (B).

  The gradient elution program was as follows:

  0–1 min, 2% B/D; 1–9 min, 2%–50% B/D; 9–12 min, 50%–98% B/D; 12–13.5 min, 98% B/D; 13.5–14 min, 98%–2% B/D; 14–20 min, 2% B/D.

  Mass detection was carried out using electrospray ionization (ESI) in both positive and negative modes. The spray voltages were set to 3.50 kV for positive mode and 2.50 kV for negative mode. Sheath gas and auxiliary gas were set at 30 arb and 10 arb, respectively. The capillary temperature was 325 °C. Full MS scans were acquired at a resolution of 70,000 over the m/z range of 81–1,000. Data-dependent MS/MS acquisition was performed using higher-energy collisional dissociation (HCD) with a normalized collision energy of 30 eV. Dynamic exclusion was enabled to avoid repeated fragmentation of dominant ions.


### Ovary isolation and culture

#### Gelatin sponge preparation
  Gelatin (48722, Sigma, USA) was dissolved in distilled water to prepare 2-10% (w/v) sterilized solution. Transglutaminase (S10156, Yuanye, Shanghai, China) was dissolved in phosphate buffer (5-15% w/v) and sterilized. Equal volumes of both solutions were mixed, and 1 mL aliquots were transferred to 6-well plates. After gelation at room temperature, hydrogels were stored at -20°C.


#### Ovarian isolation
  Neonatal SD rats (3-day-old) were euthanized via intraperitoneal barbiturate injection (pentobarbital, 50 mg/kg). Bilateral ovaries were aseptically excised through dorsal incision and rinsed in serum-free Waymouth's MB 752/1 medium (Sigma, W1625; prepared per manufacturer: 1.4 g medium in 100 mL ddH₂O). Adherent tissues were removed under stereomicroscope using microforceps, followed by 3-4 washes in fresh medium.


#### Culture system
  Ovaries were placed on pre-equilibrated gelatin sponges in 6-well plates (6 ovaries, n=3 rats per well). Culture medium consisted of Waymouth's MB 752/1 supplemented with 20% FBS (Sigma, F8318), 1% penicillin-streptomycin, and 7.6% NaHCO₃ (pH adjusted to 7.2-7.4). Plates were pre-incubated at 37°C with 5% CO₂ for 1 hour before use.


#### Tissues culture
  Ovarian tissues were randomly allocated to seven experimental groups:

  Control group: 20% blank serum medium; Model group: 20% blank serum medium + 50 nM triptolide (Yuanye, B20709) + 10 µM Dorsomorphin (Proteintech, BM000828); H-YJZYD, M-YJZYD, L-YJZYD groups: Model treatment + 20% high/medium/low-dose YJZYD serum; DHEA group: Model treatment + 20% DHEA serum; Apigenin group: Model treatment + 50 nM Apigenin.

  Dorsomorphin belongs to a dual-channel inhibitor. It was used to inhibit Smad1 phosphorylation at the LTCONS-00011173 binding site and simultaneously inhibit AMPK. All groups were cultured in Waymouth’s MB 752/1 medium containing corresponding drug sera. Tissues were maintained at 37 °C with 5% CO₂ for 72 h, with medium replacement every 12 h.


### Histological assays and follicle count
  Ovarian tissue was fixed, embedded, and cut into sections. 5 μm sections were stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H & E). Hematoxylin (3–5 min), water rinse, hydrochloric acid differentiation (~ 3 s), tap water rinse, ammonia water bluing (~ 3 s), and a final tap water rinse. Nuclear staining (light to dark blue) was verified by microscopy. Sections were then dehydrated through graded alcohols (85% and 95% ethanol, 4 min each), counterstained with eosin (4–5 min), and fully dehydrated in three changes of absolute ethanol (4–5 min each). Clearing was performed in n-butanol (4–5 min) and two changes of xylene (2–4 min each). Finally, sections were mounted in fresh xylene and coverslipped with neutral balsam.

  Images were obtained using a microscope (DMI1; LEICA, Germany). Then, the number of primary, secondary, and atretic follicles was measured (n = 3).


### Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)
  The levels of estradiol (E2) and follicle stimulating hormone (FSH) were detected using an Estradiol ELISA Kit (MM-0575R1, MEIMIAN, Jiangsu, China) and rat FSH ELISA KIT (MM-0566R1, MEIMIAN, Jiangsu, China), MEIMIAN, Jiangsu, China) respectively, according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The absorbance of the wells was determined using a microplate reader (ND-100c, MIULAB, Hangzhou, China) at a wavelength of 450 nm to determine the sample concentration (n = 3).


### Spectrophotometry
  The levels of SOD, malondialdehyde (MDA) and adenosine triphosphate (ATP) were detected using a SOD kit (A001-3-1, WST-1method, jiancheng, Nanjing, China), MDA kit (A003-1, TBA method, Jiancheng, Nanjing, China), ATP (A095-1-1,colorimetric method, Jiancheng, Nanjing, China) according to the manufacturer, and the absorbance of the wells was determined using a microplate reader (ND-100c, MIULAB, Hangzhou, China) at 450 nm (SOD), 532 nm (MDA), 636 nm (ATP) wavelengths to analyze the sample concentration.


### Western blotting
  Protein samples from granular cells separated from the ovaries were extracted using a Total Protein Extraction Kit. Protein concentrations were detected using a Protein Assay kit (AR0198, Boster, USA). Next, protein samples were separated by 10% SDS-PAGE gel and electrically transferred to PVDF membranes (0.2 μm, ISEQ00010, MERCK, USA). After blocking with 3% bovine serum albumin (BSA) for 1 h at room temperature, the membranes were incubated with the primary antibodies at 4 ℃ overnight. After washing with TBST 3 × 5 min, the membranes were incubated with goat-anti-rabbit IgG (H + L)-HRP (1:10000, SA00001-2, Proteintech, USA) for 1 h at room temperature. Protein bands were analyzed using an electrochemilluminescence (ECL) chemil uminescence kit and the bands intensity was quantified using the GIS1000 Imaging system with the following primary antibodies: Smad1 polyclonal antibody (10429-1-AP, 1:5000, Proteintech, USA), Anti-Mullerian hormone (AMH) polyclonal antibody (14461-1-AP, 1:5000, Proteintech, USA), phosphate and tensin homolog (PTEN) polyclonal antibody (10047-1-AP, 1:5000, Proteintech, USA), AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) polyclonal antibody (10929-2-AP, 1:5000, proteintech, Wuhan, China) and SIRT1 polyclonal antibody (A00018-1, 1:1000, Boster, Wuhan, China). PGC-1α monoclonal antibody (66369-1-Ig, 1:10000, Proteintech, Wuhan, China). Mammalian sterile 20-like kinase (MST) polyclonal antibody (29985-1-AP, 1:5000, Proteintech, USA), growth differentiation factor 9 (GDF9) polyclonal antibody (PB1103, 1:1000, 1:5000, proteintech, USA), and GAPDH monoclonal antibody (60004-1-Ig) (n = 3).

### Molecular docking
  The 3D structure of the hub genes protein was downloaded from the RSCB PDB database. The 2D structures of the key compounds were obtained from the PubChem database and saved in mol format by PyMOL software. The key components and targets were docked by Maestro-v13.5 software.


### Statistical analysis
  Data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) from three independent biological replicates. ## Discussion
  Folliculogenesis consists of two distinct phases: the initial gonadotropin-independent activation of primordial follicles, followed by the FSH-dependent stages of follicular recruitment and ovulation [18]. This process draws upon a non-renewable primordial follicle pool, established during fetal development, which constitutes the fundamental reservoir of female fertility [19]. Given the finite nature of this reserve, current clinical strategies for pathological DOR primarily focus on stimulating follicular development and preventing aberrant atresia [10]. In contrast, this study aims to identify strategies for precisely regulating the “quiescence–activation” transition of primordial follicles, thereby promoting their orderly development.

  Our histological and hormonal analyses confirmed that YJZYD administration effectively restored the pool of primordial and growing follicles, and normalized serum levels of FSH, E₂, and AMH in DOR model rats. This restorative effect isn’t a consequence of broad-scale activation of the primordial follicle pool.

  The activation of primordial follicles is tightly regulated by key inhibitory factors, including AMH, PTEN, and MST. Our findings reveal that YJZYD significantly downregulates PTEN expression, thereby alleviating its inhibitory effect on primordial follicle activation. YJZYD also modulates MST, a downstream effector of the Hippo pathway known to suppress follicle activation and cell proliferation [20, 21]. These results imply that YJZYD is involved in fine-tuning the primordial follicle activation. Notably, while low-dose YJZYD did not counteract Triptolide-induced upregulation of MST and PTEN, medium and high doses markedly suppressed their expression, suggesting a dose-dependent inhibitory effect on PTEN/MST that merits further investigation. Furthermore, YJZYD upregulates AMH—a recognized inhibitor of primordial follicle activation—and GDF9, contributing to a balanced regulation among PTEN, MST, AMH, and GDF9. This harmonized regulatory profile likely helps prevent excessive primordial follicle activation and premature depletion, while fostering a supportive microenvironment that promotes orderly follicular selection and maturation [22].

  Mitochondrial function in oocytes and granulosa cells is crucial for primordial follicle development. Evidence indicates that oocytes in DOR exhibit downregulation of 466 genes related to mitochondrial function and protein synthesis [23]. In DOR, mitochondrial energy metabolism is closely associated with oxidative stress. The AMPK/SIRT1/PGC-1α pathway acts as a central regulator of both mitochondrial metabolism and the cellular response to oxidative stress [24], activating through metabolic reprogramming to restore energy homeostasis [25]. Dysregulation of AMPK impairs mitochondrial antioxidant capacity and compromises organelle integrity in oocytes [26]. Previous studies suggest that PTEN negatively regulates AMPK, indicating a protective role in ovarian function—a finding corroborated by our data [27]. Meanwhile, SIRT1 serves as a key mediator of antioxidant defense and facilitates the transition from primordial to growing follicles. By enhancing SOD activity, SIRT1 helps protect oocytes from oxidative damage [28, 29]. Additionally, both AMPK and SIRT1 activate PGC-1α, a co-activator of peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma [30–32], which in turn promotes mitochondrial biogenesis and oxidative phosphorylation.

  The dysregulation of the AMPK/SIRT1/PGC-1α pathway represents a central mechanism underlying mitochondrial dysfunction in DOR [33]. ATP, as the primary energy source for follicular development, yields metabolic byproducts including ROS, which can perturb redox homeostasis and promote oxidative stress [26, 33]. The AMPK/SIRT1/PGC-1α pathway contributes to redox equilibrium by regulating antioxidant enzymes such as SOD, which scavenges ROS to maintain cellular balance. The extent of oxidative injury is reflected by MDA, a well-established biomarker of lipid peroxidation. In line with these mechanisms, our study demonstrates that YJZYD activates the AMPK/SIRT1/PGC-1α pathway, thereby enhancing mitochondrial function and antioxidant capacity, and ultimately ameliorating ovarian oxidative stress.

  Notably, upon pharmacological inhibition of Smad1, PGC-1α expression displayed an inverse dose–response relationship to YJZYD compared to baseline transcriptional levels. This observation suggests either a functional crosstalk between PGC-1α and Smad1, or the involvement of additional upstream regulators of PGC-1α—such as the PI3K/AKT or p38MAPK pathways [34, 35]—as potential targets of YJZYD. To further investigate the latter possibility, we integrated prior network pharmacology data [17], which indicated that YJZYD concurrently influences the PI3K/Akt, MAPK, and AMPK signaling pathways. Consistent with this, molecular docking analyses identified apigenin and curcumin as key active constituents of YJZYD with affinity for the MAPK pathway, thereby providing preliminary mechanistic support for the hypothesis that YJZYD modulates PGC-1α through multiple upstream signaling axes.

  LncRNAs have been recognized as important regulators of follicular development, with a number of stage-specific LncRNAs identified in recent years [36–39]. Among them, LTCONS-00011173 is an ovary-specific LncRNA that shows differential expression during primordial follicle initiation and has been shown to directly target Smad1 [40]. In line with prior predictions [10], pharmacological inhibition of Smad1 by dorsomorphin reduced the binding sites of LTCONS-00011173 and led to its upregulated expression. Smad1 functions as a key transducer within the BMP-like branch of the TGF-β superfamily, forming heterodimers with phosphorylated Smad5/8 [41], and helping to synchronize oocyte–granulosa cell communication, thereby supporting normal primordial follicle development [42]. Furthermore, Smad1 serves as a signaling node interfacing with the homologous AMH pathway: YJZYD was found to activate Smad1, thereby promoting AMH translation. Dorsomorphin also concurrently inhibits AMPK, and both Smad1 and AMPK signaling converge on PGC-1α, a central regulator of mitochondrial gene transcription. Additionally, SIRT1 facilitates Smad1 phosphorylation by promoting Smad7 degradation [43, 44]. Although apigenin, a key active component of YJZYD, replicated the regulatory effects of the full formula on the LTCONS-00011173/Smad1 and AMPK pathways and partially restored follicular development, it failed to upregulate TGF-β1 and GDF9, highlighting the synergistic capacity of the multi-component formulation.

  While this study delineates the contributions of both the LTCONS-00011173/Smad1 and AMPK/SIRT1/PGC-1α pathways to the therapeutic effects of YJZYD, it is important to note that our data does not establish a direct, linear regulatory relationship between Smad1 and AMPK. Instead, we posit that functional crosstalk and convergence underlie their coordinated actions. This interplay is likely mediated through shared downstream effectors and regulatory nodes. A pivotal convergence point appears to be PGC-1α, a master regulator of mitochondrial biogenesis, which can be influenced by both pathways [30, 45]. Therefore, the mechanism of YJZYD is described as a synchronized modulation of two synergistic signaling networks, rather than a simple linear cascade.

  We propose that the crosstalk between the LTCONS-00011173/Smad1 and AMPK pathways may involve Smad1 and PGC-1α. YJZYD appears to coordinate these pathways to establish an “activation–limitation” balance in the primordial follicle microenvironment, promoting transition to growing follicles. This mechanism could potentially rescue otherwise dormant follicles, offering a promising therapeutic strategy for DOR. However, further mechanistic studies and clinical validation are needed to confirm this hypothesis.


## Limitations
  (1) Although we identified LTCONS-00011173/Smad1 as a therapeutic target, it is still necessary to verify the crosstalk between AMH and AMPK by immunoprecipitation test. In the future, we can try to establish a threshold model of PTEN/MST/AMH balance to predict the relationship between follicular activation. Moreover, we need to improve the gene knockout test in vivo.

  (2) Dorsomorphin was used as a “dual-pathway inhibitor” to simultaneously block Smad1 and AMPK. Although the selection of the tool compound is reasonable, this makes it difficult to determine which pathway is responsible for the observed effect.


## Conclusion
  YJZYD can reduce the inhibition of primordial follicle initiating by regulating the LTCONS-00011173/Smad1 pathway, while repairing oxidative stress and promoting follicle energy metabolism by strengthening the energy metabolism pathway downstream of Smad1 on the AMPK/SIRT1/PGC-1α pathway. Then, overall the microenvironment of primordial follicle activation and follicle growth can be improved through the balanced regulation of primordial follicle initiating factor, the reversed regulation of the damage of ovarian reserve, and enhanced ovarian function.
