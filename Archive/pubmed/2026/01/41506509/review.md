## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a potentially interesting study on the oligomerization of hnRNPA2 LCD, a protein relevant to RNA metabolism and neurodegeneration. However, the abstract lacks quantitative results, specific mechanistic conclusions, and sufficient methodological detail to evaluate the validity or significance of the claims. The central claim of a "mechanism" for oligomerization is not substantiated by any specific finding described in the abstract. The proposed method for quantifying oligomerization kinetics is mentioned but not described in any testable way. As presented, the work is not assessable for scientific rigor or novelty.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying RNA-binding proteins, liquid-liquid phase separation, and neurodegeneration mechanisms would be interested, as hnRNPA2 is a well-known component of ribonucleoprotein granules and its dysfunction is linked to amyotrophic lateral sclerosis and related disorders. Additionally, biophysicists and colloid scientists interested in protein adsorption and oligomerization kinetics may find the methodological aspects relevant.
- **Major strengths** The combination of experimental and theoretical approaches is commendable. The focus on pH and ionic strength as variables is relevant to physiological and pathological conditions. The potential to develop a quantitative method for unstable protein solutions addresses a practical need in the field.
- **Major Concerns** The abstract does not provide any quantitative data, such as measured oligomer sizes, adsorption rates, or simulation results. The claimed "mechanism" of oligomerization is not articulated. The relationship between the theoretical predictions and experimental observations is not described. The novelty of the proposed quantification method cannot be assessed without details.
- **Minor Comments** The abstract would benefit from stating the key quantitative findings. The relevance of the chosen pH and ionic strength conditions to physiological contexts should be clarified. The term "mechanism" should be used more precisely, as the abstract describes observations of oligomer formation rather than a molecular mechanism.

- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Claim-evidence mismatch
- **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that the study reveals the "mechanism" of pH-driven changes in protein conformation and their influence on the oligomerization process.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, molecular dynamics modeling section; location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract states that molecular dynamics modeling was applied to gain insight into the mechanism, but no specific mechanistic finding is reported. No conformational changes, interaction energies, or oligomerization pathways are described.
- **Why it matters** The central claim of the paper, as implied by the title and abstract, is the elucidation of an oligomerization mechanism. Without any mechanistic detail, the claim is unsupported and the scientific contribution is unclear.
