# The role of cellular senescence-related genes in ischemia–reperfusion injury and the identification of their biomarkers


## Supplementary Information
  The online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-36076-2.


## Introduction
  Ischemia–reperfusion injury (IRI) represents a significant pathological phenomenon that occurs following the restoration of blood flow to ischemic cardiac tissue, which paradoxically leads to further cellular damage1. This condition is a major contributor to morbidity and mortality in cardiovascular diseases, especially during myocardial infarction, cardiac surgery, and transplantation2,3. It also imposes substantial economic burdens by prolonging hospital stays, increasing healthcare costs, and reducing workforce productivity. Current diagnostic strategies include electrocardiographic techniques, measurement of cardiac biomarkers, and various imaging modalities. Therapeutic strategies include ischemic preconditioning and pharmacological interventions. Nevertheless, these approaches suffer from limitations such as poor early detection, low specificity, and suboptimal therapeutic efficacy. Consequently, there is a pressing need to develop novel diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets for IRI, underscoring the necessity of this research endeavor.

  This research investigates the role of cellular senescence in myocardial IRI, a critical pathological process that exacerbates cardiovascular morbidity and mortality. This process involves a cascade of pathological events, specifically including oxidative stress, inflammatory response, mitochondrial dysfunction, impaired mitophagy, ferroptosis, and cellular senescence4–11. Previous studies have established a link between cellular senescence and cardiovascular dysfunction12,13. These studies emphasize that IRI triggers oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and DNA damage in sequence, which activate the p53/p21 and p16/Rb senescence pathways and lead to myocardial cellular senescence14,15. Senescent cardiac myocytes secrete the senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP), continuously releasing inflammatory factors, chemokines, proteases, and other molecules that form an "inflammatory microenvironment." This microenvironment both amplifies the systemic inflammatory response and directly inhibits myocardial repair processes, ultimately exacerbating myocardial injury and dysfunction16,17. The interplay between cellular senescence and the inflammatory microenvironment suggests that targeting aging-related pathways can provide new diagnostic and therapeutic options. This study improves understanding of IRI mechanisms by identifying genes related to cellular senescence and developing a diagnostic model. It highlights the potential of these biomarkers to facilitate early diagnosis and guide treatment, addressing a critical clinical need in cardiovascular medicine.

  This study employs an integrated bioinformatics approach, complemented by experimental validation. It investigates the role of cellular senescence in myocardial IRI. The methodological framework uses multiple Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) datasets and advanced machine learning algorithms such as Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) and Support Vector Machine (SVM) to improve feature selection robustness and model accuracy. This approach’s advantages lie in its ability to integrate diverse datasets, thereby increasing statistical power; it also ensures comprehensive validation through both computational and experimental methodologies. The primary objective of this research is to identify reliable cellular senescence-related biomarkers for the diagnosis of IRI, construct a predictive diagnostic model, elucidate the underlying molecular mechanisms, and propose potential therapeutic targets through regulatory network analysis. By addressing these objectives, this study aims to improve diagnostic and therapeutic strategies for IRI, ultimately enhancing clinical outcomes in cardiovascular disease management.

  Next, we further performed machine learning to construct a diagnostic model by using high-throughput data to escalate the diagnostic performance of IRI. ### In vivo validation of model genes
  The reliability of expression levels of 6 CSRDEGs in IRI model samples was validated using quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) and immunohistochemistry (IHC). A brief experimental process is illustrated in Fig. 9a. qRT-PCR results showed that CDKN2B mRNA and protein levels gradually increased within 7 days after IRI, with consistent expression levels. The mRNA expression levels of ID1, STAT3, TP53, TERF2, and ZNF277 significantly decreased 24 h after IRI, reaching the most pronounced reduction by day 3, followed by gradual recovery. This phenomenon indicates time-dependent differences in the expression of the model genes at both transcriptional and protein levels. ## Discussion
  Ischemia–reperfusion injury is a prevalent and serious condition associated with cardiovascular diseases, often occurring during cardiac surgeries, heart transplants, and reperfusion therapies following myocardial infarction29,30. This condition severely impairs heart function and significantly deteriorates the prognosis of cardiovascular diseases. Recent studies show that cellular senescence triggered by cardiac IRI critically contributes to cardiac remodeling and chronic heart injury progression14,31. Walaszczyk and colleagues have provided evidence that the anti-ageing medication navitoclax effectively eradicates senescent cardiomyocytes in aged mice, decreases the presence of profibrotic proteins in aged mice, enhances myocardial remodeling and diastolic function, and improves survival rates following myocardial infarction32. In recent years, research on cardiac IRI has increased, resulting in some advancements. Nevertheless, current diagnostic and treatment methods still have significant limitations33. A major clinical challenge remains the lack of effective biomarkers to predict, diagnose, and assess the severity and prognosis of the injury. In-depth bioinformatics research on cellular senescence in IRI is essential to uncover potential strategies for its diagnosis and treatment.

  This study comprehensively analyzed the roles of CSRDEGs in the IRI process. It also constructed a novel diagnostic model composed of six genes. This model provides new insights into the pathological mechanisms of IRI and offers strategies to improve early diagnosis and targeted therapy. The six-gene diagnostic model developed in this study shows higher diagnostic specificity and greater clinical application potential than existing biomarkers for IRI. Currently, clinical biomarkers commonly used for IRI include creatine kinase isoenzyme (CK-MB), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), cardiac troponin I (cTnI), and B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP). These serological biomarkers show some sensitivity in the acute phase. However, their detection is influenced by factors such as measurement timing and various inflammatory reactions. These limitations reduce their effectiveness for early and accurate diagnosis. In contrast, six cellular senescence-related genes (CDKN2B, ID1, STAT3, TERF2, TP53, ZNF277), identified by integrating three transcriptome datasets with a machine learning algorithm, can reflect the pathobiological process of IRI at the molecular level. The model achieved an AUC above 0.9 in internal validation, demonstrating excellent diagnostic performance and stability. Previous studies have shown that ROC curve analysis of CK-MB and LDH typically yields AUC values in the moderate range (0.6–0.8)34,35. In gene-based diagnostic models, Pan et al. reported an AUC of 136, whereas Pei et al. found that the AUC of all four model genes exceeded 0.737. These findings suggest that gene-based models have higher sensitivity and improved robustness compared to traditional biomarkers such as CK-MB and LDH. Moreover, the six-gene model in this study achieved an AUC higher than 0.9, consistent with or surpassing these advanced models. Notably, it shows superior specificity, further supporting its potential as a supplementary tool for IRI diagnosis in clinical practice.

  In this study, KEGG enrichment identified the MAPK pathway, PI3K-AKT pathway, and cellular senescence pathway as interconnected participants in IRI, rather than isolated ones. Instead, they drive cellular aging by interacting via oxidative stress, inflammatory response, and cell cycle regulation, ultimately exacerbating tissue damage38–40. The burst of reactive oxygen species (ROS) during IRI is central to oxidative stress and directly initiates the MAPK pathway and cellular senescence pathway. On the one hand, oxidative stress damages DNA and causes lipid peroxidation, together inducing irreversible cell cycle arrest and activating the cellular aging pathway41. These effects are consistent with the enrichment of ‘regulation of mitotic cell cycle’ and ‘cellular senescence’ in GO analysis. On the other hand, oxidative stress leads to rapid phosphorylation and activation of the MAPK family members such as ERK, JNK, and p3842. Overactivation of MAPK signaling accelerates cellular senescence by upregulating cell cycle inhibitory proteins such as p16INK4a and p2143. At the same time, oxidative stress inhibits the activity of the PI3K-AKT pathway. This reduction decreases cellular antioxidant capacity and anti-apoptotic potential, further amplifying oxidative damage and senescence processes. These effects are highly consistent with the functional annotation of the PI3K-AKT pathway as ‘cell survival regulation’ in KEGG. All three pathways regulate cell cycle progression and ultimately stabilize the cellular senescence phenotype. The sustained activation of the MAPK pathway blocks progression through the G1/S and G2/M transitions in the cell cycle44. This sustained activation promotes the binding of p16INK4a and p21 to retinoblastoma protein (Rb), inhibits E2F transcription factor activity, and leads to cell cycle arrest45. The decrease in PI3K-AKT pathway activity reduces downstream mTOR signaling activation, disrupts the balance between cell proliferation and senescence, and further consolidates the cycle arrest state46. The cellular senescence pathway itself is characterized by irreversible cell cycle arrest as its core feature47. The enrichment of the ‘regulation of mitotic cell cycle’ term in GO analysis directly confirms the central role of cell cycle abnormalities in senescence associated with IRI. The three pathways jointly maintain the cellular senescence phenotype by synergistically regulating cell cycle-related molecules.

  The six model genes were linked to cell cycle regulation and telomerase damage processes, with ID1, STAT3, and TP53 showing significant associations with IRI. It is worth noting that TP53 may regulate cellular senescence through the classical TP53-p21 axis. KEGG analysis revealed significant enrichment of TP53 in the ‘p53 signaling pathway,’ whereas GO enrichment linked CSRDEGs to ‘cell cycle regulation’ and ‘DNA damage response.’ Activated TP53 transcriptionally upregulates p21 (CDKN1A), which inhibits CDK4/6, leading to cell cycle arrest at the G1/S transition. Previous studies have shown that p21-mediated cell cycle arrest is a hallmark of cellular senescence in myocardial injury, supporting this mechanism. Although p21 was not directly detected in this study, the dynamic expression of TP53 in IRI mice suggests a potential time-related correlation with p21-dependent senescence induction. Recent studies have shown that TP53 has a key role in the development of aging-related coronary artery disease48. This is consistent with our results. Specifically, TP53 mRNA levels decreased at 24 h, reached their lowest point at 3 days, and recovered by 7 days. STAT3 is acknowledged for its positive effects on ischaemic hearts by regulating myocardial functions such as apoptosis, inflammation, autophagy, immune response, oxidative stress, and hypertrophy49. Activation of STAT3 has a twofold impact on cardiac remodeling following myocardial IRI. When STAT3 is activated moderately, cardiac remodeling is reversible; however, when it is continuously activated, cardiac remodeling is aggravated50. Following IRI, the JAK/STAT3 pathway becomes active. And pretreatment with a JAK inhibitor can reduce phosphorylated STAT3 expression, promoting apoptosis, while activation of STAT3 can improve cell survival and reduce cell death following IRI51. This finding aligns with our findings and confirms the dependability of the study’s results. Activation of CDKN2B in human cardiomyocytes has been shown to induce cardiomyocyte degeneration, highlighting its significance in regulating the cell cycle, apoptosis, and senescence52. Furthermore, decreased expression of CDKN2B/p15 in heart failure induced by ischemia can worsen myocardial fibrosis and cardiac dysfunction, while increased expression of CDKN2B/p15 can improve myocardial fibrosis and cardiac dysfunction through the control of myocardial fibroblasts’ proliferation and migration53 Thus, CDKN2B is anticipated to serve as a promising target for IRI treatment.

  We also constructed regulatory networks based on model genes to better understand the interrelationships within gene regulation. The analysis of the mRNA-miRNA regulatory network identified three specific genes—STAT3, ID1, and ZNF277—along with 47 miRNAs. The study identified 33 miRNAs targeting STAT3, highlighting it as a major miRNA target. The mRNA-drug regulatory network revealed 4 model genes and 34 potential drugs and compounds, with TP53 emerging as the most strongly associated gene, aligning with Ori Hassin’s redefinition of p53 as a promising target54. Additionally, the mRNA-TF regulatory network showed interactions between 6 model genes and 38 transcription factors. Notably, TERF2, STAT3, and TP53 were identified as high-throughput nodes regulated by transcription factors. The GeneMANIA protein–protein interaction network reveals a direct interaction between CDKN2B and TP53, indicating that TP53 activates CDKN2B transcription during cellular senescence. This activation results in p15INK4b expression, which, alongside p21, inhibits cell cycle progression. This joint effort corresponds with the GO enrichment results, highlighting cell cycle regulation among differentially expressed genes associated with cellular senescence. Notably, in mouse models, the expression of CDKN2B shows a sustained increase within 7 days following IRI, whereas TP53 levels return to baseline by day 7. This observation suggests that CDKN2B may function as a downstream effector, helping to maintain long-term cell cycle arrest during the later phases of IRI. Collectively, these regulatory networks not only enhance our understanding of the molecular underpinnings of IRI but also provide a comprehensive platform for future research aimed at developing effective diagnostic and therapeutic strategies.

  The immune infiltration analysis provides compelling evidence for the involvement of specific immune cell types in the pathophysiology of IRI. Among the notable findings, activated CD4 + T cells emerged as a critical component of the immune response. These cells play a pivotal role in orchestrating adaptive immunity, particularly through the production of cytokines that enhance the inflammatory response. Their increased infiltration in the IRI group suggests a heightened immune activation, which may contribute to tissue damage and repair processes. Previous studies have demonstrated that CD4 + T cell activation is associated with adverse outcomes in various inflammatory conditions, underscoring their potential as therapeutic targets in IRI55. Central memory CD4 + and CD8 + T cells also exhibited significant differences in infiltration levels between the IRI and control groups. These cells are essential for long-term immune surveillance and rapid response upon re-exposure to antigens. The strong correlation observed between CDKN2B, a gene associated with cellular senescence, and central memory T cells (r = 0.8883) suggests that immune aging may influence the functionality of these cells in the context of IRI. This relationship aligns with findings that indicate senescent T cells can exhibit impaired proliferation and effector functions, potentially exacerbating the inflammatory milieu during IRI56,57. Furthermore, the infiltration of mast cells was notably correlated with the expression of the CSRDEG TP53 (r = 0.7737). Mast cells are known to release a variety of mediators that can modulate inflammation and tissue repair. Their activation in the IRI context may contribute to both protective and detrimental effects, depending on the timing and nature of the mediators released. The dual role of mast cells in promoting inflammation while also facilitating tissue healing highlights their complex involvement in IRI and suggests that targeting mast cell activity could be a promising strategy for therapeutic intervention58,59. Collectively, these findings underscore the intricate interplay between immune cell infiltration and the inflammatory microenvironment in IRI, warranting further investigation into the mechanistic pathways involved.

  Finally, we verified the expression of diagnostic model genes identified by bioinformatics through experiments. During the early stage of IRI, defined as the first 24 h, ID1 mRNA significantly decreases and shows its highest diagnostic efficacy. When ID1 is downregulated, it lifts the inhibition of the TP53 pathway and paves the way for the initiation of cellular senescence. This induces a stress state in myocardial cells, which triggers early immune cell infiltration60. Meanwhile, CDKN2B gradually increases, indicating pre-activation of the cellular senescence pathway, while the mRNA levels of the other four genes are temporarily downregulated as an adaptive response to cellular stress61. Three days after IRI marks a critical turning point. At this time, mRNA levels of five genes, including ID1, reach their lowest, while CDKN2B continues to rise, indicating peak diagnostic accuracy for IRI assessment. The downregulation of TP53 and TERF2 mRNA involves transcriptional or post-transcriptional regulation. Specifically, phosphorylation of the TP53 protein activates the cellular senescence pathway, while TERF2 downregulation exacerbates telomere damage62,63. CDKN2B promotes activation of the p16/Rb pathway64. Downregulation of STAT3 leads to enrichment of M1 macrophages65. Additionally, downregulation of ZNF277 is associated with maintenance of inflammation, resulting in dual activation of cellular senescence and immune responses during this stage66. Seven days post-IRI, the repair phase begins. TP53 and related proteins return to normal levels. CDKN2B continues to rise to maintain senescence homeostasis. STAT3 mRNA levels increase, while protein levels increase asynchronously, reflecting a delay between transcription and translation in preparation for repair. The increase in TERF2 expression suggests telomere repair processes are underway67. In clinical practice, ID1 is suitable for early diagnosis within 24 h post-IRI, while assessment at three days post-IRI is appropriate for evaluating severity and risk. CDKN2B can indicate poor repair in the later phases, and combined detection of these gene markers can improve diagnostic efficiency. Gene-specific targeted interventions can be implemented in stages for precise treatment.

  Inevitably, our research has several inherent limitations. First, it relies on secondary analysis of existing datasets. The limited gene expression data available in public databases and the variability among samples may reduce the generalizability of our findings. Second, the study’s limited sample size and internal validation may increase the risk of overfitting, leading to an overestimation of the AUC. Therefore, we employed multiple machine learning algorithms for feature selection and applied cross-validation to minimize overfitting caused by a single algorithm or feature selection bias. Additionally, we integrated multiple GEO datasets and removed batch effects to reduce technical variability, which enhanced the model’s robustness and generalizability. In future studies, the model’s effectiveness will be further validated using an external cohort. Third, it remains uncertain whether the model can distinguish injury caused by post-acute myocardial infarction from that caused by percutaneous coronary intervention. Further in vivo and in vitro research is necessary to elucidate the roles and mechanisms of these key genes in IRI.


## Conclusion
  Our study constructed a diagnostic model with six genes (CDKN2B, ID1, STAT3, TEFR2, TP53, and ZNF277) and performed a preliminary validation. These results have also enriched the understanding of CSRDEGs during IRI and provided theoretical and experimental support for improving diagnoses and prognoses of IRI. Future research should aim to confirm these findings in clinical samples and investigate specific therapeutic targets within the identified pathways.


## Material and methods

### Gathering and processing data
  We selected microarray datasets profiling genome-wide gene expression in Mus species hearts, including both IRI and non-injured samples, while excluding datasets with drug-treated samples. After rigorous evaluation, we narrowed our selection to three datasets, GSE61592, GSE67308, and GSE83472. Datasets were acquired from the GEO database (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) via the GEOquery R package68. More details about the datasets are provided in Supplementary Table S1. To ensure data consistency and comparability, the original expression matrices of these three IRI datasets are first integrated. Then, based on the sva69 package (ComBat) function, batch adjustment is first performed—batch variables are defined from the dataset source, and batch specific technical variations are separated and eliminated through empirical Bayesian models, while retaining the biological differences between the IR group and the control group; Next: Normalize the batch adjusted matrices using the Normalize Between Arrays function to further unify the sample expression distribution, ultimately obtaining a standardized integrated dataset. The distribution changes between samples were evaluated using Principal Component Analysis (PCA)70 before and after batch correction. Before batch correction, the samples clustered mainly according to the dataset source, while after ComBat correction, the sample distribution was mainly distinguished by biological characteristics (IR group and control group), indicating that the batch effect had significantly weakened. At the same time, the box plot shows that the gene expression distribution tends to be consistent among different datasets, further verifying the correction effect. In addition, based on PCA analysis of the integrated dataset after correction, sample GSM2203978 showed significant independent deviation. It did not cluster with either the control or IR groups, indicating systematic differences in gene expression compared to other samples. Therefore, it was removed in subsequent analysis to avoid analysis bias.

  Data of the CSRGs were obtained from the GeneCards database (https://www.genecards.org/). After utilizing the term ‘cellular senescence’ in our search, we identified a total of 964 CSRGs by filtering for ‘protein coding’ and ‘relevance score > 5’. Supplementary Table S2 displays the titles of the CSRGs.


### Identification of differential expression analysis
  Gene expression levels for all genes in the combined GEO dataset, including

  IRI and control samples, were calculated using the R package limma. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using criteria of |logFC|> 0 and adj. p < 0.05. Venn diagrams were utilized to identify CSRDEGs. A volcano plot was generated with the ggplot2 package in R after conducting differential expression analysis.


### Enrichment analysis using GO and KEGG pathways
  The R package clusterProfile71r was used to analyze enriched genes in GO and KEGG terms within the CSRDEGs, applying a significance threshold of adj. p < 0.05 and FDR < 0.05. Pathway maps for KEGG enrichment analysis were visualized using the R package Pathview72.


### Construction of an IRI diagnostic model
  Logistic regression analysis was used to identify the CSRDEGs associated with IRI, using a significance threshold of p < 0.05. Six genes with p-values greater than 0.05 were excluded, leaving 20 genes that met the threshold for preliminary screening of significant genes. Next, we used the R package glmnet73 to perform LASSO regression on these 20 CSRDEGs, with the parameter set.seed(2022) and a tenfold cross-validation method. This method performs feature selection to facilitate model construction. To improve the robustness of feature gene selection, we also built an SVM74 model to screen the CSRDEGs, and then intersected the feature genes identified by both LASSO and SVM to determine the final set of model genes. We did not use the Ridge or Elastic Net models because LASSO offers sparsity, which is advantageous for analyzing small sample, high-dimensional data. Furthermore, we calculated the GO semantic similarity among the six model genes using the R package GOSemSim to demonstrate their functional association and similarity. Finally, the LASSO regression coefficients were combined with the gene expression levels in the integrated dataset to calculate the risk score of the diagnostic model. ### Validation of the diagnostic model for IRI
  A nomogram75 was developed using the rms package in R using logistic regression results to demonstrate variable relationships within the model. The ggDCA76 package generates decision curve analysis (DCA) maps based on model genes in combined GEO datasets. Subsequently, the pROC software was employed to produce the ROC curve and determine the AUC for the merged GEO dataset. Samples from the GEO dataset were divided into high-risk and low-risk groups based on the median risk score to assess its influence on IRI progression.


### Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA)
  In this study, we first sorted the genes in the integrated GEO dataset by their logFC values, then performed GSEA on all genes using the R package clusterProfiler. The gene set c2.cp.all.v2022.1.hs.symbols.gmt [all canonical pathways] (3050) was obtained from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB)77. In the initial analysis, we used the c2.cp.all gene set to ensure a comprehensive coverage. During the interpretation stage, pathways meeting the statistical significance threshold (adj. p and FDR < 0.05) were further manually reviewed. Pathways unrelated to the biological context of ischemia–reperfusion injury were removed, and redundant pathways were merged.


### Gene set variation analysis (GSVA)
  The c2.Cp.All.V2022.1.Hs was accessed using MsigDB. Symbol gene sets and GSVA were performed to analyze gene set variation in the combined dataset. Functional enrichment discrepancies between the two groups were analyzed using criteria of |logFC|> 0 and p < 0.05.


### Immune infiltration analysis
  The ssGSEA and CIBERSORT algorithms were utilized to conduct immune cell analysis on the combined GEO dataset. Next, the Spearman correlation was employed to examine the connections among immune cells and target genes, which were then displayed using the ggplot2 package in R.


### Construction of the PPI network and regulatory network
  We utilized GeneMANIA to predict genes with similar functions, ENCORI to identify miRNAs related to Model genes, the Comparative Toxicogenomics Database (CTD) to predict drug targets, and ChIPBase to analyze the regulatory effects of transcription factors on model genes. Finally, we utilized Cytoscape for visualizing regulatory networks。


### IRI mouse models
  This study involved 40 male C57BL/6 J mice, aged 8 weeks, with weights ranging from 20 to 24 g. The mice were obtained from the Air Force Medical University’s Experimental Animal Center. The subjects were kept in a controlled setting with a temperature of 23 °C ± 2 °C, humidity ranging from 42 to 70%, a 12-h light/dark cycle, and unrestricted access to food and water. Four groups of mice were established, each containing 10 animals: the sham surgery group (control), and the three IRI groups assessed at 1 day (IRI 1d), 3 days (IRI 3d), and 7 days (IRI 7d) post-injury. The sample size of mice in this study was determined based on a comprehensive consideration of the experimental purpose, model stability, and animal ethical requirements. This decision was informed by previous laboratory experience with the same IR model and pre-experimental results. Additionally, sample sizes commonly used in related studies (about 6–10 C57BL/6J mice per group) were referenced78,79. Ultimately, this study adopted a design with 10 animals per group. Mice were anesthetized using 2.0%-2.5% isoflurane, connected to a small ventilator, and then subjected to thoracotomy to expose the heart. Needles were inserted 2–3 mm from the lower edge of the left atrial appendage and then withdrawn near the pulmonary conus area, after which the left anterior descending artery was ligated using 6.0 sutures. When myocardial blanching was observed distal to the ligation site, the chest was closed in layers. In the control group, the chest was opened, but the artery was not ligated. On days 1, 3, and 7 after surgery, the hearts of mice in each group were rapidly excised after 3% isoflurane anesthesia combined with cervical dislocation. The Ethics Committee of the Chinese Air Force Medical University (IACUC20241289) approved the animal experiments. This study complied with the ARRIVE guidelines during the experiment. All methods were performed following the relevant guidelines and regulations.


### Tissue specimens and qRT‒PCR analysis
  The RNA was extracted from frozen IRI heart tissue using RNA extraction solution (#G3013). cDNA was synthesized using the SweScript All-in-One RT SuperMix for qPCR (#G3337), and subsequent qPCR was conducted with 2 × Universal Blue SYBR Green qPCR Master Mix (#G3326, Servicebio, Wuhan, China) on a CFX96 Real-Time PCR system (Bio-Rad, USA). Primers were sourced from Beijing Tsingke Biotech Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). Refer to Supplementary Table S3 for the primer sequence. Gene expression levels were evaluated using the 2-∆∆CT method with GAPDH as the internal reference for PCR data analysis.


### Immunohistochemical (IHC) staining
  Cardiac tissue samples were paraffin-embedded and sectioned into 5 μm slices. The sections underwent deparaffinization, rehydration, and antigen retrieval using 0.01 M sodium citrate. Subsequently, the sections were treated with 3% hydrogen peroxide, followed by 5% normal goat serum for blocking. The slides were incubated with primary antibodies: anti-CDKN2B (1:50, Biodragon, China), TP53 (1:50, ProteinTech Group, China), TERF2 (1:50, ProteinTech Group, China), STAT3 (1:600, Servicebio, China), and ID1 (1:800, Servicebio, China). Following incubation with secondary antibodies, sections were treated with DAB solution and counterstained using hematoxylin. The sections were examined and photographed using a Nikon microscope. Image analysis was performed using ImageJ software combined with the IHC-box plugin. Five high-magnification fields (× 400) were randomly selected from each group, avoiding tissue edges and non-specific staining areas. Then, uniform image threshold and measurement parameters were set to ensure consistent analysis conditions across all groups. Areas showing target protein-positive staining were selected. The software’s built-in density analysis function then calculated the mean optical density for each field, which was used in subsequent statistical analysis.


### Statistical analysis
  This study utilized R software (version 4.1.2) for data processing and analysis. A Student’s t-test assessed the statistical significance of normally distributed continuous variables between two groups. The Wilcoxon rank sum test was used for variables that did not exhibit a normal distribution. Spearman correlation analysis was used to calculate correlation coefficients between molecules, unless specified otherwise. All p-values were two-tailed, with significance defined as p < 0.05.
