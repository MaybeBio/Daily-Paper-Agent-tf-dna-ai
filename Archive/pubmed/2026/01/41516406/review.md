## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text, including abstract, introduction, methodology, results, discussion, and conclusions. No supplementary materials, tables, or figures were provided beyond those referenced in the text.
- **Assessment boundary** This review assesses the scientific claims, methodological rigor, and interpretive validity of the manuscript as presented. It does not evaluate the editorial fit for any specific journal.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report five rare SIM1 variants (two frameshift, two missense, one synonymous) identified in eleven pediatric patients with severe early-onset obesity. They use in silico prediction tools and AlphaFold3 structural modeling to infer potential pathogenicity and mechanistic effects, proposing that these variants expand the mutational spectrum of SIM1-related obesity.
- **Visible evidence base** The manuscript text, including descriptions of patient phenotypes, variant details, in silico predictions, and structural modeling results. Tables and figures are referenced but not provided in the submitted material.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Tables 1, 2A, 2B, and 3, Figure 1, ACMG classification details, raw sequencing data, population frequency data for all variants, and any supplementary information. The absence of these materials limits verification of variant interpretation and phenotypic correlations.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript addresses a clinically relevant topic, the identification of novel SIM1 variants in severe early-onset obesity, and attempts to provide structural context for variant effects. However, the evidence presented is largely descriptive and computational, with no functional validation. The structural modeling, while interesting, is speculative and not experimentally confirmed. The synonymous variant is presented with conflicting in silico predictions, and its inclusion as a potentially pathogenic finding is not well supported. The manuscript would benefit from clearer variant classification, additional segregation data, and functional studies to substantiate the claims.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Clinicians and researchers in pediatric endocrinology, obesity genetics, and hypothalamic biology would find this report useful as a catalog of potential SIM1 variants. Those studying genotype-phenotype correlations in monogenic obesity and the structural basis of transcription factor dysfunction may also be interested. The structural modeling approach could appeal to computational biologists, though its speculative nature limits broader impact.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses an under-recognized genetic cause of severe obesity and adds to the limited literature on SIM1 variants. The use of AlphaFold3 for structural prediction is a modern approach that may generate testable hypotheses. The clinical phenotyping is reasonably detailed, and the inclusion of a recurrent synonymous variant highlights an often-overlooked class of variants.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims that the identified variants are "likely pathogenic" and expand the spectrum of SIM1-related obesity, with the synonymous variant potentially affecting splicing.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section, Discussion section, Tables 2A, 2B, and 3 (not provided)
  - **Concern** The pathogenicity of the variants is inferred primarily from in silico tools and structural modeling, without functional validation. For the synonymous variant, c.1173G>A, the splicing prediction tools yield conflicting results, and the authors themselves acknowledge this uncertainty. The claim of pathogenicity is therefore not established. Additionally, the ACMG classifications referenced in Table 3 are not visible, preventing assessment of whether standard criteria were applied consistently.
  - **Why it matters** Without functional evidence or clear segregation data, the clinical significance of these variants remains uncertain. Overinterpretation of computational predictions can lead to misdiagnosis or inappropriate clinical management. The core claim of expanding the pathogenic spectrum requires stronger evidence.
  - **Resolution test** Provide functional assays (e.g., luciferase reporter, co-immunoprecipitation, RNA splicing assays) demonstrating altered SIM1 function. Include segregation analysis from family members. Present ACMG classification criteria and evidence for each variant in a transparent manner.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Methodological rigor
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript states that structural modeling "suggests" the missense variants disrupt protein-protein interactions, specifically P30A weakening dimerization and Ser663Leu affecting transcriptional activity.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section, Figure 1 (not provided)
  - **Concern** The AlphaFold3 predictions are presented as supportive evidence for mechanistic effects, but the models are not experimentally validated. The claim that P30A "could weaken this interface" is speculative, and the authors do not provide quantitative measures of interaction stability or functional readouts. The structural interpretation is plausible but not proven.
  - **Why it matters** Structural predictions can generate hypotheses but do not constitute evidence of pathogenicity. The manuscript's conclusions rely heavily on these models, and without experimental confirmation, the mechanistic claims are unsupported.
  - **Resolution test** Perform co-immunoprecipitation or BRET/FRET assays to test dimerization, and luciferase assays to assess transcriptional activity for the missense variants. If such experiments are not feasible, temper the language to clearly indicate hypotheses rather than findings.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Phenotypic correlation
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript implies a causal relationship between the SIM1 variants and the observed obesity phenotypes in the eleven patients.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section, Table 3 (not provided)
  - **Concern** The phenotypic descriptions are presented for individual cases, but the relationship between genotype and phenotype is not systematically analyzed. For example, the patient with the synonymous variant is not clearly distinguished from those with frameshift or missense variants in terms of phenotype severity. The lack of segregation data and the absence of information on other potential genetic contributors (e.g., polygenic risk) weaken the causal inference.
  - **Why it matters** Establishing causality in monogenic obesity requires demonstrating that the variant is necessary and sufficient for the phenotype. Without segregation or functional data, the association could be coincidental, especially given the high prevalence of obesity in the general population.
  - **Resolution test** Provide family segregation data, if available, and discuss the possibility of oligogenic or polygenic contributions. Compare phenotypes across variant groups and with published SIM1 cases.

  - **Concern ID** R1-m4

- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** The lack of functional validation for all variants, the absence of segregation data, and the reliance on conflicting in silico predictions for the synonymous variant are critical gaps. The structural modeling, while informative, is not sufficient to establish pathogenicity. The manuscript must either provide experimental evidence or substantially temper its claims.

## Assessment against Nature-style criteria
- **Originality** Moderate. The identification of novel variants is incremental, and the use of AlphaFold3 is a novel approach, but the overall concept of SIM1-related obesity is well established.
- **Scientific importance** Limited. The findings are clinically relevant but do not fundamentally change the understanding of SIM1 biology or obesity genetics. The lack of functional data reduces the impact.
- **Interdisciplinary readership** Narrow. The manuscript is primarily of interest to specialists in obesity genetics and pediatric endocrinology. The structural modeling may attract computational biologists, but the speculative nature limits broader appeal.
- **Technical soundness** Weak. The methodology is appropriate for variant discovery, but the interpretation is overextended. The structural predictions are not validated, and the synonymous variant is not convincingly linked to disease.
- **Readability for nonspecialists** Acceptable. The manuscript is generally clear, but the case vignettes are disjointed, and the structural modeling section may be difficult for non-experts to follow.

## Recommendation posture
Currently not established from the provided evidence. The manuscript presents interesting observations but lacks the functional and genetic evidence required to support the claim of expanding the pathogenic spectrum of SIM1-related obesity. The authors should either provide experimental validation or revise the claims to reflect the speculative nature of the findings.
