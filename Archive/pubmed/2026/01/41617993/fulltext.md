This study was aimed at elucidating the therapeutic effects of hirudin on renal interstitial fibrosis (RIF) and at delineating the molecular mechanisms underlying its antifibrotic actions. A comprehensive research approach was adopted, integrating network pharmacology, molecular docking, molecular dynamics simulations, and in vitro experimental validation, to explore the mechanisms through which hirudin alleviates RIF. Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analysis identified 185 enriched signalling pathways, with the primary ones being the VEGF signalling pathway, TNFA signalling pathway and NF-kappaB signalling pathway. Gene Ontology (GO) analysis revealed that hirudin's antifibrotic effects were associated with inflammatory responses, canonical NF-kappaB signal transduction, and cellular responses to oxidative stress. Protein-protein interaction (PPI) network analysis identified TNF, HIF1A, HO-1, CASP3, IKBA, KEAP1, and RELA as key hub proteins. Experimental validation demonstrated that hirudin significantly reduced the protein levels of fibronectin (FN) and collagen I (Col I) in TGF-beta1-stimulated HK-2 cells. Additionally, hirudin downregulated pro-inflammatory markers (TNF-alpha, MCP-1, p-P65, and p-IkappaBalpha) while upregulating antioxidant proteins (Nrf2, HO-1, and SOD-1). These findings suggest that hirudin mitigates TGF-beta1-induced inflammation and oxidative stress in HK-2 cells by modulating the Nrf2 and NF-kappaB signalling pathways, thereby impeding the progression of RIF.
