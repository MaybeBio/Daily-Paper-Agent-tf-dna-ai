# Altered AP-1, RUNX, and EGR chromatin dynamics drive human fibrotic lung disease


## Objectives:
  Pulmonary fibrosis can result from autoimmune, inflammatory, or idiopathic conditions, including systemic sclerosis-associated interstitial lung disease (SSc-ILD). Previous studies have implicated myofibroblasts and SPP1hi macrophages as drivers of fibrosis in lungs, as well as other tissues. Single-cell RNA sequencing has delineated fibroblast and macrophage transcriptomes but provides limited insight into the transcriptional control of profibrotic gene programs.


## Methods:
  To determine the chromatin accessibility dynamics and transcription factors driving profibrotic gene programs in human pulmonary fibrosis, we performed multiomic single nucleus ATAC/RNA-sequencing on explanted SSc-ILD and control lungs. Using the neural network tool ChromBPNet, we analyzed pseudobulk fibroblast and macrophage populations to infer transcription factor binding dynamics at single base pair resolution. Hierarchical causal modeling for single cell multi-omics data (HALO), a novel algorithm developed to derive low-dimensional representations of sparse multi-omics data, revealed transcription factor-regulatory element-gene networks.


## Results:
  ChromBPNet inferred increased transcription factor binding to profibrotic genes, including CTHRC1 and ADAM12 in fibroblasts, and SPP1 and CCL18 in macrophages. SSc-ILD fibroblasts showed increased binding and enhancer activity by AP-1, RUNX and EGR transcription factors to profibrotic genes, while macrophages displayed enhanced binding and activity of AP-1 and bHLH-ZIP transcription factors in genes regulating the SPP1hi phenotype. HALO confirmed AP-1, RUNX, and EGR activity controlling profibrotic gene programs.


## Conclusions:
  These data provide comprehensive insights into the complexity of transcriptional control of SSc-ILD gene expression programs and define specific AP-1, RUNX and EGR enhancers in key myofibroblast marker genes CTHRC1 and ADAM12, and a bHLH-ZIP enhancer in SPP1hi macrophages.


## INTRODUCTION
  Fibrosis is a lethal feature in numerous diseases. Myofibroblasts drive fibrosis due to their excessive production of extracellular matrix, resulting in tissue stiffness and organ dysfunction. While necessary for normal physiologic wound healing, dysregulated myofibroblasts contribute to fibrosis in pulmonary, renal, dermal, hepatic, and cardiac tissues. Macrophages, particularly a subset marked by SPP1, are implicated in activating myofibroblasts and infiltrate fibrotic tissues, including the lungs, liver [1], skeletal muscle [2] and heart[3]. Deleting or silencing SPP1, encoding Osteopontin, ameliorates bleomycin induced lung fibrosis[4]. Single cell RNA-sequencing (scRNA-seq) meta-analyses reveal that SPP1hi macrophages share similar transcriptional states across organs affected by fibrosis [1, 5].

  Systemic sclerosis-associated interstitial lung disease (SSc-ILD) is the leading cause of disease-associated mortality in patients with SSc [6]. Despite current therapies targeting immune dysregulation, vasculopathy, and fibrosis, limited progress has been made in ameliorating disease-associated mortality or improving quality of life. Myofibroblasts and SPP1hi macrophages are prominent cell populations associated with progressive lung disease [7]. Thus, understanding the transcriptional programs regulating these cells’ profibrotic phenotypes is critical for elucidating molecular mechanisms underlying disease and informing therapeutic strategies.

  ScRNA-seq has delineated the transcriptomes of various cell types in the healthy and fibrotic lung[8], revealing perturbed cell states and altered cell-cell communication networks in disease[9]. Despite generating biologically relevant inferences of altered cell phenotypes and signaling pathways, these studies are limited in their ability to decipher how regulatory networks are perturbed, leading to the altered cellular states present in fibrosis. Gene expression is regulated in a cell type- and tissue-specific manner by transcription factors (TFs) interacting with cis-regulatory elements (CREs) in the genome. Understanding changes in TF dynamics at CREs in key profibrotic cells, namely myofibroblasts and SPP1hi macrophages, can provide direct insights into disrupted regulatory networks in fibrosis.

  Single-nucleus ATAC-seq (snATAC-seq) identifies open (accessible) chromatin regions (OCRs), that represent candidate CREs, in diverse cell populations. Using multiomic snATAC-seq/snRNA-seq, we identified differentially accessible chromatin regions in cells isolated from human SSc-ILD compared to control lungs. We applied ChromBPNet, a convolutional neural network model, to infer TF binding in accessible chromatin at single-base-pair resolution [10], inferring perturbations in gene-regulatory networks in SSc-ILD cell populations. Focusing on fibroblasts and SPP1hi macrophages, we identified altered binding and enhancer activity of AP-1, RUNX and EGR TFs in SSc-ILD fibroblasts, as well as basic helix-loop-helix(bHLH) ZIP and AP-1 TFs in SSc-ILD macrophages targeting CREs associated with genes promoting fibrosis. These studies provide direct in vivo insights into the altered TF dynamics driving profibrotic cell states.


## METHODS
  The University of Pittsburgh Institutional Review Board approved procedures involving human samples. Explanted subpleural peripheral lung tissue was digested to single-cell suspensions as previously described [11]. For the snATAC-seq only samples, single-cell suspensions were split, with a portion used for performing scRNA-seq as previously described [12], and the remaining suspension used for snATAC-seq as previously described [13]. For the multiome samples, transposition of nuclei and library preparation were carried out per the Chromium Next GEM Single Cell Multiome ATAC + Gene Expression protocol. For the ChromBPNet analysis, pseudo-bulk ATAC-seq profiles for SSc and control fibroblasts and corresponding SPP1hi macrophages were used to train bias models on non-accessible genomic regions which were frozen and utilized for training secondary models on accessible (peak) genomic regions. After removing bias, corrected signal profiles for each of the four cell states were used to generate single-base pair resolution contribution scores. TF-MoDISco [14, 15] enabled de novo motif discovery, complemented by calling analysis peaks using the ChromBPNet contribution score tracks, intersection with JASPAR motif coordinates, and generation of cluster-specific aggregate contribution scores. Dual-luciferase reporter assays were performed with a pGL4.26 minimal promoter vector in IMR90 and THP1 cells with and without mutation of the TF binding site of interest. We utilized HALO, a variational autoencoder model[16], to infer latent representations from scRNA-seq and snATAC-seq data, followed by aggregating of individual cells exhibiting similar states into METAcells, which accounts for potential time-lag events between RNA-seq and ATAC-seq data.


## RESULTS

### Epigenomic markers of healthy and SSc-ILD lung cell populations
  We performed paired scRNA-seq and snATAC-seq on 3 SSc-ILD and 2 control lung samples, and multiome snRNA-seq/snATAC-seq on nuclei from an additional 6 SSc-ILD and 7 control explants. Subpleural tissue was collected at the time of lung transplant in patients with SSc-ILD, and from organ donors without preexisting lung disease. Sixteen samples were partially enriched for mesenchymal cells. Histology showed usual interstitial pneumonia in 8 SSc-ILD samples and predominant vascular disease in one (Fig. S1, Table S1). After filtering, 67,446 nuclei (average 3,747 per donor) were included for analyses. Normalization, batch correction, and dimensionality reduction were performed using Seurat, Signac, and Harmony [17, 18]. SnATAC data alone was utilized for initial clustering after delineating OCRs/peaks using MACS2. We identified 35 distinct clusters based on gene expression and promoter accessibility (Fig. 1A–B, Fig. S2), and aligned our cell populations with those from prior single-cell studies when possible[12, 19–21]. All major pulmonary cell types were identified with 446,087 OCRs among 67,446 nuclei. Nuclei clustered by cell type and were well integrated by chemistry, disease state, and individual sample after batch correction (Fig. 1C–D, Fig. S3A–B). Low quality clusters were excluded from further analyses.

  OCRs were annotated to the nearest gene and region (Fig. 1E), with 20.32% of OCRs occurring within 3 kb of transcription start sites, and most OCRs in distal intergenic or intronic regions. Cell clusters were distinguished by differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and differentially accessible chromatin regions (DARs), with cluster 9 and 31 fibroblasts showing the most unique DARs (Fig. 1G) when each cluster was compared to all other clusters. DEGs and DARs were identified between SSc and control for each population (Fig. 1F), with fibroblast, SPP1hi macrophage, mixed epithelial, and arterial endothelial cells having the largest number of significant DARs, reflecting significant chromatin accessibility changes in these cell types with fibrosis. Next, we identified cell type-specific TFs by gene expression and motif enrichment using chromVAR [22] (Fig. S4 A–B), revealing known regulators such as ETS1 in NK and T cells, SPI1 in macrophages, and GATA2 in mast cells, validating the analysis. Enriched fibroblast motifs included AP-1, Tal-related (including TWIST1 and HAND2), and TEAD TF family motifs. TF motif activity and gene expression showed significant positive correlations in 23 TFs, including TWIST1 in fibroblasts, SPI1 in macrophages, and SOX18 in endothelial cells (Fig. S4C). We also evaluated enriched motifs for control lungs only, with similar results (Fig. S5).


### Altered Chromatin accessibility of fibrotic mesenchymal cell populations in SSc-ILD
  Given the significant chromatin accessibility changes between SSc and control fibroblasts, we subclustered fibroblast, smooth muscle, and pericyte populations (n=13,766 nuclei; Fig. 2A), identifying 10 fibroblast populations, including 4 adventitial, 2 alveolar, a CXCL2hi fibroblast, and 3 myofibroblast-related clusters (myofibroblast, inflammatory myofibroblast, and activated myofibroblast). All myofibroblast clusters were expanded in SSc-ILD, along with smooth muscle cells and pericytes (Fig. 2B–C, Fig. S6A), consistent with prior scRNA-seq analyses [12], and reflective of both severe ILD and the group 3 pulmonary hypertension present in these patients.

  We compared SSc-ILD fibroblasts to controls, SSc-ILD myofibroblasts vs SSc non-myogenic fibroblasts (including the adventitial, alveolar, and CXCL2hi populations), and activated myofibroblasts to other fibroblasts to robustly investigate the DEGs in these fibroblast populations (Fig. S6C, S7). Activated myofibroblasts exhibited the highest expression of myofibroblast hallmark genes including CTHRC1, POSTN, and COL8A1 (Fig. 2D, Fig. S6C) and were associated with profibrotic pathways (Fig. S6D, E). Forty-two DEGs were upregulated in common in SSc fibroblasts, SSc myofibroblasts, and activated myofibroblasts, including POSTN, TWIST1, and COL8A1 (Fig. S7).

  In fibroblast clusters, 6,002 DARs were more accessible in SSc vs 6,693 in control fibroblasts (Fig. 2E, Data file S1) with Cell migration and Collagen metabolic processes amongst the top upregulated pathways (Fig. 2F). Of the 671 DARs more accessible in both SSc fibroblasts and activated myofibroblasts (Fig. S8D), 96 were annotated to upregulated genes for this comparison, including COMP, COL8A1, and TWIST1. Many shared DARs occurred in genes previously related to fibrosis such as COL8A1, FBN2 and STAT3 (Fig. 2G).

  Mapping TF motifs in OCRs of SSc vs control fibroblasts revealed that AP-1, RUNX, and EGR families had the greatest average difference motif activity, while C/EBP motifs showed more activity in control fibroblast OCRs (Fig. 2H). As related TFs often share nearly equivalent motifs, enriched TFs with adjusted p-value <0.05 and absolute average difference of 0.5 or greater were ranked based on the top score for each TF family.


### Enhanced binding of AP-1, RUNX and EGR transcription factors by fibrotic fibroblasts
  We further analyzed the snATAC-seq data using ChromBPNet, a deep learning tool, to detect TF action at base pair resolution [10]. To ensure sufficient read depth and Tn5 bias model performance for ChromBPNet analyses, we performed this modeling using aggregate SSc-ILD versus control fibroblasts. ChromBPNet generated single-base pair contribution scores, indicating the strength of each nucleotide in determining chromatin accessibility and thus inferring TF binding. Peaks were called from short DNA sequences (seqlets), annotated by TF-MoDISco [15] and JASPAR TF motifs [23] and were used to generate cluster-specific aggregate contribution scores (Fig. 3A). Remarkably, the seqlets inferred de novo by ChromBPNet as showing TF binding were consistent with known binding for several families of TFs and showed substantial differences in seqlet numbers between control and SSc-ILD fibroblasts for AP-1, RUNX, EGR, and C/EBP TF families (Fig. 3B).

  The most frequently identified de novo seqlet in SSc and control fibroblast peaks matched the consensus AP-1 binding site, showing both unique and overlapping AP-1 contribution score products in control and SSc-ILD fibroblasts (Fig. 3C). We then clustered seqlets and visualized the average contribution weight matrix for each cluster (Fig. 3D–E, Fig. S9A–B). Although scores were higher in nearly every cluster for SSc fibroblasts, cluster 7 had distinctly higher scores in SSc, while cluster 8 had higher scores in control fibroblasts. Pathway analysis of AP-1 cluster 7 seqlets annotated to nearest genes indicated stronger AP-1 binding in genes involved in Cell junction organization including CDH8, TNC, and ITGB3 (Fig. 3G, H). The computational process of ChromBPNet in refining the pseudobulk ATAC-seq data is shown in analysis of ADAM12 (Fig. 3F), a metalloprotease whose expression is increased in lung and skin myofibroblasts[24], correlates with the severity of skin disease[25], and predicts response to anti-TGF-β therapy in SSc skin[26]. A strong TF footprint for AP-1 is identified in intron 11 of ADAM12 in SSC fibroblasts. Similarly, a duplicate AP-1 site was detected upstream of the transcription start site of CTHRC1, a marker of pathogenic fibroblasts in IPF and murine fibrosis [8, 19] (Fig. 3I). We confirmed the activities of these AP-1 CREs by cloning of the regions with and without mutation of the AP-1 motif into minimal promoter-reporter plasmids. These assays verified the activity of the AP-1 binding sites depicted in CREs of ADAM12 and CTHRC (Fig. 3J).

  RUNX, EGR, C/EBP, and TEAD motifs were analyzed in an analogous manner. C/EBP-binding seqlets were decreased in SSc fibroblasts, most strikingly in cluster 8 (Fig. S9C–D). RUNX footprints were present in a small number of seqlets, yet markedly increased in SSc fibroblasts (Fig. 4A, Fig S10A–B) with enriched pathways linked to genes involved in Anatomical Structure Morphogenesis (cluster 9) and Regulation of Signal Transduction and Anatomical Structure Development (cluster 10; Fig. 4B–E). Other genes linked to lung fibrosis with upregulated expression in SSc fibroblasts and enhanced RUNX binding included FHL2, PAPPA and ECM2, as well as CTTNB2, a gene linked to dermal fibrosis (Fig. S10C–F).

  EGR motifs in clusters 1, 2, 9, and 10 had the greatest contribution score increases in SSc fibroblasts and were associated with Actin-filament Based Processes and Cell Morphogenesis (Fig. 4F–H, Fig. S10G–H). Increased EGR footprinting was present in ADAM12 (Fig. 4I). Both the RUNX motif in MYOA3 and the EGR motif in ADAM12 showed enhancer activity decreased with mutation of the binding sites, confirming the biological importance of these CREs in regulating these genes and inferring more generally the robust detection by ChromBPNet of enhancers elements and their critical TF binding sites (Fig. 4J). TEAD footprints were more evenly distributed to CREs in SSc and control fibroblasts (Fig. S11A–B). TEAD cluster 8 contained higher contribution scores in SSc fibroblasts, and genes annotated to this cluster were associated with Regulation of Actin-Filament Based Processes and Cardiac Muscle Tissue Growth, consistent with the TEAD TFs known role in muscle differentiation (Fig. S11C–D) [27].


### Altered Chromatin accessibility of macrophage populations in SSc-ILD
  As in prior scRNA-seq studies, we identified macrophage clusters implicated in driving fibrosis in SSc-ILD and IPF by increased expression of SPP1 and other marker genes (Fig. 1A, 5A) [12, 21]. We combined the SPP1hi macrophages to compare DARs between SSc-ILD and control lungs (Fig. 5B, Data file S3). Upregulated pathways included Cell-matrix Adhesion, PDGFR Signaling, and ERK1/2 Cascade (Fig. 5C). Top DARs in SSc SPP1hi macrophages were annotated to CHIT3, PSD3, and PRC (Fig. 5E). ChromVAR identified bHLH-ZIP (USF1, USF2, MXI1, MITF, TFEB), EGR and NFAT TFs as highly enriched in SSc, while CTCF motifs were enriched in control SPP1hi macrophages (Fig. 5D).

  ChromBPNet and TF-MoDISco analysis indicated that Ets-related binding sites were the most common motifs showing TF binding in SPP1hi macrophages, followed by C/EBP, AP-1, and CTCF (Fig. 6A, S15A–D). Comparing SSc SPP1hi macrophage seqlets to control inferred increased binding to AP-1, bHLH-ZIP, and EGR motifs. AP-1 sites in cluster 7 had distinctly higher contribution scores in SSc (Fig. 6B, Fig. S14 A–B) and were linked to genes in Cell-matrix Adhesion and Cell Migration pathways (Fig. 6C–D). In contrast, cluster 8 had higher AP-1 seqlet scores in controls and was enriched for ERBB and JAK-STAT signaling pathways (Fig. S14 C–D). The AP-1 site in LOX2 showed enhancer activity (Fig. 6F). bHLH-ZIP footprinting showed increased seqlets in SSc in cluster 9 (Fig. 6E, Fig. S14 E–F) linked to Metalloendopeptidase Activity and Autophagy pathways (Fig. 6G–H). Notably, these analyses inferred increased binding by SSc SPP1hi macrophages to a duplicated bHLH-ZIP motif in the proximal promoter of CCL18 (Fig. 6J), a chemokine highly upregulated by SSc SPP1hi macrophages [28]. Both duplicated bHLH-ZIP binding sites showed enhancer activity (Fig. 6I).


### Gene regulatory network construction
  To further examine the dysregulated gene regulatory networks of fibroblasts and SPP1hi macrophages, we constructed TF-CRE-gene linkages from our multiome data (Fig. 7A). To better account for the sparsity of single-nucleus genomic data and to utilize multiomic representations delineating cellular states, we first inferred latent representations using hierarchical causal modeling for single cell multi-omics data (HALO) and constructed metacells of paired RNA-seq and ATAC-seq from our multiome chemistry dataset (n=6 SSc, 7 Control samples) [16]. We then utilized our larger independent scRNA-seq dataset (n=17 SSc, 13 control lungs) [29] to identify DEGs and CREs (both promoters and more distal elements) occurring within 250 kilobases of the DEGs narrowed to DARs for the comparison of interest. Enriched TF motifs within these DARs were identified through motif matching, followed by TF-CRE-gene linkages.

  For the fibroblast nuclei we first re-clustered the multiome samples based on HALO representations, followed by annotation, metacell construction, and identification of cell states enriched in SSc fibroblasts using neighborhood-level differential abundance testing (Fig. 7B–C, Fig. S17A) [30]. RNA velocity was applied to assess cellular dynamics of the myofibroblast population [31]. Velocity projections indicated that myofibroblasts may arise from both alveolar and adventitial fibroblast (cluster 1) populations (Fig. 7D). In comparing SSc to control fibroblasts, AP-1, EGR, YY1, and ETS-related family TFs had the largest positive difference in motif activity (Fig. 7E) and have increased motif activity (along with upregulated gene expression of their target genes) along the myofibroblast differentiation trajectories implicated by RNA velocity (Fig. S17D–G). Consistent with ChromBPNet inferred binding, members of the AP-1 family with significantly enriched motif activity (JUND, JUNB, JDP2, FOSL1) were linked to 280 unique DEGs (243 upregulated, 37 downregulated) for the SSc vs control fibroblasts, including the upregulated genes CTHRC1, ADAM12, SPP1 and TGFBI (Data file S4). Enriched EGR TFs were linked to 320 unique DEGs (273 upregulated, 46 downregulated) including the upregulated genes ADAM12, IGFBP2, and SPP1.

  Myeloid cells were examined in a similar manner. SPP1hi macrophages were increased in SSc, while non-classical monocytes were increased in control samples (Fig. 7F–G, Fig. S18D). Velocity projections predicted that SPP1hi macrophages arise from both the FABP4hi macrophage and monocyte populations. (Fig. 7H, Fig. S18E). EGR, AP-1, bHLH-ZIP, and Ets-related TFs had the largest positive difference in motif activity (Fig. 7I; Data file S4). Members of the bHLH-ZIP family with significantly enriched motif activity (MITF, MXI1, and TFEB) were linked to 64 unique DEGs (53 upregulated, 11 downregulated) for SSc vs control SPP1hi macrophages, including the upregulated genes PAQR5, MREG, RTN4R, and APOE. Enriched AP-1 TFs FOSL1 and JDP2 were similarly linked to 69 unique DEGs (54 upregulated, 15 downregulated) including the upregulated genes CHIT1, CHI3L1, MMP7, RTN4R, and CDH23.


## DISCUSSION
  Here, we delineate the dynamic chromatin changes and altered TF binding in fibrotic lung disease. Our study provides comprehensive insight into TF regulation in the human lung, focusing on the TFs regulating the transition of fibroblasts and macrophages to profibrotic states [29, 32]. Applying the deep learning model ChromBPNet, we detected TF binding at single nucleotide resolution, indicating known TF motifs and inferring their altered binding in profibrotic fibroblasts and macrophages in an unbiased manner. Although initial analyses highlighted the expansion of myofibroblast subsets in SSc-ILD, TF modeling was performed on aggregated fibroblasts to ensure robust ChromBPNet performance. We confirmed the activity of several of these enhancers in key regulated genes, inferring the generalizability of our observations. HALO metacells enabled robust trajectory analysis and linked TF and CREs to regulated genes, supporting the TF binding indicated by ChromBPNet. Most importantly, our data inform and markedly extend numerous in vitro studies over many years that speak to potential roles of TFs in fibrosis, here confirming direct in vivo roles for several TF families in promoting human lung fibrosis.

  In SSc fibroblasts, we observed enriched binding to AP-1 motifs and high contribution score footprints of AP-1 binding in multiple fibrosis-related genes. AP-1 is comprised of homo- and hetero-dimers of Jun, Fos, Atf, and Maf TF families, several members of which are implicated in driving fibrosis [33, 34]. Fra-2 transgenic mice develop skin fibrosis, ILD, and vascular disease [35]. AP-1 is upregulated by TGF-β in SSc dermal fibrosis, with reduced collagen synthesis in vitro and prevention of dermal fibrosis in vivo following treatment with a selective AP-1 inhibitor [36]. Our data indicate that in SSc-ILD fibroblasts AP-1 regulates key myofibroblast marker genes, including CTHRC1 and ADAM12. CTHRC1 is a hallmark myofibroblast marker in fibrotic human lung diseases ranging from IPF to COVID-19 [8, 37, 38]. Surprisingly its deletion aggravates bleomycin induced lung fibrosis and cholestatic liver fibrosis, possibly as a counterregulatory mechanism inhibiting TGF-beta, although its deletion is protective in carbon tetrachloride models of liver fibrosis [39–41]. These findings suggest that while CTHRC1 may exert feedback inhibition on profibrotic signaling, its AP-1 promoted upregulation occurs within a broader fibroblast activation program that ultimately supports the fibrotic response. Deletion of ADAM12, another hallmark gene of myofibroblast differentiation, blocks the development of profibrotic progenitors [42]. Increased accessibility at CTHRC1 and ADAM12 cREs in pathogenic fibroblasts suggests active transcriptional regulation, but these likely reflect nodes within complex pro-fibrotic networks rather than discrete signaling pathways.

  The significant redistribution of accessible AP-1 sites across the genome, and the identification of clusters of seqlets with AP-1 motifs showing increased binding in control fibroblasts highlights the complex action of this TF family. Several mechanisms are likely in play. Specific AP-1 TFs may increase, while others decrease accessibility in SSc-ILD fibroblasts [33]. Other TFs may influence binding to AP-1 sites, though our data did not indicate consistent binding of TFs immediately adjacent to the AP-1 sites. More likely altered AP-1 binding reflects distal TF interactions, DNA loop formation, and/or epigenetic changes at the level of histone or DNA modifications. Determining the molecular mechanisms driving AP-1 binding differences between healthy and SSc-ILD fibroblasts will clarify determinants of AP-1 action, the resulting gene dysregulation, and strategies for intervention.

  RUNX footprinting was significantly increased in SSc and appeared in few control fibroblast OCRs. Inhibition of RUNX1 in vitro blocks myofibroblast differentiation [43], RUNX footprinting is enriched in SSc dermal fibroblasts [44] and RUNX1 has been implicated in myofibroblast differentiation in SSc skin [24]. RUNX2 is upregulated in both SSc-ILD and IPF fibroblasts, and conditional deletion of RUNX2 in alveolar fibroblasts reduces the development of pathological fibroblasts and attenuates fibrosis in the murine intratracheal bleomycin model [45]. Seqlets showing increased RUNX binding in genes upregulated in SSc-ILD fibroblasts included FHL2, ECM2 and PAPPA. Notably, FHL2 is one of 12 genes overexpressed in both rat and human lung fibrosis [46]. FHL2 binds to focal adhesions and transmits mechanical signals to regulate gene expression [47] and its pharmacologic inhibition by Arbutin ameliorates rat lung fibrosis [48]. ECM2 and PAPPA are markers of IPF and IPF severity [49]. Senescent cells secrete increased PAPP-A, suggesting a role in IPF [49, 50]. ECM2 deletion inhibits skeletal muscle-derived satellite cell differentiation [51], suggesting a possible role in myofibroblast differentiation. Small molecule inhibitors for both RUNX-DNA binding and interaction of RUNX proteins with their shared cofactor CBFB [52, 53] as well as its significant post-transcriptional regulation by ubiquitin-proteasome degradation[54] offer potential therapeutic approaches to inhibiting RUNX activity.

  EGR1 is implicated in fibrotic signals from IL-13 and TGF-β, and is rapidly induced by type I interferons [55–57]. EGR1 deletion inhibits wound healing and myofibroblast differentiation, while overexpression enhances wound repair [58] and upregulates genes associated with wound healing and fibrosis [59]. EGR1 deletion also protects against renal fibrosis [60], but aggravates experimental liver fibrosis [61]. EGR2 and EGR3 both mediate TGF-β dependent signals including collagen expression and myofibroblast differentiation but regulate distinct genes[62]. Previous in vitro studies indicate SSc dermal fibroblasts have enhanced EGR1 accessibility [62], suggesting EGR1 may play parallel roles in myofibroblast differentiation in the skin and lungs.

  SSc SPP1hi macrophages showed increased binding by bHLH-ZIP, AP-1, and EGR TFs. BHLH-ZIP TFs demonstrated the most highly significant motif enrichment, and the largest increase in SSc seqlets, suggesting a distinct regulatory program in SPP1hi macrophages compared to fibroblasts. The bHLH-ZIP TFs include USF1 and USF2, as well as MiT/TFE members MITF, TFEB, TFEC, and TFE3 [63]. We previously inferred TFEB as a putative regulator of pro-fibrotic macrophages in SSc-ILD based on SCENIC analyses of scRNA-seq regulon activities and motif recognition in open chromatin analyzing a much smaller snATAC-seq dataset (2 samples) [29]. However, since the bHLH-ZIP TFs share the same consensus motif, we cannot clearly distinguish whether TFEB or another bHLH-ZIP TF is most important in regulating SSc SPP1hi macrophages. The enriched duplicate bHLH-ZIP footprint in proximal CCL18 is notable, as elevated CCL18 is associated with SSc-ILD severity and performs well as a diagnostic biomarker of SSc-ILD [64]. CCL18 is decreased in the serum and skin of patients with SSc following IL-6 inhibition [65], and is downregulated by JAK inhibitors [66]. CCL18 increases collagen production by lung fibroblasts [67], and genetic variability in CCL18 affects gene expression and mortality in IPF [68]. bHLH-ZIP TFs may represent a key influence on the feedback loop between fibrotic macrophages and fibroblasts, warranting further investigation.

  Upstream regulators of the transcription factor dynamics we observed likely includes cytokines previously implicated in SSc. As discussed, AP-1 and EGR participate in TGF-β signaling, as does RUNX[43, 69]. In fibroblasts, additional profibrotic mediators including IL-6, IL-4/IL-13, PDGF, and TNF-α converge on signaling cascades that activate AP-1, RUNX, EGR, and C/EBP as key regulators of extracellular matrix gene expression[70]. Similarly, in macrophages IL-1β and TNF-α promote MAPK-AP-1-dependent enhancer accessibility, while IL-4/IL-13 signaling induces bHLH-ZIP TFs, supporting alternative activation and lysosomal remodeling[71–73]. Type I interferons (IFNs) may act as upstream regulators that epigenetically prime cells for enhanced responsiveness to profibrotic cytokines and transcriptional activation[74]. Prior transcriptomic studies have shown that type I IFN signatures are enriched in fibroblasts, endothelial cells, and myeloid populations in SSc, and we previously found that type I IFNs are upregulated in SSc-ILD compared with IPF[11]. AP-1, RUNX, and EGR family members have each been linked to type I IFN pathways[57, 75, 76], suggesting that the regulatory modules we identify may integrate both direct profibrotic signaling and prior innate immune activation. In this framework, analogous to how prior IFN exposure shapes response to subsequent infections[74], IFN-mediated chromatin remodeling may epigenetically ‘prime’ macrophages and fibroblasts, enhancing their downstream AP-1, RUNX, and EGR action in response to TGF-β and IL-6 family cytokines.

  Endothelial cell activation and vasculopathy, hallmarks of early SSc-ILD, contribute to microvascular dysfunction and may promote fibrosis via impaired vascular barrier integrity, inflammatory microenvironment, and extracellular matrix protein deposition[77–79]. Consistent with this, we identified enrichment of vasculature-related pathways in SSc-ILD fibroblast and SPP1hi macrophage DARs, fibroblast AP-1 and RUNX seqlets regions, and SPP1hi macrophage AP-1 seqlet regions. Specifically, endothelial activation may reinforce the implicated signaling circuits through VEGF and PDGF pathways that engage AP-1 and RUNX motifs[77, 80, 81]. Rather than ruling out any previously implicated cytokines in SSc-ILD, our data helps delineate the specific cis-regulatory programs through which these cytokines are acting in SSc-ILD.

  This large snATAC-sequencing study of adult healthy and fibrotic lungs provides a rich resource on chromatin accessibility in the human lungs. We utilized deep learning models to uncover intricate sequence rules governing TF binding, providing profound new insights into transcriptional programs regulated by CREs in fibroblasts and macrophages. As our study used explanted tissue from patients with SSc-ILD undergoing lung transplantation, it may not reflect changes in mild disease or the broader SSc-ILD population. Due to the limited availability of SSc-ILD lung tissue and the younger median age of our donor control population, samples could not be fully age- and sex-matched or compared by autoantibody profiles. As additional snATAC-seq lung cohorts become available, integrated analyses will allow for sex and age differences to be systematically investigated. As OCRs were annotated by least base-pairs distance, not all annotations may reflect the regulated gene, as CREs can act through long-range chromatin interactions. While our study focused on OCRs and TF binding sites, additional non-coding regulatory mechanisms including long non-coding RNAs, microRNAs, and transposable elements may also influence gene expression.

  In summary, we identified chromatin accessibility and TF binding changes that underpin fibroblasts and SPP1hi macrophages differentiation in SSc-ILD, highlighting cis-regulatory changes responsible for the diseased phenotypes. Functional reporter assays confirmed the predicted TF dependence for several key regulatory regions; however, comprehensive experimental validation across all identified loci and the phenotypic changes resulting from loss of such activity remains an important future direction. Our study validates and extends to human fibrotic lung disease numerous prior in vitro studies and in vivo observations, pointing to the TFs promoting lung fibrosis and providing a roadmap to understanding the GRNs modulating cell differentiation in fibrotic lung disease.
