A series of fourteen N-(1-ferrocenyl-2-(methylthio)ethyl)anilines was prepared using a simple synthetic route. Their antimicrobial activities were evaluated by assays against seven bacterial and five fungal strains. Seven derivatives displayed moderate antibacterial activity, while two compounds exhibited the most notable antifungal effects against Aspergillus brasiliensis and Trichoderma harzianum, with minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) of 1.20 mM. The antioxidant potential was evaluated using DPPH(*) and ABTS(*+) radical scavenging assays. Compound 5b exhibited the strongest activity in the DPPH(*) assay, with an IC(50) value of 55.99 muM, which was significantly higher than that of the reference standard ascorbic acid (IC(50) = 168.58 muM). Pronounced activity in neutralizing ABTS(*+) radicals was a dominant feature of nearly the entire series, with IC(50) values for most compounds below the lowest tested concentration. The presence of the ferrocene moiety enabled DNA binding studies using cyclic voltammetry (CV) and hydrodynamic linear sweep voltammetry (HLSV). CV investigations indicated different diffusion coefficient values for the derivatives in the absence and presence of DNA, suggesting the formation of aggregates between the synthesized derivatives and DNA. The interactions were confirmed by HLSV, which proved to be an easily performed and effective electrochemical technique. Molecular docking was used to characterize the interaction of the synthesized ferrocene-containing compounds with DNA, and calculations indicate groove binding as the dominant form of interaction. Since antioxidant properties and protein tyrosine kinase (PTK) inhibition are closely connected, the synthesized compounds were also docked into the active site of beta protein tyrosine kinase to explore their inhibition potential. The results indicate three derivatives with relatively high docking scores and the ability to inhibit PTK by binding to the key amino acid center Tyr 505.
