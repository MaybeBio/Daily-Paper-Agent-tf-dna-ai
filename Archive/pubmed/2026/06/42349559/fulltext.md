In this study, Polygala crotalarioides, a traditional Chinese medicinal herb, was investigated. Proteins were extracted and hydrolyzed to obtain peptides with high antioxidant activity. The results showed that, compared with the unhydrolyzed protein, the soluble fraction after enzymatic hydrolysis exhibited a significant increase in DPPH radical scavenging activity from 21.7% to 76.2%, accompanied by changes in the relative content of amino acids. The purified peptide fraction markedly alleviated H(2)O(2)-induced oxidative damage in PC12 cells. Compared with the model group, this fraction reduced LDH release and MDA levels to 64.4% and 56.1%, respectively, while restoring SOD activity from 45.3% to 79.1%, indicating that its protective effect is mediated by the enhancement of endogenous antioxidant capacity. Combining LC-MS/MS analysis with molecular docking, three novel bioactive peptide sequences were identified, among which WGFLF was selected for cellular validation. At a concentration of 1.6 mg/mL, WGFLF increased the viability of H(2)O(2)-damaged PC12 cells from 48.6% to 83.24%. Further RT-qPCR analysis revealed that this peptide exerts cytoprotective effects against oxidative stress through modulation of the Keap1-Nrf2 signaling pathway. This study provides important theoretical support for the development of Polygala crotalarioides.
