# Multi-omics reveals fibroblast subtypes with distinct roles in inflammatory bowel disease


## Background
  Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is a chronic inflammatory disorder of the gastrointestinal tract involving complex interactions among epithelial, immune, and stromal components. Fibroblasts are key regulators of intestinal inflammation and fibrosis, but their heterogeneity, distribution, and therapeutic potential remain unclear.


## Methods
  We systematically identified and characterized intestinal fibroblast subtypes in IBD while exploring the intercellular communication between fibroblasts and immune cells through the integration of single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) and spatial transcriptomics (ST) analyses. Functional gene signatures derived from fibroblast subtypes were applied to bulk RNA-seq datasets to predict therapeutic responses using logistic regression and random forest models. Concurrently, pertinent findings were validated in the intestinal tissues and primary fibroblasts of patients with IBD using multiplex immunohistochemistry (mIHC), western blotting, and quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR).


## Results
  We identified 10 distinct stromal subpopulations comprising two fibroblast subtypes strongly associated with IBD pathology: ICAM1+ inflammatory fibroblasts (iFibro_ICAM1) and FAP+ matrix-producing fibroblasts (mFibro_FAP). These subsets demonstrate distinct transcription factor activity, metabolic reprogramming, and spatial proximity to immune infiltrates, and they undergo remodeling within the inflammatory microenvironment of IBD. Pseudotime trajectory analysis suggested that the iFibro_ICAM1 subset potentially represents a precursor state. CellChat analysis revealed enhanced intercellular communication between fibroblast subsets and immune cells in ileal and colorectal IBD tissues. Furthermore, a machine learning model based on gene expression signatures derived from these fibroblasts effectively predicted poor therapeutic responses to anti-TNF treatments in patients with IBD.


## Conclusions
  This study provides a comprehensive characterization of fibroblast heterogeneity in IBD, highlighting the crucial roles of inflammatory and fibrotic fibroblast subsets as key mediators of immune-stromal interactions and potential predictors of therapeutic responsiveness.

## Introduction
  Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), encompassing Crohn’s disease (CD) and ulcerative colitis (UC), is a chronic gastrointestinal disorder involving complex interactions among epithelial, immune, and stromal compartments [1, 2]. Despite the clinical success of biological agents, a substantial proportion of patients fail to achieve sustained remission, frequently progressing to complications such as strictures and obstructions [3]. While dysregulated fibroblasts are known drivers of IBD pathogenesis, particularly in the context of intestinal fibrosis (IF), prior research has predominantly focused on their terminal fibrotic roles [4–6]. A comprehensive atlas detailing fibroblast behavior during active inflammation remains elusive, and a systematic understanding of how these subpopulations remodel during disease progression is still lacking. Therefore, delineating fibroblast heterogeneity across the spectrum of IBD onset and progression is essential for identifying novel therapeutic avenues.

  Fibroblasts are increasingly recognized as central orchestrators of tissue inflammation, matrix remodeling, and immune recruitment, exhibiting profound anatomical and functional heterogeneity along the intestinal crypt-villus axis [7–9]. Recent single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) studies have identified specialized fibroblast subsets, such as inflammation-associated fibroblasts (IAFs) in UC and the GIMATS module in ileal CD (iCD), which are highly enriched in inflamed mucosa [10–13]. These activated populations, characterized by specific cytokines-chemokines profiles and distinct gene modules, have been strongly linked to histological severity, treatment resistance, and fibroblast activation [13, 14]. Despite these advancements, a comprehensive spatial atlas characterizing fibroblast heterogeneity and their diverse intercellular communication networks across different IBD subtypes have not been fully elucidated, limiting the identification of precise fibroblast-targeted therapies.

  To address these challenges, we integrated scRNA-seq and spatial transcriptomics (ST) to profile 165,618 cells from 75 ileal and colonic biopsies encompassing CD, UC, and healthy controls. This approach allowed us to high-resolution map the reconfiguration of fibroblast subpopulations and their metabolic signatures. By analyzing pseudotime trajectories and spatial co-localization, we identified distinct stromal remodeling patterns unique to CD and UC. Furthermore, we demonstrated that these fibroblast-derived signatures possess robust predictive power for therapeutic outcomes, offering a potential framework for patient stratification and the precision management of IBD.


## Methods

### Single-cell transcriptomic data
  We analysed single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-Seq) data from 75 ileal and colorectal biopsy samples across four GEO datasets (GSE134809 [13], GSE202052 [15], GSE214695 [16], GSE231993 [17]). The cohort included patients with CD, UC, and NC. Self-control non-inflamed samples were used as NC only when healthy control samples from the corresponding anatomical region were unavailable, therefore 4 paired non-inflamed UC samples from GSE231993 were excluded from the final integrated analysis to keep the number of IBD and NC samples approximately balanced. Processed gene expression matrices generated with Cell Ranger pipelines were retrieved from GEO. Computational analyses were performed with the Seurat package (version 5.2.1) [18].


### Data quality control and doublet removal
  Quality control was based on mitochondrial gene expression in individual cells. Immune cells with mitochondrial gene expression > 5% were excluded. Epithelial, endothelial, and stromal cells with mitochondrial gene expression > 25% were also excluded. Cells co-expressing lineage-exclusive marker genes were classified as doublets and removed.


### Cell type annotation and abundance calculation
  High-quality cells passing QC underwent dimensionality reduction and unsupervised clustering using Seurat. Major cell populations were classified (T, B, plasma, myeloid, stromal, endothelial, and epithelial) using marker genes. Stromal cells underwent batch correction with Harmony (v1.2.3) and re-clustering [19]. Stromal subclusters were annotated by differentially expressed genes, and re-evaluated using canonical lineage markers to distinguish true stromal populations from potential doublets or ambient RNA contamination.

### Differential gene expression, functional enrichment, and metabolite analysis
  Differentially expressed and marker genes were identified using Presto (v1.0.0). Gene sets were analyzed for KEGG pathway enrichment with clusterProfiler (v4.10.0) [20]. Genes/pathways with p < 0.05 were considered significant. Metabolic flux and metabolite accumulation were inferred with scFEA (v1.1.2) per standard workflow [21].


### Transcription factor and pseudotime analysis
  Transcription factor activity in stromal cells was quantified with DecoupleR (v2.9.7) [22]. Mean activity scores were calculated for each stromal subcluster. Stromal pseudotime trajectories were reconstructed with monocle3 (v1.3.1) [23]. Analysis focused on the pro-inflammatory fibroblast skeleton.


### Cell-cell communication analysis
  Communication networks between stromal subclusters and other cell types were inferred in IBD (CD/UC) vs. NC using CellChat (version 2.1.2) [24]. Low-strength interactions were captured using the truncatedMean algorithm (trim = 0.1).


### Spatial transcriptomic analysis
  Because no comparable open-access ileal spatial transcriptomic dataset was available, analysis was restricted to colorectal tissues and conducted on nine colorectal samples (CD, UC, NC) from GSE234713 [16]. Pre-processed matrices and spatial coordinates were retrieved from GEO and analyzed with Seurat. ST spots were annotated using marker genes from scRNA-seq. Stromal cells were sub-annotated with Seurat addModuleScore using top 10 stromal markers from scRNA-seq. Each spot was assigned to the subcluster with the highest score. Cell type density variations in ST data were evaluated with the same Ro/e and Chi-Square methods as scRNA-seq. Densities of major cell types within 150 μm of each stromal subcluster were computed.


### Bulk transcriptomics and prognostic analysis
  Bulk RNA-seq analysis included 724 ileal and colorectal tissue samples from patients with IBD across seven GEO datasets (GSE12251 [25], GSE16879 [26], GSE23597 [27], GSE234736 [28], GSE206285 [29], GSE207022 [30], GSE171770 [31]). The cohort comprised 121 patients treated with anti-TNF, 95 with anti-integrin (vedolizumab), 421 with anti-IL-12/23 (ustekinumab), and 87 with anti-IL-6 (olamkicept) therapy, all of whom were baseline/pre-treatment samples with treatment-response annotations; any post-treatment samples were excluded. Gene signatures from scRNA-seq stromal subclusters were used to calculate enrichment scores with ssGSEA (GSVA package v1.50.0) [32]. Logistic regression models, constructed using the pROC package (version 1.18.5), were developed to predict treatment response based on these stromal signature scores within each disease subtype (CD and UC), followed by the generation of receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves [33]. Then the randomForest package (v4.7) identified the top 10 predictive genes from each stromal subcluster signature within each disease subtype (CD and UC). Novel enrichment scores were computed exclusively based on the top ten genes using ssGSEA, followed by the development of optimized logistic regression models and subsequent ROC curve analysis.


### Study approval
  The study complied with ethical regulations and was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University (Guangzhou, China; Approval [2025] No.655). Intestinal tissues were obtained from patients with IBD and healthy individuals after obtaining informed consent.


### Isolation and culture of primary human intestinal fibroblasts
  Primary fibroblasts were isolated using a previously described protocol [34]. Briefly, surgically resected intestinal mucosal tissues from patients with IBD were washed thrice with Hanks’ balanced salt solution (HBSS). Colonic tissues were minced into small fragments and digested in digestion buffer at 37 °C with shaking. The resulting suspension was filtered through a 100-µm strainer and centrifuged at 500 g for 5 min to pellet the cells. The cell pellet was resuspended in primary culture medium consisting of DMEM (Gibco) supplemented with 10% heat-inactivated fetal bovine serum (FBS) and antibiotics. Cells were incubated at 37 °C until fibroblasts adhered to the culture plate and were passaged upon reaching 90% confluency. Fibroblast identity was confirmed by morphology and collagen I expression. Cells between passages two and four were used in the experiments.


### Multiplex immunohistochemistry
  Multiplex immunohistochemistry (mIHC) was performed using the BeyoTSA™ Kit (Beyotime) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Paraffin-embedded tissue sections were prepared and incubated with primary antibodies at 4 °C overnight. Subsequently, sections were incubated with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibodies for 30 min at room temperature. After each round of the horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-based reaction, antibodies were removed by treatment with stripping buffer (Beyotime, P1455) for 10 min. Finally, sections were mounted with DAPI (Invitrogen) for nuclear counterstaining. Images were acquired using the TissueFAXS Spectra Imaging System. All the antibodies used are listed in Table S2.


### Quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction
  Total RNA was extracted from biopsy tissues using TRIzol reagent. cDNA was synthesized using a Transcript First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Roche, Basel, Switzerland). Quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) was performed on a LightCycler 480 (Roche) per manufacturer’s protocol. Relative quantification was calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method. All the primers used are listed in Table S1.


### Western blotting (WB)
  WB was conducted as previously described [35]. Briefly, proteins were extracted in RIPA lysis buffer (Sigma-Aldrich, USA). Following measurement of protein concentration, the protein samples were subjected to sodium dodecyl sulfate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and then transferred to polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes. After blocking with 5% milk, membranes were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies (ICAM1, FAP, GAPDH), then with secondary antibodies. All antibodies used in this study are listed in Table S2. The immunoblots were visualized using the iBright™ FL1500 Imaging System. All the antibodies used are listed in Table S2.


### Statistical analysis and visualization
  Analyses were performed in GraphPad Prism 9, R (v4.3.3), and Python (v3.18.18). Data visualization was performed using ggplot2 (version v0.1.2) and pheatmap (version v1.0.1) packages in R. Statistical significance was denoted as follows: *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001, ****p < 0.0001.


## Results

### Single-cell landscape of intestinal stromal cells in IBD and controls
  We analyzed scRNA-seq data from 75 ileal and colorectal biopsy samples, including those from patients with CD, UC, and NC, across four GEO datasets to construct a comprehensive single-cell atlas of the intestinal mucosa. In total, 165,618 high-quality cells were identified and annotated into seven major lineages: T, B, plasma, myeloid, stromal, endothelial, and epithelial (Fig. 1A-B, Figure S1). The distribution of cell types across different anatomical regions and disease conditions was systematically evaluated (Fig. 1C-D). Notably, the abundance of stromal cells was significantly decreased in IBD tissues compared to that in NC controls, as determined by chi-square analysis (Fig. 1E). To further investigate stromal cell alterations in IBD, we performed a sub-clustering analysis of 7,748 high-quality stromal cells and identified 10 distinct subpopulations based on marker gene expression profiles (Table S3, Fig. 1F-G). These subpopulations included four pro-inflammatory fibroblast subsets (iFibro_CCL13, iFibro_CCN1, iFibro_C3, and iFibro_ICAM1), matrix producing fibroblast subset (mFibro_FAP), cycling fibroblasts (cycFibro_MKI67), telocytes subset (Telocyte_PDGFRA) smooth muscle cells (SMC_MYH11), pericytes (Pericyte_RGS5), and enteric glial cells (Glial_S100B). The marker gene expression patterns of each sub-cluster are shown in Figure S2. Comparative chi-square analysis across CD, UC, and NC samples stratified by ileal and colonic origin revealed that the iFibro_ICAM1, Telocyte_PDGFRA, and mFibro_FAP subsets were significantly expanded in IBD tissues (Fig. 1H-I).

### Functional heterogeneity and differentiation hierarchy of intestinal fibroblasts
  Further analysis of the proportional dynamics of fibroblast subpopulations in IBD and normal NC cohorts revealed a significant expansion of iFibro_ICAM1 and mFibro_FAP in the intestinal tissues of IBD patients (Fig. 2A). To characterize the functional diversity of the fibroblast subsets, we performed KEGG pathway enrichment analysis of their respective marker genes (Table S4). The iFibro_ICAM1 subset demonstrated significant enrichment in the TNF, NF-κB, and IL-17 signaling pathways, confirming its role in inflammatory activation. The mFibro_FAP subset was enriched in pathways associated with protein processing in the endoplasmic reticulum, which is consistent with its involvement in extracellular matrix synthesis. In contrast, the Telocyte_PDGFRA subset was enriched in the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway. In addition, the iFibro_CCN1 subset displayed enhanced oxidative phosphorylation (Fig. 2B). Metabolic flux analysis using scFEA revealed distinct metabolic profiles among the fibroblast subsets (Fig. 2C). Specifically, iFibro_ICAM1 was associated with elevated glutamate, serine, and glucose levels, suggesting a glycolytic metabolic phenotype. Conversely, mFibro_FAP consumed glucose-6-phosphate and accumulated 3-phosphoglycerate metabolites, previously implicated in inflammation [36–38]. To further investigate the regulatory mechanisms underlying fibroblast heterogeneity, we inferred transcription factor (TF) activity using decoupleR (Fig. 2D, Table S5). The iFibro_ICAM1 subset exhibited increased activity of inflammatory TFs, such as NFKBIZ, STAT2, and STAT1, whereas the mFibro_FAP subset was enriched for Endothelial PAS domain-containing protein 1 (EPAS1), a hypoxia-related TF. Pseudotime trajectory analysis using Monocle3 identified a putative developmental trajectory in which iFibro_ICAM1 likely functions as a progenitor population that differentiates into the Telocyte_PDGFRA and mFibro_FAP lineages (Fig. 2E). During the differentiation of iFibro_ICAM1 toward Telocyte_PDGFRA and mFibro_FAP, the expression levels of ICAM1 and ADAMDEC1 progressively decline, whereas those of PDGFRA and FAP steadily increase (Fig. 2G-H). This trajectory supports a model in which iFibro_ICAM1 acts as a bipotent precursor capable of inducing both inflammatory and fibrotic cell states in patients with IBD.

### Transcriptional and metabolic reprogramming of fibroblasts in IBD
  Differential expression analysis comparing fibroblasts from IBD and NC samples revealed significant upregulation of inflammatory mediators, including TNFRSF12A, CXCL2, CXCL3, CXCL8, and IL6 in IBD-derived fibroblasts (Fig. 3A). KEGG pathway enrichment analysis of these differentially expressed genes (DEGs) demonstrated significant enrichment of the TNF- and IL-17 signaling pathways in IBD fibroblasts (Fig. 3B, Table S6), which is consistent with a pro-inflammatory reprogramming signature. Among the fibroblast subpopulations, the iFibro_ICAM1 subset exhibited marked upregulation of TNFRSF12A, CXCL2, and CXCL8 (Fig. 3C), whereas the Telocyte_PDGFRA subset showed increased expression of complement-related genes such as C3 and CFD (Fig. 3D). Subpopulation-specific KEGG enrichment analyses further confirmed the activation of TNF signaling in iFibro_ICAM1, complement pathway activation in Telocyte_PDGFRA, and IL-17 signaling in both iFibro_ICAM1 and mFibro_FAP (Fig. 3E). Metabolically, the mFibro_FAP and iFibro_ICAM1 subsets exhibited the most pronounced metabolic reprogramming, with elevated levels of 3-phosphoglycerate and alanine in IBD samples (Fig. 3F). The remaining fibroblast subsets exhibited minimal metabolic alterations.

### Spatial organization of fibroblast subtypes in IBD
  To elucidate the alterations and distinctive cellular microenvironments associated with fibroblast subpopulations in IBD intestinal tissues, we conducted spatial transcriptomic analysis on 9 colorectal tissue samples (GSE234713) because publicly available spatial transcriptomic data were available only for colorectal tissues, yielding 482,277 high-quality spatial spots across 171 tissue Sect [16]. Major cell types and stromal subclusters were annotated using marker genes derived from scRNA-seq data (Fig. 4A-B, Figure S3). Notably, increased densities of iFibro_ICAM1 and mFibro_FAP subsets were detected in IBD tissues (Fig. 4C-E). Subsequently, mIHC revealed a substantial increase in the iFibro_ICAM1 and mFibro_FAP population in IBD tissues (Fig. 4H-I). To evaluate spatial associations, we quantified local immune cell densities within a 150 μm radius surrounding stromal subcluster spots. In CD tissues, iFibro_ICAM1 colocalized with T cells, whereas mFibro_FAP colocalized with both T and myeloid cells (Fig. 4F). In UC tissues, iFibro_ICAM1 colocalized predominantly with B and plasma cells, whereas mFibro_FAP was spatially associated with plasma and endothelial cells (Fig. 4G). These results indicated that iFibro_ICAM1 and mFibro_FAP contribute to the formation of spatially organized inflammatory niches in IBD.

### iFibro_ICAM1 and mFibro_FAP subsets constitute T cell-associated neighborhoods within the CD compartment
  To conduct an in-depth analysis of the cellular interactions between fibroblasts and distinct T cell subsets within the intestinal tissues of patients with CD, we re-clustered 50,108 high-quality intestinal T cells and identified 12 subpopulations (Fig. 5A-B, Table S7), including six CD4+ subsets (Th1, Th17, Tfh, naïve, central memory, and Treg), four CD8+ subsets (naïve, effector memory, resident memory, and stressed), NK cells, and ILCs. Marker expression in the different T cell subsets is shown in Figure S4. Spatial analysis revealed that in CD, iFibro_ICAM1 preferentially co-localized with CD4_Th17_IL17A and CD8_Tem_GZMK cells, whereas mFibro_FAP-associated regions were enriched in CD8_Tem_GZMK and CD8_Tn_IL7R cells (Fig. 5C-F). CellChat analysis revealed increased fibroblast-T cell interactions in CD tissues, specifically involving the iFibro_ICAM1, mFibro_FAP, and CD8_Tem_GZMK T cell subsets (Fig. 5G-J, Figure S5). In the CD compartment, the interaction pathways were predominantly enriched in the integrin, TNF, and chemokine signaling pathways (Fig. 5K-L). mIHC analysis further confirmed that iFibro_ICAM1 and mFibro_FAP in the intestinal tissues of patients with CD co-localize in close spatial proximity to CD8 T cells, forming distinct immune cell neighborhoods.

### iFibro_ICAM1 and mFibro_FAP form plasma cell niches in UC
  In stark contrast to CD, ST analysis revealed remarkable heterogeneity in UC, characterized by distinct cellular microenvironments, primarily composed of iFibro_ICAM1 and mFibro_FAP subsets, and plasma cell populations (Fig. 6A-B). The intricate cellular architecture of UC tissues was further elucidated through comprehensive CellChat analysis, which demonstrated significant intensification of fibroblast-plasma cell crosstalk. Notably, the interactions between iFibro_ICAM1 and mFibro_FAP subsets and plasma cells exhibited particularly pronounced enhancement, suggesting their potential pivotal role in UC pathogenesis (Figs. 6C-E, Figure S6). Within the UC microenvironment, interaction network analysis revealed a predominant enrichment of critical signaling pathways, including plasma cell function, the TNF signaling axis, and the chemokine signaling network, which collectively contributed to the complex inflammatory milieu (Fig. 6F). mIHC analysis further confirmed that iFibro_ICAM1 and mFibro_FAP in the intestinal tissues of patients with UC co-localize with plasma cells, forming a specialized cellular niche (Fig. 6F-G).

### The distinct inflammatory microenvironments of IBD drive differential phenotypic transformation of fibroblasts
  Initially, we validated the expression profiles of FAP and ICAM1 in intestinal tissues obtained from patients with IBD. qPCR and WB analyses demonstrated that both ICAM1 and FAP expression was significantly upregulated in patients with IBD compared with the NC group (Fig. 7A-E). To elucidate the effect of the inflammatory microenvironment on fibroblast phenotypic remodeling in patients with IBD, we established in vitro cultures of primary intestinal fibroblasts derived from these patients. Guided by transcription factor enrichment analysis from single-cell sequencing data, we stimulated intestinal fibroblasts with TNF-α (activating the NF-ĸB pathway), IFN-γ (activating the STAT1 pathway), and M1002 (a HIF-2α agonist). Our experimental results demonstrated that TNF-α and IFN-γ stimulation markedly enhanced ICAM-1 expression in intestinal fibroblasts without significantly altering FAP expression, suggesting a transition toward the iFibro_ICAM1 phenotype (Fig. 7F-G). Conversely, M1002 stimulation significantly upregulated FAP expression while maintaining stable ICAM-1 levels, indicating fibroblast differentiation towards the mFibro_FAP phenotype (Fig. 7H-I).

### Predictive value of fibroblast subsets for treatment response
  To evaluate the clinical relevance of stromal signatures for therapeutic response, we analyzed bulk transcriptomic datasets from pre-treatment IBD samples receiving anti-TNF, anti-IL-12/23, anti-integrin, or anti-IL-6 therapy. All predictive analyses were re-evaluated after stratification by disease subtype (CD and UC). Among the stromal signatures, mFibro_FAP showed the most consistent predictive value for anti-TNF response in both CD and UC, with higher signature scores associated with an increased likelihood of non-response (Fig. 8A-B). Interestingly, the iFibro_ICAM1 signature also showed predictive value for anti-TNF response, but with opposite directions in the two disease subtypes: in CD, higher iFibro_ICAM1 scores were associated with response rather than resistance (Fig. 8C), whereas in UC, higher scores were associated with non-response (Fig. 8D).

  To improve model performance, we used random forest to identify the top 10 predictive genes within the mFibro_FAP and iFibro_ICAM1 signatures separately for CD and UC. ssGSEA scores derived from these refined gene sets yielded strong predictive performance for anti-TNF response in both disease subtypes (all AUCs > 0.8, Fig. 8E-F). ## Discussion
  Fibroblasts are increasingly recognized as key regulators of IBD pathogenesis and progression [7, 39]. Novel targets that disrupt inflammatory fibroblast activation are crucial for mitigating disease progression and fibrosis. We comprehensively characterized fibroblast heterogeneity in IBD using single-cell, spatial, and bulk transcriptomics. We identified ten fibroblast subsets with pro-inflammatory, matrix-remodeling, and pro-fibrotic phenotypes enriched in inflamed IBD tissues and closely linked to immune infiltration. In addition, we demonstrated distinct fibroblast neighborhood remodeling patterns in CD versus UC, which likely direct disease-specific immune interactions. We also developed a fibroblast-derived transcriptional signature with strong predictive power for therapeutic response.

  Advances in single-cell and spatial transcriptomics have rapidly expanded our understanding of intestinal fibroblasts in IBD. Recent studies underscore fibroblast heterogeneity and their distinct roles in remodeling stromal compartments during IBD [10, 40, 41]. Previous work identified IAFs expanded in IBD tissues with pro-inflammatory and pro-fibrotic gene signatures [11]. Another study had identified MMP/WNT5A+ fibroblasts and CXCL14+ fibroblasts enriched for pro-fibrotic and extracellular matrix (ECM) production signatures in CD stricture tissues [41]. TWIST⁺FAP⁺ fibroblasts also play a central role in IBD fibrosis [42]. Despite extensive research, the dominant fibroblast subtypes driving intestinal inflammation remain unclear. We constructed a high-resolution fibroblast atlas using single-cell and spatial data from a large number of IBD samples. Characterizing transcriptional, metabolic, and spatial features revealed pathological fibroblast subsets enriched in IBD tissues and strongly associated with immune infiltration and therapy resistance.

  We characterized the gene expression profiles of the fibroblast subpopulations. The iFibro_CCL13 subset expressed high levels of chemokines (e.g., CCL13 and CCL8), suggesting a role in immune cell recruitment. The iFibro_CCN1 subset exhibits elevated CCN1 expression, which potentially contributes to collagen deposition and fibrosis. The iFibro_C3 subset expresses complement factors (e.g., C3 and C7), implicating it in intestinal inflammation. The iFibro_ICAM1 subset expressed inflammatory markers (TNFAIP2, NFKBIZ, ICAM1), suggesting a role in amplifying signals and recruiting immune cells. We also identified the Telocyte_PDGFRA subset, defined by PDGFRA expression, which has been associated with pro-fibrotic functions in other tissues (e.g., lung) [43]. The mFibro_FAP subset, known for ECM synthesis and promotion of intestinal fibrosis, was also identified [42].

  Among identified subsets, iFibro_ICAM1 and mFibro_FAP emerged as key subpopulations enriched in inflamed IBD tissues. Using mIHC, we validated the expansion of these two fibroblast subtypes in the IBD tissues. Previous studies showed enrichment of ICAM1⁺ and FAP⁺ fibroblasts secreting ECM and chemokines in tumors [44, 45]. Specifically, ICAM1+ cancer-associated fibroblasts (iCAFs) were found to recruit myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs; THBS1+ monocytes) and contribute to ECM remodeling [44]. Disruption of the interaction between FAP+ fibroblasts and SPP1+ macrophages enhances the response to immunotherapy in colorectal cancer [45]. In intestinal fibrosis, FAP⁺ fibroblasts act as major ECM producers, and their crosstalk with CD150⁺ monocytes is implicated in fibrostenotic CD [42, 46]. Notably, a previous study identified an expanded subset of colonic fibroblasts characterized by the upregulation of fibrosis-associated genes, including FAP and TWIST1 [11]. While mFibro_FAP shares core transcriptomic signatures with these IAFs, our high-resolution multi-omics approach reveals distinct functional nuances. Specifically, mFibro_FAP exhibits unique metabolic reprogramming and a specific spatial predilection for CD8+ T cell-rich niches, which adds a layer of resolution to the broader IAF population described in earlier studies. Our findings extend these observations by demonstrating that both iFibro_ICAM1 and mFibro_FAP are expanded during active inflammation in IBD, indicating their early involvement in sustaining inflammatory microenvironments and priming tissue for fibrotic remodeling. Expansion of mFibro_FAP may facilitate matrix deposition and immune modulation, whereas iFibro_ICAM1 acts as an inflammatory hub promoting acute activation and lineage diversification. Our in vitro data further support this, showing ICAM1 and FAP upregulation at mRNA and protein levels in IBD tissues. Moreover, TNF-α and IFN-γ stimulation shifted fibroblasts toward an iFibro_ICAM1-like phenotype, while HIF-2α agonist induced a similar shift, underscoring fibroblast plasticity under inflammatory conditions.

  Spatial transcriptomic analysis confirmed distinct spatial niches occupied by these fibroblast subsets in patients with CD and UC. Previous work using MERFISH in murine colitis models revealed conserved inflammation-associated fibroblast subpopulations (IAFs) and their spatial organization [9]. Notably, human IBD tissue features resemble those in mouse neighborhoods [13, 14]. Although FAP⁺TWIST1⁺ fibroblasts are expanded in CD, their transcriptional profile differs from those in UC, highlighting disease-specific stromal responses [11, 42]. For validation, we used spatial transcriptomic and CellChat to delineate these differences. In CD, iFibro_ICAM1 and mFibro_FAP colocalized with CD8_Tem_GZMK cells, an effector subset that exacerbates tissue damage via mediator release and protease-activated signaling [47, 48]. In contrast, in UC, fibroblast niches are enriched with plasma cells, aligning with the humoral immune dominance characteristic of this disease [49, 50]. This divergence likely reflects fundamental immunological distinctions between CD and UC. CD is driven primarily by T cell-mediated mechanisms, whereas UC pathology is characterized by sustained humoral responses. The spatial and functional plasticity of fibroblasts highlights their therapeutic potential, and targeting these cells can disrupt disease-specific inflammatory networks and mitigate both inflammation and fibrotic progression.

  We also demonstrated the translational relevance of these subsets through predictive modeling of bulk RNA-seq data from patients on IBD therapies. Prior studied have linked resistance to anti-TNFα therapy with the expansion of IAFs [11]. Our findings extend this by showing that enrichment of iFibro_ICAM1 and mFibro_FAP signatures consistently predicts poor responses to both anti-TNF and IL12/IL23 biologics. Several mechanisms may explain how these fibroblast subsets drive resistance to biological therapies. First, iFibro_ICAM1 fibroblasts, acting as inflammatory hubs, produce high levels of ICAM-1 and chemokines (e.g., CCL13, CCL8), which may promote the persistent recruitment and retention of effector T cells (e.g., CD8_Tem_GZMK) and inflammatory monocytes. This creates a highly localized, self-sustaining inflammatory niche that remains refractory to systemic TNF-α inhibition. Second, the mFibro_FAP subset may contribute to resistance by remodeling the extracellular matrix (ECM) to form a fibrotic shield. Increased collagen deposition and altered ECM stiffness can physically impair drug penetration into deep tissue layers and provide survival signals to inflammatory cells through integrin-mediated pathways, bypassing the pathways targeted by current biologics. Furthermore, the secretion of alternative pro-inflammatory ligands (e.g., IL-6) by these subsets may activate parallel survival pathways in immune cells, rendering them less dependent on the TNF-α signaling axis. Enrichment of iFibro_ICAM1 and mFibro_FAP signatures was consistently associated with poor response to anti-TNF therapy. Refining these signatures to top predictive genes improved model performance, highlighting their potential as biomarkers for therapeutic stratification and suggesting anti-TNF resistance may be reduced by combining anti-TNF with fibroblast inhibition.

  This study has several limitations. First, because our analyses were based primarily on publicly available datasets, the scRNA-seq discovery cohort showed an uneven distribution of anatomical sites and disease subtypes, with a predominance of ileal CD samples. To maximize data inclusion for stromal atlas construction, we integrated both ileal and colorectal samples from CD and UC in the discovery phase; however, most comparative analyses were stratified by anatomical location and disease subtype whenever possible. In addition, spatial validation was restricted to colorectal tissues because no comparable open-access ileal spatial transcriptomic dataset was available. This should be considered when interpreting cross-site generalizability, although colorectal validation remains informative because both CD and UC can involve the colon, whereas ileal disease is largely restricted to CD. Second, the datasets were cross-sectional and could not fully resolve dynamic transitions or treatment-induced changes in fibroblast states over the course of IBD. Third, while transcriptomic signatures strongly implicate fibroblasts in shaping immune responses, functional validation of predicted fibroblast-immune interactions and differentiation trajectories requires in vitro or in vivo models. Future studies using human primary cells and organoid co-cultures are recommended to verify these mechanisms. Fourth, although we focused on fibroblasts, other stromal and epithelial cell populations may also influence treatment outcomes and warrant further study.


## Conclusions
  Ten distinct stromal sub-populations were identified in this study, with iFibro_ICAM1 and mFibro_FAP being strongly associated with IBD pathology. These two subtypes expand in the IBD inflammatory micro-environment. Different fibroblast subsets have distinct metabolic reprogramming features, and there are different fibroblast neighborhood remodeling patterns in CD and UC, with enhanced inter-cellular communication between fibroblast subsets and immune cells. Enrichment of iFibro_ICAM1 and mFibro_FAP signatures is associated with poor therapeutic responses. These findings substantially deepen our understanding of fibroblast spatial heterogeneity in IBD and provide a solid foundation for the development of fibroblast-targeted therapeutic strategies and patient stratification methods for the clinical management of IBD.
