Nuclear receptors (NRs) are protein transcription factors that regulate key genes across diverse physiological processes. The DNA-binding domain (DBD) binds specific DNA sequences (response elements, REs) in target gene promoters. Previous studies identified 43 putative NRs in the Magallana gigas genome. However, limited information is available regarding the functionality of these NRs. DBD analysis can provide insight into binding modes and potential target REs. This study aimed to investigate the structure and functionality of the DBDs of NRs in M. gigas. Initially, 3D modeling of M. gigas DBDs revealed three primary structural conformations. DNA interaction structures for all M. gigas DBDs were obtained through molecular docking. To validate the DNA-protein poses, a Martini 3 DNA model was built and 30 mus triplicate production MD simulations were performed for each DBD-DNA system, totaling  approximately 4000 mus of simulation time. In total, 20 M. gigas NRs were identified as promising candidates, exhibiting structural similarity to known NRs. Likely DNA-binding poses and REs were proposed for these receptors, including homologs such as RXR, RAR, PPAR, THR, FTZF1, NGF1, HNF4, and members of the newly described P family. 13 NRs were classified as intermediate, suggesting minor modifications could optimize DNA binding, while 10 were structurally divergent or novel in their DNA-binding features. This study provides valuable insights into the functional mechanisms of M. gigas NRs. Furthermore, it introduces a simplified Martini 3 DNA model tailored for DNA-protein interaction studies.
