## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence as presented in the abstract
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report that two families of proteins sharing a conserved three-dimensional fold, bacterial transcription factors and periplasmic binding proteins, have evolved distinct "energetic blueprints" despite binding the same sugars. Using multiple biophysical and computational methods, they show that these energetic differences support different functional requirements, and that rationally redesigning the fold's energy landscape yields synthetic transcription factors with predictable ligand-driven transcriptional outputs. They conclude that decoding energetic blueprints among conserved folds enables functional diversification, offers a new route for protein design, and provides an approach for engineering challenging drug targets.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no figures, tables, methods, or supplementary materials provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, all figures and tables, experimental methods, statistical analyses, structural coordinates, and any supporting data

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a compelling and potentially high-impact concept, namely that energetic features, rather than static structure alone, govern functional diversification in conserved protein folds. The integration of multiple biophysical techniques is appropriate and ambitious. However, the abstract provides insufficient detail to evaluate the rigor of the evidence, the strength of the claims, or the novelty relative to existing literature. The central claim that "energetic blueprints" can be decoded and redeployed for protein design is intriguing but not substantiated from the abstract alone. The work is likely of interest to a broad readership in structural biology, biophysics, and protein engineering, but the current evidence base is too limited to assess technical soundness or the validity of the conclusions.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Structural biologists and biophysicists studying protein dynamics and allostery would be interested in the concept of energetic blueprints as a functional determinant. Protein engineers and synthetic biologists would be interested in the potential to rationally design transcription factors with tunable responses. Researchers working on drug discovery, particularly for challenging targets involving protein-protein interactions or allosteric sites, may find the approach relevant. The work also speaks to evolutionary biologists interested in how conserved folds diversify functionally.
- **Major strengths** The abstract addresses a fundamental and unresolved question in protein science, namely how conserved folds achieve disparate functions. The use of multiple complementary methods (hydrogen exchange-mass spectrometry, bioinformatics, X-ray crystallography, molecular dynamics) is a strength, as it suggests a multi-pronged approach. The inclusion of a functional test through rational redesign and the demonstration of predicted transcriptional outputs is a strong feature, as it moves beyond descriptive analysis to functional validation. The potential translational relevance to drug target engineering adds significance.
