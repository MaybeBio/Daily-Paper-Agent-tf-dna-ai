The ethyl acetate fraction of Gentiana kurroo was investigated to identify constituents responsible for anti-melanogenic activity. Among solvent-partitioned extracts evaluated in alpha-melanocyte-stimulating hormone (alpha-MSH)-stimulated B16 murine melanoma cells, the ethyl acetate fraction exhibited the strongest suppression of melanin production without apparent cytotoxicity. Six compounds previously isolated and structurally characterized by our group were re-evaluated, among which cyperenoic acid showed the most potent inhibitory effect, reducing melanin content to 56.3%, 45.1%, and 5.7% of the alpha-MSH-stimulated control at 10, 30, and 100 muM, respectively (IC(5)(0)  approximately  21 muM). Mechanistic studies revealed that cyperenoic acid significantly downregulated tyrosinase and tyrosinase-related protein-2 (TRP2) expression and attenuated phosphorylation of microphthalmia-associated transcription factor (MITF). In contrast, phosphorylation levels of extracellular signal-regulated kinase (ERK) and cAMP response element-binding protein (CREB), two major upstream regulators of MITF, were not significantly affected. These results suggest that cyperenoic acid suppresses melanogenesis through modulation of MITF phosphorylation independently of ERK and CREB signaling under the present conditions. Molecular docking analysis using the MITF bHLH-LZ domain indicated structural compatibility of cyperenoic acid with the MITF kink pocket, a recently proposed ligand-binding site. Collectively, these findings suggest that cyperenoic acid is a promising anti-melanogenic natural product acting via MITF modulation.
