## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This abstract presents a broad and timely review topic with clear relevance to crop stress biology. The framing is coherent and the scope is ambitious, covering multiple regulatory layers and technologies. However, the abstract is largely descriptive and programmatic, with no specific examples, quantitative claims, or critical synthesis visible. The scientific case for the central thesis, that targeted engineering of transcriptional regulators will yield resilient root systems, is asserted rather than evidenced. The abstract reads as a roadmap rather than a critical review, and the absence of any specific findings or comparative insights limits its current impact.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Plant molecular biologists, abiotic stress physiologists, and crop breeders working on cereal stress tolerance would find this review relevant. Researchers in transcriptional regulation, root biology, and integrative omics would also be interested. The emphasis on translational tools (CRISPR, QTL/GWAS, AI) may attract those focused on applied crop improvement.
- **Major strengths** The abstract identifies a clear and important knowledge gap, namely the integration of transcriptional networks across multiple stress types and root zones. The list of transcription factor families and the mention of cell-type-specific programs reflect current trends. The inclusion of emerging technologies (single-cell omics, AI modeling) signals forward-looking synthesis.
- **Major Concerns** The abstract lacks any specific evidence, examples, or critical evaluation. It does not state what is known versus unknown, nor does it identify controversies or gaps in the literature. The claim that engineering transcriptional regulators will produce resilient root systems is presented without supporting examples or caveats. The abstract is too general to distinguish this review from existing ones on similar topics.
- **Minor Comments** The abstract would benefit from naming one or two specific regulatory modules or genes as illustrative cases. The phrase "real-world agroecosystems" is vague and could be replaced with a more concrete statement about field conditions or yield outcomes. The final sentence is aspirational and could be tempered to reflect the current state of evidence.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** None identifiable from the abstract alone, but the absence of any specific data or examples means the central thesis is not yet supported by visible evidence.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: moderate, as the topic is active but the abstract does not reveal a novel angle. Scientific importance: high, given the relevance to food security and stress tolerance. Interdisciplinary readership: moderate, as the abstract bridges molecular biology and breeding but does not yet demonstrate cross-disciplinary insights. Technical soundness: not assessable from the abstract. Readability for nonspecialists: good, with clear language and logical flow, though some terms (e.g., "cis-element engineering") may require definition.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The abstract is promising but too general to support the strong claims made. A full manuscript with specific examples, critical synthesis, and evidence-based conclusions would be needed to assess the case.
