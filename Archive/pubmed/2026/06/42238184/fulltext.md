# Loss of KLF15 expression characterizes proximal tubule injury in cisplatin-induced acute kidney injury: A multi-omics study


## Graphical abstract
  Graphical abstract


## Introduction
  Since its clinical approval in 1978, cisplatin (CSP) has served as a cornerstone chemotherapeutic agent for a wide array of solid tumors (Gómez-Ruiz et al., 2012, Silver et al., 2010, Arriagada et al., 2004, Studer et al., 1994). CSP treatment triggers the formation of DNA crosslinks and adducts, which subsequently activates DNA damage response that ultimately leads to cancer cell-cycle arrest and death (Pabla and Dong, 2008). Despite its potency, its clinical utility is frequently limited by severe side effects in normal tissues, including ototoxicity, neurotoxicity, and particularly nephrotoxicity (Yu et al., 2020, Alhowail, 2025, Zazuli et al., 2018). CSP-induced acute kidney injury (AKI), a condition characterized by a rapid loss of renal function, affects up to 30% of patients receiving the drug and represents a major dose-limiting toxicity (Latcha et al., 2016). Therefore, elucidating the cellular and molecular mechanisms underlying CSP-induced AKI is crucial for the development of novel renoprotective strategies.

  The pathophysiology of CSP-induced AKI is primarily driven by the injury to the renal proximal tubule (PT) cells, which serve as the main site of drug absorption and accumulation (Yao et al., 2007). Within these cells, CSP triggers a cascade of cellular stressors, including DNA damage, mitochondrial dysfunction, oxidative stress, and endoplasmic reticulum stress, which converge to provoke downstream pathological responses such as inflammation, cell death, and metabolic dysfunction. These complex processes are orchestrated by intricate epigenetic and transcriptional regulatory networks (Xiang et al., 2019). While specific transcription factors (TFs) have been implicated in mediating distinct facets of this injury, such as NF-κB in driving inflammation (Ozkok et al., 2016) and p53 in promoting apoptosis (Yang et al., 2008), this understanding remains fragmented. It is particularly unclear whether master regulatory TFs exist that coordinate the crosstalk among these pathological pathways, especially the critical interplay between inflammation, cell death, and metabolic collapse. Thus, a more comprehensive mapping of the transcriptional landscape governing PT injury is essential to identify novel and more effective therapeutic targets.

  In the present study, we aimed to comprehensively map the pathological pathways and identify master TF regulators of CSP-induced AKI. Inspired by recent approaches in transcriptional network analysis (Eberhardt et al., 2021), we first integrated bulk transcriptomics and proteomics to infer dysregulated TFs. We then utilized single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) to validate the cell-type-specific activity of these candidates, identifying Krüppel-like factor 15 (KLF15) as a robustly downregulated regulator in injured proximal tubules. Finally, we experimentally validated KLF15 expression in a CSP-induced AKI mouse model and performed molecular docking to screen for potential therapeutic agents targeting this factor.


## Material and methods

### Data acquisition
  All RNA sequencing datasets were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) database under the accession numbers GSE220674 (Deng et al., 2026), GSE207587 (Doke et al., 2023), GSE147256 (Luan et al., 2021), and GSE197266 (Chen et al., 2023). The proteomic data were retrieved from the supplementary materials of Zhang et al (Zhang et al., 2024). Datasets used in this study, including bulk RNA-seq (GSE220674, GSE207587, GSE147256), scRNA-seq (GSE197266), and LC-MS/MS-based proteomics, were all obtained from the kidney tissues of sham- or CSP-treated mice. Details regarding the composition and experimental conditions of these datasets are summarized in Supplementary Table S1. The Comparative Toxicogenomics Database (CTD; https://ctdbase.org/) was utilized to identify potential links between KLF15 and CSP-induced AKI (Davis et al., 2025). (Accessed on 21/4/2026) The AOP-Wiki (version 2.6; https://aopwiki.org/) was utilized to construct a mechanistic framework illustrating how KLF15 regulates CSP-induced kidney failure (Jaylet et al., 2024). (Accessed on 20/4/2026).


### Bulk RNA data processing
  Bulk RNA-seq data were normalized to transcripts per million (TPM). Batch effects were corrected using the ComBat function from the sva R package (v3.57.0). Principal component analysis (PCA) was performed with the prcomp function. Differential expression analysis (DEA) was conducted using the limma package (v3.65.4). Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were defined as those with an absolute log2 fold change (|log2FC|) > 1 and an adjusted P value < 0.05. Volcano plots were generated using the ggplot2 package (v4.0.0) to visualize the DEGs.


### scRNA-seq data processing
  The scRNA-seq dataset GSE197266, comprising transcriptomic data from various AKI models (CSP, folic acid, sodium oxalate, ischemia–reperfusion injury, and unilateral ureteral obstruction), was analyzed in this study (Chen et al., 2023). Data from control and CSP groups were extracted and processed using the Seurat package (v5.3.0) (Hao et al., 2021). The NormalizeData, FindVariableFeatures, and ScaleData functions were applied for data normalization. The RunPCA function was subsequently used to perform linear dimensionality reduction. Batch effects were corrected using the Harmony package (v1.2.3) with the RunHarmony function (Korsunsky et al., 2019). Optimal principal components were determined using the GetPC function of SCNT package (Qing et al., 2025). Cell clustering was performed by constructing a KNN graph and applying the Louvain algorithm with a resolution of 0.1. Nonlinear dimensionality reduction was conducted with the RunUMAP function on the first 27 Harmony-corrected dimensions. Cluster annotation was based on previously reported cell type-specific markers (Balzer et al., 2022). Marker genes for each cluster were identified using the FindAllMarkers function. DEA between specific groups was conducted using the FindMarkers function. The ClusterGVis (v0.1.4) and scRNAtoolVis (v0.1.0) packages were used to visualize cluster functions and DEGs, respectively. TF regulatory networks were inferred using pySCENIC (v0.12.1) (Van De Sande et al., 2020) and decoupler (v2.1.1) (Badia et al., 2022). The BisqueRNA package (v1.0.5) was applied to deconvolve cell type proportions from bulk RNA-seq data (Jew et al., 2020).


### Functional enrichment analysis
  Transcription factor perturbation followed by expression analysis was performed using the enrichR package (v3.4) (Xie et al., 2021). Metabolism-related gene sets from the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) and Hallmark pathway collections were obtained from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB, v2025.1, https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/). Gene set variation analysis (GSVA) and ssGSEA were conducted using the GSVA package (v2.3.1). Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) and Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis were carried out with the clusterProfiler package (v4.14.6). Results were visualized using the circlize (v0.4.16) and GseaVis (v1.26.6) (Zhang et al., 2025) packages. Pathway activities in scRNA-seq data were assessed using the irGSEA package (v3.3.2) (Fan et al., 2024).


### Molecular docking
  The amino acid (AA) sequence of KLF15 (UniProt ID: Q9UIH9) was obtained from the UniProt database (https://www.uniprot.org/). KLF15 domains were annotated using the SMART website (Letunic et al., 2021) (https://smart.embl.de/). (Accessed on 10/1/2025) The three-dimensional (3D) protein structure of KLF15 was predicted with the AlphaFold 3 online tool (Abramson et al., 2024) (https://alphafoldserver.com/). (Accessed on 9/26/2025) A library of 3D structures for FDA-approved drugs was downloaded from the ZINC database (Irwin et al., 2020) (https://zinc.docking.org/). (Accessed on 9/26/2025) Drug structures in MOL2 format were converted to the PDB format using Open Babel (v 3.1.1). Virtual docking between KLF15 and the drug library was performed using AutoDock Vina (v1.2.6) (Eberhardt et al., 2021). Docking results were visualized with PyMOL (v3.2.0a). The target and application of the drug were retrieved from the ZINC database and DurgBank (Knox et al., 2024) (https://go.drugbank.com/).


### Cisplatin-induced AKI model
  Male C57BL/6J mice (6–8 weeks old; 20–24 g; SPF grade) were purchased from Gempharmatech (Nanjing, China) and housed five per cage on a 12 h light/dark cycle with ad libitum access to distilled water and standard chow diet (in compliance with Chinese National Standard GB 14924.3). The environment was maintained at a controlled temperature (22–24 °C) and humidity (50–65%). Male mice were utilized to minimize potential variability introduced by the female estrous cycle and due to their well-characterized susceptibility to CSP-induced AKI. Females were excluded as they are generally known to be less susceptible to cisplatin nephrotoxicity. SPF-grade animals were used to exclude confounding effects from potential pathogens. Young adult mice were chosen to avoid variations in drug sensitivity associated with developmental differences. Mice were randomly divided into two groups (n = 5 for each group). To induce AKI, mice were administered a single intraperitoneal (i.p.) injection of cisplatin (20 mg/kg; HY-17394, MCE, USA; CAS:15663-27-1; Purity: 98.0%). Control mice received an injection of normal saline. The cisplatin dose (20 mg/kg) and time-point (72 h) were determined based on previous studies (Li et al., 2018, Xie et al., 2023). Body weights were recorded at baseline and prior to euthanasia. During the 72-h post-treatment interval, mice were monitored daily for health status and clinical signs of morbidity. No mice were excluded due to mortality or morbidity. While no mortality occurred, CSP-injected mice exhibited significant body weight loss, listlessness, lethargy, and a rough hair coat, indicating the successful induction of a systemic yet sublethal toxic response. At 72 h post-injection, mice were anesthetized with isoflurane and euthanized by cervical dislocation, and blood and kidney tissues were collected. Renal function was evaluated by measuring serum creatinine levels with a Creatinine (Cr) Assay Kit (C011-2-1, Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering, China). For histological analysis, kidney tissues were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, embedded in paraffin, sectioned at a thickness of 5 μm, and stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E). All animal procedures were performed in accordance with the Guide for Care and Use of Laboratory Animals and were approved by the Ethics Committee for Animal Experimentation at the Fifth Affiliated Hospital of Zunyi Medical University (protocol code: ZMU21-2407-014).


### RNA isolation and quantitative real-time PCR (RT-qPCR)
  Total RNA was extracted from kidney tissues using TRIzol Reagent (15596026, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Complementary DNA (cDNA) was synthesized using the RT Master Mix for qPCR II (HY-K0510A, MCE, USA). RT-qPCR was performed on a QuantGene 9600 Real Time PCR System (BIOER, China) using HS Universal qPCR Master Mix (BR0014-01, ACE, China). Relative gene expression was calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method, with β-actin serving as the endogenous control. All primer sequences are listed in Supplementary table S2.


### Western blotting
  Renal tissues were homogenized in RIPA lysis buffer (P0013B, Beyotime, China) supplemented with a protease inhibitor cocktail (HY-K0010, MCE, USA). Protein concentrations were determined using the Pierce BCA Protein Assay Kit (23225, Thermo Fisher, USA). Equal amounts of protein (50 μg per lane) were separated on 12.5% SDS-PAGE gels (PG223, Epizyme, China) and a 0.45 μm polyvinylidene fluoride membrane (IPVH00010, Millipore, USA). After blocking with 3% BSA, membranes were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies, followed by HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies for 1 h at room temperature. Protein bands were visualized using an Ultra High Sensitivity ECL Kit (HY-K1005, MCE, USA) and quantified with ImageJ software (NIH, Bethesda, MD, USA). A detailed list of antibodies used is provided in Supplementary table S3.


### Statistical analysis
  Statistical analyses of bulk and single-cell RNA-seq were performed in R (v4.5.0) using the packages specified above. Experimental data from mouse models were analyzed using GraphPad Prism 10. Comparisons between two groups were made using a two-tailed unpaired Student's t-test. All data are presented as the mean ± SEM. A P-value or adjusted P-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.


## Results

### Dysregulation of inflammation, apoptosis, and metabolism pathways in CSP-Induced AKI
  To elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying CSP-induced AKI, we first integrated three bulk RNA-seq datasets (GSE220674 (Deng et al., 2026), GSE207587 (Doke et al., 2023), and GSE147256 (Luan et al., 2021), comprising 10 normal and 11 AKI samples. PCA confirmed that batch effects were effectively corrected using the Combat algorithm (Fig. 1A and 1B). DEA of 15,343 common genes identified 1750 upregulated and 877 downregulated genes in injured kidneys (Fig. 1C). GSEA revealed activation of multiple immune- and apoptosis-related pathways, such as Apoptosis and TNF signaling, while various metabolism-related pathways were suppressed (Fig. 1D and 1E).   Several transcriptional regulators, such as FXR (Kim et al., 2022), FOSL1 (Cuarental et al., 2023), and TFEB (Zhu et al., 2020), have previously been implicated in CSP-induced AKI. To identify additional upstream regulators, we performed TF analysis using the EnrichR database of TF perturbations followed by expression profiling (Xie et al., 2021). The top-ranked TFs included HEY2, ELF4, GLIS2, RUNX1, AIREL, NF2L2, KLF15, STAT3, and FOSL1 (Fig. 1F, Supplementary table S4). Comparative expression analysis between control and injured kidneys revealed elevated levels of Elf4, Fosl1, Nfe2l2, Runx1, Mft2, and Stat3, and reduced levels of Glis2, Klf15, and Ovol1 (Fig. 1G). Notably, several of these TFs have been previously associated with kidney inflammation and metabolism in AKI contexts (Fontecha-Barriuso et al., 2024, Liu et al., 2025, Lee et al., 2024).

  Collectively, these findings suggest that CSP-induced AKI involves transcriptional reprogramming of inflammation, apoptosis, and metabolic pathways.


### KLF15, GLIS2, and NFE2L2 were key TF regulators of differentially expressed proteins
  To further elucidate the dysregulated pathways and TF programs involved in CSP-induced AKI, we analyzed previously reported proteomic data (Zhang et al., 2024). A total of 384 upregulated and 240 downregulated proteins were identified (Fig. 2A). Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis of these differentially expressed proteins (DEPs) revealed significant enrichment in biological processes (BP) such as carboxylic acid metabolism, coagulation, and wound healing (Fig. 2B). The associated molecular functions included endopeptidase inhibitor activity, oxidoreductase activity, and lipid binding (Fig. 2B). These DEPs were primarily localized to the basal part of the cell, the collagen-containing extracellular matrix, and the mitochondrial envelope (Fig. 2B).   TF analysis identified HNF4A, NFE2L2, POU1F1, PPARA, NR2C2, HES1, GLIS2, MYCN, E2F1, and KLF15 as top-ranked regulators. PPARA and HNF4A are well-established TFs that maintain renal homeostasis under AKI conditions (Sun et al., 2022, Clark et al., 2023). Importantly, NFE2L2, GLIS2, and KLF15 were also among the top-ranked TFs identified in the transcriptomic analysis, suggesting consistent regulatory involvement across multiple omics layers (Fig. 2D, Supplementary table S5). We thus prioritized these three TFs for further investigation in this study.


## scRNA-seq defined a pro-inflammatory and metabolically impaired state in injured proximal tubules
  The cellular heterogeneity of the kidney, which comprises over 30 distinct cell types, can significantly confound the interpretation of bulk RNA-seq and proteomic data due to shifts in cell-type composition (Abedini et al., 2024). To achieve a precise, cell-type specific examination of transcriptional network alteration during CSP-induced AKI, we reanalyzed the public scRNA-seq dataset GSE197266 (Chen et al., 2023). Following quality control and batch correction, 10 major clusters, including PT, fibroblast (Fib), and macrophage (Macro), were annotated using established marker genes (Fig. 3A, 2B, and Fig. S2). BP enrichment analysis of cluster-specific markers confirmed that cluster identities aligned with known cell-type roles (Fig. 3C). Upon injury, the proportions of PT and principal cell/distal convoluted tubule (PC/DCT) populations were substantially reduced, whereas Fib and Macro populations expanded (Fig. 3D). These shifts were corroborated by deconvolution of the bulk RNA-seq data using BisqueRNA (Fig. 3E).   GO analysis on the 907 DEGs between control and AKI conditions in PT cells revealed a strong enrichment for terms related to metabolic processes, indicating that the core metabolic functions of PT cells were severely impaired during AKI (Fig. S3A, S3B). Furthermore, GSEA demonstrated that pathways associated with the cell cycle and apoptosis were significantly activated, while those essential for energy production, such as the TCA cycle and oxidative phosphorylation, were suppressed (Fig. S3C). Consistently, hallmark pathway analysis using the irGSEA package confirmed the activation of molecular signatures related to inflammation and apoptosis (Fig. S3D).


## Loss of Klf15 marked a metabolically impaired and pro-apoptotic state in injured PT
  NRF2, GLIS2, and KLF15 have been reported to modulate tubular inflammation or apoptosis in AKI or chronic kidney disease (Attanasio et al., 2007, Liu et al., 2009, Shao et al., 2023). We first assessed their transcriptional dynamics. Our analysis revealed that while Nfe2l2 (encoding NRF2) and Glis2 displayed distinct cell-type-specific expression, their transcript levels remained stable during AKI (Fig. 3G, Fig. S2H). In contrast, Klf15 was expressed in PT, Fib, and PC/DCT cells, and its expression level, along with the proportion of Klf15-positive cells, was significantly reduced in the AKI state, suggesting that its downregulation may be closely linked to the acute injury response (Fig. 3G, Fig. S3A).

  Since TF activity is often governed by post-transcriptional modifications rather than mRNA abundance, we employed decoupleR and pySCENIC to infer TF regulatory networks. Although Glis2 mRNA was expressed in PT cells, neither pySCENIC nor decoupleR could infer a corresponding regulatory network. The results for NRF2 were inconsistent; pySCENIC indicated no significant difference, whereas decoupleR predicted an increased NRF2 regulon score in the AKI state (Fig. 4B, Fig. S3E).   We identified a total of 137 DEGs between Klf15+ and Klf15- PT cells (Fig. 4C). Functional analysis of these cells provided a cohesive picture of KLF15′s role. GSEA revealed that Klf15+ cells exhibited significantly higher activity in Metabolic pathways and Tryptophan metabolism, while showing lower activity in detrimental pathways such as P53 signaling, NF-κB signaling, and Apoptosis (Fig. 4C). This functional distinction was mirrored at the regulatory level. Klf15+ cells had elevated regulon scores for HNF4A and ESRRA (master regulators of tubular homeostasis), but decreased scores for Tp53 and RELA (key regulators of inflammation and apoptosis) (Fig. 4A, 4E). Additionally, hallmark pathway analysis confirmed increased metabolism-related signatures, including Fatty acid metabolism and Oxidative phosphorylation, alongside a suppression of the IL6-JAK-STAT3 signaling pathway in Klf15+ cells (Fig. 4F). Given KLF15′s established role in metabolism, we further compared the activities of 86 KEGG metabolism pathways using irGSEA package. This comprehensive analysis confirmed that Klf15 expression was positively associated with 19 metabolic pathways, notably including the TCA cycle, Fatty acid degradation, and Oxidative phosphorylation, thereby implicating KLF15 as a critical regulator of tubular energy production (Fig. 4G).

  Collectively, these results strongly suggest that the loss of Klf15 was associated with a state of impaired energy production, along with enhanced pro-inflammatory and pro-apoptotic tendencies in PT cells.


### KLF15 was downregulated in the CSP-AKI mouse model
  To further investigate the association between KLF15 and cisplatin-induced AKI, we queried the CTD database. CSP and oxaliplatin were identified among the 146 chemicals known to interact with KLF15 (Supplementary Table S6). Furthermore, KLF15 exhibited high inference scores for several top-ranked conditions, including inflammation, kidney diseases, and acute kidney injury (Supplementary Table S7). Notably, CSP and oxaliplatin are integral components of the inference networks for these three diseases (Supplementary Table S7). These findings further implicate KLF15 in the pathogenesis of CSP-induced nephrotoxicity.

  Based on the above integrative analyses, KLF15 emerged as the most promising candidate compared to other potential regulators like NRF2 and GLIS2. To experimentally validate this computational prediction, we established a CSP-induced AKI mouse model and examined its expression. CSP injection caused weight loss due to the gastrointestinal toxicity of the drug (Fig. S4). As shown in Fig. 5A, CSP injection resulted in a significant increase in serum creatinine concentration compared to the saline-injected control group (192.70 ± 28.84 μM vs 26.79 ± 0.78 μM), indicating severe renal dysfunction. Consistently, histological analysis by H&E staining further revealed extensive injury to the renal tubular compartment (Fig. 5B). Consistent with these observations, RT-qPCR analysis demonstrated a significant upregulation of the well-established kidney injury markers, Havcr1 and Lcn2. In contrast, the mRNA expression levels of Klf15 and its downstream target gene Acadm, were markedly reduced in AKI condition (Fig. 5C). Furthermore, Western blot analysis confirmed a substantial reduction in KLF15 protein levels within the injured kidneys (Fig. 5D). ### Identification of potential KLF15-targeting drugs by molecular docking
  Since KLF15 might be a crucial regulator of PT homeostasis in CSP-induced AKI, finding small molecules that stabilize or modulate its activity represents a logical therapeutic strategy to counteract cisplatin nephrotoxicity. To identify potential KLF15-targeting drugs, we performed molecular docking studies targeting the human KLF15 protein. Analysis using the SMART tool revealed that human KLF15 contains three ZnF-C2H2 domains and six low-complexity regions (Fig. 6A). As a full-length experimental structure of KLF15 is unavailable, we predicted its 3D structure using AlphaFold3.0 (Abramson et al., 2024). The resulting model showed that all 3 ZnF-C2H2 domains adopt α-helical and β-turn conformations (Fig. 6B). ## Discussion
  AKI, regardless of etiology, is characterized by enhanced inflammation, increased cell death, and suppressed energy production (Tang et al., 2023, Ramkumar et al., 2021, Wang et al., 2020). By integrating bulk transcriptomics, proteomics, and scRNA-seq data, this study consistently identified the dysregulation of inflammation, apoptosis, and metabolic pathways as the primary forces driving tubular injury. The top-ranked TF regulators of DEGs and DEPs included NRF2, GLIS2, and KLF15. Crucially, loss of Klf15 expression in PT cells was closely associated with a critical shift toward a pro-inflammatory, pro-apoptotic, and metabolically impaired state in mice. Virtual screening identified six FDA-approved drugs exhibiting high binding affinity for KLF15.

  Functional enrichment analysis of DEGs and DEPs in CSP-induced AKI mouse model revealed a clear activation of inflammatory and apoptotic pathways alongside a marked suppression of metabolism. While TFs such as STAT1 and the farnesoid X receptor are known to regulate these processes (Xu et al., 2022, Peng et al., 2025, Li et al., 2023), our analysis specifically implicated NRF2, GLIS2, and KLF15 as crucial upstream regulators. Intriguingly, our findings for NRF2 and GLIS2 presented a nuanced picture. Although NRF2 activation is known to mitigate PT injury (Qi et al., 2023, Wang et al., 2022, Hu et al., 2022), our scRNA-seq analysis did not detect a significant change in either its expression or its inferred transcriptional activity post-injury. However, its therapeutic activation might still be beneficial due to its antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects. Glis2, a nephronophthisis-related gene, appeared downregulated in the bulk RNA-seq data. However, at the single-cell level, we observed no reduction of its expression within the PT cells. We thus concluded that the bulk downregulation was an artifact of the reduced proportion of PT cells in the injured tissue. Nevertheless, we cannot definitively exclude the possibility that Glis2′s transcriptional activity was repressed through post-transcriptional mechanisms.

  KLF15 emerged as the only candidate TF to exhibit a consistent and significant downregulation in both gene expression and transcriptional activity across bulk and single-cell analyses. This finding is particularly significant given that KLF15, a pleiotropic TF governing metabolism and immunity, is increasingly recognized for its protective role in PT cells during AKI. It cooperates with PPARA to maintain fatty acid oxidation in aristolochic acid I induced AKI and suppresses ferroptosis by activating the NRF2/GPX4 axis in folic acid induced AKI (Piret et al., 2021, Yang et al., 2023). Despite this established protective profile in other contexts, the role of KLF15 in CSP-induced AKI remains unaddressed. We demonstrated a marked reduction of KLF15 protein levels in our in vivo mouse model. At single-cell resolution, we established a mechanistic link between the loss of Klf15 expression and the dysregulation of key pathological pathways, including P53 signaling, NF-κB signaling, apoptosis, alongside broad metabolic disruption. This finding aligns with the known functions of KLF15 in diverse cell types such as vascular cells (Lu et al., 2013), cardiomyocytes (Lu et al., 2023, Haldar et al., 2010), and hepatocytes (Han et al., 2019, Jiang et al., 2022). Thus, our study strongly implicates the loss of KLF15 as a key pathogenic event in CSP-induced AKI.

  Analysis of the CTD database revealed a robust interaction network connecting KLF15, cisplatin, and acute kidney injury/inflammation. In accordance with the AOP-Wiki framework (AOP:472), the established sequence of key events (KEs) during CSP-induced kidney failure includes DNA damage (KE1), mitochondrial dysfunction (KE2), the release of reactive oxygen species (ROS) (KE3), oxidative stress (KE4), cell death (KE5), inflammation (KE6), and tubular necrosis (KE7) (Supplementary Fig. S7). Notably, KLF15 is recognized as a negative regulator of these pathological processes (KE2–KE7) in the kidney and various other cellular and disease contexts (Lu et al., 2013, Haldar et al., 2010, Wang et al., 2019, Chen et al., 2025, Li et al., 2021, Gao et al., 2017). We propose that CSP-induced DNA damage triggers the robust downregulation of KLF15 expression as a pivotal nodal event. The subsequent loss of KLF15 drives metabolic reprogramming, which directly exacerbates mitochondrial dysfunction and ROS generation. This metabolic crisis, combined with the removal of KLF15-mediated anti-inflammatory and anti-apoptotic control, activates downstream p53 and NF-κB pathways. Ultimately, this cascade leads to widespread tubular cell loss and kidney failure.

  Despite our significant findings, this study has several limitations that warrant future investigation. First, our current focus was primarily on KLF15. Future studies should be expanded to systematically examine the specific roles and regulatory mechanisms of other potential TFs, including RUNX1, HEY2, and HNF4A, in CSP-induced AKI. Second, while our findings based on integrative multi-omics discovery framework and in vivo mouse model showed that KLF15 downregulation is an important event in tubular injury in CSP-induced nephrotoxicity, the precise mechanistic link requires rigorous confirmation. Future studies utilizing experimental models, such as Klf15-overexpressing cell lines and PT-specific Klf15 transgenic or knockout mice, are essential to validate these findings. These approaches will establish the causal relationship between KLF15 and PT cell homeostasis during CSP-induced injury. Third, our study lacks a detailed characterization of the concentration–time relationship regarding KLF15 expression following cisplatin injury. Future investigations elucidating the pharmacokinetics of CSP in relation to KLF15 downregulation dynamics during early injury phase and recovery phase are warranted. Additionally, given the protective functions of KLF15 in cardiomyopathy and hepatocytes, investigating its expression in the context of CSP-induced cardiotoxicity and hepatotoxicity will be crucial for evaluating the consequence of targeting KLF15. Such studies are necessary to evaluate the systemic consequences of targeting KLF15 and its potential to mitigate the side effects of cisplatin. Fourth, while KLF15 downregulation is a prominent feature in the male model, future studies incorporating female cohorts are essential to determine the universality of this mechanism across sexes. Fifth, another significant limitation of the current study is the absence of functional validation for the drug candidates promising binding affinities identified through molecular docking. Biophysical assays such as surface plasmon resonance, isothermal titration calorimetry, or thermal shift assays are essential to confirm the direct drug-protein interaction. More importantly, a comprehensive evaluation using both in vitro and in vivo assays is necessary to determine if these drugs can effectively enhance KLF15 transcriptional activity and mitigate injury in CSP-treated PT cells and kidneys.


## Conclusion
  Our study suggests that KLF15 downregulation is a significant molecular feature associated with CSP-induced nephrotoxicity. KLF15 potentially functions as a regulatory factor linked to the pro-inflammatory, pro-apoptotic, and metabolically impaired state of PT cells, marking it as a potential candidate for future therapeutic investigation.
