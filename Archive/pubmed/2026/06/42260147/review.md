### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that "serine and aromatic residues promote miscibility, while charged amino acids drive immiscibility" based on 28 IDRs in 378 pairwise combinations.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract provides no quantitative data, effect sizes, statistical significance, or reproducibility metrics for the pairwise screening results. It is unclear how miscibility was measured, what threshold defined "miscible" versus "immiscible," and whether the observed trends were robust across the full set of combinations or driven by a subset of IDRs.  
- **Why it matters** The central claim of the paper rests on this screening result. Without evidence of the magnitude and consistency of the effects, the proposed residue-level grammar cannot be evaluated.  
- **Resolution test** Provide quantitative results from the screening, including statistical analyses, effect sizes, and a clear definition of miscibility. Show that the serine and charge effects are consistent across multiple IDR pairs and are not confounded by other sequence features.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Causal inference  
- **Claim pointer** The authors state that "mutagenesis experiments establish these as causal relationships."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract does not describe the mutagenesis design, the number of mutants tested, the magnitude of phenotypic changes, or whether controls for off-target effects were included. It is also unclear whether mutagenesis was performed on multiple IDRs or only a few representative cases.  
- **Why it matters** Establishing causality is essential to distinguish sequence determinants from mere correlations. Without details on the mutagenesis strategy and results, the causal claim is unsupported.  
- **Resolution test** Describe the mutagenesis approach, including which residues were mutated, how many constructs were tested, and the quantitative effects on miscibility. Include appropriate controls and statistical comparisons.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Mechanistic support  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that "protein-protein interaction network analyses and molecular simulations reveal that serine and aromatic residues favor heterotypic interactions, whereas high charge content reinforces homotypic association."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided  
- **Concern** No details are given about the network analyses or simulation methods, such as the type of simulations, force fields, system sizes, or how heterotypic versus homotypic interactions were quantified. The abstract does not indicate whether the simulations recapitulate the experimental screening results or whether they provide independent mechanistic insight.  
- **Why it matters** The proposed mechanism is a key part of the paper's conceptual contribution. Without methodological transparency and validation, the mechanistic interpretation remains speculative.  
- **Resolution test** Provide details of the simulation and network analysis methods, including validation against experimental data. Show that the predicted interaction preferences are consistent with the mutagenesis results.

- **Concern ID** R1-M4  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Functional relevance  
- **Claim pointer** The authors state that "miscibility between transcription factor (TF) and RNA polymerase II (Pol II) condensates directly influences transactivation" and that "modulating charged residue content in TFs correspondingly tunes transcription."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract does not provide any quantitative readouts of transactivation or transcriptional output, nor does it describe the experimental systems used. It is unclear whether the observed effects are specific to condensate miscibility or could be explained by other properties of the TFs, such as DNA binding affinity or protein stability.  
- **Why it matters** The link to transcriptional regulation is a major claim that extends the relevance of the study beyond biophysics. Without direct evidence, this connection is not established.  
- **Resolution test** Provide quantitative measurements of transactivation and transcription for TFs with varying charge content, along with controls that isolate the effect of miscibility from other TF properties.

### Minor Comments

- **Concern ID** R1-m1  
- **Severity** Minor  
- **Axis** Clarity  
- **Affected element** Definition of miscibility  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided  
- **Issue** The term "miscibility" is used throughout but is not defined in the abstract. It is unclear whether it refers to the ability of two condensates to fuse, to mix within a single condensate, or to co-partition of specific components.  
- **Required correction** Provide a clear operational definition of miscibility as used in the study, ideally in the abstract or introduction.

- **Concern ID** R1-m2  
- **Severity** Minor  
- **Axis** Generalizability

- **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided  
- **Issue** The study examines 28 IDRs, but the abstract does not indicate how these were selected or whether they represent a diverse range of sequences, lengths, or biological functions.  
- **Required correction** Describe the selection criteria for the IDR set and discuss the potential limitations of generalizing the findings beyond this set.

- **Concern ID** R1-m3  
- **Severity** Minor  
- **Axis** Terminology  
- **Affected element** "Residue-level grammar"  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided  
- **Issue** The phrase "residue-level grammar" is evocative but not defined. It is unclear whether this refers to a predictive rule, a quantitative model, or a conceptual framework.  
- **Required correction** Define what is meant by "grammar" in this context and specify whether the findings enable quantitative prediction of miscibility from sequence.

- The claim that mutagenesis establishes causality is unsupported without experimental details.
- The mechanistic claim about heterotypic versus homotypic interaction preferences is unsupported without simulation and network analysis details.
- The claim that TF-Pol II miscibility directly influences transactivation and transcription is unsupported without functional data.
- The claim of a "residue-level grammar for predicting and engineering condensate miscibility" is not established from the abstract alone.
