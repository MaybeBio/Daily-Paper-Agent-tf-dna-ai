## Reviewer

- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a potentially interesting mechanism of action for a natural product in sepsis, centered on selective p50-mediated IL-6 inhibition. However, the claims are broad and mechanistically specific, and the evidence base is entirely inaccessible from the supplied material. Several statements, particularly regarding allosteric inhibition, selectivity, and in vivo efficacy, cannot be evaluated without the underlying data. The work may be of interest to the inflammation and natural product communities, but the current submission does not allow for a rigorous assessment of technical soundness or reproducibility.

- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying sepsis pathophysiology, NF-kappaB signaling, IL-6 targeted therapies, and natural product pharmacology would find the results relevant. The proposed selectivity for IL-6 over other NF-kappaB targets could attract those interested in precision anti-inflammatory strategies. The allosteric mechanism, if validated, would be of interest to structural biologists and drug discovery groups focused on transcription factor modulation.

- **Major strengths** The abstract identifies a clear and potentially novel mechanistic hypothesis, namely selective allosteric inhibition of p50 DNA binding to the IL-6 promoter. The use of both cellular and in vivo models is appropriate for the stated aims. The focus on a natural product with a defined molecular target provides a testable framework for further development.

- **Major Concerns**

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Technical soundness
- **Claim pointer** Baimaside allosterically inhibits NF-kappaB p50 binding to the kappaB DNA element in the IL-6 promoter, inducing steric hindrance at residues Gly61, Ser63, Asn136
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided
- **Concern** The claim of allosteric inhibition is mechanistically specific and requires structural and biophysical evidence, such as X-ray crystallography, NMR, surface plasmon resonance, or isothermal titration calorimetry, along with functional validation of the proposed binding site. Molecular docking alone is generally insufficient to establish allosteric mechanisms. The abstract does not describe how allostery was distinguished from orthosteric competition or how the mutagenesis results were interpreted.
- **Why it matters** Allostery is a strong and unusual claim for a small molecule targeting a transcription factor DNA binding domain. If the mechanism is not rigorously established, the central novelty of the paper collapses. The distinction between allosteric and competitive inhibition has direct implications for drug design and for understanding p50 regulation.
- **Resolution test** Provide biophysical binding data, structural evidence or detailed mutational analysis showing that Baimaside binds outside the DNA binding interface and induces conformational changes that reduce DNA affinity. Show that the proposed residues are not directly involved in DNA contact and that their mutation alters Baimaside binding or efficacy in a manner consistent with allostery.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Technical soundness
- **Claim pointer** Baimaside selectively suppresses IL-6 expression while sparing TNF-alpha and IL-1beta, which are regulated by classical NF-kappaB pathways
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided
- **Concern** The claim of selectivity is central to the paper's significance, but no data are shown to support it. The abstract does not report the range of cytokines measured, the magnitude of suppression, the time points, or the statistical power. Selectivity over other NF-kappaB dependent genes is plausible but requires comprehensive transcriptomic or protein level analysis to rule out broader effects.
- **Why it matters** If Baimaside broadly suppresses NF-kappaB activity, the proposed mechanism is not selective and the therapeutic rationale changes substantially. The claim of sparing TNF-alpha and IL-1beta is a key differentiator and must be robustly demonstrated.
- **Resolution test** Provide dose response and time course data for IL-6, TNF-alpha, IL-1beta, and other NF-kappaB target genes in BMDMs and in vivo. Include a broader panel of inflammatory mediators to demonstrate selectivity. Show that the effect is not due to general cytotoxicity or upstream NF-kappaB pathway inhibition.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Reproducibility and completeness
- **Claim pointer** Baimaside pretreatment significantly reduces LPS-induced organ injury, improves survival rates, and attenuates systemic inflammation in mice
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided
- **Concern** The in vivo efficacy claims are stated without any numerical data, group sizes, dosing regimen, route of administration, or statistical methods. Survival improvement is a strong claim that requires careful experimental design, including blinding, randomization, and appropriate controls. The abstract does not specify whether the effect was dose dependent or whether Baimaside was tested in a therapeutic rather than prophylactic setting.
- **Why it matters** Sepsis models are notoriously variable, and survival claims are often not reproducible across laboratories. Without detailed methods and effect sizes, the translational relevance cannot be assessed. The distinction between pretreatment and treatment is critical for clinical applicability.
- **Resolution test** Provide full in vivo methods, including animal numbers, sex, age, LPS dose and source, Baimaside dose and formulation, timing of administration, and survival curves with statistical analysis. Show organ injury scores or biomarkers and correlate them with IL-6 levels. Ideally, include a therapeutic dosing schedule to support clinical relevance.

- **Concern ID** R1-M4
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** No
- **Axis** Scientific importance
- **Claim pointer** Baimaside acts through a unique mechanism targeting the p50-IL-6 axis, providing a foundation for precision medicine in IL-6 driven disorders
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided
- **Concern** The claim of uniqueness is not supported by comparison with existing literature. Several small molecules and peptides have been reported to modulate NF-kappaB DNA binding, and IL-6 targeted therapies are already in clinical use. The abstract does not discuss how this mechanism differs from known inhibitors or why it would offer advantages over existing IL-6 blockade strategies.
- **Why it matters** The significance of the work depends on its novelty relative to the existing landscape. If similar mechanisms have been described, the contribution is incremental. The precision medicine claim requires a clear articulation of patient selection or biomarker driven application.
- **Resolution test** Provide a comparative discussion of known NF-kappaB DNA binding inhibitors and IL-6 targeted agents. Show data that Baimaside has a distinct binding site or mechanism compared to existing compounds. Clarify the proposed clinical context and how the p50 selectivity would translate to patient benefit.

## Risk / unsupported claims

- The allosteric mechanism of Baimaside on p50 is not supported by the abstract alone and requires structural and biophysical evidence
- The selectivity for IL-6 over TNF-alpha and IL-1beta is stated without data and cannot be evaluated
- The in vivo efficacy claims, including survival improvement and organ protection, are not supported by any numerical or statistical data in the abstract
- The claim of a "unique mechanism" is not contextualized against existing literature and is therefore unverifiable from the supplied material
- The precision medicine application is speculative and not supported by any patient derived data or biomarker analysis
