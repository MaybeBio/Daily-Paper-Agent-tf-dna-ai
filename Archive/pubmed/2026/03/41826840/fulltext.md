# Comparing multi-b-value diffusion models and ROI delineation strategies for prediction of p53 abnormality and microsatellite instability in endometrial cancer


## Background
  Multi-b-value diffusion-weighted MRI holds potential for noninvasively predicting p53 abnormality and microsatellite instability (MSI) in endometrial cancer (EC), but the optimal diffusion model and region of interest (ROI) strategy remain undetermined. This study aims to identify the optimal diffusion model–ROI strategy combination for predicting p53 abnormality and MSI status by comparing diffusion MRI–derived parameters from three ROI approaches.


## Methods
  This retrospective study included patients with EC who underwent preoperative multi-b-value diffusion MRI between December 2020 and June 2025. Two radiologists independently quantified apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC), intravoxel incoherent motion (IVIM), and diffusion kurtosis imaging (DKI) parameters using three ROI strategies (single-slice ROI [ssROI], three-slice ROI [tsROI], and whole-tumor volume ROI [wtROI]. Multivariable logistic regression identified parameters associated with p53 abnormality and MSI. Diagnostic performance was assessed using the receiver operating characteristic curve and compared with the DeLong test. Additionally, the reproducibility of the parameters was evaluated.


## Results
  Among 143 enrolled patients (mean age, 57.73years ± 10.01[SD]), 136 were assessed for p53 status (37 abnormal) and 109 for mismatch repair status (17 MSI). All parameters showed good-to-excellent reproducibility. Elevated D* and lower MD in ssROI, lower D in tsROI, and decreased MD in wtROI were associated with p53 abnormality. Lower ADC across all ROIs was associated with MSI. For predicting p53 abnormality, ssROI-MD yielded the highest AUC of 0.711 (95% CI: 0.627, 0.785), which showed similar performance to other parameter-ROI combinations. For predicting MSI, tsROI-ADC (AUC,0.755 [95%CI:0.646, 0.844]) and wtROI-ADC (AUC,0.758 [95%CI:0.649, 0.846]) significantly outperformed ssROI-ADC (AUC,0.700 [95%CI:0.587, 0.797]; p = 0.006 and 0.025, respectively), with wtROI-ADC showing higher sensitivity (90.9%).


## Conclusions
  MD from a single-slice ROI is optimal for the identification of p53 abnormality, combining diagnostic performance with operational simplicity and high reproducibility. For MSI prediction, ADC from a whole-tumor ROI is preferred due to its superior sensitivity and excellent reproducibility.

## Introduction
  Endometrial cancer (EC) is one of the most common gynecological malignancies worldwide, and its incidence continues to rise [1]. A major revision of the International Federation of Gynecology and Obstetrics (FIGO) staging system in 2023 formally incorporated molecular classification to improve risk stratification and guide treatment decisions [2, 3]. Within this framework, p53 abnormality and microsatellite instability (MSI) have emerged as pivotal biomarkers that guide clinical management. P53 abnormality signifies highly aggressive tumor behavior, being significantly associated with a higher risk of peritoneal metastasis and recurrence [4], as well as shorter progression-free survival and overall survival [5]. In advanced-stage disease, the p53-abnormal subtype may derive greater survival benefit from bevacizumab in combination with chemotherapy [6]. Meanwhile, MSI is associated with a higher rate of lymph node recurrence after treatment [4, 7], which may influence decisions regarding nodal assessment at surgery. Moreover, for patients with recurrent or advanced disease, the addition of immune checkpoint inhibitors to chemotherapy significantly improves survival outcomes [8]. However, current detection of p53 and MSI status relies on immunohistochemical staining or molecular assays. These methods are costly, time-consuming, less accessible in primary care settings, and their accuracy can be compromised by sample quality and quantity [9]. Consequently, there is a critical need to develop non-invasive, efficient, and reliable methods for pretreatment biomarker evaluation.

  Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is the preferred imaging modality for pretreatment evaluation of EC. As a commonly used functional MRI technique, diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) provides the apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC), a quantitative parameter derived from a mono-exponential model that reflects the overall restriction of water molecule diffusion. It serves as a core indicator for clinically assessing tumor cell density, distinguishing benign from malignant tumors, and monitoring treatment response [10]. Building on this, intravoxel incoherent motion (IVIM) modeling disentangles tissue perfusion from pure water diffusion, providing microcirculatory information [11]. Diffusion kurtosis imaging (DKI) employs a non-Gaussian model to characterize tissue microstructural complexity and heterogeneity, utilizing parameters such as mean kurtosis (MK) and mean diffusivity (MD) [12]. Recent studies indicate that p53-abnormal tumors exhibit lower ADC values than p53-normal tumors [13, 14], and quantitative parameters derived from IVIM and DKI hold promise for assessing MSI [15–17]. Despite the potential of these three DWI models, comparative studies assessing their performance in predicting both p53 abnormality and MSI are scarce. Furthermore, the strategies for delineating regions of interest (ROIs) vary considerably across studies. These differences in ROI strategies affect the reproducibility and diagnostic efficacy of the derived parameters [18]. A systematic investigation to identify the optimal ROI strategy is currently lacking.

  We hypothesized that DWI and its advanced models (IVIM and DKI) may offer predictive value for p53 abnormality and MSI status in EC, and that the choice of ROI delineation strategy may influence the diagnostic performance of derived parameters. Therefore, this study aims to systematically compare the value of DWI, IVIM, and DKI-derived parameters obtained using three distinct ROI delineation strategies for predicting p53 abnormality and MSI status in EC, and to determine the optimal “diffusion model–ROI strategy” combination for each specific molecular marker.


## Methods

### Patients
  This single-center retrospective study received approval from our Institutional Review Board, with a waiver of informed consent due to its retrospective design. We initially screened consecutive patients with pathologically confirmed EC who had undergone preoperative pelvic MRI at our institution between December 2020 and June 2025.

  Inclusion criteria were: (1) surgical resection performed at our hospital within two weeks after MRI examination; (2) availability of a preoperative MRI protocol including a multi-b-value DWI sequence; and (3) complete postoperative histopathological records.

  Exclusion criteria comprised: (1) history of neoadjuvant therapy or chemoradiation prior to MRI (n = 4); (2)incomplete pathological data or missing immunohistochemical testing for P53 and mismatch repair (MMR) protein expression (n = 21); (3) tumor visible on fewer than three consecutive axial slices (n = 9); (4) absence of multi-b-value DWI sequences or poor image quality (e.g., significant motion artifacts) (n = 18); and (5) history of other malignant tumors (n = 11). The patient selection process is detailed in Fig. 1.

### MRI protocol
  All MRI examinations were conducted on a 3.0-T scanner (Magnetom Skyra, Siemens Healthineers, Erlangen, Germany) equipped with an 18-channel phased-array surface coil. Before the scan, patients were asked to empty the rectum and maintain a partially filled bladder. The imaging protocol included the following sequences: sagittal and oblique axial T2-weighted imaging (T2WI) without fat saturation (acquired perpendicular to the uterine cavity), oblique axial T1-weighted imaging (T1WI), oblique axial fat-saturated T2WI, oblique axial DWI acquired at b-values of 50 and 800 s/mm² for apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) mapping, and oblique axial contrast-enhanced T1-weighted imaging(CE-T1WI).

  Additionally, an oblique axial multi-b-value DWI sequence based on spin-echo echo-planar imaging (SE-EPI) was applied during the same session to simultaneously derive parameters for IVIM and DKI. The acquisition parameters were set as follows: repetition time (TR) / echo time (TE) = 4700/96 ms; field of view (FOV) = 228 × 228 mm²; slice thickness = 3.5 mm; matrix = 114 × 114; and a set of b-values = 0, 50, 100, 150, 200, 400, 800, 1200, 1600 s/mm². The total scan time for the multi-b-value DWI sequence was 5 min 45 s. Detailed sequence parameters are summarized in Table S1.


### Imaging analysis
  The ADC value was calculated from conventional DWI using b-values of 50 and 800 s/mm². The multi-b-value DWI dataset was processed using custom MATLAB scripts (R2024a, MathWorks) to extract subsets for IVIM and DKI modeling. Corresponding parameter maps were generated using MITK-Diffusion (v2014.10.02).

  where MD represents the mean diffusivity and MK the mean kurtosis. B-values of 0, 400, 800, 1000, and 1600 s/mm² were employed.

  One radiologist (with 2 years of experience in gynecological MRI), blinded to all clinical and pathological details except the initial EC diagnosis, manually delineated regions of interest (ROIs) for ADC, IVIM-, and DKI-derived parameter maps using ITK-SNAP (v.3.8.0). Prior to the analysis, both radiologists underwent unified training on ROI delineation protocols, including standardized instructions for tumor boundary identification and exclusion of necrotic, cystic, or hemorrhagic areas. To evaluate repeatability and reproducibility, the same observer repeated ROI delineation on a randomly selected subset of 20 patients one month after the initial analysis. A second independent reader (with 10 years of MRI experience) delineated ROIs in another randomly selected cohort of 20 patients.

  Tumor localization was performed with reference to T2-weighted and contrast-enhanced T1-weighted images, and ROIs were carefully drawn on high b-value images (b = 800 s/mm² for ADC; b = 1600 s/mm² for IVIM and DKI analyses) to highlight regions with restricted diffusion, while excluding areas exhibiting cystic changes, necrosis, or hemorrhage.

  In accordance with previously reported methodologies [19–21], this study utilized three ROI strategies for quantifying ADC, IVIM, and DKI parameters in tumor regions: (1) A single-slice ROI was manually delineated along the tumor margin on the oblique axial slice exhibiting the largest tumor cross-sectional area (single-slice ROI method, ssROI); (2) A three-slice ROI was drawn by outlining the tumor contour across three consecutive slices, including the above maximum slice, together with the immediate superior and inferior adjacent slices (three-slice ROI method, tsROI); (3) A whole-tumor volume ROI was generated by delineating the tumor boundary on every slice where the tumor was visible (whole-tumor volume ROI method, wtROI). ### Histopathological analysis
  All patients underwent total hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy. Histopathological evaluation, including assessment of hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) and immunohistochemical slides, was reviewed by a specialized pathologist with 15 years of experience, who was blinded to all imaging findings and clinical information except for the initial diagnosis of endometrial cancer.

  MMR status was evaluated by immunohistochemistry for MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, and PMS2. Retention of all four proteins was classified as microsatellite stability (MSS), whereas loss of any one protein indicated microsatellite instability (MSI). Abnormal p53 expression patterns included [22]: overexpression (strong nuclear staining in > 80% of tumor cells), null (complete absence of nuclear staining with intact internal control), or cytoplasmic (diffuse cytoplasmic staining in > 80% of tumor cells) (Figs. 3 and 4).

Comprehensive surgical-pathological staging was performed according to the 2009 FIGO guidelines.


### Statistical analysis
  All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS (version 27.0; IBM Corporation) and MedCalc (version 22.0; MedCalc Software Ltd). The intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) was used to evaluate inter- and intra-observer agreement for each of the three1 ROI delineation strategies. ICC values were interpreted as follows: 0.00–0.20, poor; 0.21–0.40, fair; 0.41–0.60, moderate; 0.61–0.80, good; and 0.81–1.00, excellent agreement. Categorical variables were summarized as numbers and percentages (%). Continuous variables were assessed for normality using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Normally distributed data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation and compared between groups using the independent samples t-test. Non-normally distributed data were reported as median (interquartile range) and compared using the Mann–Whitney U test. Univariable and multivariable logistic regression analyses were performed for DWI, IVIM, and DKI parameters. Variables with p < 0.05 in univariable analysis were included in the multivariable model using a stepwise backward selection method to identify independent predictors of abnormal p53 expression and MSI. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was used to evaluate the diagnostic performance of significant predictors, with sensitivity and specificity reported. The DeLong test was applied to compare areas under the ROC curves (AUCs) between different ROI strategies. To assess model robustness and mitigate overfitting, internal validation was performed using 5-fold cross-validation repeated 1000 times to estimate cross-validated AUC (mean, 95% CI). Additionally, decision curve analysis (DCA) was performed to evaluate the clinical utility of the most promising diffusion parameters for predicting p53 abnormality and MSI status. All tests were two-sided, and p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

### Reproducibility of different ROI measurements
  The intra-reader reproducibility, assessed from two measurements by Reader 1 performed one month apart, ranged from good to excellent for the three ROI methods(ICC range, 0.668–0.988). The inter-reader reproducibility between Reader 1 (first measurement) and Reader 2 was also good to excellent (ICC range, 0.710–0.966) (Table S4). Bland-Altman plots demonstrated good agreement across all parameters and ROI strategies, with the majority of differences within the limits of agreement and no systematic bias observed (Figures S1 and S2).


### Associations between MRI diffusion parameters and molecular status
  Significant differences in diffusion MRI parameters were observed between molecular subgroups across various ROI strategies (Tables S5 and S6).

  For p53 status, the abnormal group showed significantly lower D values in ssROI (p = 0.004), tsROI (p = 0.001), and wtROI (p = 0.005), along with reduced f values in ssROI (p = 0.019) and tsROI (p = 0.012). D* values were elevated across all ROI methods (p = 0.012–0.029), while MD values were consistently lower in all three approaches (p < 0.001–0.002). MK values were higher in ssROI (p = 0.043) and wtROI (p = 0.049). No significant differences were observed in ADC values.

  For MMR status, the MSI group exhibited significantly lower D (p = 0.005–0.009), MD (p = 0.010–0.023), and ADC values (p = 0.003–0.034) across all ROI methods compared to the MSS group. No significant associations were found for f, D*, or MK values with MSI status.


### Multivariable logistic regression analysis

For p53 abnormality, significant independent predictors included: elevated D* (OR = 1.036, 95% CI: 1.003–1.069, p = 0.031) and reduced MD (OR = 0.057, 95% CI: 0.008–0.410, p = 0.004) in ssROI, lower D values in tsROI (OR = 0.018, 95% CI: 0.001–0.397, p = 0.011), and decreased MD in wtROI (OR = 0.058, 95% CI: 0.008–0.445, p = 0.006).

### Diagnostic performance of significant parameters
  In predicting p53 abnormality, ssROI-MD showed an AUC of 0.711 (95% CI: 0.627–0.785, p < 0.001), with a cvAUC (cross-validated AUC) of 0.710 (95% CI: 0.486–0.907). The diagnostic performance was comparable to that of ssROI-D* (apparent AUC = 0.640 [0.554–0.721], cvAUC = 0.639 [0.425–0.836]; p = 0.327), tsROI-D (apparent AUC = 0.680 [0.594–0.757], cvAUC = 0.680 [0.454–0.879]; p = 0.305), and wtROI-MD (apparent AUC = 0.675 [0.589–0.752], cvAUC = 0.675 [0.462–0.875]; p = 0.162). Pairwise comparisons of the ROC curves revealed no statistically significant differences in AUC among these parameters (p = 0.162–0.853, Table 4; Fig. 5a). Decision curve analysis confirmed the clinical utility of ssROI-MD (Table S7, Fig. 6a).

### Subgroup analysis stratified by tumor grade
  For p53 abnormality, in low‑grade (grades 1–2) tumors (n = 108, including 78 p53‑normal and 30 p53‑abnormal), all four diffusion parameters (ssROI‑D*, ssROI‑MD, tsROI‑D, and wtROI‑MD) showed significant differences between p53‑normal and p53‑abnormal groups (all p < 0.05; Table S9).

  For MSI, in low‑grade tumors (n = 87, including 75 MSS and 12 MSI), all three ADC parameters (wtROI‑ADC, ssROI‑ADC, and tsROI‑ADC) demonstrated significantly lower values in the MSI group compared to the MSS group (all p < 0.05; Table S9).

  In high‑grade (grade 3 and non-endometrioid subtypes) tumor, similar trends were observed, but statistical testing was not performed due to the limited sample sizes (p53: n = 28, p53‑abnormal = 7; MMR: n = 22, MSI = 5) (Table S10).


## Discussion
  Accurate assessment of p53 abnormalities and MSI status is crucial for personalized treatment of endometrial cancer. This study systematically compared three ROI delineation strategies and multiple diffusion model parameters for pretreatment prediction of these molecular markers. For p53 abnormality, the D and D* values from the IVIM model and the MD value from the DKI model demonstrated predictive value across different ROI strategies. Among these, the DKI-MD from ssROI exhibited the relatively highest diagnostic performance (apparent AUC = 0.711[0.627–0.785], cvAUC = 0.710[0.486–0.907]), with good intra-observer (ICC = 0.891) and inter-observer (ICC = 0.944) reproducibility. Combined with its operational simplicity, it represents a favorable approach for evaluating p53 abnormality. For MSI status, ADC proved to be the sole independent predictor. The diagnostic performance of wtROI and tsROI significantly surpassed that of ssROI (apparent AUCs of 0.758 and 0.755 vs. 0.700, respectively). Additionally, wtROI demonstrated higher sensitivity (90.9%) and better measurement repeatability (intra-observer ICC = 0.988, inter-observer ICC = 0.956). Our findings indicate that ssROI-MD for p53 assessment and wtROI-ADC for MSI assessment provide an imaging approach that balances diagnostic efficacy, reproducibility, and clinical feasibility.

  Regarding the diffusion characteristics of p53 abnormality, this study found no significant difference in ADC values between p53-abnormal and normal groups across three ROIs, consistent with Sun et al.‘s [14] findings based on the tumor’s largest slice, but different from reports by Tian et al. [13] and Jiang et al. [23] indicating lower ADC values in the p53-abnormal group. This inconsistency may originate from variations in p53 diagnostic criteria, b-value selection for DWI, or population characteristics. It also suggests limitations of traditional mono-exponential models in characterizing microstructural alterations associated with p53 abnormality. In advanced diffusion models, multiple parameters from IVIM and DKI displayed good discriminatory power. Elevated D* may reflect increased microvascular flow associated with mutant p53-mediated angiogenesis, as D* is an IVIM parameter sensitive to microcirculatory blood velocity [11, 24]. Alternative explanations include increased interstitial fluid motion from microscopic necrosis or altered stromal dynamics [25]. Multivariable logistic regression further identified several parameter-ROI combinations as independent predictors, including ssROI-MD, ssROI-D*, tsROI-D, and wtROI-MD, suggesting that IVIM and DKI models potentially offer more information than conventional ADC. Specifically, IVIM-D values based on wtROI were significantly decreased in the p53-abnormal group, consistent with the findings of Wang et al. [26], which supports that the IVIM bi-exponential model more specifically unveils true diffusion restriction caused by increased cellular density due to p53 abnormalities via eliminating perfusion interference. Notably, DKI-derived MD demonstrated stronger correlations than IVIM-D in wtROI analysis. To our knowledge, this is the first investigation exploring the value of the DKI model in predicting p53 abnormalities in endometrial cancer. We hypothesize that p53-abnormal tumors not only manifest as high cell density but may also present extreme microstructural complexity (such as marked nuclear atypia and disordered cell arrangement). By quantifying non-Gaussian diffusion behavior, DKI-MD may more sensitively capture such microstructural heterogeneity. Clinically, MD parameters from ssROI based on the tumor’s largest plane demonstrated the highest diagnostic efficacy, indicating that key biological characteristics of p53 abnormalities are concentrated in the most prominent tumor regions. Combined with good inter-observer consistency and operational simplicity, ssROI-MD shows promising clinical application potential.

  In the assessment of MSI status, this study discovered that ADC, IVIM-D, and DKI-MD values in the MSI group were consistently lower than those in the MSS group across all ROI strategies, indicating that MSI-type endometrial cancer may feature a more compact tumor microenvironment, consistent with reports by Bhosale et al. [27], Wang et al. [15](using a tsROI strategy), and Ma et al. [16] (using a ssROI strategy). From a pathological mechanism perspective, MSI tumors are commonly associated with numerous subtle microsatellite sequence alterations [28], significant lymphocytic infiltration [29], and high intra-tumoral heterogeneity [30]. These features increase cellular structural complexity and spatial crowding, manifesting as reduced D, MD, and ADC values. Notably, previous studies generally shared limitations such as small sample sizes (n = 12–81) and a lack of systematic comparison between different diffusion models. Through multivariate analysis, this study further revealed that among all diffusion parameters, only the ADC value serves as an independent predictor of MSI status. We speculate that ADC, despite being based on a single-exponent model, may effectively integrate the overall impact of various MSI-related microstructural alterations, thereby showing greater robustness.

  On ROI strategy selection, this study found that expanding the sampling range significantly enhances the diagnostic efficacy of ADC. Both wtROI and tsROI demonstrated significantly superior diagnostic performance compared to ssROI, with no statistically significant difference between the two approaches (p = 0.879). Given its higher sensitivity (90.9%), excellent repeatability, and clinical feasibility, wtROI is deemed the preferred choice for assessing MSI status.

  There remain several limitations in this study. First, the retrospective design of this single-center study may introduce sampling bias, and the lack of an external validation cohort limits the generalizability of our findings. Second, the absence of immunohistochemical results for p53 and MMR in some patients led to inconsistent populations across molecular biomarker subgroup analyses, potentially affecting the stability of the findings. Additionally, due to the limited accessibility of POLE gene sequencing in retrospective studies, we could not evaluate the predictive value of multi-b-value diffusion parameters and ROI delineation strategies for POLE gene mutation status, and thus the 2009 FIGO anatomical staging system was retained. Moreover, the MSI subgroup (n = 17) limited statistical power for detecting smaller effect sizes and resulted in wide confidence intervals. Lastly, we primarily evaluated three conventional diffusion models and did not incorporate more advanced novel models such as FROC (Fractional Order Calculus) or CTRW (Continuous-Time Random Walk), which may better capture tumor microstructural heterogeneity.

  In conclusion, this study offers crucial insights for non-invasive assessment of key molecular markers in endometrial cancer by systematically comparing diffusion models and ROI delineation strategies. Our findings indicate that ssROI-MD demonstrates promising diagnostic efficacy for p53 abnormality, while wtROI-ADC shows similar potential for MSI status. Both approaches exhibit good measurement repeatability and clinical feasibility. Future multicenter validation in larger cohorts with standardized acquisition protocols, consistent post‑processing algorithms, and semi-automated segmentation tools is warranted to facilitate clinical translation, along with uniform radiologist training to reduce variability.
