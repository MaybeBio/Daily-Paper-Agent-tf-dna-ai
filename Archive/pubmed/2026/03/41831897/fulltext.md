Spodoptera litura is a highly destructive and serious agricultural insect pest and has developed different degrees of resistance to various insecticides. However, the potential resistance molecular mechanisms and regulatory mechanisms remain unclear. Here, we identified and cloned a nuclear receptor transcription factor SlHR96, which was overexpressed in the indoxacarb-resistant strains and upregulated in response to indoxacarb induction. SlHR96 knockdown suppressed expression of multiple indoxacarb resistance-related detoxification genes (SlCOE050, SlCOE090, SlUGT33J17 and SlABCB3-2) and increased susceptibility to indoxacarb in S. litura. Furthermore, dual-luciferase report assays and yeast one-hybrid assays verified the binding of SlHR96 to the promoters of these detoxification genes. In addition, molecular modeling and insecticide docking showed that SlHR96 protein could bind to indoxacarb and DCJW. These results indicated that SlHR96 could sense the stress signals of indoxacarb and DCJW, and rapidly regulate the overexpression of multiple detoxification genes conferring resistance to insecticide in S. litura, which is critical for a deeper understanding of the regulatory mechanism of insecticide resistance and pest resistance management.
