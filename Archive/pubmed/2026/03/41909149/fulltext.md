# Identification and experimental validation of biomarkers associated with integrated stress response in sepsis


## Background
  Strategies that specifically target the integrated stress response (ISR) as a therapeutic approach in sepsis remain largely unexplored. This study aimed to identify and validate ISR-related biomarkers in sepsis.


## Methods
  This study used 529 ISR-related genes (ISRRGs) alongside sepsis datasets. First, differentially expressed gene between sepsis and normal samples, key module genes associated with sepsis, and ISRRGs were intersected to identify candidate genes. Next, biomarkers were selected through three machine learning methods. Subsequently, enrichment analysis, immune infiltration analysis, regulatory network analysis, and drug prediction were conducted. Finally, the biomarkers were experimentally validated using RT-qPCR.


## Results
  Four biomarkers of sepsis (DYRK2, BCL2, NUP93, and NFATC2) were identified; collectively, these biomarkers are associated with the enrichment of translation initiation in sepsis. A total of 783 significantly upregulated pathways and 1203 significantly downregulated pathways were identified. These biomarkers were co-regulated by multiple microRNAs and transcription factors. Importantly, 726 drugs were predicted to interact with these biomarkers. Additionally, RT-qPCR results demonstrated that the expression levels of DYRK2, BCL2, NUP93, and NFATC2 differed significantly between sepsis and normal samples.


## Conclusion
  DYRK2, BCL2, NUP93, and NFATC2 were identified as biomarkers of sepsis, offering new diagnostic and therapeutic targets for its treatment.


## Introduction
  According to the Sepsis-3 consensus criteria, sepsis is clinically defined as life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection [1]. The Surviving Sepsis Campaign reports that millions of people worldwide are affected by sepsis and septic shock each year, with mortality rates ranging from one-quarter to one-third. Among survivors, there is an increased risk of new or worsening functional impairments, including reduced quality of life, the need for respiratory, nutritional, or technological support, and recurrent severe infections [2]. Therefore, early recognition and intervention are particularly crucial the treatment of sepsis. However, only a limited number of biomarkers are currently used routinely in clinical practice. Acute-phase proteins, such as C-reactive protein (CRP), and more specific markers of bacterial infection, including procalcitonin (PCT), serve as diagnostic markers of infection and can assist in monitoring the response to antibiotics. The search for new biomarkers in sepsis is particularly important for the individualized treatment of patients, especially in the early stages.

  The integrated stress response (ISR) is an evolutionarily conserved signaling network that orchestrates cellular adaptation by coordinating gene expression, metabolic fluxes, and survival decisions under stress conditions [3]. The ISR is initiated when any one of four stress-sensing kinases—protein kinase R-like endoplasmic reticulum kinase (PERK), general control non-derepressible 2 (GCN2), double-stranded RNA-dependent protein kinase (PKR), or heme-regulated eukaryotic translation initiation factor 2α kinase (HRI)—becomes activated and phosphorylates the protein translation initiation factor eukaryotic translation initiation factor 2α (eIF2α). This phosphorylation shifts gene expression toward a comprehensive rewiring of cellular machinery to promote adaptation [3]. In sepsis, ISR hyperactivation contributes to both organ protection, via antioxidant responses, and pathology, through prolonged protein synthesis shutdown [4,5]. Pharmacological ISR modulators, such as PERK inhibitors or ISRIB, have demonstrated efficacy in restoring immune homeostasis in preclinical models [6].

  In this study, we employed a comprehensive bioinformatics pipeline—including differential expression analysis, weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA), and machine learning algorithms (LASSO, SVM-RFE, and Boruta)—along with expression validation across independent cohorts and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve assessment to systematically identify and validate sepsis-associated biomarkers linked to the ISR. Subsequent multi-omics analyses elucidated the functional roles of these biomarkers through pathway enrichment, immune cell infiltration profiling, miRNA-transcription factor regulatory network reconstruction, and computational drug repurposing predictions. Importantly, the clinical relevance of these biomarkers was confirmed via reverse transcription quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR) in patient-derived samples. Our findings provide novel mechanistic insights into ISR-mediated sepsis pathogenesis and propose actionable therapeutic targets for precision management.


## Materials and methods

### Data collection
  Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) provided datasets relevant to sepsis, specifically GSE65682 and GSE232753. The training set, GSE65682 (GPL13667), consisted of 760 sepsis blood samples and 42 normal blood samples. The validation set, GSE232753 (GPL24676), included 20 sepsis blood samples and 8 normal blood samples. Additionally, 534 integrated stress response-related genes (ISRRGs) were obtained from the literature [7], and after removing duplicate genes, 529 ISRRGs remained (Table S1).


### Differential expression analysis
  Using a filtering criterion of P < 0.05 and |log2 (FC)| > 1.5, differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between sepsis and normal samples in GSE65682 were identified [8]. The DEGs were visualized with a heatmap and a volcano plot.


### WGCNA
  In GSE65682, WGCNA was employed to identify key module genes that exhibit significant associations with specific traits (sepsis and normal). Initially, 802 samples from GSE65682 were clustered, and outlier samples were identified and excluded.

  The optimal soft threshold was identified when the mean connectivity approached zero and the R2 value was set to 0.85. Subsequently, coexpression matrices were constructed, and a correlation heatmap was generated to investigate the relationships between modules and traits. Genes with both |GS| > 0.5 and |MM| > 0.5 were designated as key module genes.


### Acquisition and analysis of candidate genes
  Candidate genes were identified by overlapping DEGs, key module genes, and ISRRGs [9]. Subsequently, GO and KEGG analyses were conducted to elucidate the functional roles and associated pathways of the candidate genes. Additionally, a PPI network was established to examine the interactions among the candidate genes at the protein level. Finally, Cytoscape software (v3.9.1) [10] was used to visualize the network.


### Machine learning and identification of biomarkers
  In GSE65682, LASSO analysis was performed [11], and feature genes 1 were identified at the model's minimum error rate [log(lambda.min)], where the coefficients were not penalized to zero. Simultaneously, SVM-RFE was conducted [12], and genes corresponding to the lowest error rates were selected as feature genes 2. Additionally, candidate genes were analyzed [13], and those identified through importance ranking were designated as feature genes 3. To evaluate the stability of the three machine learning algorithms for feature selection, ten-fold cross-validation was performed 100 times for LASSO regression. For each round of cross-validation, the feature genes selected at the minimum lambda value were recorded, and the selection frequency of each gene across the 100 repetitions was calculated. For SVM-RFE, the same 100 repetitions were conducted with a different random seed set for each run, and the selection frequency of feature genes was documented accordingly. For Boruta, repeated validation analyses were carried out with 100 distinct random seeds configured, and the selection frequency of feature genes was recorded as well.

  The intersecting genes shared among feature gene 1, feature gene 2, and feature gene 3 were designated as key genes. Candidate biomarkers were defined as those genes demonstrating consistent expression patterns across both the GSE65682 and GSE232753 datasets, alongside significant differential expression between sepsis and control samples. To assess the discriminatory capacity of these candidate biomarkers in differentiating sepsis from normal samples, ROC curve analyses were conducted for each gene. Genes exhibiting an AUC exceeding 0.7 in both datasets were subsequently selected as definitive biomarkers.


### Enrichment analysis
  To further understand the biological functions of the biomarkers, correlation coefficients between the biomarkers and all genes in GSE65682 were calculated. The clusterProfiler package was then employed to perform gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) with criteria of P < 0.05, FDR <0.25, and |NES| > 1. The top five pathways with the lowest P-values for each biomarker were presented. Gene set variation analysis (GSVA) scores for all samples in GSE65682 were calculated using the GSVA package (v1.46.0) [14] to identify significantly enriched pathways. Subsequently, differences in pathway activity between sepsis and control samples were analyzed. Heatmaps depicting the five most significantly upregulated and downregulated pathways were produced.


### Functional similarity analysis and subcellular localization of biomarkers
  Evaluate the functional similarity among biomarkers. GO annotation data for three different aspects of the human species were first compiled. Based on the obtained annotation data, semantic similarity was calculated. Additionally, protein sequences for the biomarkers were retrieved from NCBI (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/), and subcellular localization was analyzed using the mRNALocater database (http://bio-bigdata.cn/mRNALocater/).


### Immune infiltration analysis
  The CIBERSORT algorithm from the IOBR package (v0.99.8) [15] was applied to the GSE65682 dataset to estimate the infiltration abundance of 22 immune cell types [16] in sepsis and normal samples, excluding samples with no expression data. Differences in immune cell infiltration between sepsis and normal samples were assessed using the Wilcoxon test. Additionally, Spearman correlation analysis was performed to investigate correlations among differential immune cells and between biomarkers and differential immune cells.


### Regulatory network analysis and drug prediction
  MicroRNAs targeting the biomarkers were predicted using the CHEA database within the miRNet 2.0 platform (https://www.mirnet.ca/). Subsequently, TFs were also predicted through the CHEA database in miRNet 2.0. MiRNAs and TFs that targeted all biomarkers were then selected to construct a miRNA-biomarker-TF network using Cytoscape software. Additionally, potential drugs targeting the biomarkers were identified using the DSigDB database (http://dsigdb.tanlab.org/DSigDBv1.0/) and the enrichR package (v3.1) [17], applying a significance threshold of P < 0.05. For each biomarker, the top three drugs ranked by combined score were selected to build a drug-biomarker network, which was visualized using Cytoscape software.


### RT-qPCR
  To preliminarily explore the mRNA expression trend of biomarkers in real clinical samples. Whole blood samples were collected from five patients with sepsis and five healthy controls at Beijing Anzhen Nanchong Hospital, Capital Medical University & Nanchong Central Hospital. The clinical characteristics of the five patients with sepsis are detailed in Table S2. This research protocol has received approval from the Ethics Committee of Beijing Anzhen Nanchong Hospital of Capital Medical University & Nanchong Central Hospital (Approval No. 2024-093). All research activities are conducted in strict accordance with the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. Furthermore, the study fully complies with the applicable laws and regulations stipulated in China's Measures for the Ethical Review of Biomedical Research Involving Humans. All patients provided informed consent. First, total RNA was extracted from the 10 samples using TRIzol reagent, followed by RNA quantification with the NanoPhotometer N50. Then, equal amounts of mRNA were reverse transcribed to synthesize cDNA. Next, qPCR was performed. Finally, gene expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCT method. Primer sequences for the genes are provided in Table S3.

## Discussion
  Sepsis is a life-threatening syndrome caused by a dysregulated host response to infection, and septic shock is one of the leading causes of death among ICU patients. The ISR is a signaling network that detects various cellular stresses, including mitochondrial dysfunction. Identifying therapeutic targets for sepsis through the ISR may offer new survival opportunities for critically ill patients. This study systematically characterized the expression patterns of ISR-related genes in sepsis through the integration of transcriptomic data and multiple machine learning approaches. Our analysis revealed that, despite the distinct functions of the four identified biomarkers (DYRK2, BCL2, NUP93, and NFATC2) they are all significantly enriched within the "eukaryotic translation initiation" pathway and exhibit a positive correlation with the infiltration of CD8+ T cells and activated NK cells. These findings imply that the ISR in sepsis may play a coordinated role in the assembly of the translation initiation complex, thereby modulating the rate of protein synthesis and the effector functions of immune cells, rather than solely regulating apoptosis in isolation.

  DYRK2, a member of the CMGC kinase family, is a critical regulator of cell survival, differentiation, and proteasome-mediated protein degradation [18]. Although the role of DYRK2 in sepsis has not been explicitly documented, its established function as a stress response kinase implies a significant potential relevance. In oncological studies, DYRK2 has been shown to inhibit epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) by facilitating the degradation of Twist [19]. Given that vascular endothelial cells may undergo EMT-like alterations during sepsis, resulting in compromised vascular barrier integrity [20], it is plausible to hypothesize that DYRK2 contributes to endothelial barrier dysfunction in sepsis beyond its effects on apoptosis. GSEA conducted in this study reveals that DYRK2 is significantly associated with the "extracellular matrix degradation" pathway, thereby reinforcing its prospective role in the pathogenesis of vascular leakage through modulation of extracellular matrix homeostasis. These findings provide a theoretical framework to guide future investigations into the involvement of DYRK2 in maintaining vascular barrier function.

  BCL2, a prototypical anti-apoptotic member of the BCL2 protein family, was originally identified in follicular lymphoma characterized by the t(14; 18) chromosomal translocation [21]. Our findings demonstrate a negative correlation between BCL2 expression and M0 macrophages, alongside a positive correlation with memory B cells, aligning closely with the well-established anti-apoptotic role of BCL2 in preserving lymphocyte homeostasis [22]. Recent investigations have further revealed that in patients experiencing septic shock, BCL2 expression in immature or transitional B cells is diminished, concomitant with elevated levels of cleaved caspase-9, implicating the intrinsic apoptotic pathway in the selective depletion of specific B cell subsets [23]. Given the mitochondrial dysfunction commonly observed in sepsis [24], we hypothesize that BCL2 not only governs lymphocyte survival but may also influence the metabolic profiles of innate immune cells through modulation of mitochondrial dynamics. This dual pattern of association—positive correlation with adaptive immune cells and negative correlation with innate immune cells—underscores the potential involvement of BCL2 in the immunopathology of sepsis.

  NUP93, a key component of the nucleoporin family, is essential for the assembly and maintenance of the nuclear pore complex. It facilitates the bidirectional transport of macromolecules between the nucleus and cytoplasm, thereby regulating fundamental cellular processes [25]. Emerging evidence has linked NUP93 mutations to pediatric steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome, underscoring its significance in renal pathophysiology [26]. Notably, a comprehensive review of the literature reveals a lack of direct reports on the involvement of NUP93 in sepsis. Given its critical role in maintaining cellular homeostasis, it is plausible that NUP93 may be implicated in the complex pathophysiological mechanisms of sepsis. Future research should focus on exploring this potential association to enhance our understanding of sepsis and potentially identify novel therapeutic targets.

  The NFATC2 gene encodes the activated T-cell nuclear factor 2 protein, a DNA-binding transcription factor that plays a critical role in gene expression during immune responses [27]. Although direct evidence linking NFATC2 to sepsis is currently lacking, its well-established function in T-cell activation offers a plausible framework for understanding the immunological alterations observed in sepsis. In the present study, the observed downregulation of NFATC2 in sepsis patients may be indicative of T-cell exhaustion. We propose that reduced NFATC2 expression could impair the transcription of key cytokines, such as IL-2, thereby inhibiting T-cell proliferation. This mechanism may provide molecular insight into the attenuation of delayed hypersensitivity reactions commonly seen in sepsis. Notably, all four biomarkers identified in this investigation exhibit positive correlations with CD8+ T cells and activated NK cells. In conjunction with previous findings that the ISR modulates T-cell function via eIF2α phosphorylation [28], the diminished expression of these genes may reflect a state of adaptive immune paralysis. These results suggest potential targets for therapeutic interventions aimed at immune monitoring and modulation in sepsis.

  The four biomarkers identified in this study were all significantly downregulated in sepsis samples. Although this finding may initially appear counterintuitive, it is both plausible and clinically interpretable within the framework of underlying pathophysiological mechanisms. Firstly, the observed expression pattern reflects the protective roles of these genes in maintaining immune homeostasis. Specifically, DYRK2 functions as a regulator of stress responses [29], BCL2 acts as an anti-apoptotic protein [21], NUP93 is involved in preserving nuclear-cytoplasmic transport [25], and NFATC2 modulates immune responses [27]. In the context of sepsis, the excessive inflammatory response coupled with immune paralysis may suppress the pathways associated with these genes, leading to their downregulation. This phenomenon aligns with the documented decreased expression of classical immune molecules such as HLA-DR and CX3CR1 in sepsis [30]. Secondly, the downregulation of these biomarkers holds significant clinical relevance for sepsis diagnosis. Prior studies have reported reduced expression of immune markers including HLA-DR and CD14 in sepsis, which also demonstrate robust diagnostic performance [30]. Consistently, our ROC analysis revealed that the AUC values for these four genes exceeded 0.9, indicating strong discriminatory capacity. Clinically, this "low expression indicating high risk" pattern suggests that values below a defined threshold may serve as indicators of sepsis onset. Finally, this expression profile provides a rationale for considering these genes as potential therapeutic targets. Pharmacological upregulation of these protective genes may help restore immune homeostasis and improve patient outcomes. The candidate drugs predicted in this study, such as vitamin D and lobeline, are grounded in this mechanistic understanding. Therefore, despite the inverse expression pattern relative to conventional biomarkers, these four genes possess clear diagnostic and therapeutic potential, with their downregulated expression reflecting fundamental pathophysiological features of sepsis.

  The analysis of the immune microenvironment further substantiated the clinical significance of these molecules. Our findings revealed significant differences in the infiltration levels of 17 immune cell types between sepsis and control samples. Notably, neutrophils exhibited the highest infiltration in sepsis samples, aligning with the well-documented hyperactivation of the innate immune system in sepsis [31]. Additionally, the observed negative correlation between BCL2 expression and M0 macrophages, alongside its positive correlation with memory B cells, corroborates the established role of BCL2 in promoting B cell survival [23]. All four genes examined demonstrated positive correlations with CD8+ T cells and NK cells. Given that lymphocyte exhaustion frequently manifests during the late stages of sepsis, reduced expression of these genes may serve as indicators of immune paralysis. This relationship between gene expression and immune cell infiltration offers novel insights into the immune dysregulation characteristic of sepsis.

  Bulk RNA sequencing data are limited in their capacity to disentangle the relative contributions of cell type-specific gene expression from alterations in cell population proportions to overall gene expression profiles [32]. This methodological constraint necessitates careful consideration, as variations in cellular composition may confound the interpretation of gene expression signals. Notably, the peripheral blood of sepsis patients exhibits systematic shifts in cell composition compared to healthy controls, characterized by an increased proportion of neutrophils and a decreased proportion of lymphocytes [33]. The four biomarkers identified in this study may be preferentially expressed in particular immune cell lineages. Consequently, the observed differences in gene expression may partially reflect changes in cell type abundance rather than genuine transcriptional regulatory alterations within specific cell populations. To more precisely delineate the mechanistic roles of these genes in the immune dysregulation associated with sepsis, future investigations should integrate higher-resolution methodologies, such as single-cell RNA sequencing or flow cytometry, to validate gene expression patterns at the single-cell level within defined immune cell subsets.

  We predicted 726 drugs using these four biomarkers and constructed a biomarker-drug network based on the top three drugs associated with each biomarker. Among these, vitamin D, predicted by NUP93, may offer protection against sepsis-induced liver injury [34]. Lobeline, also predicted by NUP93, is a respiratory stimulant commonly used to treat respiratory depression caused by various etiologies. Given its established efficacy in enhancing respiratory function, lobeline may potentially alleviate respiratory dysfunction associated with sepsis. Alantolactone, a sesquiterpene lactone compound derived from the root of Inula helenium, is known for its anticancer activity in multiple cancer types [35] and exhibits potential therapeutic effects in sepsis through various mechanisms. It is important to acknowledge that the prediction outcomes include certain compounds with potential toxicity or non-therapeutic applications, such as the organophosphorus insecticide isocarbophos and the industrial solvent methanol. The inclusion of these substances highlights an intrinsic limitation of the current drug prediction database, which encompasses a broad spectrum of molecular entities (including chemical probes and environmental chemicals) rather than being restricted solely to approved clinical drugs [36]. Notably, isocarbophos functions as an acetylcholinesterase inhibitor with a toxicity mechanism that contradicts the therapeutic requirements for sepsis management [37]. Consequently, these predictive findings should not be construed as clinical treatment recommendations but rather as preliminary indications of the chemical space to which biomarkers might potentially bind. To advance these insights, comprehensive in vitro and in vivo investigations are essential for activity screening, toxicity evaluation, and pharmacodynamic validation, alongside structural optimization of candidate compounds, with the aim of identifying molecules genuinely suitable for sepsis therapy.


## Limitation
  This study, employing bioinformatics analyses, identified DYRK2, BCL2, NUP93, and NFATC2 as potential biomarkers for sepsis-related ISR and preliminarily validated their differential expression via RT-qPCR. Nonetheless, several limitations warrant consideration. First, the clinical validation was conducted on a limited sample size, potentially compromising the statistical power and the generalizability of the findings. Second, drug predictions were solely based on computational biology approaches, and the candidate compounds have not undergone experimental validation; thus, these predictions should be regarded as hypothesis-generating tools, necessitating subsequent rigorous in vitro and in vivo screening, toxicity assessments, and pharmacodynamic evaluations. Furthermore, although the four biomarkers were collectively enriched in the "eukaryotic translation initiation" pathway, the functional status of this pathway has not been experimentally confirmed. In summary, the findings of this study are exploratory, and the precise relationships between these biomarkers and components of the ISR pathway require further empirical substantiation. To address these limitations, future research will focus on several key areas: expanding the clinical validation cohort across multiple centers and conducting prospective cohort studies to assess the diagnostic and prognostic utility of the biomarkers; employing conditional gene knockout mouse models to elucidate the stage-specific roles of each biomarker during the hyperinflammatory and immunosuppressive phases of sepsis; experimentally verifying the activation state of the translation initiation pathway in cellular and animal models and establishing its causal involvement in sepsis pathophysiology through rescue experiments; selecting 3–5 high-potential candidate drugs based on predictive analyses for in vitro and in vivo validation, including safety and efficacy assessments; and finally, utilizing techniques such as co-immunoprecipitation and chromatin immunoprecipitation sequencing to investigate the molecular interactions between the biomarkers and core ISR components, thereby providing a theoretical foundation for targeted therapeutic interventions.


## Conclusion
  In summary, this study identifies DYRK2, BCL2, NUP93, and NFATC2 as key ISR-related regulators in sepsis through multi-omics analysis, highlighting their potential roles in modulating translation initiation, immune cell function, and apoptosis.
