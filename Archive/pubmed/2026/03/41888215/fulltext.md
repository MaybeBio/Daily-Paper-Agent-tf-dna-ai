# Immunoinformatic-based design of a multi-epitope subunit vaccine against Ruminococcus torques using subtractive proteomics and molecular dynamics simulations


## Abstract
  Ruminococcus torques is an anaerobic, Gram-positive gut bacterium that has been associated with gastrointestinal diseases including irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) and inflammatory bowel disease (IBD). Its mucolytic effect disrupts the integrity of the intestinal barriers, which are involved in dysbiosis and pathogenicity. Recent clinical evidence has also reported its involvement in extraintestinal infections like pneumonia, keratitis, bacteremia and ocular infection in immunocompromised individuals. Although, R. torques has emerged as a clinically more significant pathogen, no vaccine is yet approved against it, highlighting the urgent need for novel prophylaxis strategies. The primary objective of this investigation was to employ bioinformatics and immunoinformatics strategies in the construction of a candidate multi-epitope vaccine. The complete proteome of Ruminococcus torques strain ATCC 27,756 was obtained and subjected to a series of analyses to find strong B- and T-cell epitopes with high antigenicity, non-allergenicity and non-toxicity. Key proteins such as cell division protein FtsX, single-stranded DNA binding protein and probable peptidoglycan glycosyltransferase FtsW were identified as the final target candidates. A multi-epitope vaccine was designed by linking the selected epitopes with the assistance of suitable spacers and adjuvant. The three-dimensional structure of the vaccine was modeled, refined and validated by in-silico approach. Furthermore, the protein-protein molecular docking was conducted to define probable binding poses on the multi-epitope vaccine against the Toll-like receptor (TLR4) protein. The stability of the vaccine-receptor complex was further validated by molecular dynamics simulation. In silico cloning of the vaccine construct yielded a GC content of 49.29% and a Codon Adaptation Index (CAI) of 0.875 indicating maximum expression potential in the host system. Although computational analyses provide valuable insights, experimental validation is required to confirm the immunogenicity and protective efficacy of the vaccine proposed.


## Introduction
  Ruminococcus torques is gram-positive, obligate anaerobic bacterium that resides in the human gastrointestinal tract7. It belongs to the family Lachnospiraceae, class Clostridia and phylum Firmicutes and was first described by Holdeman and Moore in 197423. A distinguishing characteristic of R. torques is its strong mucin-degrading capability mediated by an extensive repertoire of carbohydrate-active enzymes (CAZymes) including glycoside hydrolases and polysaccharide lyases32. These enzymes enable the bacterium to degrade mucin glycoproteins and their O-linked glycans which are major structural components of the intestinal mucus layer50.

  Under normal physiological conditions, mucin degradation contributes to microbial ecological balance. However, excessive colonization or dysbiosis involving R. torques can compromise the integrity of the mucus barrier. Depletion of mucin weakens epithelial protection, increasing intestinal permeability and facilitates microbial translocation thereby triggering inflammatory responses65. Elevated levels of R. torques have been reported in patients with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) including Crohn’s disease and ulcerative colitis, as well as in other dysbiosis-associated conditions54. Its overabundance has also been linked to metabolic disorders such as obesity potentially contributing to systemic inflammation through gut barrier disruption44.

  From an immunological perspective, mucin degradation by R. torques exposes intestinal epithelial cells to luminal antigens and microbial components, leading to activation of innate immune pathways. Disruption of the mucosal barrier promotes recognition of microbial-associated molecular patterns (MAMPs) by pattern recognition receptors such as Toll-like receptors (TLRs), resulting in the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines and chemokines. Persistent immune activation may contribute to chronic intestinal inflammation and disease progression in susceptible individuals. Thus, R. torques plays a dual role in gut ecology, functioning as a commensal organism under balanced conditions but potentially contributing to inflammatory pathology when dysregulated 51.

  Given its association with gut barrier dysfunction and chronic inflammatory diseases, R. torques represents a potential target for preventive and therapeutic strategies3. In contrast, advances in computational biology and bioinformatics have revolutionized vaccine development by enabling rapid, cost-effective and targeted vaccine design with minimal reliance on laboratory experimentation59. Reverse vaccinology has emerged as a powerful in-silico approach that allows vaccine candidate identification directly from genomic and proteomic data without the need to culture the pathogen. This strategy focuses on predicting immunogenic epitopes capable of eliciting both humoral and cellular immune responses while minimizing adverse effects and has been successfully applied to numerous pathogenic organisms52.

  In this context, this investigation seeks to design a targeted vaccine R. torques using comprehensive in-silico approaches to elucidate its pathogenic role in IBD. The entire proteome of R. torques was screened using a subtractive proteomics pipeline integrated with reverse vaccinology to identify essential, antigenic, and non-homologous proteins as potential vaccine candidates. These selected proteins were further analyzed for T-cell and B-cell epitope prediction, with emphasis on highly conserved and strongly antigenic epitopes. Suitable linkers and adjuvants were incorporated to construct a multi-epitope subunit vaccine. The designed vaccine was evaluated for physicochemical properties, structural stability, antigenicity and allergenicity using various online bioinformatics tools. Additionally, molecular docking was performed to assess the interaction between the vaccine construct and Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4), followed by in silico cloning to evaluate its feasibility for polyprotein expression. Collectively, this study proposes a novel computationally designed vaccine that combines multiple antigenic peptides of R. torques and holds potential as an effective immunization strategy to mitigate R. torques-associated gut dysbiosis and inflammatory bowel disease.


## Methodology

### Retrieval of protein sequences and identification of conserved regions
  Complete proteome sequences of Ruminococcus torques strain ATCC 27,756 (Proteome ID: UP000003577) in FASTA format were retrieved from UniProt (https://www.uniprot.org/ ), a free database storing protein sequences and functions63. For identifying essential proteins in R. torques, a BLASTp (https://blast.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Blast.cgi ) search was conducted against the Database of Essential Genes (DEG) (https://www.essentialgene.org/ ) of known essential proteins, compared with the R. torques proteome71. These proteins are vital for the survival, growth and the significant biological processes of the bacterium. These proteins are significant for the survival of an organism in a particular environment47. To identify membrane-associated proteins among the important protein candidates, subcellular localization was predicted using the CELLO tool (https://cello.life.nctu.edu.tw/ ), as membrane-bound proteins are more accessible to the host immune system68. Although membrane-associated proteins have historically been favored for their surface accessibility, important cytoplasmic proteins with high antigenicity and immunogenic potential can potentially be useful vaccine candidates20. The filtered proteins were then analyzed for antigenicity, with a cut-off value of 0.5, using the Vaxijen server (https://www.ddg-pharmfac.net/vaxijen/VaxiJen/VaxiJen.html ), since proteins with high value are reported to induce a strong immune response upon exposure to the host12. Prediction of potential transmembrane helices of the target proteins was carried out using the TMHMM v-2.0 server (https://services.healthtech.dtu.dk/services/TMHMM-2.0/).

### Computational prediction of cytotoxic T lymphocyte epitopes
  Cytotoxic T lymphocyte (CTL) cells are an important aspect of the immune system because they include certain antigens4. In MHC-I complex, CTLPred (https://tools.iedb.org/mhci/ ) was utilized to predict CTL epitopes from the protein of interest in FASTA format. A combined epitope prediction was used and stress was laid in the choice of CTL epitopes having a consensus score of less than 222. The reduction of the score reflects an enhanced capacity of these epitopes to bind to the MHC-I molecules, making them favorable candidates for the construction of vaccine sequences. CTLPred is designed to identify putative T cell epitopes in a given sequence of proteins based on their capacity to be bound by MHC-I molecules so that these may be recognized by the cytotoxic T cells30. To assess the antigenicity of the chosen epitopes, Vaxijen 2.0 server (https://www.ddg-pharmfac.net/vaxijen/VaxiJen/VaxiJen.html) was used with a 0.5 threshold. The word antigenicity is employed when referring to the capacity of an epitope to induce an immune response. AllerTop 2.0 server (https://www.ddg-pharmfac.net/allertop_test/) and ToxinPred server (https://webs.iiitd.edu.in/raghava/toxinpred2/ ) were also employed to predict allergenicity and toxicity10,49. The fact that the chosen epitopes contain widespread antigenicity but lack allergenicity and toxicity renders them a promising target candidate in vaccine development5.


### Prediction of helper T lymphocytes epitopes
  The helper T lymphocytes (HTLs) are significant parts of the adaptive immune system, wherein they organize cell-mediated and humoral immunity to extraneous pathogens. MHC-II binding tool which can be accessed through Immune Epitope Database (IEDB) (https://tools.iedb.org/mhcii/) was used to predict the HTL epitopes from the targeted proteins41. HTL epitopes were chosen based on their low percentile rank values, indicating strong MHC-II binding. These T cells can act in synergies with B cells to form antibodies specific to neutralize active pathogenic cells. This is a collaborative process because they react in collaboration with cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTL) and macrophages to work together in destruction and elimination of the unwanted pathogens67.


### Identification and analysis of linear B-cell epitopes
  Linear B-cell epitopes (LBL) are amino acid sequences on the protein surfaces that may be recognized by antibodies. They can stimulate cellular and humoral immunity by means of antibodies or B cell receptors because they are detected by them35. Vaccine development has such vital epitopes as they boost the adaptive immunity by enhancing the defense against the immune response. The ABCPred server (https://webs.iiitd.edu.in/raghava/abcpred/ABC_submission.html) was used to predict B-cell epitopes in the target protein linearly, and the threshold value was taken as 0.5 for prediction37. The antigenicity, toxicity and allergenicity of the predicted B-cell epitopes were analyzed by using the Vaxijen 2.0, ToxinPred and AllerTop 2.0 servers. This overall examination has made sure that the anticipated B-cell epitopes that exist within vaccine constructs not only can induce an immune response (antigenicity) but are non-toxic and non-allergic making it the perfect candidates in vaccine constructs25.

  Furthermore, to avoid potential autoimmune responses, all shortlisted CTL, HTL and B-cell epitopes were BLASTp compared to Homo sapiens proteome. Epitopes having considerable resemblance to human proteins based on percent identity and query coverage were eliminated from further analysis. Only non-homologous epitopes were chosen. Lastly, epitopes that met all of the selection criteria were ranked and the best-performing candidates were added to the multi-epitope vaccine design.


### Population coverage analysis
  To ensure that the predicted T-cell epitopes are applicable globally, the Immune Epitope Database (IEDB) Population Coverage tool (https://tools.iedb.org/population/)42 was used. The HLA alleles corresponding to the shortlisted CTL and HTL epitopes were submitted into the program which estimated the projected population coverage percentage (PPC %) across various geographical regions worldwide. The epitope prediction was done using a comprehensive set of globally prevalent MHC class I and class II alleles to make sure that many people are covered in the population. Selection of epitopes used was not dependent on preference of an individual allele but instead to be selected based on binding affinity, antigenicity score, non-allergenicity, non- toxicity and global population coverage. The analysis was run with the default parameters. Epitopes that contribute to increased cumulative population coverage were targeted throughout the final multi-epitope vaccine construct design to improve the vaccine candidate’s effectiveness worldwide.


### Computational design of vaccine constructs
  The multi-epitope vaccine (MEV) was constructed by prudently integrating adjuvants and epitopes from CTL, HTL and B cells62. Adjuvants are important in improving the immunogenicity of vaccine constructions, which stimulate a better immune response in vaccines. The adjuvant selected in this study was cholera enterotoxin subunit B (Accession No: P01556) was placed at the N-terminal of the vaccine16. To connect the components, EAAAK linkers were employed to conjugate the CTL epitopes to the adjuvant, which offered the components structural stability and preserved the functional activity of the epitopes. CTL and HTL epitopes were joined with GPGPG and AAY linkers, thus making them easy to present and improve their immune responses. The bi-lysine (KK) linkers were used to maintain the exact immunogenic functionality of linear B-cell (LBL) epitopes. The resultant vaccine design was further assessed using the ANTIGENpro server, an alignment-free and pathogen-independent predictor based on protein microarray data, in order to increase the accuracy of antigenicity prediction. Probable antigenicity was indicated by a score greater than 0.5. The immunogenic efficiency and structural stability of a construction require a systematic arrangement of the construction. Therefore, the MEV construct will be a potent candidate in vaccine development38.


### Structural analysis
  The MEV sequence was put through various rigorous evaluations and analyses to ensure that it is safe and effective as a candidate vaccine. To ensure that the MEV did not exhibit significant similarity to human proteins, a BLASTp search against Homo sapiens proteome was made18. The physiochemical characteristics of the construct were evaluated through ProtParam server (https://web.expasy.org/protparam/), such as aliphatic index (AI), theoretical isoelectric point (PI), instability index (II), molecular weight (MW), Grand Average of Hydropathicity (GRAVY) and in vivo in vitro half-life. The immunological efficacy was confirmed through testing the antigenic and immunogenicity profiles of the multi-epitope vaccine with the IEDB immunogenicity tools and the Vaxijen v2.0 server. The AllerTop v2.0 server was used to calculate the number of allergenic potentials to ensure that the construct could be used safely in its possible application in human beings. Prediction of secondary structural features of the designed MEV was employed through the SOPMA tool (https://npsa.lyon.inserm.fr/cgi-bin/npsa_automat.pl?page=/NPSA/npsa_sopma.html) to measure the percentage of random coils, alpha-helices, beta-turns, and extended chains. These intricate structural studies enable scientists to comprehend stability, functionality and applicability in vaccine development17.


### Prediction of tertiary structure, refinement and validation
  As the vaccine construct has multiple epitopes, its 3D structure was predicted by the AlphaFold2 server (https://colab.research.google.com/github/sokrypton/ColabFold/blob/main/AlphaFold2.ipynb), a web server that is defined by the generation of extremely accurate protein models64. The 3D structure was further improved, refined and optimized for better stereochemical purity by the GalaxyRefine server (http://galaxy.seoklab.org/refine)21. This was to increase the precision and quality of the predicted protein structure. Lastly, the quality of the model was evaluated on the RAMPAGE server (https://saves.mbi.ucla.edu/), which calculates the quality based on Ramachandran plot statistics. The occurrence of potential errors, the quality and correctness of predicted model of MEV construct were verified by using the ERRAT server (https://www.doe-mbi.ucla.edu/errat/)66.


### Screening of discontinuous B-cell epitopes
  The B-cell epitopes are linear epitopes (continuous) and conformational epitopes (discontinuous). ElliPro program (https://tools.iedb.org/ellipro/) was utilized to find the discontinuous B-cell epitopes46. The 3D structure of the proteins was taken as an input in ElliPro to find the areas where amino acids assemble to form the epitope. The theoretical isoelectric point (PI) score was utilized in the identification of discontinuous B-cell epitopes. Higher PI values show the proportion of the protein that shall be included in an antibody binding. This is important for multi-epitope vaccine construct which can be linked with antibodies and immune response29.


### Molecular docking of host immune receptor with designed vaccine
  Molecular docking was performed to ascertain the binding capacity of the constructed vaccine with the immune receptors36. The specific immunological receptor that will be targeted as part of the study is Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4). TLR4 lies at the heart of the human immune system as it is specific and sensitive to the detection and response of foreign microbes27. The ClusPro server (https://cluspro.org/login.php), a reliable molecular docking server was employed to generate protein-protein complex models of the vaccine with the receptor28. The known ligand-binding interface of TLR4 was taken into consideration to ensure biological relevance, even though ClusPro conducts blind protein-protein docking without the need for preset binding sites. From the previously published structural and functional studies of the TLR4-MD-2 complex, key residues implicated in ligand recognition were identified60. This made it possible to assess the docked complexes in relation to the interaction surface that is biologically significant. The resulting docked complexes were visualized using Chimera (https://www.cgl.ucsf.edu/chimera/), 3D molecular structure viewer software. The docked complex was visualized using the educational version of PyMOL (https://www.pymol.org/)69. Finally, the docked complex interactions were analyzed using the PDBsum server (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/thornton-srv/software/PDBsum1/) that gives detailed information regarding interface residues and binding interactions31.


### Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations
  MD simulation is a computational approach used to study the dynamic behavior and stability of the protein-protein complexes24.The methodology used for the MD simulations performed subsequent to the protein-protein molecular docking includes a few important steps. After identifying the most likely complex, the MD simulations were conducted using the GROMACS 2019 package to determine the most favorable interaction orientations between the vaccine candidate and receptor proteins9. To set up the physiological environment, sodium and chloride ions were added at a neutral concentration of 150 mM salt15. The energy for each system was minimized with the steepest descent method until the maximum force (Fmax) was minimized to be less than 10 kJ/mol to have an energetically favorable initial configuration. Constant bond lengths of all covalent bonds in the simulation were kept by the LINCS algorithm. The algorithm used to deal with long-range electrostatic interactions was the PME method having a cutoff radius of 0.9 nm being employed in Columbic and Van der Waals force70. The systems were equilibrated in two stages: 100 ps NVT ensemble to stabilize the system with fixed particle number (N), pressure (P) and Temperature (T). The equilibration steps are necessary to stabilize the systems prior to the production runs. Periodic boundary conditions (PBC) were run in the XYZ coordinates so that the atoms remained within the simulation box. On equilibration, the following analyses were performed with GROMACS modules and visualization and plotting with visual molecular dynamic between the receptor proteins and vaccine candidate43.


### Normal mode analysis of the MEV-receptor complex
  Normal mode analysis (NMA) is among the standard techniques to study the mobility of proteins and identify the protein’s shared motions relative to their native coordinates. In this analysis, iMODS server (https://imods.iqf.csic.es/) was employed, which provides some helpful information regarding the intrinsic motions of proteins with complex nature14. It calculates various parameters including eigenvalues and covariance, B-factors and deformability to represent the stability and dynamics of the protein53.


### Immune simulation
  C-ImmSim 10.1 server (https://kraken.iac.rm.cnr.it/C-IMMSIM/index.php) was employed to predict the immune response against the vaccine construct predicted. C-ImmSim is employed to simulate the interactions of the immune system with modeling of the key components i.e., bone marrow, lymph nodes and thymus48. The C-ImmSim server chose the following HLA alleles: HLA-A01:01, HLA-A02:01, HLA-B07:02, HLA-DRB101:01, and HLA-DRB1*04:01. In order to replicate primary and booster doses, the simulation includes three injections of the vaccine at various time steps (time steps 1, 84, and 168, where each time step represents 8 h). The number of steps per simulation, random seed, and simulation volume were all left at their default values. This type of in-silico would be very helpful in identifying the potential effectiveness of the vaccine and the immunological effective use of the vaccine and reduces the need for conducting either in vitro or in vivo studies39.


### Codon optimization and in-silico cloning
  Codon usage is species-specific, so if there is variation in codon utilization in the two organisms, codon adaptation is a process of increasing the translation efficiency of the foreign genes within the host. After a vigilant evaluation of MEV characteristics and immune response, codon optimization was performed and in silico cloning was accomplished33. MEV codon optimization was conducted at the Java Codon Adaptation Tool (JCAT) server (https://www.jcat.de/) to facilitate expression with a widely used prokaryotic expression system, E.coli K1219. Other available options were excluded to prevent rho-independent transcription termination, prokaryotic ribosome binding-site and restriction enzyme cleavage-site. GC content and codon adaptation index (CAI) were determined72. The optimized vaccine construct was subsequently cloned into the PTZ57R vector using NAEI and HindIII restriction sites using SnapGene software (https://www.snapgene.com/) to ensure a smooth cloning process and subsequent experimental confirmation26.

### Construction of multi-epitope vaccine
  A vaccine construct was also made by amalgamating the most advantageous epitopes, including 6 CTL epitopes, 4 HTL epitopes and 2 B-cell epitopes with a compatible adjuvant and linkers. The 236 amino acid cholera enterotoxin subunit B was placed at the N-terminus of the vaccine through an EAAAK linker to enhance the immunogenicity. CTL, HTL and B-cell epitopes were cross-linked with AAY, GPGPG and KK linkers respectively to maintain the individual immunological characteristics. The construct vaccine contained 330 amino acid residues (Fig. 1A–B). VaxiJen was used to predict the final multi-epitope vaccine construct’s antigenicity (threshold = 0.5), and the construct was categorized as antigenic. The ANTIGENpro server was used to conduct cross-validation in order to better validate this prediction and lessen methodological bias. The construct’s high antigenic potential and the accuracy of the in silico prediction were confirmed by its antigenicity score of 0.716140.

### Evaluation of population coverage
  For designing multi-epitope vaccine, population coverage analysis was conducted based on the particular CTL and HTL epitopes58. There are 6 CTL and 4 HTL epitopes available to be selected in order to identify the population coverage for the respective allele. The statistical data depict that it has a global coverage of 95.65% using the selected epitopes. Though other countries’ registered population coverage was included in Finland for 93.01%, Italy for 92.31%, and France for 90.79% and South Korea for 90.31%. Briefly, this study validated that the filtered epitopes would be a suitable candidate for constructing MEV. (Fig. 1C)


### Physiochemical and immunogenic analysis
  The ProtParam tool was used to determine the stereochemical properties of the vaccine construct. The structural characteristics of the vaccine were a molecular weight of 36418.57 Da and an isoelectric point (pI) of 9.41. The vaccine design exhibited average half-life 30 h in vitro, greater than twenty hours in vivo (yeast) and greater than ten hours in vivo (E.coli). The GRAVY (grand average of hydropathy) was estimated to be 0.168. Given its advantageous antigenic, physicochemical and immunological characteristics, R. torques is a suitable candidate for the creation of multi-epitope vaccines.

  The tertiary structure of R. torques was predicted by the web platform AlphaFold2. Besides, Galaxy-refine server was also utilized to improve structural refinement of the vaccine. ### Molecular docking with host immune receptor
  Molecular docking is an evaluative tool of comprehending the binding activity of vaccine constructs and immune receptor proteins. The constructed multi-epitope vaccine was docked with human toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4) through ClusPro server. ClusPro a popular protein-protein docking platforms, which employs a hybrid algorithm involving experimental binding site information combined with small-angle X-ray scattering data for enhancing the docking accuracy. Docking of 3D optimized vaccine construct against TLR4 receptor (PDB ID: 2Z62) resulted in 10 docking models. The first one met the criteria and had 65 members in cluster and − 1135.9 kcal/mol energy, indicating the stability of the docked complex. Receptor-MEV interaction was confirmed with PDBsum that indicated MEV interacted well with chain A of the receptor, established 18 hydrogen bonds (Fig. 4B-C). The 3D structure of the complex was also checked with PyMOL software (Fig. 4A). Thermodynamic binding energy complex parameters were calculated using PRODIGY website. Specifically, the Gibbs free energy ΔG (ΔG = RT in Kd) or the equilibrium dissociation constant Kd was calculated to be 5.2 × 10-15 at 37 °C and ΔG -20.3 kcal/mol.

### Molecular dynamic simulations
  In this research, MD simulations were carried out with GROMACS software to observe the stability of an interaction between a multi-epitope-based vaccine and the docked TLR4 complex at the microscopic level (Fig. 5). To ensure enough sampling of conformational space had been done and to assess structural stability, a 100 ns production run was carried out. It is a very common length used in computational vaccine design research to measure parameters. Various imperative parameters such as root mean square deviation (RMSD), root mean square fluctuation (RMSF), radius of gyration (Rg) and solvent-accessible surface area (SASA) as a function of time were observed by using the trajectory data36. The RMSD analysis exhibited that both Chain A (the receptor) and Chain Z (the vaccine construct) remained stable across the 100 ns trajectory, with no notable structural changes. Chain A had an average variation of ~ 1.0–2.0 nm, while Chain Z ranged between ~ 1.5–2.5 nm, signifying the complex reached equilibrium and maintained structural stability. The RMSF analysis also revealed the elasticity of each chain’s individual residues. Chain A showed extremely minor changes in the majority of its residues, with substantial peaks in the loop and terminal regions. On the same note, Chain Z exhibited modest oscillations throughout the residues under study, with binding site residues remaining stable, showing that interchain interaction was still present and the core binding region was less flexible. The vaccine-receptor complex’s size was assessed using the radius of gyration (Rg). The Rg values remained comparable (about 3.5–4.0 nm) throughout the 100 ns trajectory, indicating that the complex did not undergo any unfolding-like conformational changes but instead maintained its structural compactness. The solvent-accessible surface analysis (SASA) demonstrated that the solvent exposure decreased (from around 380 nm2 at the start of simulation to about 340 nm2 at the end of equilibration). This decrease suggests that the complex was packed better and stabilized on the surface during the simulation. In general, all MD simulation metrics (RMSD, RMSF, Rg, and SASA) supported the idea that the vaccine-receptor complex was structurally stable, dynamically favorable, and compact during the simulation, hence validating its potential biological relevance.

### Immune simulations
  The immunological simulations are a full description of humoral and cytokine-mediated responses following exposure to antigens. Based on the antibody profile, the concentration of antigens rises gradually but rapidly declines under the effect of both IgM and IgG antibodies. The level of IgM increases first and it reaches its peak in the early phase while IgG (IgG1 and IgG2 specifically) builds up steadily and sustains the high levels, which is an indication of the establishment of long-term adaptive immunity. This interactive mechanism renders antigen permission effective and creates immunological memory. The cytokine dynamics also emphasize the efficacy of the immune. IFN-g then emerges as the primary cytokine with a very strong peak at day 15, and the predominance of the marker mirrors the contribution of the cytokine to cell-mediated immunity. Early IL-2, IL-4, IL-12 and IL-18 promote activation and differentiation of T cells, while anti-inflammatory IL-10 and TGF-b suppress overactivation of the immune response through regulatory balance. Together, these facts indicate that the immune system is able to integrate both cytokine and antibody-mediated responses to produce a highly effective, adaptive and tightly regulated response to the pathogen, which ultimately results in the destruction of the pathogen and lasting immunity. ## Discussion
  R. torques as an important member of being gut micro biota with both pathogenic and commensal function, demonstrated its clinical importance by contributing to conditions like inflammatory bowel disease, making this bacterium a latent target for therapeutic interventions50. It is challenging to carry out traditional approaches because the bacterium is aggressive, making the expansion of innovative solutions, for instance, vaccine as an imperative requirement6. Immunoinformatics and reverse vaccinology are the promising approaches to identify new vaccine targets against the dangerous pathogens in a timely and cost-effective manner. This kind of integration of advanced technologies and computing software has made the process of vaccine construct evolve significantly easier in identifying and analyzing potential antigens45. The vaccine is also constructed to work on both the innate and adaptive immune system and by doing so, boost the human immune system to defend against a wide range of diseases caused by this bacterium. In this study, MEV-based R. torques vaccine was constructed using subtractive proteomics in conjunction with immunoinformatics, molecular docking, and simulation approaches40.

  The central proteome analysis identified the essential proteins that were non-homologous with human proteins and possess strong antigenic characteristics. To minimize potential off-target effects and retain beneficial gut bacteria, a homology screening process was included as critical step in target prioritization. A subtractive proteomics approach was used to eliminate proteins that shared significant similarities with host proteins and non-target species. This strategy enhances pathogen specificity while reducing the likelihood of cross-reactivity with closely related commensal Firmicutes. Such selectivity is especially relevant in microbiome-targeted vaccine development where maintaining ecological balance is essential to prevent dysbiosis and unintended alterations in host microbial homeostasis11,18. Among the recognized proteins, single-stranded DNA-binding protein (SSB), FtsX and FtsW are imperative in bacterial virulence and survival. SSB is very significant in maintaining the genome stability during DNA repair and replication, predominantly in circumstances of stress-induced conditions to ensure the resilience of R. torques55. FtsX most probably controls peptidoglycan reorganization during cell division in R. torques and improves septal strength and survival during competition in the gut. FtsW probably acts as peptidoglycan glycosyltransferase, strand polymerization of glycans takes place in septum formation to ensure cell wall stability and flexibility in the gut61. Collectively, these findings facilitated the selection of highly antigenic proteins that constituted the basis of the multi-epitope vaccine, underscoring the rational, structure-informed, and target-specific design strategy employed in this study. Intensive selection was applied to screen out CTL, HTL and B-cell epitopes with high antigenicity but simultaneously with non-allergenicity and non-toxicity. It employs B-cell epitopes to induce humoral immunity responses that neutralize the disease-causing agents and enable the formation of a memory response that would be utilized upon subsequent encounters. However, T cells such as CTLs and HTLs play central role in the development of cellular immunity that prevents disease process by destroying infected cells or by the production of anti-microbial cytokines providing decade’s long immunity. Multi-epitope chimeric vaccine constructs were built based on overlapping lead MHC-I, MHC-II and B-cell epitopes and with particular linker and adjuvant sequences employed to boost efficacy57. Analytical methods of the designed vaccine revealed that the multi-epitope vaccine was antigenic, non-allergenic and non-toxic. Predictive solubility tests revealed that the vaccine would be easily expressible and bioavailable in the host system. The reason is that solubility is highly important in determining the effectiveness of subunit vaccines in developing strong immune responses11. The docking experiments showed active binding of the vaccine construct with the TLR4 receptor, another central player in the innate immune system. Molecular dynamics simulations also determined that the vaccine-TLR4 complex is stable, which is indicative of its ability to mediate innate immunity1. The codon optimization enabled the construct to be utilized to express the vaccine in E.coli K12, with a codon adaptation index of 1.0 and GC content of 49.29 which are both high transcription and translation efficiency. Although codon optimization was done for E. coli expression to aid in initial cloning and experimental validation, large-scale vaccine manufacture may necessitate adaption to eukaryotic expression systems like CHO or HEK293 cells. Future research could include mammalian codon optimization and comparative expression analysis to assess manufacturability and translational scalability. Immune simulation analysis also proved that the design construct is able to induce strong humoral and cellular responses that justify its potential efficiency as a vaccine candidate8. Although C-ImmSim provides valuable preliminary insights into potential immunological dynamics, its predictions rely on predefined host genetic parameters, immune repertoires, and immunization schedules. Therefore, the simulated responses should be interpreted as theoretical projections rather than definitive evidence of protective efficacy. As with other in silico approaches employed in subtractive proteomics-driven vaccine design, these findings require subsequent in vitro and in vivo validation to confirm their biological relevance and translational potential. The physiochemical attributes are expected to be increased in hydrophilicity as well as stability towards the vaccine constructions. The models are also showing good solubility, low molecular weight and thermodynamic stability so that the constructions of the vaccine can be prepared and delivered in the host in an efficient manner13. Other in-silico methods have been found beneficial in constructing vaccine constructions that are competent in protecting against a number of diseases. These approaches have successfully been utilized to provide vaccines against pathogens as diverse as Mycoplasma pneumoniae, Salmonella Typhimurium and Campylobacter jejuni among others. The study has its limitations despite providing promising results. Immunoinformatics predictions were highly dependent on computer algorithms that could not exactly imitate biological behavior56.

  Hence, in vitro and in vivo tests for confirmation of the vaccine’s safety and efficacy are needed. Further information regarding the pathogenesis of R. torques and its interaction with the host immune system would assist in concentrating the areas of vaccine focus and producing better results. It is rationally designed construct of multi-epitope vaccine, which would potentially trigger optimal immune responses against R. torques. Further experimental evidence is required, yet such a potential vaccine for this type would be best in countering the complications of this opportunistic pathogen without disturbing the equilibrium of gut micro biota34. Collectively, the findings highlight the feasibility and translational potential of a computationally designed multi-epitope vaccine (MEV) against R. torques, establishing a robust framework for experimental validation and future vaccine development strategies.


## Conclusion
  This study applied an integrated subtractive proteomics and reverse vaccinology approach to design a novel multi-epitope vaccine (MEV) candidate against Ruminococcus torques. Essential, non-homologous and antigenic proteins, including SSB, FtsX, and FtsW, were identified as promising targets based on their roles in bacterial survival. The MEV construct was rationally engineered with suitable adjuvants and linkers to enhance immunogenicity and structural stability. In silico analyses demonstrated favorable physicochemical properties, strong antigenicity, non-allergenicity, solubility, and stable interaction with TLR4, suggesting potential activation of innate immune responses. Immune simulations predicted robust humoral and cellular immunity, while codon optimization supported efficient expression in E. coli K12. Overall, the findings highlight the translational potential of the proposed MEV; however, in vitro and in vivo studies are necessary to validate its safety, immunogenicity, and protective efficacy.
