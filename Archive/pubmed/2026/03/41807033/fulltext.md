# AGPAT3 reshapes tumor cell vulnerability to IFNγ-mediated ferroptosis and enhances immunotherapy efficacy through lipid remodeling


## Background
  Ferroptosis plays a critical role in immune regulation and tumor microenvironment remodeling. However, its therapeutic potential in enhancing immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) efficacy remains incompletely understood and warrants further investigation.


## Methods
  To investigate the potential of ferroptosis in improving ICI response, we constructed a machine learning-based predictive model using ferroptosis-related genes and analyzed large-scale single-cell RNA sequencing datasets. Mechanistic studies were performed to examine the role of interferon (IFN)-γ signaling in ferroptosis sensitization, including functional validation in vitro and in vivo. Lipidomic, transcriptomic, chromatin Immunoprecipitation sequencing (ChIP-seq) and Cleavage Under Targets and Tagmentation analyses were employed to dissect downstream pathways, focusing on IRF1 and AGPAT3.


## Results
  Our model successfully predicted ICI response based on ferroptosis-related gene signatures, identifying IFN-γ as a key enhancer of ferroptosis sensitivity in tumor cells. IFN-γ treatment induced activation of the transcription factor IRF1, which in turn upregulated AGPAT3 expression, driving lipid remodeling and accumulation of polyunsaturated ether phospholipids. This lipid remodeling significantly increased tumor cell susceptibility to ferroptosis and enhanced ICI efficacy. Loss of AGPAT3 impaired IFN-γ-mediated tumor elimination both in vitro and in vivo. Clinically, higher AGPAT3 expression in tumors was associated with increased immune activation and improved overall survival in ICI-treated patients.


## Conclusion
  The IFN-γ-IRF1-AGPAT3 axis represents an important antitumor mechanism that promotes ferroptosis. Targeting this pathway in combination with our ferroptosis-driver model prediction may improve ICI efficacy and patient outcomes.


## Introduction
  Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have gradually evolved into first-line strategies, significantly prolonging patient survival with both neoadjuvant and advanced treatments.1 2 However, most patients will develop primary or acquired drug resistance during treatment, limiting their therapeutic efficacy.3 To achieve precision treatment with ICIs and enhance therapeutic efficacy, predictive models have emerged as widely adopted and effective approaches. Recent studies have used omics data and machine learning methods to identify novel biomarkers and immune cell markers,4 5 thereby effectively predicting patient responses to ICI therapy. In addition, machine learning has been applied to predict tumor lymph node metastasis6 and uncover tumor heterogeneity.7 These advances highlight the potential of integrating multiomics data with machine learning models to explore ICI response mechanisms and improve precision therapy.

  Ferroptosis is a unique form of regulated cell death distinct from apoptosis, necrosis, and autophagy and is characterized by its dependency on intracellular iron and the accumulation of lethal levels of lipid peroxides.8 9 Studies have shown that ferroptosis exerts a natural antitumor effect by interacting with tumor suppressor genes,10 11 and specific oncogene mutations enable cancer cells to evade ferroptosis.12 13 With the increasing number of clinical applications of ICIs, ferroptosis-mediated cell death has also been implicated in modulating antitumor immunity and shaping the immune response to ICIs.14 For instance, CD8+ T lymphocytes activated by immunotherapy stimulate lipid peroxidation in tumor cells through interferon-gamma (IFN-γ), increasing the susceptibility of tumors to ferroptosis.15 Despite these advances, however, the precise mechanisms linking IFN-γ, ferroptosis, and ICI efficacy remain unclear.

  In this study, we developed a ferroptosis-based machine learning model to predict ICI responses, which led to the identification of IFN-γ as a key regulator of ferroptosis and ICI efficacy. Through a multidisciplinary approach combining transcriptomics analysis, lipidomics, and various tumor models, we demonstrated that IFN-γ upregulates AGPAT3 expression, leading to the accumulation of polyunsaturated ether phospholipids (PUFA-ePLs), increased vulnerability to ferroptosis and enhancing ICI response. These findings reveal critical crosstalk between IFN-γ, AGPAT3, ferroptosis, and the efficacy of ICIs, offering new insights into how ICI responses can be optimized for broader patient benefit.


## Methods and materials

### Pan-cancer scRNA-seq datasets
  In order to obtain the ferroptosis-driver signature (FD.sig), we collected 23 single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) datasets containing both tumor cell and immune/stromal cell data from the TISCH portal (http://tisch.comp-genomics.org/). These datasets cover 10 cancer types, including glioma (GSE89567, GSE102130, GSE131928, GSE70630, GSE84465, GSE103224, GSE138794, GSE139448, GSE141982 and GSE148842), breast cancer (BRCA) (GSE143423 and SRP114962), head and neck squamous carcinoma (HNSC) (GSE103322), medulloblastoma (GSE119926), neuroendocrine tumor (GSE140312), non-small cell lung cancer (EMTAB6149, GSE117570 and GSE127465), ovarian serous cystadenocarcinoma (OV) (GSE118828), pancreatic adenocarcinoma (PAAD) (CRA001160 and GSE111672), skin cutaneous melanoma (SKCM) (GSE72056), and uveal melanoma (UVM) (GSE139829),1637 which are summarized in online supplemental table S1.


### Pan-cancer bulk transcriptomic data
  Transcriptome sequencing and clinical data of 30 different solid tumors in The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database were downloaded from the UCSC Xena data portal (https://xenabrowser.net)38 to verify the function of FD.sig and AGPAT3 in tumor immune microenvironment (TIME) and different pathways, including adrenocortical carcinoma, bladder urothelial carcinoma (BLCA), BRCA, cervical squamous cell carcinoma and endocervical adenocarcinoma, cholangiocarcinoma, colon adenocarcinoma, esophageal carcinoma (ESCA), glioblastoma, HNSC, kidney chromophobe, kidney renal clear cell carcinoma, kidney renal papillary cell carcinoma, brain lower grade glioma, liver hepatocellular carcinoma (LIHC), lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC), mesothelioma, OV, PAAD, pheochromocytoma and paraganglioma, prostate adenocarcinoma, rectum adenocarcinoma, sarcoma, SKCM, stomach adenocarcinoma (STAD), testicular germ cell tumors, thyroid carcinoma, thymoma, uterine corpus endometrial carcinoma, uterine carcinosarcoma, UVM.


### Pan-cancer RNA-seq and scRNA-seq ICI cohorts
  To investigate the value of FD.sig in ICI response, we obtained transcriptome and clinical data from a series of ICI RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) cohorts. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) patients with available transcriptomic data and clinical response information to ICIs; (2) treatment with programmed cell death protein-1 (PD-1)/programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) or cytotoxic T-lymphocyte associated protein 4 blockade, either as monotherapy or in combination, as annotated in the original publications. There are 13 datasets with six cancer types used to develop ferroptosis-driver model (FD.model), which includes six SKCM cohorts (GSE165278, PRJEB23709, GSE78220, Van2016, GSE115821 and GSE91061), two NSCLC cohorts (GSE126044 and GSE135222), two BLCA cohorts (Snyder2018 and GSE111636), one ESCA cohort (GSE165252), one BRCA cohort (GSE173839), one STAD cohort (PRJEB25780).3950 To confirm the importance of FD.sig and ferroptosis-related features in the immunotherapy response, we also applied three scRNA-seq ICI cohorts SKCM (GSE115978 and GSE120575) and renal cell carcinoma (SCP1288).5153 Besides, two other datasets (GSE35640 and IMvigor210) were applied to confirm the roles of AGPAT3 in ICIs.54 55 The details of these ICI datasets are summarized in online supplemental table S2.


### Pathway enrichment analysis
  Ferroptosis-related genes were obtained from the FerrDb database (http://www.zhounan.org/ferrdb), and the enrichment analysis was conducted on each sample with the “GSVA” R package to calculate the FD.sig score (FD.score) and the relative enrichment of genetic characteristics, including some lipid metabolism pathways.


### Model construction and comparison
  The integrated ICI RNA-seq dataset was randomly divided into training and testing sets at a ratio of 7:3. Model training and validation were performed using the “caret” R package based on the 12 genes included in FD.sig. Eight machine learning algorithms were used for the training cohort, including support vector machine, random forest (rf), k-nearest neighbors, naïve Bayes, generalized linear model, neural network, recursive partitioning and logitBoost. To reduce overfitting, fivefold cross-validation was performed during model training using the trainControl function in R. Model generalizability was further evaluated using a pseudo–leave-one-cohort-out validation across independent immunotherapy cohorts, in which each cohort was iteratively held out as an external test set while the remaining cohorts were used for training. Predictive performance was assessed using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC). All comparative models were evaluated using identical training–testing splits and outcome definitions to ensure fair comparison. And some other ICI response signatures (IFNG.Sig,56 T.cell.infamed.Sig,56 PDL1.Sig,57 TcellExc.Sig,52 NLRP3.Sig,58 IMS.Sig,59 Cytotoxic.Sig,60 TRS.Sig,61 CRAM.Sig62 and IPRES.Sig50) were gathered to compare with FD.sig to further assess its effectiveness.

### Cell proliferation and viability assay
  For viability assays, tumor cells were inoculated into 96-well plates with human interferon-γ (hIFN-γ) (Abclonal, #RP01038, China), mouse interferon-γ (mIFN-γ) (Novoprotein, #C746, China), or above lipids. After incubation for a certain time, tumor cells were treated with different concentrations of RSL3 with or without Fer-1. Then, the drug-containing medium was subsequently removed and replaced with FBS-free medium containing 10% Cell Counting Kit 8 reagent (ABclonal Technology, #RM02823, China). After incubation at 37℃ for 2 hours, the absorbance was measured by SpectraMax i3x (Molecular Devices, USA) with a 450 nm laser.


### Colony formation assay
  To demonstrate that knockdown of AGPAT3 did not affect cell growth in vitro, human cells (A375 and H1299) and mouse cells (B16 and LLC) were counted and seeded in 12-well plates at a low density of 500 cells per well. After allowing the cells to adhere, they were cultured under standard conditions (37°C, 5% CO₂) for 10 days to permit colony formation. The medium was changed every 3 days. At the endpoint, colonies were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min, stained with 0.1% crystal violet solution for 20 min at room temperature, gently washed with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), and air-dried. Colonies containing more than 50 cells were counted manually or using ImageJ software. The colony formation rate was calculated as follows: colony formation rate (%)=(amount of colonies/number of seeded cells)×100%.

The cells were resuspended in serum-free medium containing 5 µM BODIPY 581/591 C11 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, #D3861, USA), and incubated at 37°C and 5% CO2 for 30 min. The cells were then washed with PBS and lipid peroxidation levels were measured via a CytoFLEX flow cytometer (Beckman Coulter, USA) with a 488 nm laser. A minimum of 10,000 single cells were collected per well. The results were analyzed with FlowJo software (V.10.8.1, TreeStar, USA).

### Western blotting
  The drug-treated and control cells were washed three times with PBS and lysed in RIPA buffer containing protease and phosphatase inhibitors. The gel was prepared with a 10% One-Step Colored PAGE Gel Fast Preparation Kit (Share-bio, #SKV-0055, China), and the protein was isolated, and then transferred to polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membranes (Millipore, #IPVH00010, France). In addition, after being blocked in Tris-buffered saline and Tween 20 (TBST) with 5% skim milk for 1.5 hours, the membrane was washed with TBST and then incubated with the primary antibody at 4°C overnight. The chemiluminescent solution (Biosharp, #BL520B, China) was prepared according to the instructions and added to PVDF membranes. The images were scanned by the ChemiScope (Clinx, Shanghai, China).


### Immunohistochemistry
  Formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissue sections were mounted on glass slides. The slides were baked at 60°C for 1 hour, then they were dewaxed with fresh xylene two times for 15 min each and rehydrated with graded alcohol (100%, 95%, 75%). EDTA buffer (pH 9.0) was used with a microwave for 15 min, followed by cooling to room temperature. Endogenous peroxidase activity was quenched by incubating the sections with 3% hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂) for 10 min. After rinsing, the sections were blocked with 10% normal serum for 30 min to reduce non-specific binding. The slides were then incubated with primary antibodies diluted in antibody diluent buffer at 4°C overnight, followed by incubation with a biotinylated secondary antibody for 45 min at room temperature. Color development was carried out using 3,3’-diaminobenzidine substrate solution until a brown precipitate was visible under a microscope. Nuclei were counterstained with hematoxylin for 1 min, followed by rinsing in distilled water. Sections were then differentiated in 1% acid alcohol for 2 s, washed again, and blued in a bluing solution for several seconds. Staining was observed and imaged using a bright-field and fluorescence scanning (NanoZoomer S360).


### RNA-seq analysis
  After dividing A375 and H1299 into four groups and adding 60 ng/mL of hIFN-γ, the samples were collected after 0, 6, 24 and 72 hours of incubation. Total RNA was isolated and purified using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, California, USA) following the manufacturer’s procedure. The RNA amount and purity of each sample were quantified using NanoDrop ND-1000 (NanoDrop, Wilmington, Delaware, USA). The RNA integrity number (RIN) was assessed by Bioanalyzer 2100 (Agilent, California, USA) with RIN >7.0, and confirmed by electrophoresis with denaturing agarose gel. Poly (A) RNA is purified from 1 µg total RNA using Dynabeads Oligo (dT) 25–61005 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) using two rounds of purification. Then the cleaved RNA fragments were reverse-transcribed to create the complementary DNA (cDNA) by SuperScript II Reverse Transcriptase (Invitrogen, #1896649, USA), which were next used to synthesize U-labeled second-stranded DNAs with Escherichia coli DNA polymerase I (NEB, #m0209, USA), RNase H (NEB, #m0297, USA) and dUTP Solution (Thermo Fisher Scientific, #R0133, USA). An A-base is then added to the blunt ends of each strand, preparing them for ligation to the indexed adapters. Each adapter contains a T-base overhang for ligating the adapter to the A-tailed fragmented DNA. Single-index or dual-index adapters are ligated to the fragments, and size selection was performed with AMPureXP beads. After the heat-labile UDG enzyme (NEB, #m0280, USA) treatment of the U-labeled second-stranded DNAs, the ligated products are amplified with PCR by the following conditions: initial denaturation at 95°C for 3 min; eight cycles of denaturation at 98°C for 15 s, annealing at 60°C for 15 s, and extension at 72°C for 30 s; and then final extension at 72°C for 5 min. The average insert size for the final cDNA library was 300±50 bp. At last, we performed the 2×150 bp paired-end sequencing (PE150) on an Illumina Novaseq 6000 (LC-Bio Technology, Hangzhou, China) following the vendor’s recommended protocol.

  The fastp software (https://github.com/OpenGene/fastp) was used to remove the reads that contained adapter contamination, low-quality bases and undetermined bases with default parameters. Then, sequence quality was also verified using fastp. We used HISAT2 (https://ccb.jhu.edu/software/hisat2) to map reads to the reference genome of Homo sapiens GRCh38. The mapped reads of each sample were assembled using StringTie (https://ccb.jhu.edu/software/stringtie) with default parameters. Then, all transcriptomes from all samples were merged to reconstruct a comprehensive transcriptome using gffcompare (https://github.com/gpertea/gffcompare/). After the final transcriptome was generated, StringTie was used to estimate the expression levels of all transcripts. StringTie was used to perform expression level for messenger RNAs by calculating fragments per kilobase of transcript per million mapped reads (FPKM) (FPKM=(total exon fragments/mapped reads (millions)×exon length (kB))).

### ChIP-seq analysis and Cleavage Under Targets and Tagmentation (CUT&Tag) sequencing
  The raw data of chromatin immunoprecipitation sequencing (ChIP-seq) was obtained from GSE141606,63 GSE20188164 and GSE186168.65 Adapters and low-quality sequences were removed from raw reads by trimming them using Trim Galore and the “-q 20 -paired” options. Next, using Bowtie2, trimmed reads were uniquely mapped to the human reference genome (hg38). SAMtools was used to transform the output SAM files into sorted BAM files. PCR duplicates were eliminated by using Picard’s MarkDuplicates tool with the “--REMOVEDUPLICATES = true” argument. With the settings “--nomodel -f BAMPE -g hg”, MACS2 was used to identify peaks. To identify broad peaks, the parameter “-broad” was supplied for the H3K27ac and H3K9ac changes. The BEDtools genomecov and bedGraphToBigWig tools produced genome coverage bigwig files with the argument “-scale1,000,000/ (the number of reads mapped to the dm6 genome)”. The deepTools computeMatrix and plotProfile functions produced aggregation charts.

  Cleavage Under Targets and Tagmentation (CUT&Tag) assay was performed using Hyperactive In-Situ ChIP Library Prep Kit for Illumina (cat.no.TD903-TD904, Vazyme Biotech, China) according to manufacturer’s instruction. Briefly, prepared concanavalin A-coated magnetic beads (ConA beads) were added to resuspended cells and incubated at room temperature to bound cells. Non-ionic detergent digitonin was used to permeate cell membrane. Then, primary antibody, secondary antibody and the Hyperactive pA-Tn5 Transposase were incubated with the cells that were bounded by ConA beads in order. Therefore, the Hyperactive pA-Tn5 Transposase can exactly cut-off the DNA fragments that were bound with target protein. In addition, the cut DNA fragments can be ligated with P5 and P7 adaptors by Tn5 transposase and the libraries were amplified by PCR with the P5 and P7 primers. The purified PCR products were evaluated using the Agilent 2100. Bioanalyzer (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, California, USA). Finally, these libraries were sequenced on the Illumina NovaSeq6000 platform and 150 bp paired-end reads were generated for the following analysis. The raw sequence data were first quality trimmed by fatsp software to obtain the clean reads. Then the clean reads were aligned to the human genome GRCh38.p12 using Bowtie2 and subsequently analyzed by the SEACR software based on the “stringent” parameter to detect genomic regions enriched for multiple overlapping DNA fragments (peaks) that we considered to be putative binding sites. Visualization of peak distribution along genomic regions of interested genes was performed with IGV. Peaks were then annotated using chipseeker software to obtain the genes and gene annotations about peaks. Significant motifs of peaks were analyzed by MEME and DREME software and aligned to the motif database.

### Statistical analysis
  R software (V.4.3.1) was used for all statistical analyses. When analyzing differences between two groups, the Wilcoxon test was used, and when analyzing differences between more than two groups, the Kruskal-Wallis test was used. ## Results

### Prediction of ICI outcomes using the FD.sig model with IFN-γ as a key factor
  Given the close link between ferroptosis in tumor cells and immunotherapy response, we hypothesized that developing a FD.sig could help predict ICI efficacy. To test this hypothesis, we developed a machine learning model using multiple pan-cancer scRNA-seq and ICI RNA-seq datasets (figure 1A). First, the FD.sig was established by analyzing 23 scRNA-seq datasets. Differential expression analysis was performed between tumor cells and non-tumor cells within each dataset, focusing on the overlap between downregulated genes in tumors and known ferroptosis-driver genes (online supplemental figure S1A). Using the GSVA, we calculated a driver score based on ferroptosis-driver genes (FD.score), and subsequently performed correlation analysis with the intersecting genes. Genes with a Spearman correlation coefficient (R)>0.5 were integrated into the FD.sig, and it consists of 12 genes (IFNG, THFAIP3, SAT1, ANO6, KDM6B, ABCC1, MICU1, SLC39A7, SLC39A14, ACSL4, FADS1, and TFRC). On this basis, we evaluated the role of FD.sig in different solid tumors in the TCGA database. Among the 30 cancer types, lung cancer exhibited the highest FD.score, with significant differences in LUAD (p=1.18E-13) and LUSC (p=7.46E–12) between tumor and adjacent normal tissues (figure 1B and online supplemental figure S1B). Specifically, IFNG was highly expressed in lung cancer and melanoma, whereas ACSL4 was highly expressed in LIHC (online supplemental figure S1C). In addition, FD.score correlated with immune cell enrichment (online supplemental figure S1D), highlighting the association between FD.sig and TIME. For clinical characteristics, FD.score was negatively correlated with TCGA-CESC and TCGA-UVM (online supplemental figure S1E) but positively correlated with prognosis in only the TCGA-SKCM cohort (p=0.013) (figure 1C), indicating the important role of ferroptosis in SKCM. Thus, we further explored the relationships between FD.sig and ferroptosis and lipid metabolism in lung cancer and SKCM. As shown in online supplemental figure S1F,G, the FD.score was positively associated with ACSL4 expression and ether lipid levels, and negatively correlated with GPX4 expression.

  Based on FD.sig, we constructed a machine-learning model (FD.model) to predict ICI response through 13 cohorts (online supplemental figure S2A). It was modeled and validated using eight techniques, with the rf model (FD.model) chosen for its highest AUC score (figure 1D,E). In both training and testing sets, the AUCs of FD.model are 1 and 0.74, respectively, surpassing those of existing ICI response prediction models (figure 1F). The FD.model showed robust predictive capability with AUCs >0.75 in independent validation cohorts, and the average AUC was higher than that of other models (figure 1G and online supplemental table S4). Among these genes from FD.model, IFNG ranked highest in importance (figure 1H). To confirm the relationship between FD.sig, IFNG expression and ICI efficacy, we used a series of ICI cohorts. Patients who responded to ICI treatment (responders, R) presented significantly higher FD.score than non-responders (NR) (online supplemental figure S2B), suggesting increased ferroptosis susceptibility with enhanced ICI effectiveness. Besides, IFNG and FD.score were strongly positively correlated (all p<0.05) in the lung cancer and melanoma datasets (GSE115821, GSE126044, PRJEB23709 and GSE165252) (online supplemental figure S2C). IFNG expression was significantly lower in NR patients than in R and treatment naive (TN) patients (figure 1I, online supplemental figure S2D,E), suggesting that IFNG may enhance ICI efficacy by promoting ferroptosis. In the TCGA dataset, IFNG expression was associated with overall survival in most cancer types, especially with a negative relation in SKCM (online supplemental figure S2F).

  To verify the role of IFN-γ in ferroptosis, we treated melanoma (A375) and lung cancer (H1299) cell lines with varying IFN-γ concentrations and incubation times. IFN-γ alone did not induce cancer cell death (online supplemental figure S2G), but IFN-γ pre-incubation significantly increased RSL3-induced cell death (figure 1J,K). Lipid peroxidation assays revealed elevated reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels with prolonged IFN-γ incubation (figure 1L,M and online supplemental figure S2H). These findings suggest that FD.sig can predict immunotherapy response, with IFNG playing a critical role in enhancing ferroptosis sensitivity.


### IFN-γ enhances ferroptosis sensitivity through lipid remodeling
  Ferroptosis is closely linked to lipid metabolism. To investigate the underlying mechanism by which IFN-γ increases ferroptosis sensitivity, we examined lipidomic data following IFN-γ incubation. Lipids were categorized into prenol lipids, fatty acyls, glycerolipids (GL), sterol lipids, glycerophospholipids (GP) and sphingolipids. Cells treated with IFN-γ exhibited a significant increase in the GP fraction with a concurrent decrease in GL (figure 2A,B). Free fatty acids (FFA) are an essential part of lipids and are intricately linked to tumor progression.66 All FFA were separated into polyunsaturated fatty acid (PUFA), monounsaturated fatty acid (MUFA), and saturated fatty acid (SFA). Differential analysis results indicated that MUFAs and long-chain PUFAs increased following IFN-γ incubation, whereas SFA and very-long-chain PUFAs decreased (figure 2C, online supplemental figure S3A–C).

  An examination of phospholipid species, including phosphatidylethanolamine (PE) and phosphatidylcholine (PC), further indicated that ether lipids were preferentially increased over ester lipids (figure 2D). Further analysis on ePLs profile revealed that IFN-γ significantly elevates levels of ether-PC and ether-PE (figure 2E,F and online supplemental figure S3D,E). Among these altered ether-PE and ether-PC, there is a substantial increase in PC-O-18:1_20:4 and PE-O-16:1_20:4 in both A375 and H1299 (figure 3A,C). Overall, the intersection of the three groups of differential ePLs in A375 and H1299 was 29 and 13, respectively (figure 3B,D). We conducted further in vitro experimental verification on these lipids, and it showed that although PE/PC18_18:1 had no effect on ferroptosis sensitivity (figure 3E,F), PE/PC18_20:4 (figure 3G,H) and PE/PC18_22:6 (figure 3I,J) increased ferroptosis sensitivity, especially in A375 cells, with the PE18_20:4 (figure 3H) exerting a more pronounced effect, which is supported by previous study,67 68 connecting IFN-γ to ePLs. The enrichment of lipid metabolism pathway from Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) including GP and ether lipid metabolism was also enhanced after 24 hours IFN-γ incubation (figure 3K), which is consistent with the results of lipidomic analysis.


### IFN-γ alters ether lipid metabolism via the IRF1-AGPAT3 axis
  As shown in the schematic diagram (figure 4A), comparative transcriptomic analysis revealed the upregulation of these genes following IFN-γ treatment, with AGPAT3 exhibiting the most pronounced increase (figure 4B), a finding that was further supported by western blot analysis (figure 4C and online supplemental figure S4A–C). Besides, IFN-γ incubation increased ACSL4 expression while reducing GPX4 and FSP1 levels in both H1299 and A375 cells (online supplemental figure S4D,E), indicating the ferroptosis-promoting effect of IFN-γ. To investigate the roles of AGPAT3, AGPS, and GNPAT in ferroptosis, we knocked them down (online supplemental figure S4F,G). It showed that IFN-γ induced ferroptosis was abrogated, with no significant increase in lipid ROS and cell death in A375 and H1299 cells (figure 4D,E). These effects were particularly pronounced for AGPAT3 and AGPS, suggesting their critical role in mediating ferroptosis sensitivity in response to IFN-γ. Transcriptomic analysis showed elevated expression of the IRF family (figure 4F), in which IRF1 functions as a downstream transcription factor of IFN-γ and affects a range of genes to change biological activities.69 70 ChIP-seq analysis revealed a significant increase in IRF1 binding to the AGPAT3 promoter in human lung cancer cell lines from GSE186168 (A549 and H1993), while no changes were observed for AGPS and GNPAT (figure 4G,H and online supplemental figure S5A). Similar results were found in mouse breast cancer (AT3) and pancreatic cancer (AsPC1) cell lines after IFN-γ treatment based on the data of GSE201881 and GSE141606 (figure 4I,J and online supplemental figure S5B). To verify these results in melanoma, we performed CUT&Tag sequencing on A375 cells after IFN-γ incubation. Similarly, the peak AGPAT3 level increased after IFN-γ treatment (figure 4K) and some potential downstream targets involved in lipid metabolism and ferroptosis (such as AKAP1, MGLL and SLC1A1) were also increased (online supplemental table S5). To directly confirm the functional validation of IRF1 regulation of AGPAT3, we performed loss-of-function experiments targeting IRF1 (online supplemental figure S5C). IRF1 knockdown significantly reduced AGPAT3 protein expression, as confirmed by western blotting (online supplemental figure S5D), and markedly attenuated cell ferroptosis in response to IFN-γ stimulation (online supplemental figure S5E). Then, the role of AGPAT3 on tumor cells in vitro was verified, and the results showed that knockdown of AGPAT3 did not affect the proliferation of A375 and H1299 cells (online supplemental figure S5F,G) and does not affect the expression of ACSL4, FSP1 and GPX4 (online supplemental figure S5H,I), indicating that AGPAT3 regulates ferroptosis sensitivity independently of basal proliferation and ACSL4 expression.

  To further study the role of AGPAT3 in the response to IFN-γ signaling for ferroptosis, we first investigated lipid profiles under different experimental conditions, including normal control (NC), AGPAT3 knockdown (sh-AGPAT3), and AGPAT3 knockdown with IFN-γ treatment for 24 hours (sh-AGPAT3+IFN-γ). As shown in figure 5A, ePL levels in A375 cells in the AGPAT3 knockdown group were significantly lower than in the control group and were not rescued by IFN-γ incubation. A similar pattern was observed in H1299 cells, highlighting the lipid-specific effects of AGPAT3 across different cancer cell types (figure 5B). Notably, AGPAT3 knockdown led to a marked decrease in ether-PC levels, which IFN-γ stimulation failed to reverse this decrease (figure 5C,D). In detail, compared with those in the NC group, PE-O-18:1_22:6 and PE-O-18:1_20:4 were significantly lower in the sh-AGPAT3 or sh-AGPAT3+IFN-γ groups of A375 cells (figure 5E and online supplemental figure S6A), whereas PE-O-16:1_20:4 and PE-O-18:1_22:6 were significantly lower in H1299 cells (figure 5F and online supplemental figure S6B). In addition to individual lipid species, we also compared the differences in other lipid categories. AGPAT3 knockdown led to a decrease in the GP ratio compared with the NC group in both A375 and H1299 cells, with a concurrent reduction in GL content in A375 cells and no significant change in IFN-γ incubation after AGPAT3 knockdown (online supplemental figure S6C). AGPAT3 reduction also affected the FFA profile (online supplemental figure S6D), reinforcing the critical role of AGPAT3 in lipid metabolism and ferroptosis sensitivity. Previous results showed that lipids can directly enhance the ferroptosis effect of RSL3, so we performed cell-killing experiments by knocking down AGPAT3. The results showed that, compared with the control group, after AGPAT3 knockdown, PE/PC18_20:4 and PE/PC18_22:6 no longer increased cell death (online supplemental figure S6E–H), supporting a key role of AGPAT3 in regulating ferroptosis sensitivity.


### IFN-γ increases ferroptosis sensitivity in vivo
  To investigate whether AGPAT3 is critical for IFN-γ-mediated enhancement of ferroptosis sensitivity in vivo, we first knocked down Agpat3 in mouse melanoma (B16F10) cells and lung cells (LLC) and confirmed its role in vitro (online supplemental figure S7A). Agpat3 knockdown did not affect the proliferation of LLC and B16 cells (online supplemental figure S7B,C), and the expression of Agpat3 increased after incubation with IFN-γ (online supplemental figure S7D), which was consistent with the results in human lung cancer and melanoma cells. In addition, Agpat3 knockdown eliminated the ferroptosis-sensitizing effect of IFN-γ (online supplemental figure S7E,F). NC and sh-Agpat3 mice were divided into eight groups, each receiving a different treatment, and the tumor volume and weight were measured and compared across groups (figure 6A). In mice with lung cancer (LLC) and melanoma (B16), Agpat3 knockdown did not affect the growth rate of the tumor (G1 and G2), thus we further compared the tumor sizes from all groups (G1-G8). As shown in figure 6B–G, IFN-γ treatment alone had no significant tumor-inhibitory effect, regardless of Agpat3 status (G3 and G4). RSL3 treatment inhibited tumor growth, and this effect was significantly enhanced when RSL3 was combined with IFN-γ (G5 and G7). However, when Agpat3 was knocked down, the synergistic tumor shrinkage effect of RSL3 and IFN-γ was lost (G7 and G8).


### Tumor AGPAT3 affects the efficacy of IFN-γ-combined ICIs
  To determine the role of AGPAT3 in IFN-γ-combined ICI therapy, we performed in vivo experiments using mouse models of melanoma and lung cancer (figure 7A). Five groups were established: the control group (C1), IFN-γ treatment group (C2), sh-Agpat3 cancer model group (C3), anti-PD-1 treatment group (C4) and IFN-γ+anti-PD-1 dual treatment group (C5). After therapy, the tumor size and weight in each of the five groups were compared (online supplemental figure S8A,B). In both the lung cancer and melanoma models, anti-PD-1 treatment alone significantly reduced tumor size compared with that of the controls, whereas IFN-γ alone had no discernible antitumor effect (figure 7B,C). However, when combined with anti-PD-1 therapy, IFN-γ treatment had a statistically significant tumor-inhibitory effect. This enhancement was not observed when Agpat3 was knocked down. Flow cytometry analysis of tumor-infiltrating immune cells (online supplemental figure S8C) revealed that the combination of IFN-γ and anti-PD-1 significantly increased the proportion of CD8+ IFN-γ+ T cells and reduced the population of CD8+ TIM3+ T cells (figure 7D–G and S8D-S8G). These results suggest that IFN-γ enhances immune activation, resulting in improved therapeutic effects. In addition, we contrasted the variations in macrophages. Compared with those in the C1 group, the number of M1 macrophages in the C4 and C5 groups was significantly greater, whereas the number of M2 macrophages in all the macrophage populations was lower (figure 7H and online supplemental figure S8H,I). Compared with IFN-γ or anti-PD-1 antibody treatment alone, the dual treatment of IFN-γ+anti-PD-1 antibody showed a combined effect in depleting the M2 population. As a result, the M1/M2 ratio was the highest in the C4 group. However, these changes were not observed in the sh-Agpat3 group (C3), suggesting that IFN-γ enhances antitumor immune responses and ICI efficacy through Agpat3-mediated pathways. Thus, IFN-γ and PD-1 blockade may synergistically remodel the TIME by shifting macrophage polarization toward an M1 phenotype and promoting T cell-mediated antitumor activity, provided AGPAT3 is present. Furthermore, we evaluated a sequential administration regimen in tumor-bearing mice with LLC cell line and found that both schedules produced comparable tumor control under the conditions tested (online supplemental figure S9A,B). These results do not exclude potential differences in immune dynamics, indicating that the observed antitumor effect of the combination is not restricted to a single dosing sequence.

  In addition to observing changes in the TIME, we also verified the ferroptosis and lipid metabolism phenotypes of tumor tissues after treatment. In LLC, the levels of Gpx4 and Fsp1 in C4 and C5 were significantly lower than those in C1-C3, indicating a higher level of ferroptosis (online supplemental figure S9C). Similar results were obtained in B16. Although the difference in Fsp1 was not significant, Gpx4 was the lowest in C5 (online supplemental figure S9C). AGPAT3, GNPAT and AGPS are important genes for ether lipid synthesis, which can be promoted by their increased expression. Therefore, compared with the C4 group, that of the C5 group was significantly greater, indicating that IFN-γ can promote lipid synthesis, while Agpat3 knockdown had no corresponding effect (online supplemental figure S9D).


### The clinical and immunological roles of AGPAT3 across cancer types
  To further understand the clinical and immunological significance of AGPAT3, we investigated its role across various cancers. First, we studied the relationship between the expression level of AGPAT3 and ferroptosis in TCGA. In most cancers, AGPAT3 was positively correlated with FD.score, including SKCM (p=1.1E−5) and LUSC (p=0.049) (figure 8A). Besides, AGPAT3 was also correlated with ferroptosis-related genes, including IFNG, especially in LUSC and SKCM (figure 8B), which supports the results of the IFN-γ-IRF1-AGPAT3 axis. As for TIME, AGPAT3 expression was positively correlated with immune parameters in solid tumors, particularly HNSC and LUSC (online supplemental figure S10A). In HNSC, LUSC and UVM, higher AGPAT3 expression was strongly associated with a higher CD8+ Teff score (figure 8C) confirming that AGPAT3 is involved in the ferroptosis process and is related to the activated TIME. Furthermore, we verified the role of AGPAT3 in the ICI cohort. As shown in the figure, not only was AGPAT3 significantly positively correlated with IFNG (figure 8D), but also patients with high AGPAT3 expression had a better prognosis in Snyder2017 (figure 8E). AGPAT3 expression was also elevated in the responder in all ICI cohorts (online supplemental figure S10B), especially Imvigor210 and GSE165278 datasets (figure 8F), and additional analyses showed the correlation between high AGPAT3 expression with ICI responder in monotherapy subgroup and combination therapy subgroup (online supplemental figure S10C,D). AGPAT3 expression did not differ between PD-1/PD-L1 monotherapy and PD-1/PD-L1 combination therapy groups overall (online supplemental figure S10E), indicating that AGPAT3 is unlikely to reflect treatment selection alone. PD-L1 expression, tumor mutational burden (TMB), and microsatellite instability are important predictive biomarkers for immunotherapy response. In the ICI-treated cohorts, including 15 RNA-seq datasets of online supplemental table S2, AGPAT3 expression exhibited a significant positive correlation with PD-L1 expression (online supplemental figure S10F). Since the ICI cohorts used in our study did not provide TMB or microsatellite instability (MSI) data, we analyzed these correlations using TCGA datasets. The results showed that AGPAT3 expression was not significantly associated with MSI-high status (online supplemental figure S10G) but TMB in TCGA-OV and TCGA-LUAD (online supplemental figure S10H). These findings support its role as a context-dependent modulator of immunotherapy sensitivity.


## Discussion
  In this study, we developed an FD.model that effectively predicts immunotherapy response and uncovered a novel mechanistic link between IFN-γ signaling and ferroptosis sensitivity mediated by the IRF1-AGPAT3 axis. By integrating transcriptomic and lipidomic analyses, we demonstrated that IFN-γ enhances tumor cell susceptibility to ferroptosis through lipid remodeling, specifically by enriching PUFA-ePLs. These findings highlight a previously unrecognized connection between immune signaling, lipid metabolism, and ferroptotic vulnerability, providing mechanistic insights with potential translational implications.

  The development of gene-feature-based predictive models has become a powerful approach in biomedical research for estimating therapeutic efficacy.7174 Compared with existing immunotherapy response models, our ferroptosis-related FD.model exhibited better predictive accuracy in both melanoma and lung cancer cohorts, which is biologically aligned with the mechanistic focus of this study and may confer advantages in ferroptosis-enriched or immunotherapy-treated contexts. But to some extent, this improvement reflects the growing appreciation that ferroptosis and its associated pathways exert dual effects on tumor progression and the TIME. Compared with existing models, our ferroptosis-based model demonstrates good predictive power for ICI response in lung cancer and melanoma. The impetus for developing this model arises from growing evidence that ferroptosis and its associated genes exert dual effects on tumor progression and TIME. Thus, the FD.model holds significant clinical promise and potential application value. In our FD.model, IFNG emerged as a central regulator linking ferroptosis to ICI efficacy, as IFN-γ signaling promotes tumor ferroptosis in a manner analogous to CD8+ T cell-mediated cytotoxicity.67 These results suggest that incorporating ferroptosis-related signals into immunotherapy prediction frameworks can improve model performance and clinical applicability. Our findings demonstrated that IFN-γ monotherapy was unable to inhibit tumor growth in vivo, despite IFN-γ being a crucial effector cytokine in the TIME. This finding is in line with earlier studies showing that sustained IFN-γ signaling might trigger compensatory immune evasion strategies, such as overexpression of PD-L1,75 desensitization of the JAK/STAT system,76 and lipid metabolic reprogramming that reduces ferroptotic sensitivity.77 Systemic IFN-γ therapy is limited by significant toxicity and is therefore not widely applicable in clinical oncology. But the translational relevance of our findings lies in identifying AGPAT3-mediated lipid remodeling as a downstream effector of IFN-γ signaling that enhances ferroptosis sensitivity and immunotherapy efficacy. Alternative strategies, such as tumor-localized IFN-γ delivery, immune cell-restricted IFN-γ production, or pharmacological targeting of AGPAT3-associated lipid metabolic pathways, may recapitulate the beneficial effects observed in this study while avoiding systemic IFN-γ exposure.

  Our study demonstrated that IFN-γ increases tumor cell vulnerability to ferroptosis by modulating lipid composition, specifically by enriching PUFA-ePLs. While ether lipids are known to regulate ferroptosis sensitivity,78 their direct connection to IFN-γ has not been previously established. Here, we show that IFN-γ promotes the synthesis of PUFA-ePLs by upregulating AGPAT3 expression. These results emphasize AGPAT3 as a crucial metabolic mediator connecting IFN-γ signaling to increased ferroptotic sensitivity and better ICI efficacy, given the crucial role of PUFA-ePLs in lipid peroxidation during ferroptosis. Interestingly, although IFN-γ stimulation increased ACSL4 expression and concomitantly reduced GPX4 and FSP1 levels, knockdown of AGPAT3 did not affect ACSL4 expression. These findings indicate that AGPAT3 regulates cellular sensitivity to ferroptosis by modulating lipid availability rather than directly engaging the canonical ferroptosis execution machinery. By coupling IFN-γ-IRF1 signaling to ether lipid remodeling, AGPAT3 defines a distinct upstream immunometabolic layer that shapes ferroptosis susceptibility. Collectively, these results support the conclusion that AGPAT3 functions independently of the classical ACSL4-GPX4 axis. Lipidomic investigations supported this paradigm by showing that AGPAT3 knockdown significantly decreased total ePLs, such as PE-O-18:0_22:6. All of these findings point to AGPAT3 as a hitherto unknown IFN-γ-responsive regulator of ether lipid remodeling that supports the effectiveness of immunotherapy and ferroptosis sensitization (figure 5). These lipids have previously been identified as critical peroxidation substrates during ferroptosis, which are highly susceptible to oxidative degradation.79 AGPAT3 is known to catalyze the acylation of lysophosphatidic acid, a precursor in the biosynthesis of both diacyl and ether-linked GP.80 Its downregulation disrupts this pathway, leading to impaired incorporation of PUFA into ether lipid species. High expression of AGPAT3 in patients was positively correlated with survival and response to immunotherapy. However, due to a lack of publicly available clinical covariate data in the cohorts, multivariate Cox regression analysis could not be performed. Nevertheless, this study can preliminarily determine the role of AGPAT3 in immunotherapy. Future research in well-annotated clinical cohorts is needed to validate the independent predictive value of AGPAT3.

  Despite these revelations, our research has several shortcomings. First, further research is needed to determine the changes in different cell types during ferroptosis. Second, the majority of samples in the scRNA-seq and ICI cohorts came from lung cancer and melanoma. This may restrict the generalizability of our findings. Additional study encompassing a wider range of tumor types is necessary to confirm the pan-cancer significance of AGPAT3-associated ferroptosis regulation. Third, the immune-specific function of AGPAT3 in human tumor microenvironments is still unclear, despite the fact that murine models support the IFN-γ-IRF1-AGPAT3 regulation axis. Additional investigations using patient-derived xenografts or humanized mouse models will be necessary to confirm its translational value.

  In conclusion, we developed a machine learning model based on ferroptosis-related genes to predict ICI efficacy and uncovered an important mechanism by which IFN-γ enhances ferroptosis sensitivity and strengthens immunotherapy efficacy. Through the IRF1-AGPAT3 axis, IFN-γ stimulates the production of PUFA-ePLs, increasing tumor cell vulnerability to ferroptosis and thereby improving the immune response. To our knowledge, this is the first study to establish a direct link between IFN-γ signaling, IRF1-AGPAT3 axis-mediated lipid remodeling and ICI efficacy. Targeting AGPAT3 may serve as a novel therapeutic strategy to enhance ferroptosis-driven immunotherapy for aggressive tumors.
