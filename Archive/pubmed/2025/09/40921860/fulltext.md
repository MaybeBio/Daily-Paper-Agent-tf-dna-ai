# Robinin decreases myocardial ischemia/reperfusion injury via Nrf2 anti-oxidative effects mediated by Akt/GSK3β/Fyn in hypercholesterolemic rats


## Abstract
  Robinin (RB) is an accepted antioxidant herbal product with known cardio-protective activity. To explore the anti-oxidative potential of RB in treating myocardial ischemia or reperfusion (MI/RI) damage in rats after inducing hypercholesterolemia (HC). HC was induced by administering cholesterol (2%) to rats for eight weeks. The rats were given RB (50 mg/kg bw) for the last two weeks. The rats were arbitrarily divided into four groups: (Group I) normal control, (Group II) hypercholesterolemic (HC) alone, (Group III) HC + RB (50 mg/kg body weight), and (Group IV) RB alone (50 mg/kg body weight). LV-developed pressure (LVDP), and left ventricular end-diastolic pressure (LVEDP) were recorded during the perfusion process. Histopathology staining was used to analyze liver and kidney damage in heart tissue (H&E, MT, and PAS stains), and in silico techniques, such as molecular docking and MD simulation, were employed. Results revealed that RB administration reduced MI/RI in HC rats due to Akt/GSK3β/Fyn-as facilitated Nrf2 anti-oxidative function. Administering RB to HC rats resulted in increased expression of Akt, whereas it reduced the Fyn and GSK3β levels, which activated Nrf2 activity. When RB is administered to HC rats. Glide (a Schrodinger module) was used to dock RB with NQO1, Nrf-2, HO-1, GSK-3β, and Akt to select the best interacting drug action on inflammatory markers for the therapeutic action of RB and followed OH-1 and Nrf2 MD simulation study were carried out. These results highlight how Nrf2 antioxidative effects are mediated by Akt/GSK3β/Fyn are enhanced by RB, protecting the HC heart from MI/RI.


## Introduction
  A remarkable type of ischemic heart disease (IHD) is myocardial infarction (MI), which results from the disruption of the blood flow and may cause cardiovascular disability and mortality around the world (Thygesen et al. 2018). It also negatively impacts the myocardium by reversibly initiating signaling pathways, inducing apoptosis, and elevating endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress (Wu et al. 2015; Frampton et al. 2023). It is now known that a key pro-inflammatory route that contributes significantly to the development of neuroinflammation is the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK)-mediated signaling cascade (Islam et al. 2022).

  So far, the most beneficial intervention for MI appears to be the prompt restoration of blood flow to the damaged area of the coronary artery (Pollard 2000). Nevertheless, restoration of blood flow exacerbates reversible myocardial ischemia injury (MI/I), culminating in irreversible MI/I, more precisely myocardial ischemia/reperfusion injury (MI/RI) (Ferrari et al. 2017). As a disorder, MI/RI causes physical harm, myocardial dysfunction, and disruption of electrical movement, thereby leading to a substantial rise in MI-associated mortality after retrieval of the coronary bloodstream in IHD (Mao et al. 2019). Numerous researches have revealed that hypercholesterolemia (HC) contributes to acute MI progression and exacerbates MI/RI (Andreadou et al. 2017; Ma et al. 2017). Thus, HC and MI/RI have emerged as crucial factors that pose a threat to human health.

  Increased oxidative stress, decreased PI3K/Akt signaling, and the creation of mitochondrial permeability transition pores (mPTPs) are all potential outcomes of HC-induced MI/RI (Torge et al. 2022). In IHD, oxidative stress and the generation of ROS are the dynamic causes of apoptosis. Remarkably, myeloid deletion of an anti-oxidative transcription factor, namely Nrf2, elevates inflammation, oxidative stress, and atherosclerosis in the HC-induced mouse model (Wu et al. 2014). The primary regulator of oxidative stress is Nrf-2. The transcription of Nrf-2 begins on antioxidant inheritable factors, particularly HO-1, which are implicated in a key reaction to oxidative stress (Collins et al. 2012). Nrf-2/HO-1 is a significant healing goal for IHD, which is facilitated the molecular action of numerous natural products (Li et al. 2019). MI/RI discloses that MI diminishes ion-exchange channels as according to MI/RI, MI reduces ion-exchange channels in an ATPase-dependent manner, interfering with the volume of cell regulation that influences the collapse of organelles to stop enzyme activity (Xie et al. 2018). Similarly, reperfusion augments ROS generation by suppressing the role of the mitochondria and initiates a chain reaction of release of cytokines, ER strain, and cell impairment. I/R-induced cell damage is a well-known end effector, and death is the mPTP opening (Chen et al. 2015). Several reports highlight the association between the mitochondrial dysfunction process and MI/RI (Kalogeris et al. 2012). Hence, preserving damaged myocardium could be a useful therapeutic objective of MI/RI. With the development of novel drugs for the treatment of MI/RI-induced cardiac dysfunction, complementary therapies have attracted significant attention. Polyphenolic chemicals work in concert to effectively combat oxidation, myocardial infarction, cancers, and other conditions. Polyphenols have anti-inflammatory properties, scavenge free radicals, reduce the synthesis of advanced glycation end products, inhibit aldose reductase, and alter blood flow in the eyes (Emran et al. 2022).

  There have also been reports of antibacterial, antifungal, anti-inflammatory, and estrogenic properties, as well as anticancer activities through apoptosis (Mitra et al. 2022). Robinin (RB), a kaempferol derivative and a flavone natural glycoside derived from a plant source, is one such phenolic flavonoid. It possesses potent anti-inflammatory, anti-tumor, and antioxidant pharmacological activities (Ibáñez et al. 2015). In a rabbit model, RB is one of the flavonoids that has been shown to have cardioprotective properties (Hayashi et al. 2017). With a molecular weight of 740.66 Da, RB may also help to lower immune response and inflammation.

  According to Hayashi et al. (2017), Drp 1 plays a crucial role in Diabetes Melius and MIRI rats. In their findings, dimethyl fumarate (DMF), a Nrf-2 agonist, improved cardiac function, mitochondrial fission, and reduced Drp-1 expression through oxidative stress. According to Zhang et al. (2024), flavones that target Nrf-2 are effective therapeutic agents for a variety of diseases that are dependent on oxidative stress. In hPBMCs, RB has been shown to have anti-inflammatory effects. It has also been shown to inhibit the TLR/NF-κB pathway in hPBMCs exposed to oxidized LDL (oxLDL), and its active ingredients have cardio-protective qualities (Janeesh and Abraham 2014; Janeesh et al. 2014).

  The findings of previous research reveal that RB targets anti-oxidative and cardioprotective properties, although the latent molecular action is unknown. The positive effects of RB are still unexplored in MI/RI-prompted cardiac dysfunction in hypercholesterolemic rats. Hence, the current work aims to determine if RB administration can limit the mPTP and improve Akt-enabled Nrf2/HO-1 antioxidant efficacy to reduce MI/RI in the HC rats, and try to identify a potential mechanism of action with the assistance of histology and Molecular Docking analysis.


## Materials and methods

### Chemicals and reagents
  RB (RB): Purity ≥ 90.0% (HPLC), MTT, and collagenase were procured from Gibco (CA, USA). total cholesterol (TC), HDL-C, LDL-C, triglycerides (TG), and lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) assay kits were procured from Ebioscience, USA. The primary and secondary antibodies were obtained from Roche Diagnostics (Risch, Switzerland). Primers were obtained from Abcam (Cambridge, MA, USA). Additionally, biochemicals and analytical-grade solvents were used.


### Animals
  For the experimental trials, male Sprague–Dawley rats weighing 220-260 g and aged 8–10 weeks were used. A regular pellet meal and unlimited water were given to the animals, which were kept in polypropylene cages. 21% protein, 5% fats, 4% crude fiber, 8% ash, 1% calcium, 0.6% phosphorus, 3.4% glucose, 2% vitamins, and 55% nitrogen-free extract (carbohydrates) make up a typical pellet diet. Whole animals were kept in conventional conditions, which included room temperature, 55–60% humidity, and 12-h cycles of light and dark. They were given drinking water and fed regular pellets. Approval for the experimental procedure was obtained from the Institutional Animal Ethical Committee of Medicine, Jiangxi Provincial People's Hospital, and The First Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang Medical College (Approval No: JXPPH230609).


### Induction of hypercholesterolemia in experimental rats
  A Laboratory pellet diet (97.5%) mixed with a cholesterol-enriched diet containing 2% cholesterol and 0.5% sodium cholate was provided for eight weeks to induce HC (Janeesh and Abraham 2013). The NC rats were served a typical pellet diet for eight weeks. By the end of the experiment, the level of cholesterol was found to increase in the plasma of animals nourished with HC, and hence they were designated as HC. Since its LD50 in mice is greater than > 1 g/kg, RB is a fairly safe chemical. This acute toxicity research led to the selection of a safe dose of 50 mg/kg of RB for this experimental setup (Sokolova et al. 1978; Vasilchenko and Sokolova 1973).


### Experimental design
  To assess the RB against MI/RI in the HC-induced rats were arbitrarily separated into 4 sets (n = 6/group): (Group-I) normal cholesterolemic control (NC), (Group-II) hypercholesterolemic rats (HC), (Group-III) HC administered with RB (50 mg/kg bw), and (Group-IV) RB alone (50 mg/kg bw). RB was given for two successive weeks, starting from the sixth week after they had received a cholesterol diet. After eight weeks, the ventricular myocytes were separated and maintained in 200 μM Ca2+. In order to assess how RB affected mPTP opening in hypercholesterolemic hearts, rats were divided into 4 groups at random (n = 6 per group) as previously mentioned. The opening of mPTP and fluctuations in ΔΨm were subsequently examined. The animals were fasted overnight (for about either 12 or 18 h or actual hours) on the last day of the experimental model before the induction of anesthesia or the collection of blood samples.


### Assay of lipid profiles in plasma
  Following the experiment, spectrophotometric measurements were made using the biochemical assay kits by the manufacturer's instructions for the TG Triglyceride (TG) Colorimetric Assay Kit (Catalog number: EEA028), HDL-C Invitrogen™ High-density Lipoprotein Cholesterol (HDL-C) Colorimetric Assay Kit (Catalog number: EEA012), LDL-C Low-density Lipoprotein Cholesterol (LDL-C) Colorimetric Assay Kit (Catalog number: EEA014), and TC Invitrogen™ Total Cholesterol (TC) Colorimetric Assay Kit (single reagent, COD-PAP method) (Catalog number: EEA026) in plasma.


### Induction of MI/RI
  Animals were anesthetized and heparinized, and the heart was promptly isolated, then perfused retrogradely with a modified solution of Krebs–Henseleit at 37 °C on a Langendorff apparatus. On neutralizing for 20 min, the heart was prone to MI for half an hour, and reperfusion was allowed for 120 min to induce MI/RI. LV-developed pressure (LVDP), and left ventricular end-diastolic pressure (LVEDP) were recorded during the perfusion process. LVDP × HR was used for estimating the rate-pressure product (RPP).


### Histological analysis
  Using a Leica Microsystem tissue processor (ASP 300S, Munich, Germany), samples for histological examination were first embedded in paraffin blocks after being fixed in 10% formaldehyde at ambient temperature. Sections 3 μm thick were cut with a Microtome (Model RM 2265, Munich, Germany) in order to prepare them for histological staining. Slices of heart tissue were stained with hematoxylin–eosin staining (H&E) of the heart, liver, and kidneys, PAS, and Masson's trichrome, and then blindly inspected under a light microscope.


### Estimation of mPTP opening and ΔΨm
  Following the prescribed protocol, single ventricular myocytes were extracted from the rat's heart (Yang et al. 2015). Briefly, the rat's heart was perfused with 100% oxygenated Tyrode's solution containing Ca2 + -free radicals, and then Tyrode's solution was re-dispensed a with minimal Ca2+ at 37 °C for ten minutes. After filtering, the isolated single ventricular myocytes were added to Tyrode's solution with Ca2+. Before the mitochondrial experiment, the cells were given an hour to stabilize after the mixture was administered gradually for 40 min. By measuring the intensity of the calcein fluorescence (488 nm excitation/505 nm emission), the myocytes' capacity to pass via the mPTP opening was assessed. ΔΨm in the myocytes was calculated using the fluorescence intensity of the JC-1 aggregates (488 nm excitation/590 nm emission).


### RT-qPCR analysis
  Whole RNA was isolated and purified, which was followed by reverse transcription, and qPCR was conducted as per the specified method (Chandra et al. 2000). GSK3β, Akt, Nuclear Fyn, Nrf2, NQO1, HO-1, and GAPDH primers were used, which were obtained from Bio-Rad Laboratories, Inc., Hercules, CA, USA. The sequestered RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA by utilizing a High-Capacity cDNA Reverse Transcription kit (Himedia, USA), following the kit’s protocols. The strength of the band was inspected by consuming 1.5% agarose gels and conducting electrophoresis; lastly, the band thickness was analyzed by Image J 1.48 software. GAPDH was utilized to determine the ΔCt values and to calculate the mRNA fold change. The sequences of the primers are as follows Table 1. ### Evaluation of Akt, Quinone reductase, GSK-3β, HO-1, and Nrf-2 molecular docking

### In silico analysis

### Preparation of proteins and ligands
  Molecular docking studies were determine the RB binding affinities and interactions with the target proteins. The protein data bank (PDB) provided the 3D structures of proteins, including Akt (3CQW), NQO1 (1D4A), GSK-3β (1I09), HO-1 (1S8C), and Nrf-2 (7X5E). The spatial data file format for the baicalin structure was obtained from PubChem (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/). The preliminary docking investigation was conducted using Autodock Vina (version 1.5.6). The 3D structure (Biovia Dassault system, Inc., USA) was viewed using Discovery Studio Version 4.5, which also removed the water molecule's interaction containing metal ions and heavy atoms from the protein structure. The docking analysis was then performed after the addition of hydrogen atoms. After grouping positional Root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) of the data of 1 Å, the result with the best binding free energy was used to represent it. The site of the highest binding affinity or lowest binding energy was the subject of additional analysis.


### Molecular dynamics simulation
  Desmond of the Schrodinger Bio suite was used to do molecular dynamics simulation on the docked complexes that were chosen from the molecular docking analysis in order to verify the complexes' stability (Hayashi 2017). A 50 ns MD simulation was performed using the docked complex of the HO-1 (PDB ID: 1S8C) and Nrf-2 (PDB ID: 7X5E). The MD simulation was mostly initiated using the system builder for the force field, boundary conditions, and solvent model settings. After adding TIP3P water molecules to the system, the complexes were centered in an orthorhombic box with a 10 Å distance as a boundary condition, and MD simulation was performed using the OPLS 2005 force field (Zhang et al. 2024). The steepest descent technique, which is employed as the equilibration protocol in Desmond, was found to minimize the energy of complex structures (Janeesh and Abraham 2014). The NPT ensemble class was used to mimic complex structures for up to 20 ns (Janeesh et al. 2014). The Nose–Hoover chain method was used to process the temperature at 300 K, and the Martyna-Tobias-Klein method was used to fix the pressure coupling at 1 bar. Simulation event analysis was used to generate the RMSD of the protein and ligand bond, RMSF of the protein and ligand, and protein–ligand interactions to assess the stability of the ligand inside the protein binding site.


### Statistical analysis
  IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, version 25 (IBM Corp., Armonk, N.Y., USA) and GraphPad Prism v. 8.0.2 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, USA) were used to statistically evaluate the data from each group. The Median (min and max) was used to report the measurement results. The Kolmogorov–Smirnov test was used to determine whether the distribution was normal. Non-parametric tests were used since the data were not regularly distributed. Several groups were evaluated for significant differences using the Kruskal–Wallis test. For pairwise comparisons, Dunn's post hoc test was used to reduce the false-positive rate that comes with multiple testing. In order to control type I error rates, the significance levels were further modified using the Bonferroni correction to take into consideration the six pairwise comparisons. P-values below 0.05 were regarded as statistically significant. Every statistical test was two-tailed.

Natural elements found in plants, neurodegenerative illnesses are treated with polyphenols as genistein, luteolin, curcumin, quercetin, celastrol, berberine, resveratrol, and epigallocatechin-3-gallate.
