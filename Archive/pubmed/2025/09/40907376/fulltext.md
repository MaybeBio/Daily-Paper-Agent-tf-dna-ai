# Quantitative Structure-activity Relationship-based Neural Network Model in Screening Potential Inhibitors from Brown Algae against RelA in Oral Squamous Cell Carcinoma


## Introduction and Aim
  Oral squamous cell carcinomas (OSCCs) are one of the most frequently diagnosed head and neck cancers with a poor prognosis despite the advancements in diagnostic techniques and treatment strategies. The progression of OSCC is driven by several molecular mechanisms, among them the overexpression of transcription factor RelA, which plays a crucial role by correlating with the clinicopathological characteristics.


## Methods
  This systematic investigation focused on identifying the top 25 crucial molecular descriptors to predict the RelA inhibitor through the quantitative structure-activity relationship (QSAR)-based artificial neural network model.


## Results
  In this study, the developed multilayer perceptron model showed an accuracy of 91.37% in the classification of active inhibitors, with a Matthews correlation coefficient (MCC) of 0.89. Then the model was assessed for the 1221 brown algae-derived compounds, identifying 1014 as the most active RelA inhibitors. Further, molecular docking revealed that phlorethopentafuhalol-A had a higher affinity based on the binding energy of −8.45 kcal/mol than the known RelA inhibitors (−5.30 to −1.31 kcal/mol). Molecular dynamics (MD) simulation confirmed that phlorethopentafuhalol-A formed a stable conformation with the RelA based on the trajectory analysis.


## Conclusions
  Overall, this analysis demonstrated that phlorethopentafuhalol-A could be a potential RelA inhibitor that may be useful in the treatment of OSCC on further investigation.

## Introduction
  Oral squamous cell carcinomas (OSCCs) are one of the most common tumours of the head and neck region.1 According to GLOBOCAN 2022, oral cancer accounts for an incidence of 389,846 and mortality of 188,438 cases annually. Recent progress in the understanding of molecular mechanisms underlying disease incidence and the development of new treatment approaches has improved early detection and treatment strategies. Despite these advancements, the prognosis for OSCC patients remains a major issue, with resistance to treatment remaining as one of the challenges.2 The treatment resistance contributes to poor prognosis and high recurrence rates. Given these limitations, understanding the molecular processes that drive the progression of OSCC is of paramount importance in the advancement of innovative therapeutic strategies.

  One of the key pathways in OSCC progression is the nuclear factor-κB (NF-κB) signalling pathway, which is involved in cellular and physiological functions.3 The NF-κB signalling pathway is regulated by the nuclear factor (NF)-κB transcription factor, which plays a crucial role in immune regulation, cell cycle, and oncogenic processes.4 Particularly, NF-κB is regulated by various divergent stimuli, leading to chronic inflammation that contributes to immunosuppression and thereby provides a suitable microenvironment for tumorigenesis, development, and metastasis.5 NF-κB is a heterodimeric protein complex consisting of the p65 (RelA) and p50 subunits, and overexpression is known to generate cancer cells by blocking apoptosis and promoting the cell cycle.6 Numerous studies have highlighted the overexpression of the transcription factor RelA in OSCCs, demonstrating its significant correlation with clinicopathological features.7 Notably, RelA has been linked to tumour, node, and metastasis staging, as well as lymphatic metastasis in OSCC patients.8 Furthermore, RelA directly interacts with the promoter of the transcription factor AP-2 alpha (TFAP2A), driving the activation of various malignant and metastatic phenotypes via the Wnt/β-catenin signalling pathway.9 Since RelA drives multiple signalling pathways associated with OSCC progression and metastasis, it emerges as a promising therapeutic target for OSCC.10

  Computational drug screening and discovery methods have gained significance for their potential to expedite the drug discovery process by enabling efficient identification of potential therapeutic candidates, streamlining target validation, and optimising drug design while minimising time, human resources, and costs.11 A multitude of methodologies, including quantitative structure-activity relationship (QSAR),12 pharmacophore modeling,13 molecular docking,14 and virtual screening,15 have been effectively utilised to create a diverse array of groundbreaking pharmaceuticals. Artificial neural network (ANN)-based screening has become increasingly important in recent years for selecting biologically active molecules that can inhibit specific target proteins.16 This screening involves training an ANN and efficiently prioritises high-potential inhibitors, reducing computational costs with high sensitivity and specificity. By utilising molecular descriptors and inhibitory activities, it is possible to develop effective models that can generate new active compounds.17 Through the implementation of such a pipeline, multiple studies have shown the considerable effectiveness of utilising 2D structure-derived features to model physicochemical properties and identify potential molecules for various diseases.18 Interestingly, the majority of innovative pharmaceutical products have been derived from natural substances known as secondary metabolites.19 Approximately 25% of all medications approved by the FDA are derived from natural resources, including widely recognised compounds such as paclitaxel and morphine. The utilisation of medications derived from natural sources has indeed brought about a significant transformation in the field of medicine.20

  This study employed a comprehensive computational approach to identify natural compounds from marine sources as potential inhibitors of the RelA protein for OSCC treatment. We developed an automated neural network (ANN)-based multilayer perceptron (MLP) model using 2D molecular descriptors derived from a dataset of known RelA inhibitors, with inhibitory values based on bioassay results. The ANN-based MLP model extracted relevant descriptors to predict the inhibitory properties of each compound. Using these descriptors, potential inhibitory molecules were predicted from a dataset of compounds sourced from brown algae. The predicted molecule was then evaluated for its interaction with the RelA protein through molecular docking and molecular dynamics (MD) simulations.


## Materials and methods

### Protein-protein interaction and pathway enrichment
  Identifying the interacting proteins of RelA is crucial for understanding its role in the molecular pathways that drive OSCC progression. The protein interaction network was built using the STRING database (https://string-db.org/, accessed on November 2, 2023), with RelA as the central protein. This network was expanded to include 50 proteins that directly interact with RelA and are involved in downstream signalling pathways in OSCC. By analysing these interacting proteins, we gain a deeper understanding of the molecular mechanisms driven by RelA in OSCC progression. The RelA interaction network was further evaluated to showcase its participation in molecular pathways using the Enrichr program (https://maayanlab.cloud/Enrichr/, accessed on November 4, 2023).


### Dataset selection and descriptor generation
  The ChEMBL database21 (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chembl/, accessed on November 10, 2023) is a comprehensive resource that provides bioactivity data on inhibitors and activators, making it an ideal source for potential RelA inhibitors. The RelA inhibitors were collected and curated to select those with a half-maximum inhibitory concentration (IC50) value. The inhibitors were then categorised into 2 groups: high-active (class 1, IC50 < 10 µM) and low-active (class 0, IC50 ≥ 10 µM), allowing for a focused analysis. Next, PaDEL software22 was employed to generate molecular descriptors for each inhibitor, using the SMILES format derived from the ChEMBL database. Although PaDEL generates 1444 descriptors for each inhibitor, only those with significant variance across both high- and low-active classes were retained.


### Neural network model and evaluation
  Next, a classification models were generated using STATISTICA version 13.5 to efficiently analyse large datasets and identify patterns between molecular features and biological activity. Before constructing the model, the predictive power of each descriptor was assessed using the Chi-square test. The top 25 molecular descriptors were selected based on their high Chi-square test scores and significant P-values (P < .001). Then, an ANN-based MLP classifier23 and logistic regression model (benchmark) was constructed using the top 25 molecular descriptors to evaluate the classification performance, for which the dataset was divided into training (70%), test (15%), and validation (15%) sets. Furthermore, a maximum of 1000 ANNs were generated. Among these, the classifier that exhibited outstanding performance with the highest predictive ability as determined through accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, F1 score, Matthews correlation coefficient (MCC), and receiver operating characteristic (ROC)24 was selected and assessed using an external dataset.


### Brown algae compounds as an external dataset and descriptor generation
  For the selected classifier, additional predictions were made using compounds derived from marine organisms. The chemical structures of these substances were obtained from the Comprehensive Marine Natural Products Database (CMNPD, accessed on November 15, 2023),25 which provides SMILES format for each compound. CMNPD allows the extraction of chemicals specific to selected marine species and can be accessed at https://www.cmnpd.org/. A total of 1119 compounds derived from brown algae were collected for this study. The PaDEL software was then used to generate 25 molecular descriptors for all 1119 compounds. The ANN classifier, utilising these 25 descriptors, was employed to classify each molecule as either a ‘high-active’ or ‘low-active’ inhibitor of RelA. Compounds classified as ‘highly active inhibitors’ by the ANN were selected for further docking analysis.


### Molecular docking with selected brown algae compounds
  The CMNPD database was utilised to retrieve the structures of brown algae compounds in structure-data (SDF) format, which were identified as high-active inhibitors by the ANN model and further exported for molecular docking studies. Simultaneously, the SDF structures of well-known RelA inhibitors (classified as high-active with IC50 < 10 µM) were obtained from the ChEMBL database for comparative analysis. All collected structures were subjected to energy minimisation using the OPLS force field in the LigPrep module of the Schrödinger Maestro 11.2 version.26 Meanwhile, a search was conducted in the protein data bank (https://www.rcsb.org/) to retrieve the 3-dimensional structure of RelA. The RelA subunit, a key player in the NF-κB signalling pathway, cooperatively functions with p105 to bind DNA and initiate downstream signalling events. To explore this further, we examined the functional complex structure of the RelA and p105 subunits in the PDB database. The X-ray structure (PDB ID: 2O61; resolution: 2.80 Å) was retrieved, which contains the functional heterodimeric complex of RelA. The structure was then processed via the protein preparation wizard, Schrodinger Suite.

  Consequently, a receptor grid was generated to cover the entire RelA structure to explore potential binding/inhibitory sites, aiming to disrupt either heterodimer formation or DNA binding. Blind docking using Glide in Schrödinger Maestro 11.2 was conducted for known RelA inhibitors (class 1, IC50 < 10 µM) from the ChEMBL database as well as the brown algae compounds, enabling the identification of crucial amino acids contributing to inhibitor binding. The RelA-docked compounds, classified as ‘highly active inhibitors’ by the ANN model, were ranked based on their Glide scores. The top-ranking molecule with the highest binding affinity was selected and further evaluated for the structural stability of the protein-inhibitor complex using MD simulations.


### MM-GBSA calculations
  The molecular mechanics/generalised born surface area (MM-GBSA) provides a faster and computationally efficient method for estimating the binding free energy in protein-ligand interactions, with high success rates. The free energy of binding (ΔG bind) for the ligand molecules was calculated using the Prime MM-GBSA27 algorithm, based on the docked pose obtained from the Glide algorithm. The MM-GBSA includes the calculation of solvation energy, minimised energies, and surface area energies for the protein-ligand complex, as well as for the unbound protein and ligand.


### Molecular dynamics simulation of the RelA complex
  The MD simulation was used to explore the dynamic state and stability of the protein-inhibitor complex. This simulation technique tracks protein dynamic movements to observe structural changes over the simulation period of 100 ns. GROMACS software version 2019.5 was used to perform the MD simulation.28 Both protein and ligand employed the CHARMM36m force field parameters for their topology construction.29 The system was placed in an orthorhombic periodic boundary box with dimensions of 1 Å. A salt concentration of 0.150 M was maintained, and the system was solvated using TIP3P water molecules, with chloride ions added to neutralise the system. Electrostatic interactions were managed through Particle Mesh Ewald (PME)30 to avoid electrostatic artifacts. The heavy protein atoms were confined, and energy minimisation was conducted with the steepest descent method over 50,000 steps. The simulation system underwent 100 ps equilibration under canonical ensembles (NVT) and isothermal isobaric ensembles (NPT). Van-der-Waals and Coulomb interactions were computed with a 1.4 nm cut-off. Subsequently, a 100 ns MD simulation was executed under constant temperature and pressure of 300 K and 1 bar, utilising a time step of 2 fs, and trajectory data was saved every 5 ps. Finally, the simulation trajectories, such as root mean square deviation (RMSD), root mean square fluctuation (RMSF) for protein and ligand, solvent accessible surface area (SASA), radius of gyration (Rg), and hydrogen bonds (HB) were calculated.31

### Descriptor generation for the RelA inhibitors
  We then collected 156 inhibitors of the RelA protein from the ChEMBL database, in the SMILES format (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chembl/), each associated with its respective bioassay IC50 values. We further classified the 156 compiled compounds into 2 categories: ‘high-active’ or ‘low-active’ inhibitors based on the minimum inhibitory concentration (IC50) value of each molecule. In this classification, 76 molecules were classified as ‘high-active’, whereas the remaining 80 molecules were designated as ‘low-active’ RelA inhibitors. Further, the PaDEL software was used to generate 1444 molecular descriptors for each molecule. However, since not all descriptors significantly influence inhibitory activity, feature selection was necessary to enhance computational efficiency and predictive accuracy. We employed the Chi-Square test, a commonly used technique for feature selection in ANN classifiers, to choose exclusive molecular descriptors that are pertinent to the inhibitor action.46 We shortlisted the top 25 descriptors with significant P-values (P < .05) that were most suitable for class prediction among the generated descriptors. This feature selection strategy effectively reduces computational complexity, minimises redundancy, and mitigates the risk of overfitting during ANN classification.


### Classification model with the crucial descriptors
  The STATISTICA software was employed to construct an ANN-based MLP and logistic regression model for classifying molecules as either ‘high-active’ or ‘low-active’ inhibitors based on the predictor factors. The predictor variables for this investigation consisted of 25 molecular descriptors. A total of 156 molecules were evaluated based on these characteristics. The molecules were classified as ‘high-active’ or ‘low-active’ inhibitors, according to the ChEMBL report. The training, test, and validation sets were separated into 10 random data divisions, with 70% assigned to training, 15% to testing, and 15% to validation. Then, the prediction capacity of the ANN-based MLP classifier was evaluated using accuracy and MCC metrics. Our analysis determined that the training, test, and validation sets achieved a higher prediction rate of 91.37%, 95%, and 95%, respectively. Particularly, the selected classifier model with the training set showed an accuracy of 91.37%, 94.44% sensitivity, 88.06% specificity, an F1 score of 91.90%, 0.82 MCC, and an ROC plot (Figure 4).

  Remarkably, the ANN-based MLP classifier utilising all of the top 25 descriptors exhibited superior performance to the logistic regression model (Table 1). ### Molecular docking and free energy calculations of 1014 ANN-based MLP-predicted inhibitors
  Through the use of an ANN-based MLP classifier, a total of 1014 was classified as high-active inhibitors of RelA. The binding affinities of 1014 inhibitors from brown algae, along with the 76 high-active inhibitors of RelA from the ChEMBL database, were evaluated through molecular docking. Molecular docking is essential to validate the ANN-based MLP predictions by assessing the binding modes, affinities, and stability of the brown algae compounds with RelA. Maestro Glide performs an accurate screening involving the ligand’s conformational, orientational, and positional space. It involves initial rough positioning and scoring, followed by torsionally flexible energy optimisation. The best poses are further refined using Monte Carlo sampling with a model energy function combining empirical and force-field-based terms.53 Post docking using Glide, the ANN-based MLP-predicted high-active inhibitors were ranked based on their negative values (kcal/mol) obtained from the Glide Score. The least negative glide score indicates stronger interactions, which correspond to a favourable binding between the inhibitor and the receptor protein. The 76 high-active RelA inhibitors from the ChEMBL had affinities ranging from −5.30 to −1.31 kcal/mol. Among them, CHEMBL218434 (C22H50N4O2S) showed an affinity of −5.30 kcal/mol with the RelA. Interestingly, the glide scores of the 1014 high-active inhibitors ranged from −8.45 to −1.50 kcal/mol. Phlorethopentafuhalol-A exhibited the highest ranking among brown algae-derived molecules, displaying a high affinity of −8.45 kcal/mol with RelA. However, limited studies have explored the impact of phlorethopentafuhalol-A, with reports highlighting its anti-viral,54 anti-diabetic, and anti-oxidant properties.55

  Figure 5 illustrates the interaction of phlorethopentafuhalol-A (Figure 5A) and CHEMBL218434 (Figure 5B) with the RelA protein. The RelA binding site residues of phlorethopentafuhalol-A were found to be located at or around the DNA binding site, as well as within the heterodimer-forming domain with p105, as indicated by the structural data from PDB IDs: 9BDV and 2O61. This indicates that phlorethopentafuhalol-A may directly interact with key regions involved in DNA binding and protein-protein interactions, potentially exerting a stronger influence on the biological activity of the target complex compared to CHEMBL218434.

  Additionally, the MM-GBSA analysis further validated and confirmed the high binding behaviour of the RelA-phlorethopentafuhalol-A compared to the RelA-CHEMBL218434 complex. The results revealed a more favourable binding interaction for the RelA-phlorethopenta-fuhalol-A complex, with a Gibbs free energy change (ΔG bind) of −41.09 kcal/mol, compared to −39.65 kcal/mol for the RelA-CHEMBL218434 complex.   The SASA represents the degree to which an amino acid interacts with its environment, including the solvent and other protein residues. Changes in the SASA plot reflect alterations in the exposure of specific amino acid residues, which can influence the protein's structure. The analysis revealed that the SASA of apo RelA was 150 nm², while the RelA-phlorethopentafuhalol-A complex (Figure 8A) had an equivalent SASA of 150.3 nm². The RelA-CHEMBL218434 complex (Figure 9A) showed the highest SASA of 151.2 nm², indicating greater surface exposure compared to the other complexes. This increased SASA in the RelA-CHEMBL218434 complex suggests greater structural flexibility, potentially leading to complex instability. The Rg is a crucial parameter that reflects the overall compactness or expansion of a protein structure. The Rg values for all 3 systems, RelA (apo state) in water, RelA-phlorethopentafuhalol-A, and RelA-CHEMBL218434 were found to be below 2.3 nm, indicating increased rigidity and stability in their folded states (Figures 8B and 9B). The hydrogen bond analysis of the RelA structure in its apo state, before ligand binding, showed an average of 188.53 HB. After binding with phlorethopentafuhalol-A (Figure 8C), the number of HB in the RelA structure decreased to 184.43, and it further decreased to 183.83 after binding with CHEMBL218434 (Figure 9C). Consequently, the phlorethopentafuhalol-A upon binding to RelA increased the HB compared to CHEMBL218434, suggesting enhanced stability of the RelA structure. We also computed the number of HB between the ligand and protein throughout the 100 ns simulation period, which provides a precise measure of complex stability. Phlorethopentafuhalol-A formed a maximum of 11 HB with RelA (Figure 8D), while CHEMBL218434 formed 8 HB, fewer in comparison (Figure 9D). ## Discussion
  MAP kinase activation is implicated in a variety of cancers, which are involved in cancer cell proliferation, tumorigenesis, metastasis, and cancer drug resistance.41 Yang et al reported that RelA is frequently overexpressed in OSCC and correlates with advanced tumour node metastasis stage. RELA drives tumour progression through direct activation of TFAP2A‑mediated Wnt/β‑catenin signalling, enhancing cell proliferation and invasiveness.42 Also, Kumar et al showed that RelA directly binds the hnRNPD promoter in OSCC cells, increasing its expression, which is closely associated with poor prognosis and aggressive tumour progression.43 In recent in vivo studies, it has been confirmed that canonical NF‑κB signalling involving RelA is activated in aggressive OSCC, where inhibition of this pathway significantly reduces tumour invasion and metastasis.44 Additionally, RelA has been identified as a central regulator of pro-tumorigenic genes controlling proliferation, anti-apoptosis, migration, angiogenesis, and therapy resistance.45 Our analysis highlights the implications of RelA in carcinogenesis and as a pivotal target for precision therapeutics.

  The top 25 molecular descriptors are R_TpiPCTPC, AATS5s, GGI3, SpMAD_Dzs, SpMin1_Bhm, SpMin3_Bhv, gmin, SpMin1_Bhv, SpMax1_Bhi, MAXDN2, hmax, SpDiam_Dzm, MAXDN, MPC4, ATS4m, MATS1e, SpDiam_DzZ, MWC8, GGI4, GATS7c, nAtom, nAtomLAC, AATS4m, SpMin8_Bhm, and SRW10. Moreover, these chosen descriptors were evaluated and had a strong correlation with each other, ranging from −0.98 to 0.99, with a significant P-value < .001 (Figure 3). Among the chosen descriptors, the 5 (SpMin1_Bhv, SpMax1_Bhi, SpMin1_Bhm, SpMin3_Bhv, and SpMin8_Bhm) were associated with Burden modified eigen values that correspond to atomic masses, atomic van der Waals volumes, atomic Sanderson electronegativities, and atomic polarizabilities descriptor types. Similarly, AATS5s, AATS4m, MATS1e, and GATS7c were linked with autocorrelation descriptors, which encompass both the molecular structure and the physicochemical properties of a molecule. Similarly, the MAXDN2, MAXDN, hmax, and gmin descriptors were related to the atom type electrotopological state; R_TpiPCTPC and MPC4 were associated with path counts; GGI3 and GGI4 with topological charge; SpMAD_Dzs, SpDiam_Dzm, and SpDiam_DzZ were associated with the Barysz matrix; ATS4m and nAtom were linked with atom count; SRW10 and MWC8 were related to walk counts; and nAtomLAC was associated with the longest aliphatic chain descriptors.47,48 Overall, these chosen descriptors were significant as they represent important physicochemical characteristics of the inhibitors. Particularly, these descriptors inherently represent crucial drug-like properties by demonstrating ADME-relevant physicochemical characteristics. Notably, electrotopological state indices such as MAXDN, hmax, and gmin integrate both electronic and topological attributes of atoms within the molecular scaffold. These descriptors have been extensively utilised in QSAR/QSPR studies for predicting toxicity, aqueous solubility, and membrane permeability, which are crucial parameters in early-stage drug screening.49 In parallel, autocorrelation descriptors such as AATS5s and MATS1e capture the spatial distribution of atomic properties like mass and electronegativity across the molecular graph.50 These features are particularly relevant to absorption, distribution, and plasma protein binding, which are the key determinants of a compound’s pharmacokinetic behavior.51 Additionally, Burden eigenvalues and topological indices, including SpMin1_Bhm and GGI3, reflect aspects such as molecular size, structural flexibility, lipophilicity, and charge dispersion.52 These properties play a pivotal role in influencing metabolic stability and overall bioavailability.   The CMNPD database provides comprehensive structural data on marine-derived small molecules, which are valuable for pharmacological research, including drug discovery and data analysis.25 For this evaluation, the 1212 compounds, represented as SMILES codes, were processed to calculate molecular descriptors, which served as input features for the ANN model. The classifier predicted that 1014 of these compounds could act as highly active RelA inhibitors, highlighting the potential of brown algae as a source for novel therapeutic agents.

  Overall, our molecular docking, binding free energy scores, and trajectory analysis using root-mean-square and hydrogen bond analysis indicate a more stable binding of phlorethopentafuhalol-A with RelA compared to CHEMBL218434. However, this study has a few limitations, including the lack of experimental validation of the biological activity of phlorethopentafuhalol-A, despite its identification as a potential RelA inhibitor through molecular docking and MD simulations. Additionally, the model's predictive performance across different OSCC populations or other cancer types was not experimentally assessed. Subsequent validation should emphasise the in vitro assessment of phlorethopentafuhalol-A in OSCC cell lines to evaluate its cytotoxicity and anti-inflammatory properties. Moreover, in vivo studies are crucial for examining its pharmacokinetics, bioavailability, and effectiveness in tumour suppression. These measures will be essential in promoting phlorethopentafuhalol-A as a prospective treatment drug aimed against RelA in OSCC.


## Conclusions
  Through a series of computational investigations, we have demonstrated that RelA links cancer-related pathways and mediates key oncogenic signalling processes and could be a promising therapeutic target for OSCC. By identifying critical molecular descriptors associated with RelA inhibitory activity, we developed an ANN-based MLP model with strong classification capabilities. This model efficiently selects marine compounds with the potential to inhibit RelA, providing valuable insights for targeting this protein in cancer therapy. Molecular docking and MD simulation studies revealed that phlorethopentafuhalol-A, derived from brown algae, demonstrated a high affinity for RelA, resulting in a stable binding conformation. A comparison with known RelA inhibitors showed that phlorethopentafuhalol-A outperformed these compounds, exhibiting stronger binding within the RelA active site. This report provides the first evidence suggesting that phlorethopentafuhalol-A has the potential to act as an effective inhibitor of RelA. However, further in vitro and in vivo studies are needed to confirm and validate these in-silico findings.
