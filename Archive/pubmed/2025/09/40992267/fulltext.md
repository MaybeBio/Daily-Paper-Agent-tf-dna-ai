Breast cancer is the second most common cancer among women worldwide, highlighting the need for safer and more effective treatments. Ziziphus jujuba, known for its rich bioactive compounds, shows promise, but its precise mechanisms in breast cancer are not fully understood. This study used an integrated approach combining network pharmacology, gene expression profiling, molecular docking simulations, and in vitro assays to investigate the therapeutic potential of Z. jujuba and its active flavonoid, spinosin. A total of 202 target genes were identified, with AKT1 and TP53 as central nodes enriched in the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway. Gene expression analysis revealed that TP53 was significantly overexpressed in breast cancer tissues, while AKT1 showed a non-significant trend towards elevated expression. Molecular docking revealed strong binding affinities of spinosin to AKT1 (-7.72 kcal/mol) and TP53 (-6.27 kcal/mol). In vitro MTT assays demonstrated that spinosin reduced MCF-7 cell viability with an LC(50) of 101.945 mug/mL and induced 21.40 % total apoptosis, mainly in the late apoptotic phase. In contrast, doxorubicin, used as the positive control, demonstrated greater efficacy with an LC(50) of 8.81 mug/mL and induced a higher level of early-stage apoptosis (31.35 %). The inclusion of doxorubicin strengthens the experimental model, while the findings support spinosin as a natural, multi-targeted anti-cancer agent that acts through AKT1 and TP53 to reduce cell viability and induce apoptosis. Further experimental research, including in vivo validation, is necessary to validate these findings and clarify the underlying mechanisms of spinosin and Z. jujuba in cancer treatment.
