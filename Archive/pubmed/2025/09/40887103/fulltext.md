# Glycyrrhizin alleviates contrast-induced acute kidney injury via inhibiting HMGB1-mediated renal tubular epithelial cells ferroptosis


## Abstract
  Contrast-induced acute kidney injury (CI-AKI) is the third leading cause of AKI, but there are no effective preventive or therapeutic measures in clinical practice. Glycyrrhizin, a bioactive compound isolated from the Glycyrrhiza glabra L., exhibits anti-inflammatory effects; however, the effects and mechanisms of glycyrrhizin on CI-AKI remain unknown. In present study, the effects of glycyrrhizin on renal dysfunction and tissue damage were evaluated in CI-AKI rats and mice. And the mechanisms were further investigated in iohexol treated renal tubular epithelial cells. Molecular docking and network pharmacology were used to discover the binding targets of glycyrrhizin and identify potential pathogenic pathway. Gene knockout mice and gene silencing cells were used to detect whether glycyrrhizin alleviated CI-AKI through target proteins mediated pathway. Results showed that both pretreatment and co-treatment with glycyrrhizin could alleviate iohexol-induced renal dysfunction and pathological damage in vivo. Similarly, glycyrrhizin could improve iohexol-induced decrease in cell viability of both HK-2 cells and primary mice renal tubular epithelial cells. Mechanistically, glycyrrhizin could directly bind to the active site of HMGB1, then blocking iohexol-induced ferroptosis of renal tubular epithelial cells. HMGB1 silencing was able to inhibit overactivation of AMPK/Beclin-1 axis during CI-AKI, and iohexol-downregulated protein expressions of GPX4 and SLC7A11 were reversed in kidneys of AMPK knockout mice. Comparable results were obtained in vitro with AICAR treatment. Our study is the first to demonstrate that glycyrrhizin exerts both protective and therapeutic effect on CI-AKI by inhibiting tubular epithelial cell ferroptosis via HMGB1/AMPK/Beclin-1 axis, providing a potential choice for treating CI-AKI.


## Introduction
  Contrast induced acute kidney injury (CI-AKI), which accounts for around 12% of all iatrogenic acute kidney injuries [1] and affects approximately 13.3 million individuals around the world [2], has become an important public health problem. Although some articles have pointed out that the use of isotonic contrast agents can reduced the risk of CI-AKI [3]. However, when complicated by other diseases, such as renal insufficiency, hypertension, diabetes, and cardiovascular disease, the incidence of CI-AKI may increase to 50% [4,5]. However, the mechanisms of CI-AKI are complex and there is still no effective preventive or therapeutic intervention for CI-AKI in clinical practice. Therefore, elucidating the underlying mechanisms and discovering new treatment strategies of CI-AKI are essential.

  Renal tubular epithelial cell death and inflammation are often considered to be the important pathological characteristics of AKI [6], especially when exposed to toxic drugs or their metabolites [7]. Our previous studies also found that inflammatory processes played a significant role in the pathogenesis of CI-AKI [8]. Ferroptosis, which was first described in 2012 [9] and closely linked to inflammation [10] and oxidative stress [11], has been reported to be a main form of regulatory cell death in renal tubular epithelial cells in nephrotoxic folic acid-induced AKI [12]. Recently, Dai et al. reported that contrast media could induce the tubular ferroptosis [13]. However, the role and mechanism of ferroptosis during CI-AKI remains to be clarified.

  As a principal driver of a pro-inflammatory network, high-mobility group protein box 1 (HMGB1) has been extensively reported. Although HMGB1 is primarily located in nucleus, it can be transferred to cytoplasm to exert its pro-inflammatory effect and initiate intracellular ferroptosis [14–18]. We previously have found that contrast media, both iohexol and urografin, could increase the release of HMGB1 in human renal proximal tubular cells (HK-2 cells) supernatant accompanied by increased expression of TLR2 and CXCR4 [8]. Consequently, HMGB1 deserves further investigation whether it is also a key regulator in initiating ferroptosis in CI-AKI.

  Glycyrrhiza glabra L., as a commonly used adjuvant, has been used in the clinical practice for its effects of clearing heat and detoxifying, moistening the lungs and relieving cough, and harmonizing various medicines [19]. The components of Glycyrrhiza glabra L. are complex, among which glycyrrhizin, as one of its functional ingredients, seems to be protective in renal ischemia reperfusion injury and cisplatin-induced AKI [20, 21]. Glycyrrhizin is a specific inhibitor of HMGB1, which can directly bind with the active site of HMGB1 [22]. Nevertheless, it is unclear whether glycyrrhizin can alleviate ferroptosis by inhibiting HMGB1 and its downstream signaling pathway to bring its protective effect on CI-AKI.

  Consequently, this study was performed to investigate the role and mechanism of HMGB1 in mediating renal tubular epithelial cell ferroptosis in CI-AKI, and explore whether glycyrrhizin can serve as a preventive or therapeutic choice for CI-AKI.


## Materials and methods

### Animal models
  Animal studies were approved by the Committee on the Ethics of Animal Experiments of Central South University (No. 2020-S493) and were performed in accordance with the National Institutes of Health Guidelines for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals. Both male SD rats and male C57BL/6 mice were purchased from Borui Xin (Changsha, China). AMPKα2 knockout (KO) mice in the C57BL/6 background were purchased from the Cyagen Biosciences (Suzhou) Inc. All animals (3∼5 mice per cage,4 rats per cage) were housed under SPF conditions (12 h light/dark cycle, 21∼24 °C and 40%∼60% humidity) with ad libitum access to water and standard laboratory chow diet. Experimental groups contained 3∼5 mice or 3∼4 rats. As we previously reported [23], to induce the CI-AKI rat model, male rats (250 g) were treated with water deprivation for three days before intramuscular injection of furosemide (10 mL/kg). After 30 min, iohexol (350 mg iodine/mL, GE Healthcare, China) (20 mL/kg) was administered via the tail vein. To induce the CI-AKI mouse model [24], male mice (6 ∼ 8 weeks) who underwent unilateral right nephrectomy before 3 weeks were treated with water deprivation for 48 h and intraperitoneally injected with furosemide (15 mL/kg). After 30 min, iohexol (350 mg iodine/mL, GE Healthcare, China) (20 mL/kg) was administered via tail vein. Glycyrrhizin was purchased from Selleck (Houston, USA) and intragastrically administered to rats one week before iohexol treatment (150 mg/kg) or co-treated with iohexol (150 mg/kg).


### Measurement of kidney function and injury
  Serum creatinine (SCr) and blood urea nitrogen (BUN) were measured using a fully automated biochemical analyzer (Hitachi 7600 A, Japan) in the clinical laboratory of the Third Xiangya Hospital to evaluate renal function. Formalin-fixed and paraffin-embedded left renal tissues were dyed with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) for pathological assessment of renal tissues. The following qualitative scoring system was used to assess the tubular injury [25]: 0, normal; 1, mild tubular injury with 0% ∼ 10% of the affected area; 2, moderate tubular injury with 11% ∼ 25% of the affected area; 3, severe tubular injury with 26% ∼ 50% of the affected area; 4, severe injury with 51% ∼ 75% of the affected area; 5, highly severe tubular injury with 76% ∼ 100% of the affected area.


### Cell culture and treatment
  HK-2 cells were purchased from Zhongqiao Xinzhou Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). The primary renal tubular epithelial cells were extracted from the kidneys of WT mice within 5 min after euthanasia. Briefly, renal cortex was chopped and incubated at 37 °C for 30 min with 1 mg/mL collagenase II. Then, Dulbecco modified Eagle medium/F12 medium (DMEM/F12, Gibco, Thermo Fisher Scientific) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Gibco, Thermo Fisher Scientific) was added to inactivate collagenase. Next, the digested kidney tissue passed through 100, 70 and 40 μM filter, successively. The filtrate was centrifuged at 1000 r/m for 5 min, and the suspension was inoculated into a culture bottle until fusion occurs. Cells were cultured in DMEM/F12 containing 10% FBS, 1% penicillin (Gibco, Thermo Fisher Scientific), and 1% streptomycin (Gibco, Thermo Fisher Scientific) at 37 °C and 5% CO2 in an incubator until reaching to a density of 70% in a 6 well, 12 well, or 96 well porous plate. To induce CI-AKI cell models, Cells were treated with iohexol (75 mg iodine/mL) for 48h. Glycyrrhizin (100 μM) was used 90 min before iohexol treatment, and then co-intervened with iohexol for 48 h. Ferrostatin-1 (Fer-1) (10 μM) was used 60 min before iohexol treatment, and then co-intervened with iohexol for 48 h. Erastin (5 μM) was treated for 48 h. Cell viability was measured by Cell Count Kit-8 (CCK-8) (GLPBIO, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.


### Transmission electron microscope (TEM)examination
  As previously described [26], at the end of iohexol (75 mg iodine/mL, 48h) treatment, the collected cells were stored at 4 °C and fixed with transmission electron microscopy (TEM)-grade fixative under light-protected conditions (provided by Servicebio). Following two PBS washes, the cells underwent standardized dehydration, osmication, embedding, sectioning, and staining procedures. The ultrastructure of HK2 cells and mitochondria were examined using TEM (Hitachi H-7700).


### Cell transfection and interference
  The siRNA of HMGB1 and its matched scramble control were purchased from Qingke Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). According to the manufacturer’s instructions, the transfection reagent (Qingke, China) was used to transfect HK-2 cells with 100 nmol/L HMGB1 siRNA. Following the manufacturer’s instructions, cells were incubated in antibiotic-free medium for 12 h. Tsnanofect-V2 (Qingke, China) and siRNA were prepared in low-serum medium (2%FBS) and applied to the cells. After 4 h, the medium was replaced with complete medium (10%FBS), and same concentration of siRNA and Tsnanofect-V2 was administered. HMGB1 expression levels were quantified 48 h post-transfection with Western blotting and real-time PCR. The sense sequences for HMGB1 siRNA was ‵5-GAAGACCAUGUCUGCUAAA-3′.


### Western blotting
  In this study, the following primary antibodies were used: anti-HMGB1 (ABclonal, A19529, 1:1000), anti-AMPKα1/α2 (ABclonal, A12718, 1:1000), Anti-Beclin-1 (ABclonal, A17028, 1:1000), Anti-GPX4 (ABclonal, A11243, 1:1000), Anti-ACSL4 (ABclonal, A6826, 1:1000), Anti-SLC7A11 (ABclonal, A2413, 1:1000), Anti-p-AMPK Thr172 (Cell Signaling Technology, 2535S, 1:1000), Anti-p-Beclin-1 Ser93 (Cell Signaling Technology, 14717S, 1:1000), Anti-β-actin (Proteintech, 66009-1-Ig, 1:5000). All antibodies were stored at −20 °C, the pre-configured antibodies are stored at 4 °C and pre-configured antibodies were reused within 10 times. Western blotting was performed, and band intensities were adjusted to internal control.


### Immunofluorescence (IF)
  For animal tissue, paraffin sections were converted to water using dewaxing solution and anhydrous ethanol. After washing with PBS, they were closed with 3% BSA for 30 min. Antigen repair solution was used for antigen repair. Primary antibodies and fluorescein were incubated at 4 °C overnight. After three washes with PBS, the secondary antibody was added and incubated for 50 min at room temperature away from light. DAPI dye solution was added and incubated at room temperature for 10 min without exposure to light after three PBS washes. For cellular immunofluorescence (IF), iohexol-treated HK-2 cells were washed three times with PBS before fixing the cells with 4% paraformaldehyde fixative for 20 min at 37 °C, followed by fixation with 0.3% Triton X-100 at 4 °C for 20 min. Primary antibodies were then added and incubated for 4 h at room temperature. Secondary antibody (diluted with 5% BSA) was added and incubated for 30 min at room temperature after washing with PBS. Anti-fluorescent burst film sealing was performed after staining the nuclei in DAPI dye solution. Finally, confocal microscopy was used for photographic analysis (Leica). The following primary antibody was used: anti- HMGB1 (ABclonal, A25444, 1:200).


### Immunohistochemistry (IHC)
  After dewaxing, the slices were sealed at room temperature for 30 min, and then incubated with the primary antibody (anti- HMGB1, ABclonal, A25444, 1:200) at 4 °C overnight. Then, the slices were left at room temperature for 15 min (rewarming), rinsed 3 times with TBST, and soaked 3 times for 3 min each time. After that, the slices were covered with secondary antibody at 37 °C for 45 min, and then 50 ∼ 100 mL (depending on tissue size) freshly prepared DAB working solution were added to each slice after the sections had been TBST-washed. Hematoxylin was used to stain the nucleus once more before being viewed under an optical microscope. The IHC Toolbox in Image J software was used to analyze the proportion of positive areas. ImageJ software (version 2) was used for analysis and randomization.


### Reactive oxygen species (ROS) assay, malondialdehyde (MDA) assay, lipid hydroperoxide (LPO) assay, glutathione (GSH) assay, and detection of divalent iron
  ROS assay kit (Beyotime, S0033S, China), MDA assay kit (Beyotime, S0131S, China), LPO assay kit (Solarbio, BC5240, China), and GSH assay kit (Beyotime, S0052, China) were used to detect the biochemical indicators related to ferroptosis. FerroOrange (DOJINDO, F374, Japan), a fluorescent probe, was used to detect the divalent iron. All experimental steps were strictly carried out in accordance with the instructions of the reagent kits.


### Molecular docking and protein docking
  The PDB format structures of HMGB1 (separated from PDB code:2YRQ), AMP-activated protein kinase alpha 2(AMPKα2) (PDB code:4cff), and Beclin-1 (PDB code:4zw1) were downloaded from the PDB database. Regarding protein-molecule docking, the structure of glycyrrhizin was downloaded from PubChem database (PubChem CID:14982) and PDB database (PDB code: 7c2x), and the format was converted to PDB using Open ball (version 3.1.1). Based on the information from these two databases, the 3D structure of glycyrrhizin was computed in Pymol software (version 1.5.0.4). PyMol software was used to dehydrate proteins and small molecules. Hydrogen bonds were added to proteins using Autodock software (version 4.2.6), with their charge and rigidity properties calculated. Then, the HMGB1 was docked with glycyrrhizin, and the optimal result were visualized using PyMol software. The affinity between glycyrrhizin and HMGB1 based on binding energy was evaluated, and the optimal conformation was selected.

  Regarding protein-protein rigid docking, after processing the proteins using the above methods, the ClusPro website [27–30] (Doctoral University, https://cluspro.bu.edu/) was used to dock. Results visualization was demonstrated by Pymol software, with the optimal conformation displayed based on the Lowest Energy and Weighted Score.


### Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) enrichment
  The CTD database (https://ctdbase.org/) was used to obtain the target of glycyrrhizin. The Metascape website (https://metascape.org) was used to perform KEGG enrichment analysis. Finally, the Omicstudio website (https://www.omicstudio.cn/) was used to visualize the results.


### Statistical analysis
  Data were expressed as mean ± SD. Student t-test was used to compare the differences between two groups. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), two-way analysis of variance, and three-way analysis of variance, along with Tukey’s posterior, were used to compare the differences between multiple groups when needed. Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism 9.5.0. The statistically significant difference was defined as a two tailed value with p < 0.05.


## Results

### Iohexol significantly induces ferroptosis of renal tubular epithelial cells, and inhibiting ferroptosis attenuates iohexol-induced kidney injury
  Based on the previous cornerstone of our group, we successfully established the CI-AKI rats and mice models (Figure 1a). Iohexol significantly increased SCr, BUN, and renal tubular injury score, and induced tubular vacuolization and glomerular atrophy at 24 h (Figure 1b–h). The indicators of ferroptosis, including protein expressions of Glutathione Peroxidase 4 (GPX4), Acyl-CoA synthetase long-chain family member 4 (ACSL4), and Solute Carrier Family 7 Member 11 (SLC7A11), were detected and showed that protein expressions of GPX4 and SLC7A11 were significantly down-regulated in the kidneys of AKI rats treated with iohexol, while ACSL4 protein expression was up-regulated (Figure 1i–j). Similarly, down-regulated mRNA (Supplementary Figure 1) and protein (Figure 1i, k) expressions of GPX4 were found in the kidneys of CI-AKI mice. Additionally, we detected the other ferroptosis-related markers, including the levels of ROS, MDA, LPO and GSH, in the kidneys of CI-AKI rats and mice, which found that iohexol significantly increased MDA (Figure 1l) and LPO (Figure 1m) levels, while depleting GSH (Figure 1n) in rats, with increased levels of ROS (Supplementary Figure 2) and MDA (Supplementary Figure 3) also shown in mice.

  The iohexol-induced ferroptosis in kidneys of CI-AKI in vivo was corroborated in vitro experiments. After treating HK-2 cells with 75 mg iodine/mL, we observed that iohexol induced the mitochondrial swelling, cellular vacuolization, and other classical alterations of ferroptosis using TEM (Figure 2a). Down-regulated GPX4 protein expression, and increased protein expressions of ACSL4 and Tfr1 were also found in iohexol treated HK-2 cells (Figure 2b, c). Similarly, the increased levels of MDA and LPO, and the decreased level of GSH in iohexol treated HK-2 cells were consistent with what we observed in vivo (Figure 2d). Additionally, we used a probe to detect the intracellular ferrous ion content, and results showed that iohexol significantly induced the accumulation of ferrous ions in HK-2 cells (Figure 2e).

  We subsequently assessed the contribution of ferroptosis during CI-AKI using Fer-1 (ferroptosis inhibitor) and erastin (ferroptosis inducer). The decreased cell viability (Supplementary Figure 5, 6), down-regulated protein expression of GPX4 (Figure 2f, g), up-regulated protein expression of Tfr1 (Figure 2f, h), MDA generation (Figure 2i) and GSH depletion (Figure 2j) induced by iohexol were all significantly reversed by Fer-1 treatment in HK-2 cells. Furthermore, as a ferroptosis inducer, the erastin-induced decrease in cell viability was significantly rescued by Fer-1 (Supplementary Figure 5).

  Collectively, above findings demonstrate that ferroptosis of renal tubular epithelial cells may participant in CI-AKI.


### HMGB1 accumulates in the kidneys of CI-AKI rats, and HMGB1 silencing alleviates iohexol induced ferroptosis of renal tubular epithelial cells
  To investigate the key target involved in the Iohexol-induced ferroptosis, the protein expressions of HMGB1, which we previously reported to be a crucial driver of pro-inflammation network in CI-AKI [8] and might participate in the initiation of ferroptosis [14–18], were detected in CI-AKI rats, mice, and HK-2 cells. Results showed that protein expressions of HMGB1 were up-regulated in above three models (Figure 3a, b). IHC and IF also showed that HMGB1 positive staining significantly increased in kidneys of CI-AKI rats after Iohexol treatment (Figure 3c, d).

  To better illustrate the role of HMGB1 in iohexol-mediated ferroptosis, we performed gene silencing of HMGB1 in HK-2 cells. The iohexol induced increase in protein expression of ACSL4, and decrease in protein expression of GPX4 and GSH level were significantly reversed in HMGB1 siRNA treated cells compared with those treated with scramble siRNA (Figure 3e–i).

  All the above results suggest that HMGB1 may play a crucial role in CI-AKI by mediating ferroptosis of renal tubular epithelial cells.


### Both pretreatment and co-treatment with glycyrrhizin protect against iohexol-induced kidney injury partly via blocking HMGB1 mediated ferroptosis of renal tubular epithelial cells during CI-AKI
  To evaluate whether the specific inhibitor of HMGB1 [22], glycyrrhizin, which is the active ingredient of Glycyrrhiza glabra L., a drug used in the clinic for the treatment of liver disease, can be a potential choice to protect against CI-AKI, we simultaneously observed the preventive and therapeutic effects of glycyrrhizin in CI-AKI rats (Figure 4a). In rats pretreated with glycyrrhizin before injecting iohexol, iohexol-induced glomerular atrophy as well as tubular vacuolization were significantly alleviated by glycyrrhizin pretreatment (Figure 4b), with recovered levels of SCr, BUN and tubular injury score (Figure 4c–e). TUNEL staining also demonstrated the protective effect of pre-intervention glycyrrhizin on cell death in CI-AKI rats (Supplementary Figure 4). Comparable resluts emerged in groups co-administered glycyrrhizin and iohexol. (Figure 4f–i). Then we also assessed the protective effects of glycyrrhizin in CI-AKI cell models, and results showed that glycyrrhizin pretreatment significantly attenuated iohexol decreased cell viability in both HK-2 cells and primary mice renal tubular epithelial cells (Figure 4j, k).

  Based on the molecular docking analysis, glycyrrhizin might directly bind to HMGB1 (Figure 5a), and the conformations binding energy was shown in Supplementary Figure 8. To further explore whether the protective effect of glycyrrhizin on CI-AKI is partly through blocking HMGB1, the protein expression of HMGB1 was firstly detected in the kidneys of CI-AKI rats and HK-2 cells pretreated or co-treated with or without glycyrrhizin. Results showed that glycyrrhizin pretreatment significantly reversed iohexol-upregulated mRNA (Supplementary Figure 9) and protein expressions of HMGB1 in both rats and HK-2 cells (Figure 5b–e). Similar results were found in rats with glycyrrhizin co-treatment (Figure 5b, f). Additionally, IHC and IF also showed that Iohexol-increased HMGB1 positive staining in kidneys of CI-AKI rats were reversed by glycyrrhizin pretreatment (Figure 5g–i).

  To verify the effect of HMGB1 blocking by glycyrrhizin on iohexol-induced ferroptosis, we detected the biomarkers of ferroptosis, including protein expressions of GPX4, ACSL4, and SLC7A11, and the levels of MDA, LPO, and GSH, in CI-AKI rats and HK-2 cells with or without glycyrrhizin pretreatment or co-treatment. Results showed that iohexol-induced decrease in protein expressions of GPX4 and SLC7A11, and increase in protein expression of ACSL4 were significantly alleviated by glycyrrhizin pretreatment (Figure 6a–d) in kidneys of CI-AKI rats. Similar results were also shown in the kidneys of rats with glycyrrhizin co-treatment in the protein expressions of GPX4 and ACSL4 (Figure 6a, e, f). Above results were further verified in HK-2 cells with glycyrrhizin pretreatment, evidenced by reversed protein expressions of GPX4 and ACSL4 (Figure 6a, g–h). Additionally, generation of MDA and LPO, and GSH depletion induced by iohexol were all significantly reversed by glycyrrhizin pretreatment in CI-AKI rats (Figure 6i–k) and HK-2 cells (Figure 6l–n). To elucidate the underlying mechanisms, we validated the protective efficacy of glycyrrhizin on both erastin and iohexol induced models (Supplementary Figure 5). The glycyrrhizin significantly restored cell viability impaired by these stimuli. Under iohexol treatment, HMGB1 deficient (HMGB1 siRNA) partially attenuated glycyrrhizin’s protection. Similarly, HMGB1 deficiency altered glycyrrhizin’s efficacy in erastin-treated cells, corroborating glycyrrhizin’s HMGB1 antagonizing mechanism to Iohexol induced ferroptosis.

  Taken together, both preventive and therapeutic protective effects of glycyrrhizin on CI-AKI may be partly through inhibiting HMGB1 mediated ferroptosis of renal tubular epithelial cells.


### Glycyrrhizin may alleviate HMGB1 mediated ferroptosis partly through inhibiting overactivation of AMPK/beclin-1 axis
  As KEGG enrichment analysis showed that AMPK-related pathway might be involved in the glycyrrhizin’s protective effect on CI-AKI, we firstly used molecular docking to model the binding of glycyrrhizin and AMPK, but results showed that glycyrrhizin could not directly bind to AMPK. Interestingly, we found that HMGB1 could directly bind to the AMPK α2 subunit by protein-protein docking (Figure 7a; Supplementary Table 1). Consequently, we hypothesized that AMPK might function as a downstream of HMGB1, with the finding that iohexol could significantly upregulate protein expression of p-AMPK Thr172 (Figure 7b–d). Meanwhile, we also found that the phosphorylation of Beclin-1, a reported downstream target of AMPK [31], at Ser93 site was significantly up-regulated in HK-2 cells treated with iohexol (Figure 7b, e, f). What’s more, HMGB1 silencing could reverse iohexol-induced phosphorylation of AMPK (at Thr172 site) and Beclin-1 (at Ser93 site) (Figure 7g–k). Therefore, we speculated that AMPK/Beclin-1 axis might participate in the HMGB1 mediated ferroptosis during CI-AKI.

  To further investigate whether and how AMPK activation at Thr172 site participated in the HMGB1 mediated ferroptosis, a concentration gradient of AICAR, an AMPK α2 specific activator, was used in HK-2 cells. And surprisingly, we found that when given at the concentrations more than 1 mM, AICAR aggravated the iohexol-induced decrease in cell viability (Supplementary Figure 10, 11). What’ more, the iohexol-decreased protein expression of GPX4 was exacerbated in HK-2 cells treated with AICAR at the concentration of 2 mM (Supplementary Figure 12). Then, AMPK α2 subunit knockout mice were used (Figure 8a). Although the absence of AMPKα2 could not alleviate the iohexol induced renal dysfunction and tissue damage (Figure 8b–e), iohexol-decreased protein expressions of GPX4 and SLC7A11 were significantly reversed by AMPK deficiency (Figure 8f–h).

  Above results indicate that overactivation of AMPK/Beclin-1 may be involved in the HMGB1 mediated ferroptosis during CI-AKI.


## Discussion
  CI-AKI, one of the most common causes of AKI, which was previously considered as a self-limiting disease, is now known to be associated with progression of CKD, ongoing functional impairment, and even death [32]. However, medical interventions are insufficient to prevent the development of AKI or to improve the long-term outcomes of AKI since the underlying pathophysiology is unclear [33]. In present study, we firstly found that glycyrrhizin, the active component of Glycyrrhiza glabra L., had both preventive and therapeutic protective effects on iohexol-mediated renal injury. Mechanistically, glycyrrhizin, acting as the specific inhibitor of HMGB1, might alleviate HMGB1 mediated ferroptosis of renal tubular epithelial cells via inhibiting overactivation of AMPK/Beclin-1 axis during CI-AKI.

  In our previous study, we found that contrast media, both iohexol and urografin, could increase the release of HMGB1 in supernatant of HK-2 cells, and HMGB1 inhibitor, glycyrrhizin, could alleviated iohexol-decreased cell viability [8]. However, whether protein expression of HMGB1 is up-regulated in renal tubular epithelial cells during CI-AKI remains unknown. In present study, we firstly reported significantly up-regulated protein expressions of HMGB1 in kidneys of iohexol treated rats and mice, and in the HK-2 cells and primary renal tubular epithelial cells exposed to iohexol. In the CI-AKI rats, Hyewon et al. reported that the HMGB1 specific inhibitor, glycyrrhizin, exerts a protective effect on CI-AKI through inhibiting HMGB1-mediated oxidative stress [34]. To further clarify the role of HMGB1 during CI-AKI, we pretreated and co-treated glycyrrhizin with iohexol and found that, no matter pretreatment or co-treatment, glycyrrhizin seems to be protective against iohexol-induced renal dysfunction and tissue injury, which may provide potential preventive or therapeutic strategies for CI-AKI treatment.

  Ferroptosis in renal tubular epithelial cells has been reported to be crucial in nephrotoxic folic acid-induced AKI [12]. Recently, Dai et al. also reported that contrast media induced the onset of tubular ferroptosis [13]. In consistent, we also confirmed that iohexol significantly induced ferroptosis in renal tubular epithelial cells. Although, Fer-1, the ferroptosis inhibitor, has been widely used as a tool drug in studies, it cannot be used in clinical practice for its hydrophobicity chemical property [35]. In myocardial ischemia-reperfusion (I/R) injury, Zhu et al. reported that glycyrrhizin could inhibit ferroptosis by blocking HMGB1 [36]. However, there is no report on whether glycyrrhizin can inhibit ferroptosis and protect the kidneys in CI-AKI. The results of present study demonstrated that the preventive or therapeutic effect of glycyrrhizin on CI-AKI may be partly through inhibiting HMGB1 mediated ferroptosis, providing the feasibility for targeting ferroptosis to treat CI-AKI in clinical practice, for several well-established formulations of glycyrrhizin has been already on the market.

  As an important gene involved in energy metabolism, AMPK is usually activated by a higher AMP/ATP ratio [37]. Structurally, AMPK is a trimeric complex consisting of a catalytic subunit (α-subunit) and two regulatory subunits (β- and γ-subunits). Activation of AMPK mostly relies on the phosphorylation of the conserved threonine in the activation loop of the structural domain of the kinase, i.e. the Thr172 site, which is also the main active site of AMPK [38]. In previous studies, it has been reported [39] that activation of AMPK can inhibit the ferroptosis and protect against kidney injury which is indeed one of the strategies from the perspective of metabolism and energy supply. In contrast to prior observations, our investigation reveals although AICAR protected against iohexol induced decrease in cell viability when given at 200 μM, high concentrations (≥1mM) of AICAR significantly exacerbate iohexol-decreased cell viability, which indicating that either overactivation or complete inhibition of AICAR might be detrimental. Similar paradoxical finding of AMPK was reported recently by Cai et al. that metformin, as an activator of AMPK, induced renal injury via ferroptosis [40].

  This study also has some limitations. Firstly, Clinical data indicates that patients undergoing iodinated contrast procedures frequently exhibit preexisting comorbidities (e.g. renal/cardiovascular dysfunction [41–43]), rendering kidneys hemodynamically stressed and metabolically vulnerable due to systemic pathologies. This significantly heightens CI-AKI susceptibility. However, it must be acknowledged that animal models frequently fail to fully recapitulate real-world complexity. Therefore, we employed dehydration combined with furosemide administration to reproduce renal hypoperfusion, thereby establishing injury specificity to iohexol exposure—the central focus of this investigation. Secondly, glycyrrhizin’s clinical association with pseudo-hyperaldosteronism raises concerns about undetected adverse effects in our limited-sample study. Although nephroprotection was observed without overt toxicity in mouse and rats, clinical validation remains critical to ensure the safety in renal dysfunction patients. Thirdly, we used the purified glycyrrhizin to investigate its preventive or therapeutic effect on CI-AKI in present study. To promote its clinical application, the effect of marketed products of glycyrrhizin on CI-AKI should be confirmed in vivo and in vitro. It is important to highlight that, we used iohexol to conduct the CI-AKI models. However, there are other types of CI-AKI or AKI, and further studies should be conducted to evaluate the effect of glycyrrhizin. Finally, although we used molecular docking and protein docking to explore the potential inter-relationships of glycyrrhizin with HMGB1 or AMPK, further studies are needed to explore the specific mechanisms.


## Conclusion
  Our study demonstrates both preventive and therapeutic effects of glycyrrhizin on CI-AKI, and the underlying mechanism may be partly through inhibiting HMGB1/AMPK/Beclin-1 mediated ferroptosis of renal tubular epithelial cells. These findings may provide potential strategies for the prevention and treatment of CI-AKI and expand the indication of marketed products of glycyrrhizin.
