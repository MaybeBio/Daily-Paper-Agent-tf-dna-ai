# Tabersonine inhibits inflammation and apoptosis through the JAK1/STAT3 signaling pathway to alleviate LPS-induced acute lung injury


## Supplementary Information
  The online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-17885-3.


## Introduction
  Acute lung injury (ALI), a prevalent condition, continues to exhibit high morbidity and mortality. Its pathogenesis is linked to apoptosis, oxidative stress, and inflammation. Acute respiratory insufficiency, cyanosis, decreased lung compliance, and diffused alveolar infiltration are all key clinical manifestations of ALI1. The most common etiology of ALI is infectious pneumonia2, while sepsis remains the primary cause of mortality in ALI patients3. Sepsis induces ALI by activating plenty of lung-resident macrophages and promoting the release of numerous inflammatory factors. Despite advances in ALI research, a major clinical challenge persists due to the absence of targeted and effective treatments, with current approaches being mainly supportive. Therefore, innovative therapeutic approaches are critically needed in the clinic.

  Tabersonine (Tab), isolated from Rosa sp., is an indole alkaloid reported to possess anti-apoptosis and anti-inflammatory activities4,5, and is regarded as a promising candidate for treating cancer and Alzheimer’s disease. Tab inhibits activation of inflammatory corpuscles in macrophages and mitigates diseases caused by NLRP3 in mice, such as ALI6. However, the specific mechanism underlying Tab’s therapeutic effects in ALI remains to be elucidated. Network pharmacology is a powerful tool for uncovering the mechanism of drug treatment. This study aims to shed light on the key genes and pathways involved in Tab-mediated regulation of macrophages in ALI, and seeks to further elucidate Tab’s underlying mechanism in ALI treatment via a combination of in vitro and in vivo approaches.


## Materials and methods

### Processing and differential expression analysis of single-cell dataset
  The GSE225664 single-cell transcriptome data was retrieved from the GEO database. Seurat package (v 4.3.0) was employed for the analysis of three groups of samples: the normal group, the lipopolysaccharide (LPS) group, and the anti-Gr-1 antibody (aGr) + LPS group. aGr was utilized to block the recruitment of blood mononuclear cells, thereby isolating only interstitial macrophages. The remaining two groups of samples included alveolar macrophages and interstitial macrophages. Gene expression was normalized through the “LogNormalize” method. The “Harmony” R package (v 0.1.1) was applied for the removal of batch effects across sample categories. Based on Harmony analysis results, the first 20 dimensions were selected for clustering and UMAP analysis. A UMAP diagram was generated based on the clustering results. The cluster resolution was set to 0.7, and the cells were divided into 16 different clusters. Gene expression difference between each cluster cell and the whole cell was analyzed, and a heat map was drawn for the first five different genes in each cluster. Cell annotation was performed via SingleR (v 2.0.0). In terms of the different ways of experimental treatment, the cells were categorized into the LPS-treated group (LPS and aGr + LPS) and the Naïve group. Subsequently, an analysis of the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between the LPS and Naïve groups was performed.


### Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) of single-cell dataset
  The WGCNA package (v 1.71.1) was employed to analyze GSE225664. First, samples were clustered, and outliers were removed. Subsequently, a heat map of sample clustering and clinical features was drawn. The independence and average connectivity of the samples were assessed, and a scale-free topology test of the samples under power value was carried out. Based on the power value, a weighted gene co-expression network was constructed with a minimum gene count of 30 and a mergeCutHeight of 0.2. The correlation between sample characteristics (e.g., LPS treatment) and module genes was analyzed, and a correlation heat map was generated for visualization. The ALI module gene was determined by selecting the gene exhibiting the highest correlation in the module.


### Screening and analysis of ALI-Tab targets
  PharmPaper, SuperPred, and SwissTargetPrediction databases were utilized to predict Tab targets, respectively. ALI-Tab targets were identified by intersecting ALI-related DEGs, ALI module genes, and the predicted Tab targets. A protein-protein interaction (PPI) network was constructed by importing ALI-Tab targets into STRING, and the network diagram was drawn via CytoScape (v 3.10.1). The top 10 core targets were identified with the matthews correlation coefficient (MCC) algorithm. The pathway enrichment analysis of ALI-Tab target was performed with the use of the KEGG database, and a gene-pathway relationship map was developed.


### Molecular docking
  The target protein receptor’s three-dimensional structure was retrieved from the RCSB PDB database, while that of ligand molecule was sourced from PubChem. The structures of protein receptor and ligand were imported into AutoDockTools (v 1.5.7), and the original ligands and metal ions in protein receptor were deleted, dehydrated, hydrogenated, and the number of charges was calculated. The semi-flexible docking mode was selected. The coordinate and size of the docking box were calculated with PyMol (v 2.5.0), while the binding conformation was searched through a genetic algorithm. The conformation with the lowest energy in the docking results was selected and visualized through the PLIP tool and PyMol.


### Animal preparation and grouping
  Eight-week-old male C57BL/6 mice were procured from an accredited supplier and kept in the Animal Experimental Center of Hangzhou Medical College (certification no. SCXK (Zhe) 2021-0002). They were allowed free access to food in a barrier environment (temperature: 22 ± 2 ℃, humidity: 50 ± 10%, light/dark period: 12 h/12 h). This experiment gained approval from the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Zhejiang Center of Laboratory Animals (No.: ZJCLA-IACUC-20020125). All animal experimental procedures follow the ARRIVE guidelines and were carried out following the regulations of the Animal Welfare Committee of Zhejiang Experimental Animal Center.

  Following one week of adaptive feeding, 60 mice were randomly assigned into five groups, with each consisting of 12 mice: control (CN) group, LPS group, low-dose Tab (L-Tab) group (20 mg/kg), high-dose Tab (H-Tab) group (40 mg/kg) and dexamethasone (DEX) group (5 mg/kg). 1 h before modeling, mice in the L-Tab and H-Tab groups were administered with Tab (Cat. No.: HY-N1431, MedChemExpress, dissolved in 0.5% CMC-Na) intraperitoneally, while those in the DEX group received DEX (dissolved in dimethyl sulfoxide) (Cat. No.: HY-14648, MedChemExpress), and those in the LPS and CN groups were injected intraperitoneally with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). Intratracheal instillation of LPS (5 mg/kg, L8880, Solarbio, China) was applied to develop an ALI model6, while mice in the CN group were given PBS, with samples collected for analysis after 6 h. The experimental design and research procedures are summarized in Fig. 1.

### Cell culture and treatment
  RAW264.7 cells (CL-0190, Procell, China) were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (C11330500BT, Gibco, USA) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (16140071, Gibco, USA) and 0.1% penicillin/streptomycin (C0222, Beyotime, China) under controlled conditions (37 °C, 5% CO2). An 1-h pretreatment of RAW264.7 cells with Table (5 µM or 10 µM) was carried our prior to 6 h of stimulation with LPS (1 µg/mL). Based on the above treatment, the separated groups were: CN group, LPS group, 5 µM Tab group, and 10 µM Tab group.

  Overexpressed Janus kinase 1 (oe-JAK1) and its negative control (oe-NC) plasmids (PL20250312B) were provided by Shanghai GenePharma Co.,Ltd. RAW264.7 cells with oe-JAK1 were constructed using Lipofectamine 3000 transfection reagent (L3000150, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) following the protocol provided by the reagent manufacturer. After pretreatment with Table (10 µM) for 1 h, the cells were continually subjected to LPS (1 µg/mL) stimulation for 6 h. Following the above treatment, four groups were set accordingly: LPS group, LPS + Tab group, oe-NC + LPS + Tab group, and oe-JAK1 + LPS + Tab group.

  In order to establish a co-culture system, a 1-hour pretreatment of RAW264.7 cells with Table (10 µM) was performed, followed by 6 h of LPS (1 µg/mL) stimulation. Subsequently, the RAW264.7 cell supernatant was taken to stimulate A549 cells (CL-0016, Procell, China) for 24 h. After the above treatment, the following five groups were constructed: CN group, LPS group, LPS + Tab group, oe-NC + LPS + Tab group, and oe-JAK1 + LPS + Tab group.


### Preparation of tissue samples and calculation of lung wet/dry weight (W/D) ratio
  Excessive Zoletil 50 (88SHA, Kunming Boatman Bio, China), a mixture of Zolazepam and Tiletamine with a mass ratio of 1: 1, was used to euthanize mice, and their tissues were extracted. The exudation and residual blood were removed with the use of filter paper. The entire lung tissues from six randomly selected mice were initially weighed while wet, followed by drying at 180 ℃ for 24 h. After drying, the tissues were weighed again, and the W/D ratio was calculated. The left lung tissues of the remaining six mice were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (P0099, Beyotime, China) at 4 ℃ for 24 h and dehydrated, followed by embedding in paraffin and subsequent sectioning for further examination. Observation of the tissue histopathological changes were carried out following hematoxylin-eosin (HE) staining. The right lung tissue was washed with PBS, cut, and preserved at -80 ℃ for subsequent use.


### HE staining and histopathological observation
  After dewaxing and hydrating, paraffin Sect. (4 μm) were dyed with HE (C0105M, Beyotime, China), soaked in water for 5 min, dehydrated, cleared, sealed, and dried. Observations were made and images were captured through an optical microscope. Lung tissue injury was evaluated based on parameters including: pulmonary hemorrhage, neutrophil infiltration, pulmonary capillary congestion, and diaphragm thickening. Injury severity was graded on a scale from 0 to 4, as follows:7 0 indicating normal tissue, 1 indicating mild damage (< 25% injury of the field), 2 indicating moderate damage (25%-50% injury), 3 indicating severe damage (50%-75% injury), and 4 indicating extremely severe damage (> 75% injury). Each mouse’s score was averaged from five randomly selected regions.


### TdT-mediated dUTP nick-end labeling (TUNEL) assay
  We dyed the paraffin sections of mouse lung tissue using the Colorimetric TUNEL Apoptosis Assay Kit (C1094, Beyotime, China) as per the manufacturer’s guidelines. After 3,3’-diaminobenzidine staining, the cell nucleus underwent staining with hematoxylin (C0107, Beyotime, China). The apoptotic cells with TUNEL staining were brown, and the cell nucleus with hematoxylin staining was blue. Finally, apoptosis of the tissue cells was examined under an optical microscope.


### Extraction of bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF), counting of inflammatory cells, and determination of protein concentration in mice
  The mouse lung was washed twice with 1.5 mL of PBS, followed by BALF collection through 5 min of centrifugation at 500 ×g. The total protein concentration in the BALF was determined with the BCA Protein Assay Kit (P0012, Beyotime, China). The cells were observed in Wright-Giemsa Staining Solution (C0131, Beyotime, China), and counted in blood cell counting plate.


### Cell counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay
  Culture of RAW264.7 cells (4 × 103/mL) was performed in serum-free medium in 96-well plates, with three replicate wells in each group. After proper treatment of the cells, 10 µL of CCK-8 reagent (C0038, Beyotime, China) was added for 24 h of culture. The optical density (OD) (490 nm) value was measured with the use of enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA, MK-3, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA), followed by subsequent calcaulation of the cell survival rate.


### lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) release assay
  The detection of LDH release of RAW264.7 cells and LDH content in mouse BALF were performed using the LDH Cytotoxicity Assay Kit (C0017, Beyotime, China) as per the provided instructions. The OD value was detected via enzyme-labeled instrument, and the LDH activity (U/L) was calculated.


### ELISA
  Following the manufacturer’s guidelines, we measured the levels of Interleukin (IL)-1β (E-EL-M0037c), tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) (E-EL-M3063), and IL-6 (E-EL-M0044c) from RAW264.7 cell culture and BALF using the ELISA kit (Elabscience, Wuhan, China).


### Flow cytometry
  Following the protocol of the Annexin V-FITC cell apoptosis detection kit (C1062L, Beyotime, China), we collected A549 cells, washed them twice with PBS, resuspended them in binding buffer, and subsequently stained them with Annexin V-FITC and propidium iodide. Ultimately, flow cytometry (BD Biosciences, USA) was employed for identifying the fluorescence signal in order to assess apoptosis.


### Western blot
  We extracted the protein from cells, BALF, or lung homogenate by RIPA buffer (P0013B, Beyotime, China), and then added protease inhibitor (P1050, Beyotime, China). The BCA method was adopted to measure the protein concentration (P0010, Beyotime, China). The gel was prepared with SDS-PAGE Gel Preparation Kit (P0012A, Beyotime, China). The isolated protein was transferred to a polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membrane through electrophoresis and electrotransformation, and underwent overnight incubation with primary antibodies at 4 ℃, as detailed in Table 1. Following the washing step, the PVDF membrane was subsequently subjected to incubation with the secondary antibody (ab6721, Abcam, Shanghai, China) coupled with horse radish peroxidase for 2 h. The immunoreactive bands were observed with the Hypersensitive ECL Chemiluminescence Kit (P0018M, Beyotime, China). The Image J software was applied for quantifying the strength of the strip. All primary antibodies were procured from Cell Signaling Technology, Inc. (CST, USA).

### Statistical analysis
  GraphPad Prism (v 10.1.2) was utilized for our statistical analysis, with results expressed as mean ± standard deviation. A one-way ANOVA was applied for carrying out comparison between groups, and Sidak’s multiple comparisons test was employed for conducting post-hoc analysis. A p-value below 0.05 was deemed statistically significant.


## Results

Harmony analysis was adopted to remove the batch effect of sample categories (Fig. 2B), and the first 20 dimensions of Harmony analysis were selected for analysis through UMAP (Fig. 2C). UMAP visualization was used to analyze cells subjected to different experimental conditions (Fig. 2D). The cells were divided into 16 different clusters based on UMAP clustering (Fig. 2E), and the first five DEGs in each cluster were visualized through gene expression thermogram (Fig. 2F). Two cell types were identified based on UMAP clustering and cell markers, namely Monocytes and Macrophages (Fig. 2G). Differential analysis of LPS vs. CN yielded 429 DEGs (p-value ≤ 0.05, Supplementary Table 1), with the first 50 DEGs visualized by thermogram (Fig. 2H).

### WGCNA
  Initially, the samples were clustered (Supplementary Fig. 1). After 64 outliers were deleted, 2,559 samples and 4,860 genes were retained. In order to ensure that the network was scale-free, power (scale-free r2 = 0.95, slope=−1.6) with β = 5 was set as the soft threshold (Fig. 3A, B). Based on this power value, a weighted gene co-expression network was constructed with a minimum gene number of 30 and a mergeCutHeight of 0.2 (Fig. 3C). A heat map correlating sample features and modules is shown in Fig. 3D. A total of 1,235 module genes were obtained by taking one positive correlation module, blue (684 genes) and two negative correlation modules, yellow (116 genes) and brown (435 genes) with the highest correlation (Supplementary Table 1).

### Screening and analysis of ALI-Tab targets
  A total of 384 predicted targets for Tab were obtained from the drug-related database (Supplementary Table 1). Among these, 299 were identified from PharmPaper (Norm fit > 0.48), 96 from SuperPred (Probability > 50%) and 85 from SwissTargetPrediction (Probability > 9%). Differential expression analysis revealed 9 intersections between 429 DEGs and 384 Tab targets. WGCNA demonstrated 22 intersections between 1,235 module genes and 384 Tab targets. After merging and removing duplicates, 24 ALI-Tab targets were obtained (Fig. 4A, Supplementary Table 1). The PPI network relationship of the 24 targets is illustrated in Fig. 4B. Through the MCC algorithm, the top 10 core targets: JAK1, IGF1R, MAPK14, IL6R, IL6ST, MMP14, IFNGR1, ROCK2, EIF2S1 and XPO1, were identified (Fig. 4C). KEGG-pathway enrichment analysis showed the primary enrichment of the 24 targets in 19 pathways (Supplementary Table 1), with the JAK-STAT signaling pathway standing out as the most critical one (Fig. 4D). The binding energy between JAK1 and Tab was − 8.43 (Fig. 4E), which indicated the strong binding activity of JAK1 with Tab.

### Tab reduces LPS-induced ALI in mice
  To elucidate Tab’s effects on ALI, we used LPS to establish an ALI mouse model. As depicted in Fig. 5, the lung injury score (P < 0.001, Figs. 5A-B) and the W/D ratio of mice were markedly elevated in the LPS group relative to the CN group (P < 0.001, Fig. 5C). Additionally, we noted a marked increase in the total protein concentration, LDH content, inflammatory cell count, and the levels of pro-inflammatory factors (IL-1β, TNF-α, and IL-6) in BALF (P < 0.001, Figs. 5D-J). At the same time, there was a notable elevation in the level of apoptosis and Cleaved-Caspase3 protein expression in the mouse lung tissue (P < 0.001, Figs. 5K-M). In contrast to the LPS group, the L-Tab, H-Tab and DEX treatment groups demonstrated a marked reduction in the lung injury score (P < 0.001) and the W/D ratio (P < 0.05) among mice. Additionally, significant reductions were observed in the total protein concentration, LDH content, inflammatory cell count, and the levels of proinflammatory factors (IL-1β, TNF-α, and IL-6) in BALF (P < 0.01). Both the apoptosis level and Cleaved-Caspase3 protein expression in the mouse lung tissue showed a marked reduction (P < 0.01). There was no obvious difference in the therapeutic effect against ALI between the H-Tab and DEX groups, and both of the groups exhibited were stronger effect than that of the L-Tab group. Therefore, it can be concluded that Tab could ameliorate lung injury, inflammatory infiltration, as well as apoptosis in mice with LPS-induced ALI.

### Tab inhibits the JAK1/STAT3 signaling pathway activation in the lung tissue of mice with LPS-induced ALI
  To verify the prediction of network pharmacology on Tab in ALI treatment, JAK1 and STAT3 were assessed in the lung tissue of mice with LPS-induced ALI. The mouse lung tissues from the LPS group exhibited significantly elavated levels of p-JAK1 and p-STAT3 expressions (P < 0.001, Figs. 6A-C) relative to the CN group. Notably, Tab intervention significantly suppressed this elevation (P < 0.05). Therefore, Tab’s therapeutic effect on ALI is associated with the suppression of the JAK1/STAT3 signaling pathway.

### Tab suppresses LPS-induced inflammation and the JAK1/STAT3 signaling pathway activation in RAW264.7 cells
  Figures 7A-B demonstrate that Tab concentrations ranging from 0 to 10 µM had no significant effect on the activity of RAW264.7 cells. However, with the increase of Tab’s dosage, it was found that the activity of RAW264.7 cells was significantly affected when Tab’s dosage reached 20 µM (P < 0.01). As presented in Fig. 7BB, 5 µM and 10 µM Tab exhibited protective effects on LPS-stimulated RAW264.7 cells (P < 0.01). Figures 7C-E revealed that Tab intervention can markedly suppress the production of LPS-induced proinflammatory factors (IL-1β, TNF-α, and IL-6) (P < 0.05), and significantly reduce p-JAK1 and p-STAT3 expression (P < 0.01, Figs. 7F-H). Therefore, a conclusion can be drawn that Tab can inhibit LPS-induced inflammation and the JAK1/STAT3 signaling pathway activation in RAW264.7 cells.

### Tab suppresses LPS-induced inflammation of RAW264.7 cells through the JAK1/STAT3 signaling pathway
  To clarify the JAK1/STAT3 signaling pathway’s role in the treatment of ALI with Tab, RAW264.7 cells with oe-JAK1 were constructed. The oe-JAK1 + Tab + LPS group demonstrated a marked increase in the contents of proinflammatory factors (IL-1β, TNF-α, and IL-6) produced by RAW264.7 cells relative to the oe-NC + Tab + LPS group (P < 0.05, Figs. 8A-C). Additionally, a significant increase was observed in p-JAK1 and p-STAT3 expression (P < 0.01, Figs. 8D-F). These findings substantiate that Tab suppresses LPS-induced inflammation of RAW264.7 cells through the JAK1/STAT3 signaling pathway.

### Tab regulates the inflammatory response of macrophages via the JAK1/STAT3 signaling pathway to affect the apoptosis of lung epithelial cells
  In order to determine the JAK1/STAT3 signaling pathway’s role in treatment of ALI with Tab, a co-culture system was established, and the LPS-induced RAW264.7 cell culture supernatant was used to treat A549 cells. In the Tab + LPS group, there were lower levels of IL-1β, TNF-α, and IL-6 in A549 cells (P < 0.001, Figs. 9A-C), but significantly higher survival rate of A549 cells relative to the LPS group (P < 0.001, Fig. 9D). Conversely, a significant reduction was observed in the apoptosis rate, Cleaved-Caspase3 protein expression (P < 0.001, Figs. 9E-H), and p-JAK1 and p-STAT3 expression (P < 0.01, Figs. 9I-K). In comparison to the oe-NC + Tab + LPS group, the oe-JAK1 + Tab + LPS group showed higher contents of IL-1β, TNF-α, and IL-6, but lower survival rate of A549 cells. Additionally, an increase was noted in p-JAK1 and p-STAT3 expression (P < 0.001), as well as in the apoptosis rate and the expression of Cleaved-Caspase3 protein (P < 0.001). These results demonstrate that Tab can regulate the inflammatory response of macrophages via the JAK1/STAT3 signaling pathway, thus affecting lung epithelial cell apoptosis.

## Discussion
  During inflammation, the excessive production of pro-inflammatory mediators (e.g., TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6) activates inflammatory signaling pathways, which exacerbates the cytokine cascade and promotes alveolar epithelial cell apoptosis, thereby aggravating ALI1. ALI is a severe, acute, and progressive form of lung inflammation, with a paucity of treatment options. Most patients afflicted by ALI experience sequelae (e.g., muscle weakness and cognitive impairment)3. In recent years, clinicians and researchers have switched their focus to exploring novel therapeutic drugs aimed at halting the progression of ALI and improving patients’ quality of life. Ongoing in-depth research has unveiled plenty of natural compounds8 that exhibit potential therapeutic effects on ALI, including flavonoids, alkaloids, terpenoids, polyphenols, and quinones.

  Tab is a natural indole alkaloid demonstrating anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, as well as anti-apoptotic properties4,5. It has shown promise in treating a variety of inflammatory diseases, including neuritis, kidney injury, and cardiomyopathy9–11. Research by Zhang et al. demonstrated that Tab could mitigate LPS-induced ALI in mice by inhibiting TRAF6 ubiquitination12. However, its underlying molecular mechanism remains to be clarified. This study employed network pharmacology to predict potential targets of Tab. As a result, 24 Tab targets were obtained. KEGG-pathway analysis demonstrated that the primary signaling pathways linked to these targets are JAK/STAT and cGMP-PKG signaling pathways. Molecular docking results showed that JAK1 and Tab have strong binding activity. Therefore, the JAK/STAT signaling pathway is a potential target pathway for Tab ALI treatment.

  The JAK/STAT signaling pathway stimulated by interferon are critical in the regulation of many biological processes, including hematopoiesis, tissue repair, inflammation, apoptosis, and immune response13. JAK1 is widely expressed in tissues and phosphorylates transcription factor STAT3, which can be activated by members of the IL-6 family, IL-10 family, and interferon-I, and parimarily involves the regulation of cell growth, differentiation, apoptosis, immune response, as well as tumor occurrence and metastasis13. Plenty of studies have proved that the JAK/STAT signaling pathway is involved in the development of ALI, indicating that targeting pathway may be pivotal for effective treatment of ALI14–16. Gp130 regulates inflammation and apoptosis via the JAK1/STAT3 signaling pathway, thereby alleviating lung injury due to LPS stimulation in mice17. Hexahydrocurcumin from Zingiberis rhizoma has been shown to induce acute pneumonia in mice through the JAK1/STAT3 signaling pathway18. JAK1/JAK2 inhibitor ruxolitinib was found to mitigate excessive inflammation in COVID-19 through inhibition of the JAK/STAT pathway19,20. These findings have proved that targeting the JAK/STAT pathway serves as an effective strategy for ameliorating excessive lung inflammation and tissue damage.

  Based on the aforementioned research findings, we hypothesize that the JAK1/STAT3 signaling pathway is a potential promising target for Tab in ALI treatment, and has subsequently verified this hypothesis. In vivo studies revealed that Tab could alleviate lung injury and inflammatory infiltration caused by LPS in ALI mice, and suppress the JAK1/STAT3 signaling pathway activation in the lung tissue. In vitro studies showed Tab’s inhibitory effects on the inflammatory response caused by LPS and the JAK1/STAT3 signaling pathway activation in RAW264.7 cells. Furthermore, the use of JAK1 inhibitor enhanced the therapeutic efficacy of Tab. Further research suggested that LPS-induced RAW264.7 cells promoted A549 cell apoptosis, and Tab intervention reduced A549 cell apoptosis, with the use of JAK1 inhibitor further alleviating this effect.


## Conclusion
  In summary, this study has validated in vivo and in vitro that Tab, which ameliorates LPS-induced ALI, is a promising therapeutic drug for treating ALI, with the mechanism closely related to the JAK1/STAT3 signaling pathway regulating inflammatory response and apoptosis. This study presents Tab as a promising monomer derived from traditional Chinese medicine for ALI treatment, provides theoretical insight to support its clinical use, and lays foundation for further in-depth research of Tab. Nevertheless, these findings require further validation through in vivo experiments, which are expected to constitute a major focus of our future investigations.
