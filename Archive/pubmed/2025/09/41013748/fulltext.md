# Diagnostic and prognostic significance of Super-enhancer-associated genes in Crohn’s disease and immune regulatory roles


## Background
  Super-enhancers (SEs) are implicated in the regulation of the onset and progression of Crohn’s disease (CD). However, the precise mechanisms remain unclear. This study aims to investigate the diagnostic potential and molecular mechanisms of SE-related genes (SERGs) as biomarkers for CD from a transcriptomics perspective.


## Methods
  Data on CD and SERGs were retrieved from public databases. Differential expression analysis, the Maximal Clique Centrality algorithm, and two machine learning methods were applied, complemented by receiver operating characteristic analysis and expression level assessment to identify biomarkers and construct a nomogram for evaluating their diagnostic value. Enrichment and immune infiltration analyses were performed subsequently. Unsupervised clustering categorized CD samples into distinct subtypes based on the biomarkers, and functional and immune microenvironmental differences among these subtypes were examined. Immunohistochemical experiments confirmed the elevated protein expression of hub genes in colonic tissues from patients with CD relative to healthy controls (P < 0.05).


## Results
  PAQR5, IFITM3, PSMB8, and IRF1, were identified as biomarkers for CD, demonstrating robust diagnostic performance, with area under curve (AUC) values of 0.988, 0.973, 0.959, and 0.940, respectively. When integrated into a nomogram, the AUC reached 0.991. Except for PAQR5, the other three genes showed significantly higher expression in CD samples, a trend confirmed clinically by immunohistochemical experiments. These biomarkers were involved in areas such as immune regulation, cell biology, and sensory perception. Immunoassays further elucidated the specific infiltration patterns of different immune cells in CD and highlighted the importance of these biomarkers in regulating immune cell functions. Additionally, we classified the CD samples into two subtypes, with the biomarkers exhibiting significant expression differences between the subtypes. These two subtypes possessed distinct immune microenvironments, with the biomarkers showing moderate correlation with the differential immune cells between the subtypes.


## Conclusion
  PAQR5, IFITM3, PSMB8, and IRF1 serve as biomarkers for CD, offering substantial diagnostic and immune regulatory value. These biomarkers differentiate CD into two subtypes, providing insights for personalized therapeutic strategies.


## Introduction
  Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) encompasses a range of inflammatory disorders, primarily Crohn’s disease (CD) and ulcerative colitis (UC) [1]. CD is a chronic inflammatory condition affecting various parts of the gastrointestinal tract and is increasingly recognized as a global health concern. Its prevalence continues to rise in both developed and developing countries [2]. Notably, CD is associated with an elevated risk of colorectal cancer (CRC), particularly in individuals with long-standing disease [3], imposing significant emotional and financial burdens on patients. Given the complexity and severity of CD, understanding its etiology is essential. While the exact cause remains unresolved, it is widely believed to involve the interplay of environmental factors, immune responses, genetic susceptibility, and the intestinal microbiota [4].

  CD is typically characterized by transmural inflammation, potentially affecting any region of the gastrointestinal tract, from the mouth to the anus [5]. The disease exhibits heterogeneous behavior, with distinct inflammatory, fibrostenotic, and penetrating subtypes. However, reliable biomarkers to predict these subtypes at diagnosis and biological mechanisms that explain their differences are yet to be identified. Furthermore, CD manifests in diverse ways, often overlapping with other forms of IBD. Extraintestinal complications of CD, such as anemia, aphthous stomatitis, anterior uveitis, cholelithiasis, and inflammatory arthropathies, can be subtle and challenging to diagnose, contributing to significant etiological uncertainty [6]. Current treatments primarily involve surgical resection, anti-inflammatory medications, and immunosuppressants, but these options are often associated with a high rate of short-term recurrence [7]. Consequently, elucidating the pathogenesis of CD and developing effective preventive and therapeutic strategies is an urgent priority.

  Enhancers are cis-regulatory elements that precisely modulate the expression of target genes [8]. Super-enhancers (SEs), a distinct class of enhancers, are clustered genomic regions composed of multiple constituent enhancers, which densely bind key transcription factors and cofactors to predominantly orchestrate the gene expression programs in embryonic stem cells. SEs have been implicated in the development of a wide range of diseases [9]. While much of the research on SEs has focused on cancer progression, recent studies in IBD have uncovered disease-specific SE sites. For instance, Li et al. identified two SEs associated with the pathogenic genes PCK1 and EFNA1, which are highly expressed in colitis [10]. Additionally, Chen et al. discovered a disease-specific SE that remotely regulates the expression of the oncogene ETS2 in IBD and Colorectal Cancer (CRC) [11]. Emerging evidence indicates that SEs and their chromatin architectures can dynamically respond to inflammatory stimuli even in terminally differentiated cells, leading to aberrant expression of inflammatory genes. Notably, SE-associated genes exhibit heightened sensitivity to enhancer-targeted inhibition, suggesting that SE suppression may represent a promising therapeutic strategy for inflammatory disorders [12]. In the context of CD, SEs likely play pivotal roles in disease pathogenesis by orchestrating immune responses and inflammatory processes. Therefore, elucidating the regulatory mechanisms of SE-related genes will not only advance our understanding of CD etiology but also provide novel therapeutic targets for mitigating inflammatory responses through SE modulation.

  This study investigated the diagnostic potential of SE-related genes (SERGs) as biomarkers for CD and explored their molecular regulatory mechanisms. This analysis, based on publicly available transcriptomic data of CD, leveraged bioinformatics tools to offer new insights for the clinical diagnosis, prevention, and treatment of CD.


## Materials and methods

### Data source
  Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gds) stored a large amount of gene expression data and related high-throughput genomics data. The transcriptome datasets related to CD were downloaded from the GEO database. The training dataset, GSE102133, was based on the GPL6244 platform and included 65 CD ileal tissue samples and 12 normal ileum tissue samples, with chip sequencing as the method [13]. The validation set, GSE75214, also based on the GPL6244 platform, comprised 67 CD ileum tissue samples and 11 normal ileum samples, with chip sequencing utilized for data generation [14].

  Additionally, SERGs associated with CD were retrieved from the Super-Enhancer Archive (SEA) v.3.0 database (http://sea.edbc.org) [15] and the Super-Enhancer database (SEdb) (http://www.licpathway.net/sedb) [16]. The data from both sources were combined and duplicates removed, resulting in a total of 735 SERGs.


### Identification and enrichment analysis of differentially expressed SERGs (DE-SERGs)
  Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified by comparing CD and control samples in the training set, using a threshold of |log2Fold Change (FC)|> 0.5 and adj.P < 0.05. The limma package (v 1.38.0) [17] was employed for this analysis. Visualization of DEGs was carried out using a volcano plot (ggplot2 package, v 3.4.1) [18] and a heatmap (ComplexHeatmap package, v 2.14.0) [19]. The ggvenn package (v 0.1.9) [20] was used to identify the overlap between DEGs and SERGs, with the intersecting genes referred to as DE-SERGs.

  To further investigate the signaling pathways and biological mechanisms associated with DE-SERGs, Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses were performed using the ClusterProfiler package (v 4.7.1.3) [21], applying an adj.P threshold of < 0.05. GO analysis covered three major categories: Cellular Components (CC), Molecular Functions (MF), and Biological Processes (BP), offering a comprehensive view of the roles and interactions of DE-SERGs.


### Selection of potential biomarkers using machine learning algorithms
  A protein–protein interaction (PPI) network for DE-SERGs was established using the Search Tool for the Retrieval of Interacting Genes/Proteins (STRING) database (http://string-db.org) with an interaction score threshold of > 0.4. The interaction score greater than 0.4 indicated that the interaction between the proteins was relatively strong and held biological significance. The network was visualized using Cytoscape software (v 3.7.2) [22].

  The cytoHubba plugin was employed for further analysis, utilizing the Maximal Clique Centrality (MCC) algorithm to refine the selection of DE-SERGs, resulting in the identification of the top 20 hub genes for further investigation. MCC not only considered the direct connections of nodes, but also analyzed larger clique structures, uncovering more complex and potential multi-layered relationships, which helped in gaining a more comprehensive understanding of the intricate interactions between nodes in the network. These hub genes underwent analysis using two distinct machine learning methods. Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression analysis applies L1 regularization penalty, shrinking some regression coefficients to zero, thereby automatically selecting important features. It is especially suitable for handling high-dimensional data and effectively addresses noise and redundant features. LASSO regression analysis was performed with the glmnet package (v 4.1.4) [23], using tenfold cross-validation to identify the optimal model, minimizing error and selecting genes when the lambda reached its lowest value. Simultaneously, a Random Forest (RF) model was developed using the randomForest package (v 4.7-1.1) [24], also with tenfold cross-validation, to assess the importance of each gene. Random Forest was able to handle classification and regression problems and effectively address class imbalance through adaptive methods, such as aggregating multiple sample subsets, thereby reducing its impact. Genes with the highest accuracy levels from both models were selected. The intersecting genes from these two machine learning approaches were identified as potential biomarkers related to SE in CD, providing valuable insights into the molecular markers associated with the disease’s pathology.


### Identification and evaluation of the diagnostic value of biomarkers
  To further evaluate the diagnostic potential of the identified biomarkers, both training and validation datasets were analyzed using the pROC package (v 1.18.0) [25] to construct receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and calculate the area under the curve (AUC) values. An AUC greater than 0.7 typically indicates strong discriminative ability. Genes surpassing this threshold were selected for further expression analysis. Specifically, the expression levels of these genes were compared between CD and control samples in both datasets. Genes exhibiting consistent expression trends and significant differences (P < 0.05) across both datasets were designated as potential biomarkers for CD.


### Construction of nomograms
  Subsequently, two nomograms were constructed using the rms package [26], based on the identified biomarkers in both the training and validation sets to enhance their clinical utility in diagnosing CD. In these nomograms, the “Points” column represents the score for each gene, while the “Total Points” column displays the cumulative score. The “points” for each gene were derived by drawing a line from each gene to the points scale on the nomogram. The overall risk of CD was determined by summing the “points” and locating the total on the “Total Points” scale. To assess the nomograms’ performance, ROC curves were plotted, and decision curve analysis (DCA) was performed to evaluate both their ability to distinguish between samples and their clinical utility.


### Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) of biomarker
  To explore signaling pathways associated with the identified biomarkers, the “c2.all.v7.2.symbols.gmt” file was downloaded from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB) (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/msigdb) as the background gene set for the analysis. Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was conducted using the clusterProfiler package. CD samples in the training set were divided into high- and low-expression groups based on the median expression of the biomarkers, and differential analysis was performed. Samples were ranked by Log2FC for this analysis. The False Discovery Rate (FDR) method was used to adjust for multiple testing, and pathways with adj.P < 0.05 were considered significantly enriched. The top 5 pathways for each biomarker were selected based on their significance ranking and visualized using the enrichplot package (v 1.18.3) [27].


### Immune infiltration analysis
  Immune infiltration assays were conducted to assess differences in the immune microenvironment between patients with CD and healthy controls. In the training set, the CIBERSORT algorithm (v 1.03) [28] was used to estimate the proportions of 22 different immune cell types [29] in each sample. Results were filtered with a sample confidence threshold of P < 0.05. Comparative analyses were then performed to examine the distribution of these immune cells between CD and control samples (P < 0.05), visualized using a box plot generated with the ggplot2 package. Additionally, Spearman correlation analyses were carried out using the corrplot package (v 0.92) [27] to identify potential associations between immune cells that exhibited significant differences and correlations between differential immune cells and biomarkers, with a cutoff of |cor|> 0.3 and P < 0.05.


### Construction of regulatory network and drug prediction analysis
  To elucidate the potential molecular mechanisms underlying the biomarkers, miRNAs targeting the biomarkers were retrieved from the miRNet database (https://www.mirnet.ca), and a miRNA-mRNA regulatory network was constructed. Furthermore, the Drug SIGnatures database (DSigDB) (https://dsigdb.tanlab.org/DSigDBv1.0/) was used to predict potential drugs targeting the biomarkers, and a drug-biomarker network was created. Both networks were visualized using Cytoscape software.


### Identification of CD subtypes and functional analysis
  CD is a highly heterogeneous disease. In this study, distinct subtypes of CD were identified to better understand its complexity and explore the biological foundations of these subtypes, supporting future personalized medical strategies and more precise diagnostic approaches. Specifically, the ConsensusClusterPlus package (v 1.62.0) [30] was used for unsupervised hierarchical clustering analysis of all CD samples in the training set, with biomarkers as input. The optimal number of subtypes was determined, and samples were assigned to different subtypes based on this number. Based on this analysis, CD samples were classified into subtypes, and a box plot was generated to illustrate the expression differences of biomarkers among the subtypes (P < 0.05). To assess the biological pathways involved in each subtype, this study downloaded “c2.cp.kegg.v2023.1.Hs.symbols.gmt” from MSigDB as the background gene set and employed the single-sample GSEA (ssGSEA) algorithm from the GSVA package (v 1.42.0) [31] to calculate gene set scores for each sample in the training set. Pathway scores between subtypes were compared, and the top 20 differentially regulated pathways were visualized using a heatmap (P < 0.05). Additionally, differential expression analysis was performed among the different subtypes in the training set using the limma package, identifying DEGs with |log2FC|≥ 0.5 and P < 0.05. GO and KEGG enrichment analyses were then conducted on the DEGs among the subtypes using the clusterProfiler package to further investigate their biological functions and signaling pathways (P < 0.05).


### Differential immune cell infiltration analysis and correlation in CD subtypes
  Further analysis was performed to investigate the abundance of immune cell infiltration among different CD subtypes. The CIBERSORT algorithm was employed to evaluate the infiltration of 22 immune cell types in the training set samples, excluding those with P > 0.05. Comparisons were made to assess differences in immune cell infiltration abundance between the subtypes (P < 0.05). Subsequently, Spearman correlation analyses were conducted using the corrplot package to evaluate the relationships between differentially infiltrated immune cells and between these immune cells and biomarkers, with a threshold of |cor|> 0.3 and P < 0.05.


### Analysis of immunohistochemical results
  To validate the differential expression of key genes in colonic tissues from patients with CD and healthy controls, IHC staining was performed. Archived colon tissue samples were obtained from ten patients with CD who had undergone colonoscopy and ten healthy subjects at Sir Run Run Hospital, Nanjing Medical University (Nanjing, China). Ethical approval was obtained for the study (2023-SR-S078), and informed consent was provided by all participants. Primary antibodies, including PAQR5 (PH29141, Abmart), IFITM3 (T57091, Abmart), PSMB8 (T58222, Abmart), and IRF1 (T55507, Abmart), were incubated overnight at 4 °C. The sections were washed three times with PBS buffer, and secondary antibodies were applied for 20 min at room temperature. Sections were stained with 3,3′-diaminobenzidine and hematoxylin. For each Marker, 40× magnification fields were randomly selected, and ImageJ software was used to calculate the mean optical density (MOD) values.


### Statistical analysis
  Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol (Invitrogen, USA), and cDNA was synthesized from the RNA using the Prime Script RT Reagent Kit (TAKARA, Japan). The cDNA was then quantified using SYBR Green qPCR Master Mix (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Primer sequences used in the PCR assays are listed in Table 1. ### Statistical analysis
  All statistical analyses were performed in R software (v 4.2.3). The differences between groups were assessed using the non-parametric Wilcoxon test, with P < 0.05 considered statistically significant.


## Results

### Identification of 53 DE-SERGs and their associated biological functions

The intersection of these DEGs with 735 SERGs yielded 53 DE-SERGs (Fig. 1C). Enrichment analysis showed 130 GO terms, including 118 BPs, three CCs, and nine MFs, which were associated with immune response regulation, cell differentiation, molecular binding activities, and cellular structures (Fig. 1D). Two KEGG pathways were identified: “Type II diabetes mellitus” and “Carbon metabolism,” which are involved in metabolic processes related to glucose regulation and cellular energy pathways (Fig. 1E). ### Recognition of PAQR5, IFITM3, PSMB8, MYOF, and IRF1 as potential biomarkers
  In order to understand the interactions between the candidate genes, a PPI network was constructed with 53 nodes and 179 edges, showing strong interactions among CASP1, JAK2, and IRF1 (Fig. 2A). The MCC algorithm identified the top 20 hub genes for further analysis, including PAQR5, IFITM3, PSMB8, MYOF, IRF1, AHNAK, ANXA1, ANXA2, BTN3A1, BTN3A3, CAPN3, CASP1, CAV3, CXCL1, CYB5D2, FCGR3A, HIF1A, IDO1, IFITM1, and PAQR7 (Fig. 2B).Fig. 2Identification of potential biomarkers. A Protein–protein interaction (PPI) network of 53 DE-SERGs. B Top 20 genes identified by the MCC algorithm (represented by gray triangles). F Venn diagram showing the overlap between DE-SERGs and intersected DE-SERGs identified by LASSO analysis and RF algorithm

  The Hub genes were then integrated into the LASSO and RF algorithms. In the LASSO analysis, with lambda min set to 0.102, nine genes were identified: PAQR5, IFITM3, PSMB8, MYOF, SPRED2, IRF1, IDH2, ID3, and NR4A2 (Fig. 2C, D). Similarly, the RF algorithm identified nine genes: ST5, PAQR5, IFITM3, PSMB8, MYOF, IRF1, FFAR2, PDE9A, and SLC11A1 (Fig. 2E). By intersecting these two sets, five potential biomarkers were identified: PAQR5, IFITM3, PSMB8, MYOF, and IRF1 (Fig. 2F).


### Biomarkers PAQR5, IFITM3, PSMB8, and IRF1: potent diagnostic indicators for CD
  ROC analysis was performed to evaluate the diagnostic performance of these five potential biomarkers. In the training set, the AUC values for PAQR5, IFITM3, PSMB8, MYOF, and IRF1 were 0.988, 0.973, 0.959, 0.973, and 0.940, respectively (Fig. 3A). In the validation set, their AUC values were 0.983, 0.965, 0.955, 0.638, and 0.966, respectively (Fig. 3B). These AUC values suggested that, in the training set, the tests for PAQR5, IFITM3, PSMB8, MYOF, and IRF1 exhibited high diagnostic performance with sensitivity and specificity close to optimal, particularly for PAQR5, IFITM3, and PSMB8. However, MYOF showed a relatively lower AUC value in the validation set, indicating a decreased ability to accurately distinguish between diseased and healthy individuals. Consequently, PAQR5, IFITM3, PSMB8, and IRF1 (with AUC > 0.7) were selected for subsequent expression analysis. These genes exhibited consistent and significantly different expression patterns in both the training and validation sets (Fig. 3C, D). Except for PAQR5, the other three genes showed significantly higher expression in CD samples (P < 0.05). Thus, PAQR5, IFITM3, PSMB8, and IRF1 were designated as biomarkers for this study.Fig. 3Diagnostic performance of biomarkers confirmed by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis. ### Enhanced diagnostic value of biomarkers for CD using nomograms
  Nomograms were constructed based on these identified biomarkers to further enhance their diagnostic value for CD in both the training and validation sets (Fig. 4A, B). ROC curve analysis revealed that the AUCs for the nomogram were 0.991 for the training set and 0.992 for the validation set (Fig. 4C, D), significantly outperforming the AUCs of any individual biomarker in both datasets. This suggests that the nomogram offers a robust ability to differentiate between CD and normal samples. Additionally, DCA indicated that both nomograms had satisfactory clinical utility, supporting their potential for future clinical application and validation (Fig. 4E, F).   GSEA of the biomarkers revealed several key signaling pathways associated with each biomarker. ### Assessing differences in the immune microenvironment between individuals with CD and normal controls
  Immune cell infiltration is an important feature in characterizing the disease defense mechanism, and immune infiltration can provide a reference for studying the degree of immune cell infiltration in specific diseases. Immune cell infiltration was analyzed using the CIBERSORT algorithm, followed by the Wilcoxon rank-sum test to compare immune cell differences between the CD and control groups. Figure 6A illustrates the infiltration abundance of 22 immune cell types in the training set samples. A box plot further emphasized significant differences in 10 immune cell types between CD and control samples (P < 0.05) (Fig. 6B). Notably, CD samples exhibited higher infiltration abundances of M0 macrophages, M1 macrophages, activated myeloid dendritic cells, neutrophils, resting NK cells, and resting memory CD4 + T cells (P < 0.05). Conversely, naive B cells, M2 macrophages, CD8 + T cells, and regulatory T cells (Tregs) were less abundant in CD samples (P < 0.05). Correlation analysis among these immune cells revealed varying degrees of association (Fig. 6C). Specifically, M1 macrophages showed the strongest negative correlation with CD8 + T cells (cor = − 0.68, P < 0.05) and the strongest positive correlation with neutrophils (cor = 0.70, P < 0.05). Further analysis of correlations between immune cells and biomarkers revealed significant relationships (Fig. 6D). IRF1 showed the strongest positive correlation with M1 macrophages (cor = 0.81, P < 0.05), while IFITM3 exhibited a strong negative correlation with CD8 + T cells (cor = − 0.62, P < 0.05). ### Identification of potential miRNAs regulating the biomarkers and potential drug targets for biomarkers
  A miRNA-Hub gene network was constructed to deeply reveal the regulatory relationships between miRNA and key genes, aiding in the comprehensive understanding of gene functions and their roles in biological phenomena. Additionally, 229 potential miRNAs regulating the biomarkers using miRNet were identified. A miRNA-mRNA regulatory network was constructed, consisting of 233 nodes and 270 edges (Fig. 6E). Notably, miRNAs such as hsa-miR-21-3p, hsa-miR-26b-5p, hsa-miR-106b-5p, hsa-miR-16-5p, and hsa-miR-129-2-3p were predicted to regulate all four biomarkers simultaneously. Furthermore, the Drug SIGnatures database (DsigDB) was used to predict potential drugs targeting these biomarkers, resulting in a drug-biomarker network comprising 120 nodes and 134 edges (Fig. 6F). This network identified drugs such as Tetradioxin, Puromycin, Phenobarbital, Cyclosporin, resveratrol, quercetin, and Calcitriol, which could potentially target the identified biomarkers in CD. However, the specific mechanisms of action still require further investigation.


### Identification of subtypes and pathway alterations in CD
  CD is characterized by heterogeneity in clinical presentation, affected regions, disease mechanisms, and treatment responses. To address this variability, biomarkers were identified, and unsupervised hierarchical clustering analysis was employed to classify CD samples in the training set into two distinct subtypes (cluster 1 and cluster 2) (Fig. 7A). A box plot demonstrated that IFITM3, IRF1, and PSMB8 exhibited significantly higher expression levels in cluster 2 (P < 0.05), while PAQR5 showed the opposite trend (P < 0.05) (Fig. 7B). ### Verification of biomarkers expression
  The expression differences of the four biomarkers were consistent in both the training and validation sets, with IFITM3, PSMB8, and IRF1 showing significantly higher expression levels in CD samples compared to control samples (P < 0.05), while PAQR5 exhibited significantly lower expression levels (P < 0.05) (Fig. 3C, D). These four biomarkers were further experimentally validated. This prompted further validation of the clinical expression levels of these four genes in patients with CD using IHC techniques and RT-qPCR. The RT-qPCR results confirmed the expression patterns of these genes, consistent with the data from the validated dataset in the database (Fig. 9A). These results suggest that the four genes are significantly associated with CD and may serve as potential diagnostic biomarkers. ## Discussion
  CD is a chronic inflammatory condition that can affect the entire gastrointestinal tract [1]. Despite extensive research, the exact etiology of CD remains unresolved, and therapeutic options for many patients are still limited [7]. SEs are composite regulatory elements with enhancer-like properties, playing a significant role in gene transcription enhancement [9]. However, their specific role in CD pathogenesis remains unclear. In this study, bioinformatics analysis was performed to explore the diagnostic potential and molecular mechanisms of SERGs as potential biomarkers for CD.

  A PPI network was constructed for the identified DE-SERGs, and LASSO regression and RF analyses selected PAQR5, IFITM3, PSMB8, and IRF1 as biomarkers. These biomarkers exhibited superior and stable diagnostic performance compared to previous studies [32, 33]. In both public databases, expression levels of IFITM3, PSMB8, and IRF1 were significantly elevated in CD samples, whereas PAQR5 was downregulated. IHC and RT-qPCR results confirmed these trends, substantiating the reliability of our analyses.

  PAQR5, a member of the class II progestin and adipoQ receptor family, is significantly downregulated in CD tissues, which aligns with the observed reduction in progesterone levels in patients with CD [34]. As progesterone is known to enhance epithelial barrier function and suppress inflammation, the diminished expression of PAQR5 may contribute to CD pathogenesis via non-classical progesterone signaling pathways. Recent studies have linked PAQR5 to cell adhesion and epithelial integrity, suggesting that its downregulation may impair intestinal barrier function and tissue stability, thereby exacerbating intestinal inflammation. These findings indicate that alterations in PAQR5 expression may be involved in the pathophysiology of CD, highlighting its potential role in maintaining intestinal epithelial structure and function. Further investigation into the precise mechanisms of PAQR5 in CD may provide novel therapeutic targets for disease management.

  IFITM3, which is upregulated during the interferon response and associated with poor prognosis in colon cancer [35], was also found elevated in CD [36], highlighting its potential as a therapeutic target. Recent research has shown that microglia in the aged brain exhibit increased expression and activation of IFITM3 following stroke, with IFITM3 being significantly induced alongside other inflammatory marker genes, suggesting its critical role in neuroinflammation. Given that CD involves extensive immune and inflammatory responses, IFITM3 may play a significant role in the pathogenesis of CD. Its regulatory effects on immune responses could influence disease progression and inflammatory activity, indicating a potential mechanistic link and therapeutic relevance in CD.

  PSMB8 is one of the 17 essential components required for the assembly of the 20S proteasome complex. Studies have demonstrated that PSMB8 deficiency in mice leads to increased infiltration of inflammatory cells, particularly pro-inflammatory Ly6Ch1 macrophages. These macrophages, after phagocytosing myoblast debris, exhibit elevated expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines and reduced levels of anti-inflammatory cytokines, which are closely associated with the activation of the NF-κB signaling pathway [37]. Furthermore, knockdown of PSMB8 has been shown to inhibit the expression of interleukin (IL)−1β, IL-6, and TNF-α, thereby alleviating the inflammatory response. In the present study, PSMB8 was found to be upregulated in the intestinal tissues of patients with CD, indicating its potential involvement in the immune and inflammatory processes of the disease. Considering the key role of PSMB8 in regulating inflammation and immune responses, silencing PSMB8 may represent a promising strategy to modulate the inflammatory cascade in CD. Further research into the precise mechanisms of PSMB8 in CD pathogenesis may help uncover its role in disease progression and support the development of more effective targeted therapeutic strategies.

  IRF1, a transcription factor regulating various disease-related genes [38], is significantly elevated in CD [39], indicating its potential role in disease pathogenesis. In addition to its role in immune regulation, IRF1 is involved in the modulation of cell death and inflammatory responses. Recent studies have shown that IRF1 promotes the activation of the PANoptosome by enhancing the function of molecules such as ZBP1, thereby inducing PANoptosis, a form of inflammatory cell death. As a central component of the PANoptosome, IRF1 facilitates cell death by regulating interactions among inflammasomes, caspase-8, and RIPK3. This mechanism may contribute significantly to the immune-inflammatory response observed in CD. Targeting molecular pathways regulated by IRF1 may offer novel therapeutic strategies for CD and other diseases characterized by aberrant inflammatory cell death. These biomarkers play critical roles in CD and hold promise as therapeutic targets, although further studies are required to explore their full implications for CD therapy.

  Incorporating PAQR5, IFITM3, PSMB8, and IRF1 into the nomogram resulted in high diagnostic accuracy, with AUC values of 0.991 and 0.992 for the training and validation sets, respectively. These values were significantly higher than those derived from models based on individual genes, confirming the robustness and reliability of the nomogram. DCA further supported its superior clinical applicability. Notably, these genes may play a critical role in monitoring the progression of CD. As potential early biomarkers, they could facilitate disease surveillance and enable timely therapeutic adjustments, thereby contributing to more precise and personalized clinical management strategies.

  GSEA revealed that the identified biomarkers are involved in several critical biological pathways in CD, including “Cytokine-Cytokine Receptor Interaction,” “Proteasome,” and “Cell Cycle” signaling. Cytokine-cytokine receptor interactions are pivotal in CD pathogenesis, driving inflammation and immune cell recruitment, which disrupts gut barrier integrity [40]. The proteasome, involved in antigen processing and inflammation, promotes NF-κB activation and pro-inflammatory cytokine production [41], with increased activity observed in CD. Dysregulation of cell cycle control in CD leads to abnormal cell proliferation, with lamina propria T cells demonstrating increased entry into the S and G2/M phases, as well as reduced expression of the cell cycle inhibitor p21 in CD-related carcinomas [42]. The enrichment of these pathways suggests that they may play central roles in the inflammatory responses, immune dysregulation, and cellular proliferation associated with CD. The identified biomarkers may contribute to the initiation and progression of CD by modulating these critical pathways, thereby serving as key drivers of disease pathogenesis.

  Immune infiltration analysis further revealed significant differences in immune cell populations between CD and healthy controls. CD samples exhibited high infiltration levels of six immune cell types: M0 macrophages, M1 macrophages, activated myeloid dendritic cells, neutrophils, resting NK cells, and resting memory CD4 + T cells. Macrophages can polarize into M1 and M2 phenotypes, with M1 macrophages being associated with the secretion of various proinflammatory cytokines and driving the inflammatory response. In contrast, M2 macrophages and M0 macrophages (the non-polarized state) predominate in healthy individuals [43]. The polarized M1 phenotype has been linked to the pathogenesis of IBD [44]. Dendritic cells and neutrophils play central roles in inflammation. Dendritic cells present antigens via MHC molecules to activate T cells, triggering acute inflammation [45], while neutrophils, as the first responders in inflammation, exacerbate tissue damage through excessive formation of neutrophil extracellular traps and heightened activity [46]. Overactive neutrophils recruit monocytes, T cells, and additional neutrophils, promoting the onset of IBD [47]. Like several of the aforementioned cells, NK cells are innate immune cells involved in inflammatory and immune response processes. NK cells in IBD are more readily activated, with recent studies linking long-term memory responses of NK cells to the production of resting NK cells [48]. CD tissues also contain specific subpopulations of memory CD4 + T cells. Resting memory CD4 + T cells, which are derived from CD4 + T cells following an immune response, may increase as a result of continued immune activation or repeated exposure to chronic antigens. The elevated infiltration of these immune cells indicates a significant increase in the inflammatory response in CD, which is likely closely related to its tissue damage and pathophysiological processes.

  Four immune cell types were found to be infiltrated at low levels in CD samples: naive B cells, M2 macrophages, CD8 + T cells, and Tregs. Naive B cells in gut-associated lymphoid tissues generate IgA antibody-secreting plasma cells involved in immune responses. However, the exact role of B lymphocytes in CD pathogenesis remains unclear. M2 macrophages, in contrast to the pro-inflammatory M1 macrophages, exhibit an anti-inflammatory phenotype that helps mitigate inflammation and promotes tissue repair [49]. CD8 + T cells and Tregs, key components in our diagnostic model, have been shown to be reduced in patients with CD according to prior research [50], which aligns with our findings. However, there remains some controversy regarding the precise alterations of these cell types in CD tissues. In conclusion, the reduced infiltration of these cell types may be linked to the heightened inflammatory state and defective immunoregulation observed in CD.

  CD is highly heterogeneous, exhibiting diverse clinical manifestations, affected sites, and responses to treatment [5, 51]. Identifying subtypes of CD through biomarkers can facilitate personalized treatment strategies. In our analysis, cluster 2 exhibited high expression of IFITM3, IRF1, and PSMB8, coupled with low PAQR5 expression. GSVA revealed overall pathway inhibition in this cluster, with the exception of “KRAS Signaling Down” in cluster 1. Previous studies have linked KRAS upregulation to IBD-related lesions and metastatic CRC [52], while upregulation of the Wnt/β-catenin pathway is associated with IBD-related psychiatric symptoms and epithelial dynamics [53]. Immune microenvironment analysis indicated that cluster 1 displayed immune cell infiltration close to normal levels, with increased activated NK and CD8 + T cells, suggesting an active immune response. In contrast, cluster 2 exhibited heightened pro-inflammatory macrophages and neutrophils, indicating a more inflammatory state, with elevated resting NK cells potentially reflecting immune modulation. These differences underscore the need for personalized diagnostic and therapeutic strategies in CD and offer valuable insights for future drug development.

  Building upon these subtype-specific immune signatures, we further explored the associations between selected biomarkers and immune cell populations. Four biomarkers demonstrated correlations with immune cells to varying extents. Notably, IRF1 exhibited a significant positive correlation with M1 macrophages, while IFITM3 showed a notable negative correlation with CD8 + T cells. Previous studies have linked IRF1 to the polarization of M1 macrophages. For example, Zhu et al. highlighted the role of the STAT1/IRF1 pathway in promoting M1 macrophage polarization [54]. Guo et al. revealed that miR-130b-3p directly inhibits IRF1, influencing this polarization [55]. The relationship between IFITM3 and CD8+ T cells in CD remains insufficiently explored. Unlike findings in respiratory infections, where IFITM3 expression has been shown to positively correlate with CD8⁺ T cell accumulation [56], our study demonstrates a negative correlation between IFITM3 and CD8⁺ T cells in CD. This discrepancy may arise from fundamental differences in the underlying immune mechanisms of these diseases. In respiratory infections, IFITM3 is typically involved in enhancing antiviral immunity by promoting the activation and recruitment of CD8⁺ T cells. In contrast, within the chronic inflammatory environment of CD, IFITM3 may contribute to the modulation of immune responses by suppressing CD8⁺ T cell function or recruitment. Moreover, the immune landscape in CD is likely to be more complex, involving multifaceted interactions among various immune cell types and signaling pathways, in which IFITM3 may exert distinct, context-dependent roles. The observed negative correlation may thus reflect a unique pattern of immune dysregulation characteristic of CD. Further studies are warranted to validate this hypothesis and to elucidate the precise role of IFITM3 in the immunopathogenesis of CD. These findings contribute to a deeper understanding of immune regulation in CD and offer potential pathways for the development of targeted therapeutic strategies.

  To explore potential therapeutic avenues targeting these immune-related biomarkers, we constructed a gene–drug interaction network and identified multiple candidate compounds. Among them, quercetin emerged as a promising agent with sustained anti-inflammatory activity across various cell types in both animal and human models. It is known to stabilize mast cells, protect the gastrointestinal tract, and exert modulatory effects on immune and inflammatory responses [57]. These properties suggest that quercetin may mitigate intestinal inflammation and improve immune function in CD patients, thereby holding therapeutic potential.

  Resveratrol also demonstrated anti-inflammatory activity, primarily by suppressing the production of ROS and nitric oxide (NO). The accumulation of ROS promotes oxidative stress, a key driver of chronic inflammation and tumorigenesis [58]. In animal models of stress-induced depression, resveratrol reduced inflammation via enhancement of hepatic antioxidant pathways. Moreover, it downregulated iNOS mRNA and protein expression in lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-stimulated intestinal epithelial cells in a dose-dependent manner, thereby reducing NO levels. Based on these findings, we propose that resveratrol may exert beneficial effects in CD by suppressing iNOS and IL-6 expression, dampening intestinal inflammation, and improving clinical outcomes. However, further mechanistic studies are needed to validate these hypotheses.

  This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, the datasets used in this analysis may have inherent biases, as they were primarily derived from specific geographic regions or populations. Such limitations could affect the generalizability of our findings, as they may not fully capture the genetic variability and clinical heterogeneity present in broader patient populations. Future studies should incorporate multicenter and large-scale data collection to minimize sampling bias, enhance the diversity and representativeness of the data, and thereby improve the robustness and external validity of predictive models. Second, the number of clinical samples used for validation was relatively small. A limited sample size may reduce the statistical power and reliability of the results. Therefore, expanding the clinical cohort and conducting multicenter validation studies will be crucial to better assess the generalizability and clinical applicability of the proposed biomarkers and predictive models. Third, bioinformatic predictions based on transcriptomic data and computational algorithms inherently have certain limitations. While these approaches provide valuable insights into potential biomarkers and molecular pathways, they may not fully capture the multifactorial nature of Crohn’s disease. The pathogenesis of CD is influenced not only by genetic factors but also by environmental exposures, lifestyle, and host–microbiome interactions, which are not adequately represented in current gene expression datasets. As such, the integration of bioinformatics findings with clinical evidence and other diagnostic modalities is essential to ensure a more accurate and comprehensive understanding of disease mechanisms.

  Despite the encouraging findings from our initial analyses, further validation in larger, multicenter clinical studies is necessary. Additionally, given that immune cell infiltration patterns may vary significantly across different CD subtypes and clinical phenotypes, further investigation is required to explore these differences in depth. Future research will aim to delineate the specific roles and regulatory mechanisms of key biomarkers in CD pathogenesis, ultimately providing a stronger foundation for the development of personalized treatment strategies and novel therapeutic interventions.


## Conclusion
  Based on bioinformatics analysis, PAQR5, IFITM3, PSMB8, and IRF1 have been identified as potential diagnostic biomarkers for CD. These findings elucidate the intricate immunoregulatory network underlying CD and offer new perspectives for personalized and targeted therapies. Although preliminary results emphasize the diagnostic potential of these markers, further validation in larger, multicenter studies is essential.
