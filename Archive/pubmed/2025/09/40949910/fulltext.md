# Identification of potential necroinflammation-associated necroptosis-related biomarkers in necrotizing enterocolitis based on bioinformatics analysis and machine learning


## Background
  Necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC) stands as one of the most lethal conditions afflicting premature infants. There is a close relationship between necroptosis, necroinflammation, and the potential mechanisms of NEC. The purpose of this study was to investigate the mechanism of necroinflammation-associated necroptosis-related genes (NiNRGs) in NEC, identify NiNRGs-related diagnostic markers for NEC, and construct a diagnostic model for NEC through bioinformatics analysis and machine learning.


## Methods
  Differentially expressed NiNRGs (DE-NiNRGs) were identified through differential expression and correlation analysis, followed by gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) and protein-protein interaction (PPI) network establishment. Three machine learning methods were used to find potential diagnostic biomarkers, evaluated through a receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve and a nomogram model. Immune infiltration scores for 28 immune cell types in NEC were calculated, along with correlation coefficients for diagnostic marker genes. Various databases predicted interactions between these genes, small molecule drugs, microRNAs, and transcription factors. A single-gene GSEA (sgGSEA) identified significantly enriched signaling pathways associated with diagnostic marker genes in NEC.


## Results
  A total of 29 DE-NiNRGs were identified, linked to 17 pathways, including tumor necrosis factor (TNF), interleukin (IL)-17, and cytosolic DNA-sensing pathways. The PPI network showed close interactions among DE-NiNRGs. Three biomarkers, DAPK1, PARP1, and BIRC3, were selected using machine learning, showing area under the curve (AUC) values ≥0.8 in ROC analysis. The nomogram indicated significant diagnostic score differences between NEC and healthy controls. Type 2 T helper (Th2) cell infiltration differed significantly between NEC and controls, with DAPK1 and BIRC3 expression correlating with immune cells. Transcription factors, microRNAs, and small molecule drugs regulating these markers were identified, and sgGSEA revealed 198, 240, and 217 pathways for DAPK1, PARP1, and BIRC3, respectively.


## Conclusions
  Necroinflammation-induced necroptosis significantly contributes to the progression of NEC. DAPK1, PARP1, and BIRC3 demonstrate substantial diagnostic potential for the condition. Employing bioinformatics to explore potential mechanisms aids in elucidating the genetic pathogenesis of NEC and offers valuable insights for future investigations.


## Introduction
  Necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC) is one of the most fatal diseases in premature infants (1). Data showed that in the United States, from 2013 to 2018, NEC was diagnosed in 8.9% of extremely preterm infants, and 3.9% underwent surgery (2). Meta-analysis found that gestational diabetes, premature rupture of membranes, low birth weight, small for gestational age, septicemia, blood transfusion, congenital heart disease, respiratory distress syndrome, premature birth, and pneumonia might increase the risk of NEC in neonates (3,4). Despite significant advances in understanding NEC pathogenesis over the past 50 years, the underlying pathophysiological processes remain unclear, limiting clinical and research developments (5,6).

  Damage to the intestinal epithelial barrier (IEB) plays a critical role in the onset of intestinal inflammation and the progression of NEC (7). The functional IEB, formed by tightly arranged intestinal epithelial cells (IECs), creates a monolayer that separates the organism from the extra-intestinal environment (8). A fundamental question in NEC research is how IECs undergo pathological death, which is essential for understanding the disease’s pathogenesis (7). IECs associated with NEC primarily exhibit several known death modes, including apoptosis, necroptosis, pyroptosis, ferroptosis, and abnormal autophagy (9). Among these, necroptosis is the predominant mode of cell death in NEC.

  In the development of NEC, necroptosis is considered an important pathological process. Studies have shown that in NEC patients and animal models, the expression of necroptosis-related proteins such as receptor-interacting protein kinase (RIPK)1, RIPK3, and mixed lineage kinase domain-like protein (MLKL) is significantly upregulated, which is closely related to the activation of Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4) (10,11). Additionally, some short-chain fatty acids (such as sodium formate and butyrate) have also been found to induce intestinal damage similar to NEC in young mice, and this damage is primarily achieved through the activation of the necroptosis pathway (12,13).

  Necroptosis and necroinflammation are two important concepts that have received widespread attention in recent years in the study of cell death and inflammation. Necroptosis is a form of programmed cell death primarily achieved through the activation of RIPK1, RIPK3, and MLKL (14). This process leads to cell membrane rupture, releasing intracellular components and triggering local and systemic inflammatory responses (15). Necroinflammation, on the other hand, refers to the immune response triggered by damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) released after cell death, which is often accompanied by tissue damage (16). Although the mechanisms of the two are different, there is a complex interaction between them. Necroptosis can directly cause tissue damage and induce inflammation; on the other hand, a persistent inflammatory state may promote more cells to undergo necroptosis, thus forming a vicious cycle.

  Necroptosis and necroinflammation are significantly associated with different manifestations of ischemic diseases, including kidney injury, stroke, and myocardial infarction (17-19). NEC is a typical ischemic disease characterized by ischemic necrosis of the intestine, suggesting a close relationship between necroptosis, necroinflammation, and the underlying mechanisms of NEC. However, there is a lack of comprehensive descriptions of necroptosis-related genes (NRGs), particularly necroinflammation-associated NRGs (NiNRGs), in terms of their expression at NEC.

  The rapid development of sequencing technologies and the integration of machine learning algorithms in bioinformatics have enhanced the identification of disease biomarkers (20,21), enabling early prediction, precise diagnosis, targeted intervention, and personalized treatment of diseases. Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the expression landscape of NiNRGs in the context of NEC, identify biomarkers, and construct diagnostic models using machine learning algorithms based on the gene expression differences between NEC and control groups. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tp.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tp-2025-247/rc).


## Methods

### Data sources and preprocessing
  The GSE64801 dataset was downloaded from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/), which contains nine samples of NEC and five healthy control samples. In the original research article for GSE64801 (22), it was pointed out that the diagnosis was confirmed by pathologists and clinical staging of NEC was based on the criteria of Bell. Patients who had undergone bowel resection for stage III acute NEC constituted NEC cases, and patients who had undergone resection for diseases other than NEC, such as small intestinal perforation, small intestinal atresia, and bowel obstruction, made up the control group. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments.

  The expression data were standardized using log(x+1), while genes expressed as 0 in more than 50% of the samples were excluded. NRGs and necroinflammation-related genes (NiRGs) were obtained by searching for “necroptosis” or “necroinflammation” using specific criteria (category protein-coding and score >0.8) in the GeneCards database (https://www.genecards.org/), or from previous literature reports (17).


### Differential expression and correlation analysis
  The classic Bayesian and linear regression methods were utilized to perform differential expression analysis of NEC compared to healthy controls, based on gene expression data, using the R package limma (version 3.58.1; http://www.bioconductor.org/packages/2.9/bioc/html/limma.html). Significant differential expression genes (DEGs) were screened from the perspectives of significance and fold change (FC), with thresholds set at P value <0.05 and |log2FC| >1, to identify genes with significant differential expression between diseased samples and control samples.

  Expression data of NRGs and NiRGs from GSE64801 were extracted. The “cor.test” function in R software was used to perform “Spearman” correlation analysis on the expression matrix of NiRGs and NRGs from the extracted NEC samples, with thresholds set at correlation coefficient |R| >0.6 and P value <0.05, resulting in the identification of NiNRGs.

  The intersection of DEGs and NiNRGs was taken, and Venn analysis was conducted using the R package ggvenn (version 0.1.10; https://mirrors.pku.edu.cn/CRAN/web/packages/ggvenn/index.html) to obtain differentially expressed NiNRGs (DE-NiNRGs). The R package pheatmap (version 1.0.13; https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/pheatmap/index.html) was used to create a heatmap to display the expression of DE-NiNRGs.


### Functional enrichment analysis and protein-protein interaction (PPI) network construction of DE-NiNRGs
  Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) is generally used to assess the distribution trend of genes in a predefined gene set during analyses related to clinical phenotypes, thereby determining their contribution to the phenotype. Using the limma package, differential expression analysis of NEC vs. control was performed based on gene expression data, ranking all genes in descending order according to the size of log2FC. The R package clusterProfiler (version 4.10.1; https://bioconductor.org/packages/release/bioc/html/clusterProfiler.html) was then used in conjunction with the corresponding subset c2.cp.kegg.v2023.1.Hs.symbols.gmt from the MSigDB database (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/msigdb/index.jsp) as the background dataset to conduct Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis to identify activated or inhibited signaling pathways in NEC, with a significant enrichment threshold set at adjusted P<0.05, and the enrichment results were displayed.

  For the DE-NiNRGs, Gene Ontology (GO) analysis was performed using the R package clusterProfiler (version 4.10.1; https://bioconductor.org/packages/release/bioc/html/clusterProfiler.html) to determine the enrichment of biological process (BP), cell component (CC), and molecular function (MF) terms, as well as KEGG enrichment, with adjusted P<0.05 considered as significant enrichment results.

  Combining the STRING database (version 12.0; http://www.string-db.org/), a predictive analysis was conducted to determine whether there are interaction relationships between the proteins encoded by the DE-NiNRGs. The input gene set consisted of the DE-NiNRGs obtained above, with the species being Homo sapiens, requiring that the interacting protein nodes be included among the aforementioned genes, and only those interactions that were experimentally validated and had a total score higher than 0.4 were used to construct the PPI network.


### Diagnostic biomarker screening
  Three methods were used for feature selection of the aforementioned DE-NiNRGs: support vector machine-recursive feature elimination (SVM-RFE), random forest (RF), and least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (Lasso) regression. SVM-RFE feature selection was performed using the e1071 package (version 1.6-8; https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/e1071) with 10-fold cross-validation (https://github.com/johncolby/SVM-RFE). Next, the RF algorithm was applied using the randomForest package (version 4.7-1.1; https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/randomForest/) to set up 250 trees for the RF, selecting the top 20 genes based on importance scores. Finally, Lasso regression was conducted using the glmnet package (version 4.1-7; https://glmnet.stanford.edu), setting family = “binomial” and employing 10-fold cross-validation for selection.

  Venn analysis was performed on the genes selected by the three methods using the R package ggvenn to identify diagnostic marker genes.


### Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve and nomogram model of diagnostic marker genes
  The R package ggplot2 (version 3.5.0; https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/ggplot2/index.html) was employed to generate box plots illustrating the expression levels of the aforementioned diagnostic marker genes, while limma tests were performed to reveal intergroup disparities. The R package pROC (version 1.18.56; https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/pROC/index.html) was utilized to depict the ROC curve of the diagnostic marker genes in order to evaluate the precision of the diagnostic features. A nomogram was constructed based on the diagnostic marker genes, which was founded upon multifactorial regression analysis, amalgamating various predictive indicators, and subsequently employing a scaled line segment drawn on the same plane in accordance with a specific proportion to convey the interrelations among the variables in the predictive model. The nomogram function of the R package rms (version 6.8-0; https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/rms/) was utilized to illustrate the nomogram, assessing the predictive capability of model genes concerning the disease from the standpoint of the expression levels of each gene.


### Immune infiltration analysis
  The single-sample GSEA (ssGSEA) immune microenvironment analysis algorithm from the R package gene set variation analysis (GSVA; version 1.50.5; https://www.bioconductor.org/packages/release/bioc/html/GSVA.html) was used to calculate the immune infiltration scores of the samples. Based on the expression of immune cell gene sets in each sample, GSEA is used to calculate the enrichment scores of different immune cell types in each sample. The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was employed to compute the P values for the differences between immune infiltration scores and sample groups. Box plots were created based on the immune cell scores of samples in different groups, with a significance threshold set at P<0.05.

  The correlation coefficients and P values between diagnostic marker genes and each immune cell were calculated using the “Spearman” method, with a significance threshold set at P<0.05, and a correlation coefficient heatmap was drawn to illustrate the correlation between diagnostic marker genes and immune cells.


### Prediction and analysis of small molecule drugs
  The drug-gene interaction database (DGIdb; https://dgidb.org/) is a database for drug signatures and gene interaction relationships. The DGIdb database integrates existing literature reports on drug-gene interaction relationships, as well as drug-gene interaction data from over 30 databases including DrugBank, PharmGKB, Chembl, Drug Target Commons, and TTD. It provides two types of data: the first type is known drug-gene interaction relationships based on literature reports; the second type is potential drug-gene interaction relationships predicted based on functional and structural characteristics between drug or gene families. This database was used to predict the targeted relationships between diagnostic marker genes and drugs, and the Cytoscape software (version 3.9.1) was utilized to construct gene-drug interaction network diagrams.


### Construction of the upstream regulatory factors network
  Gene expression is regulated by transcription factors and microRNAs. The transcription factors regulating diagnostic marker genes were analyzed using the transcription factor database (hTFtarget; https://guolab.wchscu.cn/hTFtarget/#!/). At the same time, microRNAs regulating diagnostic marker genes were predicted using the miRWalk database (http://129.206.7.150/), with screening criteria set for a score greater than 0.8, combined with the location being 3'-untranslated region (UTR), and appearing in at least one of the databases: TargetScan, miRDB, or miRTarBase. A Venn diagram is to be utilized to illustrate the quantity of shared upstream regulatory factors associated with the diagnostic genes. The network was then constructed using Cytoscape software.


### Single-gene GSEA (sgGSEA) of diagnostic marker genes
  sgGSEA involved selecting a target gene and then calculating the correlation coefficient of this gene with other genes in a given expression matrix. This coefficient list could then be utilized for GSEA. Using the “Spearman” method, the correlation between the diagnostic marker gene and other genes was computed based on gene expression levels, and all genes were ranked in descending order according to the magnitude of the correlation. The R package clusterProfiler (version 4.10.1; https://bioconductor.org/packages/release/bioc/html/clusterProfiler.html) was then employed in conjunction with the corresponding subset c2.cp.kegg.v2023.1.Hs.symbols.gmt from the MSigDB database (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/msigdb/index.jsp) as the background dataset to conduct KEGG pathway enrichment analysis to identify activated or inhibited signaling pathways in the disease. The significance enrichment threshold was set to adjusted P<0.05, and the enrichment results were presented.


### Statistical analysis
  All the statistical analyses were conducted using R software (version 4.2.0). The Wilcoxon test was used to compare the diagnostic marker genes levels, predict score, and immune infiltration scores between the NEC and control groups. The Spearman test was used to assess the correlation between NiRGs and NRGs, diagnostic marker gene and each immune cell, as well as diagnostic marker gene and other genes. A P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.


## Results

### Differential expression and correlation analysis
  Differential expression analysis of gene expression data from the GSE64801 dataset was performed for NEC vs. control, using P value <0.05 and |log2FC| >1 as thresholds for screening DEGs, resulting in 2,190 DEGs, including 2,154 upregulated genes and 36 downregulated genes. The differential analysis volcano plot and heatmap were shown in Figure 1A,1B.

  A “Spearman” correlation analysis was performed on the expression matrix of NEC’s NiRGs and NRGs utilizing the corr.test function. Under the parameters of a correlation coefficient |R| >0.6 and a P value <0.05, 230 NiNRGs were identified. The intersection of the 2,190 DEGs and 230 NiNRGs was taken, and Venn analysis was performed using the R package ggvenn, resulting in 29 DE-NiNRGs (Figure 1C). The list of 29 DE-NiNRGs can be found in Table S1. The R package pheatmap was used to create and display a heatmap of the expression of DE-NiNRGs (Figure 1D).


### Functional enrichment analysis and PPI network construction
  Based on the differential analysis results of NEC vs. control sorted by log2FC values, KEGG pathway enrichment analysis was performed on the genes using clusterProfiler, filtering for adjusted P<0.05 enrichment results, resulting in 121 pathways. The top 5 upregulated pathways and two downregulated pathways with the highest enrichment scores were selected for visualization, as shown in Figure 2A.

  For 29 DE-NiNRGs, GO function and KEGG pathway analyses were conducted using the R package clusterProfiler. Under the condition of adjusted P<0.05, there were significant results for 353 GO_BP enrichments, 24 GO_CC enrichments, 42 GO_MF enrichment results (Figure 2B), and 17 significant KEGG enrichments, including tumor necrosis factor (TNF), interleukin (IL)-17, and cytosolic DNA-sensing pathway (Figure 2C).

  The functional connections between DE-NiNRGs were annotated using the Search Tool for the Retrieval of Interacting Genes (STRING) online database. The PPI network was constructed exclusively with interactions that had been experimentally validated and possessed a cumulative score exceeding 0.4. As illustrated in Figure 2D, the interactions among the majority of DE-NiNRGs were found to be closely associated.


### Diagnostic biomarker screening
  Feature selection was conducted on the 29 DE-NiNRGs genes previously mentioned, utilizing SVM-RFE, RF, and Lasso regression methods. Ten variables were selected by SVM-RFE (Figure 3A,3B); six variables were chosen through Lasso regression (Figure 3C,3D); and 20 variables were identified by RF (Figure 3E,3F). The intersection of these three methodologies resulted in three common genes (Figure 3G): death-associated protein kinase 1 (DAPK1), poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase-1 (PARP1), and baculoviral IAP repeat containing 3 (BIRC3), which were subsequently employed as diagnostic marker genes for further analysis.


### ROC analysis and nomogram model of diagnostic marker genes
  The R package ggplot2 was employed to generate box plots illustrating the expression levels of the aforementioned diagnostic marker genes. Intergroup differences were demonstrated by the limma test (Figure 4A). Simultaneously, the R package pROC was utilized to depict the ROC curves of the diagnostic marker genes to evaluate the accuracy of the diagnostic features (Figure 4B). The results of the limma test indicated that the three genes DAPK1, PARP1, and BIRC3 were significantly expressed in the disease, revealing intergroup differences, and the predictive performance of each gene was deemed satisfactory, with area under the curve (AUC) values being greater than or equal to 0.8.

  Based on three diagnostic marker genes, a nomogram model for NEC was constructed using the R package rms (Figure 4C). The expression levels of each patient’s diagnostic marker genes were projected onto the first row “Points” scale to obtain the Points corresponding to each indicator. By summing all the indicator Points, the “Total Points” were obtained and projected onto “Risk” to determine the likelihood of the patient developing the disease. At the same time, the predict scores for each specific patient can be calculated using the formula “DAPK1 × 15.323 + PARP1 × 113.915 + BIRC3 × 7.150 − 275.186”. To validate the reliability of the nomogram, ROC curve plotting was performed for evaluation (Figure 4D). Considering the limited sample size of the GSE64801 dataset, the nomogram demonstrated excellent predictive performance. Subsequently, the Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used to analyze the differences in predict scores between the NEC group and the control group (Figure 4E). The results indicated a significant difference in predict scores between the NEC group and the control group.


### ssGSEA immune infiltration analysis
  The immune infiltration scores of the samples were calculated based on the GSE64801 dataset using ssGSEA to investigate the infiltration levels of NEC and healthy control samples. The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was employed to determine the P values for the differences in immune infiltration scores between the sample groups, and box plots were generated. With a significance threshold established at P<0.05, intergroup differences were observed in type 2 T helper (Th2) cells (Figure 5A).

  The correlation coefficients and P values of the three diagnostic marker genes, DAPK1, PARP1, and BIRC3, with various immune cells were calculated using the “Spearman” method. These results were then displayed in a correlation coefficient heatmap (Figure 5B). Under the significance threshold of P<0.05, the DAPK1 gene was significantly correlated with central memory CD8 T cells (r=0.7), mast cells (r=0.733), natural killer T cells (r=0.783), neutrophils (r=0.733), and T follicular helper cells (r=0.667). The BIRC3 gene was significantly correlated with activated B cells (r=0.733), activated CD8 T cells (r=0.967), CD56bright natural killer cells (r=0.783), central memory CD4 T cells (r=0.783), effector memory CD8 T cells (r=0.967), eosinophils (r=0.75), immature B cells (r=0.933), mast cells (r=0.683), T follicular helper cells (r=0.667), and type 1 T helper (Th1) cells (r=0.683).


### Prediction and analysis of small molecule drugs and upstream regulatory factors
  The DGIdb database was utilized to predict the targeted relationship between diagnostic marker genes and drugs, while Cytoscape was employed to construct a gene-drug interaction network diagram (Figure 6A).

  The hTFtarget was used to analyze the transcription factors that regulated diagnostic marker genes, the miRWalk database was employed to predict the microRNAs that regulated these genes. Figure 6B,6C illustrated the quantity of shared transcription factors and microRNAs that regulated the diagnostic genes. The specific list of transcription factors and microRNAs, as well as the network constructed using Cytoscape software, can be found in Tables S2,S3 and Figures S1,S2.


### sgGSEA of diagnostic marker genes
  The correlation between diagnostic marker genes and other genes was calculated using the “Spearman” method, and the genes were ranked in descending order based on the strength of the correlation. GSEA enrichment analysis was then performed. Under the condition of adjusted P<0.05, there were 198 significantly enriched pathways corresponding to the DAPK1 gene, 240 significantly enriched pathways corresponding to the PARP1 gene, and 217 significantly enriched pathways corresponding to the BIRC3 gene. The top 20 most significant pathways were displayed using ridge plots (Figure 7).


## Discussion
  Ongoing multi-omics studies may help identify biomarkers for predicting or early diagnosing NEC. Bioinformatics analysis has revealed the involvement of processes such as ferroptosis, autophagy, pyroptosis, and mitophagy in NEC, identifying several key biomarker genes (23-27). Building on this, the present study further investigates NEC biomarker genes from the perspective of necroinflammation-associated necroptosis.

  In this study, we thoroughly investigated necroinflammation-related necroptosis in NEC, identifying 29 DE-NiNRGs through differential expression and correlation analysis. Additionally, machine learning revealed three key genes (DAPK1, PARP1, and BIRC3) that can serve as diagnostic markers. A series of analysis results, including ROC curve and nomogram model, indicate that these three genes have a high diagnostic value for NEC. In addition, we also analyzed the transcription factors and microRNAs that regulate these marker genes through database analysis, and identified potential small molecule drugs associated with these marker genes, further revealing their upstream regulatory mechanisms and providing an important theoretical basis for disease treatment.

  Among these three diagnostic genes, PARP1 has been identified to be associated with the occurrence and development of NEC. PARP1 is an important enzyme that helps repair DNA by using nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) as a substrate (28). Under severe oxidative stress and DNA damage, PARP1 can become overactivated. This overactivation depletes cells of NAD+ and ATP, leading to cell death due to metabolic failure (29,30). Research indicates that dysregulated nitric oxide (NO) levels in IECs during NEC result in increased PARP1 expression. Furthermore, treatment with a PARP1 inhibitor, nicotinamide, has been shown to alleviate intestinal injury in a newborn rat model of NEC (31). Researchers found that FK866 lowers intercellular NAD+ levels and reduces the expression of the NAD+-dependent enzyme PARP1. This effect is important for intestinal mucosal immunity and helps ensure the survival of NEC pups (32). In addition, parthanatos, similar to necroptosis, is a programmed inflammatory cell death and has been implicated in participating in ischemia/reperfusion-induced intestinal tissue injury. Recent studies have shown that PARP1 dependence is one of the key features of parthanatos in NEC (33).

  Research on BIRC3 and DAPK1 in NEC is currently limited, and this study is the first to identify their diagnostic value using bioinformatics analysis and machine learning. BIRC3 is part of the inhibitor of apoptosis protein (IAP) family and inhibits apoptosis (34), whereas DAPK1 is an apoptosis-promoting protein associated with various cell death pathways (35). Considering the roles of DAPK1 and BIRC3 in various physiological and pathological processes, we can infer their potential impact on NEC. BIRC3 appears to significantly regulate the survival and repair of IECs by inhibiting necroptosis. Activating DAPK1 may worsen intestinal damage by inducing necroptosis in IECs. This process could promote the onset and progression of NEC. BIRC3 and DAPK1 are key players in regulating intestinal inflammation and cell survival and death, suggesting their significant potential for investigation in NEC research. Future research should explore the specific roles and regulatory networks of these two molecules in NEC’s pathogenesis to identify new targets and strategies for clinical intervention.

  Furthermore, this bioinformatics analysis revealed additional significant findings. The immune infiltration analysis indicated intergroup differences in Th2 cells. Studies show that T helper cells are crucial in the onset and progression of NEC (36,37), especially regarding the development of Th2 cells (38). Th2 cells regulate immune responses and encourage the repair and regeneration of IECs (39). They achieve this by producing cytokines, including IL-4, IL-5, and IL-13. By enhancing mucosal barrier function and regulating inflammatory responses, these cells may influence the onset and progression of the disease in NEC (40). The KEGG enrichment analysis of differential genes reveals that, in addition to the well-known TNF and IL-17 signaling pathways (41-44), the cytosolic DNA-sensing pathway is also highly enriched. The cytosolic DNA-sensing pathway activates the immune response by detecting exogenous DNA or DAMPs in the cytoplasm (45). In the pathological process of NEC, the disruption of the intestinal barrier may allow bacteria or bacterial DNA to enter (46), thus activating the cytosolic DNA-sensing pathway and triggering an inflammatory response. Excessive inflammatory responses can worsen intestinal damage, contributing to the onset and progression of NEC.

  This study is primarily based on the GSE64801 dataset for analysis. Although this dataset provides rich gene expression information, its sample size is relatively limited, which may affect the generalizability and reliability of the results. Future research should consider integrating more clinical samples and multi-center data to validate the DE-NiNRGs we identified and their role in NEC. Moreover, most bioinformatics analyses, including this study, rely on samples from surgically excised intestinal tissues, which are difficult to obtain in clinical practice. Therefore, future research should investigate the expression of these diagnostic marker genes in stool or peripheral blood from NEC patients to enhance their clinical value. Additionally, further exploration is needed in areas such as functional validation, targeted drug development, and immune mechanisms of marker genes. Future studies will provide a more comprehensive solution for the clinical management of NEC.


## Conclusions
  In summary, this study not only reveals the molecular mechanisms of necroptosis and necroinflammation in NEC but also identifies potential diagnostic marker genes and therapeutic targets in NiNRGs, providing an important theoretical basis for future clinical applications. These findings offer new insights for the early diagnosis and personalized treatment of NEC, with significant clinical translational potential.
