# Role of SAA1 in mediating renal fibrosis through TLR4-dependent NF-κB activation


## Background
  Renal fibrosis is a key and irreversible pathological event in the progression of chronic kidney disease (CKD), and inflammatory response plays an important role in this process. Serum amyloid A1 (SAA1), an acute-phase reactant protein, which plays an important role in inflammation as a cytokine; but its specific role in renal fibrosis remains to be studied.


## Methods
  In this study, SAA1 and TLR4 knockout mice were used to establish unilateral ureteral obstruction (UUO) animal renal fibrosis model, and HK-2 cells were used to establish TGF-β-induced cell fibrosis model. The specific role of SAA1 in renal fibrosis was explored by immunoblotting and immunohistochemistry. Subsequently, this study used molecular docking and Co-Immunoprecipitation to verify the specific mechanism of SAA1 promoting renal fibrosis progression.


## Results
  We found that SAA1 levels were significantly increased in both in vivo and in vitro fibrosis models. Functional studies have shown that gene knockout or knockdown of SAA1 can significantly reduce the progression of renal fibrosis and limited fibrin deposition. On the contrary, overexpression of SAA1 in HK-2 cells aggravated the process of fibrosis. At the mechanism level, we found that in HK-2 cells, SAA1 can directly bind to TLR4, activate downstream NF-κB signaling pathway, promote the production of various cytokines, and then promote the progress of renal fibrosis. Importantly, knockdown of TLR4 alleviated this process.


## Conclusions
  This study demonstrates that SAA1 promotes the progression of renal fibrosis through TLR4/NF-κB signaling pathway.

## Introduction
  Chronic kidney disease (CKD) represents a substantial global public health challenge, with an estimated prevalence of 10–14% worldwide [1, 2]. The progression of CKD toward end-stage renal disease (ESRD) is predominantly driven by renal fibrosis, a pivotal pathological hallmark in the disease trajectory [3, 4]. Renal fibrosis unfolds as a progressive process marked by the establishment of a pro-fibrotic microenvironment, the heightened secretion of inflammatory mediators, injury to renal tubular epithelial cells, and the activation of myofibroblasts. These cellular and molecular events culminate in the excessive deposition of extracellular matrix (ECM), which fosters structural remodeling of the renal, and eventually leading to irreversible loss of renal function [5–8]. In this series of processes, excessive inflammatory response is often one of the starting factors for the occurrence and development of renal fibrosis. Studying the mechanism of inflammatory response in the process of renal fibrosis is conducive to finding new therapeutic targets for CKD.

  Serum amyloid A1 (SAA1) is a notable acute phase inflammation-related protein, with its expression markedly upregulated in response to external stimuli, involving tissue injury, infection, or surgical trauma [9]. The human genome harbors four functional SAA genes, designated SAA1 through SAA4. Of these, the SAA1 gene, situated on the short arm of chromosome 11, encodes the SAA1 protein [10]. Accumulating evidence highlighted the principal function of SAA1 in the pathogenesis and progression of numerous illnesses, comprising nonalcoholic fatty liver disease [11], coronary artery disease [12] and diabetes mellitus [13]. Additionally, SAA1 has been implicated in activating the inflammatory pathways, thereby exacerbating inflammatory responses and fibrosis in nonalcoholic fatty liver disease driven by high-fat diet [14]. As research on SAA1 continues to evolve, its principal function as a cytokine in mediating inflammatory responses has been progressively elucidated. Nevertheless, the specific mechanisms through which SAA1 influences renal fibrosis have yet to be comprehensively figured out.

  In this study, we screened SAA1 as a hub gene for renal fibrosis by transcriptome sequencing combined with GEO databases, and verified the high expression of SAA1 in unilateral ureteral obstruction (UUO)-induced renal fibrosis mouse model and transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β)-stimulated HK-2 cells model. To figure out the mechanistic function of SAA1 in renal fibrosis, we constructed SAA1 knockout mice, TLR4 knockout mice and SAA1 overexpression cell lines. Investigations revealed that SAA1 can activate NF-κB signaling pathway by directly binding and activating TLR4, and promote the expression of intracellular SAA1. In conclusion, our study verified the important role of SAA1-mediated TLR4/NF-κB inflammatory signaling pathway in the process of renal fibrosis, and provided a new direction for further research on targeted therapy of renal fibrosis.


## Materials and methods

### Mice
  Systemic Knockout mice of SAA1 were purchased from Cyagen Biosciences (Jiangsu, China). Saa1+/- mice were crossed with C57/BL6 mice to produce heterozygous mice (Saa1+/-, F1), and heterozygous mice were further crossed to produce homozygous Saa1-/- mice. PCR amplification was performed with the following primers: Forward primer (F1): 5’ – AGACTGAATGAGCTTGTCTGCTG − 3’, Reverse primer (R1): 5’ – GGAGAGTCTTACTACACTAACTGCTT − 3’. Homozygous mice can produce one band with 459 bp, and wild-type mice can produce one band with 4594 bp. Commercialized TLR4 Systemic knockout mice of 6–8 weeks were from GemPharmatech (Jiangsu, China), and C57BL/6J mice (RRID: IMSR_JAX:000664) were from China Three Gorges University. All mice lived in a standard environment without specific pathogens with a 12-hour light-dark cycle and were free to eat.


### Animal models
  The mouse unilateral ureteral obstruction (UUO) model was used to simulate the process of human obstructive renal fibrosis. 8-week-old C57BL/6J mice, SAA1 knockout mice or TLR4 knockout mice, weighing 20 ± 2 g, were fed adaptively for one week. Randomly allocation of wild-type (WT) and knockout (KO) mice to either the Sham operation group or the UUO group was undertaken. Prior to the procedures, all mice were subjected to anesthesia through an intraperitoneal injection of pentobarbital sodium (30 mg/kg). In the UUO group, the right ureter was subjected to a double ligation through 5 − 0 silk sutures, positioned approximately 0.5–1 cm distal to the renal pelvis, following a right abdominal incision post-laparotomy. In contrast, the sham operation group only underwent the surgical exposure of the right ureter without subsequent ligation. Seven days post-surgery, mice (n = 6 per group) were euthanized via cervical dislocation, and their kidneys and serum were promptly harvested and stored at -80 °C. All procedures involving animals were implemented in strict adherence to the guidelines and principles approved by the Animal Protection and Use Committee of Tongren Hospital of Wuhan University (WuSan YiShiLun SY2022-014).


### Cells
  HK-2 cells (RRID: CVCL_0302) were from Stem Cell Bank, Chinese Academy of Sciences. HK-2 cells were cultured in DMEM/F12 medium (Servicebio, # G4612), which augmented with 10% fetal bovine serum (Servicebio, # G8003) and 1% P / S (Servicebio, # G4003) in a 5% CO2 environment. After the cells were enriched to 80% -90% of the culture flask area, 1 × 106 cells were evenly inoculated into 6-well plates for transfection.

  plv-SAA1 was obtained from Biotech (Wuhan, China), and siSAA1 and siTLR4 were obtained from Genecreate (Wuhan, China). The transfection efficiency was evaluated by qPCR. After the cells were enriched to 80% in 6-well plates, Lipofectamine 2000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, # 12566014) was used for transfection. After transfection, the cells were cultured in complete medium for 24 h.

  For cell experiments, transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β) (Peprotech, #100 − 21) was used to induce modeling. The cells in the 6-well plate were incubated overnight in a medium without fetal bovine serum, and then treated with or without TGF-β (20 ng/ml). After 24 h of incubation in complete medium, cells were collected to extract RNA or protein for further analysis.


### QPCR
  Total RNA was extracted from mouse kidney tissue or cells using the Eastep TM Super Total RNA Extraction Kit (Promega, # LS1040) and reverse transcribed using the ReverTra Ace qPCR RT kit (TOYOBO, # FSQ-101). SYBR Green qPCR master mix (Servicebio, # G3327-01) was used for initial denaturation at 95 ℃ for 15 min, followed by 35 cycles at 94 ℃ for 10 s, 60 ℃ for 30 s and 72 ℃ for 1 min. The relative changes of mRNA level were determined by 2−△△CT method with GAPDH as control. The qRT-PCR primers used in this study are shown in Table S1.


### Western blotting (WB) and co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP)
  WB assays were executed in accordance with established methodologies as delineated in prior literature [15]. Gray values were quantified by Image J (NIH, USA, RRID: SCR_003070) and normalized to ACTB level. The source of the antibodies used is listed in Table S2.

  For Co-IP assays, HK-2 cells were lysed using precooled IP lysis buffer (Servicebio, # G2038). After centrifugation, supernatants were collected and incubated with different primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C temperature. Then, the mixed rProtein A Beads (Smart-Lifesciences, #SM037050) were added to the incubated supernatant and incubated for 2 h at 4 °C. Next, the beads were rinsed four times at 2000 g for 5 min at 4 °C. After the last wash, the supernatant was aspirated and boiled with 1×loading buffer, and the expression levels of eluted proteins were analyzed by WB. The source of the antibodies used is listed in Table S2.


### Histological staining and scoring
  For histopathological analysis, murine kidney specimens were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde (Servicebio, #G1101), which subsequently embedded into paraffin, sectioned at a thickness of 4 microns, and stained using hematoxylin-eosin (HE) staining protocols as outlined previously [15]. Quantitative evaluation of renal tissue injury was implemented through selecting random fields of view at 400× magnification from six independent samples per experimental group. The degree of renal tubular damage was quantified by computing the percentage of injured or necrotic renal tubules, characterized by features, involving tubular necrosis, cast formation, and loss of the brush border, relative to the total tubular population. A damage score ranging from 0 to 4 was allocated on the basis of percentage of affected tubules, with 0 representing no damage, and 1, 2, 3, and 4 corresponding to 0%-25%, 25%-50%, 50%-75%, and greater than 75% of damaged tubules, respectively. For statistical robustness, a minimum of six non-overlapping cortical regions were analyzed for each kidney.


### Sirius red staining and Masson staining
  Sirius red and Masson’s trichrome staining techniques were utilized to precisely evaluate the extent of fibrous collagen deposition in renal tissues. Following fixation, it was attempted to stain paraffin-embedded kidney sections with Picrosirius red or Masson’s trichrome reagents. Quantification of collagen deposition was implemented on non-overlapping cortical regions at magnifications of 200× and 400×, with ImageJ software employed to quantify the percentage of positively stained collagen structures. The area of Sirius red-positive or Masson-positive structures was expressed as a percentage of the total area in each microscopic field. To ensure a comprehensive assessment, at least six distinct, non-overlapping cortical regions were analyzed per kidney.


### Immunofluorescence (IF) and immunohistochemistry (IHC)
  For IHC staining, paraffin-embedded tissue sections underwent a precise dewaxing and rehydration process, followed by antigen retrieval. Nonspecific binding was blocked at room temperature for 30 min. Subsequently, it was attempted to incubate tissues overnight at 4 °C with the primary antibodies, which had been appropriately diluted to achieve optimal specificity. After four thorough washes with TBST, a proper secondary antibody was applied, followed by incubation at room temperature for one hour. DAB (3,3′-diaminobenzidine) was utilized as the chromogenic substrate for visualization of antibody binding. The quantification of intensity of IHC staining particularly in renal cortical sections was undertaken at 400× magnification through ImageJ, allowing for precise measurement of positively stained areas in the observed microscopic fields.

  For the IF staining procedure, cells were initially prepared and subjected to thrice rinsing with PBS, each lasting around 3 min. The slides were subsequently fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde to preserve cellular morphology, followed by permeabilization with 0.5% Triton X-100 particularly at room temperature (20 min). After permeabilization, it was attempted to thrice rinse the slides with PBS, 3 min esach, to remove excess Triton X-100. To mitigate non-specific binding, animal serum was exerted dropwise, and its incubation was implemented for 30 min particularly at room temperature. Thereafter, the slides were exposed to the primary antibody overnight at 4 °C to ensure optimal antigen-antibody binding. Subsequently, the slides were thrice thoroughly rinsed particularly with PBST to eliminate unbound primary antibody. A fluorescence-conjugated secondary antibody was employed dropwise and incubated particularly at room temperature (one hour) to allow for specific binding to the primary antibody. For nuclear staining, DAPI was exerted dropwise and incubated in the dark to highlight cell nuclei. It was attempted to carry out fluorescence imaging at magnifications of 200× and 400× through a fluorescence microscope, and images were subsequently analyzed via ImageJ software to figure out staining intensity and distribution.


### ELISA
  The serum samples of mice were extracted, Mouse SAA1 ELISA Kit (Beyotime, # PS823) was used to detect SAA1 protein levels in mouse serum according to the reagent instructions.


### Protein binding site prediction
  To facilitate molecular docking, AutoDockTool 1.5.6 was employed to prepare the proteins by the addition of hydrogen atoms and the assignment of partial charges, ensuring the appropriate electrostatic environment for accurate docking predictions. Utilizing HDOCK software, it was attempted to discover the molecular docking interaction of SAA1 with TLR4. The pre-processed protein structures were imported into the HDOCK software, where rigid-body docking simulations were undertaken. Local docking was applied, generating 100 distinct conformations of the protein complex, while all other parameters were maintained at their default configurations to ensure consistency. The conformation exhibiting the lowest binding energy was selected as the most stable interaction model. Visualization of the protein-protein interaction interface, including hydrogen bonds, van der Waals forces, and key amino acid residues involved in the binding, was implemented via PyMOL (RRID: SCR_000305).


### Gene expression data source and core gene screening based on machine learning algorithm
  The gene expression data of the control group (Sham) and the 7-day UUO model group in this study were derived from GSE118339, GSE145053 and GSE217654 (GEO DateSets, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gds/?term=). We employed the Absolute Least Shrinkage and Selector Operation (LASSO) algorithm, a penalty parameter adjustment with 10-fold cross-validation to select hub genes [16]. At the same time, another algorithm, The Support Vector Machines Recursive Feature Elimination (SVM-RFE) algorithm for searching lambda with minimal classification was also used for the screening of hub genes [17]. Finally, we used the differential genes in LASSO and SVM-RFE algorithms to obtain the core differential genes for subsequent analysis.


### Data analysis
  The data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism 8.0.2 (RRID: SCR_002798) and presented as mean ± standard error (SEM). One- or two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was employed for comparisons involving multiple groups, while an unpaired two-tailed t-test was used for two independent groups. A P-value below the threshold of 0.05 reflected statistical significance. Additionally, to elucidate functional annotations, extensive Gene Ontology (GO) analysis were implemented via R 4.3.0.


## Results

### SAA1 is significantly upregulated in kidneys of UUO mice
  First, in order to screen the key genes in the fibrosis process, we used the public data set GSE118339 and GSE145053 (Fig. 1A). The LASSO logistic regression algorithm was utilized to identify 6 key genes associated with unilateral ureteral obstruction (UUO) (Fig. 1B), while the SVM-RFE algorithm recognized 31 optimal characteristic genes (Fig. 1C). By integrating the gene sets derived from both the LASSO and SVM-RFE models, SAA1 emerged as the central hub gene (Fig. 1D). Subsequently, we found that in the above GEO data set (including a total of 7 control group and 8 UUO group mouse kidney specimens), the expression of SAA1 gene in the kidney of UUO group was significantly up-regulated compared with the sham operation group. Our own sequencing results (GSE217654, including 4 control group and 4 UUO group mouse kidney specimens) also showed consistent results (Fig. 1E-F).

  In order to further investigate the changes of SAA1 expression level in the process of renal fibrosis, we successfully constructed a mouse model of renal fibrosis induced by UUO. To attain deeper insights into the dynamic variations in SAA1 expression level during renal fibrosis, it was attempted to successfully establish UUO-induced mouse model of renal fibrosis. In vivo assays revealed that, relative to the Sham group, 7-day UUO mice showed noticeable renal tubular damage and extensive collagen fiber deposition in the renal interstitium (Fig. 1G-J). WB outcomes showed a progressive upregulation of SAA1 protein expression in the kidneys, correlating with the duration of UUO, alongside a concomitant elevation in the fibrosis markers α-SMA and FN (Fig. 1K-N). Moreover, IHC and qPCR confirmed a marked elevation in SAA1 expression level following 7-day UUO, with predominant localization to renal tubular epithelial cells (Fig. 1O-Q). Notably, one day after UUO, the serum SAA1 level of mice increased rapidly and maintained at a high level (Fig. 1R). Taken together, these results highlight that up-regulation of renal SAA1 may play a major regulatory role in the progression of renal fibrosis.

### Knockout of SAA1 attenuates UUO-induced renal fibrosis in mice
  In order to further investigate the possible regulatory relationship between SAA1 and renal fibrosis, we constructed SAA1 knockout mice and studied whether SAA1 gene deletion can alleviate UUO-induced renal fibrosis (Fig. 2A-B). First, we randomly divided 8-week-old WT mice or SAA1 knockout mice into 4 groups (n = 6 per group) to construct renal fibrosis models or perform Sham surgery: (a) WT-Sham, (b) WT-UUO, (c) SAA1-KO-Sham and (d) SAA1-KO-UUO. After 7 days, the kidneys were taken out for detection. The results unveiled that, in the Sham group, SAA1 knockout did not exhibit any significant alterations in renal tissue relative to WT mice. Conversely, in the UUO-induced fibrosis models, SAA1 knockout notably diminished the expansion of renal tubules, the renal tubular injury scores, and collagen fiber deposition (Fig. 2F-H, Figure S1A-B). Moreover, SAA1 knockout led to a remarkable reduction in the expression of key fibrosis markers, e.g., α-SMA, FN, and Col-1 proteins (Fig. 2A, C-F, I-J, Figure S1A, C). The above results indicates that the deletion of SAA1 substantially reduce UUO-induced renal fibrosis in mice.

### SAA1 promotes the progression of TGF-β-induced HK-2 fibrosis
  In vitro assays, we used transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β) (20ng/ml) to stimulate HK-2 for different time (6 h, 12 h and 24 h) to evaluate alterations in the protein expression level of SAA1 and prominent fibrosis markers. The outcomes demonstrated a time-dependent upregulation of SAA1, FN, and α-SMA in response to TGF-β treatment, with progressively higher protein levels observed with extended exposure (Fig. 3A-D). To further elucidate the function of SAA1 in renal fibrosis, SAA1 expression in HK-2 cells was altered through overexpression (plv-SAA1) or knockdown (siSAA1) strategies (Figure S2A-B). The results showed that in TGF-β-treated cells, overexpression of SAA1 caused a remarkable elevation in the levels of fibrosis-associated proteins (FN, α-SMA, and Col-1) relative to the TGF-β-treated Vector group (Fig. 3E-H). Conversely, SAA1 knockdown in the TGF-β-induced fibrosis model led to a notable reduction in FN and α-SMA protein levels, while E-cadherin levels exhibited an upward trend (Fig. 3I-L). These in vitro results underscore a positive link between SAA1 expression level and the severity of renal fibrosis.

### SAA1 regulates renal fibrosis through the NF-κB pathway
  To figure out the mechanistic function of SAA1 in regulating renal fibrosis, GO enrichment analysis was implemented on renal specimens from both WT and UUO mice in the GSE217654 dataset. A remarkable enrichment was noteworthy in pathways linked to acute inflammatory response (Figure S3A). In vitro assays using TGF-β-stimulated HK-2 cells, it was observed that, while the mRNA expression levels of TLR2 and TLR4 remained relatively stable in the plv-SAA1 group compared with the control group, there was a noticeable upregulation in the mRNA levels of principal inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, IL-10, and NLRP3) (Figure S2D-H). This reflects a strong link between elevated SAA1 expression and enhanced inflammatory responses. Further, by investigating previous studies, we found that NF-κB is one of the core pathways in the inflammatory response [18]. Based on this information, we speculate that SAA1 may regulate renal fibrosis through the NF-κB pathway.

  Subsequently, we found that in UUO mice, the levels of p-P65 and p-IκBα were remarkably diminished in the kidneys of SAA1 KO mice relative to WT mice (Fig. 4A-C), confirming the involvement of SAA1 in the NF-κB pathway. Additionally, in TGF-β-treated HK-2 cells, SAA1 knockdown resulted in a notable reduction in p-P65 and p-IκBα levels, whereas SAA1 overexpression led to elevated levels of these phosphorylated proteins (Fig. 4D-K). Moreover, a substantial escalation was noteworthy in the nuclear localization of p65 protein in the plv-SAA1 group compared with controls, with this effect was further amplified upon TGF-β stimulation (Figure S3B). These outcomes collectively demonstrate that SAA1 plays a notable function in renal fibrosis by regulating the NF-κB signaling pathway.

### The TLR4/NF-κB pathway regulates SAA1-associated renal fibrosis
  The NF-κB pathway is a classical downstream inflammatory signaling pathway mediated by TLR4 [19]. To figure out the involvement of TLR4 and NF-κB pathway in SAA1-mediated renal fibrosis, TLR4 KO mice were employed in a UUO model (Fig. 5G, Figure S4A). Our results uncovered a marked reduction in renal tubular damage and interstitial fibrin deposition in TLR4-KO UUO mice, concomitant with a significant attenuation of NF-κB pathway activation (Fig. 5A-G; Figure S4C-H). Notably, the absence of TLR4 also resulted in a remarkable diminution in SAA1 protein level (Fig. 5G, Figure S4B), suggesting that TLR4 may be a necessary condition for SAA1 to regulate renal fibrosis.

  Further validation in vitro was undertaken through the TGF-β-induced HK-2 cell model. Firstly, we knocked down TLR4 (Figure S2C). In these cells, TLR4 knockdown effectively abrogated the upregulation of fibrosis markers FN and α-SMA, restored E-cadherin level, and suppressed NF-κB pathway activation relative to the control group. Intriguingly, overexpression of SAA1 in TLR4-deficient cells led to exacerbated fibrosis markers and reactivation of the NF-κB pathway (Fig. 5H; Figure S4I-N). These results indicate that SAA1 regulates renal fibrosis dependent on TLR4 and NF-κB pathways.

### SAA1/TLR4 activates the NF-κB pathway and promotes the progression of renal fibrosis
  It was documented that SAAs are endogenous agonists of TLR4 [20]. We used HDOCK software to predict the molecular docking of SAA1 and TLR4. The results showed that SAA1 and TLR4 proteins produced a hydrogen bond interaction network. The amino acid sites of protein interaction included SER3, ARG2, ASN65, GLU27, ARG26 sites of SAA1 and ASP209, GLU135, LYS362, GLU505, ASN433, HIS456, SER432 amino acid sites of TLR4 (Fig. 6A). And our Co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) results also showed the binding of SAA1 to TLR4 (Fig. 6B). Combined with the previous results, SAA1 may regulate the progression of renal fibrosis by directly binding to TLR4.

  To further elucidate the principal function of SAA1 in renal fibrosis, we further evaluated the influences of recombinant human SAA1 protein (rh-SAA1, 2 µg/mL) on HK-2 cells. Comparative analysis demonstrated that, relative to the TGF-β treatment group, the TGF-β + rh-SAA1 co-treatment resulted in a noticeable upregulation of fibrotic markers, comprising FN and α-SMA, while the expression of the epithelial marker E-cadherin was remarkably diminished. Concurrently, the activation of the NF-κB signaling pathway was substantially enhanced (Fig. 6C, Figure S5B-G). Intriguingly, a parallel trend was noteworthy when SAA1 was overexpressed or when rh-SAA1 was exogenously introduced (Figs. 3E and 4H, and 6C). Notably, following addition of rh-SAA1 to the culture supernatant of HK-2 cells, there was a notable escalation in SAA1 expression in the cells (Fig. 6C, Figure S5A), reflecting that SAA1 might induce an autocrine loop that potentiates its effects by activating the TLR4/NF-κB axis, thereby enhancing its own expression.

  To further dissect the involvement of SAA1 in inflammatory and fibrotic processes, we added rh-SAA1 to further induce HK-2 cells on the basis of knocking down TLR4. Notably, while the TGF-β group exhibited robust NF-κB pathway activation and an elevated fibrotic response, these influences were notably mitigated in the TGF-β + siTLR4 group, representing that TLR4 is a principal mediator in these processes. However, in the TGF-β + rh-SAA1 + siTLR4 group, NF-κB pathway activation was restored, and the fibrotic phenotype was reaggravated. Additionally, it was noteworthy that TLR4 knockdown diminished SAA1 expression in the TGF-β + siTLR4 group relative to the TGF-β group, whereas exogenous rh-SAA1 partially reversed this effect, leading to the escalated SAA1 expression in the cells (Fig. 6D-J). Consequently, these outcomes delineate a model in which SAA1 directly binds to and activates TLR4, which in turn drives NF-κB pathway activation, establishing a positive feedback loop that amplifies SAA1 expression and modulates renal fibrosis’ progression.

## Discussion
  Inflammation is a double-edged sword, which can both prevent fibrosis and promote fibrosis [21]. Chronic inflammation caused by tissue damage is an important condition for fibrosis [22]. In renal diseases, especially after tissue damage caused by ureteral obstruction or renal ischemia-reperfusion, inflammatory factors are continuously secreted, which promotes the transformation of inflammation to fibrosis [5, 23]. However, despite these associations, the precise molecular mechanisms by which inflammation drives the progression from renal injury to fibrosis remain incompletely explored, necessitating further research.

  In this investigation, a comprehensive analysis of SAA1, a principal inflammation-associated protein, across both animal and HK-2 cell models were implemented, to elucidate its role in renal fibrosis. The study uncovered key insights into the involvement of SAA1 in fibrotic pathways and delineated its mechanistic functions. The principal findings of the study were documented as follows: [1] SAA1 expression was remarkably risen in both the unilateral ureteral obstruction (UUO)-induced renal fibrosis model and in the TGF-β-stimulated HK-2 cell model, reflecting the link of elevated SAA1 level with fibrotic processes; [2] In vivo and in vitro, SAA1 knockout noticeably attenuated renal injury, as evidenced by reduced histopathological damage, and significantly lowered the expression levels of key fibrotic markers, in contrast to the fibrosis model groups; [3] Mechanistic exploration unveiled that SAA1 directly interacts with TLR4, initiating activation of the downstream NF-κB signaling, driving the autocrine production of SAA1, thereby emphasizing its role in promoting the progression of renal fibrosis.

  SAA1, a member of the SAAs protein family, is predominantly expressed in adipose and hepatic tissues and functions as an acute-phase reactant protein. Previous studies have shown that SAA1 expression is elevated in a variety of tumors and plays an important role in regulating lipid metabolism and promoting tumor metastasis [24, 25]. In order to verify the expression level of SAA1 in the process of renal fibrosis, we conducted in vitro and in vivo studies. In the present investigation, a remarkable upregulation of SAA1 protein was noteworthy in both the UUO-induced renal fibrosis model and the TGF-β-stimulated HK-2 cell model, reflecting its induction during the fibrotic process. This unveils a potential function of SAA1 in the pathogenesis and progression of renal fibrosis. Furthermore, IHC revealed that SAA1 protein was predominantly accumulated in renal tubular epithelial cells particularly in the UUO group, aligning with the cell types typically affected by renal fibrosis.

  In the human gene spectrum, the SAAs family genes are divided into four types, SAA1-SAA4. Among them, SAA1 and SAA2 are notable for their inducibility by pro-inflammatory cytokines, involving IL-1β and IL-6, reflecting their integral role in acute-phase responses [26]. In clinical practice, both serum SAA1 and C-reactive protein (CRP) have regarded as reliable biomarkers for evaluating the intensity of inflammatory and infectious conditions. Beyond their role in inflammation, SAA1 has gained attention as a promising diagnostic biomarker in a wide array of pathological states, involving renal clear cell carcinoma, acute aortic dissection, and rheumatoid arthritis [27–29]. At the same time, the expression level of SAA1 is also closely related to the occurrence and development of various kidney diseases. In immune-related renal injury, the abnormal activation of complement factor H will lead to the increase of SAA1 expression level and destroy the barrier function of endothelial cells [30]. STAT3 can also promote the high expression of SAA1, which in turn aggravates renal injury by inducing endoplasmic reticulum stress [31]. Although SAA1 has been studied in the kidney field, the specific molecular mechanism of precise regulation of downstream pathways after SAA1 activation to aggravate renal injury has not been elaborated in the process of renal fibrosis. Therefore, this study further explored the core regulatory role of SAA1 in the process of renal fibrosis. Firstly, we constructed SAA1 knockout mice and found that the degree of renal fibrosis in SAA1-KO-UUO group was significantly reduced compared with WT-UUO group. This remarkable reduction highlights the function of SAA1, particularly as a notable regulatory factor in the occurrence and development of renal fibrosis.

  The NF-κB pathway is an important driver of inflammation and fibrosis in kidney injury [18]. In the cytoplasm, the central transcription factor subunit P65 is typically maintained in an inactive state through its association with IκBα. Upon exposure to various stimuli, IκBα undergoes phosphorylation, leading to its subsequent degradation via the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway, thereby releasing P65. This release facilitates the translocation of P65 into the nucleus, where it drives the transcription of pro-inflammatory genes [32]. Previous studies have shown that that SAA1 triggered atrophy in skeletal muscle cells by activating the TLR2/TLR4/NF-κB axis in myocardial nuclei, while inhibition of NF-κB signaling attenuated this muscle degeneration [33]. In the present investigation, it was uncovered that overexpression of SAA1 remarkably promoted the nuclear translocation of P65, whereas knockout or knockdown of SAA1 effectively inhibited NF-κB pathway activation. These outcomes emphasized that SAA1 has a critical function in modulating renal fibrosis, at least in part, through the activation of the NF-κB pathway.

  Previous studies have illuminated the noticeable interactions of SAAs with various membrane proteins, contributing to a range of complex biological functions. SAAs has exhibited to activate TLR2, subsequently triggering the NLRP3 inflammasome, fostering the polarization of macrophages toward M2 phenotype [34]. Additionally, SAAs engages with the scavenger receptor class B type I (SR-BI), facilitating cholesterol efflux, thereby highlighting its function in lipid metabolism [35]. In addition, SAA could activate TLR4, promoting the release of NOS and NO [36]. The present investigation corroborated these findings, as it was observed that SAA1 could directly interact with TLR4, triggering its downstream biological effects.

  SAA also has similar functional activities to other cytokines, but its cytokine characteristics have two sides. Previous studies have pointed out that recombinant human SAA (R-SAA) escalated the expression levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines, IL-1β and IL-6, in THP-1 cells [37]. In contrast, in 2015, Lei Sun et al. discovered that recombinant SAA (R-SAA) promoted macrophage M2 polarization, suggesting an anti-inflammatory function in specific contexts [38]. In this investigation, the addition of recombinant human SAA1 (rh-SAA1) to the supernatant of TGF-β-induced HK-2 cells remarkably elevated intracellular SAA1 expression and activated the NF-κB pathway. This activation was found to be dependent on the expression level of TLR4, suggesting that rh-SAA1 activated TLR4, which in turn activated the NF-κB pathway, leading to enhanced SAA1 synthesis. This regulatory feedback loop can be a preliminary mechanistic explanation for the function of SAA1 in promoting renal fibrosis.

  In conclusion, this study uncovered the principal function of the SAA1 gene in the pathogenesis of renal fibrosis. However,

  Several limitations are noteworthy: [1] The mechanistic exploration of role of SAA1 was limited to HK-2 cells, whereas SAA1 was also known to play a notable function in immune cells, suggesting the need for more extensive research across various cell types [2]. The autocrine regulation of SAA1 was only superficially examined, leaving room for future studies to dissect the comprehensive signaling networks involved [3]. Our observations of a sharp escalation of SAA1 RNA expression and serum protein levels in the early stages of UUO suggest a potential involvement of SAA1 in acute kidney injury, which will be figured out in subsequent research.

  In conclusion, this study reveals the important role of SAA1 gene in renal fibrosis, that is, SAA1 can directly bind to TLR4, activate TLR4 / NF-κB signaling pathway, and then mediate inflammatory cascade reaction to aggravate the degree of renal injury. In addition, the addition of rh-SAA1 to HK-2 cells can also activate the NF-κB pathway. Therefore, in the process of renal fibrosis, the use of specific antibodies to neutralize SAA1 may play a good protective role.


## Conclusions
  In summary, our study suggests that SAA1 activates the NF-κB pathway by directly binding to TLR4 and promotes SAA1 production by HK-2 cells, thereby driving the progression of renal fibrosis. Targeting SAA1 may provide new ideas for the diagnosis and treatment of renal fibrosis (Fig. 7).
