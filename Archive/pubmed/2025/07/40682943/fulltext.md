BACKGROUND: Although Achyranthes Bidentata Radix (ABR) demonstrates neuroprotective potential in NDDs, its anti-apoptotic mechanisms remain unclear. PURPOSE: To decipher ABR's key bioactive compounds and their regulation of neuronal apoptosis via the JUN/CREB-cAMP axis. METHODS: An integrated discovery-validation strategy synergized LC-MS characterization, multi-omics analysis, computational modeling and qRT-PCR technologies were carried to identify and validate ABR's key bioactive compounds and apoptosis targets through zebrafish for in vivo verification and HT22 cells for in vitro mechanistic analysis. RESULTS: UPLC-MS/MS profiling integrated with ADME screening and network topology analysis identified beta-Ecdysone, Quercetin, 25R-inokosterone, and 25S-inokosterone as ABR's key active compounds targeting JUN/CREB. Initial in vivo and in vitro studies demonstrated ABR's has neuroprotection in AlCl(3)-induced models in a dose-depended manner. Integrated metabolomic-transcriptomic mapping of zebrafish models revealed cAMP signaling biomarkers including cAMP, AMP, and dopamine, establishing JUN and CREB as key therapeutic targets. Machine learning modeling with 11 algorithms further prioritized JUN/CREB for neuronal apoptosis risk prediction, while molecular docking and dynamics simulations characterized stable compound-target interactions. Ultimately, qRT-PCR validation confirmed ABR's dose-dependent modulation of JUN and CREB, thus mechanistically anchoring the neuroprotective effects. CONCLUSION: ABR facilitates neuroprotection through bioactive compound-driven regulation of the JUN/CREB-cAMP axis, providing a mechanistic framework for NDDs therapy.
