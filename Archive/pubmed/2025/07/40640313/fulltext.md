# Integrative analysis identifies shared therapeutic pathways in thyroid eye disease and diabetes mellitus


## Abstract
  Thyroid eye disease (TED) and diabetes mellitus (DM) cause vision loss, with DM aggravating TED. In this study, we aimed to explore the mechanisms of this interaction, and therefore, analyzed gene expression data from representative TED and DM datasets using bioinformatic tools. Following normalization and differential expression analysis, common differentially expressed genes (CDEGs) between the datasets were identified and subjected to Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA), as well as Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) functional enrichment analyses. Interaction networks were constructed and the diagnostic potential of the CDEGs was assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. The TED_Dataset and DM_Dataset contained 449 and 108 DEGs, respectively, with seven CDEGs. GO and KEGG analyses linked the CDEGs to biological processes including leukocyte adhesion and apoptosis. GSEA emphasized their roles in inflammation and fibrosis. Protein-protein interaction network analysis identified MFAP4 as a key hub gene. Constructed mRNA-transcription factor, mRNA-RNA binding protein, mRNA-microRNA, and mRNA-drug interaction networks revealed extensive regulatory relationships. ROC curve analysis demonstrated that CXCL12 and SFRP4 were potential diagnostic biomarkers for TED, and SFRP4, IL6, MFAP4, and CRISPLD2 were potential diagnostic biomarkers for DM. Altogether, our findings provide comprehensive molecular insights into DM and TED, identifying novel targets for therapeutic intervention and diagnostic biomarkers.


## Introduction
  Diabetes mellitus (DM) is a long-term metabolic disturbance characterized by insulin resistance and relative insulin inadequacy. This condition is a formidable public health issue, affecting approximately 537 million individuals worldwide, with its prevalence notably escalating among younger populations1. DM may lead to significant adverse outcomes, such as cardiovascular disease, neurological complications, renal insufficiency, and retinopathy, all of which are intimately linked to elevated morbidity and mortality indices in patients2. DM management involves lifestyle modifications and pharmacological interventions aimed at achieving glycemic control and preventing complications3. However, patients with diabetes require insulin or oral medications daily and administer them prior to meals to maintain stable blood glucose levels—an inconvenience to patients in their daily life; additionally, patient compliance is very poor. Various genetic and environmental factors contribute to the onset of DM; recent studies have highlighted the role of specific genes and proteins in disease susceptibility and progression4.

  Several genes and proteins involved in glucose metabolism and insulin signaling pathways play critical roles in DM. For example, the PARγ gene is essential for adipocyte differentiation and glucose homeostasis, and its mutations are associated with DM5. Additionally, the TCF7L2 gene is strongly associated with DM risk6. Proteins such as insulin receptor substrate 1 and glucose transporter type 4 are also pivotal for insulin signaling and glucose uptake, respectively. Research has demonstrated that modifying these genetic sequences and protein structures can disrupt the regular metabolic mechanisms, ultimately resulting in insulin insensitivity and elevated blood glucose levels, both of which are indicative of DM7.

  Thyroid eye disease (TED), alternatively designated as Graves’ orbitopathy, is an autoimmune pathology marked by inflammatory processes and hypertrophy of the extraocular muscular tissues, along with augmentation of the orbital adipose tissue8. It predominantly affects individuals with thyroid dysfunction, notably those diagnosed with Graves’ disease, and may cause medical sequelae, including proptosis, diplopia, and potential vision impairment or even loss9. TED has considerable influence on overall well-being because of its disfiguring and incapacitating presentation. The etiopathogenesis of TED encompasses an intricate interplay between genetic predispositions, environmental stimuli, and immunological mechanisms, wherein autoantibodies directed against the thyroid-stimulating hormone receptor (TSHR) play a pivotal role10.

  Several genes and proteins are implicated in the development and progression of TED. In autoimmune thyroid orbitopathy, TSHR is the primary antigen recognized by autoantibodies that precipitates orbital inflammation and a subsequent cascade of tissue alterations. This highlights the central importance of TSHR in mediating autoimmune processes that lead to orbital manifestations in this disease state11. Additionally, the insulin-like growth factor-1 receptor, a protein similar to insulin, has been implicated in the underlying mechanism of TED and is overexpressed in orbital fibroblasts; notably, it contributes to tissue expansion and fibrotic processes12. Research has further emphasized the significance of cytokines, including interleukin (IL)-6 and tumor necrosis factor-alpha, in modulating the inflammatory cascade associated with TED13.

  Chronic hyperglycemia, a common phenotype of DM, arises from compromised insulin production and function, resulting in increased blood sugar levels. In DM, persistently high blood sugar levels play a significant role in the onset of both microvascular and macrovascular complications, such as retinopathy, nephropathy, and cardiovascular diseases14. Similarly, persistent hyperglycemia has been linked to increased oxidative damage and inflammatory responses, thereby accelerating the development and severity of DM and its associated complications15. TED is not directly associated with chronic hyperglycemia; however, thyroid dysfunction can influence glucose metabolism, potentially complicating the management of coexisting diabetes16. The interplay between DM and thyroid dysfunction underscores the importance of comprehensive management strategies in patients with these conditions.

  TED is characterized by inflammatory changes and hypertrophy of the periorbital muscles, as well as adipose tissue expansion within the orbital cavity, all of which stem from immune-mediated inflammation and tissue remodeling17. In individuals with DM, ongoing low-level inflammation plays a role in the development of insulin resistance and β-cell dysfunction, which adds complexity to the disease progression18. The shared inflammatory pathways in DM and TED suggest potential common therapeutic targets and emphasize the need for integrated treatment approaches. Co-occurrence of DM and TED can exacerbate the clinical manifestations of both diseases, necessitating careful monitoring and management to mitigate adverse outcomes. This study aimed to explore the molecular mechanisms that were common to DM and TED to identify potential diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets.

  By using the R package sva to remove batch effects from the GSE58331 and GSE41762 datasets of thyroid-associated ophthalmodisease & DM, the TED_Dataset and DM_Dataset were derived, representing distinct batches of the original dataset.

  Subsequently, the distribution boxplot (Fig. 1A–D)—used to compare the expression values of the datasets before and after batch effect removal—confirmed that the batch effect of samples in TED_Dataset and DM_Dataset was eliminated after removing the batch.

  The differential analysis identified 449 DEGs in the TED_Dataset. Among these, 103 genes were upregulated, whereas 346 genes were downregulated. A volcano plot was constructed to visualize these findings (Fig. 2A). Similarly, in the DM_Dataset, 108 DEGs were detected, of which 67 were upregulated, and 41 were downregulated. ### Hub gene differential expression validation and ROC curve analysis
  The expression differences of six hub genes (CXCL12, SFRP4, IL6, MFAP4, CRISPLD2, and THBS2) between the TED and control groups are presented in a comparison chart (Fig. 7A). The expression of two hub genes, CXCL12 and SFRP4, significantly differed (p < 0.01) between the TED and control group in the TED_Dataset. ## Discussion
  TED and DM are two common yet severe medical conditions. TED, an autoimmune disorder often accompanied by hyperthyroidism, manifests with ocular symptoms such as exophthalmos, diplopia, and visual impairment8. DM is a metabolic disorder that affects millions of people worldwide and increases the risk of complications such as cardiovascular diseases, nephropathy, and retinopathy1. Because of the widespread occurrence and serious nature of these conditions, exploring their connections and common molecular pathways is crucial and may have important implications for clinical practice.

  Mainstay therapy, which aims to mitigate inflammation and edema, has the potential for adverse effects, thereby necessitating a thorough risk-benefit analysis. Radiotherapy is another modality that can effectively reduce swelling but may inadvertently compromise the retina or optic nerve, necessitating careful consideration. Although surgical intervention is effective in alleviating symptoms in severe cases of TED, it is associated with a high risk and prolonged recovery periods19.

  The management of DM requires a multifaceted approach that encompasses lifestyle modifications, oral hypoglycemic agents, and insulin therapy. Although beneficial, lifestyle interventions often face sustainability challenges. Oral hypoglycemic agents, which are effective in lowering glucose levels, can cause side effects. Insulin therapy, although highly efficacious, requires self-monitoring and regular injections, which is challenging for some patients20.

  Despite the availability of diverse treatment options, the limitations inherent to each modality hinder the achievement of optimal outcomes. Therefore, future research should prioritize the development of safer, more effective, and patient-friendly therapies. Customized healthcare, designed to meet the specific requirements and attributes of each patient, offers the potential to enhance both results and quality of life.

  Furthermore, a comprehensive consideration of the interplay between TED and DM is crucial for devising comprehensive management strategies. By acknowledging the intricate relationship between these two conditions, healthcare providers can better understand the challenges faced by patients and develop targeted and effective treatment plans.

  This study investigated shared molecular mechanisms between TED and DM, with a particular focus on analyzing DEGs in TED samples, including those from the lacrimal gland. The gene expression analyses comparing TED and DM profiles identified CDEGs. Subsequent GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses revealed that these CDEGs were significantly enriched in immune and inflammatory responses and in metabolic processes. They were also significantly enriched in fibrosis-related pathways in TED tissues, including the lacrimal gland. In addition, PPI network analyses revealed important genes that could be significantly involved in the development of TED and DM. These findings strengthen the credibility of the potential pathological link between TED and DM and suggest that the identified CDEGs may play a significant role in the underlying pathological mechanisms of both conditions.

  Compared to previous studies, this study systematically uncovered, for the first time, the common molecular characteristics of TED and DM, offering fresh perspectives on the concurrent diagnosis and management of these two conditions. However, given the heterogeneity of TED samples, future studies should involve larger patient cohorts to further validate the specific roles of these key DEGs.

  CXCL12, also known as stromal cell-derived factor 1 (SDF-1), is a chemokine that facilitates the movement of different cell types, such as hematopoietic stem cells, leukocytes, and endothelial cells. It primarily engages with the chemokine receptor CXCR4 and, to a lesser degree, with CXCR7. The CXCL12/CXCR4 axis is involved in various physiological processes, including immune cell movement, embryonic growth, and blood vessel formation. However, it has also been implicated in various pathological conditions, including cancer and inflammatory diseases. For example, CXCL12 can stimulate the progression of tumors and their spread to other parts of the body in different types of cancers, such as lung, breast, and gallbladder carcinomas21. Moreover, the CXCL12/CXCR4 axis is involved in the inflammatory response and has been linked to diseases such as Crohn’s disease and experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis22. In TED and DM, the role of CXCL12 in inflammation and fibrosis may be particularly relevant. Studies have shown that posttranslational modifications to CXCL12, such as nitration and citrullination, can significantly alter its biological activity and affect its ability to bind to its receptors and mediate chemotaxis and signaling23. These results indicate that CXCL12 may play a crucial role in the common molecular pathways associated with TED and DM, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic target.

  Enrichment analysis of CDEGs in TED and DM revealed significant BPs and pathways that could illuminate the shared mechanisms underlying these conditions. Among the identified CDEGs, CXCL12, SFRP4, IL6, MFAP4, CRISPLD2, PPP1R1A, and THBS2 are notable for their potential roles in disease pathogenesis. CXCL12 (SDF-1) is involved in inflammation, immune responses, and tissue repair. Specifically, it attracts many cell types, including monocytes, lymphocytes, and endothelial cells, and plays an essential role in the attachment and movement of white blood cells24. The involvement of CXCL12 in inflammatory processes is particularly relevant in both TED and DM because chronic inflammation is a hallmark of both diseases.

  The GO and KEGG enrichment analyses revealed that these CDEGs play significant roles in regulating the adhesion of leukocytes to vascular endothelial cells, as well as epithelial cell apoptosis and other related processes. These findings suggest that inflammatory and immune responses, as well as tissue remodeling processes, are critical in the pathogenesis of TED and DM. For instance, the role of CXCL12 in leukocyte migration and its interaction with its receptors, CXCR4 and CXCR7, are essential for inflammatory cell recruitment and tissue infiltration, which are crucial processes in both autoimmune and metabolic disorders25.

  Furthermore, the GSEA highlighted proinflammatory- and profibrotic pathway-related enriched gene in both datasets. This is consistent with the known roles of IL-6 and THBS2 in promoting inflammation and fibrosis26. IL-6, a cytokine possessing both pro- and anti-inflammatory characteristics, is upregulated in numerous chronic inflammatory disorders such as TED and DM. Moreover, it plays a role in the inflammatory environment by stimulating T-cell differentiation and the generation of acute-phase proteins.

  In contrast, SFRP4, a modulator of Wnt signaling, has been implicated in tissue fibrosis and inflammation. Its upregulation in both TED and DM datasets suggests a potential role in the fibrotic processes observed in these diseases27. Similarly, MFAP4, a protein found in the extracellular matrix, plays a role in tissue restructuring and the development of fibrosis, further supporting the involvement of fibrotic pathways in the shared pathophysiology of TED and DM.

  The identification of these pathways and the roles of specific CDEGs offer important insights into the molecular processes shared by TED and DM. This understanding may lead to advancements in specific treatments designed to modulate these pathways to address or avoid these conditions. For instance, targeting CXCL12/CXCR4 signaling could potentially ameliorate inflammatory responses and tissue remodeling in both diseases, offering a novel therapeutic strategy.

  The immune system is also involved in the development and progression of TED and DM. In TED, the autoimmune response directed at orbital tissues is driven by immune cells, such as T cells, B cells, and different cytokines, resulting in inflammation and tissue restructuring28. Specifically, T helper cells (Th1, Th2, and Th17) and regulatory T cells have been implicated in disease progression, with Th17.1 cells being significantly involved in the development of fibrosis and adipocyte differentiation within the orbits3. The inflammatory milieu in TED is marked by increased concentrations of cytokines such as IL-6, IL-17 A, and interferon-γ, which drive the autoimmune response and tissue damage.

  Similarly, in type 1 DM (T1DM), immune system dysregulation is central to disease development. T1DM is defined by immune system attacks against pancreatic β-cells, conducted by autoreactive T cells and inflammatory cytokines. Macrophage polarization also plays a significant role, with M1 macrophages promoting inflammation and β-cell destruction, whereas M2 macrophages can potentially ameliorate the disease through anti-inflammatory pathways29. In T2DM, chronic low-grade inflammation, involving effector memory T cells and other immune cells, is associated with the onset of insulin resistance and β-cell dysfunction.

  The identification of CDEGs, such as CXCL12, SFRP4, IL6, MFAP4, CRISPLD2, PPP1R1A, and THBS2, in both TED and DM suggests shared immunopathogenic mechanisms. These genes are involved in key BPs, including leukocyte adhesion, epithelial apoptosis, and extracellular matrix composition, which are critical for the inflammatory and fibrotic responses observed in both diseases. Further analysis using GO and KEGG enrichment demonstrates the significant role of these genes in immune-related pathways, emphasizing their potential as targets for therapeutic intervention.

  The PPI network analysis and subsequent identification of central genes, such as CXCL12, SFRP4, and IL6, highlight their crucial involvement in the immune response and suggest their potential as diagnostic factors and progression biomarkers. ROC curve analyses indicate that these hub genes have significant diagnostic accuracy, particularly in differentiating disease states from controls, which could be instrumental in the development of diagnostic tools and targeted therapies. This study validates the diagnostic value of CXCL12 and SFRP4 in TED and DM through ROC curve analysis. These biomarkers may aid in the early detection of the disease and enable stratified patient management. Additionally, IL6 and MFAP4 are involved in inflammatory pathways, indicating their potential as therapeutic targets and contributing to advancements in precision treatment23.

  In summary, the immune system’s involvement in TED and DM is multifaceted and involves various immune cells, cytokines, and genetic factors. Understanding these processes not only offers insights into disease development but also promotes the development of innovative treatment approaches focused on adjusting immune reactions to manage or avoid these disorders.

  Although this study utilized two independent data sets, corrected for batch effects, and applied various analyses—including PPI networks, GSEA, and ROC curves—to strengthen the robustness of the results, certain limitations remain. In particular, our findings have not been confirmed through wet experiments, such as reverse transcription-quantitative polymerase chain reaction or Western blotting. Therefore, additional experimental support is needed to validate the expression patterns and mechanisms of the key genes at the biological level. In the future, we plan to validate the functions of these genes through in vitro and in vivo experiments to enhance the credibility of the study findings and its potential for clinical application. Further, while our statistical analyses rigorously controlled for multiple testing and estimated effect sizes, we recognize that validating our findings with an independent dataset would strengthen their robustness. However, due to limitations in data availability, this was not possible in the current study. Future research should include independent datasets to validate our proposed biomarkers, which would enhance the generalizability of our findings. Moreover, while we ensured the study’s reliability through rigorous data standardization and various bioinformatics analysis methods, we acknowledge that limitations in sample size may impact the robustness of our findings. The limited public datasets for TED and DM temporarily prevented us from adding more independent cohorts for validation. Therefore, the generalizability of our findings may be somewhat restricted. Future studies should aim to collect larger independent datasets to further validate the identified key genes and molecular pathways to enhance the generalizability of our findings for clinical applications.


## Conclusion
  We identified CDEGs between TED and DM using bioinformatic analyses. This research emphasizes the important genetic factors and pathways that could potentially contribute significantly to the development of both diseases. These findings were further supported by GO and KEGG enrichment analyses, GSEA, and PPI network construction. Despite some limitations, these findings offer a valuable understanding of the molecular mechanisms involved in TED and DM and present potential areas for future therapeutic interventions. Future studies should aim to validate these findings through wet-lab experiments and clinical trials to enhance their applicability in clinical settings.

### Data retrieval and processing
  We retrieved the diabetes dataset GSE41762 from the GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/), which is based on the Homo sapiens GPL6244 chip platform and comprises 77 samples. GSE41762 was selected for its high data quality and frequent use in previous research. The sample size was deemed adequate for statistical analysis requirements. To eliminate potential batch effects, we utilized the R package sva for batch effect correction during data processing. This step was crucial to ensure data accuracy.

  The R package GEOquery (version 2.70.0)30 from the GEO database31 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) was used to download the TED and DM datasets—GSE5833132–37 and GSE4176238,39, respectively. Both GSE58331 and GSE41762 were derived from Homo sapiens. Specifically, GSE58331 contains expression data from anterior orbit and lacrimal gland tissues, while GSE41762 contains data from pancreatic islet tissue. The microarray platforms for GSE58331 and GSE41762 were GPL570 and GPL6244, respectively (Table 8). GSE58331 comprised 27 TED and 22 control samples, and GSE41762 included 20 DM and 57 control samples. All of the selected TED, DM, and control samples were incorporated. The R package sva (version 3.50.0)40 was used to retrieve the TED and DM datasets from GSE58331 and GSE41762, respectively. The R package limma (version 3.58.1)41 was used to preprocess the TED and DM datasets, including probe annotation, standardization, and normalization steps.

### Determination of DEGs between TED, DM, and control samples
  DEGs between groups were examined. The limma package in R (version 3.58.1)41 was used to perform a genetic difference analysis between the TED and control groups and the DM and control groups in the TED and DM datasets, respectively. The criteria for DEGs were a |logFC| >0.5 and p values < 0.05; moreover, genes with logFC values > 0.5 and p < 0.05 were categorized as upregulated genes, i.e., increased expression, and those with logFC values < 0.5 and p < 0.05 were designated as downregulated genes, i.e., decreased expression. The differential analysis results were visualized using a volcano plot, generated with the R package ggplot2 (version 3.4.4).

  To identify CDEGs in TED and DM, a variance analysis was performed on the respective datasets. All genes with a |logFC| > 0.5 and p < 0.5 at the intersection of the Venn diagram were considered CDEGs. The R package pheatmap (version 1.0.12) was used to create a heatmap of the CDEGs. To identify potentially important regulators among the screened DEGs, we applied the criteria of log2FC > 0.5 for up-regulation and log2FC < − 0.5 for down-regulation. The threshold selection was based on several considerations. A fold change of approximately 1.414 (20.5) ensures a significant change in the expression levels of the selected genes42,43. Notably, this criterion is widely used in bioinformatic studies to ensure that important genes are not overlooked while preventing false negatives from overly strict filtering. Additionally, to improve the reliability of the screening, we applied a p value criterion of less than 0.05 to confirm the statistical significance of these genes.


### GO and KEGG enrichment analyses
  To ensure tissue-specific enrichment, we performed GO and KEGG functional enrichment analyses for DEGs within the GSE58331 (TED) and GSE41762 (DM) datasets, assessing whether their biological functions were significantly affected in TED- and DM-related tissues (lacrimal gland and islet tissue, respectively). Furthermore, we validated our findings through GSEA to identify key enriched genes in the TED dataset, particularly those involved in inflammation, extracellular matrix remodeling, and other related processes.

  GO analysis examines enrichment in BP, CC, and MF ontologies44 while KEGG45 enrichment focuses on genome, biological pathway, disease, and drug data. We used the R package clusterProfiler (version 4.10.0)46 for GO and KEGG enrichment analyses of the CDEGs. Statistical significance was determined using p values < 0.05 and a false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.25.


### GSEA
  GSEA47 examines predefined sets of genes within a designated dataset. The gene distribution patterns that are associated with phenotypic features are analyzed to quantify their individual contributions to the observed phenotype. In this study, we arranged genes from TED_Dataset and DM_Dataset according to their log FC values. Subsequently, GSEA was performed on all genes in the TED_Dataset and DM_Dataset using the R package clusterProfiler (version 4.10.0), with). parameters: seed value of 2020; 1000 permutations, and gene set sizes between 10 and 500. The c2 gene sets were accessed using the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB)48specifically utilizing the “c2.cp.all.v2022.1.Hs.symbols.gmt” dataset. Subsequently, GSEA was conducted using the “cs.cp.alll.v2022.1.hs.symbols.gmt [all canonical pathways] (3050)” gene set. Significance was set at, adjusted p < 0.05 and FDR (q-value) < 0.25, corrected using the Benjamini–Hochberg (BH) method. Differential expression analysis was conducted using the limma package, applying BH correction to manage multiple testing (FDR < 0.25), and effect sizes were calculated using Cohen’s d to assess their practical significance.


### PPI networks and hub gene screening
  The PPI network consists of proteins that interact with each other and participate in various processes, such as signal transduction, gene expression regulation, energy and material metabolism, and cell cycle control. A comprehensive examination of protein interactions within biological systems is important for comprehending how proteins function within these systems, the mechanisms underlying biological signal reactions and energy, substance metabolism under specific physiological conditions such as diseases, and functional connections between proteins. The STRING database (https://string-db.org/)49 contains known and predicted interactions between proteins. We utilized the STRING database to construct the PPI network associated with the identified CDEGs, using a minimum correlation coefficient of 0.150 as the threshold for the interaction score (low confidence). Interconnected neighboring areas within the PPI network signified potential molecular assemblies with distinct biological functions. Genes interacting with other genes within the PPI network were selected as hub genes for subsequent analyses. Cytoscape50 software was used for network visualization.

  The semantic similarity of GO terms44 provides a quantitative method to calculate the similarity between genes and genomes and has become an important foundation for many bioinformatics analysis methods. We used the R package GOSemSim (version 2.28.0) to evaluate the functional relatedness of the CDEGs by calculating the semantic similarity between their associated GO terms51. GO annotations were obtained for all CDEGs and semantic similarity scores were computed using the Resnik method. Higher scores indicate stronger functional connections. Friends analysis was applied to evaluate correlations between hub genes.


### Hub gene mRNA-TF, mRNA-RBP, mRNA-miRNA, and mRNA-drug interaction network analyses
  The ChIPBase database (version 3.0)52 (https://rna.sysu.edu.cn/chipbase/) includes ChIP-seq data for DNA-binding proteins, which has led to the identification of numerous base sequence matrices and their corresponding binding sites. Furthermore, it predicts the transcriptional regulatory connections between a wide array of TFs and genes. We searched the ChIPBase database (version 3.0) to identify TFs that bind to the hub genes. The screening criteria for mRNA-TF interaction pairs involved identifying > 6 total upstream and downstream samples. Subsequently, we used Cytoscape software to visualize the mRNA-TF interaction network.

  RBPs53 are crucial for gene regulation and for RNA synthesis, alternative splicing, modification, transfer, and translation of life activities. The starBase v3.0 database (https://starbase.sysu.edu.cn/)54 was used to forecast the hub genes that target RBPsRBP, and a clusterNum > 1 was used as the screening criteria of mRNA-RBP interactions. Cytoscape software was used to visualize the mRNA-RBP interaction network.

  The ENCORI database version 3.055 is a starBase database that contains miRNA-non-coding RNA (ncRNA), miRNA-mRNA, ncRNA-RNA, and RNA-RNA interactions. The RBP-ncRNA and RBP-mRNA interactions were identified using CLIP-seq and biotome sequencing data mining techniques for plants. We used the ENCORI database to predict miRNAs that engage with hub genes. A PancancerNum > 8 was employed as the screening criteria for mRNA-miRNA interaction pairs, and the mRNA-miRNA interaction network was visualized using Cytoscape software.

  The Comparative Toxicogenomics Database (https://ctdbase.org/)56 can be used to anticipate the direct and indirect hub gene targets of drugs and investigate the interactions between hub genes and pharmaceuticals. The screening criteria for mRNA-drug interaction pairs involved selecting those with a “Reference Count” >3. The mRNA-drug interaction network was visualized using Cytoscape software.


### ROC curve analysis of differentially expressed hub genes
  To examine the differential expression of hub genes in the TED and control groups of the TED_Dataset and in the DM and control groups of the DM_Dataset, we conducted a comparative analysis of their respective gene expression profiles. The R package pROC (version 1.18.5)57 was used to plot the ROC curve of the hub genes and calculate the AUC. The diagnostic impact of hub gene expression on the occurrence of TED and DM was also assessed. The AUC of the ROC curves ranged from 0.5 to 1, with higher values indicating better diagnostic performance. An AUC from 0.5 to 0.7 suggested low accuracy, 0.7–0.9 indicated moderate accuracy, and > 0.9 signified high accuracy.


### Statistical analysis
  The R software (version 4.2.2) was used for data processing and analysis. Unless otherwise specified, comparisons of continuous variables between two groups were performed using an independent Student’s t-test to evaluate the statistical significance for normally distributed variables, while differences in non-normally distributed variables were assessed using the Mann–Whitney U Test (Wilcoxon Rank Sum Test). The Kruskal–Wallis test was used to compare multiple groups, the Spearman correlation was used to calculate molecular correlations, and p values < 0.05 were considered significant.
