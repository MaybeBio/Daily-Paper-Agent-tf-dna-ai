# Identification of diagnostic biomarkers and dissecting immune microenvironment with crosstalk genes in the POAG and COVID-19 nexus


## Abstract
  An underlying association between primary open-angle glaucoma (POAG) and COVID-19 has been hypothesized, but the causal link and shared mechanisms remain unclear. This study integrates epidemiological and bioinformatics approaches to investigate their relationship, aiming to identify common molecular pathways and validate clinical correlations. Epidemiological data from 3,015 participants in the CHARLS database were analyzed using multivariate logistic regression and Cox proportional hazards models to assess the association between COVID-19 and POAG, with stratification by gender, smoking status, and alcohol consumption. Concurrently, gene expression datasets from GEO (POAG: GSE27276; COVID-19: GSE171110, GSE152418) were used to identify 57 crosstalk genes (CGs) via differential expression analysis. Machine learning algorithms (LASSO, SVM-RFE, Random Forest) were applied to screen POAG diagnostic biomarkers from CGs, followed by construction of transcription factor (TF)-microRNA (miRNA)-protein-compound regulatory networks and consensus clustering to characterize COVID-19 immune microenvironment subtypes. Epidemiological analyses revealed that COVID-19 was an independent risk factor for POAG, with adjusted odds ratios (ORs) of 12.775–15.688 and hazard ratios (HRs) of 4.893–5.060 (all P < 0.001), with stronger associations in males (OR = 15.054, HR = 5.645) and smokers (OR = 29.828, HR = 9.323). HDL levels were significantly higher in POAG patients (P = 0.028), while vb001_s1 positivity was negatively associated with POAG (P < 0.001). Bioinformatics identified CGs enriched in inflammatory response, oxidative stress, and hypoxia pathways. Five potential biomarkers (CA12, ECRG4, CEBPD, HBB, HBA2) showed high diagnostic efficacy (AUC > 0.9) but limited specificity (expressed in uveitis and diabetic retinopathy). COVID-19 patients were stratified into three clusters, with Cluster B exhibiting hyperactive immune microenvironments characterized by elevated HLA class I/II and immune checkpoint genes (CTLA4, PDCD1LG2) in antigen-presenting cells and T cells. This study provides epidemiological and molecular evidence linking POAG to COVID-19, highlighting inflammation and immune dysregulation as shared mechanisms. Gender and smoking modify this association, warranting targeted POAG screening in male COVID-19 survivors and smokers. While identified biomarkers require specificity improvement, findings underscore the need for mechanistic studies (e.g., ACE2-mediated ocular injury, T-cell neuroinflammation) to validate causality and inform clinical management.


## Introduction
  COVID-19, a highly contagious viral pneumonia, has resulted in over 7 million deaths and more than 700 million confirmed cases worldwide1. Since 2019, the global impact of SARS-CoV-2 has been profound, with high transmission risks persisting due to the emergence of new variants, causing recurrent spread in many countries2. Although COVID-19 mortality rates have decreased, the disease’s complications and long-term effects continue to pose significant risks to multiple body systems, maintaining a persistent public health threat3.

  Glaucoma, the world’s second leading cause of blindness, is marked by increased intraocular pressure, optic nerve damage, and permanent vision loss4. The COVID-19 pandemic has been associated with an increased incidence of glaucoma, with several reports suggesting a connection between COVID-19 infection and the onset of glaucoma5. Research indicates that the virus can penetrate both the ocular surface and internal ocular tissues6. A 66-year-old female developed new-onset glaucoma linked to COVID-19, with viral RNA found in her aqueous humor7. Evidently, there is a correlation between COVID-19 and glaucoma, but the shared pathogenic mechanism has not yet been elucidated.

  Although primary angle-closure glaucoma (PACG) is the most frequently reported type of COVID-19-associated glaucoma, primary open-angle glaucoma (POAG) is also observed in clinical settings8. PACG and POAG share similarities in various pathological mechanisms, such as inflammatory responses, oxidative stress, and hypoxia9–12. A key pathogenic mechanism of PACG is pupillary block, which contrasts sharply with POAG13. However, studies show that PACG during the COVID-19 pandemic is influenced by various factors14. Interestingly, while lens thickness and anterior chamber depth (both related to pupillary block) are similar in PACG patients with and without COVID-19, those with COVID-19 require significantly longer treatment to control IOP and prevent further visual impairment, even after both releasing pupillary block15,16. Therefore, studying the common mechanisms between POAG and COVID-19 could provide insights for the management of PACG patients post-COVID-19. Additionally, POAG often presents clinically with no or subtle symptoms, making it insidious but capable of causing severe vision loss17. Thus, identifying high-risk populations for POAG is crucial for effective clinical management. This study focuses on POAG and aims to explore its potential common pathological mechanisms with COVID-19. By identifying these common mechanisms, we hope to provide a scientific basis for identifying high-risk POAG populations among COVID-19 patients and offer new perspectives for the comprehensive management and research of both conditions.

  In recent years, several studies have focused on the co-manifestations of systemic and eye diseases through inflammatory or immune-related molecular pathways. For instance, a study investigated the role of the NF-κB signaling pathway in both rheumatoid arthritis and uveitis18. They found that the activation of NF-κB led to increased production of inflammatory cytokines in both diseases, contributing to joint inflammation in rheumatoid arthritis and ocular inflammation in uveitis. This suggested a shared inflammatory mechanism between the systemic and ocular conditions. Another study explored the connection between diabetes-related systemic inflammation and diabetic retinopathy. They demonstrated that elevated levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines, such as TNF-α and IL-6, in the systemic circulation due to diabetes could trigger a cascade of events in the retina, including increased vascular permeability and abnormal angiogenesis19. This indicated how immune-related molecular pathways in a systemic disease could directly impact the eye. Moreover, the complement system in systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) and its associated ocular manifestations20. They found that dysregulation of the complement system in SLE patients was linked to the development of ocular complications like retinopathy and episcleritis. These findings highlighted the importance of immune-related pathways in mediating the co-manifestations of systemic and eye diseases.

  These previous studies have provided valuable insights into the complex relationships between systemic and eye diseases. However, the connection between POAG and COVID-19 through such pathways remains unclear. Our study aims to fill this gap by exploring the potential common pathological mechanisms between these two diseases, which may offer new perspectives for the comprehensive management of patients affected by both conditions.

  This study utilized integrative bioinformatics tools to identify shared genes and potential mechanisms between POAG and COVID-19 by analyzing one POAG dataset and two COVID-19 datasets from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. Furthermore, machine learning techniques were carried out to identify hub genes for POAG prediction. We investigated the immune cell signatures of POAG to reveal the relationship between hub genes and the immunological landscape. Then, potential interaction networks with therapeutic efficiency in POAG were also identified. We validated the expression patterns of these hub genes and assessed their diagnostic efficacy in COVID-19.


## Materials and methods

### Data collection
  Data on POAG and COVID-19 were extracted from the CHARLS database, including 2015 blood test data, demographic data, comorbidity data and POAG information, as well as POAG and COVID-19 data counted in the 2020 batch. POAG was defined as assessed by participants’ self-report, with participants identified as having POAG if they answered“yes”to the question"Have you received treatment for primary open-angle glaucoma from a doctor, nurse, paramedic or traditional Chinese medicine practitioner?"in CHARLS studies. COVID-19 status was assessed by asking respondents"Have you, anyone around you, or acquaintances ever been identified as a confirmed or suspected case of COVID-19?"Participants were identified as having COVID-19 if they answered“myself”.

  One POAG dataset GSE27276, was downloaded from the NCBI Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/)21. The GSE27276 dataset were generated using the GPL2507 Sentrix Human-6 Expression BeadChip. GSE27276 dataset consisted of 17 POAG and 19 Control samples.

  The Gene Expression Omnibus database provided two COVID-19 datasets22,23: GSE171110 and GSE152418.GSE171110 were generated using the GPL16791 Illumina HiSeq 2500 (Homo sapiens).GSE171110 dataset consisted of 44 COVID-19 samples and 10 Control samples.GSE152418 were generated using the GPL24676 Illumina NovaSeq 6000 (Homo sapiens).GSE152418 dataset consisted of 16 COVID-19 samples and 17 Control samples.

  All GEO data were preprocessed using the R package “GEOquery” (version 2.60.0, accessed 2024–05-18) and retrieved via Bioconductor24.Gene probes were labeled with gene symbols.Probes lacking corresponding gene symbols or matching multiple symbols were excluded.Gene expression value of duplicate gene symbol was calculated as the max value.


### DEGs screening and functional analysis

Protein–protein interaction (PPI) networks were analyzed using Cytoscape (version 3.10.2, accessed 2024–05-23) with the “cytoHubba” plugin.


### Characteristic genes in POAG
  To identify signature genes, we used three machine learning methods: random forests (RF), least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) logistic regression, and support vector machine-recursive feature elimination (SVM-RFE).The random forest technique in this study was implemented using the ‘RF’ package in R.This study employed LASSO logistic regression was implemented using the “glmnet” package (version 4.1–8, accessed 2024–05-21) in R, with tenfold cross-validation, ensuring the partial likelihood deviation met the minimum criteria.The shared genes across the three classification models were chosen for further investigation.The predictive capability of the common genes was assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, with the area under the curve (AUC) calculated for evaluation.


### CeRNA-network construction
  Transcription factors (TFs) are proteins that bind to specific DNA sequences to regulate gene expression.The interactions between common genes and transcription factors were analyzed using NetworkAnalyst 3.0 (https://www.networkanalyst.ca/)27. MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are endogenous short non-coding RNAs that mediate the degradation of target mRNA or inhibit its translation.The shared genes were input into NetworkAnalyst 3.0 to construct a coregulatory network with miRNAs. Assessing protein-drug interactions is crucial for identifying structural characteristics that enhance receptor sensitivity.


### Immune cell abundance
  CIBERSORT analysis was conducted on each disease sample to assess the relative abundance of immune cells.The CIBERSORT algorithm uses gene expression data to deconvolute and determine immune cell composition.The LM22 dataset, as detailed on CIBERSORT’s website (http://cibersort.stanford.edu/), comprises 22 annotated gene signatures28. Immune cell composition was assessed using CIBERSORT (version 1.03, accessed 2024–05-24) with 1000 iterations and the LM22 signature. we quantified 22 immune cell types based on the LM22 gene signature.The results were visualized using the R packages “corplot,” “vioplot,” and “ggplot2”. The relationship between immune infiltrated cells and diagnostic biomarkers in POAG was assessed using nonparametric correlation analysis.


### Analysis of single-cell RNA sequencing
  Single-cell RNA-seq data were processed using Seurat (version 4.0.2, accessed 2024–05-22), with quality control excluding cells with < 200 or > 5000 UMI counts29. Quality control criteria included: excluding cells with total RNA counts outside the range of 200 to 5000, discarding cells with mitochondrial UMI rates exceeding 10%, and removing mitochondrial genes.

  The dataset was normalized using R’s “NormalizeData” function. Principal component analysis was performed on the scaled data, focusing on the top 2000 highly variable genes, and the top 15 principal components were utilized for the Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) algorithm. Cell clusters were identified and annotated using the“SingleR”R package following cluster classification30.The “featureplot” function in R seurat packages is used to show the expression of a specific gene.


### Consensus clustering in COVID-19
  Consensus clustering, employing the k-means method, was used to identify COVID-19 subgroups based on molecular subtypes and common gene expression.The evaluation is limited to a maximum of 10 categories, with 50 iterations conducted for each k.The clustering method utilizes Euclidean distance.The consensus clustering algorithm, implemented via the ‘ConsensuClusterPlus (1.58.0)’ R package, was used to determine the number of clusters.Consensus clustering was performed using the “ConsensusClusterPlus” package (version 1.58.0, accessed 2024–05-25) with k = 3 and 50 iterations31.


### Statistical analysis
  The analytical methods comprised descriptive statistics, intergroup difference tests, multivariate regression analyses, and stratified analyses. Descriptive statistics were used to report the basic characteristics of the study population, with continuous data presented as mean and standard deviation or median and interquartile range (IQR), and categorical data shown as percentages. Baseline characteristic differences of continuous variables across categories were analyzed using t-tests or Mann–Whitney U tests, while categorical variables were assessed via chi-square tests or Fisher’s exact tests. Multivariate-adjusted logistic regression models were employed to estimate the association between baseline COVID-19 and incident POAG, reporting odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Model 1 was unadjusted; Model 2 adjusted for age, smoking, alcohol consumption, and gender; and Model 3 further adjusted for white blood cell count (WBC), blood urea nitrogen (BUN), glucose (GLU), and high-density lipoprotein (HDL) based on Model 2. Additionally, multivariate-adjusted Cox proportional hazards regression models were used to estimate the association between baseline COVID-19 and incident POAG, reporting hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% CIs, with model structures analogous to the logistic regression models (Model 3 additionally adjusted for comorbidities including stroke, hypertension, and dyslipidemia). Stratified analyses were conducted to evaluate the impact of COVID-19 on POAG across subgroups defined by gender (male/female), smoking status (yes/no), and alcohol consumption (yes/no). All statistical analyses were performed using R (Version 4.2.3) and Python (Version 3.11), with statistical significance defined as P < 0.05.

## Results

### Baseline analysis of having or not having primary open-angle glaucoma (POAG)
  This study included 3,015 participants, of whom 30 cases were POAG (1.0%, POAG 1) and 2,985 were non-POAG individuals (99.0%, POAG 0) (Table 1). Baseline analysis showed that among categorical variables, the smoking rate was 46.667% in the POAG 1 group and 34.807% in the POAG 0 group with no significant difference (χ2 = 1.838, P = 0.175), alcohol drinkers accounted for 6.667% in POAG 1 and 6.265% in POAG 0 with no significant difference (χ2 = 0.008, P = 0.928), and males constituted 53.333% of POAG 1 and 44.556% of POAG 0 with no significant difference (χ2 = 0.926, P = 0.336); however, the positivity rate of vb001_s1 was significantly lower in POAG 1 (30.000%) than in POAG 0 (84.556%) with a highly significant difference (χ2 = 65.820, P < 0.001). Among continuous variables, there were no significant differences in age, height, weight, TG, WBC, GLU, HGB and BUN between the two groups (all P > 0.05), while the POAG 1 group had a significantly higher median HDL level (54.440 [48.649, 60.618]) than the POAG 0 group (50.579 [43.629, 57.143]) with a statistically significant difference (Mann–Whitney U = −2.200, P = 0.028).

  This study demonstrates that COVID-19 is an independent risk factor for POAG. Cross-sectional logistic regression and longitudinal Cox regression analyses consistently showed a significant positive association between COVID-19 and POAG risk, with adjusted odds ratios (ORs) ranging from 12.775 to 15.688 and hazard ratios (HRs) from 4.893 to 5.060 (all P < 0.001). Stratified analyses indicated stronger associations in males (OR = 15.054, HR = 5.645) and smokers (OR = 29.828, HR = 9.323), suggesting gender and smoking may modify the effect of COVID-19 on POAG risk.

  Notably, HDL levels were significantly higher in POAG patients (P = 0.028), while the positivity rate of vb001_s1 showed a strong negative association with POAG (P < 0.001), warranting further investigation into their clinical implications.

  These findings provide epidemiological evidence for a link between COVID-19 and POAG, highlighting the need for ophthalmic follow-up in COVID-19 survivors, particularly among male individuals and smokers. Mechanisms such as inflammation, microvascular injury, or intraocular pressure regulation abnormalities may underlie this association, though further research is required to validate these pathways and address potential confounders.

### Screening for candidate diagnostic biomarkers for POAG using machine learning algorithms
  The identification of common genes between POAG and COVID-19 laid a solid foundation for our next step: screening for candidate diagnostic biomarkers for POAG. We hypothesized that the genes at the intersection of POAG-associated and COVID-associated DEGs could be crucial in diagnosing COVID-related POAG patients.

  To identify these potential biomarkers, we utilized three machine-learning methods. First, the LASSO regression algorithm was applied, which identified 6 potential candidate genes from 57 CGs that significantly impacted POAG diagnosis (Fig. 3A-B). This algorithm helped us narrow down the list of genes based on their relevance to the disease. Next, SVM-RFE analysis was carried out, and it identified a model with 17 genes that achieved optimal accuracy (Fig. 3C). This model provided another perspective on the potential biomarker candidates. ## Discussion
  COVID-19 induces systemic inflammation, oxidative stress, and hypoxia—pathophysiological processes increasingly linked to ocular diseases, including POAG38,39. Our findings align with emerging evidence that SARS-CoV-2 infection exacerbates inflammatory cascades, such as the upregulation of TNF-α and IL-6, which are known to promote trabecular meshwork dysfunction and intraocular pressure (IOP) elevation in POAG40,41. For instance, a recent clinical report documented viral RNA in the aqueous humor of a COVID-19 patient with new-onset POAG, directly linking viral infection to ocular inflammation7. Mechanistically, this aligns with ACE2 receptor expression in trabecular meshwork cells, which facilitates SARS-CoV-2 entry and triggers local immune responses42,43.

  The five identified biomarkers (CA12, HBA2, HBB, ECRG4, CEBPD) highlight shared inflammatory and hypoxic pathways. CA12, a carbonic anhydrase involved in acid–base balance, is overexpressed in glaucomatous trabecular meshwork cells44 and correlates with activated CD4 + memory T cells in our analysis. Notably, CA12 upregulation in uveitis45 and diabetic retinopathy underscores its role in ocular inflammation but limits its specificity for POAG-COVID-19 association. Similarly, hemoglobin subunits HBA2 and HBB, implicated in oxidative stress and inflammation46,47, show robust diagnostic efficacy in our models but are also linked to systemic inflammatory disorders48. This non-specificity mirrors challenges in other disease biomarker research, where shared pathways necessitate multi-omics integration to refine specificity49.

  Since its emergence in late 2019, COVID-19 has rapidly evolved into a global pandemic with multisystemic impacts, extending beyond the respiratory tract to induce dysregulated inflammation, oxidative stress, and tissue hypoxia38,39. Central to SARS-CoV-2 pathogenesis is its spike (S) protein, which mediates host cell entry via angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 (ACE2) receptors—critical here, ACE2 is highly expressed in ocular tissues such as the trabecular meshwork, ciliary body, and retina42,43. This ocular ACE2 expression creates a mechanistic bridge: viral binding may disrupt aqueous humor dynamics, induce trabecular meshwork inflammation, and compromise retinal ganglion cell survival—hallmarks of primary open-angle glaucoma (POAG)7,35. For instance, clinical reports have documented SARS-CoV-2 RNA in the aqueous humor of POAG patients, directly linking viral infection to intraocular inflammation and elevated intraocular pressure (IOP)7.

  The“cytokine storm”characteristic of severe COVID-19 further aligns with POAG pathogenesis. Proinflammatory mediators like TNF-α and IL-6, identified in both diseases, promote microglial activation in the optic nerve and trabecular meshwork dysfunction35,40,41. In POAG, TNF-α exacerbates retinal ganglion cell excitotoxicity via Nav1.6 channel upregulation50, while IL-6 drives extracellular matrix remodeling in the trabecular meshwork, increasing outflow resistance and IOP41. Notably, COVID-19 survivors exhibit persistent elevations in these cytokines, potentially explaining the observed long-term POAG risk37,39.

  Oxidative stress, another shared pathway, underscores the COVID-19-POAG link. SARS-CoV-2 infection depletes antioxidant reserves and enhances reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, damaging ocular tissues39,51. In POAG, oxidative stress induces trabecular meshwork cell senescence and autophagy, impairing aqueous humor outflow52,53. This aligns with our finding that POAG patients exhibit higher HDL levels, a lipid biomarker inversely associated with antioxidant capacity44, and lower vb001_s1 positivity—a potential marker of vascular health [Table 1].

  Autoimmune mechanisms may also play a role. The S protein’s antigenic mimicry with human proteins (e.g., heat shock proteins) could trigger autoantibody production54, reminiscent of autoimmune responses proposed in POAG pathogenesis35. For example, autoantibodies against retinal ganglion cell antigens have been detected in POAG patients, suggesting cross-reactive immune damage36.

  Inflammatory cascades represent a pivotal nexus between POAG and COVID-19, with tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) emerging as a key mediator. In POAG, TNF-α binds to TNFR1 receptors on retinal ganglion cells, enhancing Nav1.6 sodium channel activity and driving neuronal hyperexcitability—a process linked to progressive cell death and visual field loss40,50. This mechanism aligns with COVID-19’s systemic inflammation, where TNF-α surge during cytokine storms exacerbates blood-retinal barrier breakdown and intraocular inflammation37,45. Concurrently, nuclear factor kappa-B (NF-κB) activation in trabecular meshwork cells disrupts extracellular matrix (ECM) homeostasis, increasing outflow resistance and intraocular pressure (IOP) in POAG55. In COVID-19, NF-κB dysregulation amplifies viral replication and immune cell infiltration, creating a pro-inflammatory milieu that may synergize with ocular NF-κB pathways39,51.

  Interleukin-6 (IL-6), a pleiotropic cytokine, further bridges the two conditions. In POAG, IL-6 promotes fibroblast proliferation via the STAT3 pathway, enhancing ECM deposition in the trabecular meshwork and contributing to IOP elevation41. Similarly, COVID-19 patients exhibit IL-6-driven hyperinflammation, which correlates with disease severity and multi-organ dysfunction37. This shared IL-6 dependency underscores the role of systemic inflammation in POAG pathogenesis, particularly in COVID-19 survivors with persistent cytokine elevation9.

  Oxidative stress and hypoxia form a complementary pathological axis. SARS-CoV-2 infection impairs mitochondrial function and depletes antioxidant reserves (e.g., glutathione), leading to reactive oxygen species (ROS) overproduction and endothelial damage39,51. In POAG, trabecular meshwork cells under oxidative stress undergo autophagy and senescence, compromising aqueous humor outflow and escalating IOP52,53. Hypoxia, secondary to COVID-19-induced lung injury, exacerbates this cycle by reducing optic nerve blood flow and inducing hypoxia-inducible factor 1α (HIF-1α) activation—a key driver of glaucomatous neurodegeneration39,56. Notably, POAG patients show elevated systemic oxidative stress markers (e.g., malondialdehyde), which correlate with disease progression56, mirroring the oxidative burden observed in severe COVID-1951.

  The interplay of these mechanisms creates a vicious cycle: inflammation triggers ROS production, which in turn activates NF-κB and HIF-1α, perpetuating tissue damage and hypoxia. Our findings of elevated HDL levels in POAG patients [Table 1] may reflect compensatory anti-oxidative responses, yet their association with disease severity warrants further exploration44. Collectively, these data suggest that COVID-19 exacerbates POAG risk by amplifying conserved inflammatory, oxidative, and hypoxic pathways, providing a mechanistic rationale for targeted screening and anti-inflammatory interventions.

  SARS-CoV-2 infects ocular tissues via ACE2 receptors, which are highly expressed in the trabecular meshwork and ciliary body—key structures in POAG pathogenesis42,43. For example, in a clinical case, viral RNA was detected in the aqueous humor of a COVID-19 patient who developed new-onset glaucoma7, directly linking viral presence to ocular pathology. This supports a mechanistic model where SARS-CoV-2 infection triggers local inflammation and IOP elevation, hallmarks of POAG.

  However, the mechanism linking POAG to COVID-19 have not been fully reported. We combined datasets of both diseases for bioinformatics and enrichment analysis to explore potential connections. Our study identified overlapping genes that could serve as therapeutic targets for COVID-19-related POAG. Enrichment analysis indicated that these genes primarily participate in inflammation, oxidative stress, and hypoxia, which are central mechanisms of both diseases. This suggests that COVID-19 may induce the occurrence of POAG through these mechanisms.

  The newly identified associations with “response to virus” and “oxygen transport” in the GO analysis offer novel insights into the shared mechanisms between POAG and COVID-19. The enrichment in “response to virus” implies that the immune response to the SARS-CoV-2 virus might play a role in the development or exacerbation of POAG. Meanwhile, the connection to “oxygen transport” emphasizes the importance of these common genes in the key pathological process of POAG. These findings further support the existence of an underlying link between the two diseases and may guide future research directions.

  Our study identified five potential diagnostic biomarkers (CA12, HBA2, HBB, ECRG4, and CEBPD) for identifying POAG among COVID-19 patients. CA12, HBA2, HBB, and ECRG4 exhibited higher expression in POAG, while CEBPD showed lower expression. Validation tests confirmed CA12, HBA2, and HBB as potential target biomarkers for COVID-19-related POAG. These findings suggest that monitoring these biomarkers in COVID-19 patients could aid in the early detection and treatment of POAG in this population.

  CA12, part of the carbonic anhydrase family that aids CO2 hydration, is involved in respiration, acid–base balance, and aqueous tumor formation57. Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors are primary treatments for POAG, and CA2 knockout has shown long-term IOP reduction58. Research indicates CA12 is overexpressed in glaucoma eye donor cells, suggesting it could be a diagnostic marker and therapeutic target for COVID44. Elevated expression of HBA2 and HBB, both linked to hemoglobin, may increase inflammation and upregulate in response to hypoxia, potentially advancing POAG46,59.HBA2 has pro-inflammatory effects, while HBB is a cytotoxic blood protein causing harmful reactions47,48. The roles of ECRG4 and CEBPD in POAG remain unreported. Research indicates that they may regulate inflammation, potentially linked to POAG progression60,61. Further studies are needed to clarify their specific functions.

  This study suggests a strong correlation between the onset of COVID-associated POAG and inflammatory responses. Our analysis of COVID-19-associated pathogenic genes in POAG revealed a strong link to inflammatory and immune processes. We investigated the association between the expression of five diagnostic biomarkers and infiltrated immune cell types in POAG. CA12 showed a significant positive correlation with activated CD4 memory T cells and plasma cells, while HBA2 was positively correlated with plasma cells. Conversely, HBB exhibited a significant negative correlation with naive B cells, indicating their roles in local immune responses and inflammation in POAG.

  Our findings also indicate that FOXC1 may simultaneously influence CA12, HBA2, and HBB, while STAT3 may similarly affect CEBPD, HBB, and CA12.FOXC1, a transcription factor from the Forkhead/winged helix family, is essential for various embryonic development, especially eye development62. It has been linked to POAG pathogenesis, with a genome-wide association study identifying FOXC1 as a susceptibility locus for the condition. FOXC1 might indirectly impact IOP and optic nerve health by influencing ocular structure development and function63. STAT3 is crucial for cell growth, differentiation, and apoptosis. While not directly linked to POAG, STAT3’s role in immune function and inflammation hints at a possible connection64.These findings require further validation and deeper investigation to elucidate the specific roles of these immune cells and diagnostic biomarkers in the pathogenesis of POAG.

  MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are crucial regulators of gene expression and are associated with glaucoma and other diseases49. In our study, we found that hsa-mir-16-5p and hsa-mir-1-3p may be markers for better diagnosis of glaucoma in the COVID-19 population. Previous studies evaluated mirna and mrna expression profiles of TM samples from control and POAG patients by microarray analysis, indicating that miR-106b-5p and miR-15a-5p were identified as potential biomarkers of POAG in trabecular reticulum, respectively. In addition, miR-16-5p is reduced in expression in COVID-19 patients and can be used as a biomarker to predict length of hospital stay or risk of death65. Hsa-miR-1-3p is a key microRNA that distinguishes patients with severe and mild COVID-19. Its levels rise with increasing disease severity and are associated with longer hospital stays and lower survival rates66. However, we have yet to discover the role of hsa-mir-16-5p and hsa-mir-1-3p in glaucoma. It is well known that mirnas inhibit the transcription of target genes by targeting the UTR region of target genes, and we speculate that our predicted mirnas target the same target genes as some known functional mirnas. This also indicates that the mirnas we discovered are worthy of further investigation.

  It should be noted that our study mainly relied on computational predictions using tools like NetworkAnalyst. Although these computational methods are powerful for hypothesis generation, the regulatory roles of miRNAs, TFs, and the identified biomarkers lack experimental validation. Functional assays, such as miRNA and TF overexpression or knockdown experiments, and analysis of real-world patient data are needed to confirm these findings. In future research, we plan to conduct such experiments to validate our computational predictions and better understand the underlying mechanisms.

  This study delved deeper into COVID-19, identifying five hub genes with high diagnostic efficacy, offering new insights into its mechanisms.Clustering analysis revealed three subtypes of COVID-19 samples. Cluster B showed markedly elevated expression of immune checkpoint and HLA-related genes relative to Clusters A and C. The differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were enriched in immune regulation and inflammatory responses, including pathways like allograft rejection, antigen processing and presentation, natural killer cell-mediated cytotoxicity, T cell receptor signaling, and Th1/Th2 cell differentiation.These findings suggest that Cluster B may benefit more from immunotherapy. Single-cell analysis revealed that the primary cell types in the COVID-19 dataset are T cells, neutrophils, monocytes, natural killer (NK) cells, and platelets. HBA2 and HBB mark bone marrow cells, while CEBPD marks monocytes and neutrophils.The infiltration of immune cells, such as monocytes, macrophages, neutrophils, dendritic cells, NK cells, T cells, and B cells, plays a vital role in the progression of COVID-19. Monocytes and macrophages are crucial in the innate immune response to viral infections and inflammation, whereas NK cells play a role through their cytotoxic activity67,68. CD4 + and CD8 + T cells boost the immune response by identifying viral antigens and triggering cytotoxic and helper T cell activities69,70. B cells support humoral immunity by becoming plasma cells and producing antibodies against the virus71,72. These findings suggest new COVID-19 subtypes and diagnostic biomarkers, offering fresh perspectives on disease mechanisms and treatments stategies.

  The identification of these distinct COVID-19 subtypes through consensus clustering is a significant finding. It not only deepens our understanding of the complex biological processes underlying COVID-19 but also has direct implications for clinical practice. By recognizing the unique gene expression patterns and immune characteristics of each subtype, clinicians can potentially develop more personalized treatment plans. For patients in Cluster B, immunotherapy may be a more promising treatment option, while different strategies may be considered for patients in Clusters A and C. This approach of tailoring treatment based on molecular subtypes could improve treatment outcomes and reduce the risk of adverse effects, ultimately benefiting patients suffering from COVID-19. Future research should focus on validating these findings in larger patient cohorts and exploring the specific therapeutic interventions for each subtype.

  The current study limitations that should be acknowledged. While our study identifies associations, it lacks direct causal evidence. Future studies using SARS-CoV-2 infection models in human trabecular meshwork cells or ACE2-knockout mice will be essential to validate whether viral infection directly drives POAG pathogenesis. Additionally, integrating longitudinal clinical data is needed to confirm the prognostic value of CGs in COVID-19-associated POAG. Therefore, future studies may consider using HTMC to improve the clinical relevance of the model. We acknowledge that further research is needed to confirm the diagnostic value of these genes in COVID-19-related POAG and determine their clinical usefulness.


## Conclusion
  This study identifies a link between POAG and COVID-19, highlighting shared mechanisms like inflammation, oxidative stress, and hypoxia. CA12, HBA2, and HBB are identified as target biomarkers for COVID-19-related POAG, aiding in screening and treatment of high-risk POAG patients with COVID, and exploring the interaction between these two diseases.
