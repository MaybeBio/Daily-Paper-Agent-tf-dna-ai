## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text including abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, and conclusions. Figures, tables, and supplementary material referenced but not provided.
- **Assessment boundary** Assessment is limited to the scientific claims, methodological soundness, and evidentiary support as presented in the supplied text. Experimental validation data, structural coordinates, and simulation parameter details beyond those described are not independently verifiable from the provided material.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The manuscript claims that SARS-CoV-1 and SARS-CoV-2 NSP1 bind to Pol α-primase, with SARS-CoV-2 NSP1 exhibiting stronger binding affinity, and that this binding reduces the interaction between DNA and Pol α-primase, thereby inhibiting DNA replication. The authors further claim that electrostatic interactions, particularly involving specific charged residues and hydrogen bonds, drive the binding differences.
- **Visible evidence base** The evidence base consists of all-atom steered molecular dynamics (SMD) simulations, coarse-grained umbrella sampling (US) simulations, MM-PBSA binding free energy calculations, per-residue interaction energy analyses, and hydrogen bond occupancy analyses. All results are presented as text descriptions with references to figures and tables that were not included in the supplied material.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Figures 1 through 5, Tables 1 through 3, Tables S1 through S3, Figures S1 through S4, and the supplementary material are referenced but not provided. Experimental binding affinity data for SARS-CoV-1 NSP1 to Pol α-primase is mentioned but not shown. Structural coordinates for the constructed SARS-CoV-2 NSP1 complex and docking validation metrics are not provided.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript addresses a timely and potentially important question regarding the molecular mechanism by which SARS-CoV-1 and SARS-CoV-2 NSP1 proteins interfere with host DNA replication through binding to Pol α-primase. The computational approach combining multiple simulation methods is appropriate in principle. However, the manuscript as presented has several significant limitations. The central claims regarding differential binding affinity and the inhibitory effect on DNA replication rest on computational predictions that lack experimental validation. The construction of the SARS-CoV-2 NSP1 complex by mutation from the SARS-CoV-1 structure without subsequent validation is a major concern. The MM-PBSA results, while suggestive, are presented without sufficient methodological detail to assess their reliability. The claim that SARS-CoV-2 poses a greater clinical risk based solely on computed binding free energy differences is an overreach beyond what the data can support. The manuscript would benefit from a more cautious interpretation of the results and acknowledgment of the limitations of the computational methods employed.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in computational biophysics and molecular simulation would be the primary audience, particularly those interested in protein-protein interactions and viral-host interfaces. Structural biologists studying SARS-CoV-2 host factor interactions may also find the predicted binding interface informative. Drug discovery groups targeting NSP1-host interactions could use the identified hot spot residues as a starting point for inhibitor design. However, the lack of experimental validation limits the broader appeal to virologists and cell biologists who would require biochemical confirmation of the predicted effects.
- **Major strengths** The study employs complementary computational methods (all-atom SMD and coarse-grained US) to assess binding affinities, which provides a degree of methodological cross-validation. The per-residue energy decomposition analysis offers mechanistic insight into the binding interface. The comparison of SARS-CoV-1 and SARS-CoV-2 NSP1 binding provides a useful comparative framework. The authors acknowledge the non-equilibrium nature of their SMD simulations and appropriately focus on relative rather than absolute binding affinities.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims that SARS-CoV-2 NSP1 exhibits stronger binding affinity to Pol α-primase compared to SARS-CoV-1 NSP1, based on SMD and US simulations.
  - **Evidence pointer** Figure 1, Table 1, Figure 2, Table 2, Section "Binding Affinity of SARS-CoV-2 NSP1 to Pol α-Primase Is Stronger Than SARS-CoV-1 NSP1: All-Atom SMD Simulations", Section "Binding Affinities of SARS-CoV-1 NSP1 and SARS-CoV-2 NSP1 to Pol α-Primase: Coarse-Grained US Simulations"
  - **Concern** The SARS-CoV-2 NSP1 complex was constructed by mutating the SARS-CoV-1 NSP1 structure from PDB 7OPL using PyMOL. This approach assumes that the binding mode is conserved between the two viruses and that the mutation does not induce conformational changes in the binding interface. No structural validation of the mutated complex is described, such as equilibration stability analysis, interface packing assessment, or comparison with any experimentally determined SARS-CoV-2 NSP1 complex structure. The binding free energy difference between the two complexes could be an artifact of the modeling procedure rather than a genuine biological difference. Additionally, the SMD simulations used only 5 trajectories per system, which is a small sample size for reliable free energy estimates, particularly for systems of approximately 40,000 atoms. The authors acknowledge this limitation for the absolute free energy values but claim the relative comparison is reliable, yet this assertion is not rigorously justified.
  - **Why it matters** The central conclusion of the manuscript, that SARS-CoV-2 NSP1 binds more strongly to Pol α-primase and therefore poses a greater risk of inhibiting DNA replication, depends entirely on the reliability of the comparative binding affinity assessment. If the modeled SARS-CoV-2 complex is not representative of the true structure, the entire comparative analysis is compromised.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should validate the modeled SARS-CoV-2 NSP1 complex through extended equilibration with RMSD and interface stability analysis, compare the modeled interface with any available experimental structures or cross-linking data, increase the number of SMD trajectories to demonstrate convergence of the free energy estimates, and ideally perform independent binding affinity calculations using a different method such as umbrella sampling with the all-atom model or free energy perturbation.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims that both SARS-CoV-1 NSP1 and SARS-CoV-2 NSP1 reduce DNA binding to Pol α-primase, with the order of binding strength being Pol α-primase-DNA < SARS1 NSP1-Pol α-primase-DNA < SARS2 NSP1-Pol α-primase-DNA, suggesting impaired DNA synthesis.
  - **Evidence pointer** Table 3, Section "Binding of DNA to Pol α-Primase Is Reduced by Both SARS-CoV-1 NSP1 and SARS-CoV-2 NSP1, with SARS-CoV-2 NSP1 Exhibiting a More Pronounced Inhibitory Effect"
  - **Concern** The claim that NSP1 binding reduces DNA binding to Pol α-primase is based solely on MM-PBSA calculations performed on three complexes. The manuscript does not describe how the DNA-containing complexes were constructed, whether the DNA binding site overlaps with the NSP1 binding site, or whether the simulations were long enough to allow for conformational rearrangements. The MM-PBSA method is known to be sensitive to the choice of dielectric constants, entropy estimation methods, and the conformational ensemble used. The manuscript states that snapshots from the last 130 ns were used, but the total simulation length and convergence criteria are not specified. Most critically, there is no experimental validation of the predicted reduction in DNA binding affinity. The claim that this reduction "suggests impaired DNA synthesis" is an extrapolation from binding free energy calculations to a functional cellular outcome, which requires experimental support.
  - **Why it matters** The title of the manuscript makes a definitive claim that NSP1 inhibits DNA replication through reduction of DNA interaction with Pol α-primase. This is the central functional claim of the work. If the MM-PBSA calculations are not robust or the complexes are not properly constructed, the title claim is not supported by the evidence presented.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should provide details of the complex construction, validate that the DNA binding site is accessible and properly positioned in the presence of NSP1, perform longer simulations with convergence analysis, test the sensitivity of the MM-PBSA results to methodological choices, and ideally provide experimental validation such as electrophoretic mobility shift assays or surface plasmon resonance showing reduced DNA binding in the presence of NSP1.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Scientific importance
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims that the findings "lay the groundwork for innovative strategies aimed at inhibiting the interaction between these entities, offering promising avenues for therapeutic intervention against COVID-19."
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract, Section "Conclusions"
  - **Concern** The therapeutic relevance of targeting the NSP1-Pol α-primase interaction is not well established. The manuscript does not discuss whether inhibition of this interaction would be beneficial or detrimental in the context of COVID-19. NSP1 is known to inhibit host translation, and its interaction with Pol α-primase may be one of several mechanisms by which it modulates host cell biology. It is unclear whether disrupting this specific interaction would have therapeutic value, particularly given that the manuscript itself notes that the functional significance of NSP1 binding to Pol α-primase in viral pathogenesis remains elusive. The claim of therapeutic promise is therefore speculative and not supported by the data presented.
  - **Why it matters** The stated motivation for the study is to advance development of small molecule inhibitors for COVID-19 treatment. If the therapeutic rationale is not clearly established, the significance of the work is diminished.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should either provide a more nuanced discussion of the potential therapeutic implications, acknowledging the uncertainties, or cite experimental evidence that disruption of NSP1-Pol α-primase interaction would have antiviral effects. Alternatively, the therapeutic claims should be tempered to match the level of evidence provided.

- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 and R1-M2 are blocking concerns. The construction of the SARS-CoV-2 NSP1 complex by mutation without validation undermines the comparative binding affinity claims. The MM-PBSA-based claim of reduced DNA binding requires more rigorous methodological detail and ideally experimental validation. Without addressing these issues, the central conclusions of the manuscript are not established from the provided evidence.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: The application of SMD and US simulations to compare SARS-CoV-1 and SARS-CoV-2 NSP1 binding to Pol α-primase is a novel contribution, though the general approach of computational binding affinity assessment is well established. Scientific importance: The question of how NSP1 interferes with host DNA replication is of interest, but the manuscript does not establish the functional significance of the predicted differential binding. The clinical implications are speculative. Interdisciplinary readership: The manuscript is written primarily for computational biophysicists and would have limited accessibility to virologists or cell biologists without additional context on the biological significance. Technical soundness: The methods are appropriate in principle, but the lack of validation for the constructed SARS-CoV-2 complex and the limited sampling in SMD simulations are significant technical concerns. Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript assumes familiarity with molecular dynamics terminology and would benefit from clearer explanations of the methods and their limitations for a broader audience.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The manuscript addresses an interesting question with an appropriate computational toolkit, but the central claims require additional validation and methodological rigor. The authors should address the blocking concerns regarding complex construction and the MM-PBSA-based functional claims before the case for differential binding affinity and inhibited DNA replication can be considered established.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that SARS-CoV-2 poses a greater risk than SARS-CoV-1 in impeding DNA replication is an extrapolation from computed binding free energy differences to clinical outcomes, which is not supported by the data presented.
- The claim that Asp33 in SARS-CoV-2 NSP1 "shows increased solubility and stability" compared to SARS-CoV-1 NSP1 is not supported by any quantitative metric provided in the text.
- The claim that the findings "lay the groundwork for innovative strategies aimed at inhibiting the interaction" is speculative and not substantiated by specific proposals or evidence of therapeutic relevance.
- The comparison of the computed binding free energy for SARS-CoV-1 NSP1 to an experimental value of -6.82 kcal/mol is mentioned but the source of this experimental value is not provided, making the comparison unverifiable.
- The manuscript states that the all-atom SMD and coarse-grained US results are "consistent" but does not provide a quantitative comparison of the two methods or a discussion of potential systematic differences between them.
