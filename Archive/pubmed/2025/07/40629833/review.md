## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and conclusions as presented in the abstract; no evaluation of full methodology, data, or statistical analyses
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report that N(2)-furfuryl and N(2)-cinnamyl deoxyguanosine modifications at G-tetrad positions of the G-quadruplex-forming oligonucleotide T40214 preserve the parallel G4 conformation, enhance thermal stability when placed in the top G-quartet, maintain 5'-5' dimer formation, and confer potent antiproliferative and pro-apoptotic activity in DU145 prostate cancer cells via Stat3 pathway suppression.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no figures, tables, methods, or supplementary data provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, experimental methods, raw data, statistical analyses, structural characterization data (e.g., CD, NMR, melting curves), molecular dynamics simulation details, cellular assay protocols, and any control experiments

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a potentially interesting chemical biology approach to stabilizing G-quadruplex-forming oligonucleotides targeting STAT3, with plausible structural and cellular outcomes. However, the evidence base is limited to the abstract, and several claims regarding mechanism, specificity, and therapeutic potential cannot be independently evaluated. The work may be of interest to researchers in nucleic acid chemistry, G-quadruplex biology, and cancer therapeutics, but the current evidence does not establish the case beyond reasonable doubt.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers working on G-quadruplex structure and stability, nucleic acid chemical modification, STAT3 signaling in cancer, and oligonucleotide-based therapeutics would find these results relevant. The combination of chemical modification with functional cellular outcomes addresses both structural and translational questions.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses a clinically relevant target (STAT3) with a chemically modified G4-forming oligonucleotide, which is a relatively underexplored strategy. The observation that single modifications at specific positions enhance thermal stability while preserving conformation and dimerization is mechanistically informative. The inclusion of molecular dynamics simulations to rationalize stability changes adds a computational layer that strengthens the structural narrative.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
- **Claim pointer** "A single N(2)-furfuryl/cinnamyl modification preserved the stable parallel G4 conformation" and "Incorporating either of these modifications into the top G-quartet (TF15 and TC15) significantly enhanced thermal stability."
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; no figures or tables provided
- **Concern** The abstract states that the modifications preserve the parallel G4 conformation and enhance thermal stability, but no quantitative data (e.g., melting temperatures, CD spectra) are presented. Without these data, the claims cannot be verified.
- **Why it matters** The structural and stability claims are foundational to the entire study. If the modifications do not reliably preserve the G4 fold or if the stability enhancement is marginal, the downstream biological results lose their mechanistic basis.
- **Resolution test** Provide melting temperature values with error bars, CD spectra showing parallel topology, and appropriate controls (e.g., unmodified T40214) in the full manuscript.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Specificity and mechanism
- **Claim pointer** "TF15 and TC15 derivatives exhibited potent antiproliferative activity (IC(50) = 0.37-0.39 muM) and effectively induced apoptosis while suppressing the Stat3-mediated gene expressions in DU145 cells."
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; no experimental details or controls described
- **Concern** The abstract claims potent antiproliferative activity and Stat3 pathway suppression, but does not specify how specificity for STAT3 was established. It is unclear whether the effects are due to on-target STAT3 inhibition or off-target cytotoxicity from the modified oligonucleotides.
- **Why it matters** Without evidence of target specificity, the therapeutic potential of these compounds is not established. Off-target effects could explain the observed antiproliferative activity, undermining the central claim of a STAT3-targeting mechanism.
- **Resolution test** Include experiments showing STAT3 DNA-binding inhibition, knockdown or overexpression controls, and comparison with a scrambled or non-G4-forming oligonucleotide control.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** No
- **Axis** Mechanistic interpretation
- **Claim pointer** "Molecular dynamics simulation studies revealed that the aryl moieties were well accommodated at the 5'-ends without disrupting the interaction with Stat3. It is also evident that H-bonding and pi-pi stacking interactions induced due to the presence of the aryl moieties contributed to the improved thermal stabilities for TF15 and TC15, respectively."
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; no simulation details or interaction analyses provided
- **Concern** The molecular dynamics simulations are used to rationalize the stability differences, but the abstract does not provide details on simulation length, force field, or how the interaction with STAT3 was modeled. The claim that H-bonding and pi-pi stacking contribute to stability is plausible but not quantitatively supported.
- **Why it matters** The mechanistic explanation for the enhanced stability is a key intellectual contribution. If the simulation methodology is not robust or the interaction analysis is superficial, the proposed mechanism may be speculative.
- **Resolution test** Provide simulation parameters, convergence criteria, and quantitative interaction energy analyses (e.g., per-residue decomposition) in the full manuscript.

- **Concern ID** R1-M4
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** No
- **Axis** Translational relevance
- **Claim pointer** "Overall, these findings demonstrate the potential of these aryl modifications in T40214 as a promising Stat3-targeting therapeutic approach for prostate cancers."
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; no in vivo or pharmacokinetic data
- **Concern** The abstract extrapolates from in vitro cellular data to therapeutic potential. No evidence of in vivo efficacy, bioavailability, or safety is provided, and the IC50 values, while potent, are not contextualized against existing STAT3 inhibitors or G4-based agents.
- **Why it matters** The claim of therapeutic promise is a significant overreach based solely on cellular assays. Without in vivo validation or comparison to standard-of-care agents, the translational significance remains speculative.
- **Resolution test** Include in vivo tumor model studies, pharmacokinetic assessment, or at minimum a comparative analysis with known STAT3 inhibitors in the same cellular system.

### Minor Comments

- **Concern ID** R1-m2

- **Concern ID** R1-m3

- **Axis** Data presentation

- **Issue** The IC50 range is presented without error estimates or the number of replicates. It is also unclear whether this range represents variability across compounds or experimental runs.
- **Required Correction** Report IC50 values with standard deviations and specify the number of independent experiments.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that the modifications "significantly enhanced thermal stability" is unsupported without quantitative melting temperature data.
- The claim that the compounds "effectively induced apoptosis" is unsupported without apoptosis assay details (e.g., annexin V staining, caspase activity).
- The claim that the compounds suppress "Stat3-mediated gene expressions" is unsupported without specifying which genes were measured and how.
- The claim of "promising Stat3-targeting therapeutic approach" is unsupported without in vivo or comparative efficacy data.
- The molecular dynamics-based mechanistic explanation for stability enhancement is not evaluable without simulation details.
