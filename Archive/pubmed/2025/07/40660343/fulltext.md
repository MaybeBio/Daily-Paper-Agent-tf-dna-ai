# Identification and analysis of pyroptosis-related key genes in heart failure


## Background
  Pyroptosis plays a pivotal role in the pathogenesis of Heart Failure (HF). However, the current understanding of how pyroptosis-related genes (PRGs) influence HF is scarce. This study aimed to explore the link between PRGs and HF based on bioinformatics.


## Methods
  Three datasets of HF were involved in this study.Candidate genes were identified by overlapping two sets of genes. The first set consisted of differentially expressed genes from differential expression analysis. The second set included critical module genes from weighted gene co-expression network analysis. Further, the key genes were screened based on machine learning algorithms. Furthermore, immune infiltration analysis and mRNA–Transcription factor (TF)/drug regulatory networks construction were implemented. Ultimately, we also verified the expression of key genes.


## Results
  In this study, we pinpointed seven key genes (SNORD76, RPS3A, SNORD1A, CCDC159, AMT, RANBP6, and CRAT) exhibiting superior diagnostic potential in HF. We found five distinct immune cell types to be significantly associated with these key genes. Moreover, CRAT and AMT were subject to regulation by PHF8. Additionally, AMT, RPS3A, and CRAT corresponded to eight potential therapeutic drugs. Importantly, the expression of CCDC159, CRAT, and AMT was consistent with the dataset.


## Conclusion
  We identified the seven key genes that were intimately associated with HF, offering novel insights into the therapeutic targets for HF.

## Introduction
  The symptoms of heart failure (HF) are breathing issues, swelling in the lower limbs, and other consequences caused by structural or functional abnormalities of the heart [1, 2]. HF is generally caused by left ventricular dysfunction due to myocardial interstitial fibrosis [3]. The incidence and mortality rates of HF are very high, particularly in the elderly [4]. At present, cardiac magnetic resonance imaging is a common diagnostic method for HF. It assesses myocardial activity by observing myocardial tissue to help in diagnosing the severity of myocardial lesions [5]. In recent years, although the clinical treatment methods for HF have improved, the mortality rate has not been significantly reduced. Nearly one-third of patients cannot survive after HF diagnosis and treatment [6]. Therefore, searching for potentially useful biomarkers of HF is of major biological value for understanding the mechanisms of HF and facilitating its clinical diagnosis.

  Pyroptosis, also known as cellular inflammatory necrosis, is an inflammatory form of cell death caused by some inflammasomes [7]. By removing some damaged cells and causing some inflammatory reactions, this process can shield cells from internal infection and eliminate microorganisms [8]. Gasdermin-mediated pyroptosis is a typical innate immune response, which can cause some changes in its immune microenvironment by activating inflammasomes [9]. Studies have reported that pyroptosis plays an important role in various cardiovascular diseases; therefore, some new therapeutic methods have been developed based on pyroptosis regulation [10]. In the clinic, targeted drug therapy, which blocks NLRP3 inflammasome assembly and gasdermin D (GSDMD)-induced pyroptosis through small molecules, has shown promising results in trials [11]. In addition, the expression changes of pyroptosis-related genes ( PRGs ) in calcific aortic valve disease ( CAVD ), especially the association with inflammatory response and immune cell infiltration, may play an important role in the occurrence and development of CAVD [12] ). Xin Tang et al.showed that the differential expression of PRGs in hypertrophic cardiomyopathy ( HCM ) may promote the occurrence and progression of HCM by affecting biological processes such as cardiomyocyte death and inflammatory response [13]. Considering the relationship between PRGs and heart disease, personalized treatment for PRGs expression and molecular pathways is of great value in improving the quality of life of HF patients. However, in addition to the effect of lncRNA / circRNA-miRNA-mRNA regulatory network on programmed cell death [14, 15], the current research on the role of PRGs in HF is still limited.

  In this study, key biomarkers closely related to pyroptosis score and HF were screened by bioinformatics analysis. At the same time, combined with these markers, the immune microenvironment of HF and its regulatory mechanism were discussed. This study provides a potential new target gene for the diagnosis and treatment of HF, and provides theoretical support for its mechanism research and clinical treatment.


## Methods

### Data sources
  RNA-sequencing (RNA-seq) datasets of HF were acquired from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/), and the datasets included in this study have to meet the following criteria: (1) Disease keyword: heart failure; (2) The organism of samples is Homo sapiens; (3) Expression profiling by array; (4) dataset including HF samples and controls; (5) The dataset contains more than 3 samples per group. Data were downloaded via the GEOquery package (v 2.73.4) [16] and the need for logarithmic transformation was determined based on LogC and quartiles. Duplicate gene symbols were removed using distinct and genes containing missing values were filtered out by na.omit to ensure data integrity. Finally, sample grouping was redefined using ifelse to ensure accuracy and consistency of grouping. GSE59867 [17] dataset contained peripheral blood samples of 46 controls and 34 patients with HF, and Gene expression data of which was normalized using the normalizeBetweenArrays function in limma (v 3.44.3) [18] package. The GSE59867 dataset met the screening criteria set in this study, had a sufficient sample size, and the data had been standardized, making it an important dataset for analysis. Information on genes with MAD values in the top 75% of this dataset was shown in Supplementary Table 1. The GSE26887 [19] dataset contained left ventricular tissue samples of 5 controls and 12 patients with HF and no standardized treatment was performed in GSE26887. Principal component analysis (PCA) and boxplot were plotted to examine the expression of each sample in GSE59867 and GSE26887, respectively. Then the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) within GSE59867 and GSE26887 were overlapped for subsequent analysis. Moreover, the GSE57345 [20] dataset including left ventricular tissue samples of 136 controls and 95 patients with HF was used as the validation set. Besides, 33 PRGs (Supplementary Table 2) were acquired from previous studies [18, 21]. Details of the datasets used in this study were given in Table 1.

### Differential expression analysis and enrichment analysis
  In this study, DEGs between control and HF groups in the GSE59867 and GSE26887 datasets were obtained using limma (v 3.44.3) [22] package (threshold:|logFC| >0 and adjusted p < 0.05 were set for incorporating additional genes that may be missed.|logFC| >0 can screen out all expression-altered genes and is suitable for exploratory studies. Combined with functional enrichment analysis or experimental validation, it helps to explore potential targets. To control the false-positive rate, the Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) method is usually used to adjust the false discovery rate (FDR) [23], and adjusted p < 0.05 is the standard threshold for transcriptome analysis, which has high reliability). Heat maps and volcano maps of DEGs were plotted using pheatmap (v 0.7.7) [24] and ggplot2 (v 3.3.2) [25] packages, respectively. Furthermore, upregulated DEGs in the GSE59867 and GSE26887 datasets were crossed to search for the crossover upregulated DEGs common to peripheral blood and contained left ventricular tissues with HF, as did in the downregulated DEGs. Besides, to investigate the related biological functions and pathways of the above crossover upregulated and downregulated DEGs, the Gene ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses were performed using clusterProfiler (v 3.2-3) package [26] (adjusted p < 0.05 and minGsize = 2). In addition, a protein-protein interaction (PPI) network was created using the STRING (https://string-db.org/) online database to analyze the interaction between the overlapping DEGs (Default confidence = 0.4).


### Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA)
  Based on the expression of 33 pyroptosis-related genes, the gene set variation analysis (GSVA) score of each sample in GSE59867 was computed using GSVA package (v 1.34.0) [27], and WGCNA analysis was further performed on gene expression matrix in GSE59867 to screen the critical modules with pyroptosis score as a trait using WGCNA (v 1.70-3) package [28], where we selected genes with a median absolute deviation (MAD) value of the top 75% for subsequent analysis. First, outlier samples with the connectivity threshold Z.k < -2.5 were eliminated to secure the precision of the analysis by hierarchical clustering [29]. An appropriate soft threshold (β) was selected to ensure that the engagement between genes conforms to the scale-free distribution to the maximum extent. Further, according to the standard of mixed dynamic shear tree, the minimum number of genes per gene module was set to 100 [30]. MEDissThres was set to 0.25 to merge similar modules analyzed by the dynamic shear tree algorithm [31]. Subsequently, the correlation analysis was performed to evaluate the relationship between module genes and the traits including patients with HF, control, and pyroptosis GSVA score (pyroptosis) (|cor| >3 and p < 0.01). Finally, module membership (MM) and gene significance (GS) were considered for further screening critical module genes (threshold:|GS| >0.3 and|MM| >0.3). Genes with a default correlation coefficient greater than 0.3 were used for subsequent analysis.


### Screening and validation of key genes
  The candidate genes were identified by the intersection of the overlapping DEGs and critical module genes. Support vector machine–recursive feature elimination (SVM-RFE) and least absolute shrinkage and selector operation (LASSO) analyses were performed to screen the candidate key genes, respectively, and the key genes were crossed. SVM-RFE visualized each one of the RFE iterations and identification of the most relevant predictors with 5-fold cross-validation [32, 33]. LASSO (5-fold cross-validation) is a shrinkage estimation method that aims to minimize the sum of squared residuals under the constraint that the absolute sum of regression coefficients is less than a constant, which shrinks coefficients and makes some coefficients strictly equal to zero and leads to an interpretable model [34]. K-fold cross-validation [35] reduces the risk of overfitting and underfitting and provides a more stable assessment of model performance. In the feature selection process, the best regularization parameter (lambda) is determined through cross-validation, redundant features are removed, and only non-zero coefficients associated with the target variable are retained, thus reducing overfitting and improving the generalization ability of the model.

  In addition, to verify the credibility of the key genes in the GSE59867 dataset and external validation set GSE57345, a random forest (RF) model was created, and the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves of the model were plotted. RF is a supervised learning method based on decision trees, which improves classification accuracy by sampling objects and variables, generating multiple decision trees and summarizing their classification results, and finally selecting the plurality category as the prediction result. Besides, the expression level of key genes in GSE59867 and GSE57345 datasets was assessed using the Wilcoxon test.

  Next, a diagnostic nomogram based on key genes was constructed in the training (GSE59867) dataset via the ‘RMS’ package (version 6.6-0) [36] to predict the risk of HF, where the expression level of each gene corresponds to a single score (Points), and the sum of each score corresponds to a total score (Total Points). The diagnostic ability of all genes was converted into the Total Points of the nomogram, and the accuracy and discriminating ability of the nomogram for HF prediction were assessed by the calibration curves, ROC curve, and decision curve analysis (DCA) curve.


### Analysis of immune infiltration and regulatory network
  To obtain the proportion of 22 types of immune cells in each sample, the cell-type identification by estimating relative subsets of RNA transcripts (CIBERSORT) algorithm was performed on the immune cells between the HF and control samples in the GSE59867 dataset (p < 0.05) [37]. Subsequently, the Wilcoxon test was performed to analyze the significant differences in the immune cells between HF and control groups. The relationships between key genes and differential immune cells were assessed using Spearman’s correlation analysis. In addition, based on the screened key genes, using the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) online database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/), the types of key genes were searched. Besides, the transcription factors (TFs) of key genes were predicted according to NetworkAnalyst [38]online database (https://www.networkanalyst.ca/NetworkAnalyst/), and mRNA–TF regulatory networks were created. GeneMANIA [39] website (http://genemania.org) was used to construct a PPI network combined with Cytoscape [40] for observing the function hypothesis of key genes.


### Potential therapeutic drugs
  For obtaining the potential therapeutic drugs against HF, the DrugBank [41] online database (www.drugbank.ca) was used to search the key genes. Subsequently, a table of drug-target interactions was downloaded and all drugs were sorted by score. The first step in drug screening was to select approved drugs, followed by references to the relevant literature to screen for drugs that have been shown to be useful in the treatment or relief of HF.


### Reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) verification
  The blood samples were obtained from patients with consent from the Department of Cardiovascular Internal Medicine, Shanxi Bethune Hospital. In total, 7 samples each from patients with HF and controls were obtained. Total RNA from the HF group was isolated using TRIzol (Ambion) reagent as per the manufacturer’s instructions. The RNA concentration was assessed using the NanoPhotometer N50. The RNA was reverse transcribed using SureScript First strand cDNA synthesis kit (Servicebio). The cDNA was diluted 5–20 times with ddH2O (RNase/DNase free) and subjected to polymerase chain reaction (PCR) on the CFX96 real-time quantitative PCR instrument. The PCR program was as follows: 1 min at 95 ℃ (pre-denaturation), followed by 40 cycles of 95 ℃ for 20 s (denaturation), 55 ℃ for 20 s (annealing), and 72 ℃ for 30 s (elongation). Moreover, 165 TFs and 255 regulatory relationship pairs were obtained. ## Discussion
  At present, HF has become a common medical problem worldwide [42], affecting about 1 -2% of adults [2]. It is regarded as an epidemic and is usually a late manifestation of the development of heart disease. Almost all cardiovascular diseases, such as myocardial infarction, cardiomyopathy, and myocardial injury caused by inflammation, alter the structure and function of the myocardium, eventually leading to a decrease in ventricular pumping and / or filling function [43]. Therefore, it is of great clinical significance to study the molecular mechanism of cardiomyocyte death for the prevention and treatment of HF.

  Studies have shown that pyroptosis signaling pathway plays an important role in HF pathology [10, 44]. In the HF mouse model with preserved ejection fraction, the inflammatory disorder of Epicardial adipose tissue ( EAT ) -myocardial function may interact with inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis pathway activation [45]. In addition, the HF process caused by diabetic cardiomyopathy, pathological cardiac hypertrophy and acute myocardial infarction is also closely related to the changes of pyroptosis-related proteins [46–48]. However, there is still a lack of systematic research on the regulation of pyroptosis signaling pathway and key genes related to the diagnosis of Heart Failure with preserved Ejection Fraction ( HFpEF ). Therefore, this study used a variety of bioinformatics analysis methods to explore the effects of pyroptosis-related genes on cardiomyocytes.

  In our study, a total of 182 DEGs were screened in the GSE59867 and GSE26887 datasets. Moreover, GO enrichment analysis showed that many genes were related to' endoplasmic reticulum ‘,' lipid metabolism' and' peroxide ‘. KEGG analysis further showed that DEGs were enriched in pathways such as ‘fatty acid metabolism ‘,' protein export ‘, and ‘steroid hormone biosynthesis studies have shown that uncontrolled endoplasmic reticulum stress can activate inflammasomes, which in turn leads to endothelial dysfunction [49]. In Jun Ren et al.' s study, long-term endoplasmic reticulum stress can lead to cell defects, which in turn affect cardiovascular function [50]. Further studies have found that endoplasmic reticulum stress may lead to pyroptosis and eventually lead to cell death [51]. Oleic acid, as a monounsaturated omega-9 fatty acid, has been shown to significantly alleviate pyroptosis [52]. In addition, the heart needs fatty acids to maintain its activity, and changes in fatty acid metabolism may affect the structure and function of the heart [53]. The results of GO and KEGG enrichment analysis showed that in the case of HF, pyroptosis-related biological processes may be involved in ventricular tissue.

  Our study obtained seven key genes (SNORD76, RPS3A, SNORD1A, CCDC159, AMT, RANBP6, and CRAT) involved in HF through the screening of differential module genes. Nomogram generated with the expression of these key genes exhibited an excellent performance for clinically predicting the risk of HF, but further validation of their effectiveness for clinical application is needed.

  Carnitine O-acyltransferase (CRAT) was crucial in cardiomyocytes, primarily promoting fatty acid oxidation by regulating the role of carnitine, which facilitated the shift from fatty acid oxidation to glucose oxidation. This process depended on the activity of cytoplasmic CrAT and reverse CrAT [54]. Additionally, CRAT influenced the immune response of cardiomyocytes by regulating cholesterol metabolism and bile acid synthesis, thereby participating in cardiac inflammation and the progression of HF [55]. Previous studies had reported that fatty acid oxidation could activate mitochondrial damage and the cytosolic DNA sensor cGAS-STING pathway, leading to pyroptosis in cardiomyocytes [56]. This suggested that the increased expression of CRAT in HF might have overactivated the pyroptosis signaling by regulating the fatty acid oxidation process.

  Ribosomal protein S3A (RPS3A) is a multifunctional molecule that was positively correlated with most immune checkpoint molecules examined in a study and negatively correlated with tumor immune cell infiltration [57]. The significance of RPS3A as an immune-cell-related biomarker also was reported [58]. In the PPI network in this study, RPS3A was in the core group of the network, which might be most closely related to pyroptosis. However, there are few reports on the specific mechanism of RPS3A in pyroptosis. Considering the close correlation between RPS3A and immune cells, we speculate that it may mainly target immune cells and innate immunity function to regulate the expression of pyroptosis-related proteins in HF [59]. In addition, studies have shown that RPS3 A is a key factor in LPS-triggered pro-inflammatory signal transduction, suggesting that RPS3 A may be a new target for anti-inflammatory treatment [60]( PMID: 29169044 ). Therefore, it is speculated that RPS3 A may play an important role in HF-related inflammatory mechanisms. On the other hand, studies between other proteins interacting with RPS3A and pyroptosis have been reported. For example, Zhang X et al.identified RNA-cyclase-like 1 ( RCL1 ) as a characteristic gene of pyroptosis in IgA nephropathy [61]. In addition, Angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 ( ACE2 ) in the same network is involved in LPS-induced acute lung injury pyroptosis through the ACE2 / Ang ( 1–7 ) / Mas axis [62]. Studies have also shown that the recovery of ACE2 can reduce pyroptosis and apoptosis in mouse cerebral vessels [63]. Lingchuan et al.found that High-temperature requirement A serine peptidase 1 ( HTRA1 ) promotes NLRP1 inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis cell death through the miR-3-141p / HTRA3 axis [64]. It has also been reported that CD274 is a marker of pyroptosis and other cell death, and is associated with copper metabolism [65–67]. In summary, we speculate that RPS3A is also closely related to pyroptosis. In addition to its role in the PPI network, RPS3A was considered to positively regulate mitochondrial function in the adipose tissue around the human aorta, which has been reported in cardiovascular diseases [68].

  Aminomethyl transferase (AMT) is present in all living organisms. Ammonia and its conjugated ion (ammonium) are transported through the cell membrane by proteins in the AMT/Rh family [69]. A study on the intestinal tract and cardiovascular system of mice reported that the application of ammonium compounds often reduced the inflammatory damage caused by pyroptosis [70, 71]. It suggested that AMT might play a protective role in inhibiting myocardial pyroptosis and alleviating inflammation in HF.

  In HF, the effect of CCDC159, RanBP6, SNORD76, and SNORD1A on the focal death of myocardial cells is still unknown. Located on human chromosome 19, CCDC159 encoded a coil domain protein, and the coiled helix (CC) serves as an oligomerization domain for various proteins, including structural proteins, motor proteins, and transcription factors [72]. At present, reports on the function and influence of this gene are scarce. However, according to the structure, it was speculated that the coil domain could mediate the modification of SMC chromosome protein [73], which might have potential implications in cell cycle regulation. In addition, the structural protein appeared to inhibit actin binding [74, 75], which might play a potential regulatory role in the transfer and activation of inflammasomes. RanBP6 could promote nuclear translocation of STAT3 and inhibit EGFR transcription through STAT3 [76], which might hinder the repair process of damaged myocardial cells. Both SNORD76 and SNORD1A belong to the C/D box snoRNA [77]. It could guide the methylation modification of other RNAs such as ribosomal RNA, transfer RNA, and small nuclear RNA [78, 79], and then directly affect the gene expression as well as protein translation of various immune cells and involved immune responses [80]. Li MW et al. reported that SNORD1A mainly affected nucleotide metabolism, RNA polymerase, oxidative phosphorylation, and other pathways and regulated cell cycle through interaction with P53 [81]. However, currently, the research on snoRNA is still in the initial stage. The role of these two snoRNAs in pyroptosis at the onset of HF is still unknown, which still needs to be explored in subsequent studies.

  A severe inflammatory reaction is often observed after pyroptosis. In our study, analysis of immune infiltration revealed that these inflammatory responses were most likely achieved by Tregs. It is generally believed that Tregs are involved in immune regulation and tissue repair in the pathophysiology of HF [82]. Inhibition of excessive inflammatory response and promotion of stable scar formation in the early stage of cardiac injury is beneficial for the heart [83]. Besides, the majority of Treg subtypes belong to the CD4 + T helper (Th) cell lineage [84]. It was found that the activity of carnitine acetyltransferase in skeletal muscle was positively correlated with the oxidative function and metabolism of CD4 + T cells [85]. In addition, the abnormal down-regulation of RPS3A expression in nonalcoholic fatty liver may be negatively correlated with CD4 + T cell infiltration [58]. At the same time, knockout of RPS3A significantly impaired the mitochondrial function of mature adipocytes in mouse periaortic adipose tissue, increasing the risk of coronary artery disease [68]. It was found that SNORA38B promoted the infiltration of CD4 + FOXP3 + Treg cells by promoting cell proliferation and migration and inducing IL-10 secretion [86]. However, the research on the mechanism of SNORD76, SNORD1A and immune cells is very limited. Our results showed that CRAT was significantly up-regulated in HF, while RPS3A, SNORD76 and SNORD1A were significantly down-regulated. In addition, the Treg cell infiltration score in the HF group was significantly higher than that in the control group. These findings provide new evidence for the association between CD4 + FOXP3 + Treg cell infiltration and key genes, but further studies are needed to validate this relationship.

  On the other hand, the present study found that the expression of these key genes was down-regulated, suggesting that the pyroptosis process in HF patients might have been inhibited. According to relevant research reports, the exacerbation of pyroptosis was closely associated with the progression of HF, particularly through the release of IL-1β and IL-18, which promoted the production of tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) and inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS) [87] Furthermore, when caspase-1 is activated by TNF-α and iNOS, an inflammatory cascade is created that promotes cellular death and ultimately results in the loss of myocardial cells [88]. In addition, during HF, constant inflammatory stimulation leads to collagen accumulation and fibrosis in the heart muscle, which exacerbates HF [89]. Sandanger et al. reported that NLRP inflammasome has a surprising protective effect in the ischemia/reperfusion model during pyroptosis [90]. Unlike myocardial infarction, the overall rate of myocardial cell death in HF is quite low [91], which might also be one of the reasons for the different views on HF caused by pyroptosis. Therefore, the down-regulation of key genes may have inhibited the pyroptosis process, potentially providing a promising research direction for the treatment of HF.

  Additionally, the results from the GeneMANIA website in this study indicated that these key genes may be involved in pathways related to fatty acid beta-oxidation, fatty acid metabolism, carboxylic acid catabolism, and organic acid catabolism. The importance of fatty acid metabolism pathways has been highlighted in targeting the progression of cardiac hypertrophy in diabetic cardiomyopathy, which aligns with the findings from the GO and KEGG enrichment analysis mentioned above [56].

  In general, our study suggested a potential link between HF and pyroptosis. Moreover, pyroptosis might be inhibited in the process of HF. The immune response after pyroptosis was mainly reflected in the infiltration of Tregs. Finally, transcriptional regulatory networks and gene-drug networks were constructed, which provided ideas for the subsequent interpretation of the reasons for the differential expression of key genes and their clinical applications in the diagnosis and treatment of HF.


### Limitations and perspectives
  However, this study also had some limitations. First, due to the small sample size, it might not fully represent the overall characteristics of the target population, which might have affected the broad applicability and statistical significance of the results. Secondly, this study mainly relied on bioinformatics analysis and lacked further experimental verification. In order to make up for this deficiency, future studies would further explore and verify the specific functions of key genes in HF by Western blot and immunofluorescence staining, combined with overexpression and knockout experiments. In addition, more large - scale studies were needed in the future to further study the prognostic value of these key genes and their related mechanisms.


## Conclusion
  In this study, 12 differentially expressed genes were found to be significantly associated with HF and pyroptosis scores, of which 7 key genes ( SNORD76, RPS3A, SNORD1A, CCDC159, AMT, RANBP6 and CRAT ) have high diagnostic value. Immune cell analysis showed that five kinds of immune cells such as regulatory T cells and eosinophils were closely related to these key genes. Studies have also revealed that AMT, RPS3A and CRAT are potentially associated with several drugs ( such as levocarnitine and NADH ). Finally, RT-qPCR results showed that the expression of CCDC159, CRAT and AMT was up-regulated in HF group, while the expression of RPS3A and RANBP6 was down-regulated, which further supported the potential role of these genes in HF pathology. In summary, the role of these key genes in regulating the inflammatory response may play an important role in the occurrence and development of HF, which provides new ideas for the early diagnosis of HF and the development of drug targets.
