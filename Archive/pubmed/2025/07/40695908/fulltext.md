# Identification of telomere maintenance related biomarkers and regulatory mechanisms in chronic obstructive pulmonary disease by machine learning algorithm


## Abstract
  Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a progressive respiratory disease that accelerates the aging process of the lung. Despite advancements in managing symptoms and preventing acute exacerbations, significant gaps remain in our understanding of the complex mechanisms that drive disease progression and contribute to mortality in COPD. In our work, we have successfully identified a set of five robust biomarkers (including RMI1, RAD51, RAD52, SNRNP70 and CHEK1). These biomarkers effectively distinguish COPD samples from normal samples, with area under the curve (AUC) value greater than 0.65 in the training set and greater than 0.80 in the validation set. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) analysis showed that the main enrichment pathways were Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, Spliceosome, Oxidative phosphorylation, etc. We also found these five genes had high accuracy in the diagnosis of COPD in both the training and verification sets. Molecular docking showed that the TOP5 small drug molecules acting with CHEK1 were U-0126, KN-62, BX-912, LY-294,002 and AZD-7762. The results of real-time reverse transcriptase-polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) showed that there were significant differences in the expression of SNRNP70 and RAD52 between COPD and control samples (p < 0.05).


## Introduction
  Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) is a common chronic condition characterized by airflow obstruction, typically resulting from chronic bronchitis and/or emphysema1. It can further progress to pulmonary heart disease and respiratory failure. The prevalence of COPD in clinical settings increases significantly with age, especially in recent years with the arrival of an aging population. This has led to more COPD patients, negatively impacting their quality of life and raising the mortality rate among the elderly, resulting in a growing medical burden2,3. Currently, clinical management of COPD focuses primarily on symptom control and slowing disease progression. To achieve better treatment outcomes, it is essential to delve deeper into the complex pathophysiological mechanisms underlying COPD4.

  Telomeres are structural components made up of repetitive sequences of DNA and related proteins at the end of chromosomes, which play a crucial role in maintaining the stability and integrity of chromosomes5. Telomere length is usually closely related to the health status and aging process of cells, and the maintenance of telomere length is crucial for the normal function and survival of cells6. Recent studies have shown that in COPD patients, there is a shortening of telomeres and non-length-dependent telomere damage in white blood cells and various lung cells, such as type II alveolar cells and endothelial cells, which are pathological changes related to the risk of COPD development7,8. Telomere length is related to age, and it tends to shorten with age. Besides, the length of telomeres in COPD patients is also related to the degree of lung injury9. Meanwhile, studies have indicated that inflammation, oxidative stress, and other pathological processes also mediate or participate in the process of shortening telomeres in the white blood cells of COPD patients10,11. Additionally, Jin M et al. found that COPD patients with shorter telomere length had poorer health status12. Scholars like Lee J further analyzed and demonstrated that COPD patients have shorter leukocyte telomeres, which are associated with mortality in COPD patients13. The research suggests that telomere length may play a role in the pathophysiology of COPD. Studying telomere length could aid in the early diagnosis and treatment of COPD.

  This study aims to further explore the relationship between telomere maintenance-related genes and the occurrence of COPD, clarify the diagnostic and therapeutic value of telomere maintenance biomarkers in COPD and uncover the upstream key miRNAs that regulate the disease. To achieve this, four machine learning algorithms (RF, GLM, XGBoost, and SVM) were employed to select the optimal classification algorithm, utimately identifying key gene targets to guide clinical diagnosis and treatment.


## Materials and methods

### Data collection
  COPD sample transcriptome chip data were downloaded from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO, http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) database. The training set GSE112811 included 42 peripheral blood transcriptome chip data, of which 20 were COPD samples and 22 were control samples. The validation set GSE146560 included transcriptome microarray data from 16 peripheral blood monocytes, comprising 8 samples from individuals with COPD and 8 control samples. In total, 2086 telomere maintenance related genes were downloaded from the TelNet database14 (http://www.cancertelsys.org/telnet/).


### Acquisition of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in GSE112811 dataset
  The “limma” package (version 3.54.0)15 of R software and p.value < 0.05 criterion were used to screen DEGs in the GSE112811 dataset. The volcano map of up-regulated or down-regulated genes were drawn using “ggplot2” (version 3.4.1)16, and the heatmap with TOP30 up- and down-regulated genes was drawn by “pheatmap” (version 1.0.12)17. Differentially expressed telomere maintenance related genes were derived from the intersection of 2086 telomere maintenance related genes downloaded from the TelNet database and DEGs between COPD and normal controls.


### Enrichment analysis of differentially expressed telomere maintenance related genes
  We made Gene Ontology (GO) functional annotation of differentially expressed telomere maintenance related genes to explore the biological significance of each gene, and we also made Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG)18–20 functional annotation to analyze key signaling pathways. The “clusterProfiler” package (version 4.2.2)21 was used for GO and KEGG enrichment analysis.


### Protein-protein interaction (PPI) network analysis and machine learning
  Exploring the network of protein interactions between genes that we’ve identified was critical to further understanding gene function and the mechanisms that regulate COPD development. To achieve this goal, we used the website STRING (version 12.5)22 (https://string-db.org) to construct PPI networks for intersection genes of key modules (Confidence = 0.4), and the TOP10 proteins identified by CytoHubba MCC algorithm23 were used as key genes. Next, in order to reduce the number of marker genes, four machine learning classifiers (random forest (RF), generalized linear model (GLM), XGBoost, and support vector machines (SVM)) were constructed to further screen the top10 genes based on the key genes from PPI network. RF was implemented by randomForest package (version 4.7–1.1)24, GLM was implemented by stats package (version 4.2.1), XGBoost was conducted with xgboost package (version 1.7.7.1)25, and SVM was implemented by e1071 package (version 1.7–12)26. The machine learning model with the smallest residuals and the highest area under the curve (AUC) value not equal to 1 was selected as the optimal model, and the genes included in this model were identified as the characteristic genes for subsequent research. Afterthat, the characteristic genes selected by machine learning were used to construct a nomogram to predict the risk of COPD. Finally, the calibration and decision curves were plotted to validate the nomogram.


### Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) and gene set variation analysis (GSVA)
  Markers obtained by machine learning in method 2.4 were divided into high and low expression groups, and the “limma” package was used for difference analysis and the “clusterProfiler” package was used for GSEA enrichment analysis to find the common pathway of enrichment. This was followed by “hallmark pathways” for GSVA enrichment analysis of different biomarkers. When GSVA score t value < 0, the path was activated. Otherwise, the path was suppressed.


### Analysis and validation of diagnostic ability for biomarkers
  To further validate the diagnostic efficacy of RMI1, RAD51, RAD52, SNRNP70, and CHEK1 in COPD, the “pROC” package (version 1.18.5)27 was used to plot the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve and determine its diagnostic effectiveness by analyzing the AUC values in the GSE112811 and GSE146560 datasets.


### CIBERSORT algorithm was used for correlation analysis
  The CIBERSORT algorithm28 was used to analyze the proportion of 22 immune cells in different samples. Wilcoxon test was applied to compare the differences between COPD and control groups (p < 0.05). Spearman analysis was used to investigate the correlation between genes and differential immune cells.


### Construction of microRNA (miRNA) regulatory networks and molecular docking
  NetworkAnalyst (version 3.0)29 (https://www.networkanalyst.ca/) was used to predict miRNAs of characteristic genes and demonstrate their interaction networks. Interaction network between characteristic genes and miRNAs was established by Cytoscape software (version 3.8.2)30. For molecular docking, genes were introduced into the clue.io platform in the CMap database31 to screen candidate drugs. The drug candidate was imported into the PubChem database32 to obtain its three-dimensional structure. Finally, molecular linkage analysis was performed using AutoDock Vina (version 1.5.7) software33 and docking results were visualized using PyMOL software (version 3.1.1)34.


### Real-time reverse transcriptase-polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR)
  The experiments involving human tissue adhere to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki and have received approval from the Rugao People’s Hospital Institutional Review Committee. COPD samples were included based on established diagnostic criteria, which primarily rely on clinical data such as a history of exposure to risk factors, symptoms, signs, and pulmonary function tests, while excluding other diseases that may cause similar symptoms or persistent airflow limitation. Pulmonary function tests confirm that persistent airflow limitation is a key condition for diagnosing COPD. A post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC ratio of less than 70% is indicative of persistent airflow limitation. The control group comprised individuals with no history of respiratory diseases or abnormal lung function. Exclusion criteria for COPD included other conditions that might affect lung function, such as bronchial asthma, bronchiectasis, tuberculosis, severe pneumonia, interstitial lung disease, and lung cancer. Peripheral blood of COPD patients and healthy volunteers was collected, and RNA was extracted. Reverse transcribe the RNA into cDNA according to the instructions of the reverse transcription kit. The expression of the selected genes was analyzed by RT-qPCR, and the results were analyzed by 2-△△Ct.


### Statistical analysis
  Data analysis was performed by R software (version 4.2.1). Differences between the two groups of samples were analyzed by wilcoxon rank sum test. The difference of p < 0.05 was significant.


## Result

### Screening of DEGs and differentially expressed telomere maintenance related genes
  As shown in Fig. 1A (volcano map) and 1B (heat map), a total of 1090 DEGs were screened out in GSE112811 data set using p.value < 0.05 as the standard. Compared with the control group, the COPD group had 495 up-regulated genes and 595 down-regulated genes. The 2086 telomere maintenance related genes downloaded from the TelNet database were intersected with DEGs. We obtained 151 differentially expressed telomere maintenance related genes in COPD (Fig. 1C).

### Functional enrichment and PPI analysis of differentially expressed telomere maintenance related genes
  In order to explore the regulatory pathways of each gene, we conducted enrichment analysis on 151 differentially expressed telomere maintenance related genes obtained in 3.1 result, and obtained the following results: 650 GO pathways and 35 KEGG pathways were enriched in differentially expressed telomere maintenance related genes. Among them, GO enrichment analysis enriched histone modification, regulation of DNA metabolic process, regulation of mRNA metabolic process and other pathways. KEGG enriched Salmonella infection, Shigellosis, Homologous recombination and other pathways, the enrichment results could be seen from Fig. 2A and C. Then, in order to evaluate whether there were interactions between the 151 differentially expressed telomere maintenance related genes, we used the STRING website to obtain an interaction network of 119 proteins (Fig. 2D). CytoHubba MCC algorithm was used to identify the top 10 proteins as key genes, including 10 genes (FANCD2, RAD51, CHEK1, SNRNP70, NBN, RMI1, RAD52, MDC1, XRCC5, and PRPF19), as shown in the following Fig. 2E.

### Machine learning and nomogram construction
  As shown in Fig. 3A and D, in order to reduce the number of marker genes, we constructed four machine learning classifiers (RF, GLM, XGBoost, and SVM) to further select the top10 genes. We find that XGBoost had the best effect, the lowest residual and AUC value greater than 0.9 among the four models. Finally, five important characteristic genes were obtained through screening, namely RMI1, RAD51, RAD52, SNRNP70 and CHEK1. Finally, we constructed a nomogram based on five genes selected by XGBoost algorithm (Fig. 3E and G).

  According to the analysis of Fig. 6A and B, AUC values of RAD52, SNRNP70 and CHEK1 in the training set exceeded 0.7, and AUC values of RMI1 and RAD51 were around 0.7, while AUC values of the five genes in the verification set were all above 0.8. ### Correlation analysis between characteristic genes and immune cells
  By analyzing the correlation between RMI1, RAD51, RAD52, SNRNP70, CHEK1 and 22 kinds of immune cells (Fig. 7), we could obviously find that RMI1 was positively correlated with 2 kinds of immune cells (Neutrophils and T cells gamma delta), while which was negatively correlated with B cells memory. In addition, we found that RAD51 was negatively correlated with T cells CD8, and CHEK1 was positively correlated with T cells gamma delta. But SNRNP70 and RAD52 did not have statistically significant differences with immune cells. The above analysis results further indicate that Neutrophils, B cells memory and other immune cells we found play a key role in the regulation of COPD by characteristic genes.

## Discussion
  COPD is the most common chronic respiratory system disease in China, characterized by high prevalence, mortality, and disease burden. The prognosis of COPD is closely linked to factors such as disease severity, timing of diagnosis, stage and grade, and whether the patient receives treatment. Therefore, early diagnosis and treatment of COPD are crucial for improving the patient’s poor prognosis. In this study, we identified five characteristic genes (including RMI1, RAD51, RAD52, SNRNP70, and CHEK1) through analysis and validated their diagnostic capabilities in the GSE112811 and GSE146560 datasets. The results showed AUC values ranging from 0.7 to 0.9, indicating that these five genes have high accuracy and specificity for diagnosing COPD. This also reveals their involvement in the cellular molecular mechanisms underlying COPD pathogenesis.

  The RMI1 gene encodes a protein called RMI1, which plays an important role in DNA repair and chromosome stability maintenance. A study has shown that the absence of RMI1 in cells leads to increased DNA damage levels, prolonged cell cycle arrest, and impaired HR repair after IR, resulting in decreased cell viability and increased genomic instability35. This highlights RMI1’s crucial role in maintaining genomic stability. Moreno et al. found that the telomere-associated protein TZAP induces telomere dysfunction and alternative lengthening of telomeres (ALT) activity by binding to telomeres36. This leads to the generation of t-loops and c-loops in a Bloom helicase complex (BLM-Topo IIIα-RMI1-RMI2, BTR) dependent manner. Furthermore, telomere trimming can be facilitated by telomere-associated proteins to regulate telomere length37. Therefore, abnormal mutations in RMI1 may regulate telomere length by affecting t-loops and c-loops, which could be one of its pathogenic mechanisms36. RAD51 is an ATPase that forms nuclear protein filaments on a single strand of DNA. RAD51 and its gene family play an important role in double-strand break repair, replication stress response and meiosis, and are key regulatory factors for maintaining DNA integrity and fidelity38. Studies have shown that at least two different recombination pathways, defined by RAD50 and RAD51, promote telomere maintenance39. Moreover, Lanna et al. found that certain T cells can acquire telomere-containing extracellular vesicles from antigen-presenting cells independently of telomerase activity to elongate telomeres. After telomeres are cleaved by TZAP, they are transferred to extracellular vesicles at the immunological synapse, where telomere vesicles retain the RAD51 recombinase factor. Thus, the absence of the recombinase RAD51 in telomere vesicles may contribute to alterations in telomere length and participate in the development of COPD40. For RAD52, Verma P et al. found that RAD52 is crucial for maintaining telomere stability41. Tan J et al. showed that CSB-RAD52-POLD3 can suppress telomere DNA fragmentation42. Carrero D et al. also found that CHEK1 is involved in important pathways for DNA repair and telomere maintenance43. This suggests that RAD52 and CHEK1 are closely related to telomere maintenance. Given the crucial role that telomeres play in the progression of COPD, we hypothesize that the key genes identified in this study are of great importance for the diagnosis of COPD. Furthermore, ATR (ATR serine/threonine kinase)-CHEK1/CHK1 (checkpoint kinase 1) axis was associated with airway inflammation44. SNRNP70 has been identified as a critical biomarker for various inflammatory diseases and cancers. Meanwhile, the innate and adaptive inflammatory immune responses to the inhalation of toxic particles and gases play a significant role in the pathogenesis of COPD45–47. While there is currently no direct literature establishing the mechanisms of action of these genes in COPD, their roles align closely with the known mechanisms underlying COPD, thereby further substantiating the value of these biomarkers.

  In addition, we could obviously find that RMI1 was positively correlated with 2 kinds of immune cells (neutrophils and gamma delta T cells), while which was negatively correlated with memory B cells. Neutrophils, also known as neutrophilic granulocytes, are a type of white blood cell and a critical component of the immune system. They are among the body’s primary phagocytes, capable of ingesting and eliminating pathogens through phagocytosis. Additionally, neutrophils can produce various cytokines such as interleukin-1 (IL-1), interleukin-6 (IL-6), and others, participating in the regulation and promotion of immune responses. More and more studies have revealed the relationship between neutrophils and COPD. Benjamin JT et al. have found that neutrophilic inflammation during lung development predisposes adult mice to COPD48. Lokwani R et al. also found that low expression of CD62L indicates that neutrophils in the blood have undergone activation in COPD49. Additionally, there are studies that suggest that neutrophils and their products are considered to be key mediators of airway inflammation changes in COPD50. Hartjes FJ et al. also found that neutrophils have certain predictive value for the clinical efficacy of inhaled corticosteroids (ICS) therapy in COPD51. Meijer M also elucidated the mechanism by which neutrophils induce COPD lung injury52. All of the above studies indicate that neutrophils play a crucial role in the progression of COPD. Combining our research findings, it is possible that RMI1 may regulate the progression of COPD by activating neutrophils in COPD.

  To identify small molecules targeting biomarkers, we conducted drug prediction and molecular docking analyses. The results indicated that CHEK1 interacts significantly with the small molecules U-0126, KN-62, BX-912, LY-294,002, and AZD-7762. As an inhibitor of MAPK signaling pathway, U-0126 has therapeutic effects in a variety of diseases, including COPD53–55. COPD treatment drugs block the NF-κB and MAPK signaling pathways, inhibit the activation and migration of inflammatory cells, reduce the secretion of cytokines, suppress the synthesis and activation of adhesion molecules and growth factors, and regulate the cAMP-PKA and PI3K/Akt signaling pathways, thereby effectively slowing down the progression of COPD56. Moreover, KN-62 is an inhibitor of calcium/calmodulin-dependent protein kinases (CaMKs), involved in regulating many important pathophysiological processes, including energy balance, glucose homeostasis, and inflammation57. Although the therapeutic effects of KN-62 in COPD have not been systematically studied, severe inflammatory responses are a key factor in the pathogenesis of COPD58. Thus, predicted small molecule drugs may serve as a valuable reference for the treatment of COPD; however, their specific mechanisms of action require further investigation.

  In summary, we identified five telomere maintenance-related genes, namely RMI1, RAD51, RAD52, SNRNP70, and CHEK1, which may serve as potential biomarkers for COPD. Our findings provide a certain reference for exploring the molecular mechanisms of telomere maintenance-related biomarkers in COPD. Although the diagnostic performance of these biomarkers in patients with COPD is well-documented, the widespread clinical application of these tools has not been fully explored, and the XGBoost model requires further validation. Further investigation is required to consider additional factors such as cost-effectiveness, clinical feasibility, and patient population heterogeneity. Furthermore, the mechanisms of these key genes need to be further elucidated through experiments, which will explore their potential function through knockdown and overexpression of the genes in the cell line. Future studies will also supplement this part of the content.
