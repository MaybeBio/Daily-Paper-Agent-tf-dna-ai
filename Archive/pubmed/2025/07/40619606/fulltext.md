Fenthion (FTN) is a noxious insecticidal agent that has been reported to exert adverse impacts on the health of different organisms. The current investigation was conducted to evaluate the adverse impacts of FTN on cardiac tissues at different dose concentrations. Thirty-six albino rats were apportioned into four groups including the control, FTN (25 mg/kg), FTN (50 mg/kg), and FTN (100 mg/kg) treated group. Rats were treated orally for 28 days consecutively. It was revealed that the FTN (25, 50, and 100 mg/kg) intoxicated group showed high abundances of receptors for advanced glycation end products (RAGE), tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-alpha), toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4), high mobility group box 1 (HMGB1), nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-kappaB), monocyte chemoattractant protein-1 (MCP-1), interleukin-6 (IL-6), cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2), and interleukin-1beta (IL-1beta) of mRNA in qRT-PCR analysis. Moreover, the abundances of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and malondialdehyde (MDA) were higher while the activities of catalase (CAT), glutathione peroxidase (GPx), glutathione reductase (GSR), heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1), superoxide dismutase (SOD), and contents of glutathione (GSH) were reduced after all the tested concentrations in enzyme activity analysis. FTN (100 mg/kg) exposure increased the concentrations of N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP), B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP), creatine phosphokinase (CPK), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), creatine kinase-MB (CK-MB), troponin-T, and troponin-I in ELISA assessment. Furthermore, all the tested concentrations of FTN exposure promoted the levels of Caspase-9, Bax, and Caspase-3 while reducing Bcl-2 levels in ELISA analysis. Moreover, all the concentrations of FTN exposure dysregulated the normal anatomical structure of cardiac tissues. Finally, in silico investigations, including molecular docking and molecular dynamic simulation, were conducted to further support the cardiotoxic effects of FTN. These findings suggest that FTN interacts with the active sites of target proteins, potentially disrupting their normal functions and contributing to severe cardiotoxicity.
