# Machine learning unveils hypoxia-immune gene hub for clinical stratification of thyroid-associated ophthalmopathy


## Supplementary Information
  The online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-05107-9.


## Introduction
  Thyroid-associated ophthalmopathy (TAO), also known as Graves’ ophthalmopathy or thyroid eye disease, is an autoimmune orbital disease closely associated with thyroid dysfunction1. Ocular involvement is observed in approximately 40% of patients with Graves’ disease2. TAO can cause a series of irreversible and destructive changes in appearance and visual function, including eyelid retraction, exophthalmos, diplopia, corneal ulceration, and, in some cases, optic neuropathy. These changes significantly impact the life quality of patients and impose a heavy burden on society3,4. The first-line treatment of active TAO is nonspecific glucocorticoid pulse therapy. Other treatments include immunosuppressants, orbital radiation therapy, biological agents, and surgical interventions. The efficacy rates of these methods vary between 50% and 80%; however, there are various side effects associated with these treatments3,5. Thus, further investigation of the underlying mechanisms of TAO and exploration of personalized treatments are needed.

  The pathogenesis of TAO is characterized by a multifactorial cascade driven by dysregulated autoimmune responses, progressive fibrosis, and pathological adipogenesis. These mechanisms collectively drive inflammatory infiltration, remodeling, and volumetric expansion of retrobulbar tissues, particularly orbital adipose tissue, extraocular muscles, and lacrimal glands. Orbital fibroblasts (OFs) have emerged as the core immune targets and main effector cells that amplify disease progression6,7. The pathogenic cascade arises from a dynamic interplay between OF activation states and multiple modulators, including immune cells, cytokines, autoantibodies, genetic factors, and environmental triggers such as smoking3. Consequently, the symptoms, severity, and treatment responses of patients with TAO are highly heterogeneous. There remains an urgent need for a prognostic model to identify patients at risk of developing severe complications, in order that more aggressive and personalized treatment plans can be devised.

  Hypoxia is associated with a variety of biological processes, including inflammation, cellular metabolism, cell proliferation, cell death, and has therefore received extensive attention8. Hypoxia-induced inflammation, fibrosis, and adipogenesis play important roles in various diseases9–13. Previous studies have indicated that low oxygen tension may be a significant component of the TAO microenvironment that stimulates tissue remodeling14. Smoking and diabetes, which are important risk factors for TAO, are closely associated with tissue hypoxia15,16. Therefore, hypoxia-related genes may be potential therapeutic targets for TAO, although there are few relevant studies.

  Bioinformatic analysis has become an indispensable tool in biomedical research. Recently, investigators have explored hub genes, biological pathways, and immune cell infiltration in TAO, providing new insights into its pathogenesis and drug screening4,17–20. However, the methods for database screening and analysis are somewhat crude, and most have not been verified experimentally, which calls into question the reliability of the results. Furthermore, there is to date no published bioinformatics research focusing on hypoxia-related mechanisms in TAO. In the present study we identified potential hypoxia-related biomarkers; constructed a predictive model of TAO by integrating multiple methods; predicted the microRNAs (miRNAs), transcription factors (TFs), RNA-binding proteins (RBPs) that might participate in the pathogenesis and potential therapeutic drugs; characterized the connections between immune infiltration and hypoxia, validating findings in orbital tissue and fibroblasts from healthy controls and TAO patients. The results are expected to promote understanding of the involvement of hypoxia in TAO, with the goal of developing feasible therapeutic strategies at the gene level.

### Construction, evaluation and validation of HRDEGs predictive model
  We performed logistic regression based on the 10 HRDEGs and built a model, visualized using a Forest Plot (Fig. 3A). All 10 HRDEGs were found to be statistically significant (p < 0.05).

(E) Random Forest model showing differential genes (in descending order of IncNodePurity).

  We then developed an Support Vector Machine (SVM) model, using the 10 HRDEGs, to obtain the number of genes with the lowest error rate (Fig. 3B) and highest accuracy rate (Fig. 3C). The accuracy was found to be highest when the following eight HRDEGs were used: WTIP, DIO3, PLEKHN1, AGO2, PSMD14, TFAP2A, PROM1, and CP.

  Next, we used the random forest algorithm to investigate the expression of the 10 HRDEGs in the Combined Datasets (Fig. 3D), with the result that nine of the ten HRDEGs were selected: WTIP, DIO3, PLEKHN1, AGO2, PSMD14, TFAP2A, PROM1, CDH1, and CP (Fig. 3E).

  Subsequently, eight intersecting genes from the three machine learning algorithms were obtained (Fig. 4A): WTIP, DIO3, PLEKHN1, AGO2, PSMD14, TFAP2A, PROM1, and CP.

  The nomogram shows the interrelationships among the five hub genes (Fig. 4D). Calibration analysis revealed that the calibration line of the predictive model (dashed line) deviated slightly from the diagonal line of the optimal theoretical model (solid line) but was close to consistent (Fig. 4E). ### Immune infiltration analysis of TAO samples in combined GEO datasets
  To compare the differences in the immune microenvironment of TAO with varying severity, we employed the Single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) and CIBERSORT algorithms. First, differences in the expression of 28 immune cells between the high- and low-risk groups were compared using ssGSEA. As shown in the group comparison diagram (Fig. 7A), the difference between high- and low-risk groups in terms of the infiltration abundance of 12 immune cell types was statistically significant (p < 0.05). Among them, the density of type 17 T helper (Th17) cells and CD56dim natural killer (NK) cells was higher in the TAO high-risk group compared with that in the low-risk group; whereas the opposite was true for other cell types. The correlation among the infiltration abundances of the 12 cell types is shown in a heatmap (Fig. 7B). Th17 and CD56dim NK cells were negatively correlated with other immune cells, whereas most other immune cells showed positive correlations with each other. Lastly, the correlations between hub genes and immune cell infiltration abundance were examined; the results are shown in a bubble plot (Fig. 7C). WTIP and DIO3 were significantly positively correlated with Th17 and CD56dim NK cells, but significantly negatively correlated with other cell types (p < 0.05). There were significant negative correlations between AGO2 and Th17 or CD56dim NK cells, and positive correlations with other significant immune cells (p < 0.05).

### Experimental validations of hub HRDEGs expression in orbital tissues
  To verify the bioinformatics results, quantitative real time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) was used to validate the expression of five hub HRDEGs in orbital adipose connective tissues. In the TAO group, DIO3 exhibited a notable increase in expression (P < 0.01), whereas AGO2 (P < 0.05) and PSMD14 (P < 0.01) levels were significantly decreased (Fig. 8A). Similarly, the results of immunohistochemical (IHC) staining indicated that the protein expression levels of AGO2 (P < 0.05) and PSMD14 (P < 0.001) were significantly lower in the TAO group than in the control group, whereas DIO3 levels were significantly higher in the TAO group (P < 0.05) (Fig. 8B-C). These results agree with those of the bioinformatics analysis.

### Validation of the predictive model
  We validated the diagnostic efficacy and assessment capability of the predictive model for disease severity using the relative expression of five hub genes detected by PCR. There were three samples in the Mild group, and four samples in Moderate-to-Severe group. The Risk Scores of the TAO group were significantly higher than Control group (Fig. 8D, p < 0.01), and the Moderate-to-Severe group of TAO scored higher than did the Mild group (Fig. 8E, p < 0.05), demonstrating the predictive power of our model for TAO diagnosis and disease severity assessment.


### Validation of the enrichment of Th17 and CD56dim NK cells
  Flow cytometric analysis of orbital tissues found that TAO patients had a higher proportion of IL-17 A+ cells (Th17) among the CD45+CD3+CD4+ cells than healthy controls (p < 0.05), and this difference was statistically significant (Fig. 8F). Furthermore, there was a significant increase in the proportion of CD56⁺CD16⁺ cells (CD56dim NK) in the CD45+CD3− population among TAO patients (p < 0.05). (Fig. 8G)


### Proliferation, adipogenesis, protein expression of OFs, and the response to hypoxia
  The Cell Counting Kit 8 (CCK-8) (Fig. 8H), colony formation assay (Fig. 8I), and Oil Red O staining (Fig. 8J) results demonstrated that orbital fibroblasts from TAO patients proliferated and underwent adipogenesis more than those from healthy individuals, with hypoxia further amplifying these processes.

  In the OFs of TAO patients, DIO3 protein expression levels were elevated compared to those in healthy controls, while AGO2 levels are decreased; both trends were aggravated by hypoxia. The protein expression of PSMD14 is not significantly different between the TAO and control groups. However, it decreases under hypoxic conditions (Fig. 8K-N). The original blot images are presented in Fig. S2.


## Discussion
  TAO has attracted increasing attention as a potential cause of blindness. However, its pathogenesis has not been fully elucidated. In TAO, hypoxia has been reported to be involved in all significant pathogeneses, including adipogenic differentiation of orbital fibroblasts, glycosaminoglycan secretion, inflammatory cell infiltration, and production of pro-inflammatory cytokines21. It could be imaged that as adipose and muscle tissues continue to expand, delayed vascularization and vascular compression further exacerbate the inadequate oxygen supply, while the inflammation increases oxygen consumption, ultimately leading to a vicious cycle14,21. Therefore, the objective in this research was to explore the specific mechanisms between them.

  In the present study, differential expression analysis was used to identify HRDEGs, and a predictive model was constructed. We also performed GO, GSEA, and GSVA, built PPI and mRNA regulatory networks, and analyzed the molecular interaction mechanisms of the HRDEGs. Subsequently, immune filtration analysis was conducted using ssGSEA and CIBERSORT algorithms. Finally, we validated the hypoxia-immune crosstalk genes, immune cells enrichment and predictive model in orbital tissues and orbital fibroblasts.

  Three machine learning algorithms were applied to select the best feature genes connected to the TAO. Intersection genes were obtained from logistic regression analysis, the SVM model, and the random forest model. A hypoxia-related predictive model with high diagnostic accuracy was created by LASSO regression analysis (AUC = 0.976), and five hub genes (AGO2, CP, DIO3, PSMD14, WTIP) were identified. DIO3 and WTIP were highly expressed in TAO patients in the combined datasets, whereas AGO2, CP and PSMD14 were low. qPCR and IHC confirmed the differential expression of DIO3, AGO2, and PSMD14 in orbital tissues. Under hypoxic conditions, we verified changes in the expression of DIO3 and AGO2 proteins in orbital fibroblasts, along with the observed increase in cell proliferation and adipogenesis. These findings suggest a mechanism through which hypoxia could exacerbate the disease progression and clinical manifestations of TAO. Additionally, based on the expression levels determined by PCR, the Risk Score of our predictive model demonstrated good reliability in both diagnosing and assessing the severity of TAO. Future work can involve testing these hub genes in newly diagnosed TAO patients and monitoring clinical activity and treatment response.

  During hypoxic responses, global changes in the intricate control network comprising TFs and messenger proteins are induced to adapt the cells to metabolism, proliferation, DNA repair, and apoptosis. The downregulation of enriched pathways associated with cellular hypoxia response, apoptosis, and DNA double-strand break sensing in this study suggested a multifaceted role of hypoxia in TAO pathogenesis. miRNAs, as key elements that modulate gene expression through post-transcriptional procedures in these processes22play crucial roles in TAO pathogenesis23,24.

  AGO2 (Argonaute RISC Catalytic Component 2) encodes the most important Argonaute protein and is the core component of the microRNA-induced silencing complex (miRISC). Directed by miRNA, RISC silences target genes by binding to complementary mRNA25. However, in different diseases, developmental stages, metabolic states, tissue types, and sites, the final functions vary with the target genes. Tokiyoshi et al. observed higher AGO2 gene polymorphism in autoimmune thyroid disease patients, linked to development and prognosis26. Zhang et al. proposed that hypoxic stress blocks miRNA and AGO2-mediated gene silencing, leading to mRNA accumulation, which may improve stress tolerance. Nevertheless, prolonged hypoxia in tumor cells leads to significant changes in their entire gene expression profiles, which ultimately driving malignant evolution27. The reduced levels of AGO2 in TAO patients may also be part of similar responses of hypoxia tolerance protection; However, the unpredictable long-term changes in RNA could ultimately lead to the deterioration of TAO.

  Iodothyronine deiodinase 3, encoded by DIO3, is known for its role in thyroid hormone metabolism, specifically in the inactivation of thyroid hormones via deiodination25. The elevated expression of DIO3 in TAO patients suggests a compensatory mechanism in response to the altered thyroid hormone levels and a hypermetabolic state within the orbital tissues, which could contribute to the proliferation and differentiation of OFs observed in our findings. Van also reported that in anoxic TAO adipocytes, DIO3 was up-regulated by HIF-1α, decreasing the cellular supply of T3 28. The association of DIO3 with the immune microenvironment, particularly its positive correlation with Th17 cells, underscores its potential role in modulating immune responses in TAO. Thus, DIO3 not only serves as a novel biomarker of disease activity but also presents a novel target for therapeutic intervention aimed at restoring hormonal balance and mitigating inflammatory responses. Iopodic acid, is an iodine containing drug historically employed as an anti-hyperthyroidism agent and cholecystographic agent. Its ability to inhibit the upregulated expression of DIO3, provided a novel therapeutic strategy for TAO.

  PSMD14 encodes an ingredient of the 26 S proteasome, which catalyzes ATP-dependent degradation of ubiquitinated intracellular proteins, is integral to protein degradation and cellular homeostasis. Proteasomes can remove proteins that are misfolded, damaged, or no longer required, is crucial for the regulation of various signaling pathways, including those involved in apoptosis, cellular stress responses, cell cycle and DNA damage repair29. Based on the results of bioinformatics analysis, we hypothesized that the downregulation of PSMD14 and the enriched pathways for sensing DNA double-strand breaks, apoptosis, and programmed cell death in TAO disrupted the clearance of abnormal proteins and cells, leading to the accumulation of lesions and modulation of TAO development. However, the association between PSMD14 and TAO has not been mentioned before. In this study, PSMD14 emerged as the most useful gene in the HRDEGs diagnostic model, with qPCR and IHC results from orbital tissues confirming its downregulation in TAO. Nevertheless, no differences were observed in protein expression levels between orbital fibroblasts from TAO patients and normal controls, despite a decrease in expression after hypoxia. This phenomenon illustrates the intricate regulation of mRNA and protein expression, affected by factors such as post-transcriptional processes, the expression levels of other surrounding cells, and sample size limitations. Future studies will utilize larger sample sizes and various methods to clarify the mechanisms involved.

  Through GSEA and GSVA, we also found that multiple lipid metabolism-related pathways, neuropeptide-and dopamine receptor-related pathways, FGFR3b (Fibroblast Growth Factor Receptor) ligand binding and activation, and the retinoid cycle in the cone daylight vision pathway were upregulated in TAO. These findings suggest a potential association of orbital lipid accumulation, fibration, emotional abnormalities, and color vision changes. The interplay between hypoxia and these pathways underscores the complexity of the immune microenvironment in TAO. However, further studies are needed to obtain more relevant evidence.

  We established mRNA-miRNA, mRNA-TF, mRNA-RBP, and mRNA-drug regulatory networks of the hub genes and identified a new set of molecules relevant to gene regulation and drug targets for TAO. Although miRNAs are important regulators of the hypoxic response, there are limited studies on the involvement of miRNAs in TAO. We predicted 25 miRNAs targeting hypoxia-related genes in TAO. MiR-151a-3p has been reported to be related to proliferation, fibrosis, inflammation, and oxidative stress in human glomerular mesangial cells30. Lin et al. demonstrated the role and mechanism of miR-450b-5p in alleviating fibrogenesis in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis31. Pharmacological inhibition of miR-33 in macrophages could attenuate pulmonary fibrosis32; in addition, miR-33 was also been linked to white adipocyte expansion and hypertriglyceridemia33. These miRNAs are novel in TAO, warranting further investigation.

  The immune system is also of vital importance in TAO, and mounting evidence has shown that hypoxia modulates the immune system components34. Here, we integrated ssGSEA and CIBERSORT to thoroughly assess immune cell infiltration. In TAO high-risk samples, the densities of Th17 cells and CD56dim NK cells were higher. Both were positively correlated with the upregulated genes DIO3, WTIP, and negatively correlated with the downregulated gene AGO2. Th17 cells are known for their pro-inflammatory properties, and contribute to TAO immunopathology35correlating with clinical activity and severity36,37. Fang explored the interaction between Th17 cells and orbital fibroblasts, where Th17 cells enhance proinflammatory cytokines and promote fibroblast fibering and lipogenesis, while fibroblasts produce prostaglandin E2 to support Th17 cell differentiation35. In addition, previous study has indicated that CD16+ NK cells are essential constituents of TAO orbital microenvironment38and CD56dim NK (CD16+CD56+) cells are crucial for cytotoxic responses against aberrant cells39. Our research confirmed the increased infiltration of Th17 and CD56dim NK cells into the orbital tissue of TAO, as shown by flow cytometry. Nevertheless, the role of hypoxia-related genes in these immune cells on the overall immune response in TAO warrants further investigation.

  Our study has several limitations. Owing to its relatively low incidence, reliable datasets for TAO are limited. Although various bioinformatics approaches have been harnessed to improve the dependability of the results, some deviations may still exist. Although the involvement of the lacrimal gland in TAO is well recognized, it does not represent all tissues within the orbit. Therefore, we validated the selected hub genes and predictive model using anterior orbit sample data from GEO and orbital adipose connective tissue samples from patients. However, larger sample sizes are required to confirm the clinical utility of HRDEGs and extended predictive models. Additionally, we verified the increase of Th17 and CD56dim NK cells in TAO; yet, their link with HRDEGs is currently based only on data correlations, lacking direct evidence of cellular interactions, such as those from co-culture experiments. Further in vivo and in vitro experiments are imperative to verify our findings.

  Based on the results of our study, we propose three hypoxia-related genes with potential for the diagnosis and treatment of TAO. The significant changes in the expression of these genes correlated with immune cell infiltration patterns, indicating a complex interaction between hypoxia and the immune microenvironment in TAO. With this study we demonstrated a hypoxia-related predictive model associated with both the diagnosis and severity of this disease. A set of TFs, miRNAs, RBP, drugs and pathways linked to the hub genes was also identified. In the future, we will continue to validate the molecular mechanisms of HRDEGs and the clinical utility of predictive models, thereby opening new avenues for TAO research.


## Materials and methods
  The Technology Roadmap of this study is illustrated in Fig. S3. Detailed methods are provided in the Supplementary Methods section.


### Data download
  We downloaded thyroid-related ophthalmopathy (TAO) expression profile datasets GSE5833140GSE10514941 through the R package GEOquery42 from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database43. The samples were obtained from Homo sapiens, the tissue source was the Anterior Orbit or Lacrimal gland, and the chip platform was GPL570, as detailed in Table S7.

  We then used the R package sva44 to de-batch lacrimal gland tissue samples in datasets GSE58331 and GSE105149 to obtain Combined datasets. The integrated GEO Datasets (Combined Datasets) contained 12 lacrimal gland samples from the TAO group (group: TAO) and 14 lacrimal gland control samples (group: control). The Combined Datasets were normalized using the R package limma45 to annotate probes. Group comparisons (Control, TAO) and Principal Component Analysis (PCA)46 were employed to verify the efficacy of batch-effect removal. All samples of the combined datasets were analyzed, and all TAO and control samples from GSE58331 and GSE105149 were used as validation sets for subsequent analysis.

  We identified hypoxia-related genes (HRGs) using the GeneCards database47. After using the term “Hypoxia” as keyword and keeping the HRGs with “Protein Coding” and “Relevance Score > 2.0”, a total of 800 HRGs (Table S8) were obtained.


### TAO associated hypoxia-related differentially expressed genes
  Differentially Expressed Genes (DEGs) in the control and TAO groups of the Combined Datasets intersected with hypoxia-related genes. Thus, hypoxia-related differentially expressed genes (HRDEGs) were accessed. Then Group comparisons, Functional similarity analysis, and the correlation analysis among HRDEGs were performed.


### Functional enrichment analysis
  Gene Ontology (GO)48 enrichment analysis of the HRDEGs was implemented. Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA)49and Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA)50 was conducted on all genes in Combined Datasets.


### Construction and validation of hrdegs predictive model
  To develop the HRDEGs predictive models of the Combined Datasets, Logistic regression analysis was performed, and the results are shown as forest plots. The HRDEGs selection criterion was set at p < 0.05.

  Next, a Support Vector Machine (SVM) model51 was developed based on the number of genes with the highest accuracy and lowest error rate.

  Subsequently, the expression levels of HRDEGs were utilized to build the random forest (RF)52 model using the randomForest package53with parameters of set.seed (250) and ntree = 1000. ### Protein-protein interaction (PPI) network and mRNA networks
  The hub gene-related Protein-protein interaction (PPI) Network and the PPI Network for predicting functionally similar hub genes were created. Then the protein structures of hub genes were predicted and visualized. Lastly, the mRNA-miRNA, mRNA-TF, mRNA-RBP58mRNA-Drug Regulatory Network were constructed.


### Immune infiltration analysis
  Single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA)59 was used to quantify the relative abundance of infiltration by each immune cell type in each TAO sample. And the CIBERSORT60 algorithm and the LM22 feature gene matrix were applied to form an infiltration matrix in TAO patient samples from the Combined Datasets. Subsequently, group comparisons were created between the high- and low-risk groups of HRDEGs predictive model risk scores in TAO patient samples from Combined Datasets. immune cells were screened for subsequent analysis. Correlations between significantly different immune cells and those between hub genes and immune cells were calculated.


### Orbital adipose connective tissues collection, and orbital fibroblasts isolation and culture
  Orbital adipose connective tissues were collected from patients with TAO who underwent orbital decompression surgery (TAO group, n = 6, mean age 38.8 ± 15.6 years, male-to-female ratio 2:4) and healthy individuals who underwent oculoplastic surgery (control group, n = 7, mean age 42.4 ± 12.6 years, male-to-female ratio 3:4) at Xiangya Hospital, Central South University, China. This study was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of the Xiangya Hospital of Central South University (approval number: 202407131). All participants provided informed consent, the privacy rights of human subjects were observed, and the study complied with the Declaration of Helsinki.

  OFs from TAO patients and healthy individuals were isolated and expanded, with the 3–6 generations employed for subsequent experiments.


### Quantitative real time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR), immunohistochemistry (IHC) of orbital tissues and predictive model validation
  qRT-PCR of the 13 orbital tissue samples was performed in triplicate from independent experiments. The primer information is presented in Table S9. Target gene expression relative to GAPDH was calculated as 2-ΔΔCt. Simultaneously, three samples of orbital tissues from both the Control (mean age 46.0 ± 4.0 years, male-to-female ratio 1:2) and TAO (mean age 47.6 ± 7.8 years, male-to-female ratio 1:2) group were collected for IHC detection. This detection utilized primary antibodies AGO2 (1:100, AWA10435, Abiowell), PSMD14 (1:100, AWA10455, Abiowell), and DIO3 (1:50, DF8532, Affinity) to confirm the protein expression of HRDEGs validated by PCR.

  The Risk Scores of 13 samles collected were computed using the relative expression of hub genes in the predictive model, and a comparison between the Control and TAO groups was performed. The TAO samples were divided into two groups (Mild, Moderate-to-Severe) based on the severity of the disease (EUGOGO classification3), and group comparisons were performed.


### Flow cytometry
  Four samples of orbital tissues from both the Control and TAO group were used for Flow cytometric analysis, Th17 (CD45+CD3+CD4+CD8−IL-17 A+) and CD56dim NK (CD45+CD3−CD56⁺CD16⁺) cells were identified based on the expression of their markers. The surface markers were sourced from BioLegend (USA), and the PE-IL-17 A antibody was acquired from BD Biosciences (USA).


### Detection of OFs in response to hypoxia
  OFs from TAO patients and healthy individuals were cultured in either normoxic (21% O2, 74% N2, and 5% CO2) or hypoxic (1% O2, 94% N2, and 5% CO2) atmosphere. We employed the Cell Counting Kit 8 (CCK-8) and the colony formation assay to assess cell proliferation, while Western blot analysis was utilized to confirm the protein expression of hub genes in OFs. Additionally, OFs underwent adipocyte differentiation and stained with Oil Red O solution to visualize lipid accumulation. All experiments were repeated three times under independent conditions.


### Statistical analysis
  All data processing and analyses in this study was carried out using R software (Version 4.2.2). If not otherwise specified, the statistical significance of normally distributed variables was estimated using the independent Student’s t-test for comparisons of continuous variables between the two groups. The Mann-Whitney U Test (Wilcoxon rank-sum test) was used to analyze the differences between variables that were not normally distributed. The correlation coefficients between different molecules were calculated using Spearman correlation analysis. If not otherwise specified, all p values were two-sided, and p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.
