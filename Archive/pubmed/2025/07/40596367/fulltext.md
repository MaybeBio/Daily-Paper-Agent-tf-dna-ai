# Regulatory T cells and matrix-producing cancer associated fibroblasts contribute on the immune resistance and progression of prognosis related tumor subtypes in ccRCC


## Abstract
  Clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) is a prevalent malignant tumor in the field of urology. The effect of cell heterogeneity on the prognosis and reaction to treatment of ccRCC in large populations is still unclear. By analyzing public single cell RNA-sequencing and bulk RNA-sequencing data with the Scissor algorithm, we have identified three distinct prognosis related cancer cell subtypes which play an indispensable role on tumor metastasis, immune response and proliferation respectively. Besides, regulatory T cells (Tregs) and matrix producing cancer associated fibroblasts (matCAFs) were also recognized as crucial cell subtypes in the tumor microenvironment (TME). Moreover, potential interactions between Scissor + cells and other cells in TME were investigated to uncover regulatory mechanisms via ‘Cell Chat’ and cell2location algorithm. It is interesting that the interferon gamma signaling pathway and p53 signaling pathway contribute to the Scissor + transition of Tregs and matCAFs. The distinct activated transcription factor patterns were uncovered as well as the essential ligand-receptor pairs in the interactions among different cell subtypes, such as CXCL12-CXCR4 and COL6A2-SDC4. Then, we developed a risk score signature consisting of 10 genes, utilizing a 101-combination machine learning computational framework, which showed promising results in predicting the prognosis of patients. Furthermore, our study revealed variations in immune cell infiltration and the expression of immune related factors within the tumor microenvironment between different risk score groups, as well as the different sensitivity to the immunotherapy. In the end, we suggested Rapamycin as the additional therapy for the advanced ccRCC. In conclusion, our study created a signature to provide opportunities for predicting prognosis and improving treatments of ccRCC.


## Introduction
  Clear cell renal carcinoma (ccRCC) is the most frequently diagnosed histological subtype of RCC and it leads to over 179,000 deaths worldwide1. Though some patients who undergo surgery experience increased survival rates, there are still numerous people suffered from the metastasis and recurrence of ccRCC. Over the years, only the renal tumor cell vaccine and sunitinib have been shown to enhance the prognosis, with nearly all other supplementary treatments failing to show their advantages2. Immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) therapy and combination regimens have given rise to the hope of these patients but the overall response rate still remains less than 58% in most cases3. Therefore, it is pivotal to construct a method assessing the prognosis and therapeutic response and continue to discover new drugs for ccRCC.

  Extensive heterogeneity, as a significant feature of cancer cells driven by genetics, epigenetics and microenvironment, accounts for the diverse therapeutic response among patients4. The use of single-cell RNA-sequencing (scRNA-seq) and spatial transcriptome (ST) technology has significantly improved our comprehension of tumor heterogeneity in recent times5. In spite of the extensive analysis of the heterogeneity in cancer cells and tumor microenvironment (TME)6,7,27, a comprehensive understanding of prognosis-related cellular subtypes is still lack due to the costs associated with high-throughput scRNA-seq when applied to large populations simultaneously. Due to the cost-effectiveness of bulk RNA-seq for use in a sizable group, the Scissor technique, which combines phenotype-linked bulk RNA-seq information with scRNA-seq data, seems capable of pinpointing a distinct cell subset linked to survival outlook8,12. Furthermore, such illustration of prognosis related cancer cells offers a novel insight into their special biological characteristics and behaviors, which guides the development of clinical outcome.

  As an automatic data analysis method, machine learning has been employed in clinical diagnosis and therapy9. Furthermore, advancements in next-generation sequencing have led to the creation of multiple prognostic and predictive gene signature models using machine learning techniques in previous studies8,10–12,14. To gain a better extrapolation potential, researchers incorporated 10 machine learning methods used in clinical analysis and combined any two methods to establish a novel algorithm including 101 kinds of combinations11,13,14,49. Such an integrative procedure may help to further promote the clinical diagnosis and therapy.

  This study extensively categorized cancer cells and cells in the TME using publicly available scRNA-seq, ST data and bulk RNA-seq data. Then the prognosis related cancer cell subtypes and other crucial cell types in TME were revealed via the Scissor algorithm and their molecular and biological characteristics were further analyzed. Using hdWGCNA, we identified co-expressed gene modules linked to prognosis-related tumor subtypes and created a gene expression signature for this specific group using a variety of machine learning techniques, aiding in disease prognosis prediction and treatment planning.


## Materials and methods

### Data acquisition
  In total, 14 tumor samples scRNA-seq data in three separate public datasets were obtained from the NCBI GEO databases at http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/, including GSE171306 (GSM5222644, GSM5222645), GSE224630 (GSM7028034, GSM7028035, GSM7028036, GSM7028037, GSM7028039) and GSE159115 (GSM4819725, GSM4819727, GSM4819729, GSM4819734, GSM4819736, GSM4819737, GSM4819738). And one representative sample (R29_T) was obtained from Zenodo https://zenodo.org/record/8063124)15. Three bulk RNA sequencing datasets were acquired from the IMvigor210 immunotherapy group, The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA), and the International Cancer Genome Consortium (ICGC) repository in order to develop and validate the accuracy of the predictive model.


### scRNA-seq data processing
  scRNA-seq data were processed via the ‘Seurat’ R package (version: 4.2.2)16. Doublets were identified and removed using the ‘DoubletFinder’ R package (version 2.0.3)17. Cell quality was evaluated based on three criteria: total UMI count exceeding 200, number of genes exceeding 200, and percentage of mitochondrial genes below 20. To avoid unexpected noise, genes associated with mitochondria and ribosome were excluded. Following this, PCA was utilized following the normalization and scaling of raw counts using the SCTransform feature. The ‘Harmony’ R package (version: 1.2.0) was applied to reduce batch effects among individual scRNA-seq data18. The ‘FindNeighbors’ and ‘FindClusters’ functions were utilized for clustering analysis. Next, the cells were observed using the uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP) algorithm. Similarly, subclustering analysis was performed. The Seurat functions ‘FindAllMarkers’ and ‘FindMarkers’ were utilized to detect differentially expressed genes (DEGs) within various clusters. Known cell-type marker genes were used to annotate each cell cluster in succession. Furthermore, ‘Infercnv’ (version 1.14.2) R package (https://github.com/broadinstitute/inferCNV) was applied to confirm the identity of cancer cells as previous described19.


### Scissor
  We evaluated the arrangement of clusters in each sample using the 'Scissor' R package (version 2.0.0). The input data for “Scissor” consists of a single-cell expression matrix, a batch expression matrix, and a target phenotype. Afterwards, the data is used to calculate correlation matrices and cell–cell similarity networks, which are then used in a network regularization sparse regression model to pinpoint the most relevant cell subsets associated with patients’ overall survival phenotypes. Based on the estimated regression coefficient, the cells were categorized as either Scissor + or Scissor- cells, representing positive and negative correlations with the prognosis, respectively. Subsequent reliability significance testing was performed to assess the suitability of the selected data for phenotype cell association. The cells identified by the scissors would then undergo further characterization in downstream analysis.


### Deconvolution of ST data
  We employed the cell2location deconvolution method20 on ST data to reconstruct the spatial organization of the various cell subtypes within the TME. Cell2location was executed using raw counts for both spatial and single-cell reference data, with default parameters applied.


### Functional enrichment analysis
  Seurat’s ‘FindAllMarkers’ function was utilized to identify the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) within each cell cluster. A cut-off criterion was applied using a |logFC| greater than 0.25 and an adjusted p-value less than 0.05. Based on the DEGs, Hallmark pathway enrichment analysis was performed to explore common biological pathways among the three tumor Scissor + subtypes.


### Transcription factor analyses
  Activated transcription factors (TFs) for each cell type were identified using the pySCENIC (version 0.12.1)(Van 21). Following the provided tutorials, we downloaded the TF data file (hg38*.genes_vs_motifs.rankings.feather) and motif annotation data file (motifs-v10nr_clust-nr.hgnc-m0.001-o0.0.tbl) from the CisTarget database (https://resources.aertslab.org/cistarget/). A co-expression network was constructed from the scRNA-seq expression matrix using the GRNBoost2 function. Next, TF-motif enrichment analyses were conducted to identify regulons, and the activity levels of each regulon were calculated using AUCell functions.


### Pseudotime trajectory analysis
  Unsupervised pseudotemporal analysis was conducted using the DDR-Tree algorithm and default settings in the ‘Monocle’ package (version 2.26.0). The ‘plot_pseudotime_heatmap’ function was used to demonstrate the temporal expression patterns of module genes along the pseudotime trajectories in the three tumor Scissor + subtypes.


### Analysis of gene co-expression networks with high dimensions and weighted connections (hdWGCNA)
  In order to identify potential Scissor + cancer cells related genes associated with patients’ prognosis, we conducted a comprehensive analysis by “hdWGCNA” package (version 0.2.27)22. Specifically, 50 cells were aggregated into per metacells for the tumor cell subpopulations with the hdWGCNA function MetacellsByGroups. Subsequently, we subsetted the Seurat object for each tumor cell subtype and performed the standard hdWGCNA pipeline.


### Construction and validation of a 101-combination machine learning framework based predictive model
  An analysis was performed to compare the bulk RNA-seq genes with the top 100 genes in the Scissor + tumor cell-related modules identified by hdWGCNA. The overlapping genes were considered to impact the prognosis of patients at both bulk and single-cell transcriptome levels. In order to develop a reliable prognostic model with strong predictive capabilities, we proceeded with the following steps:A total of 400 patients were randomly chosen from the TCGA-KIRC dataset for training purposes, while the remaining patients were designated as an internal validation dataset. The ICGC dataset was utilized as an external validation set. The analysis utilized ten different machine learning algorithms, such as Lasso, Ridge, stepwise Cox, CoxBoost, RSF, Enet, plsRcox, SuperPC, GBM, and survival-SVM. 101 combinations of 10 algorithms were tested using the TCGA-KIRC training dataset to select variables and build models in a tenfold cross-validation setup.Each of the created models was assessed in both the TCGA internal validation set and the ICGC external validation set. The C index was computed for every model in the training, internal validation, and external validation sets, and then the models were ranked according to the average C index of internal validation and external validation datasets to assess their predictive accuracy. Genes were identified using the selected algorithms with a largest average C index to establish a final risk signature.   Then a nomogram was constructed with clinical phenotype parameters by ‘rms’ package (version 6.7–1). Further, its time dependent performance was evaluated through receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves using the timeROC package (version 0.4) as well as the calibration curves in both the TCGA-KIRC dataset and ICGC dataset.


### Cell–Cell Communication analysis
  To identify possible cell–cell interactions, the ‘CellChat’ package (version 1.6.1) was used with the default settings23 and the selected ligands and receptors (L-R) pairs were analyzed among specific cell subclusters.


### Deconvolution of tumor immune microenvironment
  The CIBERSORT algorithm24, using linear support vector regression to analyze the expression matrix of 22 human immune cell subtypes, was used to study the differences in immune cell makeup between high and low-risk groups in the TCGA-KIRC dataset. To uncover the correlation between tumor cells and other cells in TME, we applied CIBERSORTx as tutorials suggest25. In brief, we randomly sampled 100 cells in each cluster as the reference scRNA-seq data and integrated the TCGA-KIRC dataset through the online analyzing tools (https://cibersortx.stanford.edu).


### Clinical analysis
  Using the ideal risk score threshold identified by the ‘survminer’ R software (v0.4.9), the TCGA training, internal validation, ICGC, and IMvigor210 datasets were split into high and low-risk categories. Following this, an analysis of Kaplan–Meier curves was performed with the ‘survminer’ R package and the ‘survival’ R package (version 3.4) to determine if there was a significant difference in OS between the high and low-risk groups (log-rank test, p < 0.05).


### Statistical analysis
  R software (version 4.3.1) was used for statistical analyses and data visualizations. Spearman correlation coefficients were utilized to evaluate the relationships between two continuous variables. We compared quantitative data between subgroups using Wilcox test or KruskalWallis test. If not other stated, p values were corrected in multi-comparison tests using the Benjamini–Hochberg method A p-value less than 0.05 was deemed statistically significant.


## Results

### Scissor analysis reveals the characteristics and distribution of prognosis related cells with scRNA-seq data
  To explore the cellular composition and variety of cell types in ccRCC, we performed a study analyzing 14 tumor samples in conjunction with their publicly accessible single-cell RNA sequencing information. By employing UMAP analyses, we were able to distinguish 30 unique cell populations (Fig. 1A). Based on known lineage markers and copy number variation, we grouped these populations into 13 main cell populations, which consist of mast cells, dendritic cells, macrophages, NK/NKT cells, neutrophils, CD4 + T cells, CD8 + T cells, ACKR1 + endothelial cells, ACKR1- endothelial cells, intermediate endothelial cells, fibroblasts, and cancer cells (Fig. 1B; Supplementary Fig. S1). Additionally, the study presents the analysis of significant known lineage markers in the 13 clusters, along with the expression and distribution of these markers (Fig. 1D-E). Furthermore, the identity of the inferred cancer cells was confirmed by calculating somatic large-scale chromosomal copy number variation (CNV) scores using immune cells and stromal cells as normal cell references (Supplementary Fig.S1). Subsequently, we calculated the proportion of each cell population in various samples (Fig. 1C). Notably, a substantial variance in proportions among the samples was observed, highlighting the heterogeneity present in patients with ccRCC. An examination was conducted on the absolute quantity and proportional distribution of Scissor + cells within each cluster. It was observed that the cancer cell population exhibited the greatest number of Scissor + cells, consistent with expectations.   Given that T cells including CD4 + T cells, NK/NKT cells and CD8 + T cells have a larger proportion of Scissor + cells, they were separated into 10 subclusters to apply a further analysis (Supplementary Fig. S2A; Fig. 2A), according to their individual markers (Supplementary Fig. S2C). The UMAP plot shows the distribution of these marker genes in each subclusters (Fig. S2B). Besides, we investigated the distinct biological function of these subclusters by Hallmarker pathway enrichment analysis (Fig. 2E). Furthermore, 30% cells of CD4_FOXP3(Treg) subcluster were Scissor positive, which was the largest proportion among the 10 T cell subclusters (Fig. 2B; Supplementary Fig. 2C). It indicated that CD4_FOXP3(Treg) may possess an essential function in the progression of ccRCC. By exploring the signature scores of these subclusters in TCGA-KIRC tumor samples, the regulatory T cells (Treg) were further confirmed to be related with poor prognosis of ccRCC (Fig. 2D). Tregs are widely known as an immune regulatory cell population, thus we analyzed the expression of immune checkpoint related genes (ICGs) in these cell populations and found that CD4_FOXP3(Treg) subcluster possess a higher expression in activated- and inhibited- related ICGs in the TME of ccRCC (Fig. 2F). ### Development of a predictive model for ccRCC based on Scissor + cancer cell via machine learning algorithms
  Due to the largest number of Scissor + cancer cells among all the clusters and their significant impact on patient prognosis, we were supposed to create a novel risk score based on the Scissor + cancer cells to evaluate patients’ prognosis as well as their sensitivity to therapies. Before that, we conducted hdWGCNA to identify co-expressed gene modules. In total, 5 specific gene co-expression modules were discovered with a soft thresholding power of 6 (Supplementary Fig. S6A-C; Supplementary Fig. S6F). Since there is no statistically significant correlation between the brown module and the marker genes of the three subtypes, the hub genes of the brown module were not considered into following analysis. Then, a total of 101 machine-learning algorithms were utilized to analyze the genes derived from the overlap of top 100 hub genes of each module and bulk RNA-seq genes (Supplementary Fig. S6D-E). The TCGA-KIRC dataset was then divided into a training set and an internal validation set with a ratio of about 4:1. In the training dataset, 101 models were created through tenfold cross-validation, and the C index was computed for both training and validation sets (Fig. 6A). In order to avoid potential overfitting when choosing models, we decided to compute the mean C-index for both the internal validation set and the external ICGC dataset. ## Discussion
  Although there is a significant number of researches on ccRCC, the basic mechanisms that determine the prognosis are still not well understood. Furthermore, the existing therapeutic options for the prevention and management of ccRCC are constrained and frequently inefficacious3, underscoring the urgent necessity for a reliable prognostic and therapeutic predictive tool. Previous studies using scRNA-seq to explore the diversity of cell types in ccRCC have provided important information, but have not fully revealed their impact on patient’s prognosis38,39. And current prognostic models for ccRCC developed under single-cell resolution predominantly select target cell populations based on subjective biological interests or differential abundance between tumor and normal tissues 32,34. Hence, we integrated bulk RNA-seq, scRNA-seq and ST RNA-seq utilizing the Scissor and multiple machine learning algorithms to comprehensively analyze the cell populations highly related to patients’ prognosis.

  First, the study of tumor cell heterogeneity is crucial for the development of effective and safe treatment strategies40. Our analysis identified three distinct subpopulations highly related to prognosis within cancer cells, denoted as the "Tumor_EMT”, "Tumor_Immune”, and “Tumor_Proliferative” clusters. The molecular characteristics of three subpopulations were characterized and their distinct functions were identified. The findings suggest that these specific groups are involved in cell proliferation, metastasis, and immune reaction in ccRCC. Additionally, the trajectory analysis of these subtypes transitioning to a metastatic phenotype supports previous findings and suggests a diverse range of metastatic phenotypes in advanced ccRCC41. Combined with their unique patterns of activated TFs, the tumor cell subtypes may interact with TME differently during the progression of ccRCC and we provide the potential therapeutic targets for these three subpopulations.

  Treg cells are a subset of T cells that generally exert suppressive effects on various immune cells, including CD8 + T cells, CD4 + T cells and natural killer cells. Their suppressive activity within the tumor microenvironment is linked to a reduction in anti-tumor immunity, underscoring the rationale for developing Treg-targeted immunotherapies42. In our investigation, we also recognized a specific interaction L-R pair called CXCL12-CXCR4 among Tregs and other immune cells, further confirming its significant role in ccRCC. Santagata et al. isolated peripheral blood Tregs from primary RCC patients and treated them with the CXCR4 antagonist R54. The study demonstrated that R54 significantly suppressed IL-35, IL-10, and TGF-β1 secretion but promoted IFN-γ production by effector T cells, ultimately impairing the immunosuppressive function of Tregs43. In a preclinical study using syngeneic mouse models of human colon cancer and melanoma, combined CXCR4 antagonist and anti-PD-1 therapy reduced Treg infiltration, thereby enhancing anti-PD-1 efficacy against tumors44. Although the efficacy and safety of treatments targeting Tregs remain contentious, studies analyzing clinical single-cell sequencing data have identified high expression of CD177 in tumor-infiltrating Tregs in ccRCC. Furthermore, Treg-specific deletion of CD177 in mice demonstrated significant tumor shrinkage45. By integrating scRNA-seq and ST data, we further clarified the significant impact of Tregs on patient prognosis in ccRCC, which was notably stronger than that of other immune cells. And we uncovered that the interferon-gamma signaling pathway as a potential target for inhibiting the immunosuppressive function of Tregs within the TME of ccRCC. Interferon-gamma has been shown to inhibit Treg differentiation and function46, thus targeting and sensitizing tumor-infiltrating Tregs to respond to interferon-gamma in ccRCC patients may further improve the efficacy of immunotherapy and prognosis.

  As mentioned above, in addition to the issue of immunotherapy sensitivity in ccRCC, the metastasis of advanced ccRCC also requires great attention. CAFs have been widely reported to play a multifaceted role in tumor proliferation, invasion, migration, and response to immunotherapy, serving as key players in the tumor progression process47. In recent years, with the advancement of sequencing technologies, the subtypes of CAFs have been studied in greater depth. matCAFs were identified on a pan-cancer single cell transcriptional atlas and they were much more crucial to drive the progression of several cancer types48. However, limited research has explored the functional role of matCAFs in tumor progression, let alone the development of therapeutic strategies targeting these cells. In addition to the further confirmation of the significant effect of matCAFs on the prognosis of ccRCC, we also discovered that dysregulation of the p53 signaling pathway may be a critical mechanism by which metastatic matCAFs continuously promote tumor EMT. p53 deletion in fibroblasts led to a striking accumulation in left ventricular pressure overload, model of cardiomyopathy13,49. Therefore, downregulation of the p53 signaling pathway may extend lifespan for matCAFs, leading to their accumulation in the TME of ccRCC. ST analysis further confirms that matCAFs widely infiltrated around the tumor cells. During the analysis of cell–cell chat, apCAF subpopulation has also been identified as a strong bridge between tumor and immune cells, supporting the previous evidence50. However, we did not observe a strong colocalization among apCAFs, T cells and cancer cells in the ST analysis.

  Afterward, we utilized a combination of Lasso and stepwise Cox regression using both forward and backward selection that incorporated the unique gene expression profiles of three tumor subtypes to develop a model for assessing risk. The use of algorithm combinations can effectively decrease the number of variables, resulting in a more streamlined and translatable model. Ultimately, FABP7, DDX17, MAST4, PDK4, SLC25A37, PAX2, ZBTB20, IGFBP4, NKAIN4, and SEMA5B have been identified as the central genes of Scissor + cancer cells. As expected, the model indicated that individuals with a high-risk score are more likely to have a more progressive tumor and poorer overall survival rate. Additionally, patients with a high risk score predicted a poorer response to immunotherapy. Therefore, to improve the immune response and patients’ prognosis, we identified Rapamycin as a potential selection when patients are resistant to the immunotherapy.

  Moreover, we focus on the clinical translation and application. All the identified targets in our study have potential clinical value and need to be investigated deeply. Here, we focus our discussion on the relatively important targets among them. p53 is a popular target in cancer therapy due to its high mutation rate, however, there are few p53 based strategies approved to apply in clinical practice51. While p53-based small molecules, p53-based cancer immunotherapy and p53-based genetic therapies were created to mainly target the cancer cells, our findings provide a fresh insight that p53-based therapeutic strategies can also target the CAFs in ccRCC. In addition, there have been a series of CXCL12-CXCR4 signaling axis inhibitors or antagonists applied in pre-clinical studies in multiple cancer types except for renal cancer and these drugs also improve the efficacy of immune checkpoint blockade52. And it is noteworthy that rapamycin has a lowest mean IC50 in our analysis compared with other drugs for patients with high-risk score, while we have not validated the effect of this therapeutic strategy in clinical trials yet based on our own risk score model. PI3K/Akt/mTOR signaling pathway were discovered to be abnormal in research including over 100 patients with RCC53, and the FDA has approved temsirolimus and everolimus, two rapamycin derivatives, for the clinical treatment of cancer patients, including those with RCC. Though such clinical treatments were not based on a selection of patients with an identified risk score, our clinical validation may serve as a complementary strategy to enhance the precision of medical applications in future therapeutic development.

  However, it is important to recognize certain limitations. Firstly, although efforts were made to ensure the reliability of the clustering analysis, larger datasets could enhance and refine the results. Secondly, the correlation between cell subtypes and prognosis was dependent on the sample size of bulk data, the specific number of single cells and the selection of parameters of Scissor method. All the disturbance of above factors can possibly affect the distribution and the number of Scissor + cells. Additionally, overfitting is a problem worthy of attention in the machine learning. We referenced prior studies11,14,54,55, and the researchers in these studies applied cross validation, combining multiple machine learning methods and increase the data. Though we have integrated as much data as possible and use tenfold cross-validation as they did to mitigate the influence of overfitting, the risk score model maybe still not appropriate for all the other data in the real world. Subsequently, we will further increase clinical cohort data, simplify dimensional features, and apply ensemble methods. Besides, the observed correlation could be interpreted either as a direct effect of a specific cell type on patient prognosis or as an indirect effect mediated through other cell types. For direct effect, tumor cells can remodel the tumor microenvironment through metabolic reprogramming, thereby critically driving tumorigenesis and progression., for example, Zhang et al. demonstrate that lncRNA BANCR inhibits G6PD dimerization, reducing ccRCC growth in vivo 56. For indirect effect, the interplay between tumor cells, Tregs, and CAFs creates a self-reinforcing immunosuppressive and pro-metastatic ecosystem. Tumor cell-derived lactate polarizes CAFs toward a pro-angiogenic phenotype, while CAF-selected metabolites fuel tumor glycolysis 57. Moreover, Tregs and CAFs collaboratively upregulate PD-L1 and IDO1, excluding cytotoxic lymphocytes from tumor cores 58–60. In the end, as our work is computational and omic-based, experimental validation of the core genes of Scissor + cancer cells is necessary. In the future, we will not only focus on the clinical cohort study of the risk score model to verify the reliability of the model, but also conduct clinical translational research on drugs related to the identified targets. In summary, we characterized three distinct prognosis related tumor subtypes and their interaction between Tregs as well as matCAFs via scRNA-seq, ST, and bulk RNA-seq data. Besides, we quantified their impact on clinical outcomes and help to select appropriate therapy for different patients. These findings provide a potential possibility for further improving the prognosis of ccRCC.
