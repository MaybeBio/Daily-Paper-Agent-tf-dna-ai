# System biology-based assessment of the molecular mechanism of epigallocatechin gallate in Parkinson’s disease: via network pharmacology, in-silico evaluation & in-vitro studies


## Supplementary Information
  The online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-11592-9.


## Introduction
  Parkinson’s Disease (PD) is among the dreaded uncontrolled neurodegenerative disease characterized by the loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra part of the brain1. Motor and non-motor symptoms like postural instability, muscle movement incoordination, fatigue, and pain remain common in PD patients2. Recent studies have shown how natural molecules are involved in the modulation of PD3. Numerous natural molecules have been identified to modulate oxidative stress and reactive oxygen species (ROS), resulting in neuroprotection4. Numerous Asian natural molecules have been studied as neuroprotective agents5. Epigallocatechin gallate moiety (EGCG), multiple times, has a wide range of catechins, which have been involved in numerous biological mechanisms and neuroprotective studies; a pretreatment PD mice model experiment visualized that at a minimal dosage, it exhibited a neuroprotective effect and decreased cell damage6,7. EGCG has been reported as anti-cancer, diabetes mellitus, anti-viral, HIV infection, and anti-hypertension. Furthermore, EGCG has been reported as a potent down-regulator of neuroinflammation, oxidative stress, and a neuro-rescue agent8, as shown in Fig. 1.Fig. 1EGCG with multiple involvements in the pathogenesis of diseases.

  Among the other catechins with both in-vitro and in-vivo studies, EGCG has been widely reported to have antioxidative effects by downregulating ROS levels; despite the research, the mechanisms of EGCG as antioxidants remain fully understood9,10. Mitochondria have a greater contribution in the pathology of PD, oxidative stress due to excess hydrogen peroxide, hydroxy radicals, and nitrosative groups, which results in cellular death since substantia nigra is enriched with iron (Fe2+) which has resulted in the toxicity to the cells11,12. Biological tools have a greater affinity in screening the hits with various approaches and identifications of multiple targets13,14. EGCG has been previously reported and evaluated as a neuroprotective agent in the in-vitro/in-vivo models of PD, Huntington’s disease, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, and spinocerebellar ataxia15,16. In this contextual relationship, we have assigned biological tools to identify the probable molecular mechanism for EGCG in the modulation of ROS and oxidative stress in PD by employing computational tools like gene enrichment analysis, gene ontology studies, and molecular docking with protein-EGCG stability.


## Materials and methods

### Physicochemical properties of EGCG
  The physicochemical properties and drug-likeness score (DLS) of the EGCG were retrieved using databases such as PubChem, Indian Medicinal Plants (IMPPAT), Phytochemistry and Therapeutics, and the MolSoft LLC database (https://www.molsoft.com/). The targets involved in the modulation of PD were retrieved using the DisGeNET database (https://www.disgenet.org/), whereas the search keywords used were “CUI: C0949855, CUI: C0751651, CUI: C3825201, and CUI: C0030567”. These keywords are specific in the modulation of PD, with an overall gene count of 466317. The targets modulated by the EGCG were retrieved using Swiss target prediction (http://www.swisstargetprediction.ch/) using SMILES with an overall gene count of 3118.


### Gene ontology (GO) enrichment
  The GO terms modulated by the EGCG were retrieved from the STRING database, where the biological process (BP), molecular function (MF), and Cellular component (CC) were collected (KEGG) for the highest modulated target. Further, the two-tailed Pearson correlation coefficient was computed for the GO terms using GraphPad Prism 819.


#### Network construction
  The targets were collected from the DisGeNET database, using the query keywords “CUI: C0949855, CUI: C0751651, CUI: C3825201, and CUI: C0030567” were matched with the targets obtained from the Swiss target prediction for the ECGC. The common targets were further integrated into protein–protein interaction via the STRING database (https://string-db.org/)20. The KEGG pathway was obtained for the common targets, and a pathway-protein interaction network was constructed by using Cytoscape 3.10.021. The network was formed between the pathway and gene, where the interactions were treated as directed, and the network was interpreted in the form of node and edge counts, with the edge betweenness by degree and neighborhood connectivity.


#### Clustering and its analysis
  ClueGo, an additional tool in Cytoscape 3.10.022 performed clustering analysis for the targets. The clustering was analyzed for the KEGG pathway, BP, MF, and CC-enriched genes, where the enrichment/depletion (Two-sided hypergeometric test) statistical approach was used with a p value cutoff of 0.05. The network specificity was set to medium with a GO tree interval between 3 and 8. The cluster selection for GO terms was set to 3 targets with a 4% threshold. The final Kappa score was obtained, which indicates the significance of the clustering, where the analysis was at 7, which indicates the significance of the clustering, where the analysis used the Bonferroni step-down method.


#### Molecular docking studies
  In the current study, the ECGC compound obtained from the IMPPAT database was docked with 23 common targets (CREBBP, PPARGC1A, NTRK2, MAP2K1, PRKDC, PDE2A, MAP4K4, PRKAA1, NTRK3l, ADORA1, PRKAA2, HDAC1, ALOX5, FOXO1, SIRT3, CDK1, RELA, EPHX2, NTRK1, HIF1A, SOD2, UBB, IGF1R) which were found common between the DisGeNET database and compound target. Here, the 2 different algorithms, AutoDock 4.2 and Schrodinger suite, are utilized. The ECGC compound and proteins were minimized before proceeding with molecular docking studies.


#### Reason for using 2 algorithm-based molecular docking
  The AutoDock 4.0 software is free and open-source software that utilizes algorithms such as “Genetic algorithm”, “Simulated annealing”, and “Lamarckian genetic algorithm” which makes the protein act as rigid, and the ligand acts as flexible, the scoring functions depend on the empirical free energy function which includes hydrogen, electrostatics and desolvation energies which include poor accuracy for the binding energy. The Schrodinger suite applies the “grid-based” method in ligand docking, mainly. It also includes “induced fit docking”, and mainly, it also includes “induced fit docking”, where it can accommodate receptor flexibility. The scoring function utilizes a sophisticated glide score that calculates the “empirical energies” and “molecular mechanics energies,” along with more accurate solvation and entropy methods. Both software AutoDock 4.0 and the Schrodinger suite exhibited different docking scores, as AutoDock 4.0 exhibited high docking scores and the Schrodinger suite exhibited lower docking scores with the selected targets.


#### Preparation of the ECGC compound
  EGCG (ECGC compound) was prepared in “mol” format in ChemDraw 19.0, where the ligands were minimized to the “pdb”. format using ChemDraw 3D, utilizing MM2 for energy minimization, and the compounds were converted to the “pdbqt” (charged molecule) for further study23. The “LigPrep” module was utilized to (maestro Schrodinger suite) obtain the best confirmations of the molecules, whereas the pH was kept at 7.0 ± 7.2 using the OPLS4 forcefield24,25.


#### Reason for the selection of the EGCG
  The initiation of the study was carried out with different types of compounds, such as IMPHY000752, IMPHY000569, IMPHY000827, IMPHY000622, IMPHY000833, IMPHY000089, IMPHY002073, IMPHY001637, IMPHY001738, IMPHY002390, IMPHY002343, IMPHY001777, IMPHY001988, IMPHY001830, IMPHY002186, and EGCG. The phytoconstituent EGCG was the most active phytoconstituent in modulating targets mainly involved in the progression of Parkinson’s disease. The targets modulated by the compound EGCG were common with the disease targets obtained using the DisGeNET database.


#### Preparation of the targets for AutoDock 4.2
  The 23 common targets were retrieved from the Protein Data Bank (RCSB; https://www.rcsb.org/), the targets were prepared using BOVIA “Discovery Studio Visualizer”, the heteroatoms and water molecules were removed, and the missing amino acids were added, the prepared targets were further added with “polar hydrogens” and “Kollman charges” which were saved in the “pdbqt”. The grid box was generated at the center of the targets with XYZ coordinates and 0.37 Å spacing. Further, the “Genetic algorithm” and “Lamarckian Genetic Algorithm” were utilized to run the molecular docking26.


#### Preparation of protein for Schrodinger suite (Maestro 2022–24)
  The 23 common targets were prepared using the “protein-preparation wizard tool,” where the water molecules were removed using the OPLS4 force field, with an RMSD of 0.30 Å, and the optimization of hydrogen bonds was done using PROPKA27. Further, the “grid box” was generated using “receptor grid generation” in the proteins/target. Further, the “Ligand Docking” and “extra precision” (XP) module was utilized for the molecular docking28.


### Molecular dynamics (MD) simulation studies

#### Reason for the selection of specific targets
  Among all the targets, the SIRT3, FOXO1, and PPARGC1A (PGC-1α) were found to have the highest number of edge counts, numerous studies have reported that solely upregulation of SIRT3 can potentiate the activation of FOXO1 (antioxidant-gene) and PGC-1α, whereas the activation of FOXO1 downregulates the ROS level, and oxidative stress via mitochondrial access29–31. The direct activation of FOXO1 is improper, whereas it can be modulated via SIRT3. The “Desmond” module of the Schrodinger suite was utilized to perform the MD studies. The “system builder” was utilized in baking the solvation of the target-molecule complex by associating “TIP4P” as a solvent, whereas the box size was added with “buffer” with a distance of 10/10/10 Å and was minimized, the model was neutralized by adding Na + ions with an “OPLS4” forcefield. Further, the target-molecule complex was minimized at 100 picoseconds. The target/TIP4P/molecule complex residues were evaluated at 100 ns with a 50-trajectory at NPT at 311 kelvins with a pressure of 1.0132532,33. Moreover, these specific targets, SIRT3, FOXO1, and PPARGC1A (PGC-1α), were found to have the highest docking scores with the compound EGCG (ECGC compound).

The GUSAR program predicts toxicity using a collection of software, including a database of over 10,000 chemical substances. The “QSAR” (Quantitative structure–activity relationship) method was utilized to predict the LD50 (Lethal-dose) of the compound at a concentration of log 10 mg/Kg. The reports indicated the probable routes, such as, such as intraperitoneal, intravenous, subcutaneous, and oral, via which the compound can be administered35.

The cells were cultured in the 96 well plates suspended in 10% of the growth medium and 10% of the growth medium and incubated at 37 °C in a humidified 5% CO2 incubator. The compound stock solutions were prepared by dissolving 1 mg of the drug in 1 mL of DMSO. Freshly prepared compounds in DMEM perform the neurotoxicity evaluation, and the derail two-fold dilutions with 100 µg, 50 µg, 25 µg, 12.5 µg, and 6.25 µg in 500 µL of DMEM were added to the wells and were incubated at 37 °C in a humidified 5% CO2 incubator36.


#### Neuroprotective assay
  The SHSY-5Y (Neuroblastoma cells) were purchased from the National Centre for Cell Sciences, Pune, India, and were maintained in the Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s media (DMEM) (HIMEDIA) (Sigma Aldrich, USA). The cells were cultured using DMEM, 10% of the FBS, and antibiotics such as Penicillin (100 U/mL), Streptomycin (100 µg/mL), and Amphotericin B (2.5 µg/mL). The cells were cultured in the 96-well plates suspended in 10% of the growth medium and incubated at 37 °C in a humidified 5% CO2 incubator. The compound stock solutions were prepared by dissolving 1 mg of the drug in 1 mL of DMSO. MPP + (1-methyl-4-phenylpyridinium) was used to induce toxicity in the cells, and the cells were incubated for 1 h. In the meantime, 5% of the compound stock solutions were serially diluted five times, using two-fold dilutions, to obtain 25 µg, 12.5 µg, 6.25 µg, 3.1 µg, and 1.5 µg in 500 µL of 5% DMEM. Each concentration of 100 µL was added to the 96-well plates, which were then incubated at 37 °C in a humidified 5% CO2 incubator. Following a 24-h incubation period, the MTT method perform the cytotoxicity assay. MTT solutions were added to the test and control groups, and the cells were incubated for four hours. The microplate reader was used to measure the absorbance at 540 nm37.


### Gene expression assay
  The SHSY5Y human neuroblastoma cell lines were procured from the National Centre for Cell Sciences (NCCS), Pune, India, and Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM) (Sigma Aldrich, USA). The cell lines were cultured in a 25 cm2 flask containing DMEM solution (10% FBS, L-glutamine, and antibiotic solutions). The cell seeding was done in 96-well plates, whereas the cells were trypsinized and suspended in 10% growth medium and incubated at 37 °C in a humidified 5% CO2 incubator. The cells were cultured in 96-well plates. The toxicity was induced by administering 0.5 mM of MPP +. To isolate RNA, cells were induced with TRIzol reagent and incubated for 5 min before adding 200 µL of chloroform and 500 µL of 100% isopropanol. The cells were then centrifuged at 14,000 rpm for 15 min at 4 °C. The RNA pellets were dried and then suspended in a TE buffer solution. The cDNA was created using the complementary cDNA preparation kit (G BIOSCIENCES, master premix for first-strand cDNA synthesis). To the suspended RNA pellets, 5 µL of RT Easy mix, 0.5 µL of oligo dT (deoxy-thymidine), and 2 μL of RNA template were added to prepare the cDNA templates. Utilizing the SYBR Green master mix (G BIOSCIENCES, Product code-786-5062) for the gene expression analysis, the following steps were performed at 10 s at 95ºC for denaturation, 1 min at 58 °C for annealing the DNA templates, and 1 min/kb at 72 °C for 40 cycles for the extension of the DNA templates. Further, agarose gel electrophoresis was performed38.

## Discussion
  Employing several biological tools, including molecular docking, gene set enrichment studies, gene ontology studies, statistical analysis (Pearson-correlation analysis), molecular dynamic studies, and in-vitro studies, the present work aimed to hypothesize the probable involved molecular mechanism of the ECGC compound (EGCG) as the neuroprotective agent in PD. The dose-dependent ECGC compound has been reported to be effective in various PD animal models exhibiting neuroprotective effects via preventing/downregulating oxidative stress41–43. In various studies, the ECGC compound has been found to exert an effect on the modulation of mitochondrial functions, mitochondrial biogenesis, mitochondrial bioenergetic control (electron transport chain), and mitochondrial-based cell death44,45. The causes for Parkinson’s disease (PD) are still unclear and complex, despite the fact that ECGC compounds have a strong influence on neuroprotection and ROS (oxidative stress) downregulation. Initially, we used the DisGeNET database to gather the targets involved in PD modulation, and we found 4663 targets implicated in PD modulation46. Further, the ECGC compound (EGCG) predicted 31 targets, which were obtained using Swiss target prediction, and target binding databases, among both datasets we identified 23 common targets (CREBBP, PPARGC1A, NTRK2, MAP2K1, PRKDC, PDE2A, MAP4K4, PRKAA1, NTRK3l, ADORA1, PRKAA2, HDAC1, ALOX5, FOXO1, SIRT3, CDK1, RELA, EPHX2, NTRK1, HIF1A, SOD2, UBB, IGF1R) that were directly involved in the pathogenesis of PD. The matched common targets were queried into the STRING database to compare and identify the protein–protein interactions, KEGG pathway, and gene identification47. Further, the KEGG pathway predicted 81 integrated pathways, among which the top 10 pathways were selected for further analysis. The target/pathway/EGCG network was constructed using Cytoscape 3.10.0, analyzing the network, the 3 targets (SIRT3, FOXO1, and PPARGC1A) were found to have the highest number of “edge counts”. The GO enrichment studies were performed for BP, MF, and CC. The gene ontology studies for BP exhibited “Response to oxidative stress” (GO:0,006,979) with the lowest false discovery rate of 8.98E−09. The response to oxidative stress was found to be modulating 26 targets (CDC34, ATP5F1B, CREBBP, PPARGC1A, NTRK2, MAP2K1, PRKDC, PDE2A, MAP4K4, PRKAA1, NTRK3, ADORA1, PRKAA2, HDAC1, ALOX5, FOXO1, ESRRB, SIRT3, CDK1, RELA, EPHX2, NTRK1, HIF1A, SOD2, UBB, IGF1R) whereas the targets SIRT3, FOXO1, SOD2, and PPARGC1A which are also involved in the downregulation the ROS levels, and oxidative stress via mitochondrial process48, as shown in Fig. 16. The MF and CC exhibited “Organic cyclic compound binding” (GO:0,097,159) and “Cytoplasm” with a false discovery rate of 4.19E−07 and 0.00051.Fig. 16Probable mechanism for the highest modulated and docked proteins SIRT3, FOXO1, and PPARGC1A.   Further, molecular docking studies were done for the 23 common targets with the ECGC compound (EGCG) by using 2 software, “AutoDock 4.2”, and the “Glide module”. The Schrodinger suite (glide) represented that the targets CREBBP, NTRK2, MAP2K1, PDE2A, MAP4K4, PRKAA1, NTRK3, PRKAA2, CDK1, RELA, SOD2, UBB, IGF1R were found to have glide score ranging from − 6.51 to − 7.42 kcal/mol; whereas ADORA1, ALOX5, HDAC1, NTRK1, HIF1A, PRKDC, EPHX2 exhibited no interactions with the ECGC compound. The highest glide scores were exhibited by SIRT3, FOXO1, and PPARGC1A, where the glide score was found to be − 8.95, − 8.0, and − 7.217 kcal/mol. The highest glide score was exhibited by SIRT3 (PDB ID: 4FVT); the amino acid interactions were found to be SER 321, THR 320, GLU 323, GLU 177, GLN 228, ASN 229, ASP 231, and a hydrophobic amino acid interaction with ILE 230. Whereas, the AutoDock 4.2 results indicated that the targets CREBBP, NTRK2, MAP2K1, PDE2A, MAP4K4, PRKAA1, NTRK3, PRKAA2, CDK1, RELA, SOD2, UBB, IGF1R, ADORA1, NTRK1, and HIF1A exhibited binding energy between − 1.77 and 9.24 kcal/mol; whereas the targets HDAC1, ALOX5, PRKDC, and EPHX2 exhibited no amino acid interactions with the compound ECGC. The highest binding energy was observed for FOXO1, PPARGC1A, and SIRT3 with a binding energy of − 10.21, − 9.55, and − 11.42 kcal/mol. The SIRT3 (PDB ID: 4FVT) was found to have interactions with ASP A:231, PRO A:155, and ILE A:230, with a π−π interaction bonding with PHE A: 157. The network pharmacology approach revealed the “FOXO signaling pathway”, “Longevity regulating pathway multiple species”, and “AMPK signaling pathway” to be mostly modulated via SIRT3, FOXO1, PRKAA1, PPARGC1A, and CREBBP targets; these strong indications suggested by the gene enrichment studies and network pharmacology approach revealed that the modulation of SIRT3, FOXO1, and PPARGC1A could be directly involved in the downregulation of ROS levels and oxidative stress which could provide a neuroprotective effect, hence SIRT3 could be a potent target in the downregulation of ROS levels via FOXO1, and PPARGC1A49. The antioxidant effect, which is primarily necessary to counteract ROS levels and oxidative stress, may be inhibited by FOXO1 knockout; also, SIRT3 overexpression has led to the upregulation of FOXO1, which has altered ROS levels50. Further, the clustering of the network produced the “FOXO signaling pathway” to be the most modulated pathway, such as the longevity regulating pathway, AMPK signaling pathway, glucagon signaling pathways, and, prostate cancer; the involvement of the clustered pathways has been reported to be essential in aging, and cancer modulation51. In the in-vitro studies at various concentrations of EGCG and resveratrol, the highest % cell viability was obtained at 6.25 µg/mL and 12.5 µg/mL with a cell survival of 94% and 81%. In the presence of toxicity-induced groups, the compounds EGCG and resveratrol were found to maintain the number of cells with a % cell viability of 86%. The compounds EGCG and resveratrol were found to have a potent neuroprotective effect, so the gene expression studies with the targets SOD2, FOXO1, and GPx were performed, and the compound EGCG was found to overexpress the expression of the genes. In the gene expression study, it was found that the compound EGCG was found to overexpress the fold-change of the SOD2, FOXO1, and GPx. It is well known that the overexpression of SIRT3 coactivates the targets SOD2, FOXO1, and GPx.


## Conclusion
  Using a variety of targets and biological tools, including network pharmacology, molecular docking, gene set enrichment analysis, statistical methods, and molecular dynamic investigations, the current study aims to determine the likely molecular pathway of ECGC chemical in the treatment of Parkinson’s disease. Several molecular processes involved in the modulation of Parkinson’s disease may be modulated by the ECGC molecule, according to the data. Several targets, including SIRT3, FOXO1, PPARGC1A, and CREBBP, are directly involved in regulating ROS levels and oxidative stress; altering these targets may help stop additional cellular damage. These conclusions and forecasts, however, are based on the allegations that have already been published. These results, however, require comprehensive, in-depth research and validation in PD animal models. Finding the precise gene and route implicated in the pathophysiology of Parkinson’s disease (PD) is still a significant challenge, and this research will continue to focus on this area in the future.
