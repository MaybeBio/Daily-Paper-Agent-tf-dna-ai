## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, conclusions) as provided by the user.
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific and technical soundness of the reported virtual screening strategies, hit rates, and experimental validation, as presented in the provided text. No external data or literature beyond the manuscript was consulted.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report that an AI-based ultrahigh-throughput virtual screening (uHTVS) workflow, specifically Deep Docking, can achieve high hit rates (up to 50.0%) against protein-protein interaction (PPI) targets, specifically the SH2 domains of STAT3 and STAT5b and the N-terminal domain (NTD) of STAT5b, using compound libraries of varying sizes (5.59 million to 5.51 billion). They benchmark this against "knowledge-based" approaches (a targeted SH2 library and a natural product library) and a diverse subset of a larger library. They also report the first virtual screening campaign against the STAT5b NTD, identifying new dimerization inhibitors.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract, Introduction, Methods (Data Sets, Docking and Virtual Screening, Fluorescence Polarization Assay, Isothermal Titration Calorimetry), Results (Benchmarking, STAT5b SH2, STAT5b NTD), Discussion (Docking Score Distribution and Runtime Analysis), Conclusions. References to figures and tables are present but the figures/tables themselves are not provided.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Figures (including Figure 1, 2, 3, 4), Tables (including Table 1, 2), Supporting Information (Figures S1-S6, Table S1, Supplementary Data with compound structures, docking scores, IC50/Kd values, and ChEMBL/DUD-E datasets). These are critical for verifying docking performance metrics, compound structures, and experimental data.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript presents a timely and relevant evaluation of an AI-based uHTVS workflow against challenging PPI targets, with a logical progression from benchmarking on STAT3 to prospective application on STAT5b SH2 and NTD. The reported hit rates are notably high and the focus on computational efficiency is practically valuable. However, the core claims rest heavily on data presented in figures, tables, and supplementary materials that were not available for review. The experimental validation, while appropriate (FP and ITC), is limited in depth (e.g., no selectivity data, no cellular assays), and the manuscript would benefit from a more critical discussion of the limitations of the docking models for PPI targets. The technical soundness of the Deep Docking implementation appears plausible, but the lack of detailed benchmarking statistics (e.g., recall, precision per iteration) in the main text weakens the assessment. The claim of "first application" for STAT5b NTD is strong and requires careful verification against the existing literature.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Computational chemists and structural biologists working on virtual screening method development, particularly those interested in AI/ML-accelerated workflows. Researchers in the STAT signaling field, especially those seeking new chemical probes for STAT3 and STAT5b, would find the identified inhibitors of interest. The study also has broader relevance for groups targeting challenging PPI interfaces with limited structural information.
- **Major strengths**
  - The study addresses a significant and current problem: the practical application of uHTVS to difficult PPI targets.
  - The comparison between AI-based and "knowledge-based" approaches is a valuable contribution, providing a benchmark for method selection.
  - The demonstration that Deep Docking can be effective with a smaller library (millions vs. billions) is a practical and important finding for groups with limited computational resources.
  - The prospective application to a novel target (STAT5b NTD) and the identification of new inhibitors is a strong validation of the workflow.
  - The inclusion of runtime analysis provides a useful practical metric for assessing the cost-effectiveness of the methods.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **R1-M1** (Blocking: Yes): The central claims of exceptional hit rates and the success of the Deep Docking workflow are entirely dependent on the experimental data (IC50, Kd) and the docking scores, which are presented in figures and supplementary tables that were not provided. Without these data, the validity of the hit rates (e.g., 50.0%, 42.9%) cannot be assessed. The definition of "active" (IC50 < 100 μM) is also weak, and the lack of dose-response curves or raw data prevents verification of the quality of the measurements.
  - **R1-M2** (Blocking: Yes): The manuscript claims the "first application of virtual screening against its N-terminal domain" for STAT5b. This is a strong novelty claim. The provided text does not include a comprehensive literature search or a detailed comparison with any prior virtual screening efforts on this domain, making it impossible to verify this claim. The authors must provide a thorough literature review to substantiate this.
  - **R1-M3** (Blocking: No): The performance of the Deep Docking workflow is compared against a "Benchmark" dataset of 117,500 diverse compounds. However, the selection of this dataset size and the diversity picking method are not justified. It is unclear if this is a fair comparison, as the "knowledge-based" approaches use much smaller, targeted libraries. The manuscript would be strengthened by a more rigorous experimental design for the comparison, such as using multiple random subsets or different diversity selection methods.
  - **R1-M4** (Blocking: No): The manuscript reports hit rates but does not provide a detailed analysis of the chemical properties of the hits, their selectivity (e.g., against STAT3 vs. STAT5b, or against other SH2 domains), or their mechanism of action. The discussion of the "1,3-benzodioxole motif" and its interaction with R595 is interesting but is based on a single docking pose and requires further validation (e.g., mutagenesis, SAR). The lack of any cellular or functional data limits the translational significance of the findings.
- **Minor Comments**
  - **R1-m1** (Axis: Clarity): The abstract states "hit rates (50.0% for STAT3)" but the results section reports a 19% hit rate for the Mcule-in-stock library against STAT3. The 50.0% is for the Enamine REAL library. This should be clarified in the abstract to avoid confusion.
  - **R1-m2** (Axis: Reproducibility): The manuscript mentions an in-house script for Deep Docking, but the specific version and parameters used are not fully detailed. The authors should provide a more complete description of the software environment, including versions of all dependencies (e.g., Python, PyTorch, RDKit, KNIME) to ensure reproducibility.
  - **R1-m3** (Axis: Data Presentation): The manuscript refers to "Table" and "Figure" without numbers in several places (e.g., "Table  ", "Figure  "). This is a formatting error that must be corrected.
  - **R1-m4** (Axis: Statistical Rigor): The hit rates are reported as simple percentages. Given the small number of compounds tested in some cases (e.g., 8 from Enamine REAL), the confidence intervals for these hit rates are likely very wide. The authors should report confidence intervals or use a statistical test to compare hit rates between methods.
  - **R1-m5** (Axis: Terminology): The term "Virtual-Hit Likeness value (VHL value or also termed as p-value within the workflow)" is confusing. The use of "p-value" in this context is non-standard and could be misinterpreted as a statistical significance value. The authors should use a distinct term to avoid confusion.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established**
  - The lack of access to the primary data (figures, tables, supplementary) is the most significant failing, as it prevents any verification of the reported results.
  - The weak definition of "active" (IC50 < 100 μM) and the lack of detailed experimental validation (e.g., no selectivity, no cellular activity) are major technical shortcomings.
  - The novelty claim for the STAT5b NTD is not supported by a sufficient literature review in the provided text.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**
  - **Originality:** The application of Deep Docking to a novel target (STAT5b NTD) is original, but the overall methodology is not new. The comparative benchmarking is a useful contribution, but the core approach is an application of existing tools.
  - **Scientific importance:** The work addresses an important problem (targeting PPI) and provides a practical workflow that could be widely adopted. The identification of new inhibitors for STAT5b, a challenging target, is of potential importance. However, the lack of functional validation limits the immediate impact.
  - **Interdisciplinary readership:** The work is primarily of interest to computational chemists and structural biologists. The experimental validation is minimal, which may limit its appeal to a broader biological or clinical audience.
  - **Technical soundness:** The computational workflow appears sound, but the lack of detailed benchmarking data and the weak experimental validation prevent a full assessment. The comparison with "knowledge-based" methods is not rigorously designed.
  - **Readability for nonspecialists:** The manuscript is generally well-written and the logic is clear. However, the heavy reliance on specialized terminology (e.g., "DS/HA", "VHL value") and the lack of context for the biological significance of STAT proteins may make it challenging for a non-specialist to fully appreciate the impact.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The manuscript has the potential to be a valuable contribution, but the core claims cannot be verified without the missing data. The authors must provide the full dataset, strengthen the experimental validation, and address the concerns regarding the novelty claim and the comparison methodology. If these issues are resolved, the manuscript could be considered for publication in a specialized computational chemistry journal, but it is unlikely to meet the broad-impact bar for Nature.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The reported hit rates (e.g., 50.0%, 42.9%, 19%, 21.4%) are unsupported without the underlying experimental data.
- The claim of "first application of virtual screening against its N-terminal domain" for STAT5b is unverifiable from the provided text.
- The claim that the Deep Docking workflow "can achieve exceptionally good hit rates" is a general statement that is not fully supported by the limited data presented in the text.
- The statement that the identified compounds are "suitable for further chemical optimization" is an opinion that is not supported by any data on their physicochemical properties, solubility, or synthetic accessibility.
- The assertion that the "AI-based approach can work well even with evaluating a smaller dataset" is based on a single comparison and may not be generalizable.
