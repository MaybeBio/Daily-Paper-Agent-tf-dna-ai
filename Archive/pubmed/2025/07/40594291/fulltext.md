# Genome-wide analysis of the ammonium transporters gene family in Phaseolus vulgaris and its roles in response to drought and salinity stress


## Supplementary Information
  The online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-05795-3.


## Introduction
  The direct absorption of inorganic nitrogen from the soil, such as ammonium and nitrate, plays a crucial role in promoting crop growth and increasing yield formation. The uptake of ammonium nitrogen by plants through the ammonium transporter (AMT) is crucial for fulfilling their nitrogen needs, particularly in waterlogged or acidic soils with high levels of ammonium1. An AMT is defined by its arrangement of 11–12 transmembrane regions, which may exhibit slight differences in structure between one another. AMTs are plentiful in the plant kingdom and can be grouped into two distinct subcategories, AMT1 and AMT2, determined by their genetic diversity2,3. Besides the earlier discussed process of extracting ammonium from the soil, AMTs also serve diverse physiological functions in plants. Arabidopsis roots contain five AMTs: AtAMT1;1, AtAMT1;2, AtAMT1;3, AtAMT1;5, and AtAMT24,5. The simultaneous knockout of AtAMT1;1 and AtAMT1;2 led to a decrease of 60–70% in uptake6, while the triple knockout of AtAMT1;1, AtAMT1;2, and AtAMT1;3 resulted in a 90% reduction in ammonium absorption in Arabidopsis.

  The partnership between AMT-mediated symplasmic and apoplastic pathways enhances the absorption of ammonium from the soil solution1. The outer cell layers of roots predominantly show expression of AtAMT1;1 and AtAMT1;3, which function in the uptake of ammonium from the soil solution into root cells and its subsequent transport through the symplasmic pathway. Furthermore, in endothelial cells, AtAMT1;2 serves as the primary transporter for carrying ammonium into the cytoplasm to facilitate symplasmic transport4,7. In addition, there are many root-expressed AMTs that are functional and play a part in extracting ammonium from the soil solution. For instance, overexpression of GhAMT1;3, and PutAMT1;1 in Arabidopsis8,9, AMT1.3 in Nicotiana tabacum10, AMT1;1 in Oryza sativa11, as well as AMT1.1a and AMT1.3 in corn12, has been found to enhance the uptake of ammonium by plants during conditions of restricted ammonium availability. Three AMTs were investigated in in rice. Knocking out OsAMT1;1, OsAMT1;2, and OsAMT1;3 individually had no impact on ammonium uptake, but eliminating all three genes caused a 95% decrease in ammonium uptake13. On the other hand, AMT2 plays no role in Arabidopsis plants absorption of ammonium nitrogen from the soil. AMT2 becomes prominently active in the stele sheath upon treatment with ammonium, contributing to the transport of this nutrient from roots to shoots14. Clearly, the evidence gathered from investigating expression profiles of AMT genes in plants indicates that these genes are functional in various plant tissues. In soybean seedlings, the AMT family genes showed diverse spatiotemporal expression patterns in different tissues and organs15. Both LeAMT1;2 and LeAMT1;3 serve as effective ammonium transporters in tomato leaves, indicated by the presence of their transcripts16. Moreover, leaf samples show the presence of ZmAMT1.1a12, OsAMT1;117, ZmAMT1312, NtAMT1310, and GhAMT139. Additionally, the presence of AMTs in plant-pathogen relationships can be linked to their capacity for transporting ammonium. In wheat, it was stated that wheat stem rust disease infection may be influenced by TaAMTs18. Moreover, knocking out AMT1;1 in Arabidopsis leads to increased immunity against the necrotrophic fungus Plectosphaerella cucumerina and decreased susceptibility to the hemibiotrophic bacterium Pseudomonas syringae19.

  The common bean, scientifically known as Phaseolus vulgaris, is highly prized for its protein-rich properties among legumes. The European bean cultivars have a complex20 history and also the pan genome has been recently described21. The study of ammonium transporters in Phaseolus vulgaris remains a neglected area. In present study, identification and characterization of ammonium transporter encoding genes were done and also performed the expression of selected candidates under drought and salinity stress. In addition, the expression levels of PvAMT genes were examined concerning drought and salinity stress conditions. The findings revealed fresh perspectives concerning the structure, and function of PvAMT genes.


## Materials and methods

### Identification and characterization of PvAMTs
  The protein sequence of Arabidopsis thaliana AMTs was applied as query in BLASTp program of the Ensembl Plants database22 to recognize the corresponding AMT encoding genes in genome of Phaseolus vulgaris (PvAMT). All recognized proteins were checked using the Pfam to have the conserved domain (Pfam ID: PF00909). The confirmed proteins were named based on its location. Besides, AMTs were identified in the genome Oryza sativa and Medicago truncatula. Several protein physiochemical properties including GRAVY, molecular weight (MW), isoelectric point (pI), protein length, and instability index were determined using the Protparam tool23.


### Phylogeny analysis and duplication events
  In the first step, AMT protein sequences in Phaseolus vulgaris, Arabidopsis thaliana, Medicago truncatula and Oryza sativa were subjected to multiple alignment analysis using the Clustal Omega24. Then, the aligned file was introduced to IQtree software25 to create phylogeny tree using maximum likelihood with 1000 bootstrap. Finally, iTOL tool26 was applied to show the phylogeny tree. To identify duplication events, the coding sequence (CDS) of PvAMT genes was subjected to multiple alignment analysis by the GenomeNet database. Sequences that were more than 70% similar were determined as duplicated genes.


### RNA-seq data analysis
  High throughput sequencing studies of Phaseolus vulgaris in Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) were investigated. Two important studies were factored into this analysis. The first study (GEO accession: GSE192891) was conducted on cold-tolerant cultivar “120” and cold-sensitive cultivar “093”. The cold stress was applied by moving plants into an incubator at 4 °C (12 h daily light, 12 h dark) for a period of 3 days. The true leaves of the plants were selected for sampling according GEO. The second study (GEO accession: GSE156113) was on comparative transcriptome analysis of leaf and root tissues in salt-tolerant cultivar “Ispir” and salt-susceptible cultivar “TR43477”27. A Galaxy server workflow28 was employed for RNA-seq data analysis. Briefly, Fastq file quality was assessed by FastQC and adapter and unwanted sequences were removed by Cutadapt29. Cleaned reads were aligned to the reference genome by STAR30 and featureCounts31 was used to determine the number of reads mapped to each gene. Finally, differential expression analysis from the count table was performed by DESeq232.


### Co-expression network and transcription factors analysis
  Construction of a co-expression network of PvAMT genes was done using the method described by Contreras-López et al.33. Briefly, the correlation between every differentially expressed gene (DEGs) pair was calculated by R package “psych” and significant correlations between ammonium transporter genes and other DEGs were filtered. Network statistics are calculated by the “igraph” R package and visualized by Cytoscape. Upstream region (-500 bp) of ammonium transporter genes were obtained from Ensembl plant BioMart and PlantRegMap was used to scan them for TF-binding site prediction. Gene Ontology (GO) term enrichment analysis was conducted on the identified transcription factors (TF), and the findings were compiled using REVIGO34 and visualized by Cytoscape.


### Protein-protein interaction analysis
  PvAMT protein sequences and PII protein (UniProt accession: V7D130) sequences in fasta format were submitted to the SWISS-MODEL database35 and converted to PDB format. PDB files were submitted to the HADDOCK2.4 web server36 and the interaction of the C-terminal of each PvAMT and T-loop of PII protein was analyzed. Protein-protein interactions were visualized by LIGPLOT37.


### Prediction of 3D structure of PvAMT proteins
  The 3D structure of PvAMT proteins was predicted using the Phyre2 database38 based on homology models. The Phyre investigator tool on the Phyre2 server allowed us to generate predictions for pocket site locations within the 3D structure of PvAMT proteins, identifying them as potential ligand binding regions.


### Identification of phosphorylation site
  PvAMT proteins was introduced into the NetPhos Server 3.1 to predict the serine, threonine or tyrosine phosphorylation sites. In order to identify the phosphorylation sites with high potential, the probability level was set to more than 0.9.


### Plant materials and treatment
  Seeds of Phaseolus vulgaris cultivar “Kermanshah” prepared by Karaj Seedling and Seed Research Center and were sterilized with a 2% sodium hypochlorite solution for 6 min and subsequently rinsed twice with sterile water. In present study, two varieties of soil were utilized, each with varying levels of salt concentrations. First, regular soil with 45 ± 3 ppm sodium and saline soil with 120 ± 8 ppm sodium were prepared. The soils were distributed separately in the pots and then the seeds were sown at a depth of 5 cm. Following their first watering, the pots were moved to an environment with a regulated temperature range of 25 ± 3 °C and subjected to 14 h of light exposure. The pots were watered once every 4 days. When the seedlings reached 5 weeks of age, half of the pots containing regular soil (free from salinity stress) were exposed to drought stress. Drought stress was applied by cutting off irrigation for 10 days. Finally, three treatments including salinity, drought, and normal conditions (without stress) were evaluated separately. The shoot and root parts of bean seedlings were collected in the morning and quickly frozen in liquid nitrogen, then moved to a freezer set at -80 degrees for additional testing. The study was conducted in three separate biological replicates.


### Real-time PCR analysis
  The Hot phenol protocol39 was utilized to extract total RNA from both root and shoot tissues. For cDNA synthesis, 1 µg of total RNA from every sample was processed using the Easy™ cDNA synthesis Kit (Parstous Co, Mashhad, Iran), according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Six PvAMT genes (three genes from subfamily 1 and three genes from subfamily 2) were randomly selected along with actin gene, as reference gene, and their primers designed (Supplementary Table 1) based on their coding sequence by Primer 3 plus software v0.4.0 (https://www.bioinformatics.nl/cgi-bin/primer3plus/primer3plus.cgi). The ABI Step One (ABI, Germany) was utilized for all qRT-PCRs, with Ampliqon 2X qPCR Master Mix green-High Rox (Ampliqon, Stenhuggervej, Denmark) serving as the fluorescent reporter. The 10 µL reaction blend consisted of 5 µL of 2× SYBR Green Mix, 2 µL of cDNA (100 ng), 0.5 µL of both forward and reverse primers (10 pmols), along with 2 µL of deionized water. The qRT-PCR protocol included an initial denaturation phase at 95 °C for 120s, followed by 40 cycles of 95 °C for 10s and 60 °C for 40s. Each PvAMT gene was analyzed three biological replicates. The delta-delta ct method40 was utilized to calculate the relative expression levels of each gene.


## Results

### PvAMTs properties
  Eleven ammonium transporter members were identified in the bean genome (PvAMT) (Table 1). The length of encoded PvAMT proteins varied between 460 (PvAMT5) and 510 (PvAMT7) amino acids. PvAMT genes showed between 1 and 4 exons in their structure. Based on the pI index, most PvAMT proteins were considered neutral. According the instability index all identified PvAMTs belong to the class of stable proteins and have an instability index < 4041. GRAVY index with positive value determined that all PvAMT proteins are of hydrophobic nature.

### Phylogenetic analysis
  Phylogeny analysis based on amino acid sequence, separated PvAMT family members into two main subfamilies. PvAMT1, PvAMT5, PvAMT7, and PvAMT9 proteins were in subfamily I, and PvAMT2, PvAMT3, PvAMT4, PvAMT6, PvAMT8, PvAMT10, and PvAMT11 proteins were in subfamily II (Fig. 1). The all members of subfamily I had one exon, while 3 and 4 exons were observed among the members of subfamily II. Medicago truncatula AMTs showed the lowest genetic distance with PvAMT orthologs. Eleven PvAMT genes were distributed on seven bean chromosomes. Several duplication events were found among the members of this gene family (Supplementary Table 2). The results showed that there was a doubling between PvAMT2, PvAMT10 and PvAMT11, and there was also a doubling between PvAMT1-PvAMT9 and PvAMT7-PvAMT9 (Fig. 2). This is predicted that all duplicated PvAMT genes were segmentally expanded and the first duplication event was occurred between PvAMT2 and PvAMT11 (Supplementary Table 2).

### Transmembrane structure of PvAMT proteins
  The transmembrane structure of PvAMT proteins was analyzed and a highly conserved structure was observed among them (Fig. 3a). PvAMT1, PvAMT3, PvAMT8, PvAMT9, PvAMT10 and PvAMT11 proteins showed to have 11 transmembranes in their structure, with the N-terminus outside of the membrane and the C-terminus inside. PvAMT4, PvAMT5 and PvAMT6 proteins had 10 transmembranes, resulting in a predicted structure where both, N and C-terminus are located outside of the membrane (Fig. 3b). PvAMT7 protein contains9 predicted transmembrane domains resulting also in a structure with the N-terminus outside the membrane and the C-terminus inside.

  Fig. 3Distribution of conserved domains on PvAMT proteins (a), prediction of transmembrane structure of PvAMT proteins (b).


### Docking analysis of PvAMT proteins
  PII protein is one of the important multi-functional proteins in bacteria that can be involved in symbiosis and nitrogen fixation. The results revealed that there are many interaction points between PvAMT and PII proteins (Fig. 4). Glu and Gly amino acids were the most abundant in the interaction between PvAMT1 and PvAMT2 proteins with PII, respectively. Ser was more present in the interaction areas of PvAMT3 with PII, while Arg, Ser and Phe amino acids played a more prominent role in the interaction of PvAMT4 protein. Gly and Ser in PvAMT5 and Arg, Ala and Gln in PvAMT6 were mostly observed in interaction regions. In PvAMT7 protein, Ser and Glu amino acids, in PvAMT8 Glu and Gly amino acids, and in PvAMT9 Gly and Gln amino acids were the most abundant in the interaction areas with PII. Also, amino acid Gly in PvAMT10 and Gly and Arg in PvAMT11 protein are most involved in the interaction with PII.

  Fig. 4Docking analysis between PvAMT protiens and PII protein.


### 3D structure and binding site analysis of PvAMT proteins
  The prediction of the three-dimensional structure of PvAMT proteins revealed that there is an almost conserved structure in their structure (Fig. 5). There were 10 to 12 alpha-helix structures in PvAMT proteins, which act as membrane-engaging regions. The binding regions of PvAMT proteins were analyzed. The results revealed that Leu and Gly amino acids were the most abundant in the binding region of PvAMT proteins (Fig. 6). Also, Phe, Val and Lys amino acids were detected with high frequency in protein binding regions.

### Phosphorylation site
  The estimation of post-translational phosphorylation modifications in PvAMT proteins was conducted. The results showed that the members of this family were different from each other based on the number of potential phosphorylation sites (Fig. 7). The highest phosphorylation site was predicted in PvAMT3 protein and the lowest site was related to PvAMT8. The average number of phosphorylation sites in PvAMT subfamily 1 members was higher than subfamily 2. These results show that members of subfamily 1 are more subject to post-translational modifications, which probably affect the activity of these transporters.

  Fig. 7Prediction of phosphorylation regions in PvAMT proteins. The potential value was fitted more than 0.90.


### Expression profile of PvAMT genes
  Analysis of RNA-seq data revealed that the mRNA abundance of PvAMT genes tissue-specific regulated. Gene expression profile under salt stress of the Phaseolus vulgaris salt-tolerant cultivar “Ispir” (salt-tolerant) war compared to the salt sensitive cultivar “TR43477”. The comparison revealed that PvAMT1, PvAMT11 and PvAMT6 genes are specifically expressed in leaf tissues of sensitive cultivar (Fig. 8a). In the resistant cultivar, PvAMT5 and PvAMT9 genes showed increased expression in root tissues and PvAMT7 gene in leaf tissue. Also, in the resistant cultivar, PvAMT3 gene in root tissue and PvAMT2 gene in leaf tissue showed a significant decrease in expression in response to salt stress.

  For the investigation of cold stress, the cultivars “120” and “093” were inspected. Under cold stress, PvAMT6 and PvAMT10 genes showed significantly increased expression in both tested cultivars (Fig. 8b). On the other hand, the transcription level of PvAMT2 and PvAMT9 genes showed decreased expression in response to cold stress.

### Co-expression analysis of PvAMT genes
  A network was drawn on the interaction of PvAMT genes and other genes, and the group function of genes was interpreted in this network (Fig. 9). In an interaction network, the genes involved in membrane and transporter activity and DNA binding and transcription factor activity were the most frequent. Also, genes involved in hydrolase activity and carbohydrate metabolic process showed a small interaction with PvAMT genes. Genes involved in auxin and ethylene-activated signaling pathway and defense response interacted with PvAMTs with lower frequency.

### Promoter analysis of PvAMT genes
  The interaction between transcription factors and cis-requlatory elements in PvAMT promoter region was analyzed (Fig. 10). The results indicated that the upstream region of PvAMT genes are associated with transcription factors binding sites related to growth and development. Also, cis-elements related to macromolecule biosynthesis processes and transcription process were found in the upstream region of PvAMT genes. Among the term “hormones”, cis-regulatory elements responsdingto gibberellin were frequently identified in the promoter of PvAMT genes. The interaction between trans-elements was investigated and four interaction networks were predicted between them. The first network was related to the factors involved in the metabolic processes of nucleic acid, RNA, cellular biosynthesis processes and macromolecules. In the second network, the factors involved in the development of meristem, flower, root and shoot interacted with each other. The third network was related to factors involved in regulatory processes such as transcriptional regulation, metabolic regulation, biological, and cellular process regulation. Also, a small interaction network was predicted between response factors to gibberellin, response to acid chemical and compounds containing oxygen.

### Expression levels of PvAMT genes
  The expression pattern of PvAMT genes in response to drought stress and soil salinity were investigated by qRT-PCR. The level of mRNA abundance increased for all genes analyzed in shoot parts of the plant (Fig. 11). PvAMT1 gene increased its abundance by 45 and 31 times under drought and salinity stress, respectively, compared to control conditions. PvAMT2 gene expression showed a minor significant increase, specific for the drought stress response. PvAMT5 and PvAMT6 genes showed a similar expression pattern, so the mRNA abundance of both genes increased in shoot tissues under drought and salt stress. Under drought stress, the expression of PvAMT5 gene increased 69 times and PvAMT6 gene increased 109 times compared to the control. The transcription level of PvAMT7 gene also increased in response to both salt and drought stress. PvAMT10 gene had a different expression pattern from other studied genes and was mostly induced in response to salt stress in shoot tissues.

  In root tissues of bean seedlings, the expression level of PvAMT1 gene decreased under both salt and drought stress compared to control conditions (Fig. 12). There was no significant change in the transcript level of PvAMT2 gene in root tissues in response to salt and drought stress. PvAMT5 mRNA abundance showed a significant decrease under drought stress. The transcript level of PvAMT6 gene increased by 6.52 and 53.97 times under drought and salt stress, respectively, compared to the control. The expression of PvAMT7 gene under salt stress increased 17.89 times compared to the control. Also, the results indicated that the mRNA level of PvAMT10 increases in response to drought and salt stress. The highest transcription level of PvAMT10 gene in root tissues under drought stress was 32.66 times that of control condition.

## Discussion
  Ammonium and nitrate salts are sources of inorganic nitrogen that have significant effects on the growth and development of plants. Ammonium transporters are responsible for the absorption and movement of ammonium inside the plant1. The knowledge about these transporters in plants is rather limited. In present study, eleven members of AMT family were identified in the bean genome. It was stated that the number of gene family members in plants can change according to ploidy level and genome size42,43. For example, 16 AMT members have been identified in Camellia sinensis44, 20 members in rapeseed45, and 16 members in soybean15. PvAMTs were separated into two groups based on the phylogenetic tree. In previous studies, it has been reported that AMTs fall into two subfamilies3,15. Based on the gene structure, subfamily I members had one exon, while subfamily II members had between 3 and 4 exons in their structure. It seems that these two subfamilies have gone through a different evolutionary process. One factor that can impact gene expression speed is the intron-exon structure in a gene and the number of exons46. The presence of introns also introduces the option of alternative splicing, which was reported as important regulatory mechanism for transporter proteins47. The prediction of duplication events showed that three genes from subfamily II including PvAMT2, PvAMT10 and PvAMT11 and three genes from subfamily I including PvAMT1, PvAMT9 and PvAMT7 have been doubled during the evolution process. These results indicate that a major part of the expansion in this gene family was related to segmental duplication events.

  Among the amino acids present in the interaction between PvAMTs and PII, Gly, Ser, Glu, and Arg were the most prevalent. Within bacterial nitrogen pathways, the PII protein serves a vital function as a key multi-functional protein48. Considering that beans have the ability to symbiosis with nitrogen-fixing bacteria and PII is in the nitrogen pathway, determining this interaction showed that there are many interaction points between PII and PvAMTs that are involved in nitrogen and ammonium movement. Besides, the binding region of PvAMT proteins was rich in Leu and Gly amino acids. It seems that these amino acids play key role in possible interaction of PvAMTs.

  The genes related to membrane and transporter activity as well as DNA binding and transcription factor activity were prevalent in the co-expression interaction network with PvAMTs. Probably, ammonium transporters are induced together with other membrane transporters by common upstream factors through transcription factors. It was also found that PvAMT genes are co-expressed with some elements of the auxin and ethylene activated signaling pathway. These results suggest that PvAMT interacts with several molecular pathways, which are probably related to ammonium action. Analysis of the promoter region of PvAMT genes showed that the members of this family are mostly induced by factors involved in growth and development. Considering the role of ammonium in regulating the growth activities of plants, this result is predictable. It was found that among hormones, gibberellin plays a more prominent role in the induction of PvAMT genes. However, more studies are needed to determine the role of this hormone in regulating the activity of ammonium transporters. Phosphorylation events have significant effects on regulating the activity of ion transporters such as ammonium transporters49. Phosphorylation at specific regions has also been found to be necessary to alter the function of ammonium transporters such as AMT1;1 in Arabidopsis1. In this study, differences between PvAMTs were observed for the number of phosphorylation sites, and subfamily I members had more potential phosphorylation sites than subfamily II members. Probably, members of subfamily I are more likely to be subjected to kinase-dependent pathways.

  Based on RNA-seq data, PvAMT genes showed tissue-specific expression. In root tissues of the resistant variety, there was a notable increase in expression of PvAMT5 and PvAMT9 genes, while in leaf tissue, the mRNA abundance of PvAMT7 gene showed significant rise. PvAMT1;1 (accession number GQ377869) exhibits similarities to PvAMT09 as explored in this research. The analysis of PvAMT1;1 has demonstrated its role in facilitating the high-affinity, rapidly saturating electrogenic transport of ammonium50. The functionality of PvAMT1;1 is augmented by low extracellular pH, and this enhancement linked to acidic pH stimulation corresponds with alterations in the reversal potential and cytoplasmic acidification, thereby suggesting that PvAMT1;1 operates as an H/NH4 symporter. Besides, it has been reported that AMT2 in Arabidopsis causes ammonium loading in xylem1 and thus increases ammonium transport to aerial organs. It has been reported that CsAMTs have differential expression in organs and are more induced in terminal shoots and roots44. However, it seems that ammonium transporters of subfamily I are also involved in this movement. Ammonium absorption and transfer seems to be a group cooperation. For example, knocking out any single gene had no effect on ammonium absorption in rice, but knocking out three members of subfamily I caused a 95% decrease in ammonium absorption13. Based on qPCR data, all the selected PvAMT genes showed increased expression in response to drought stress in shoot organs, but only PvAMT6 and PvAMT10 showed increased expression in root tissue. Also, based on RNA-seq data, PvAMT6 and PvAMT10 showed increased expression in response to cold stress. PvAMT1 and PvAMT5 genes, both belonging to subfamily I, showed decreased expression in root tissue. It seems that the members of subfamily I, which are involved in ammonium absorption in roots, are down-regulated during stress, but are induced in ammonium transport to shoots. In response to soil salinity stress, most PvAMT genes were increased in expression, but PvAMT6, PvAMT7 and PvAMT10 genes were just induced in root tissues. The results showed that PvAMTs are involved in the response to environmental stresses, and their role in translocation is likely to be greater than absorption.


## Conclusion
  Ammonium transporter gene family in bean (PvAMT) was investigated and 11 members of this family were identified and characterized. The members of this family were divided into two sub-families based on their structure. These subfamilies shared differences in expression and post-translational modifications of phosphorylation. Based on the expression pattern, it was concluded that PvAMTs are expressed in both root and shoot tissues in response to environmental stresses such as drought and salinity. In shoot tissues, PvAMT1, PvAMT5, and PvAMT6 displayed higher inducement in response to drought stress, while PvAMT1 and PvAMT10 were more highly induced in response to salinity. The results indicated that PvAMT6 and PvAMT10 were more highly expressed in root tissues in response to both salinity and drought stress. It was also hypothesized that under adverse conditions, ammonium absorption from the roots may decrease, but movement within the organs increases. However, additional studies are necessary to determine the more precise role of these ion transporters in response to stress and how they cooperate with stress-related cellular signaling pathways.
