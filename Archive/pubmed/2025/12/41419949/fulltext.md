# Epigenetic silencing of SPHK1-IRF7 axis drives inflammaging in age-related meniscus degeneration via sphingolipid-immune dysregulation


## Background
  Age-related meniscus injury (MI) involves inflammaging, but biomarkers and mechanisms remain unclear.


## Methods
  Ribonucleic acid (RNA) sequencing of meniscal tissues from 15 young (< 45 years) and 15 aging (≥ 45 years) MI patients (training dataset) identified differentially expressed inflammation-related genes (DE-IRGs). Biomarkers were screened via least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) and support vector machines-recursive feature elimination (SVM-RFE) in the training dataset and validated through GSE191157 (testing dataset) and immunohistochemistry (IHC) staining in additional clinical specimens. Support vector machines (SVM) model and artificial neural network (ANN) model were constructed to predict the diagnostic performance of biomarkers for aging MI. Moreover, biomarkers enrichment analysis, and correlation among biomarkers, function-related genes, immune factors and immune cells were completed. Finally, transcription factor (TF)-biomarker-microRNAs (miRNAs) and competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) regulatory networks analysis, and drug prediction were performed.


## Results
  IRF7 and SPHK1 were significantly downregulated in aging MI and predicted aging MI with high accuracy (area under the curve (AUC) > 0.7). Both biomarkers correlated with chemokine/cytokine pathways, immune senescence (positively with γδT cells/Th17/monocytes; negatively with LAG3/IL6R/TNFSF13), and sphingolipid metabolism (PLPP2 synergy) (|r| = 0.49 ~ 0.86, p < 0.05). Regulatory networks implicated NORAD lncRNA (upregulated) negatively regulated the expression of SPHK1 and IRF7 (r<-0.90, p < 0.05), while transcription factors E2F4 and KLF5 (downregulated) positively regulated their expression (r > 0.95, p < 0.05). Clinically, SPHK1 and IRF7 positively correlated with preoperative Lysholm score (SPHK1: r = 0.66; IRF7: r = 0.61; p < 0.05) and negatively with preoperative VAS (SPHK1: r=−0.58; IRF7: r=−0.58; p < 0.05) in aging MI. Two drugs (decitabine, acetaminophen) targeting these biomarkers were predicted.


## Conclusion
  SPHK1 and IRF7 drive inflammaging via sphingolipid-immune crosstalk and epigenetic silencing, offering diagnostic and therapeutic potential.

## Introduction
  The meniscus, a crucial fibrocartilaginous structure in the knee, plays a vital role in load distribution, shock absorption, and joint stability [1]. Its integrity is essential for maintaining normal intra-articular load distribution and stress levels [2]. Meniscus injury (MI) is the second most common knee injury with an incidence of 62 cases per 100,000 persons [3]. MI mainly includes acute tears from trauma with an incidence of 10–20% and degenerative tears with an incidence of 31%, and the latter can significantly accelerate osteoarthritis progression and affect the quality of life of patients [4, 5].

  The meniscus’s mechanical properties are influenced by factors such as fixation methods and environmental conditions [6], and are critically dependent on its structural integrity. For instance, preserving the anterior horn volume of a discoid lateral meniscus is particularly important for improving knee biomechanical outcomes [7], whereas a medial meniscal posterior root tear can disrupt this mechanical balance, leading to increased anterior cruciate ligament stiffness and anterior tibial translation [8]. Once the meniscus is injured, it accelerates articular cartilage degeneration and can even initiate knee osteoarthritis [9, 10].

  While acute traumatic tears are common in younger or active populations, degenerative tears become the predominant form in individuals over 40 years of age, affecting more than 40% of those aged 65 and above [11, 12]. In elderly persons, the prevalence were increased with each generation: 31.8% for 65–70 years of age, 40.0% for 70–75 of age, 47.6% for 75–80 years of age, 52.2% for >80 years of age [13]. This age-related predisposition underscores a fundamental shift in the underlying pathology, moving from purely mechanical failure to a complex interplay of biomechanical stress and biological aging within the tissue. With aging and increasing BMI, the elasticity value of the meniscus increases [14]. At the histological level, collagen wear and the accumulation of advanced glycation end products increase with age, with these products accumulating more in the meniscal horns than in the central body [15]. Furthermore, meniscal cells in the elderly may dedifferentiate and initiate a proliferative phenotype, whereas cells in younger patients respond to injury while maintaining a differentiated phenotype [16].

  A hallmark of these age-related changes is a state of chronic, low-grade, sterile inflammation termed “inflammaging“ [17–19]. Unlike the acute and resolving inflammation following trauma, inflammaging is characterized by a dysregulated and persistent cytokine network. For example, age correlates positively with levels of IL-6, VEG-F, and IL-1Ra in chronic meniscal lesions [20]. This milieu, along with the accumulation of senescent immune cells and elevated matrix-degrading enzymes, collectively contributes to a pro-catabolic tissue microenvironment that accelerates extracellular matrix (ECM) degradation and drives osteoarthritis progression [21–24].

  Promisingly, interventions such as pull-out repair for medial meniscal posterior root tears can effectively promote healing, delay tissue degeneration, and protect articular cartilage [25], and treadmill exercise can maintain the integrity of cartilage and subchondral bone, alleviating post-traumatic osteoarthritis phenotypes [26]. Previous transcriptomic studies have identified broad signatures of immune cell senescence and altered inflammatory responses in aged menisci [16]. They found that, in aging MI, genes associated with cartilage and skeletal development and ECM synthesis were repressed, while those involved in immune response, inflammation, cell cycle, and cellular proliferation were stimulated. However, these findings often present an unprioritized list of changes, leaving a critical gap in our understanding: which specific drivers and biomarkers are central to initiating and sustaining this inflammaging process, and how are they regulated?

  Inflammation-related genes (IRGs) are pivotal regulators of immune homeostasis and tissue repair. Their dynamic and cell-type-specific expression dictates the balance between protective and destructive inflammation. Therefore, the systematic identification and functional characterization of key IRGs that are dysregulated in aging MI is not merely descriptive but is critical for unlocking early diagnostic biomarkers and revealing novel, targeted therapeutic strategies to halt or slow meniscal degeneration.

  To bridge this knowledge gap, our study employs an integrative bioinformatics and experimental approach. We first performed ribonucleic acid (RNA) sequencing on meniscal tissues from young and aging MI patients to identify differentially expressed inflammation-related genes (DE-IRGs). We then applied robust machine learning algorithms, least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) and support vector machines-recursive feature elimination (SVM-RFE), to pin down the most promising biomarker candidates from this pool. The identified biomarkers, SPHK1 and IRF7, were rigorously validated at the transcriptomic level in a public dataset and, importantly, at the protein level via immunohistochemistry (IHC) in our patient cohort. Moving beyond identification, we deeply investigated their functional landscape, including their association with immune infiltration, sphingolipid metabolism, and clinical symptoms. Furthermore, we constructed a comprehensive upstream regulatory network, encompassing transcription factors, miRNAs, and lncRNAs, to elucidate the potential epigenetic mechanisms silencing their expression in aging MI. Finally, we explored potential therapeutic compounds targeting these biomarkers. Through this multi-faceted strategy, our study aims to not only identify robust biomarkers of meniscal inflammaging but also to decipher their molecular context and clinical relevance, offering new insights for diagnosis and intervention in age-related meniscus degeneration.

### Preparing the training dataset: collection of samples, RNA extraction and RNA sequencing
  The meniscus tissue of 30 patients with MI who underwent arthroscopic meniscectomy were collected from sports medicine center, West China Hospital of Sichuan University, including 15 young patients aged < 45 years and 15 aging patients aged ≥45 years. Excised meniscus tissue (0.5 cm3 blocks) was taken and immediately snap frozen in liquid nitrogen for RNA sequencing. Meanwhile, collecting the clinical data of participants, including age, sex, body mass index (BMI), symptoms duration, involved knee, tear location, tear type, preoperative (Pre) Visual Analog Scale (VAS) score, Pre lysholm score, Pre Magnetic Resonance Imaging International Cartilage Repair Society (MRI ICRS) grade, Pre Kellgren-Lawrence (K-L) grade, surgical method, operation duration, and hospital stay.

  Total RNA was extracted from 30 MI tissues the above collected using TRIzol (Invitrogen), purified with column-based kits (e.g., RNeasy, Qiagen), and verified the RNA integrity (RIN > 7.0). cDNA library was prepared for each sample. Sequencing was performed on the BGISEQ-500 platform (BGI, Shenzhen) to produce paired-end reads of 100 bp. Reads were aligned to the human genome (hg38) using HISAT2, and gene counts and gene expression were obtained for further analysis. The self-prepared RNA sequencing data of these 30 MI tissues were used as the training dataset in this study.


### Public data source
  GSE191157 dataset obtained from gene expression omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) were used as testing dataset for subsequent expression validation to assist in screening biomarkers, including 4 young MI patients (aged 20–25 years) and 4 aging MI patients (aged 50–55 years). 200 IRGs were downloaded from the HALLMARK_INFLAMMATORY_RESPONSE gene set in the Molecular Signature Database (MSigDB) (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/).


### Analysis of DE-IRGs
  In the training dataset, DESeq2 R package was used to compare the variability of gene expression levels between the young group and the aging group, and the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were obtained using |log2FoldChange (FC)|>0.5 and p < 0.05 as the threshold. Then, through the ggvenn R package, the intersection of DEGs and IRGs was taken to obtain DE-IRGs. Gene ontology (GO) (adj. P < 0.05) and kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) (p < 0.05) enrichment analysis of DE-IRGs was conducted by clusterProfiler R package.


### Identifying the biomarkers from DE-IRGs
  In the training dataset, LASSO and SVM-RFE were applied to DE-IRGs to further sift out features genes, using glmnet R package and caret R package, respectively. In the LASSO analysis, the optimal λ value was determined using five-fold cross-validation with model AUC as the evaluation metric, and genes with non-zero coefficients at this λ value were selected. In the SVM-RFE analysis, five-fold cross-validation was utilised to evaluate the performance of models constructed from different feature subsets, ultimately determining the optimal feature combination achieving the highest predictive accuracy. Then, candidate genes were obtained by the intersection of two machine learning results. To obtain biomarkers, candidate genes were analyzed for the expression validation in both training dataset and testing dataset. Genes with consistent and significant expression trends in both datasets were selected as biomarkers.


### Validating the protein expression of biomarkers
  Meniscus tissues were collected from 6 young (aged 40–43 years) and 6 aging (aged 47–69 years) MI patients who underwent arthroscopic meniscectomy. A 0.5 cm3 tissue block was extracted from each surgically removed meniscus sample and fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 24 h for immunohistochemistry (IHC) staining. The IHC procedure was performed following established protocols [27]. Briefly, paraffin-embedded sections were deparaffinized in xylene, rehydrated through a graded ethanol series, and subjected to antigen retrieval. Subsequently, the sections were incubated with primary antibody followed by a secondary antibody. Rabbit anti-human SPHK1 antibody (Abcam, U.K.), rabbit anti-human IRF7 antibody (Huabio, China), goat anti-rabbit IgG (H + L) secondary antibody (Huabio, China) were diluted at a ratio of 1:500, 1:100, and 1:500, respectively. Immunoreactivity was visualized using a 3,3′-diaminobenzidine substrate kit, followed by counterstaining with hematoxylin. To confirm staining specificity, negative control experiments were performed in which the primary antibodies were replaced with phosphate-buffered saline, and all other steps remained identical.

  The stained slides were scanned at high power magnification (600×) using the Motic EasyScan Pro 6 slide scanner (Motic Digital Pathology, Barcelona, Spain). Resulting images were exported as JPEG files via Motic DSAssistant, and quantified for positive signal area using Image-Pro Plus 6 (Media Cybernetics, Bethesda, MD, USA). For each stained slide, the integrated optical density (IOD) was measured from three different fields and the average IOD value was calculated. Each experiment was repeated three times. Data were presented as mean ± standard deviation. Between-group comparisons were performed using the Mann–Whitney U test in SPSS 23.0, with a P value < 0.05 considered statistically significant.


### Evaluating the diagnostic performance of biomarkers by the support vector machine (SVM) model and artificial neural network (ANN) model
  Based on biomarkers, SVM model and ANN model were constructed to predict the diagnostic efficacy of biomarkers for aging MI in the training dataset. First, a SVM model was constructed using the caret R package. This model employed a radial basis function kernel with parameters set at cost = 10 and gamma = 1/ncol(dat), and utilized all training samples for model training. Subsequently, an ANN model was built using the neuralnet R package. It was configured with a hidden layer of two neurons, utilized the logistic activation function, was set to linear.output = FALSE, and employed the rprop + algorithm for network training. The predictive performance of the two models was evaluated by confusion matrix, precision-recall (PR) curve, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve and calibration curve. ### Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) of the biomarkers
  GSEA was used to investigate the potential mechanism of the biomarkers. First, samples within the training dataset were divided into high- and low-expression groups based on median biomarker expression values. Subsequently, DESeq2 performed differential analysis on all genes within these groups, ranking them by log2FC values. Finally, GSEA analysis was performed using the R package ClusterProfiler, employing the reference gene set c2.cp.kegg.v2023.1.Hs.symbols.gmt. Significantly enriched pathways were identified using p.adj < 0.05 as the significance threshold, with the top five pathways selected for visualization.


### Functional analysis of biomarker-associated genes and immunological correlation analysis
  Considering the close relationship between gene function and subcellular localization, subcellular localization for biomarkers were performed using the mRNALocater database (http://bio-bigdata.cn/mRNALocater/). To identify the function-related genes of biomarkers, the GeneMANIA database (https://genemania.org/) was used to construct biomarkers network. Meanwhile, we further analyzed the expression patterns of these function-related genes (wilcoxon, p < 0.05) within the training dataset, and evaluated the degree of association between biomarkers and these function-related genes by Spearman’s correlation analysis. Furthermore, we investigated the expression characteristics of 17 major histocompatibility complex (MHC) genes, 15 immunosuppressive factors, and 28 immune-activating factors within the training dataset (wilcoxon, p < 0.05), systematically assessing the correlations between biomarkers and these immune-related factors via Spearman’s correlation analysis. The strength of correlation was determined as follows: mild: 0.0 < |r| < 0.4, moderate: 0.4 ≤ |r| < 0.7; high: 0.7 ≤ |r| < 1.0 [28].


### Immune infiltration analysis
  To understand the differences in immune status during the progression of aging MI, based on the training dataset, the single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) algorithm of R package GSVA was used to calculate the infiltration abundance of 28 immune cells, and the differences of infiltration abundance between the aging and the young group were compared (wilcoxon, p < 0.05). Moreover, Spearman correlation analysis was used to analyze the correlation among differential immune cells, biomarker-function-related genes, immunosuppressive factors, and immune-activating factors.


### Transcription factors (TFs)-biomarker-microRNAs (miRNAs) and competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) regulatory networks analysis
  To explore the molecular regulation mechanism of biomarkers in aging MI, the JASPAR database (https://jaspar.genereg.net/) of miRNet platform was used to predict TFs and miRNAs related to biomarkers. Then, Starbase database (https://ngdc.cncb.ac.cn/databasecommons/database/id/169) (CLIP-seq ≥ 10) was used to predict the long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) of the above obtained miRNAs. Then, Cytoscape software was used to construct TFs-biomarkers-miRNAs regulatory network and ceRNA regulatory network. The expression difference of TFs and lncRNAs in the training dataset was analyzed.


### Difference of clinical variables and correlation between biomarkers and clinical variables
  Continuous clinical variables were compared between aging and young group using Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Categorical variables were presented in contingency tables and analyzed with Fisher’s exact tests. Continuous variables are presented as median [Q1, Q3], and categorical variables as count (percentage). For each group, associations between biomarkers (SPHK1 and IRF7) and clinical variables were assessed separately. The Spearman’s rank correlation was used to evaluate the monotonic relationships between gene expression and continuous clinical variables. The Kruskal-Wallis test was employed to detect differences in gene expression across categories of categorical clinical variables. The above statistical analyses were performed in the training dataset via R version 4.1.0. Results were visualized through scatter plots, boxplots, and correlation heatmaps. A P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.


### Drug prediction
  In order to find new therapeutic targets, DSigDB (https://dsigdb.tanlab.org/DSigDBv1.0/) was used to predict potential drugs for biomarkers.


## Results

### Analysis of DE-IRGs in aging MI
  Our initial analysis sought to characterize the global transcriptomic landscape of aging MI. A comprehensive differential expression analysis identified a total of 1358 DEGs between the young MI and aging MI groups, of which 777 were upregulated and 581 were downregulated, and the top 10 up-regulated genes and top 10 down-regulated genes sorted by log2FC were labeled in the volcano plot (Fig. 2A). The density heat map showed that the gene expression was higher and more concentrated in the aging group, and displayed the heat map of the top 10 up-regulated and top 10 down-regulated DEGs with the greatest fold change (Fig. 2B). To hone in on the inflammatory aspects of aging, we intersected these DEGs with a list of IRGs, which yielded 18 DE-IRGs (Fig. 2C). This focused set of genes was subjected to functional enrichment analysis to decipher their potential biological roles. GO enrichment analysis revealed that DE-IRGs were significantly involved in the key immune-inflammatory processes, including the cytokine-mediated signaling pathway, mononuclear cell differentiation, and regulation of leukocyte differentiation (Fig. 2D). Complementing this, KEGG pathway analysis highlighted their enrichment in several critical signaling pathways known to drive inflammation and tissue remodeling, such as cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, TNF signaling pathway, and PI3K-Akt signaling pathway (Fig. 2E). These results confirm that age-related meniscus degeneration is characterized by a pronounced inflammatory phenotype and provide a strong rationale for focusing subsequent biomarker discovery efforts within this specific set of 18 DE-IRGs.

### IRF7 and SPHK1 were identified as biomarkers
  Having established a set of 18 DE-IRGs implicated in the inflammatory of aging MI, we next sought to identify the most robust and predictive biomarkers from this pool. To achieve this, we employed two distinct machine learning algorithms to refine our candidate list. The LASSO regression analysis identified 9 feature genes that optimally minimized the prediction error (e.g., IRF7, SLC4A4, MMP14, ADORA2B, ADM, CCRL2, CSF1, SPHK1 and HBEGF) (Fig. 3A-B), while the SVM-RFE algorithm selected 9 feature genes that provided the highest classification accuracy (e.g., INHBA, NMI, CCRL2, MMP14, IRF7, MYC, ADORA2B, SPHK1 and KCNJ2) (Fig. 3C). The intersection of these two independent analyses yielded a highly confident shortlist of five candidate genes: IRF7, MMP14, ADORA2B, CCRL2 and SPHK1 (Fig. 3D).

  To validate the translational relevance and consistency of these candidates, we examined their expression patterns across the training dataset and validating dataset.

  In both two datasets, IRF7 and SPHK1 exhibited a significant and consistent downregulation in the aging MI group compared to the young group (Fig. 3E-F). In contrast, the expression patterns of the other three candidates were either inconsistent or not statistically significant across datasets. Therefore, based on their reproducible and marked dysregulation in aging MI, SPHK1 (a key enzyme in sphingolipid metabolism) and IRF7 (a master regulator of interferon and immune responses) were defined as the key biomarkers for all subsequent investigations into the mechanisms of meniscal inflammaging.

### Validating the protein expression of biomarkers
  To confirm that the transcriptional downregulation of our identified biomarkers translates to the protein level, thereby strengthening their biological and clinical relevance, we performed IHC with appropriate negative controls on a separate set of patient meniscal tissues. The representative IHC images and subsequent quantification of the IOD clearly demonstrated that the protein abundance of both SPHK1 and IRF7 was significantly lower in the meniscal tissues of aging MI patients compared to those from young patients (p < 0.05 for all) (Fig. 4B-D, F-H). The negative controls, performed without primary antibodies, confirmed the staining specificity of SPHK1 and IRF7 (Fig. 4A, E). This concordance between transcriptomic and protein data solidifies their relevance to the disease pathology and justifies their use as central elements in our downstream functional and diagnostic analyses.

### Evaluating the diagnostic performance of biomarkers
  After confirming the dysregulation of IRF7 and SPHK1 at both the transcriptomic and protein levels, we next assessed their collective utility as a diagnostic tool for distinguishing aging MI. To this end, we constructed two machine learning models based on the expression of these two biomarkers: SVM model and ANN model. Both models demonstrated robust diagnostic performance, effectively identifying the majority of true positive and true negative samples (Figs. 5A and 6A-B). The strong predictive power was further confirmed by the area under of the curve (AUC) of precision-recall (PR) and the receiver operating characteristic (ROC). The SVM model yielded a PR curve AUC of 0.67 and a ROC curve AUC of 0.73 (Fig. 5B-C), and the ANN model yielded a PR curve AUC of 0.84 and a ROC curve AUC 0.79 (Fig. 6D-E). Calibration curves further confirmed both models possessed robust predictive capabilities (Figs. 5D and 6F). Furthermore, the variable importance analysis within the ANN model indicated that SPHK1 exerted a stronger influence on the model’s predictions than IRF7, highlighting its potentially more central role in the pathogenesis of aging MI (Fig. 6C). The construction of diagnostic models demonstrates that the expression levels of IRF7 and SPHK1 possess substantial utility for classifying aging MI, which merits further investigation in the following functional analyses.

### Biomarkers are enriched in chemokines, cytokines, and cytokine receptor interactions associated pathways
  To gain a systems-level understanding of the biological processes associated with IRF7 and SPHK1, we performed GSEA enrichment analysis. This analysis revealed that lower expression of both biomarkers was significantly associated with the negative enrichment of key immune and signaling pathways. Specifically, SPHK1 low-expression phenotype is significantly correlated with depressed cell adhesion molecules, cytokines-cytokine receptor interactions and chemokine-cytokine receptor interaction (Fig. 7A). Similarly, IRF7 low-expression phenotype is significantly correlated with depressed cytokines-cytokine receptor interactions and focal adhesion (Fig. 7B). These results indicate that the downregulation of IRF7 and SPHK1 in aging MI is linked to a broad suppression of pathways fundamental to immune response and cell trafficking. This systematic association provides a basis for our subsequent investigation into the correlation between these biomarkers and immune factors.


### Correlation of biomarkers with function-related genes and immune factors
  The subcellular localization of IRF7 and SPHK1 revealed their predominant distribution in the cytoplasm and endoplasmic reticulum (Fig. 8A), provided the cellular context for their functional interactions. To elucidate this, we first constructed a functional interaction network using the GeneMANIA database, which identified 20 genes with close functional associations with the two biomarkers (Fig. 8B). Among these, PLPP2 — a gene involved in sphingolipid metabolism — was significantly downregulated in the aging group (Fig. 8C), and its expression showed a strong positive correlation with SPHK1 (r = 0.69, p < 0.05) (Fig. 8D).

  Analysis of immune-related genes revealed no significant differences in the expression of MHC molecules related to IRF7 and SPHK1 in the aging MI group (Fig. 8E-F). In contrast, significant alterations were observed in specific immune modulators. The expression of the immunosuppressive factor LAG3 was significantly elevated in the aging group (Fig. 8G), and demonstrated a significant negative correlation with SPHK1 (r=−0.46, p < 0.05) (Fig. 8H). Similarly, the immunoactivating factor IL6R and TNFSF13 were also significantly increased in the aging MI group (Fig. 8I). Notably, the expression of both IL6R and TNFSF13 exhibited strong negative correlations with both SPHK1 (r=−0.86, r=−0.81) and IRF7 (r=−0.81, r=−0.74) (p < 0.05 for all) (Fig. 8J). These correlation analyses reveal that the strong co-dysregulation of SPHK1 with the sphingolipid-related gene PLPP2, and the robust inverse correlations between the biomarkers (SPHK1/IRF7) and specific immune factors (LAG3/IL6R/TNFSF13). This immune signature provides a foundation for further investigation into the immune landscape.

### Correlation among biomarkers, immune cells, function-related genes, and immune factors
  To integrate the molecular and cellular findings, we conducted a comprehensive correlation analysis. The ssGSEA algorithm revealed a significant reduction in the infiltration abundance of three key immune cell types in the aging MI group: monocyte, type 17 T helper cell (Th17) and gamma delta T cell (γδT) (p < 0.05 for all) (Fig. 9A).

  Correlation analysis demonstrated that SPHK1 expression was positively correlated with the abundance of all three of these diminished cell populations (γδT: r = 0.86; monocyte: r = 0.83; Th17: r = 0.63), while IRF7 showed a positively correlation specifically with monocyte (r = 0.49) (p < 0.05 for all) (Fig. 9B). The SPHK1-related gene PLPP2 expression was also positively correlated with γδT (r = 0.73) and monocyte (r = 0.74) (p < 0.05 for all) (Fig. 9C). Furthermore, the immunosuppressive factor LAG3, which was upregulated in aging MI, exhibited a significant negative correlation with PLPP2 (r=−0.43, p < 0.05) (Fig. 9D), and, importantly, with the infiltration levels of all three immune cells (γδT: r=−0.49; monocyte: r=−0.48; Th17: r=−0.66; p < 0.05 for all) (Fig. 9E). Among the upregulated immune factors, LAG3, IL6R, and TNFSF13 showed significant positive correlations with one another, including LAG3 and IL6R (r = 0.48), LAG3 and TNFSF13 (r = 0.55), and IL6R and TNFSF1 (r = 0.82) (p < 0.05 for all), forming a co-regulated network of immune mediators in the aging tissue (Fig. 7F). The overall pattern of these moderate to high correlations is summary in Fig.7G. The integrated correlation analyses indicate a cohesive network of associations in aging MI. The downregulation of the SPHK1-PLPP2 axis and IRF7 is quantitatively linked to a reduction in specific immune cells (monocytes, Th17, γδ T). Concurrently, this molecular signature is associated with the upregulation of a distinct set of immune factors (LAG3, IL6R, TNFSF13) that are inversely related to both the biomarkers and the diminished immune cells. This reveals a coordinated shift in the meniscal immune landscape, providing a basis for the proposed sphingolipid-immune axis.

### TFs-biomarkers-miRNA regulatory networks
  To explore the potential upstream regulatory mechanisms of SPHK1 and IRF7, we constructed a TFs-miRNA regulatory network. This predictive network comprised 2 biomarkers, 21 TFs and 38 miRNAs, illustrating potential regulatory relationships (Fig. 10A). Expanding on this, we built a ceRNA network, predicting 20 lncRNAs that could potentially target the 38 miRNAs, thereby forming an integrated post-transcriptional regulatory axis (Fig. 10B).

  Among the predicted TFs, the expression of E2F4 and KLF5 was significantly downregulated in the aging MI group (Fig. 10C). Their expression levels showed extremely strong positive correlations with both SPHK1 (r = 0.95, r = 0.98) and IRF7 (r = 0.98, r = 0.95) (p < 0.05 for all) (Fig. 10D). Conversely, within the ceRNA network, 4 lncRNAs (AC093297.2, AC245014.3, HCG11 and NORAD) were significantly upregulated in the aging MI group (Fig. 10E), and their expression exhibited strong negative correlations with both SPHK1 (r=−0.86, r=−0.83, r=−0.79, r=−0.95) and IRF7 (r=−0.81, r=−0.79, r=−0.74, r=−0.90) (p < 0.05 for all) (Fig. 10F). Notably, NORAD was predicted to interact with four miRNAs (hsa-mir-129-2-3p, hsa-mir-155-5p, hsa-mir-30d-5p, and hsa-mir-26a-5p) that target SPHK1 and IRF7. The primary correlations within these regulatory networks are summarized in Fig. 10G.

  This analysis delineates a coherent upstream regulatory framework for SPHK1 and IRF7. The strong concordant dysregulation (downregulation of TFs (E2F4/KLF5) and upregulation of lncRNAs like NORAD) with the biomarkers, coupled with their powerful correlations, provides a architecture for their epigenetic and transcriptional silencing in aging MI.

### Difference of clinical variables and correlation between biomarkers and differentiated clinical variables in age grouped MI.
  We next assessed the clinical relevance of our identified biomarkers by first comparing baseline characteristics between the young and aging MI groups. Significant differences were observed in multiple clinical variables, including BMI, Pre K-L grade, Pre VAS score, tear type, Pre lysholm score, Pre MRI ICRS grade and symptoms duration (Fig. 11A, Supplementary Table S1). Specifically, the aging MI group presented with a significantly higher BMI, higher Pre VAS scores, longer symptom duration, and lower Pre Lysholm scores compared to the young group. Furthermore, the aging MI group had a greater proportion of high-grade cases in Pre K-L and Pre MRI ICRS grades, as well as a higher prevalence of variant tear types (Fig. 11B).

  Subsequent spearman correlation analysis within the aging group showed that both SPHK1 and IRF7 expression levels were positively correlated with Pre lysholm score (r = 0.66 and r = 0.61, respectively, p < 0.01 for both), and negatively correlated with Pre VAS score (r=−0.58, and p < 0.01 for both) (Fig. 11C). In contrast, no significant correlations were observed between the biomarkers and these clinical variables in the young group. The full spectrum of correlations is detailed in Supplementary Figure S1.

  These analyses revealed that the aging cohort exhibits a more severe clinical profile, characterized by greater pain, worse joint function, and more advanced structural degeneration, suggesting that their downregulation is particularly relevant to the pathophysiology of age-related meniscal degeneration.

### Drug prediction targeting SPHK1 and IRF7
  To explore the translational potential of our findings, we utilized the DSigDB database to computationally predict compounds that might target SPHK1 and IRF7. This analysis predicted a total of 38 drugs potentially targeting the biomarkers. Among these, seven compounds (irinotecan, anisomycin, Decitabine, AFLATOXIN B1, 0175029-0000 PC3 DOWN, acetaminophen, and estradiol) were predicted to act on both SPHK1 and IRF7 simultaneously (Fig. 12). Furthermore, these two drugs, aecitabine and acetaminophen, were also predicted to target the immune factors IL6R and TNFSF13, which our previous analyses identified as being negatively correlated with the biomarkers. The candidate compounds are linked to the core molecular players of the SPHK1-IRF7 axis and its associated immune landscape in aging MI. Future experimental validation can be conducted to assess their actual efficacy in modulating this specific pathway in the context of meniscal degeneration.

## Discussion
  This study identified the concerted downregulation of SPHK1 and IRF7 as a central event in the pathogenesis of aging MI, bridging epigenetic regulation, immunometabolic dysfunction, and clinical symptomatology (Fig. 13). This cascade is characterized by: (1) a defined dysfunction in sphingolipid metabolism, driven by the coordinated downregulation of the SPHK1-PLPP2 module, which impairs S1P-mediated immune cell trafficking; (2) a loss of inflammatory homeostasis due to IRF7 deficiency, leading to the compensatory elevation of IL6R and TNFSF13; and (3) the amplification of this immunosenescent milieu through the upregulation of the exhaustion marker LAG3. The following sections will detail how this integrated mechanism not only elucidates the molecular basis of meniscal inflammaging but also aligns with clinical symptoms and reveals potential therapeutic strategies, thereby underscoring the diagnostic and translational relevance of the SPHK1-IRF7 axis.

### SPHK1/PLPP2 axis disruption links sphingosine metabolism dysfunction to Immunosenescence
  SPHK1 is the key enzyme that catalyzes the phosphorylation of sphingosine sphingosine-1-phosphate (S1P), thereby maintaining the dynamic balance of the intracellular “sphingolipid rheostat” which governs critical processes such as cell growth, death, and immune cell trafficking [29]. The SPHK1/S1P axis is a well-established regulator of immune cell egress from lymphoid organs and recruitment to inflammatory sites [30], and its role in inflammatory arthritis is well-documented.

  For instance, SPHK1 plays a key role in TNFα-induced arthritis by attracting Th17 cells, with fewer synovial Th17 cells observed after SPHK1 reduction [31]. Modulation of SPHK1 can inhibit the release of proinflammatory cytokines such as IL-6 and TNF, suggesting its therapeutic potential [32]. Our findings that downregulation of SPHK1 was positively correlated with reduced infiltration of γδT cells (a known source of proinflammatory factors [33]), monocytes, and Th17 cells are consistent with a model of impaired S1P-mediated chemotaxis. Concurrently, the negative correlations between SPHK1 and the elevated immune factors IL6R and TNFSF13 suggest a broader disruption of inflammatory homeostasis. Given the established role of SPHK1 as the primary source of S1P, the observed immune cell depletion suggests that the age-related downregulation of SPHK1 creates a state of sphingolipid metabolism dysfunction, and may lead to a localized failure in S1P-dependent recruitment signals within the aging meniscus. This failure to recruit key innate and adaptive immune cells may be a cause of the depleted immune landscape in aging MI, representing a hallmark of local immunosenescence and compromising tissue surveillance and repair capacity.

  This sphingolipid-metabolism-driven immunosenescence is further exacerbated by the parallel downregulation of SPHK1 functional partner, PLPP2, which catalyzes the dephosphorylation of S1P, and generally opposes SPHK1 action [34]. The strong positive correlation we observed between SPHK1 and PLPP2, and their shared association with monocytes and γδT cells, indicates that their downregulation in aging MI is synergistic rather than antagonistic. This parallel reduction suggests a collapse of the dynamic sphingolipid rheostat, leading to a net loss of bioactive S1P-mediated signaling at the tissue level.

  This metabolic dysfunction fosters a pro-inflammatory tissue milieu, as evidenced by the strong negative correlations between the SPHK1-PLPP2 axis and the elevated senescence-associated and pro-inflammatory factors IL6R and TNFSF13. Thus, we propose that the sphingolipid metabolism dysfunction, inferred from the coordinated downregulation of SPHK1-PLPP2, may underpin the observed impairment in immune cell trafficking and contribute to the chronic inflammatory environment, thereby directly driving the process of immunosenescence in the aging meniscus. While PLPP2 downregulation has been noted in other age-related degenerative conditions [35, 36], our data uniquely position it as part of a concerted SPHK1-PLPP2 axis disruption that defines the sphingolipid metabolic facet of meniscal inflammaging. The precise compensatory or cooperative mechanisms between these two enzymes in this context warrant future investigation.


### IRF7 in inflammation and immune dysregulation
  While our study highlights the role of SPHK1 in immunometabolic dysfunction, IRF7 contributes to inflammaging through distinct, primarily immunoregulatory mechanisms. Originally identified as a master regulator of interferon response, IRF7 has emerged as a context-dependent driver of immunosenescence with age [37, 38]. This is consistent with our findings that IRF7 downregulation positively correlates with monocytes in aging MI. The critical role of IRF7 in joint health is supported by evidence that its deficiency exacerbates arthritis, potentially through its specific functions in macrophages [39].

  However, a seeming contradiction arises from between previous studies showing that IRF7 knockdown reduces pro-inflammatory cytokines (e.g., IL-6, TNF-α) [38] and our observation of elevated levels of IL6R and TNFSF13 in aging MI. We propose this discrepancy highlights the fundamental difference between acute gene manipulation and the chronic “inflammaging” milieu. In our study, long-term IRF7 deficiency in aged MI tissue may disrupt a crucial homeostatic or negative feedback mechanism, leading to the disinhibition and compensatory upregulation of alternative inflammatory pathways such as IL6R and TNFSF13, thereby exacerbating tissue damage. Thus, IRF7 downregulation appears to promote a state of dysregulated, rather than simply diminished inflammation.


### LAG3 as a nexus between metabolic dysregulation and T cell exhaustion
  The immunosenescent phenotype driven by the SPHK1 and IRF7 is further amplified by the upregulation of the immunosuppressive checkpoint LAG3. Our data positions LAG3 as a critical downstream effector and amplifier of the pathology. Its significant negative correlations with SPHK1 and PLPP2 suggest that sphingolipid metabolism dysfunction may contribute to its induction. This is consistent with the established literature showing LAG3 elevation in the elderly and its association with arthritis progression [40, 41].

  Moreover, the inverse relationship between LAG3 and key immune cells (γδT cells, monocytes, and Th17 cells), coupled with its positive correlation with IL6R and TNFSF13, delineates a feed-forward loop of immune inhibition and inflammation. While LAG3 is generally considered a marker of CD8+ T cell exhaustion [42], its significant inverse correlation with Th17 cells in our study underscores its role in CD4+ T cell dysfunction. This is consistent with emerging evidence that LAG3+ CD4+ T cells promote inflammation through IL-17 dysregulation [43]. Furthermore, the positive correlations we observed between LAG3 and the pro-inflammatory factors IL6R and TNFSF13 align with the understanding that LAG3 expression is induced by cytokines like IL-6 and TNF [44]. Therefore, LAG3 is not merely a correlative marker but an integral component of the immunopathogenic cascade initiated by SPHK1-IRF7 downregulation, directly linking metabolic-immune dysfunction to T cell exhaustion and tissue degradation.


### Epigenetic-Transcriptional dysregulation: an upstream Silencing mechanism
  Our regulatory network analysis provides a plausible upstream mechanism for the coordinated downregulation of SPHK1 and IRF7, bridging the gap between aging and the observed molecular phenotype. The downregulation of E2F4 and KLF5—which show exceptionally strong positive correlations with the biomarkers—likely directly impairs the transcription of SPHK1 and IRF7 in aging MI. This is supported by their known roles beyond the cell cycle, with E2F4 implicated in early experimental OA [45], KLF5 regulating chondrocyte apoptosis and the ECM degradation [46, 47].

  This transcriptional silencing is reinforced at the post-transcriptional level. The lncRNA NORAD was significantly upregulated and negatively correlated with with SPHK1 and IRF7. It is predicted to act as a molecular sponge for a network of miRNAs (e.g., hsa-mir-129-2-3p, hsa-mir-155-5p, hsa-mir-30d-5p, and hsa-mir-26a-5p) that target SPHK1 and IRF7. The relevance of this network is corroborated by prior studies: NORAD promotes cellular senescence in intervertebral disc degeneration [48], And its related miRNAs are associated with the inflammatory response in arthritis [49, 50], pain in osteoarthritis, and chondrocyte homeostasis [51]. Thus, the concerted action of the NORAD-miRNA axis and the E2F4/KLF5 TFs forms a cohesive upstream network that epigenetically and transcriptionally silences the SPHK1-IRF7 axis, initiating the downstream pathophysiological cascade.


### Clinical relevance: linking molecular signatures to patient symptoms
  The translational relevance of our molecular findings is underscored by their significant correlations with patient-centered clinical outcomes. In the aging group, both SPHK1 and IRF7 were positively correlated with Pre lysholm score and negatively correlated with the Pre VAS score. This directly links their downregulation to worse joint function and greater pain, a connection supported by established knowledge that improvements in these clinical scores are correlated with a decrease in inflammatory cytokines (e.g., IL6, TNF, IFN) [52]—the core mechanism of Non-Steroidal Anti-inflammatory Drugs (NSAIDs) and glucocorticoid to relieve pain [52, 53]. Given that SPHK1 and IRF7 were correlated with both elevated proinflammatory cytokines (e.g., IL6, TNFSF13) and decreased immune cells (e.g. Th17, monocyt, γδT) in our study, it is speculated that they may play key roles in triggering the inflammatory that underlies these symptoms. Interestingly, this molecular signature was associated with symptomatic severity but not with structural grades (K-L, ICRS). This dissociation suggests that the SPHK1-IRF7 axis may be more directly instrumental in driving the painful, inflammatory phenotype of aging MI rather than the absolute degree of structural cartilage loss over the studied timeframe, a distinction that could have implications for understanding symptomatic versus structural progression and for future patient stratification.


### Therapeutic implications and future directions
  Our integrated analysis, from upstream regulators to clinical correlations, culminates in actionable therapeutic insights. The drug prediction offers translational strategies, identifying seven drugs that potentially target both SPHK1 and IRF7. Notably, acetaminophen and decitabine were also predicted to target IL6R and TNFSF13, connecting them directly to the inflammatory components of our proposed axis. Acetaminophen, a common NASIDs for pain management in early osteoarthritis, has debates surrounding its efficacy and potential hepatotoxicity [54]. Intriguingly, SPHK1 deficiency has been shown to protect against acetaminophen-induced hepatotoxicity [55], suggesting that understanding this pathway could inform safer analgesic strategies. Decitabine, an epigenetic modulator, can alleviate symptoms in rheumatoid arthritis models, potentially by reducing pro-inflammatory Th17 cytokines and through the demethylation of factors like IRF8 [44], warranting investigation into its effect on IRF7 methylation in aging MI. Future studies should prioritize functional validation of this axis in vitro and in vivo and explore the efficacy of these predicted drugs, particularly decitabine given its epigenetic action, in rescuing the meniscal inflammaging phenotype.


## Conclusion
  In summary, we establish that the epigenetic silencing of SPHK1 and IRF7 is a pivotal driver of inflammaging in meniscal degeneration. This silencing, mediated by the lncRNA NORAD and TFs E2F4/KLF5, triggers a pathogenic cascade encompassing sphingolipid metabolism dysfunction (via SPHK1-PLPP2 downregulation) and loss of inflammatory homeostasis (via IRF7 deficiency). This immunometabolic disruption promotes a microenvironment of defective immune cell trafficking, T cell exhaustion (marked by LAG3), and chronic inflammation, which correlates strongly with clinical symptoms of pain and dysfunction. The identification of targetable nodes within this axis, including with drugs like decitabine, opens avenues for future diagnostic and therapeutic strategies aimed at mitigating age-related meniscus degeneration.
