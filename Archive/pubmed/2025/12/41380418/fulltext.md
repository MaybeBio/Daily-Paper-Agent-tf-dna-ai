BACKGROUND: 3-O-acetyl-ceanothic acid, a ceanothane-type triterpene from Colubrina yucatanensis, was previously shown to inhibit biofilm and reduce virulence factors in Pseudomonas aeruginosa. PURPOSE: To investigate the quorum sensing (QS)-related antivirulence mechanism of 3-O-acetyl-ceanothic acid. STUDY DESIGN: Exploratory study combining in vitro bioassays and in silico analysis. METHODS: QS inhibition was assessed with Chromobacterium violaceum CV026, and biofilm formation was evaluated in Pseudomonas aeruginosa PA14 WT and DeltalasR/DeltarhlR strains. Expression of QS regulators was measured by Real-Time -PCR. Molecular docking analyses evaluated binding affinities of 3-O-acetyl-ceanothic acid to key QS targets. RESULTS: 3-O-acetyl-ceanothic acid decreased lasR and pqsR expression, two key QS trasncritional regulators, suggesting interference with QS-regulated phenotypes. Docking supported preferential interaction with LasR and variable affinities across other targets. CONCLUSIONS: 3-O-acetyl-ceanothic acid from C. yucatanensis emerge as promising antivirulence agents against P. aeruginosa, opening an avenue for the study of other ceanothane triterpenes as anti-QS agents.
