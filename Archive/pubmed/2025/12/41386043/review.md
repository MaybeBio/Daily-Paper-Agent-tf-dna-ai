- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report synthesis and characterization of zinc(II), copper(II), and nickel(II) complexes of a naphthalene-bridged Schiff base ligand (Naphthim). The copper(II) complex 2 shows the highest DNA-binding affinity and G4-stabilizing ability among the series, with preferential stabilization of the cMyc quadruplex. Comparative studies against a benchmark complex [CuPhenim](2+) indicate that pi-extension enhances DNA-binding affinity and modulates selectivity. Molecular docking supports distinct topology-dependent binding modes.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no experimental sections, figures, tables, or supplementary data provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full experimental procedures, characterization data (NMR, HR-ESI-MS, elemental analysis), FRET melting curves, UV-Vis spectra, CD spectra, docking parameters and coordinates, statistical analyses, and any control experiments

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a potentially interesting contribution to the field of G4-targeting metal complexes, with a clear comparative design and a plausible structure-activity rationale. However, the evidence base available for review is limited to the abstract, which precludes verification of the central claims regarding binding affinity, selectivity, and binding modes. The work may be of interest to specialists in bioinorganic chemistry and nucleic acid recognition, but the current evidence does not establish the case beyond reasonable doubt.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in bioinorganic chemistry, nucleic acid structure and function, and anticancer drug discovery would be interested. The comparative analysis of pi-extension effects on DNA-binding selectivity offers a design principle that could inform future ligand development for G4-targeting therapeutics.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses a relevant question in G4-targeting drug design, namely how ligand pi-extension modulates binding affinity and selectivity. The use of multiple complementary biophysical methods (FRET, UV-Vis, CD) and molecular docking provides a multi-pronged approach. The inclusion of a benchmark complex for direct comparison strengthens the comparative claims.
- **Major Concerns**  
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1  
  - **Severity** Major  
  - **Blocking** Yes  
  - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
  - **Claim pointer** The copper(II) complex 2 exhibited the highest DNA-binding affinity and G4-stabilizing ability, with preferential effect on the cMyc quadruplex.  
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; specific data locations not provided  
  - **Concern** The abstract states that 2 shows the highest affinity and G4-stabilizing ability among the series, but no quantitative data (e.g., melting temperature shifts, binding constants, or CD signal changes) are provided. The claim of "preferential effect" on cMyc is not supported by comparative data across multiple G4 structures.

  - **Resolution test** Provide quantitative values (e.g., ΔTm for each G4 and duplex, binding constants with errors) and statistical comparisons across all tested structures.  
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2  
  - **Severity** Major  
  - **Blocking** Yes  
  - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
  - **Claim pointer** Pi-extension of the ligand framework substantially enhances DNA-binding affinity and modulates selectivity, as shown by comparison with [CuPhenim](2+).  
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; UV-Vis and CD data referenced but not shown  
  - **Concern** The comparative claim relies on UV-Vis and CD spectroscopy, but the abstract does not specify which spectral features were compared, how differences were quantified, or whether the observed differences are within experimental error.   - **Concern ID** R1-M3  
  - **Severity** Major  
  - **Blocking** Yes  
  - **Axis** Technical soundness  
  - **Claim pointer** The complexes were obtained via in situ or transmetallation protocols and characterized by NMR, HR-ESI-MS, and elemental analysis.  
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; characterization data not shown  
  - **Concern** The abstract does not indicate the purity or stability of the complexes in solution, particularly under the conditions used for DNA-binding assays (e.g., buffer, temperature, time). Metal complexes can undergo ligand exchange or hydrolysis, which would confound the binding results.

  - **Resolution test** Provide solution stability data (e.g., time-dependent UV-Vis or NMR spectra) and confirm the integrity of the complexes under assay conditions.  
  - **Concern ID** R1-M4

  - **Blocking** No
