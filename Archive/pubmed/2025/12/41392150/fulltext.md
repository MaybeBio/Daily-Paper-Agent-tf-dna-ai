# Identification of a chromatin-modifying gene-histone lysine N-methyl transferase (KMT2C/MLL3) as a potential immunomodulator oncogene in Indian pancreatic cancer patients


## Background
  Due to lack of early biomarkers, pancreatic cancer (PanCa), often manifests late, with few treatment options and poor prognosis, Although epigenetic regulators—particularly lysine methyltransferases like KMT2C—are becoming increasingly linked to cancer biology, their function in PanCa is still poorly understood.


## Objectives
  This study aims to investigate KMT2C’s mutational and expression landscape in Indian PanCa patients, to explore it’s possible role in carcinogenesis and immune modulation, and to assess its druggability through computational docking and dynamic simulations.


## Methods
  Clinical samples from Indian PanCa patients were used for differential expression and mutation studies. Differential expression, methylation, mutation, and immune cell infiltration profiling were also conducted using public databases (TCGA, GEO, CPTAC, and CCLE). Regulatory networks, scRNA-seq analysis, and protein–protein interaction networks were mapped. To evaluate ligand binding to KMT2C, molecular docking and 100-ns molecular dynamics simulations were used.


## Results
  KMT2C was overexpressed and exhibited a significantly higher mutational frequency (62.5%) in Indian PanCa samples in contrast with Western cohorts. Its role in immune suppression was implicated by positive correlations observed between KMT2C expression and several.

  immune-checkpoint receptor expression and regulatory T-cell infiltration. KMT2C was connected by functional enrichment to inositol phosphate metabolism and chromatin remodelling. The therapeutic potential of protodioscin was suggested by its strong binding affinity to KMT2C and the formation of stable interactions confirmed by MD simulations.


## Conclusion
  This study suggests KMT2C to be a putative oncogene in Indian PanCa patients in contrast with Western PanCa patients, with immunomodulatory effects and therapeutic potential, implicating its role as a promising biomarker, requiring additional clinical validation, emphasizing the necessity of ethnically informed precision oncology.

## Introduction
  Pancreatic cancer (PanCa) is a fatal malignancy, predominantly seen in men, characterized by non-specific symptoms, often making PanCa diagnosis a late-stage affair, accompanied with worse clinical management [1]. GLOBOCAN 2022 estimates 510,566 PanCa cases worldwide, contributing about 2.6% of cancer cases worldwide, with a much higher rate of global cancer-associated mortality at 4.8%. PanCa incidence shows wide geographical variations, with North America and Europe recording a much higher incidence rate as compared to Asian nations, with pancreatic adenocarcinoma (PAAD) the most common form (85–90%) of PanCa [2]. In India, however, PanCa is a comparatively rare site, ranking 24th amongst all cancer incidences, however North-East India records a much higher incidence rate compared to rest of India [3]. Risk factors for PanCa include diabetes mellitus, smoking, chronic pancreatitis, obesity, African-American ethnic origin, high-lipid diet, Helicobacter pylori infection in pancreas, etc. [4]. However, treatment options in PanCa is vastly limited, owing to late diagnosis, with most patients presenting with distant metastases at the time of detection and thus median survival even after surgery is approximately 10–12 months [5] [6] [7]. It is of utmost importance, thus to better understand the molecular genetics and pathophysiology contributing to PanCa tumorigenesis, to effectively fight this deadly disease.

  Histone methylation involves addition of methyl groups to amino acids of histone proteins of nucleosomes and thus play a crucial role in chromatin remodelling and consequent gene transcriptional expression [8]. The histone-lysine N-methyltransferase 2 (KMT2) family plays a potent role in the methylation of histone H3 tail at lysine 4 (H3K4) [9]. All members of the KMT2 family consist of an evolutionary conserved SET domains and include KMT2A (MLL1), KMT2B (MLL2), KMT2C (MLL3), KMT2D (MLL4), KMT2E (MLL5), KMT2F (SET1A), and KMT2G (SET1B) [10]. KMT2C (MLL3) regulates DNA damage response and repair by directly binding to DNA damage sites and mediating histone methylation, which in turn, relaxes the chromosomal structure near the damaged DNA, enabling DNA repair proteins to access the damaged sites and repair the damage [11]. KMT2C thus can be considered as an important epigenetic regulator and is believed to be mutated in a variety of human cancers, contributing to tumorigenesis, with exon sequencing revealing KMT2C to be the most common mutated gene in diffuse gastric adenocarcinoma (DGA) [8]. Furthermore KMT2C expression records wide variances in expression levels across different types of cancer, with overexpression in some cancers, while downregulation in others; thus the role of KMT2C dysregulation in tumorigenesis still remains an enigma [12].

  In this study, we intend to look into the role of KMT2C as a potential epigenetic modulator in the development and prognosis of PanCa. Our study offer insights into the wide ethno-demographic variations of differential expression and mutational frequency of KMT2C in PanCa, and consequent correlation with immune infiltration in tumor microenvironment (TME). The study also looks into the possible pharmacological targeting of KMT2C in PanCa through molecular docking analysis as a future therapeutic strategy.


## Methods

### Sample collection and ethical clearance
  Approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Indian Statistical Institute, Kolkata, the clinical samples were collected, only after informed consent. Our study included samples of 25 PanCa patients, collected from, Medical College and Hospital, I.P.G.M.E&R and SSKM Hospital, Tata Medical Center and Appollo Multi-speciality Hospital, Kolkata, during 2013–2022. Detailed methods were described in supplemental information section.


### KMT2C/MLL3 mutation profiling across TCGA PanCa dataset
  Mutational profile of KMT2C/MLL3 was assessed using cBioPortal (https://www.cbioportal.org) in TCGA PanCa dataset, to deduce a racial correlation with KMT2C/MLL3 mutations. In order to gain a deeper understanding of the underlying mechanism linking KMT2C/MLL3 mutation, an investigation was conducted to assess the impact of KMT2C/MLL3 mutation on tumor mutational burden (TMB) and microsatellite instability (MSI) in TCGA PanCa dataset.


### Analysis of differential expression and methylation status of KMT2C/MLL3 in PanCa from TCGA dataset
  mRNA expression status of KMT2C/MLL3 across 32 normal tissue types was assessed using GTEx database. Differential mRNA expression of KMT2C/MLL3 in PanCa and adjoining normal pancreas, along with stage-wise mRNA expressions in TCGA PAAD dataset was evaluated using GEPIA2. Overall survival (OS) of PanCa patients between low and high KMT2C/MLL3 expression groups were studied using Kaplan–Meier (KM) survival analysis with TCGA dataset using GEPIA2 [13]. Protein levels of KMT2C/MLL3 were assessed using Clinical Proteomic Tumor Analysis Consortium (CPTAC) dataset. Methylation extent in transcription start sites (TSS) of KMT2C/MLL3 gene and correlations between KMT2C/MLL3 gene expression and methylation in PanCa was analysed with the help of TCGA-PAAD dataset using SMART APP (http://www.bioinfo-zs.com/smartapp/) [14].


### Validation of KMT2C/MLL3 expression using independent Indian patient sample cohort
  RNA was isolated from tumor and normal tissue samples by RNeasy Kit (Qiagen) following manufacturers protocol (Cat No. 74104). cDNA was synthesised by RT-PCR using iTaq Universal SYBR Green Supermix (Bio Rad Life Sciences Research, Cat No. 1725125). Detailed methods are described in the supplemental information section. OS of Indian patients (n = 20) was analyzed using R Studio based Kaplan–Meier analysis.


### KMT2C/MLL3 differential expression and mutation analysis across cell lines
  Expression profile of KMT2C/MLL3 mRNA and degree of KMT2C/MLL3 mutation was evaluated across different PanCa cell lines using Cancer Cell Line Encyclopedia (CCLE) (https://depmap.org/portal) [15].


### Relevant dataset-based validation of KMT2C/MLL3 expression in PanCa
  Differential expression of KMT2C/MLL3 was evaluated in several GEO based PanCa RNA-Seq datasets: GSE211398 (16 tumor vs 12 adjoining normal tissues), GSE16515 (36 tumor vs 16 normal tissues) and GSE15471 (36 tumor vs 41 normal tissues). Similarly, differential methylation of KMT2C in tumor and adjacent normal tissues was evaluated from GEO based PanCa 450 K methylation microarray–GSE74071 (14 tumor vs 7 normal tissues).


### Immune cell infiltration and immune checkpoints analysis
  We conducted an analysis to investigate the relationship of KMT2C/MLL3 expression and mutation with immune cell infiltration in PanCa. Tregs are important immunosuppressive cells of immune system and promote cancer progression through their ability to limit anti-tumor immunity [16]. Therefore, to draw a relationship of KMT2C/MLL3 expression and mutation with immune infiltration level, we, used the consensus value employing multiple algorithms using the TIMER3.0 database [17]. Judging the importance of immunotherapy in PanCa, we further investigated the correlation between KMT2C/MLL3 expression and the major T cell co-inhibitory receptors in PanCa using TIMER2.0 database and conducted survival analysis of KMT2C/MLL3 high and low expression groups with variance in immune infiltration levels to investigate the clinical relevance of different tumor-immune subsets [18].


### Functional enrichment of KMT2C/MLL3 along with its correlated genes
  LinkedOmics, containing multi-omics data for 32 TCGA cancer types, with submodules like, LinkFinder (for gene association) and LinkInterpreter (for enrichment analysis) was used to investigate genetic co-expression and KEGG pathway analysis of KMT2C/MLL3 along with its associated genes in PanCa [19].


### Constructing pathway interaction network of KMT2C/MLL3
  To gain deeper insights into the involvement of KMT2C/MLL3 in PanCa, we conducted screening of genes and proteins interacting with KMT2C/MLL3 utilizing the GeneMANIA database (https://genemania.org/) and STRING database (https://string-db.org/) respectively.

  The String database was also used to conduct Gene Ontology (GO) analysis among functionally associated genes, covering three aspects: cellular component, molecular function and biological process [20]. The analyses were set according to the parameters: Similarity: > 0.8 and Maximum FDR 0.0001 to trims tails of the least significant terms between 0.01 and 0.0001, thus directing the focus of our analysis on the most significant terms (< = 0.0001).


### Analyzing RNA-seq datasets to identify lysine methyltransferase and SET domain family genes in PanCa
  Initially, we downloaded a list of 55 lysine methyltransferase genes and 35 SET domain family genes from HUGO Gene Nomenclature Committee (HGNC) (https://www.genenames.org/data/genegroup/#!/group/487). Next, we combined results from three sequencing data sets (GSE211398, GSE16515, and GSE15471) to generate a list of differentially expressed lysine methyltransferase genes and SET domain family genes for PanCa. The list of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were prepared for both the groups by considering a cut off of adjusted P < 0.05 and |log2FC|> or < 0. Functional enrichment and cluster analysis of the DEGs for both groups were done in Metascape [21]. Cytohubba plug-in of cytoscape was used to identify top 10 hub genes from amongst the set of both lysine methyltransferase genes and SET domain family genes using the maximal clique centrality (MCC) method [22].


### Identification of transcription factors and microRNAs regulating KMT2C/MLL3 expression
  The transcription factors (TFs) and microRNAs (miRNAs) regulating KMT2C/MLL3 expression were identified in our analysis with the help of RegNetwork database (http://www.zpliulab.cn/RegNetwork/home) [23].


### ScRNA data-based screening of differential expression of KMT2C in PanCa
  KMT2C expression was assessed in single cells using scRNA-seq datasets CRA001160 (24 tumor and 11 normal tissues) and GSE154778 (10 primary and 6 metastatic tumor tissues) from TISCH2 database [24].


### Identification of small drug molecules targeting KMT2C/MLL3
  Depmap portal was used to find out correlation between sensitivity to potential drugs (IC50) with KMT2C/MLL3 expression in cell lines. Additionally, using the Drug Interaction Database DGIdb (https://dgidb.org/), we identified a potential drug targeting KMT2C/MLL3 [25]. These predicted small molecules might be useful for disease management in PanCa.

  Genomics of drug sensitivity (GDSC) database (https://www.cancerrxgene.org/help) was used to analyse the sensitivity of 297 drugs between KMT2C/MLL3 wild type and KMT2C/MLL3_mut PanCa cell lines, to assess the potential implication of KMT2C/MLL3 mutation in drug sensitivity in PanCa [26].


### Virtual screening of potential ligands to KMT2C
  We conducted a virtual screening of potential ligands against KMT2C protein using the Schrödinger suite [27]. Homology models of KMT2C were built with SWISS-MODEL and evaluated using QMEAN. The protein structures were prepped with Maestro. A library of 148 candidate ligands from PubChem was processed with LigPrep and Epik. Docking was performed with Glide’s HTVS, SP, and XP modes. Top-ranked complexes underwent 100 ns of molecular dynamics (MD) simulation using NAMD to assess stability and dynamics [28]. Detailed protocols mentioned in the Supplementary Information section.


## Results

### KMT2C/MLL3 mutation profiling in our dataset and concurrence with TCGA dataset
  Using NGS method in a previous study from our lab, Saha et.al identified 48 somatic mutations from 8 PanCa patient samples in exons from 412 genes using 4 different variant callers, Varscan2, MuTect, Strelka, and BbB [29]. Out of the different gene mutations identified, KMT2C/MLL3 had the highest mutation frequency (62.5%) in our pool of East Indian PanCa patient population. Of these 5 patient samples, 3 recorded missense mutations, 1 recorded nonsense mutation, while the remaining 1 recorded silent mutation (Supplementary Table 1).

  To further look for KMT2C/MLL3 mutation frequency in PanCa across the geographic population, we conducted an analysis through cBioPortal (TCGA PanCancer Atlas) dataset. The somatic mutations identified both in our Indian dataset as well as in TCGA dataset are recorded (Fig. 1A) with KMT2C/MLL3 mutational frequency at a relatively low 5% (8 out of 175 PanCa cases) in TCGA dataset. Out of the 8 cases, 3 are missense mutations, another 3 truncating mutations, 1 amplification and 1 deep deletion (Fig. 1B). A correlation drawn between race and KMT2C/MLL3 alteration in PanCa patients revealed a statistically significant (p value < 0.05) higher alteration rate at 18.8% amongst Asian patients compared to 3.29% among Caucasian race (Fig. 1C). KM survival curve showed, OS of KMT2C/MLL3 altered group conferred poor prognosis with median OS of 12.92 months as compared to KMT2C/MLL3 unaltered group with median OS of 20.61 months (Fig. 1D). Our analysis also recorded a statistically significant positive correlation between KMT2C/MLL3 mutation and elevated TMB (p value = 0.0111) (Supplementary Fig.  1 A), however no correlation was recorded between KMT2C/MLL3 mutation and MSI in TCGA PanCa cohort (Supplementary Fig. 1B).Fig. 1Mutational profiling of KMT2C in PanCa. ### Genetic analysis of KMT2C/MLL3 expression and its possible regulation by DNA methylation in pancreatic cancer
  Expression profile of KMT2C/MLL3 gene, assessed through GTEx targeting all 32 tissue types, demonstrated a comparatively mediocre expression of KMT2C/MLL3 in normal pancreas tissues (median TPM = 5.332) (Supplementary Fig.  2 A), and analysis through TCGA database indicated that with the exception of breast, esophageal, head and neck, kidney, prostate and rectum cancer, KMT2C/MLL3 mRNA expression exhibited significant alterations in tumor tissues as compared to their adjoining normal counterparts (Supplementary Fig. 2B).

  Differential gene expression in PanCa (tumor vs tumor-adjoining normal, TCGA dataset), using Wilcoxon signed rank test showed non-significant downregulation of KMT2C/MLL3 in PanCa as compared to tumor-adjoining normal pancreas tissues (Fig. 2A). Stage-wise KMT2C/MLL3 expression plot didn’t document a significant variance in KMT2C/MLL3 expression with advancement of PanCa stages (Fig. 2B). KM survival curve showed comparable OS in both low and high KMT2C/MLL3 groups, with similar median OS (Fig. 2C). Similarly, assessing KMT2C/MLL3 protein expression through CPTAC in patient samples (tumor vs normal) demonstrated its downregulation in PanCa compared to normal pancreas tissues (Fig. 2D) and demonstrated that poorly differentiated PanCa reported significant KMT2C/MLL3 downregulation as compared to moderately and well-differentiated PanCa (Fig. 2E). In contrast, differential gene expression in our paired samples from Indian population (25 tumor vs 25 adjoining normal), showed a significant overexpression of KMT2C/MLL3 in PanCa. (Fig. 2F). Kaplan–Meier survival analysis showed that patients in the high KMT2C/MLL3 expression group had a significantly dismal survival with a p-value of 0.012. Our results suggest that KMT2C/MLL3 may be an adverse prognostic marker for Indian PanCa patients, as opposed to comparable survival in other global datasets.   Analysis of methylation extent using probes upstream of the TSS, from TCGA dataset, primarily impacting the promoter/enhancer regions of KMT2C/MLL3 gene showed a significantly higher methylation in PanCa as compared to normal pancreas (aggregate p value of 4.2 × 10–06) (Supplementary Fig.  3 A) and correlation analysis documented a significant negative correlation between KMT2C/MLL3 expression and methylation (p value = 0.026) (Supplementary Fig. 3B). Stage-wise methylation analysis concluded an increase in methylation value with progression in tumor stages (Supplementary Fig.  3 C). Strikingly, however, methylation analysis using the same probes from a GEO dataset–GSE74071 from Taiwan in Asia documented hypomethylation of TSS of KMT2C/MLL3 in PanCa tissues as compared to normal pancreas (Supplementary Fig. 3D).


### KMT2C/MLL3 mutation in various pancreatic cancer cell lines
  CCLE DepMap portal showed the median expression of KMT2C/MLL3 across PanCa cell lines (Supplementary Fig.  4 A) with a substantial amount of variation in median expression of KMT2C/MLL3 across cell lines, with individual cell lines KP4, YAPC and SUIT2 showing a very high level of KMT2C/MLL3 expression, while cell lines PACADD159 and PACADD135 showing a very low level of KMT2C/MLL3 expression (Supplementary Fig. 4B). Interestingly, the origin of all three cell lines KP4, YAPC and SUIT2 with strongest KMT2C/MLL3 expression are from Asia, specifically Japanese population, while the origin of both cell lines PACADD159 and PACADD135 are from Caucasian population. Careful examination of PanCa cell lines also revealed that the cell line T3M4, which has the highest intensity of damaging mutations in KMT2C/MLL3 gene (Supplementary Fig.  4 C), also has its origin from Asian (Japanese) population.


### Publicly available dataset-based validation of KMT2C/MLL3 expression in PanCa
  Further validation of KMT2C/MLL3 expression using 3 publicly available GEO datasets, showed downregulation of KMT2C/MLL3 in PanCa as compared to adjacent normal tissues, in concordance with the findings of TCGA dataset (Supplementary Table 2). Notably, all 3 datasets—GSE16515 (USA), GSE211398 (USA), GSE15471 (Romania) are from the Caucasian population.


### Pan-cancer immune profiling deconvolution concerning KMT2C expression in cancer microenvironment
  Our analysis also recorded pan-cancer immune profiling of KMT2C expression using 6 immune cell types on 32 cancer types, namely CD8 + T cells, CD4 + T cells, B cells, neutrophils, macrophages and dendritic cells employing multiple algorithms, namely TIMER, QUANTISEQ, EPIC, MCPCOUNTER, CIBERSORT and XCELL, and documented a significant positive correlation between KMT2C/MLL3 expression with CD8 + T cell infiltration, across 5 out of 6 major algorithms—TIMER, EPIC, MCPCOUNTER, QUANTISEQ, and XCELL, a patttern unique to PanCa and Uveal melanoma (UVM), amongst all cancer types (Supplementary Fig. 5–6).


### Correlation of immune cell infiltrates with KMT2C/MLL3 in PanCa
  Using TIMER 3.0 database, our findings revealed significant positive correlations between KMT2C/MLL3 expression and infiltration of various immune cell types, in PanCa microenvironment, including CD4 + T cells (p value = 7.45 × 10–06), CD8 + T cells (p value = 2.26 × 10–04), Tregs (p value = 8.21 × 10–04), and neutrophils (p value = 1.39 × 10–03) (Fig. 3A-B). Significant negative correlations were observed between KMTC mutation and immune infiltration level of various immune cell types, including CD4 + T cell (p value = 0.00093), CD8 + T cell (p value = 0.0018), dendritic cell (p value = 0.008) and macrophage (p value = 0.026) in PanCa microenvironment (Supplementary Fig. 7A–F).Fig. 3The relationship of KMT2C expression/mutation with immune infiltration level in PanCa using TIMER3.0 database. B Correlations of KMT2C/MLL3 expression with infiltrating levels of dendritic cells, neutrophils and macrophages

  Additionally, our analysis using TIMER2.0 database revealed a statistically significant correlation between KMT2C/MLL3 levels and expression of several T cell co-inhibitory receptors, with the most potent positive correlation with CD96, followed by C10ORF54 (coding for VISTA protein), BTLA, LAG3, IDO1, TIGIT, HAVCR2(TIM-3) and PDCD1 (coding for PD-1 protein) respectively (Supplementary Fig. 8A–B).

  Survival analysis using Timer 2.0 database between low and high expression groups of KMT2C/MLL3 with immune infiltration levels revealed a significantly higher survival in high KMT2C/MLL3 expression and low B cell infiltration group compared to high KMT2C/MLL3 expression and high B cell infiltration group(p value = 0.00939) (Supplementary Fig.  9 C).

  These findings underscore the potential implications of KMT2C/MLL3 in moderating immune responses in PanCa, providing us an idea about possible immunotherapeutic interventions in improving immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy in treating PanCa.

### Interaction network analysis of KMT2C/MLL3
  We established a comprehensive gene interaction network involving KMT2C/MLL3, encompassing KMT2A, KMT2B, KMT2D and KMT2E, leveraging the GeneMania database, with physical interactions amongst their expressed proteins to be the major form of interaction (Supplementary Fig.  10 A). ### Transcription factors and microRNAs regulating the expression of KMT2C/MLL3
  Using RegNetwork database at high confidence level, we identified 5 TFs that regulate KMT2C/MLL3 expression in humans, namely SP1, MZF1, TGIF1, EGR1, and NKX6-1(Supplementary Fig.  13 A). Our analysis also identified the list of miRNAs, which interact with KMT2C/MLL3 to regulate its expression (Supplementary Fig. 13B).


### ScRNA data-based differential expression of KMT2C in PanCa
  Analysis of scRNA-seq studies showed similar KMT2C expression in PanCa TME across malignant and stromal cells in dataset PAAD_CRA001160 from Chinese population, while elevated KMT2C expression in malignant cells as compared to stromal cells and immune cells in PanCa TME in dataset PAAD_GSE154778 from Korean population (Supplementary Fig. 14A–C).


### Identification of small molecule/drugs targeting KMT2C/MLL3
  On analysis through depmap portal, the drug sensitivity to two drugs ABT-737 and Navitoclax recorded a nearly moderate negative correlation at −0.358 and −0.341 respectively with KMT2C expression in cell lines (Supplementary Fig. 15A-B). A negative correlation with IC50 means the drug is more effective, when KMT2C/MLL3 expression is higher. On using the DGIdb database, we could identify Aromatase inhibitor, blocking a key aromatization step in estrogen biosynthesis, with a significant drug-interaction score of 26.1 as a potential drug (NCI Thesaurus code: C1740; CHEBI Id: 50,790) targeting KMT2C/MLL3 mutation. However, the drug is yet awaiting regulatory approval. Analysis of the GDSC assay of the sensitivity of PanCa cell lines to 297 drugs indicated KMT2C/MLL3 mutant PanCa cell lines to be significantly more resistant to drugs- Lestaurtinib, Docetaxel, Sorafenib, Pyridostatin, Epirubicin, Pevonedistat, Sepantronium bromide and Ulixertinib, compared to wild type PanCa cell lines (Supplementary Fig. 16).


### Molecular docking analysis of the protodioscin–KMT2C protein complex
  Among the evaluated ligands, protodioscin, a steroidal saponin, exhibited the most favorable docking score (− 10.725 kcal/mol), indicating the strongest binding affinity for KMT2C/MLL3 (Supplementary Table 3). The interaction profile of the Protodioscin–KMT2C/MLL3 protein complex is presented in (Fig. 7A–E), where Protodioscin demonstrates a specific and chemically coherent binding pattern within the active site of KMT2C/MLL3. The 2D interaction diagram (Fig. 7B) reveals that the spirostane steroidal core of protodioscin is embedded in a predominantly hydrophobic cleft, engaging in π–σ (2.83 Å) and π–alkyl (5.13 Å) interactions with the aromatic side chain of PHE4689, thereby anchoring the nonpolar core within a lipophilic sub-pocket illustrated in the hydrophobicity surface map (Fig. 7E). The adjoining sugar chains, rich in hydroxyl functionalities, extend toward the rim of the binding site and engage a diverse network of polar and charged residues.   Overall, the molecular interactions captured in (Fig. 7A–E) reflect a finely tuned ligand–receptor recognition mechanism: the hydrophobic core of the spirostane moiety is sequestered within the nonpolar central groove, while the sugar hydroxyls participate in extensive and directional hydrogen bonding with strategically positioned ionizable and polar residues at the cavity surface. This interaction landscape, reinforced by hydrophobic and electrostatic complementarity, aligns well with the high docking score (Glide GScore: − 10.725 kcal/mole; Emodel: − 83.327 kcal/mole) observed for the Protodioscin–KMT2C/MLL3 complex.


### MD Simulation analysis of the protodioscin–KMT2C complex
  To evaluate the dynamic stability of the protein–ligand complex, a 100-ns MD simulation was conducted for the Protodioscin–KMT2C/MLL3 complex. The simulation results are summarized in Supplementary Fig. 15A–F. The global structural stability of the complex was assessed using the root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) of the protein backbone (Supplementary Fig.  17 A). Following an initial equilibration phase within the first 15 ns, the RMSD stabilized around 19.2 Å ± 1.3 Å, with minimal fluctuations (< 2 Å) throughout the remaining simulation time, indicating the absence of large-scale conformational drift. Local flexibility of amino acid residues was analyzed using root-mean-square fluctuation (RMSF) (Supplementary Fig. 17B). The majority of residues exhibited moderate fluctuations (mean RMSF: 2.4 Å ± 1.4 Å), with lower values (< 1.8 Å) observed for residues lining the binding interface, such as PHE4689, TYR4691, and ARG4779, suggesting tight and stable interactions with the ligand. (Detailed analysis in supplementary information section). Thus the Protodioscin–KMT2C/MLL3 complex exhibited excellent structural and dynamic stability throughout the 100-ns MD simulation. The low and consistent RMSD and radius of gyration (Rg) values, reduced flexibility at the ligand-binding interface, persistent hydrogen bond network, and stable SASA/PSA profiles collectively indicate a robust and tightly bound complex. These results reinforce the strong binding affinity predicted by molecular docking and highlight the pharmacological potential of protodioscin as a stable KMT2C/MLL3 interactor.


## Discussion
  PanCa is one of the most lethal condition of digestive system with dismal prognostic outcomes with significant variations in frequency of incidence across regions, races and ethnic groups. Ethnic variations are considered as a high-risk feature influencing the occurrence of PanCa [28–30]. Furthermore, PanCa survival outcome in blacks and hispanics have been reported to be poorer as compared to Whites, suggesting an ethnic influence in the prognosis of PanCa [31].

  Histone proteins are subject to post-translational modifications, with the most important being lysine methylation—which play an important role in regulating chromatin structure, nuclear architecture and thus gene expression [32]. A large number of histone lysine methyltransferase (KMT) enzymes are involved in catalyzing lysine methylation primarily involving histones H3 and H4 – mutations or aberrant expression of these KMTs have been frequently linked with neoplastic transformation [33]. Noteworthy in this respect is the epigenetic modification of H3K4 methylation, which has both tumor inhibiting and promoting effects, thus the prognostic value of H3K4 methylation in cancer remains controversial [34].

  One such member, contributing to H3K4 methylation—KMT2C/MLL3, is aberrantly expressed and/or mutated in various cancers [35]. Pan-cancer analysis of KMT2C/MLL3 revealed contrasting roles of KMT2C/MLL3 in multiple cancers – while it was overexpressed in certain cancer types, it was downregulated in some others [12]. A recent study demonstrated that silencing of KMT2C/MLL3 in PanCa cell lines resulted in a significant reduction in cell proliferation, indicating to the oncogenic property of KMT2C/MLL3 in PanCa. The same study from United Kingdom, also recorded favourable survival outcomes in KMT2C downregulated PanCa patients as compared to the KMT2C/MLL3 unaltered groups [11].

  In concordance with previous work published from our lab where we have revealed ethnic variations of KRAS mutational frequency and KCNJ5 expression pattern in development of PanCa, and identified two novel hypermethylated epigenetic genes – NPY and FAIM2 in Indian PanCa [10, 28, 36], this study, based on Indian PanCa subjects documents a significant overexpression of another epigenetic gene KMT2C in PanCa as compared to adjacent normal tissues, in stark contrast to the TCGA dataset primarily from the Western world, which document KMT2C/MLL3 downregulation in PanCa as compared to adjacent normal tissues. Interestingly, in conformation with PanCa tissue samples, our analyses also found out wide variances in KMT2C/MLL3 expression levels across PanCa cell lines derived from different ethnic populations, with Asian (Japanese) cell lines including KP4, YAPC and SUIT2 showing the highest expression levels and cell lines from the Caucasian population including PACADD159 and PACADD135 showing the least KMT2C expression levels. Similarly, validation through GEO datasets from USA and Romania also point out towards KMT2C downregulation in Caucasian population. Likewise, while hypermethylation of TSS is associated with KMT2C/MLL3 downregulation, analysis in GEO based PanCa dataset from Taiwan in Asia documented hypomethylation at TSS of KMT2C/MLL3, and consequent upregulation of KMT2C/MLL3. Our analysis, thus point out towards the oncogenic potential of KMT2C/MLL3 in Asian (especially Indian) population, in contrast to the Caucasian population.

  Our analysis also detail similar ethnic variations in KMT2C/MLL3 mutation frequency amongst Caucasian and Asian populations in PanCa, with a relatively low mutation frequency of 3.29% in Caucasian population, while a relatively high mutation frequency of about 19% amongst Asian population (Fig. 1). Corroborating with the analysis, our study conducted amongst Indian PanCa patients revealed an inordinately high KMT2C/MLL3 mutation frequency at 62.5%. It is of interest to note that in our Indian cohort of PanCa patients, majority (60%) of KMT2C/MLL3 mutation was missense mutation (Table 1).

  T cells, forming an integral part of our immune system, regulate a fine balance between tolerance and autoimmunity – either with the help of immunosuppressive subset of CD4+ T cells called the Tregs [37] and/or with the help of T cell co-inhibitory receptors. It is well established that infiltration of T cells in TME is one of the primary anti-tumor immune response, and correlates with a positive clinical outcome in several solid tumors [38]. It is in this context, our immune profiling identified a significant positive correlation of CD4 + T cell and CD8 + T cell infiltration in PanCa microenvironment with KMT2C expression and a significant negative correlation with KMT2C mutation.

  However, one of the central features exploited by malignant cells in cancer development is the escape of immune surveillance – primarily by the recruitment of immunosuppressive Tregs and/or the elevated expression of T cell co-inhibitory receptor molecules, thus contributing to an immunosuppressive TME and avoiding immune-mediated killing [39]. It is in this respect our study documented a significant positive correlation between elevated KMT2C/MLL3 expression in PanCa and expression of Tregs in TME.

  Recent investigations have started exploring the association between KMT2C mutation/expression and immunotherapy response, albeit not on PanCa as of date. One such study in metastatic melanoma (MM) patients identified KMT2C as an independent factor for anti-PD-1 response with KMT2C mutated MM patients showing significantly higher OS compared to KMT2C wild-type MM patients, after anti-PD-L1 therapy of pembrolizuab.

  [45]Similar observation was recorded in colorectal cancer (CRC) patients with KMT2C mutated patients showing a higher OS compared to KMT2C wild-type CRC patients following immune-checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) therapy [42]. Furthermore KMT2C downregulation had been associated with higher objective response rate (ORR) following immunotherapy in metastatic gastric cancer patients [11].

  Our analysis also demonstrated a strong positive correlation of heightened KMT2C/MLL3 expression in PanCa with several T cell co-inhibitory receptor proteins including CD96, VISTA, BTLA, LAG3, TIGIT, TIM-3, PD-1,etc. The results suggest potential manipulation of Treg cells as a promising anticancer treatment strategy in PanCa cells documenting KMT2C/MLL3 overexpression. Given the growing influence of ICIs (blockade of T cell co-inhibitory receptors) in cancer immunotherapy [40], monoclonal antibodies targeting T cell co-inhibitory receptors especially CD96, VISTA, BTLA or LAG-3 which have the strongest positive correlation with KMT2C/MLL3 expression in PanCa might be an effective immunotherapeutic strategy in KMT2C/MLL3 overexpressed PanCa cells and needs future clinical validation.

  Importantly, our structural modelling and in-silico ligand screening further support the oncogenic role of KMT2C/MLL3 in Indian PanCa by demonstrating the feasibility of selective molecular targeting. Molecular docking identified protodioscin, a plant steroidal saponin, as a promising bioactive compound that engages the KMT2C/MLL3 catalytic domain through a network of hydrogen bonds and hydrophobic interactions with key residues such as PHE4689, ARG4779, TYR4691, and HIS4900. These interactions were further validated through 100-ns molecular dynamics simulation, which showed stable root mean square deviation (RMSD), consistent radius of gyration (Rg), and persistent hydrogen bonding, collectively confirming the dynamic stability of the protodioscin–KMT2C/MLL3 complex (Fig. 7A–E), (Supplementary Fig. 17A–F). This structural integrity under dynamic conditions underscores the druggability of KMT2C/MLL3 and supports our prediction that it may serve as a pharmacologically tractable target in Indian PanCa. The complementary chemical and electrostatic environment of the KMT2C/MLL3 binding cavity further aligns with protodioscin’s structural features, offering a rationale for its selective affinity and potential inhibitory function. These findings provide a compelling proof-of-concept for developing small-molecule inhibitors against overexpressed KMT2C/MLL3 in PanCa and may guide future drug discovery efforts, particularly in populations showing elevated mutation and expression burden.

  However, there are a few limitations to our study. Firstly, our gene expression analysis couldn’t be checked at the protein level through IHC technique, which would have been an additional validation. Secondly, we couldn’t perform the mechanistic studies to track on the impact of KMT2C mutation/overexpression in PanCa cell lines due to non-availability of cell lines in our lab. Future studies should focus on our findings of immune analysis to translational research to explore the possible association between immunotherapeutic efficacy and KMT2C mutation/expression in Asian (especially, Indian) PanCa patients, where there’s a predominantly high KMT2C mutation/expression rate. Furthermore, protodioscin identified as a potential stable pharmacological interactor with KMT2C with the help of our molecular docking analysis, provides us with a new direction in future clinical trials in assessing the drug’s therapeutic efficacy in KMT2C overexpressed PanCa patients, opening a gateway for personalized treatment opportunities.


## Conclusion
  We present a complete, ethically-informed characterization of KMT2C/MLL3 in Indian pancreatic cancer patients with evidence of KMT2C/MLL3 overexpression and greater mutational frequency in Indian PanCa patients in contrast to other Western study populations. We used the wealth of bioinformatics, transcriptomic, methylomic, and proteomic profiling to provide evidence oncogenic potential of KMT2C/MLL3 in Asian PanCa cohort, thus highlighting the ethnic variability in gene dysregulation. Interestingly, KMT2C/MLL3's overexpression was associated with dismal overall survival, highlighting KMT2C/MLL3s possible prognostic importance in a population-specific context. A strong association of KMT2C/MLL3 expression with immune regulatory pathways, especially with regulatory T cells and the co-inhibitory receptors, CD96, LAG3, and PD-1, suggested a novel immune modulating role in PanCa TME. Additionally, we performed molecular docking and 100-ns MD simulations that provided evidence that protodioscin binds KMT2C/MLL3 with high affinity and stability, thereby demonstrating the druggability of KMT2C/MLL3 for targeting in therapeutics. To conclude, this work validates the oncogenic and immuno-regulatory role of KMT2C/MLL3 in PanCa and underscores the significance of demographic diversities in cancer genomics landscape. The integration of multi-omics with machine learning and drug discovery platforms forms the foundation for personalized, precision-based interventions targeting epigenetic regulators such as KMT2C/MLL3. Future translational and functional studies will need to re-create the results of this study and evaluate KMT2C-targeted therapeutic approaches utilizing ethnically diverse patient cohorts.
