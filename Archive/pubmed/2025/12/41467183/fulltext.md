# Rare germline JAK/STAT variants in a Korean cohort amplify innate immune responses to vaccination


## Summary
  The JAK/STAT signaling pathway regulates cytokine-driven responses. Here we investigated the biological impact of germline single nucleotide variants in JAK kinases and STAT transcription factors in vaccine response. First, we applied a data-mining strategy to RNA-seq datasets from a Korean cohort to uncover JAK/STAT germline variants and analyzed their corresponding interferon transcriptomic responses. Ultra-rare variants associated with heightened interferon transcriptomic responses were identified. AlphaFold 3 predicted conformational alterations in these JAK and STAT variants, focusing on protein-protein interactions and receptor-complex assembly. Co-occurring variants in TYK2 and other interferon signaling regulators that could influence the impact of the JAK and STAT variants were explored. We found that data mining can reveal candidate germline variants with potential roles in innate immunity and assessed how analyzing vaccine-induced gene expression changes can serve as an in vivo method to functionally assess the biological significance of missense mutations in key immune signaling pathways.


## Introduction
  The human genome contains a vast number of single nucleotide variants (SNVs), leading to millions of protein variants. Some of these variants influence physiological and pathological differences in gene response.1,2,3 While certain variants have been well-studied and linked to specific measurable traits, the majority remains classified as variants of unknown significance (VUSs). Many of these individual variants likely do not act in isolation but interact with other variants within specific genetic pathways, making their effects more accurately assessed in the context of their genetic environment. Additionally, their functional significance may not be evident under normal physiological conditions but may become more pronounced in induced scenarios, such as hormonal changes during pregnancy or immune system activation through vaccination or infection.

  The Janus Kinase (JAK) and signal transducers and activators of transcription (STAT) signaling pathway plays a crucial role in immune responses to pathogens and systemic signals, making it an ideal model for studying the epistatic effects of protein variants within this pathway. This pathway is essential for converting cytokine signals into genetic responses that regulate various biological processes, including body growth,4 lactation5 and hematopoiesis.5,6,7 Virally encoded antagonists of JAK/STAT family members are potent factors influencing viral virulence through limitation of interferon (IFN) response, knowledge that is being exploited for development of new attenuated viral vaccine approaches that take into account interactions with JAK/STAT family members.8 Research on human mutations and genetic studies in mice has highlighted the critical roles of these tyrosine kinases and transcription factors.3,4,5,7,9,10,11,12,13,14,15 Missense mutations occur in roughly half of the codons across the four JAK family members (JAK1, JAK2, JAK3, and TYK2) as well as the seven STAT proteins (STAT1, STAT2, STAT3, STAT4, STAT5A, STAT5B, and STAT6).16 While some variants, such as JAK2V617F, are well-studied and linked to specific diseases,17,18 the functional relevance of most missense mutations remains largely unknown. Furthermore, the biological effects of both germline and somatic mutations may be limited to particular cell types or specific physiological conditions, such as lactation or immune responses triggered by cytokines.

  There is an existing gap in studying combinatorial SNV effects (epistasis) on vaccine-induced immune responses. While it is well-established that the degree of JAK/STAT pathway activation can influence response to viral disease and vaccination8,19 and that mutations in JAK/STAT genes are responsible for a variety of clinical phenotypes,20 understanding epistatic interactions between JAK/STAT pathway family members carrying specific SNVs remains an emerging area of investigation, particularly in vaccine response. Investigating the effects of combinatorial SNVs within the JAK/STAT pathway and the potential influence of epistasis on physiological responses in humans requires experimental strategies that enable the spatial and temporal analysis of molecular responses triggered by pathogens or systemic and local signals. JAKs and STATs become active within seconds of cytokine exposure, initiating highly predictable genetic programs. For instance, the rapid induction of IFN-stimulated genes (ISGs) plays a critical role in T cell activation. The activation cycle of these genes, followed by their return to baseline, can be tracked over hours or even days. Vaccination provides a controlled setting in which the timing of inoculation offers a reproducible framework to assess immune stimulation both before and after exposure. Measuring gene expression in immune or hormonal response pathways serves as the most direct and sensitive approach to evaluate JAK/STAT transcriptional activity. This method can help identify JAK/STAT variants that influence downstream genetic programs.

  We recently utilized RNA-seq to analyze innate immune responses in a Korean cohort following COVID-19 vaccination.21 This dataset provided a unique opportunity to examine the influence of JAK/STAT SNVs on vaccine-induced activation of ISGs within their native genetic context. Studies like this allow for precise identification of genetic variants across all JAK and STAT genes, facilitating the biological investigation of potential epistatic interactions between these variants. The findings presented here contribute to a deeper understanding of the structural and molecular impact of specific JAK/STAT variants in shaping innate immune responses to vaccination.


## Results

To explore this hypothesis, we used RNA-seq data to identify SNVs within protein-coding sequences resulting in protein variants (missense variants).   The allele frequencies (AFs) of JAK/STAT germline SNVs were documented, according to data from the Genome Aggregation Database (gnomAD), All of Us databases, and the Korean Genome Project (KGP). There ranged from a relatively common TYK2V362F (AF = 0.276 in gnomAD) to very rare variants like JAK1N226S, JAK3R222H, and TYK2R490C (AF ≈ 1.5 × 10−5 in global databases that includes 12 populations [European, Hispanic, African, Asian, and other diverse ancestries]; Table 1). In contrast, several variants showed markedly higher frequencies in the Korean population, prompting an evaluation of population-specific enrichment using Fisher’s exact test (Chi-square test). gnomAD includes 76,215 whole genome sequences (WGSs) and exome data from 730,947 individuals, All of Us hosts WGS from approximately 250,000 individuals,22 and the KGP hosts WGS from approximately 7,000 individuals. Notably, the JAK1N226S mutation has been identified at an AF of 0.0022 in the KGP cohort, compared to 2.8 × 10−5 in gnomAD and 1.6 × 10−5 in All of Us. This difference was statistically significant (p < 0.00001), indicating enrichment in Koreans. Similarly, JAK2G127D (AF = 0.0066 in KGP vs. 5.17 × 10−4 in gnomAD, p = 4.77 × 10−8), TYK2R703W (AF = 0.0319 vs. 0.00298, p = 6.80 × 10−25), and STAT6V269I (AF = 0.0254 vs. 0.00197, p = 2.31 × 10−16) also showed significant enrichment in the Korean population. The JAK3R222H variant was also more frequent in Koreans (AF = 0.001) than in gnomAD (3.95 × 10−5) and All of Us (4.00 × 10−5), though the enrichment was more modest. Rare variants such as TYK2R490C also showed increased frequency in KGP (AF = 0.0004) compared to global datasets (∼1.5–1.8 × 10−5), supporting a population-specific pattern. Although TYK2V362F variant is also prevalent in Asian populations, with an AF exceeding 0.393 (gnomAD: 0.276, All of Us: 0.237), and was identified in 26 Korean individuals, 22 of whom had no additional JAK/STAT SNVs (Table 2).   Several of the mutated residues—JAK1N226, JAK2G127, JAK3R222, TYK2R490, and TYK2R703W, but not TYK2V362F—are evolutionarily conserved among species (Figures S1 and S2), suggesting their likely importance in maintaining the structural and functional integrity of each of the JAKs. Also, the valine residue corresponding to human STAT6V269 is highly conserved among these species (Figure S3), suggesting its importance in maintaining the structural and functional integrity of STAT6. Structural mapping revealed that JAK1N226S, JAK2127D, JAK3R222H, and TYK2V362F mutations within the FERM (F: 4.1 protein, ezrin, radixin, and moesin) domain, whereas the TYK2R490C and TYK2R703W mutations are positioned within the SH2 (Src homology 2) and pseudokinase (PK) domains, respectively (Figure 1A). These domains are the key for interactions of JAKs with cytokine receptors, opening the possibility that these mutations could impact this process.

  Co-occurrence of SNVs was observed in several individuals (Table 2). The individual carrying the JAK1N226 variant and the three persons with the JAK2G127D variant also carried one TYK2 variant each (Table 2). Notably, individual #15 carried three very rare variants, JAK2G127D, JAK3R222H, and TYK2R490C. Two individuals out of the four carrying the STAT2V269I variant, were also positive for the TYK2V362F variant, consistent with possible interactive effects. Twenty-two individuals carried only the TYK2V362F variant.

  To assess the pathogenic potential of these JAK/STAT variants, we utilized the COSMIC and NIH ClinVar databases, along with several in silico prediction tools (Table S1). There were no entries of the JAK1N226S, TYK2R490C, and TYK2R703W variants in the COSMIC database suggesting that these SNVs are restricted to the germline. In contrast, JAK2G127D, JAK3R222H,TYK2V362F and STAT6V269I are known somatic mutations. According to ClinVar, TYK2R490C was classified as a VUS, while all others were labeled as benign or likely benign. AlphaMissense,23 an advanced computational prediction tool, assigned all variants a benign score (ranging from 0.04 to 0.34). Conversely, PolyPhen224 analysis identified JAK1N226S, JAK3R222H, and TYK2R703W as potentially deleterious. The Rare Exome Variant Ensemble Learner25 tool predominantly categorized these variants as neutral or benign. As expected, the varying predictions from different computational tools present challenges in determining the in vivo role of these variants in shaping innate immune responses following vaccination.


### Rare JAK/STAT variants enhanced vaccine-induced innate immune responses
  Given the rarity of these variants, no clinical or biological data have been reported on the JAK1N226S, JAK3R222H, and TYK2R703W variants. To determine functional consequences, we initially examined transcriptomic data from individuals carrying the JAK1N226S or JAK2G127D variants (Figure 2). RNA-seq data were collected from peripheral blood mononuclear cells before vaccination (day zero) and two days after receiving the ChAdOx1 vaccine (day two).21 As a reference, we used a set of 21 genes whose expression increased by more than 2-fold in the control group (individuals without JAK/STAT variants) (Figure 2; Table S2). These direct JAK/STAT target genes include ISGs encoding antiviral proteins, IFN receptors, and transcription factors. The transcriptomic response to vaccination was notably stronger in individuals carrying the JAK1N226S or JAK2G127D variants. Specifically, IFN response genes such as IFI27 and OTOF, a gene expressed in macrophages and DCs, were induced more than 400-fold and 97-fold, respectively, in these individuals (Figure 2A; Table S2). In contrast, the control group exhibited a maximum induction of only 15-fold.   Additionally, individuals with these JAK variants also carried the TYK2V362F or TYK2R703W mutations (Table 2), respectively, raising the possibility of epistatic interactions between JAKs and TYKs. To explore this, we assessed the vaccine-induced transcriptomic response in individuals with only the TYK2V362F variant. The overall induction of the reference gene set was comparable between individuals carrying the TYK2V362F variant and those carrying no missense mutations in any of the JAK/STAT components (Figure 2A), suggesting that this variant alone might not serve as a dominant activator of ISGs. The IFI27 gene appears to be an exception as it is induced in individuals carrying the TYK2V362F variant but no other JAK variants.

  The potential impact of the STAT6V260I variant was investigated in three individuals, with two also harboring the TYK2V362F variant (Figure 2B). The transcriptomic response in the two individuals carrying the STAT6V260I and the TYK2V362F variant two days following the vaccination greatly exceeded that seen in the person with only the STAT6V260I variant, suggesting cooperativity between the STAT6 and TYK2 variant. The impact of the variants was temporally restricted, and no differences were observed at day seven after the vaccination (Table S2).

  One individual was unique in carrying two very rare JAK variants, JAK2G127D, and JAK3R222H, as well as the very rare TYK2R490C variants. Unlike the previous individuals, transcriptomic analyses were performed at day zero and day four after vaccination due to unavailability of this subject’s participation in the study at day 2 (Table S2). While the overall gene induction in this individual was similar to that obtained in individuals without JAK/STAT variants, the relative activation of IFI27 was lower, suggesting that this combination of JAK and TYK variants might have a suboptimal capacity to convey IFN signaling.


### Modeling revealed structural consequences of JAK/STAT variants
  To investigate the structural implications of JAK and STAT variants, we employed AlphaFold 3 (AF3)26,27 to model the structures of the variants (Figure 3). Initially, the effects of individual variants were examined at the domain level, followed by analysis at the protein complex level. The rare JAK1N226S variant is located within the F2 subdomain of the FERM domain.28 To investigate its structural impact, we generated a variant model using the crystal structure of JAK1 FERM SH2 domains (residues 31–577) complexed with IFN lambda receptor 1 (IFNLR1, residues 260–307; PDB: 5L04)28 (Figure S4A). The variant structure closely aligned with the wild type (WT), exhibiting an overall root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) of 0.297 Å. However, notable local structural differences were observed; specifically, the N226S mutation disrupted the hydrogen bond between residues N226 and R230 (Figure 3A). This disruption could destabilize the alpha helix within the F2 subdomain. Interestingly, despite minimal overall conformational differences in JAK1, an unexpected conformational flip was detected in the IFNLR1 receptor peptide near the box1 region (residues 269–277) (Figure 3B). Given the substantial distance (∼16 Å) between the N226(S) residue and IFNLR1, this observation was unanticipated (Figure S4B).   The JAK2G127D variant, located in the F1 subdomain of the FERM domain,29 was modeled using the AF3 prediction guided by the crystal structure of the WT JAK2 FERM/SH2 domains in complex with the intracellular domain of the erythropoietin receptor (EPOR; PDB: 6E2Q)29 (Figure S4C). The model consists of JAK2 residues 36–514 and EPOR residues 273–338. Structural alignment showed overall similarity between WT and variant complexes, with an RMSD of 0.425 Å. However, significant local conformational differences were observed, particularly at the site of the variation and the EPOR interbox region (Figure 3C). In the WT structure, R130 interacts with G127 and E141 via a hydrogen bond and salt bridges, forming a stabilizing triad. In the G127D variant, these interactions are lost. Instead, R130 reorients away from G127D, and E141 establishes multiple hydrogen bonds with its own backbone and that of G139, forming a new pocket near the interface with EPOR. This structural rearrangement leads to a cascade of conformational shifts in the EPOR interbox region. Specifically, W316 flips into the newly formed pocket, displacing W317, which relocates to the site previously occupied by W316. Subsequently, Y309 flips into the former W317 site. These coordinated shifts suggest a dynamic relay of residue repositioning, potentially altering JAK2-EPOR signaling. Given that JAK2 serves as a prototypical member of the JAK family,30 the G127D variant may broadly impact downstream signaling of not only EPOR but also other JAK2-associated receptors by altering receptor interface dynamics and activation mechanisms.

  The rare JAK3R222H variant occurs in the F2 subdomain of the FERM domain. As no experimental structure for human JAK3 has been deposited in the PDB, we modeled both the WT and R222H variant structures using AF3. The closest available homolog is the human JAK2 FERM/SH2 structure (PDB: 6E2Q), which shares 42% sequence identity with JAK3.29 JAK3 primarily mediates signal transduction via the interleukin-2 receptor common gamma chain (IL-2Rγc).30,31 Based on the JAK2 template, we constructed JAK3 models encompassing the FERM and SH2 domains (residues 21–491) in complex with the intracellular domain of IL-2Rγc (residues 284–337) (Figure S4D). Structural alignment of the WT and R222H variant models revealed high similarity, with an RMSD of 0.166 Å. Unlike the JAK1N226S variant, which induced local structural rearrangement, the JAK3R222H variant did not result in significant conformational changes (Figure S4E). These findings suggest that the functional impact of R222H may be minimal when present alone but could be enhanced in the context of cooperative variants, such as JAK2G127D or TYK2R490C.

  The prevalent TYK2V362F variant and the rare TYK2R490C variant occur in the F3 subdomain of the FERM domain and the SH2 domain, respectively.32 To investigate their structural consequences, we utilized the WT structure of TYK2 in complex with an IFN alpha receptor 1 (IFNAR1) peptide (PDB: 4PO6)32 and generated corresponding variant models using AF3. The models span TYK2 residues 23–583 and IFNAR1 residues 477–507, incorporating either the V362F or R490C mutation. For the V362F mutation, the overall structure closely resembled that of the WT, with an RMSD of 0.314 Å (Figure S5A). Notably, residue V362 was unobserved in the crystal structure, likely due to its location within a large, flexible loop specific to JAK1 and TYK2 but absent in JAK2 and JAK3. Indeed, residues 331–371, which include V362, are predicted to be intrinsically disordered according to disorder prediction program such as PUNCH2 (Figure S5B).33 In the AF3 model, this region remains unstructured, and lacks defined secondary structure. Due to the inherent disorder and the absence of experimental data for this loop, even in full-length structures of JAK1 (e.g., PDB: 7T6F, 8EWY),34,35 it is difficult to predict the precise functional consequences of the V362F variant. It is possible that this SNV exerts its effect only in the presence of additional signaling components or under specific cellular contexts.

  The rare TYK2R490C variant, located in the SH2 domain, was modeled using AF3 based on the WT structure of TYK2 complexed with the intracellular domain of IFNAR1 (PDB: 4PO6).32 The overall structure of the R490C variant closely resembled that of the WT, with a root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) of 0.350 Å (Figures S5C and S5D). Unlike the V362F variant, which lies within a disordered loop, R490C is located within a well-structured β-sheet in the SH2 domain (residues L483-E508). This variant induces subtle but notable conformational rearrangements within this β-sheet (Figures 3D and 3E). In the WT structure, a network of hydrogen bonds involving S491, Q492, A493, G496, and S499 stabilizes the β-sheet. In the R490C mutant, these interactions are disrupted: the side chain of S491 no longer interacts with the amide nitrogen of Q492 but instead forms a new hydrogen bond with S499. Q492 loses its interaction with the backbone carbonyl of G496. Meanwhile, S499 shifts from interacting with the amide nitrogen of A493 to forming a new interaction with the backbone carbonyl and side chain of S491. These rearrangements displace the β-sheet by approximately 1–2 Å and may (de)stabilize the β-sheet. Although R490(C) is positioned ∼12 Å away from the receptor peptide, suggesting limited direct impact on peptide recognition (Figure S5D), the β-sheet extends toward the solvent in full-length JAK1 dimeric structures.34,35 This suggests that R490C may affect the recruitment or interaction of additional signaling molecules in a cellular context.

  The TYK2R703W variant occurs in the C-terminal lobe (C-lobe) of the PK domain.36 To assess its structural consequences, we referred to the WT structure of the TYK2 PK domain complexed with an ATP analog (ATPγS) and Mg2+ (PDB: 5C03),36 and built an AF3 model of the R703W variant. The model includes TYK2 residues 557–871, one ATP molecule, and a Mg2+ ion (Figure S5E). The overall structure of the R703W variant was similar to that of the WT, with an RMSD of 0.390 Å. However, notable local conformational changes were observed. In the WT structure, R703 forms stabilizing interactions with the side chain of E812 and the backbone of D810, which likely help maintain the integrity of the C-lobe (Figure 3F). In the R703W variant, these interactions are lost, potentially destabilizing the C-lobe and impairing the regulatory function of the PK domain in modulating TYK2 activity.

  The STAT6V269I variant occurs within the coiled-coil domain.37 To evaluate its structural impact, we used the crystal structure of WT STAT6 phosphorylated at Y641 and bound to double-stranded DNA (dsDNA), which spans the coiled-coil to SH2 domains (PDB: 4Y5W).37 Based on this structure, we generated an AF3-predicted model of the V269I variant spanning residues 113–658 (Figure S5F). The overall structure of the variant closely resembled the WT dimer, with a slight swing in the coiled-coil domain (RMSD: 0.999 Å). The V269(I) residue is located at the interface between the coiled-coil and DNA-binding domains. This site is embedded within a hydrophobic pocket formed by residues L265, L297, F301, F392, L394, I401, and L403 (Figure 3G). The V269I variant, which introduces a bulkier and more hydrophobic isoleucine side chain, induces subtle conformational rearrangements within this pocket. Specifically, residues from the DNA-binding domain—F301, F392, L394, I401, and L403—shift closer to V269I, narrowing the pocket and altering local hydrophobic interactions. These structural changes may enhance the stability of the interface between the coiled-coil and DNA-binding domains, potentially influencing STAT6’s dsDNA-binding affinity and transcriptional activity.


## Discussion
  Vaccination initiates an innate immune response, resulting in the release of proinflammatory cytokines, widespread transcriptional changes in whole blood, and the stimulation of ISG expression within 12–24 h.38,39,40,41 In this study, we show that individuals carrying the very rare JAK1N226S, JAK2G127D, JAK3R222H, and STAT6V269I variants exhibit a significantly heightened innate immune response following ChAdOx1 vaccination, as indicated by the excessive activation of ISGs. While the JAK1N226S variant is very rare without any supporting clinical and experimental literature, the JAK2G127D and JAK3R222H variants have previously been reported.42,43 Notably, in Philadelphia chromosome-negative myeloproliferative neoplasm patients the JAK2G127D variant only co-occurs with the JAK2V617F polycythemia vera mutation,42 suggesting a synergistic effect between these two mutations. Individuals with the JAK1N226S and the JAK2G127D variants also carry TYK2 variants, suggesting the possibility of epigenetic interactions between these two tyrosine kinases. The study supports the hypothesis that the presence of specific JAK/STAT variants, alone or in combination, could have a significant impact on the pathophysiological response to IFNs and other cytokines in general.

  While the JAK1, JAK2, and JAK3 germline variants reported in this study are extremely rare in the gnomAD and All of Us databases, they are found at a higher frequency in the KGP suggesting that they are enriched in the Korean population. However, this finding is based on 7,000 entries in the KGP, while the gnomAD and All of Us databases host several hundred thousand WGS. While ClinVar suggests that these mutations are likely benign; in silico predictions present a complex and somewhat contradictory picture, with some tools suggesting potential pathogenicity while others indicate benign effects. This variability in computational predictions underscores the challenges in relying solely on in silico methods for assessing the functional impact of mutations in complex signaling pathways, highlighting the need for experimental validation.

  Each study subject carrying a specific JAK variant also carries one TYK2 variant, either V362F, R490C or R703W. TYK2V362F is the most common variant found in more than 50% of the Korean cohort. Since V362 is not evolutionarily conserved across species, this variant may be tolerated without severely impacting TYK2 function. However, the TYK2V362F variant has been associated with autoimmune diseases such as systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) and type 1 diabetes (T1D).44 The enhanced innate immune response in individuals carrying the JAK1N226S and JAK2G127D variants and the TYK2V362F variant suggests that the TYK2 variant could modulate IFN signaling. Autoimmune diseases such as SLE and T1D are associated with overactive immune function that can be abrogated by JAK-inhibitors.45 In contrast, arginine at position 490 (R490) and arginine at position 703 (R703) in the TYK2 protein are highly conserved across various species, indicating their critical role in the protein’s function. Variants at these positions could further impact the JAK variants observed in our study cohort. Two individuals carry both the TYK2V362F and TYK2R703W variants suggesting a potential impact of compound heterozygosity as has been shown in individuals with primary immunodeficiency with T cell lymphopenia.46 Functional studies have demonstrated that the TYK2R703W variant leads to a reduction in cytokine signaling, particularly affecting interleukin-12 (IL-12), interleukin-23 (IL-23), and IFN-α pathways.47 While TYK2R703W is a possible hypomorphic allele it’s activity in the presence of JAK2V362F is not clear.

  JAKs and TYK2 can form heterodimers within cytokine-receptor signaling complexes, allowing for trans-phosphorylation and activation due to their proximity. Notably, TYK2 is known to pair with JAK1 in several cytokine signaling pathways, and the formation of TYK2-JAK1 heterodimers is considered essential for normal immune function, with potential therapeutic implications.48 Our study also indicates that the common TYK2V362F variant does not exhibit synergy with the JAK1N226S variant, which appears to be the primary driver of the heightened innate immune response. However, TYK2V362F may interact synergistically with other variants, such as STAT6V269I. Additionally, the co-occurrence of JAK2G127D and TYK2R703W variants leads to an enhanced IFN response signature. Of particular interest is an individual carrying three distinct tyrosine kinase variants, JAK2G127D, JAK3R222H, and TYK2R490C. However, the lack of individuals carrying only single variants makes it challenging to determine the precise contribution of each variant to this activation.

  While biochemical investigations can identify the activities of specific JAK kinases and STAT transcription factors under specific in vitro conditions, the in vivo activities within individuals, especially in the presence of additional potentially cooperating mutations, remains a challenge. However, the association of distinct JAK/STAT variants with an altered innate immune response in human subjects provides an opportunity to investigate their true biological impact. Our findings emphasize the combinatorial nature of JAK/STAT variants in humans, which likely influence both physiological and pathological processes. Moreover, additional variants can be found in other components of the extended JAK/STAT pathway, including IFN receptors and downstream regulators. This underscores the necessity of genome-wide analyses for accurate diagnosis and potential treatment planning. Since genetic variants do not occur in isolation, their effects can only be fully understood within the broader context of additional variants. Deciphering the complexity of these epistatic SNP interactions presents a significant challenge49 in understanding how multiple genetic variants collectively shape physiological and disease-related responses.


### Limitations of the study
  This study was limited to examination of the potential impact of JAK/STAT SNVs on the immune response but did not examine SNVs in other signaling pathways such as JNK, TNF-α, and NF-κB that are also involved in immune response.

### Method details

#### Single nucleotide variant (SNV) detection in RNA-seq data
  Raw data was subjected to quality control (QC) analyses (FastQC tool, version 0.11.9, https://www.bioinformatics.babraham.ac.uk/projects/fastqc/). mRNA-seq read QC was done using Trimmomatic51 (version 0.36) and STAR 2-pass procedure52 (version STAR 2.7.9a) with read alignment (hg19). Duplicates were marked (Picard tools, version 2.9.2)63 after aligned reads were filtered (BWA MEM, version 0.7.15).50 GATK analysis workflow: (i) base recalibration - GATK BaseRecalibrator, AnalyzeCovariates, and PrintReads - using the databases of known polymorphic sites, dbSNP138 (provided by the high-performance computing team of the NIH (Biowulf)); (ii) variant calling - GATK HaplotypeCaller - with the genotyping mode “discovery”, the “ERC” parameter for creating gvcf and a minimum phred-scaled confidence threshold of 30. Hard filters were applied: QD < 2.0 || QUAL <30.0 || SOR >3.0 || FS > 60.0 || MQ < 40.0 || MQRankSum < −12.5 || ReadPosRankSum < −8.0. The resulting SNVs were additionally filtered by removing those overlapping with repetitive elements53 (UCSC’s masked repeats plus simple repeats; https://hgdownload.soe.ucsc.edu/goldenPath/hg19/database/) and black regions (ENCODE54; https://mitra.stanford.edu/kundaje/akundaje/release/blacklists/hg19-human/). On an individual level, only SNVs with a genotype of 0/1 or 1/1 were kept. Further filtering steps comprised the removal of SNVs with a read depth smaller than 10, an excessive read depth64 (d+3√d, d = average read depth), an allele frequency less than 10% using a variety of tools (BEDtools, version 2.26.0; BEDOPS, version 2.4.3; VCFtools, version 0.1.17).55,56,57 All SNVs within +/− 5bp of an indel border were removed as likely false-positives. SNV position on hg19 was then converted to hg38 using Liftover.58


#### Allele frequency information and in silico pathogenicity prediction
  Allele frequency information for certain mutation was collected using multiple databases, like dbSNP (N = 14,710 in Allele Frequency Aggregator (12 populations, including European, Hispanic, African, Asian, and other diverse population ancestries), N = 7,234 in Korean Genome Project 4K; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/snp/), gnomAD (N = approximately 140,000; https://gnomad.broadinstitute.org/), All of Us (N = more than 849,000; https://databrowser.researchallofus.org/snvsindels), COSMIC (https://cancer.sanger.ac.uk/cosmic) and ClinVar (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/clinvar/).

  Multiple computational tools were employed to assess the potential pathogenicity of JAK/STAT variants. AlphaMissense23 (https://alphamissense.hegelab.org/search) was used to predict the functional impact of mutations, with scores ranging from 0 (benign) to 1 (pathogenic). The Rare Exome Variant Ensemble Learner (REVEL)25 scores, which range from 0 to 1, were calculated to assess the probability of pathogenicity. Additionally, PolyPhen-224 (http://genetics.bwh.harvard.edu/pph2/bgi.shtml) analysis was performed to predict the possible impact of amino acid substitutions on protein structure and function, with scores ranging from 0 (benign) to 1 (probably damaging).


#### Protein structure prediction and analysis
  Wild-type protein sequences of human JAK and STAT family members were retrieved from the UniProt database.65 Residue ranges used for structure prediction are listed in the STAR Methods. Structural models for wild type and mutant proteins were generated using AlphaFold3 with default settings.26,27 The model with the highest confidence score was selected for analysis. Notably, all mutated residues except TYK2 V362F had local pLDDT values greater than 50 and were therefore included in the analysis. All structures were aligned and visualized using PyMOL (version 3.1; Schrödinger, LLC, New York, NY, USA).


### Quantification and statistical analysis

#### Allele frequencies
  Statistically significant differences between allele frequencies in different populations were assessed using Fisher’s exact test (Chi-square test). Values of p < 0.00001, p = 4.77 × 10−8, p = 6.80 × 10−25, p = 2.31 × 10−16 were considered statistically significant. Additional statistical details of the experiments can be found in the results section.


#### mRNA sequencing (mRNA-seq) data analysis
  Raw data were subjected to QC analyses (FastQC tool, version 0.11.9) and mRNA-seq read QC performed (Trimmomatic, version 0.36).51 STAR RNA-seq52 (version STAR 2.7.9a), using 150 bp paired-end mode, aligned reads (hg19). Raw counts were retrieved using HTSeq59 (version 0.9.1) and the Bioconductor package DESeq260 in R (https://www.R-project.org/) was employed for normalization of counts across samples66 and to conduct differential expression gene analysis. To remove confounding factors, the RUVSeq61 package was applied. Data were pre-filtered keeping only genes with at least ten reads in total. Visualization was accomplished using dplyr (https://CRAN.R-project.org/package=dplyr) and ggplot2.62 P-values were calculated using a paired, two-side Wilcoxon test and adjusted p-value (pAdj) corrected using the Benjamini–Hochberg method. The cut-off value for the false discovery rate was pAdj >0.05. Genes with log2 fold change >1 or < -1, pAdj <0.05 and without 0 value from all sample were considered significant. Genes that were significantly differentially expressed were used for gene enrichment analysis (GSEA, https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/msigdb). The Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistic was used to evaluate the significance of each GSEA category. Values of ∗p < 0.05, ∗∗p < 0.01, ∗∗∗p < 0.001, ∗∗∗∗p < 0.0001 were considered statistically significant. Additional statistical details of the experiments can be found in the results section.
