BACKGROUND: Myocardial infarction (MI) induces significant myocardial damage through oxidative stress and inflammation, limiting the survival and effects of transplanted bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs). Stem cell-based therapies hold promise for cardiac repair; however, enhancing BMSC survival under adverse conditions remains a critical challenge. Strategies to mitigate oxidative stress and apoptosis in transplanted BMSCs can significantly improve therapeutic outcomes. PURPOSE: This study investigated, for the first time, the protective effects of Ononin, a natural isoflavone, on BMSC survival and function under oxidative stress conditions, both in vitro and in a murine MI model, with a specific focus on the novel mechanism of action via the SIRT6/Nrf2 signaling axis. METHODS: The effects of Ononin on BMSCs were assessed under oxidative stress conditions induced by tert‑butyl hydroperoxide (TBHP). Cellular oxidative stress and apoptosis were evaluated by flow cytometry, fluorescence staining, and Western blotting assay. Additionally, antioxidant enzyme expression and pathway activation were analyzed using qPCR, immunofluorescence, and Western blotting assay. Moreover, molecular docking, Drug Affinity Responsive Target Stability Assay (DARTS), cellular thermal shift assay (CETSA), and surface plasmon resonance (SPR) verified the direct interaction of Ononin with SIRT6. In vivo, Ononin-pretreated BMSCs were transplanted into the mouse MI model, and their survival, along with therapeutic outcomes such as myocardial inflammation, fibrosis, apoptosis, angiogenesis, and cardiac function, was assessed using bioluminescence imaging. Additionally, siRNA-mediated knockdown of SIRT6 was employed for investigating SIRT6/Nrf2 pathway in Ononin's protective effects. RESULTS: In vitro, Ononin pretreatment mitigated oxidative stress and apoptosis in TBHP-treated BMSCs, and up-regulated antioxidant enzyme levels by activating SIRT6/Nrf2 signaling pathway. Molecular docking, DARTS, CETSA, and SPR analyses confirmed the direct interaction of Ononin with SIRT6. In vivo, bioluminescence imaging revealed that Ononin-pretreated BMSCs exhibited enhanced survival after they were transplanted into the MI mouse model. According to the therapeutic outcomes, myocardial inflammation, fibrosis, and apoptosis were alleviated, and angiogenesis and cardiac function were improved. Importantly, knockdown of SIRT6 partially reversed these effects, underscoring the novel effect of SIRT6/Nrf2 pathway on regulating Ononin's protective actions. CONCLUSION: This study demonstrates that Ononin enhances the survival of transplanted BMSCs and improves myocardial repair by targeting the SIRT6/Nrf2 signaling pathway. These findings provide a promising pharmacological strategy to optimize stem cell-based therapies for MI.
