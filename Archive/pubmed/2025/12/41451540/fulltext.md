# CELLetter: leveraging large language model and dual-stream network to identify context-specific ligand–receptor interactions for cell–cell communication analysis


## Abstract
  Cell-to-cell communication (CCC) facilitates the coordination of various cellular behaviors in multicellular organisms. Many computational methods neglect downstream intracellular signaling and are limited by static and predefined ligand–receptor (L-R) databases. To address these limitations, we present CELLetter, a deep learning framework to identify potential L-R interactions through a novel feature learning model and decipher cellular signaling by integrating L-R co-expression with downstream transcription factor (TF) activity inferred from gene regulatory network. CELLetter begins by leveraging the protein large language model, ProstT5, for feature embedding. It then employs a dual-stream architecture for feature extraction and dimensionality reduction, a gate mechanism with dynamic weight adjustment for feature fusion, absolute difference, and element-wise product for feature interaction. After that, CELLetter combines interacting L-R pairs, single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data, and downstream TF activity to quantify communication strength. We comprehensively evaluated CELLetter using 11 evaluation metrics, benchmarking it against 4 state-of-the-art L-R classification models, 6 L-R validation tools, 10 CCC inference methods. CELLetter demonstrated superior L-R classification performance. Notably, we introduced a novel multi-faceted validation strategy employing colocalization distance, co-expression ratio, and co-detection probability on spatial transcriptomics data from human heart and distal lung epithelial tissues. CELLetter’s predicted L-R pairs exhibited significant spatial relevance compared with other baselines. When applied to human head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) data, CELLetter produced CCC inferences broadly consistent with established methods. More importantly, ligand macrophage migration inhibitory factor (MIF) and receptor CD44 were predicted as a central signaling axis within HNSCC tumor microenvironment, suggesting their potentials as therapeutic targets . CELLetter is freely available at https://github.com/plhhnu/CELLetter.


## Introduction
  In multicellular organisms, diverse cellular activities are orchestrated through cell-to-cell communication (CCC). CCC is central to interpret complex dynamic interactions and regulatory mechanisms within biological systems [1–5]. Consequently, deciphering CCC has become a crucial task in cell biology and has been broadly applied in disease diagnosis and therapeutic options. CCC involves diverse signaling pathways, many of which are usually facilitated by physical interplay between secreted ligands and plasma membrane receptors [6–9]. This process generally comprises two key stages: ligand–receptor (L-R) interacting scoring and L-R interaction-medicated communication strengthen quantification [4, 10]. With the rapid advancement of single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) technologies [11], the inference of cellular crosstalk has been increasingly promoted by leveraging co-expression patterns of signaling molecules across cell types, such as ligands, receptors, and downstream transcription factors (TFs) [12–14].

  To date, a diverse array of computational tools have been developed to decipher L-R-mediated CCC. These methods can be broadly categorized into four groups based on their core algorithmic strategies: expression-based, difference-assembly-based, network-based, and tensor-based methods [6]. Expression-based methods (e.g. SingleCellSignalR [15], CellPhoneDB [16], CellChat [17], and Cellinker [18]) calculate an interaction scores for each L-R pair and assess the statistical significance of these interactions by comparing observed scores against null distributions generated through statistical methods, such as permutation of cluster labels, non-parametric testing, or empirical modeling. Difference-assembly-based methods (e.g. iTalk [19] and CellTalker [20]) first identify significantly differentially expressed genes between cell populations and then construct communication networks. Network-based methods (e.g. NicheNet [21], CytoTalk [22], and Connectome [23]) integrate prior knowledge such as transcriptomic regulatory networks and protein–protein interactions, and infer context-specific communication patterns and downstream transcriptomic responses in the receiving cells. Tensor-based methods (e.g. scTensor [24] and Tensor-cell2cell [25]) employ higher-order data representations to simultaneously capture L-R interactions under multiple conditions, apply tensor decomposition to dissect triad communication, and identify cell-type-specific signaling axes.

  Despite their widespread application, current methods primarily focus on known, experimentally validated L-R pair databases, such as CellPhoneDB [16] and ICELLNET [26]. However, these databases are inherently incomplete. They are often curated from bulk tissue data or specific cell lines, failing to fully capture the cell-type-specific interactome within the single-cell context [10]. This limitation renders their predictive outcomes highly dependent on the quality and coverage of prior knowledge, potentially leading to missing communications involving non-canonical or novel L-R pairs [6].

  Recent advances in deep learning have greatly promoted the progress of the bioinformatics field [27–30]. Moreover, they have still stimulated to better model cellular crosstalk. Several frameworks have leveraged distinct architectural advantages. For instance, CellMsg [31] integrated L-R multimodal features and used graph convolutional network to uncover high-confidence interactions. Similarly, scDCA [32] leveraged multi-view graph convolutional networks and attention mechanisms to reconstruct intercellular signaling environments. In parallel, SpaCCC [33] employed pretrained protein language models for L-R feature embedding and enhanced communication analysis by integrating with spatial transcriptomics (ST) data. Furthermore, multiple computational frameworks proposed by Peng et al., such as CellEnBoost [34], CellComNet [9], CellDialog [35], CellGiQ [36], CellGDnG [37], and CellCDmT [38], have showcased the wide applicability of machine learning. These methods not only predict potential L-R pairs but also quantify communication through scoring strategies. Despite these promising developments, they remain a challenge in improving predictive accuracy of novel L-R interactions.

  CCC involves not only intercellular L-R binding events but also intracellular signal transduction and amplification through downstream pathways [21, 39]. Most existing methods, however, struggle to capture this critical intracellular signaling cascade, thus providing an incomplete picture of the communication process. Furthermore, the predictive accuracy of existing L-R classification models is often limited by static and predefined databases. To address these limitations, i.e. improving L-R interaction prediction accuracy, expanding the coverage of potential L-R pairs, and fully utilizing the relationships between intercellular signaling and intracellular downstream effects, to model the flow of information from the membrane to the nucleus, we introduce a novel deep learning framework, CELLetter. CELLetter begins with L-R interaction inference by incorporating the protein large language model ProstT5 for feature embedding, a dual-stream architecture for feature extraction and dimensionality reduction, a gate mechanism with dynamic weight adjustment for feature fusion, absolute difference and element-wise product operations for feature interaction. After that, CELLetter combines interacting L-R pairs, scRNA-seq data, and downstream TF activity to bridge the relationships between intercellular signaling and intracellular transcriptomic responses. ## Overview of CELLetter
  Intercellular signaling pathway prediction greatly relies on L-R co-expressions, where one gene comes from one cell and the other comes from another interacting cell. By centering on this co-expression pattern, this work introduces CELLetter, a CCC analysis model by leveraging interacting L-R prediction, and cellular crosstalk inference. As shown in Fig. 1, interacting L-R prediction mainly includes: feature embedding, extraction, fusion, interaction, and classification. Cellular crosstalk inference includes: interacting L-R filtering, TF activity calculation through pySCENIC [40], communication score, and visualization. Finally, CELLetter was applied to cellular communication analysis within the tumor microenvironment (TME) in head and neck squamous cell carcinomas (HNSCC).


### Interacting ligand–receptor prediction
  To elucidate potential L-R interactions, as illustrated in Fig. 2, first, a protein large language model, ProstT5 [41], is employed to learn sequence-based global features and residue-based local features for each L-R pair. Next, these two distinct types of features are extracted and dimensionality-reduced through a dual-stream architecture in parallel: the global features are refined via an MLP with residual connections, and the local features are processed by an MoE module [42]. Subsequently, the outputs from both streams are fused using a gating mechanism that dynamically weights the importance of global and local features, generating a context-aware representation for each L-R pair. After that, the L-R representations are transformed to interaction-aware features based on absolute difference and element-wise product. Finally, the obtained context-aware and interaction-aware features are further concatenated as inputs of an MLP classifier to compute L-R interaction probability.

### Cell-to-cell communication analysis
  Cellular crosstalk is typically mediated through specific interacting L-R pairs. Thus, comprehensive signaling pathway analysis involves two critical components: interacting L-R pairs and scRNA-seq data. As shown in Fig. 3, the workflow consists of four main components: (1) L-R Filtering. The experimentally validated and predicted interacting L-R pairs are merged and filtered using scRNA-seq data. (2) TF activity computation. TF activities are inferred using the pySCENIC toolkit [40], which combines co-expression network analysis with motif enrichment to estimate intracellular signaling potentials. (3) CCC scoring. Cellular communication strength is assessed by integrating L-R expression with global TF activity. (4) CCC visualization: the inferred CCC is visualized to highlight key communication pathways using heatmap, bubble plot, stacked bar chart, and circos plot.


#### Ligand–receptor filtration
  Cellular signaling is mediated by the co-expression of L-R pairs. Thus, we need to remove genes which are not expressed in a TME. First, the predicted interacting L-R pairs are filtered based on a stringent probability threshold of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{upgreek} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document} $\beta =99.99\%$\end{document}. This exceptionally high-confidence threshold is selected to prioritize prediction quality, ensuring that only the most reliable potential interactions are adopted to subsequent CCC analysis. Moreover, it can minimize the error, i.e. caused by false positives during downstream analysis as much as possible, thereby preserving the robustness and biological credibility of the inferred communication networks. Although this conservative approach may exclude some true interactions with moderate probabilities, it ensures a high level of confidence in the predicted L-R pairs used for further biological interpretation.

  Next, the filtered and known L-R pairs are merged. To identify whether L-R pairs are active within the specific cellular context in a TME, the merged L-R pairs are further filtered based on a minimal expression threshold (i.e. 0.1 log-normalized counts). Through the setting, the potentials for signaling events are mediated by low-expressed but biologically important genes, thereby maximizing the sensitivity of our predictions. An L-R pair is considered functionally plausible for communication between a sender and receiver only if both genes are expressed above this threshold in their respective cell populations. As a result, the filtered L-R pairs are applied to subsequent CCC analysis.

However, incorporating pathway-level information requires extensive manual curation and complex algorithm development, which are laborious and limited to the quality of prior knowledge [10]. ## Experiments

### Data

#### Ligand–receptor datasets
  In this study, we utilized four L-R datasets compiled by Peng et al. [34] for model training and evaluation. Datasets 1 (human) and 2 (mouse) were extracted from CellTalkDB [48], a comprehensive L-R repository established through manual curation of literature-supported interactions. Dataset 3 (mouse) was assembled by Skelly et al. [49] based on interactions released by the FANTOM5 consortium [13]. Dataset 4 (human) was collected by Ximerakis et al. [50] (http://baderlab.org/CellCellInteractions) by integrating interactions from BioGRID [51], iRefIndex [52], and Pathway Commons [53]. These four datasets provide an abundant yet reliable collection of interacting L-R pairs, enabling the proposed CELLetter model to minimize both false positives and false negatives to the greatest extent. After removing redundant entries and interactions lacking known amino-acid sequence information, the preprocessed data are summarized in Table 1.


#### Single-cell RNA sequencing data
  We took HNSCC as an example and analyzed cellular signaling within the TME. The scRNA-seq data [54] are derived from corresponding single-cell suspension and can be downloaded from the GEO database [55] with GEO access number: GSE103322. It involves to nine cell populations: HNSCC cancer cells, fibroblasts, B cells, myocytes, macrophages, endothelial cells, T cells, dendritic cells, and mast cells.


#### Spatial transcriptome data
  To further assess the biological credibility of the predicted interacting L-R pairs, we employed two human ST datasets from STOmicsDB [56], i.e. heart (Dataset ID: STDS0000025) and distal lung epithelial [57] (Dataset ID: STDS0000114). Human heart ST dataset contains fresh frozen heart tissue from BioIVT Asterand. It contains 4247 spatial spots and expression values of 36 601 genes. Human distal lung epithelial ST dataset involves 2220 spatial spots and expression values of 36 601 genes.


### Baselines

#### Ligand–receptor classification
  To evaluate the robustness of CELLetter when predicting new interacting L-R pairs, we compared it with the following four baselines:

  CellDialog [35] is a tree-boosting-based classification method, CellGDnG [37] and CellCDmT [38] are two models that used heterogeneous ensemble deep learning strategies for interaction prediction, and CellMsg [31] is a graph convolutional network-based framework that integrated multimodal L-R features to identify high-confidence interactions.


#### Ligand–receptor validation
  To estimate the biological relevance of interacting L-R pairs identified by CELLetter, we counted their overlap with the canonical interactions curated by six widely recognized databases. This validation strategy leveraged known biological knowledge as a ground truth for benchmarking.


#### Cell-to-cell communication inference
  To comprehensively validate the credibility of CCC predictions from scRNA-seq data, we compared CELLetter with 10 representative inference tools involved in a wide spectrum of computational methodologies when scoring communication strength.

### Evaluation metrics

Among these, AUC and AUPR served as two primary evaluation measures. Higher values across all metrics correspond to better predictive performance.

  We conducted 20 independent experiment of five-fold cross validation. In each experiment, the entire set of L-R pairs was first randomly partitioned into five non-overlapping folds with approximately equal size. In each round, one-fold was randomly held out as the test set, and the remaining four-folds were used as the training set. During the above partitions, the training and test sets were strictly separated at the pair level to prevent any data leakage. Additionally, negative L-R pairs (or non-interacting pairs) were selected by randomly sampling an equal number of pairs as interacting ones, thereby producing a balanced dataset composed of positive and negative samples with a ratio of 1:1.

### Performance comparison of CELLetter and four ligand–receptor baselines
  Taking CellDialog, CellGDnG, CellCDmT, and CellMsg as benchmarks, we conducted a comprehensive comparison experiment to investigate the performance of CELLetter with the four L-R baselines. And the maximal F1-score confirms an optimal trade-off between precision and recall under class imbalance. Their combined performance demonstrated that CELLetter provided more reliable and complete L-R prediction than the four baseline methods, elucidating that CELLetter could be highly effective in reducing the loss caused by data imbalance and false positives.

CELLetter consistently approximated to these regions, significantly surpassing the four baselines (i.e. CellDialog, CellGDnG, CellCDmT, and CellMsg), thereby further validating its superiority in interaction prediction.


### Ablation analysis
  To evaluate the contribution of each key component in CELLetter, we conducted multiple ablation experiments by removing the following modules:

  Table 3 summarizes ablation analysis results. As shown in Table 3, the performance of CELLetter decreased when removing the MoE module, the gate mechanism, or the absolute difference and product operations. The full CELLetter model generated the highest accuracy, precision, F1-score, AUC and AUPR, as well as comparable recall, demonstrating that each component was indispensable for promoting the model performance, and the best performance is highlighted in bold.

  Replacing the MoE module with four parallel CNN branches led to a noticeable decrease in precision (decreased by 0.2%–8.0%), accuracy (decreased by 0.6%–2.8%), F1-score (decreased by 0.2%–2.1%), AUC (decreased by 0.3%–1.2%), and AUPR (decreased by 0.4%–2.8%). This confirmed that the multi-expert architecture facilitated capture diverse residue-level patterns of ligands and receptors.

  Removing the gating mechanism and simply concatenating global and local features caused the most significant performance drop: precision decreased by 0.6%–4.5%, accuracy by 0.3%–3.5%, F1-score by 0.6%–3.5%, AUC by 0.3%–3.2%, and AUPR by 0.6%–3.6% compared to the full model. The gating mechanism played a vital role in adaptively combining global and local information, preventing the global features from overwhelming subtle but crucial residue-level features.

  Removing the element-wise difference operation led to a reduction in precision (decreased by 0.2%–2.9%), accuracy (decreased by 1.4%–3.1%), F1-score (decreased by 1.4%–5.1%), AUC (decreased by 1.6%–2.0%), and AUPR (decreased by 1.4%–1.9%). The difference operation explicitly encoded L-R features, and removing the operation made the model less capable of capturing subtle changes in interface features.

  Removing the element-wise product operation caused a decrease in precision (decreased by 4.0%–6.1%), accuracy (decreased by 1.3%–2.2%), F1-score (decreased by 0.9%–1.8%), AUC (decreased by 0.5%–1.0%), and AUPR (decreased by 0.2%–1.2%). The product operation assisted in modeling co-occurrence patterns. Lack of the operation made the model struggle to represent interactive features effectively.

  Removing the element-wise difference and product operations triggered a reduction in precision (decreased by 4.3%–7.9%), accuracy (decreased by 1.1%–4.0%), F1-score (decreased by 0.6%–3.5%), AUC (decreased by 0.9%–2.1%), and AUPR (decreased by 0.9%–1.9%). Both of them explicitly encoded L-R features and co-occurrence patterns. Lack of them made the model unable to learn interaction features implicitly, thereby resulting in less precise decision boundaries.

  To more clearly demonstrate the advantages of CELLetter, Fig. 7 shows its bar charts with error bars and ablation analysis results. CELLetter achieved the highest AUC and AUPR values on each dataset, with relatively smaller errors.

  Although CELLetter without Prod and without Diff&Prod achieved relatively high recall, which can be caused by more low-confidence positive samples, its performance especially precision significantly reduced. In summary, the full CELLetter model consistently demonstrated superior performance, thereby confirming powerful discriminative ability of its each module: the MoE module enhanced feature diversity through multiple experts, the gating mechanism dynamically integrated global and local L-R features, and the Diff&Prod operations explicitly encoded interaction-specific features. Their combination ultimately generated the most robust result in L-R classification.

Moreover, the vast majority of tools including CELLetter found the same top 3 cell types communicating with HNSCC cancer cells: macrophages, fibroblasts, and endothelial cells.

  Bioinformatics analysis have demonstrated that tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) are main tumor-infiltrating immune cells within HNSCC TME. The infiltration levels of TAMs in HNSCC tumor tissue are obviously higher than those in adjacent HNSCC non-tumor tissue [68, 69]. In HNSCC tissue, they usually exhibit M2 macrophage features, which assist in building an immune-suppressive TME, thus encouraging tumor escape and growth [70, 71]. Targeting them through new therapeutic strategies offers more options to promote the survival and treatment of HNSCC patients [69]. Cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) play crucial roles in tumor formation within TME. They can promote tumor development in various ways [72–75]. Known as CAFs, a persistently activated fibroblast population captured within or proximate to the tumor mass, have been reported to exert a substantial modifying effect in HNSCC and significantly contribute to its drug resistance [75, 76]. EGF, which is secreted by endothelial cells, can induce motility and prevent HNSCC cells from anoikis [77]. Endothelins, also secreted by endothelial cells, drive HNSCC anoikis resistance by facilitating IL-6 and CCLX8 expression and thus activating STAT3, PI3K/AKT/ERK pathways [78, 79].

  These findings demonstrated the key roles of macrophages, fibroblasts, and endothelial cells in regulating HNSCC TME and promoting HNSCC patient prognosis. They deepened our understanding about the progression, metastasis, and immune evasion of HNSCC. Moreover, they offered potential therapeutic targets for HNSCC patients.


#### Top 3 ligand–receptor pair analysis
  To further decipher the molecular mechanisms underlying HNSCC cellular crosstalk, we analyzed the top 3 L-R pairs mediating signaling between HNSCC cancer cells and major stromal and immune cells. Table 6 summarizes the most frequent outgoing (sent by cancer cells) and incoming (received by cancer cells) L-R pairs of each cell population, highlighting dominant communication axes in the HNSCC TME.

  Figure 11 visualizes how these top pairs mediate HNSCC cellular conversation. The combination of heatmap and histogram view quantifies the communication strength between HNSCC cancer cells and other eight cell clusters mediated by the three highest-scoring L-R pairs. The adjacent bar chart in Fig. 11A ranks the cumulative L-R scores. Figure 11B and C, rendered with CCplotR [87], depicts cellular signaling between HNSCC cells and the top 5 cell types mediated by the top 3 L-R pairs through network diagram and circos plot. The results characterized important roles of these ligands and receptors in HNSCC cellular communication.

  Moreover, to evaluate whether the predicted L-R pairs mediated HNSCC CCC, we conducted molecular docking experiments on the top 20 predicted interactions. Specifically, the initial 3D structures for these ligands and receptors were obtained from ZDOCK [88]. Next, their molecular docking simulations were conducted through PDBePISA [89] with default settings. Finally, the three key parameters of each L-R pair were counted: binding energy (BE, kcal/mol), interface area (IA, Å\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{upgreek} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document} $^{2}$\end{document} ), and hydrogen bond (HB, Å). L-R pairs with a binding energy below −5 kcal/mol were taken as potential interactions. Table 7 summarizes their docking results.

  As shown in Table 7, the binding energies of all predicted 20 interactions were below −6 kcal/mol. Moreover, they had significant binding areas and shorter hydrogen bonds, indicating that CELLetter can capture genuine interaction signals beyond co-expression information.

  Furthermore, degree centrality-based network centrality analysis identified ligand MIF and receptor CD44 as the top-ranked hubs in the HNSCC CCC network (Fig. 11). Specifically, MIF exhibited the highest outgoing degree, which was defined as the number of distinct receiver cell types to which MIF send signaling, overwhelmingly dominating outgoing signaling from HNSCC cancer cells to B cells, macrophages, endothelial cells, T cells, and dendritic cells. Additionally, CD44 exhibited the highest incoming degree, defined by the number of distinct sender cell types from which CD44 received signals, mediating incoming signaling from fibroblasts, myocytes, endothelial cells, and mast cells to HNSCC cancer cells. Moreover, MIF interacts with multiple receptors (i.e. CD74, CXCR4, and CD44), and CD44 binds with diverse ligands (i.e. VIM, CALM2, LGALS1, and MIF). These outcomes quantitatively demonstrated that MIF and CD44 may significantly affect HNSCC TME.

  The MIF-CD74 interaction has a key role in driving HNSCC tumorigenesis and metastasis, which has been strongly corroborated [82, 90]. Additionally, the MIF–CXCR4 interaction can drive the generation and recruitment of immunosuppressive Th17 cells through the mTOR pathway in nasopharyngeal carcinoma [83–85]. Moreover, MIF also signals through CD44, which is a pathway involved in the inhibition of CD8 + T cell infiltration and the enhancement of immune escape [86].

  The receptor CD44 functions as a central signaling axis within HNSCC TME [80, 81, 91]. The VIM–CD44 interaction, found in fibroblasts and endothelial cells, significantly affects the hypopharyngeal carcinoma TME [80]. The LGALS1-CD44 interaction promotes the infiltration of pro-tumor macrophages and myeloid dendritic cells and decreases B cells through high expression of ligand LGALS1. Thus, it is recognized as an indicator of HNSCC poor prognosis [81]. The CALM2–CD44 interaction highlights a potential link between calcium signaling and adhesion due to deficient calcium signaling of HNSCC T cells caused by lower expression of calmodulin (CaM, encoded by CALM genes), suggesting the pair could be a potential dysregulated signaling pathway [91].

  In conclusion, MIF participate in intercellular signaling, and CD44 mediates intracellular crosstalk. Both of them regulate critical tumorigenic processes including immune evasion, stemness, and metastasis, and play a pillar role in HNSCC cellular communication. The outcomes have been validated by clinical evidence [80, 81, 90]. Consequently, they may be potential therapeutic targets for disrupting the pro-tumorigenic communication that underlies HNSCC pathogenesis.


## Discussion
  Cellular crosstalk via secreting and receiving ligands involves numerous biological processes and widely exists in multicellular organisms. Deciphering these communication assists in offering more clues for tumor targeted therapy and drug design. Standard computational tools for inferring L-R-mediated CCC delineate cellular clusters and their lineage relationships mainly by leveraging an L-R database and scRNA-seq data. One common strategy assumes that highly co-expressed ligands and receptors are more likely to mediate cellular signaling, and then incorporates ligands and receptors for subsequent communication analysis. Another strategy utilizes the downstream targets triggered by L-R pairs in receivers to construct an L-R–target signaling network.

  In this study, we presented CELLetter, a novel deep learning framework, to identify potential linkages between ligands and receptors and decode L-R-mediated cellular crosstalk by fully employing L-R and TF information. For finding new interacting L-R pairs, CELLetter first used a protein large language model, ProstT5 [41], to obtain L-R sequence-based and residue-based representations. Next, a dual-stream architecture composed of MLP with residual connections and the CNN-MoE module is constructed to obtain representative and informative L-R features. Then, a gating mechanism with dynamical weights is designed to fuse the dimensionality-reduced features and further generate context-aware L-R representations. After that, two operations, absolute difference and element-wise product, were employed to learn the fused L-R representations and obtain interaction-aware features. Finally, the context-aware and interaction-aware representations were concatenated as inputs of MLP to perform L-R classification.

  During cellular crosstalk inference, CELLetter combined interacting L-R pairs, scRNA-seq data and downstream TF activity to precisely score communication strength. First, the identified interactions were filtered based on a probability threshold \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{upgreek} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document} $\beta $\end{document} = 99.99%. Next, the filtered interactions were integrated with experimentally validated L-R pairs. After that, the merged pairs were further filtered through a minimal expression threshold based on scRNA-seq data. Finally, cellular signaling was inferred by combining the geometric mean of L-R gene expression and global TF activity.

  To validate the performance of CELLetter, we carried out comprehensive and extensive comparative experiments from different aspects:

  CELLetter efficiently deciphered potential linkages between ligands and receptors and precisely evaluated signaling between cell populations. It may be attributed to three main advantages: (i) It employed a protein large language model and obtained abundant feature embeddings for L-R pairs. (ii) It effectively aggregated L-R hierarchical and interactive features based on a feature learning framework. The framework integrated a dual-stream architecture with the CNN-MoE module and an MLP with residual connections for feature extraction and dimensionality reduction, a gated mechanism with dynamic weights for feature fusion, and absolute difference and product operation for feature interaction, thereby enhancing its discriminative ability to classify L-R pairs. (iii) Differed with traditional co-expression methods, it integrated TF activities into CCC scores, thus accurately deciphering downstream cellular signaling events and providing a more comprehensive assessment of cellular communication potentials.

  Despite its good outcomes, CELLetter did not incorporate spatial coordinates or explicitly model spatial variability during cellular communication. Recent methodological advances, such as SPIDER [92], demonstrate promising progresses for identifying spatially variable interactions with functional support from downstream spatially variable TFs. In the future, we plan to leverage multi-omics data [93, 94] including downstream spatially variable transcriptomic gene to enable spatially resolved communication inference. Additionally, 3D structural information obtained by powerful AI analysis tools like AlphaFold [95] could further enhance L-R interaction prediction accuracy. Thus, we will fully utilize various AI techniques and multi-modal features [96] for L-R classification.


## Conclusion
  In this study, we proposed a novel deep learning framework named CELLetter for L-R interaction prediction and cellular signaling inference. CELLetter effectively identified potential interactions by incorporating protein large language model for embedding learning, dual-stream architecture for feature extraction and dimensionality reduction, gate mechanism with dynamic weights for feature fusion, and absolute difference and product operations for feature interaction. Furthermore, it precisely inferred cellular communication by leveraging experimentally validated and predicted interactions, L-R expression and downstream TF activity. As a freely available tool, CELLetter facilitates deeply understand about cellular signaling in TME and provides valuable insights for targeted therapy and drug design in cancer research.
