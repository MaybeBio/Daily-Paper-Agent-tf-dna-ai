# Deciphering N7-methylguanosine-driven immune dysregulation in unexplained recurrent spontaneous abortion based on transcriptome data and experimental validation


## Background
  Unexplained recurrent spontaneous abortion (URSA) poses significant clinical challenges, with immune dysregulation at the maternal–fetal interface implicated in 80% of cases. While RNA modifications like N7-methylguanosine (m7G) are emerging as key regulators of immune pathologies, their role in URSA remains unexplored.


## Methods
  Decidual transcriptomes (GSE165004) from URSA patients and controls were analyzed to identify differentially expressed m7G regulators. Machine learning (LASSO/random forest) prioritized diagnostic biomarkers, which were validated via qRT-PCR and immunofluorescence. Differences in the immune infiltration landscape between the two groups were assessed by ssGSEA. Furthermore, Spearman correlation analysis was performed to explore associations between m7G-related biomarkers and immune infiltration characteristics. Regulatory networks (transcription factors/microRNAs) and therapeutic candidates of biomarkers were predicted using JASPAR, mirTarbase, and Coremine medical, respectively.


## Results
  Three key m7G regulators with diagnostic value were identified: LSM1, LARP1, and NCBP2. The nomogram constructed based on these biomarkers demonstrated excellent predictive performance in URSA. qRT-PCR and immunofluorescence confirmed that LSM1 expression was upregulated in URSA samples, while LARP1 and NCBP2 were downregulated. Furthermore, distinct patterns of immune infiltration were observed between URSA and control. Spearman analysis revealed that LSM1 was negatively correlated with Treg infiltration, whereas LARP1 and NCBP2 showed positive correlations with Treg. Besides, network analysis identified regulatory relationships between these biomarkers and 167 miRNAs (e.g., hsa-miR-27a-3p) or 18 TFs (e.g., E2F1, GATA2). Sirolimus (targeting NCBP2) and AZD4547 (targeting LSM1) were predicted to be the most promising therapeutic drugs.


## Conclusions
  This study establishes m7G methylation as a novel epigenetic driver of immune dysregulation in URSA. The predictive signature offers translational tool for risk stratification and targeted therapy, bridging RNA epigenetics with reproductive immunology.


## Introduction
  Recurrent spontaneous abortion (RSA) is usually defined as three or more spontaneous abortions before 20–28 weeks of gestation [1]. RSA affects approximately 2–5% of women of childbearing age trying to conceive, imposing significant physical, emotional, and socioeconomic burdens [2, 3]. While recognized etiologies include maternal immunological dysregulation, thrombophilic disorders, anatomical abnormalities, and endocrine imbalances, nearly 50% of RSA cases remain idiopathic, namely unexplained recurrent spontaneous abortion (URSA) [4, 5]. Notably, immune-related mechanisms are implicated in approximately 80% of URSA cases, underscoring the critical role of decidual immune microenvironment dysregulation in pregnancy maintenance [6, 7]. For instance, a pivotal study demonstrated that URSA patients exhibit significantly reduced decidual regulatory T (Treg) cells and elevated pro-inflammatory cytokine levels (e.g., TNF-α, IFN-γ), directly linking immune imbalance to pregnancy loss [8]. Despite advances in understanding immune tolerance at the maternal–fetal interface, the molecular drivers of aberrant immune responses in URSA remain poorly characterized. Thus, investigating the immune microenvironment alterations and key regulators in the context of URSA may provide new directions for its accurate diagnosis and precision therapy.

  RNA methylation, including 5‐methylcytidine (m5C), N6‐methyladenosine (m6A), and N7‐methylguanosine (m7G), is the most well‐characterized RNA modification pattern, which contributes to the regulation of gene expression by epigenetic alterations without affecting RNA sequence [9–13]. Among these modifications, m7G is one of the most common types. It is frequently observed at 5′ caps of tRNA, rRNA, and eukaryotic mRNA [14]. m7G plays a crucial role in maintaining RNA stability, translation efficiency, and function, thereby participating extensively in various biological processes [15]. Its dysfunction has been implicated in several human diseases [16, 17]. Currently, m7G is attracting increasing attention. Recent studies highlight its involvement in immune cell differentiation, activation, and inflammatory responses, positioning m7G as a potential regulator of immune-related pathologies, such as autoimmune diseases and cancer [15, 18]. For instance, dysregulated m7G modification has been linked to altered macrophage polarization and T-cell dysfunction, processes that are central to immune homeostasis [19, 20]. However, the role of m7G in shaping the decidual immune microenvironment in the URSA remains unexplored, representing a critical knowledge gap.

  Advancements in high-throughput sequencing and bioinformatics have enabled systematic profiling of RNA modifications and immune landscapes in diseases. Public repositories such as the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) provide extensive transcriptomic datasets of URSA, facilitating the identification of epigenetic biomarkers and immune signatures [21]. Furthermore, machine learning algorithms offer robust tools for feature selection, enhancing the precision of diagnostic marker discovery [22–24]. Despite these technological strides, the interplay between m7G modification and immune dysregulation in URSA remains underexplored.

  To address this critical gap, we conducted the first integrative study to delineate the epigenetic-immune interplay mediated by m7G modifications in URSA. Leveraging a decidual transcriptome (GSE165004), we systematically characterized the expression patterns of m7G regulators and their cross-talk with immune dysregulation at the maternal–fetal interface. To ensure translational relevance, we designed a stepwise framework: (1) identification of dysregulated m7G regulators and immune cell populations in the decidual tissue of URSA; (2) machine learning-based identification of diagnostic biomarkers; and (3) experimental validation of screened genes and their correlation with decidual immunocytes in clinical cohorts. By bridging bioinformatics predictions with functional validation (Fig. 1), this study pioneers the exploration of m7G modification patterns in URSA pathophysiology and provides a theoretical reference for developing m7G-specific diagnostic and therapeutic strategies.Fig. 1Flowchart of this study


## Materials and methods

### Datasets and sample selection
  The GEO database (www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) was searched to obtain the disease-related datasets using the following keywords: “recurrent pregnancy loss”, “recurrent miscarriage”, and “unexplained recurrent spontaneous abortion”. Eligible datasets were selected according to the following criteria: (1) homo sapiens; (2) expression profiling by array; (3) all samples were taken from the decidual tissue; (4) each dataset contained more than ten URSA samples and ten healthy controls. Finally, one dataset was selected (GSE165004). From this dataset, 24 URSA patients and 24 healthy controls were enrolled in our study to evaluate the expression levels of m7G regulators. All gene expression levels were normalized and log2 transformed using affy in R software (version 4.1.2) [25]. When multiple probes corresponded to one specific gene, their average expression level was considered [26]. Moreover, we used the sva package to reduce batch effects [27].


### Identification of m7G-associated differentially expressed genes (DEGs)
  First, the “limma” package in R software was used to determine the DEGs between URSA and control samples, with the criteria of P-value < 0.05 and |log2 fold change (FC) |> 0.3 [28], followed by visualization via volcano plot and heatmap. In addition, the m7G regulator genes were retrieved from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB, http://software.broadinstitute.org/gsea/msigdb) [29]. To observe gene interactions, we imported m7G regulator genes into the STRING (https://string-db.org/) repository to construct the protein–protein interaction (PPI) [30]. We also analyzed the biological functions of these genes through GeneMANIA (http://genemania.org) [31]. To screen m7G-related DEGs in URSA, we intersected the m7G regulator gene with DEGs via the Venn method. Then, the regulatory relationship among the m7G-related DEGs was evaluated by Spearman correlation analysis.


### Identifying m7G-associated signature of URSA by machine learning
  To identify robust diagnostic biomarkers from the candidate m7G regulators, we employed two machine learning algorithms: least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression and random forest (RF). LASSO regression applies L1 regularization to shrink coefficients of less relevant variables to zero, effectively performing variable selection and enhancing model interpretability for high-dimensional data [32]. The “glmne” package in R software was utilized for performing LASSO regression analysis [33, 34]. Besides, RF is an ensemble classifier comprising multiple decision trees, each built from a random vector that is independently and identically distributed. It operates by randomizing both features and observations in the dataset. This method not only offers higher predictive accuracy with lower computational cost than neural networks but also demonstrates robustness to multicollinearity, missing data, and data imbalance [35]. The R package “randomForest” was utilized to construct the RF model. The overlapping genes between the LASSO model and RF model (MeanDecreaseGini > 2) were defined as candidate genes for diagnosis. Notably, to evaluate the diagnostic value of these genes, “pROC” R package was applied to calculate the area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC). To further evaluate the clinical utility of these biomarkers, the nomogram of the feature genes was generated using the “rms” package in R software [36]. Each m7G-associated signature gene was converted into an assessment point system in the model. The total score determined the final risk assessment value. Besides, the predictive performance of the nomogram was assessed by calibration curve analysis through the “rms” package in R software.


### Immune infiltration landscape analysis of URSA and its correlation with m7G-related DEGs
  Single-sample gene-set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) is an extension of the GSEA method that allows for the analysis of enriched pathways in each sample, thereby analyzing the activation degree of specific pathways. Here, ssGSEA was performed by using the “GSVA” package in R software to explore the immune cell-related pathways in URSA patients and healthy controls [37, 38]. Furthermore, immune cells or immune functions correlated with m7G-associated genes were assessed using Spearman correlation analysis. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.


### Validation of the m7G-associated signature in the URSA cohort
  This study was approved by the institutional review board of Hangzhou TCM hospital affiliated to Zhejiang Chinese Medical University (Approval No. 2024KLL119, Date of approval: 3 May 2024). All participants provided written informed consent prior to tissue collection. Decidual tissues were obtained from 10 women with URSA and 10 gestational age-matched healthy controls undergoing elective termination of pregnancy. URSA was defined as ≥ 2 consecutive pregnancy losses before 20 weeks of gestation, with exclusion of cases exhibiting: (1) anatomical abnormalities (uterine malformations, Asherman syndrome) confirmed by ultrasound; (2) parental chromosomal abnormalities; (3) endocrine disorders (thyroid dysfunction, diabetes mellitus); (4) autoimmune pathologies (anti-phospholipid syndrome, lupus anticoagulant positivity); (5) infectious etiologies (TORCH panel screening). Control subjects had ≥ 1 live birth without a history of pregnancy complications. Fresh decidual tissues were collected within 30 min post-surgery and divided into two aliquots for parallel analyses.

  First, total RNA was extracted from decidual tissue using Trizol (Cat. Subsequently, the SYBR Green Pro Taq HS premixed qPCR kit (Cat. No: AG11701, ACCURATE BIOLOGY) was employed to perform qRT-PCR. The primers are listed in Table 1. Gene expression was normalized to GAPDH using the 2−ΔΔCt method, and normalized to GAPDH. Moreover, considering the critical role of Treg cells in the URSA pathological process, we examined the correlation between FOXP3 (a key marker of Tregs) and diagnostic biomarkers, which was visualized using scatter plots. ### Immunofluorescence
  Decidual tissue segments were immediately fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 24 h at 4 °C. Frozen decidual tissues were sliced into 10 µm sections. The sections were blocked for 2 h. Next, these sections from decidual tissues were incubated overnight at 4 ℃ with primary antibodies: LSM1 (Cat. No: ab229316, abcam, 1:200), LARP1 (Cat. No: ab86359, abcam, 1:200), and NCBP2 (Cat. No: ab91556, abcam, 1:200). Following that, they were incubated with secondary antibodies: goat anti-rabbit IgG H&L (Alexa Fluor® 488) (Cat. No: ab150077; abcam, 1:200) at room temperature for 2 h. DAPI was utilized to stain nuclei. Images were obtained by the digital pathological section scanning analyzer (VS120-S6-W, OLYMPUS) and analyzed with ImageJ software (version 1.53v).


### Recognition of transcription factors (TFs) and miRNAs engaged with three diagnostic genes
  Transcriptional regulation and post-transcriptional control constitute two fundamental mechanisms governing cellular processes. TFs orchestrate genetic information flow by modulating RNA synthesis rates, whereas miRNAs execute post-transcriptional regulation through translational inhibition or targeted mRNA destabilization. Hence, we utilized the JASPAR and miRTarBase databases to establish TFs–genes and genes–miRNAs networks via NetworkAnalyst, aiming to elucidate the regulatory mechanisms of the three diagnostic genes. NetworkAnalyst is an online platform designed for the integrated analysis of complex gene expression data [39]. The JASPAR database provides high-quality, non-redundant DNA-binding profiles for TFs across six biological categories [40]. Moreover, miRTarBase records data on experimentally validated miRNA–target interactions [41]. Finally, we employed Cytoscape to visualize these interactions.


### Screening potential therapeutic agents targeting diagnostic biomarkers
  Based on the Coremine Medical database (https://coremine.com/medical), potential therapeutic agents that can target m7G-related biomarkers were predicted. Compounds were prioritized based on statistical significance (P < 0.05), with the top five agents per subtype selected to develop the drug–target network.


### Statistical analysis
  All results were expressed as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). Two-tailed t-test was utilized to assess statistical differences between groups. In all calculations, P < 0.05 was regarded as significant. All data were plotted by R software or GraphPad Prism 9 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA).


## Results

### Landscape of m7G methylation regulators in URSA
  To identify m7G methylation regulators with potential roles in URSA pathogenesis, we obtained 29 m7G regulators from the MSigDB database. PPI analysis revealed complex interactions among the protein products of these candidate genes (Fig. 2A). To further investigate the functional associations among these genes, we constructed a functional network using GeneMANIA. Results demonstrated significant functional convergence, with predominant involvement in translation regulator activity, mRNA transport, and positive regulation of post-transcriptional gene silencing (Fig. 2B). To elucidate the specific role of RNA m7G methylation in URSA, we performed differential expression analysis on the GSE165004 dataset. A total of 3795 DEGs (1846 upregulated and 1949 downregulated) were identified between URSA and control samples (Fig. 2C–D). After integration, six overlapping genes, including LSM1, NUDT16, IFIT5, LARP1, NCBP2, and NUDT4, were selected as m7G-associated DEGs for subsequent analysis (Fig. 3A). Specifically, LSM1 and NUDT16 were markedly upregulated in URSA decidua, whereas IFIT5, LARP1, NCBP2, and NUDT4 were significantly downregulated (Fig. 3B). In addition, Spearman correlation analysis revealed distinct co-expression patterns among the six m7G regulators (Fig. 3C). For example, LARP1 and NCBP2 exhibited the strongest positive correlation (r = 0.68, P < 0.001). ### Determination of m7G regulator-related diagnostic markers for URSA
  To identify robust diagnostic biomarkers for URSA, we employed two machine learning algorithms: RF and LASSO regression. In the RF model, six m7G-associated DEGs were ranked based on the mean decrease in Gini index, and then four genes with MeanDecreaseGini > 2 were extracted as potential URSA pathogenic genes, including LSM1, LARP1, NCBP2, and NUDT16 (Fig. 4A, B). LASSO regression further identified three potential pathogenic genes from the six DEGs, including LSM1, LARP1, and NCBP2 (Fig. 4C). After the intersection of the two algorithms, the core m7G genes were ultimately confirmed, comprising LSM1, LARP1, and NCBP2 (Fig. 4D). Importantly, these three genes demonstrated excellent diagnostic value, with AUC values exceeding 0.85 for each (Fig. 4E–G). To enhance clinical diagnostic utility, a nomogram was developed based on three characteristic genes, and then the calibration curve confirmed that the predicted probability of URSA by the nomogram closely matched the ideal predicted probability (Fig. 4H, I). ### Validation of the relationship between diagnostic genes and Treg in the URSA cohort
  Given the critical role of Treg deficiency in URSA pathogenesis, we further validated the correlation between the expression of three core genes and the abundance of Treg in clinical samples. FOXP3 plays a crucial role in determining and maintaining the function of Treg cells. Thus, we detected FOXP3 expression in clinical samples via qRT-PCR. Results showed that the mRNA level of FOXP3 was significantly reduced in the decidua of URSA patients (Fig. 7E), and its marked downregulation provides direct molecular evidence for the Treg deficiency observed in our immune infiltration analysis. Correlation analysis uncovered a significant negative association between LSM1 and FOXP3 (P = 0.0028), while LARP1 (P = 0.017) or NCBP2 (P = 0.015) exhibited a positive correlation with FOXP3 level (Fig. 7F–H). These findings collectively corroborate the regulatory roles of m7G modifications in Treg homeostasis, suggesting that the downregulation of LARP1 and NCBP2, together with the upregulation of LSM1, may collectively contribute to the impairment of Treg-mediated immune tolerance in URSA.

## Discussion
  URSA is intricately linked to dysregulation of the decidual immune microenvironment, where aberrant RNA modifications may disrupt fetal–maternal tolerance [42]. Our integrated analysis identified dysregulated m7G methylation regulators in URSA decidua. Among these, LSM1, LARP1, and NCBP2 emerged as high-accuracy diagnostic biomarkers that were validated in clinical cohorts through qRT–PCR and immunofluorescence. This study pioneers the role of m7G modifications in URSA pathogenesis and offers novel diagnostic tool for immune-related pregnancy loss.

  Emerging studies have elucidated the pivotal roles of m7G modifications in RNA metabolism and disease pathogenesis, particularly in cancer [43, 44] and cardiovascular disorders [45, 46]. Our findings extend this paradigm to reproductive immunology by identifying six dysregulated m7G regulators (LSM1, NUDT16, NUDT4, LARP1, NCBP2, and IFIT5) in URSA decidua. These regulators likely orchestrate aberrant m7G methylation patterns, disrupting RNA processing and immune tolerance mechanisms critical for pregnancy maintenance. Notably, Spearman correlation analysis revealed strong co-expression between LARP1–NCBP2 and NCBP2–IFIT5, suggesting functional synergy in m7G-mediated pathways. LARP1, a conserved RNA-binding protein within the LARP superfamily, governs mRNA stability and translation via interactions with 5ʹ terminal oligopyrimidine (5ʹTOP) motifs adjacent to m7G caps [47]. For the first time, we discovered its role in modulating the immune microenvironment of URSA. NCBP2, a core component of the cap-binding complex (CBC), bridges m7G caps to transcriptional machinery and modulates immune chemokines like CCL4/CCL5 in tumor microenvironments [48]. In URSA, NCBP2 downregulation may impair mRNA export and inflammatory regulation, exacerbating immune imbalance [49]. IFIT5, a potent activator of innate immunity via IRF3 and NF-κB pathways [50], exhibited an inverse correlation with Tregs, suggesting its overexpression in URSA may drive pro-inflammatory responses at the expense of immune tolerance. Collectively, these regulators may compromise m7G-dependent RNA processing (e.g., cap recognition, translation initiation) and disrupt transcriptional programs essential for immunocyte homeostasis and decidual immune equilibrium. This mechanistic framework aligns with recent evidence linking m7G dysregulation to inflammatory pathology [51, 52] and positions m7G modifiers as potential pathogenic factors for immune-related URSA.

  URSA poses significant diagnostic and therapeutic challenges due to the intricate heterogeneity of immune cell populations and functional states at the maternal–fetal interface. Current clinical strategies remain largely empirical, with limited tools to stratify immune-related URSA subtypes or predict therapeutic responses. To address the diagnostic challenges posed by URSA heterogeneity, we leveraged machine learning algorithms for robust biomarker discovery. RF and LASSO regression yielded three high-confidence biomarkers: LSM1, LARP1, and NCBP2. This dual-algorithm approach ensured that the selected genes were not only predictive, but also non-redundant, as demonstrated by their exceptional diagnostic performance in subsequent validation. Critically, the nomogram model integrates these biomarkers into a clinically actionable risk stratification tool, assigning weighted scores to LSM1, LARP1, and NCBP2 based on their independent contributions to URSA risk. This model not only quantifies individual patient risk, but also provides a framework for personalized therapeutic decision-making. Of note, these biomarkers bridge critical unmet needs in URSA management: (1) the association of LSM1 and NCBP2 with Treg deficiency offers insights into immune tolerance failure, while (2) the role of LARP1 in mRNA stability may inform RNA modification-targeted therapies. By integrating machine learning-driven discovery with experimental validation, this work advances precision diagnostics and therapy for immune-related URSA.

  The decidual immune microenvironment in URSA is characterized by a pronounced imbalance between pro-inflammatory and tolerogenic immune responses, with over 60% of cases attributed to alloimmune dysfunction [53]. Central to this pathology is the disruption of Th17/Treg equilibrium—a critical axis for maintaining fetal tolerance [54]. Our findings corroborate this paradigm, demonstrating a significant reduction in Treg cells alongside elevated T helper cell infiltration in URSA decidual tissue. Notably, m7G methylation regulators may serve as molecular arbiters of this imbalance. For instance, LSM1, which is upregulated in URSA decidua and negatively correlated with Treg abundance, may impair RNA stability in Treg-specific transcripts like FOXP3, thereby contributing to the observed Treg deficiency. Conversely, the downregulation of LARP1 and NCBP2, both positively correlated with Tregs, may further compromise Treg differentiation or function by disrupting the translation of key regulatory mRNAs [55], which promotes Th17 differentiation while suppressing Tregs. These observations align with emerging evidence that m7G modifications regulate T-cell fate decisions by modulating key cytokine and transcription factor networks [56]. Studies have confirmed that the m7G cap methyltransferase, RNMT, is a key mediator of T cell activation, which specifically regulates ribosome production, driving metabolic reprogramming, rapid proliferation and differentiation generating effector populations during T cell activation [57]. However, the precise mechanisms linking m7G dysregulation to Th17/Treg imbalance in URSA warrant further exploration.

  Our integrative analysis further revealed a complex transcriptional and post-transcriptional regulatory network governing m7G-associated diagnostic biomarkers in URSA, suggesting coordinated epigenetic control of m7G machinery. Notably, three TFs exhibited dual regulatory roles, potentially synchronizing RNA modification programs with immune-metabolic pathways critical for decidual homeostasis. This aligns with recent evidence that m7G regulators act as hubs integrating epigenetic signals with immune cell fate decisions, such as E2F1-mediated cell cycle regulation in Treg differentiation [58]. Importantly, we prioritized compounds targeting these hubs, with the majority of them possessing established roles in immunomodulation or metabolic regulation. For instance, sirolimus (mTOR inhibitor)—a high-affinity candidate for LARP1—has demonstrated efficacy in improving pregnancy outcomes by enhancing Treg/Th17 balance [59]. Similarly, FGFR inhibitor AZD4547, targeting LSM1, reverses aberrant trophoblast senescence in preeclampsia models [19], suggesting its potential to mitigate decidual immune dysregulation in URSA. While these candidates require rigorous preclinical validation, their known roles in pregnancy-related pathologies underscore the translational promise of m7G-centric therapeutic strategies. Together, these findings position m7G regulators not only as diagnostic classifiers but also as actionable nodes for precision intervention in URSA.

  Collectively, despite the novel insights into m7G-driven immune dysregulation in URSA—a previously unexplored epigenetic axis in reproductive immunology—this study has limitations that warrant consideration. First, our reliance on a single public dataset (GSE165004) with a limited sample size is a key shortcoming that may constrain generalizability. Therefore, validation in larger cohorts is needed to confirm our findings and resolve cell-type-specific m7G dynamics. Second, although we identified LSM1, LARP1, and NCBP2 as diagnostic biomarkers and linked them to Treg deficiency, mechanistic validation in in vitro (e.g., trophoblast/Treg co-cultures) or in vivo models (e.g., conditional knockout mice) remains critical to establish causality. Third, the interplay between m7G and other epigenetic regulators (e.g., m6A, DNA methylation) in URSA was not addressed due to data scarcity. Given emerging evidence of cross-talk between RNA modifications in immune diseases, future multi-omics studies should map these interactions to uncover synergistic therapeutic targets.


## Conclusions
  In conclusion, to the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to focus on the role of m7G RNA methylation in the decidual immune microenvironment of URSA. In this study, three m7G regulator-related diagnostic biomarkers (LSM1, LARP1, and NCBP2) with excellent diagnostic values in URSA were identified through bioinformatics analysis and machine learning algorithms. Besides, we discovered that the abundances of T helper cells and Treg were the key points to explain the immune dysfunction in URSA. Correlation analysis showed LSM1 negatively correlated with Tregs, while LARP1 and NCBP2 positively correlated with Tregs. Additionally, we constructed a regulatory network of these biomarkers involving key miRNAs and TFs, and prioritized potential therapeutic compounds (e.g., sirolimus, AZD4547) via drug prediction. In brief, this study may offer more insight into the pathological mechanism, diagnosis, and treatment of URSA.
