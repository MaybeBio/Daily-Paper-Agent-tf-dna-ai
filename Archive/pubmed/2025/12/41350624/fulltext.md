# Identification of sepsis pyroptosis-related genes based on single-cell sequencing technology and experimental verification


## Supplementary Information
  The online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12865-025-00785-6.


## Introduction
  Sepsis, a systemic inflammatory response to infection causing severe immune imbalance and multiorgan dysfunction, remains a leading global cause of death [1]. Despite advancements in modern medicine, diagnosing and treating sepsis pose significant challenges, with its high morbidity and mortality rates posing a serious threat to public health. The pathogenesis of sepsis is complex and includes processes such as over-activation of the immune system, storm of inflammatory factors, and programmed cell death [2]. However, existing diagnostic markers and therapeutic targets are still limited in sensitivity and specificity, and in-depth studies are urgently needed to clarify the molecular mechanisms [3].

  In recent years, cellular pyroptosis, a form of inflammatory programmed cell death, has received widespread attention for its important role in the pathological process of sepsis. Pyroptosis, primarily triggered by Caspase-1-dependent pathways, involves the swift rupture of the cell membrane and the release of inflammatory factors [4]. It serves as both a defense mechanism against infection and a contributor to uncontrolled inflammation and tissue damage [5]. Research indicates that abnormal expression of pyroptosis-related genes may influence the development and progression of sepsis [6]. Nonetheless, the specific role of pyroptosis in sepsis remains inadequately understood.

  This study utilized high-throughput RNA sequencing, weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA), multiple machine learning methods, and single-cell RNA sequencing to investigate pyroptosis’s role in sepsis by identifying key pyroptosis-related genes and analyzing their functional mechanisms. In addition, through the validation of multiple independent datasets and functional pathway enrichment analysis, this study systematically evaluated the expression characteristics and biological functions of coke death-related genes in sepsis. This study investigates the pivotal role of cellular pyroptosis in sepsis, examining its connection to immune regulation and inflammatory response, and offers novel insights and theoretical foundations for sepsis diagnosis and treatment. ## Method

### Sample collection
  First, venous blood specimens were collected from 23 sepsis patients and 10 healthy volunteers between February 2019 and December 2020 in the Affiliated Hospital of Southwest Medical University. Patients with sepsis were included based on the sepsis 3.0 criteria (infection + ΔSOFA score ≥ 2) established by the SCCM and ESICM in 2016, and they were aged between 16 and 65 years, having provided written informed consent. Healthy volunteers were individuals with no history of significant disease who underwent a routine physical examination during the same period. Exclusion criteria encompassed prior organ failure, immune or hematologic disorders, and pregnancy or lactation. Peripheral blood samples were collected within 24 h of admission for all septic patients and during the physical examination for healthy volunteers. Blood samples were collected using PAXgene tubes and stored in the hospital biospecimen bank. The study adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki and received approval from the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Southwest Medical University (Ethics No. ky2018029) and registered with the China Clinical Trial Registry (Registration No.ChiCTR1900021261).In addition, the public databases used in this study, such as GSE65682, GSE95233, etc., were obtained from the GEO public database.


### RNA extraction and sequencing analysis methods
  Peripheral blood samples were processed to extract total RNA using Trizol reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA), followed by rigorous quality control and sequencing analysis. Collected blood samples were preserved at −80 °C in a biosample bank. RNA extraction involved shaking the mixture, centrifugation to separate the supernatant, washing the RNA pellet with 75% ethanol, air drying, and dissolving in DEPC-treated water. RNA quality was assessed using an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer to confirm a 28 S/18S mRNA ratio greater than 1, and quantified with a NanoDrop. RNA meeting quality control standards was transcribed into cDNA using reverse transcriptase and random primers. The second cDNA strand was synthesized with DNA polymerase I and RNase H. After the library construction was completed, it was quantified by qPCR. Sequencing analysis was conducted by UW Genetics, utilizing the BGISEQ-500/MGISEQ-2000 platform for high-throughput RNA-seq library sequencing. SOAPnuke software was used for quality control of sequencing data, removing adapter sequences, low-quality reads (quality value < 10 with > 20% of total bases), and reads with > 5% unknown bases (N). Clean reads were then aligned to the reference genome using HISAT and Bowtie2.genome to generate sequencing results. The raw data, stored in FASTQ format, were uploaded to the Chinese Genetic Engineering Database (CNGBdb) under project number CNP0002611 (https://db.cngb.org/). The sequencing data and related quality control information of this study are described in the Supplementary Material (http://links.lww.com/SHK/B609).


### WGCNA
  Gene co-expression network analysis aims to identify co-expressed gene modules and investigate the relationship between gene networks and relevant phenotypes [7]. This study utilized weighted gene co-expression network analysis with R language 4.4.1 to investigate gene expression co-expression patterns and identify gene modules closely associated with phenotypes. RNA sequencing data were used as raw data input, and first, the gene expression matrix was normalized to filter genes with high variability for network construction. Through the soft-threshold power parameter β, the gene expression matrix was converted into a weighted correlation matrix, and the Topological Overlap Matrix (TOM) was further calculated to reflect the network connectivity among genes. Subsequently, based on the Topological Overlap Matrix, hierarchical cluster analysis combined with Dynamic Tree Cut (DTC) was used to cluster the genes into modules, each representing a set of genes with a high degree of co-expression relationship. Next, the Module Eigengene (ME) values of each module were calculated and correlated with the phenotypic data to filter out the modules that were significantly associated with the phenotype. To account for multiple testing, we applied a false discovery rate correction with a threshold of p < 0.05 to identify the significant modules. The genes related to cellular pyroptosis were obtained from the Genecards database, based on which the genes in the significantly related modules were intersected with the genes related to cellular pyroptosis to obtain the common genes, which were used for subsequent analysis [8].


### Immune infiltration analysis
  This study utilized Single-Sample Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (ssGSEA) to evaluate immune cell infiltration in sepsis samples and examined the correlation between core genes and immune cells, with all analyses conducted in the R environment [9]. The ssGSEA method from the GSVA package was employed to analyze transcriptomic data, calculating enrichment scores for 22 immune cell types [10]. Heat maps were generated to illustrate the distribution and variation of immune cells between normal control (NC) and sepsis groups, highlighting immune infiltration heterogeneity across samples via hierarchical clustering. The Spearman correlation coefficients between core gene expression and enrichment scores for each immune cell type were calculated using the cor. Test function from the stats package, resulting in the construction of a correlation matrix. In addition, heatmaps were drawn to show the two-by-two correlations between the 22 immune cell types, and the positive or negative correlation patterns between the immune cells were visualized through the color gradient, which further revealed the potential interactions between the immune cells. Finally, correlation histograms were plotted to quantify the strength and direction of the correlation between core genes and immune cells, and to clarify the significance level (P-value), thus identifying the types of immune cells that may be regulated by core genes. This analysis offers valuable insights into the potential mechanisms by which core genes influence sepsis immunoregulation, establishing a theoretical basis for future experimental research.


### Machine learning to identify core genes
  This study employed various machine learning algorithms to identify core genes from the common genes. For the selection of significant features, we performed 10-fold cross-validation and used adjusted p-values for feature significance. Additionally, to account for multiple testing, a Bonferroni correction was applied to ensure the results were statistically robust, with a significance threshold of p < 0.05. The expression matrix data of common genes were analyzed using the R language version. Random Forest is an ensemble learning technique that builds multiple decision trees using randomness and determines the final output through a voting process [11]. The mean reduction of its Gini Index (Gini coefficient) can be used to evaluate the importance of each gene, thus identifying the genes that contribute the most to the model. The number of trees in the Random Forest model was set to 1000, and the maximum depth of the trees was set to 20 to avoid overfitting. Second, Support Vector Machine, a supervised learning algorithm for classification and regression, enhances gene selection by iteratively eliminating low-impact features and identifying the optimal feature set through cross-validation [12]. The cost (C) parameter was set to 0.25, and the gamma (γ) parameter was set to 0.007. These values were chosen through grid search to achieve the best trade-off between bias and variance. Finally, LASSO regression is a linear regression method that can effectively compress unimportant regression coefficients to zero by introducing L1 regularization constraints, thus achieving feature selection and model sparsification [13]. Combining the screening results of the above three algorithms, the common genes are extracted through intersection analysis, and the core genes are finally identified. This comprehensive strategy can fully utilize the advantages of each algorithm and improve the reliability and accuracy of core gene screening.


### Expression of core genes
  For the previously screened core genes, this study verified their expression levels and identification performance in several independent databases, and all analyses were completed based on the R language. First, the expression data of the core genes were extracted from the GSE95233 [14], CNP0002611, and GSE65682 [15] databases, and the gene expression was analyzed differentially between the normal control group and the sepsis group. The difference analysis was achieved by using the Wilcoxon rank sum test, while violin plots were drawn to visualize the expression levels of core genes and the significance of differences between groups. The R package ‘pROC’ was employed to assess the diagnostic effectiveness of core sepsis genes across various datasets by calculating the Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve and the Area Under the Curve (AUC) [16]. The ROC curve reflects the balance between sensitivity and specificity of the model, and the AUC value is used to quantify the diagnostic ability of the genes. The robustness and consistency were assessed by calculating the AUC values of the core genes in the GSE95233, CNP0002611, and GSE65682 databases, respectively, in combination with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Finally, the expression differences of the core genes and their AUC results were combined to comprehensively analyze their performance in different datasets to further validate the clinical diagnostic potential of the core genes and their feasibility as biomarkers. This series of validation analyses provides strong theoretical support for subsequent functional studies.


### Meta-analysis and GSEA
  To further validate and functionally analyze the screened core genes to assess their potential role in sepsis and their biological significance. This study utilized multiple independent datasets (GSE54514 [17], GSE63042 [18], GSE95233) from the GEO database for integrated analysis using the R language. The standardized mean difference (SMD) was calculated with both fixed-effects and random-effects models, and forest plots were generated to evaluate the differential expression levels of core genes between sepsis survivor and non-survivor groups. Meanwhile, the consistency between the datasets was assessed using the heterogeneity test (I² statistic) to provide a basis for the reliability of the results. Based on the validation of the differential expression of the core genes, this study further explored the potential functional pathways of the core genes by Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA). Samples were ranked by gene expression levels, and the GSEA tool evaluated the relationship between core gene expression and biological pathways [19]. The Enrichment Score (ES) and its significance test were used to identify pathways significantly associated with the core genes and visualize their enrichment curves and significance levels.


### Single-cell sequencing
  This study utilized single-cell RNA sequencing to examine the localization of core genes within peripheral blood multicellular populations, aiming to elucidate their expression profiles in specific cell lines and inform future molecular mechanism research. This study collected five peripheral blood mononuclear cell (PBMC) samples: two from healthy volunteers, one from a patient with systemic inflammatory response syndrome, and two from patients with sepsis. Sequencing data were exported in FASTQ format, and initial quality control was performed using CellRanger software from 10× Genomics, and reads were compared to a reference genome (GRCh38). The Seurat software package was employed for data analysis, encompassing quality control, PCA downscaling, and t-SNE visualization to identify and optimize cell populations. Marker genes were identified using the FindAllMarkers function, and their distribution across various cell populations was visualized with VlnPlot and FeaturePlot functions [20]. This study constructed a library of sepsis-related single-cell RNAs, identifying IL1B, ALOX5, BCL2, and IL4R as core targets. These targets were localized in specific cell lines, providing a basis for future in vitro functional studies.


### Mendelian randomization study
  In this study, Mendelian Randomization (MR) analysis was used to investigate the causal association of BCL6 and JAK2 genes with the risk of developing sepsis (sepsis) and related outcomes. The exposure variables (exposures) were expressed as quantitative trait loci (eQTLs) of the BCL6 and JAK2 genes, and the data were obtained from the IEU OpenGWAS database. The outcome variables included sepsis, sepsis under 75, and 28-day death in critical care, and the corresponding GWAS data were also obtained from the IEU OpenGWAS database.MR analyses were performed using two-sample MR (two-sample MR) and two-sample MR (two-sample MR). (The MR analysis was performed using the two-sample MR method, and the robustness analysis was based on three different estimation methods: (1) Inverse Variance Weighted (IVW), which was used as the main effect estimation method; (2) MR-Egger regression, which was used to detect horizontal pleiotropy and provide the main effect estimation method; and (3) MR-Egger regression, which was used to detect horizontal pleiotropy and provide the main effect estimation method. Pleiotropy) and to provide complementary effect estimates; and (3) Simple Median to improve robustness and reduce the effect of potential bias. All analyses were performed in the R software environment, mainly using the TwoSampleMR (v0.5.6) R package for MR analysis, and MR-PRESSO (v1.0 R package for detecting potentially aberrant genetic variants (outliers) and correcting causal estimates. The IVW method was used for the main effects analysis, and the MR-Egger method was used to evaluate the horizontal pleiotropy, while MR-PRESSO was used to detect potentially aberrant SNPs to ensure the robustness of the results. The statistical significance level was set at P < 0.05.


### ​​Cell culture and sepsis modeling​​
  The human monocytic leukemia cell line THP-1 was cultured in RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin at 37 °C under 5% CO₂. To establish a sepsis-like inflammatory microenvironment, adherent macrophages were differentiated by incubating cells with 50 ng/mL phorbol 12-myristate 13-acetate (PMA) for 48 h. After washing, cells were maintained in antibiotic-free medium and exposed to 100 ng/mL lipopolysaccharide (LPS) for 6 h to induce inflammation. Control groups received phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) treatment. Cell supernatants were collected by centrifugation (2,000 × g, 10 min, 4 °C), aliquoted, and stored at − 80 °C for subsequent assays; cell pellets were preserved for RNA extraction.


### ​​Quantitative real-time PCR
  Total RNA was isolated from cell pellets using TRIzol reagent. RNA purity and integrity were confirmed via spectrophotometric analysis (A260/A280 ratio: 1.8–2.0) and an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer (RNA Integrity Number > 7.0). Reverse transcription was performed with 1 µg RNA using a commercial RT kit. Gene-specific primers were designed using PrimerBank and validated for specificity: JAK2​​: Forward 5-CTGCTTCACCAACCAGGGTTA-3, Reverse 5-GGGGTCGTAAGTAAAGTACAC-3.BCL6​​:Forward5-GCCAGAACCAGCTCTATAACG-3, Reverse 5-GGCCACGTCTCTTGTCCAA-3. GAPDH​​: Forward 5-GAAGGTGAAGGTCGGAGTC-3, Reverse 5-GAAGATGGTGATGGGATTTC-3. qRT-PCR amplification utilized SYBR Green chemistry under the following conditions: 95 °C for 30 s, 40 cycles of 95 °C for 5 s and 60 °C for 30 s, followed by melt-curve analysis (65–95 °C). Relative gene expression was quantified using the 2 < sup>−ΔΔCt</sup > method, normalized to GAPDH, and compared to unstimulated controls. Technical triplicates ensured assay reproducibility (CV < 10%).


### ​​Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay​​
  Target protein levels (JAK2/BCL6) in cell supernatants were quantified via sandwich ELISA. Briefly, 96-well plates were coated with capture antibodies (1:1,000 dilution) overnight at 4 °C. After blocking with 5% bovine serum albumin (120 min, RT), 100 µL of supernatant was added per well and incubated for 2 h at 37 °C. Horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated detection antibodies (1:5,000) were then applied for 1 h at 37 °C. Signal development used 3,3′,5,5′-tetramethylbenzidine (TMB) substrate (15 min, dark), terminated by 2 M H < sub > 2</sub > SO < sub > 4. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm. Standard curves were generated with recombinant proteins (0–100 ng/mL; R < sup > 2</sup > ≥ 0.99), and intra-assay variability was controlled to < 10% CV.

### Machine learning screening of core genes
  This study employed three machine learning algorithms—random forest (RF), support vector machine (SVM), and LASSO regression—to identify core genes linked to sepsis. The Random Forest model identified the top ten key genes based on the importance scores of the genes, among which JAK2, SRPK1 and BCL6 had the highest Mean Decrease Gini values (Fig. 4A).The support vector machine with recursive feature elimination (RFE) method incrementally reduced features, achieving the lowest error rate with 41 features, thus identifying the optimal feature set (Fig. 4B). LASSO regression adjusted the λ-value to determine regression coefficients, ultimately isolating key genes with significant contributions (Fig. 4D and E). Combining the results of the three algorithms, the intersection between the feature genes screened by different algorithms was shown by Venn diagram (Fig. 4C).The results showed that JAK2 and BCL6 were consistently screened as core genes significantly associated with sepsis by all three algorithms. These results further validate the potential biological importance of these two genes in sepsis and provide a solid foundation for subsequent functional studies.

### Meta-analysis and GSEA
  The results of Meta-analysis demonstrated the expression characteristics of core genes BCL6 and JAK2 in the survival and non-survival groups of sepsis patients and the results of related functional pathway analysis. A forest plot analysis was conducted to assess the standardized mean difference (SMD) in expression between BCL6 and JAK2, using datasets GSE54514, GSE63042, and GSE95233. The combined SMD for BCL6 was 0.14 (95% CI: −0.16 to 0.44), indicating low heterogeneity (I²=26%, p = 0.26) and suggesting a slightly higher expression in the survival group compared to the non-survival group, though the difference was not significant (Fig. 6A).In contrast, the combined SMD of JAK2 was − 0.04 (95% CI: −0.54 ~ 0.47) with higher heterogeneity (I²=73%, p = 0.03), suggesting that it was not significantly different between the survival and non-survival groups (Fig. 6B).In addition, based on GSEA analysis, Fig. 6C and D demonstrated the potential functional pathways of JAK2 and BCL6 in sepsis patients, respectively. The study found that elevated JAK2 expression was significantly associated with immune-related pathways, such as the Toll-like receptor signaling pathway and cytokine-cytokine receptor interactions, which are crucial in sepsis pathology. Additionally, BCL6 expression was linked to the enrichment of metabolism-related pathways, including those involved in lipid and amino acid metabolism. The findings indicate that BCL6 and JAK2 could play roles in sepsis progression via distinct biological pathways, offering valuable insights for future molecular mechanism research.

### Mendelian randomization study
  Table 2 demonstrates the results of Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis of the association between BCL6 and JAK2 gene expression levels and sepsis and related outcomes. The inverse variance weighting (IVW) method was used for primary causal estimation, and robustness analysis was performed using MR-Egger and Simple median. For the BCL6 gene (eqtl-a-ENSG00000113916), the IVW method showed that its gene expression level was significantly associated with outcome (OR = 1.6612, P = 0.0001) in critically ill patients with 28-day death in critical care (28-day mortality), suggesting that high expression of BCL6 may increase the 28-day risk of death. However, no significant correlation was observed by the MR-Egger method (OR = 0.8678, P = 0.9408) and the simple median method (OR = 1.8392, P = 0.2926). For the JAK2 gene (eqtl-a-ENSG00000096968), in sepsis analysis, the IVW method suggested that the JAK2 gene expression level was associated with sepsis risk (OR = 1.0199, P = 0.0020). However, in the analysis of sepsis patients under 75 years of age (sepsis under 75), the IVW estimate did not show a significant association (OR = 0.9490, P = 0.0308), and the P-values of both the MR-Egger and simple median methods were large, suggesting that there was no significant robustness evidence to support causality. In addition, the intercept term of MR-Egger regression did not show significant bias (P > 0.05), suggesting a low likelihood of horizontal pleiotropy. In conclusion, high BCL6 gene expression may increase the risk of death in critically ill sepsis patients, whereas JAK2 gene expression level may influence the overall risk of sepsis.

## Discussion
  In this study, we focused on the potential roles of the pyroptosis-related genes BCL6 and JAK2 in sepsis, and revealed their unique biological significance in sepsis through multiple data analysis methods. This research revealed that both genes exhibited notable differences between the sepsis group and the normal control group, highlighting their significant roles in diagnostic effectiveness and immune modulation.

  BCL6 is a key transcription factor involved in diverse biological processes, including immune regulation, inflammation, and cellular focalization. In the immune system, BCL6 maintains the balance and function of the immune system by regulating the differentiation and proliferation of B cells [21]. Its involvement in regulating the inflammatory response has garnered significant attention. BCL6 regulates inflammation by modulating the expression of inflammatory factors and activating signaling pathways. BCL6 potentially influences immune and inflammatory responses by modulating pyroptosis-related signaling pathways through its interactions with other transcription factors or signaling molecules, thereby affecting cellular pyroptosis. Pyroptosis, an inflammatory cell death process, is crucial for pathogen clearance and the progression of inflammatory diseases. BCL6 could offer novel therapeutic targets by modulating pyroptosis-related gene expression [22]. Thus, thoroughly investigating BCL6’s mechanisms in immunity, inflammation, and pyroptosis is vital for comprehending its role in disease and devising new treatments. Functions and developing new therapeutic approaches. Single-cell sequencing revealed elevated BCL6 expression in macrophages and B cells, highlighting its regulatory role in the immune system. GSEA analysis indicated that BCL6 is primarily associated with pathways related to lipid and amino acid metabolism, implying its potential impact on immune imbalance in sepsis through metabolic regulation. Immune infiltration analysis indicated a positive correlation between BCL6 and both regulatory T cells (Tregs) and Th2 cells, alongside a negative correlation with neutrophils. This suggests BCL6’s potential role in sepsis onset and progression through modulation of immune cell functional balance.MR analysis in this study suggested that high expression of BCL6 gene may increase the risk of 28-day mortality in critically ill patients, and the IVW method showed a significant association, implying that this gene may play an important role in the prognosis of sepsis.

  JAK2 (Janus kinase 2) is crucial in inflammation, immune response, and cellular pyroptosis, particularly in sepsis. It regulates cellular processes like proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis via its signaling pathway [23]. In sepsis, abnormal JAK2 activation can cause excessive inflammation and immune dysregulation, worsening organ damage and dysfunction. Research indicates that JAK2 significantly influences cellular pyroptosis by modulating downstream signaling molecules like STAT1 [24]. Additionally, JAK2 regulates the production and release of inflammatory factors crucial in sepsis, and is linked to various pathological processes in sepsis-related studies [25]. For example, aberrant expression of JAK2 may lead to endothelial cell pyroptosis, which aggravates sepsis-induced organ damage. Activation of the JAK2 signaling pathway is linked to excessive inflammatory factor production, potentially causing immune system dysregulation and worsening inflammation [26]. Modulating this pathway could lead to new therapies for mitigating sepsis-induced inflammation and immune imbalance. Inhibiting JAK2 activity could mitigate inflammatory responses and decrease cellular pyroptosis, potentially enhancing sepsis patient outcomes. A comprehensive understanding of the JAK2 signaling pathway’s regulatory mechanisms may offer novel insights and strategies for sepsis treatment. Single-cell sequencing indicated significant JAK2 expression in macrophages and B cells, with GSEA analysis highlighting its enrichment in the Toll-like receptor signaling and cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction pathways. These pathways play important roles in the sepsis inflammatory cascade response, suggesting that JAK2 may influence the pathological process of sepsis by enhancing inflammatory signaling. Immune infiltration analysis revealed a significant positive correlation between JAK2 and neutrophils as well as M1 macrophages, and a negative correlation with T-cell subsets such as Th1 and Th17 cells, indicating its potential influence on immune cell polarization and activation.The results of the present study suggest that the level of JAK2 gene expression may influence the risk of sepsis, with the IVW method showing statistical significance, suggesting that JAK2 may play a role in inflammatory regulation and immune response.

  This study systematically assessed the diagnostic potential of BCL6 and JAK2 by integrating multiple public databases. The analysis demonstrated that the ROC curves for these two genes displayed high diagnostic effectiveness across various datasets, notably achieving AUC values near or at 1.0 in the GSE95233 and CNP0002611 datasets. This indicates that BCL6 and JAK2 possess notable diagnostic potential and could serve as targets for future personalized therapies.

  In summary, the present study revealed the key roles of BCL6 and JAK2 in sepsis, especially the potential mechanisms in immune regulation and inflammatory response through a multidimensional analytical approach. The high expression of BCL6 was significantly associated with metabolism-related pathways, suggesting that it may affect the immune imbalance in sepsis by regulating lipid metabolism and amino acid metabolism. JAK2 is linked to inflammatory signaling, particularly the Toll-like receptor and cytokine interaction pathways, indicating its potential role in worsening sepsis by amplifying inflammatory responses. However, this study still has some shortcomings. For example, although the diagnostic potential of BCL6 and JAK2 was validated using machine learning and single-cell sequencing technologies, their specific molecular mechanisms are still understudied. The sample size in this study (23 sepsis patients and 10 healthy controls) is relatively small, which is a limitation in high-throughput studies. A small sample size may reduce the statistical power of the analysis and increase the risk of overfitting or spurious associations. However, this study was designed as an exploratory analysis to identify potential biomarkers for sepsis. The results should be interpreted with caution, and further validation in larger, independent cohorts is necessary to confirm these findings. To compensate for the small sample size, we employed multiple validation strategies, including the use of publicly available datasets and the application of machine learning techniques, which help increase the reliability and generalizability of the results. Future studies with larger sample sizes are needed to verify the identified biomarkers and their clinical utility. Future studies need to combine larger-scale data and multi-omics techniques to delve into the specific roles of these two genes at a deeper level and verify their feasibility as diagnostic and therapeutic targets through clinical trials. In addition, the use of LPS-treated THP-1 macrophages as an in vitro model to study sepsis has its limitations. While this model is useful for investigating the inflammatory response triggered by LPS and identifying potential biomarkers, it does not fully recapitulate the complex, multi-organ immune dysregulation and systemic inflammation observed in in vivo sepsis. The lack of tissue-specific responses and organ-level interactions in this model limits its ability to simulate the entire scope of sepsis pathology. Therefore, the findings from this model should be considered preliminary, and further validation in more complex in vivo models, such as murine sepsis models or human clinical trials, is necessary to confirm their relevance in the context of the whole organism.
