### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that "cation-DNA binding has a strong impact on lattice assembly and order."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract provides no quantitative data, statistical measures, or comparative results to support the claim of a "strong impact." No specific cation species, concentrations, or simulation conditions are mentioned. The correlation between simulation and experiment is asserted but not demonstrated with any numbers, trends, or error estimates.  
- **Why it matters** The central conclusion of the manuscript rests on this claim. Without quantitative evidence, the reader cannot assess the magnitude, direction, or reliability of the reported effect. The claim as stated is unfalsifiable from the supplied material.  
- **Resolution test** Provide quantitative results in the full manuscript, such as binding free energies, occupancy values, or lattice order parameters, with appropriate statistical comparisons across cation species and concentrations. The experimental correlation should include quantitative measures of lattice assembly quality (e.g., domain size, defect density) with error bars and statistical significance.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Methodological transparency  
- **Claim pointer** The authors state that "competitive DNA binding of different cation species is investigated by molecular dynamics simulations."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided  
- **Concern** No details are given about the simulation methodology, including force field, water model, ion parameters, simulation length, system size, or equilibration protocol. The abstract also does not specify which cation species were compared or how "competitive binding" was modeled. Without this information, the validity of the simulation results cannot be assessed.  
- **Why it matters** Molecular dynamics results are highly sensitive to force field and ion parameter choices. If the simulations are not properly validated or if the competitive binding scenario is not realistically modeled, the conclusions drawn from them may be artifacts of the computational setup.  
- **Resolution test** In the full manuscript, provide complete simulation parameters, validation against experimental or literature data, and a description of how competitive binding was implemented. Include convergence checks and error estimates for the simulation results.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Correlation versus causation  
- **Claim pointer** The authors state that simulation results are "correlated with experiments of surface-assisted DNA origami lattice assembly" and that cation-DNA binding "has a strong impact" on assembly.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract does not clarify whether the correlation between simulation and experiment is qualitative or quantitative, nor does it address potential confounding factors such as surface chemistry, pH, or temperature. The causal language ("has a strong impact") implies a direct mechanistic link, but the abstract does not establish that the observed effects are specifically due to cation-DNA binding rather than other ionic or surface-related effects.  
- **Why it matters** A correlation between simulation and experiment does not by itself establish causation. If the experimental conditions vary in ways not controlled for, the attributed role of cation-DNA binding may be overstated. The claim as presented is not adequately supported.  
- **Resolution test** In the full manuscript, show a direct quantitative comparison between simulation predictions and experimental outcomes, and include control experiments or analyses that rule out alternative explanations. Discuss potential confounding variables and how they were addressed.
