# AI-assisted identification of innovative phytochemicals from Aizoon canariense aimed at brachyury protein in chordoma: a computational strategy


## Abstract
  Chordoma is an unique and aggressive bone malignancy along with limited therapeutic options, largely due to the undruggable nature of the TBXT oncoprotein. In this study, we employed an AI-assisted drug discovery approach to optimize β-sitosterol from Aizoon canariense against TBXT. Among the generated analogs, Artificial Intelligence Ligand 2 demonstrated strong inhibitory potential, showing a stable binding free energy of − 81.09 kcal/mol and maintaining conformational stability during a 500 ns molecular dynamics simulation. Key pharmacokinetic parameters revealed blood–brain barrier permeability, high gastrointestinal absorption as well as compliance with Lipinski’s rules, while toxicity evaluation predicted moderate acute toxicity (LD₅₀ = 800 mg/kg, Class IV) with no major systemic risks. Density Functional Theory analysis confirmed molecular stability and reactivity suitable for biological interactions. Overall, these results highlight AI-Ligand 2 as a promising phytochemical derivative for targeting TBXT in chordoma, providing a foundation for future experimental validation and preclinical studies.


## Introduction
  Chordoma is an unique as well as aggressive bone malignancy, originating from notochordal remnants, with an incidence of approximately 0.18 to 0.84 cases per million individuals annually1. It predominantly affects the axial skeleton, specifically the sacrococcygeal region along with skull base, and is more prevalent in males diagnosed in their late fifties2. Despite its slow-growing nature, chordoma exhibits high recurrence and metastasis rates, leading to poor long-term survival outcomes3. Current treatment options for chordoma are limited, primarily involving surgical resection and radiation therapy4. However, complete surgical removal is often challenging because of the tumor’s position near critical structures, and chordomas are generally resistant to conventional chemotherapy. Therefore, innovative therapeutic approaches are desperately needed to enhance patient outcomes.

  Brachyury transcription factor, generated by the TBXT gene, is one of the precursor proteins vital for notochord formation during an organism’s early development5. In the context of chordoma, the newfound aberrant reactivation of brachyury has surfaced as one of the major driving forces in tumor development, aiding in the multiplication and survival of cells. The TBXT (brachyury) gene is critical for the development of notochords and is known to have high expression in notochord tumors called chordomas6. One study looking into familial cases of chordoma found germline duplications of the T gene obtained on chromosome 6q27, which acted as a major predisposition marker for the disease, thus indicating a deep nexus of familial linkage in the development of chordoma7. Several affected patients from tumor-susceptible families showed this duplication, highlighting the gene’s role in both tumorigenesis and hereditary susceptibility. Because T-brachyury is linked to tumors as well as has a function in the development of cancer, it has become a promising target for treating chordoma7. The treatment strategy aimed at disrupting brachyury expression is gaining traction as an effective treatment method, with some studies reporting that his apoptotic effects greatly surpass tumor growth in chordoma cells.

  One such Aizoon canariense blatt, identified as a “Plant of Integrated Medicine”, harbors bioactive compounds like β-sitosterol and stigmasterol8. The anti-cancerous activities of this particular phytochemical include apoptosis induction, cessation of cell proliferation in multi-cancer cells, which is why, due to its promising ability to interfere with major pharmaceuticals in cancer controlling pathways, its use by drug designers is appealing9,10. Plant-derived sources, especially those from certain medicinal herbs, are prime sources of phytosterols, and β-Sitosterol and Stigmasterol fall in this category. Their structural resemblance to cholesterol allows them to conduct various pharmacological functions in the human body, like anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, as well as anticancer activities9,10. The ability of β-sitosterol and stigmasterol to control important molecular pathways linked to cell cycle arrest, apoptosis, and tumor cell proliferation has revealed the compounds’ anticancer potential11. β-sitosterol can stop the growth of prostate, breast, along with colon cancer cells, while stigmasterol specifically targets cancer cells by affecting their energy production and stress levels, leading them to die9. Through these modalities, the two phytochemicals show promise in the invention of novel anticancer therapeutics from plants.

  Phytochemicals bioactive compounds derived from plants—have gained increasing attention in drug discovery for their structural diversity and favorable safety profiles. Among them, β-sitosterol is one of the most frequent plant sterols (phytosterols), sharing structural similarity with cholesterol. It is distributed broadly in nuts, seeds, vegetable oils, and leafy plants, and is known for cholesterol-lowering, anti-inflammatory, and antioxidant effects12. Over the past decade, β-sitosterol has also shown promising anticancer potential, functioning through many pathways including as apoptotic induction, cell cycle arrest, suppression of invasion/metastasis, oxidative stress modification, and immunological control13. Nevertheless, despite this encouraging preclinical evidence, the transition of β-sitosterol into validated anticancer therapeutics remains limited, in part due to its moderate potency, poor bioavailability, and lack of extensive mechanistic and in vivo validation14. In this context, rational modification and computational optimization of β-sitosterol derivatives may offer a viable route to enhance efficacy and drug-like properties, especially against challenging targets such as TBXT in chordoma.

  Artificial Intelligence (AI) is broadly defined as the computational ability of machines to mimic human-like intelligence through learning, reasoning, and self-correction12. Within this field, Machine Learning (ML) constitutes a core subset that enables algorithms to automatically learn patterns as well as relationships from data without explicit programming. ML methods have been successfully applied across diverse engineering domains, such as predicting shear strength in reinforced concrete beams, signal processing and fault diagnosis, and material property prediction12,15–17. Recent advancements also show that stacked and hybrid ML approaches outperform traditional algorithms in terms of accuracy in predicting, efficiency, along with interpretability12. Furthermore, the integration of ML into applied materials science has accelerated the discovery and optimization of complex material systems, reducing both computational cost and experimental effort15. By grounding our study in these foundational definitions and applications of AI and ML, we establish a robust methodological basis for employing AI-driven strategies in drug discovery against undruggable cancer targets.

  The development of AI and computer-aided drug design (CADD) technologies has fundamentally changed the approach to drug discovery18,19. The capabilities provided by AI allow for almost instantaneous locating and designing of lead compounds based on a predictive evaluation of their pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic qualities which significantly speeds the creation of tested effective therapies20. When considering rare cancers like chordoma, which are often underpinned with little data and resources for practical drug discovery, sophisticated AI and CADD systems can greatly aid in developing novel solutions. This study is, to our knowledge, the first demonstration of AI-driven pharmacophore optimization applied to β-sitosterol, generating a novel analog that directly targets TBXT a transcription factor long considered undruggable. By combining AI-optimized ligand design, molecular docking, dynamics, ADMET profiling, and DFT analysis, we present a new phytochemical scaffold with non-covalent binding potential against TBXT, offering a pioneering therapeutic avenue for chordoma.


## Methodology

### Protein-protein interaction (PPI), pathway retrieval and gene ontology of TBXT protein
  To map the interaction network of the transcription factor Brachyury (TBXT), a protein–protein interaction (PPI) network was built based on data from the STRING database (version 12.0) (accessible at:https://string-db.org/cgi/)21. With this analysis, both direct and some indirect interactors were mapped. Relational biological pathway analysis was performed by querying the Reactome Pathway Database (accessible at: https://reactome.org/) utilizing the ID R-HSA-9,758,919, which references signaling pathways TBXT is involved in within the context of chordoma22. To better understand how TBXT works and where it is located in the cell, we used Biological Ontology (GO) annotations from the QuickGO server (accessible at: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/annotations) to learn about its roles in different processes and the biological activities of the related cell parts23.


### Retrieval, validation and refinement of TBXT protein 3D structure
  The Protein Data Bank (PDB) (accessible at: https://www.rcsb.org/) entry 5QSL contains the 3D crystal structure of the Brachyury transcription factor24,25. This specific structure underwent purification, which involved the removal of water molecules as well as heteroatoms using PyMOL (version 2.5.4) to facilitate clean docking and model simulation as well as removing co-crystallized ligands26. The structure was first purified and then verified with structural-validation tools, which used the PROCHECK Ramachandran plot components and ERRAT nonbonded interaction evaluation on the UCLA DOE LAB SAVES v6.1 server (accessible at: https://saves.mbi.ucla.edu/)27. These various model geometry checks ensured the Brachyury transcription factor structure sustained reasonable spatial arrangement and a dependable framework for additional processes. After validation, the protein structure was enhanced using the GalaxyWeb server (accessible at: https://galaxy.seoklab.org/cgi-bin/submit.cgi?type=REFINE), which improves structural quality through repeated side chain repacking and relaxation of the structure28. This step improved the local geometry and accuracy of the protein model to allow better docking and simulation analyses.


### Active site prediction of TBXT protein 3D structure
  Possible ligand-binding pockets on the TBXT (Brachyury transcription factor) protein were located through the PrankWeb server (accessible at: https://prankweb.cz/), which predicts active sites using a machine learning approach based on 3D protein structures29. This server ranks possible binding cavities with respect to ligand ability and structural considerations. Active site residues identified were picked as docking targets for the virtual screening and simulation studies. Such regions are functionally important and critical for molecular interactions.


### Retrieval of phytochemicals
  The 3D conformer structures of the phytochemicals β-Sitosterol (PubChem CID: 222284) and Stigmasterol (PubChem CID: 5280794) were obtained from PubChem (accessible at: https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) as Structure Data Files (SDF)30. Open Babel (v3.1.1) was used to convert these files into Protein Data Bank (PDB) format for easy integration with docking and molecular modeling applications. Furthermore, the SMILES notations for both compounds were acquired for further cheminformatics analyses such as ADMET prediction and evaluation of the molecular properties.


### AI-Pharmacophore modelling of β-Sitosterol and stigmasterol
  The Webserver-Aided Drug Design by AI along with Classical Algorithms (WADDAICA) (accessible at: https://heisenberg.ucam.edu:5000/) was employed to create pharmacophore models for β-Sitosterol and Stigmasterol31,32. Unlike conventional pharmacophore generators, WADDAICA integrates an AI-driven graph-based deep learning model with classical conformer sampling and genetic algorithms to design novel analogs. Specifically, the server extracts pharmacophoric features (hydrogen bond donors/acceptors, hydrophobic regions, as well as aromatic moieties) using machine-learning models trained on large protein–ligand interaction datasets. These features are then recombined through evolutionary search algorithms to generate synthetically feasible analogs.

  For this study, the input SMILES strings of β-Sitosterol and Stigmasterol were uploaded. The server applied its hybrid AI/classical workflow to generate a library of new pharmacophore-based analogs. A total of 11 analogs were obtained from β-Sitosterol and 5 from Stigmasterol. Each AI-generated analog was provided in MOL2 and SMILE.dat formats, which were subsequently converted into 3D conformers (SDF and PDB) using OpenBabel. Duplicates and structurally unstable models were discarded, and only chemically valid, optimized structures were retained for downstream docking and molecular simulation studies. This AI-assisted approach ensured that the final pharmacophores reflected both learned interaction patterns from large training datasets and energy-optimized molecular geometries, thereby improving the likelihood of identifying potent TBXT inhibitors.


### Molecular dynamics (MD) Docking as well as interactions studies of AI-Generated ligands with TBXT protein
  AutoDock Vina, PyRx, along with MOE (Molecular Operating Environment) were used to study how TBXT (Brachyury transcription factor) interacts with the selected AI-Generated ligands β-Sitosterol and Stigmasterol, ensuring thorough validation and agreement on the scoring33,34. First, in MGLTools, the target protein was prepared by adding hydrogen atoms to make the structure more stable, which created a file that was then saved in PDBQT format. A docking grid was defined centered about Pocket 1, as predicted by the PrankWeb server, with the grid box parameters Size: X = 16, Y = 12, Z = 24 and Center: X = 10.280, Y = 11.260, Z= -3.250.

  Open Babel software was utilized to convert ligands to PDBQT format. To check the docking results, the same protein-ligand interactions were redocked in PyRx; the protein was brought in and changed into a macromolecule (PDBQT) structure. The ligands were uploaded, energy minimized, as well as converted to PDBQT format. The coordinates for the grid box were placed to the coordinate values for the center of Pocket 1, and docking was executed.

  We prepared the protein in MOE by adding polar hydrogens, resulting in a protonated state. The active site was defined manually with the help of PrankWeb results, and surrounding the binding pocket, dummy atoms were added to assist in guiding the docking calculation. Using all three tools to perform comparative analyses of binding poses and affinities ensured that the molecular interactions were reliable. Molecular Interaction studies of lead drug candidate were performed by Ligplot+, Discovery studio and PLIP (accessible at: https://plip-tool.biotec.tu-dresden.de/plip-web/plip/index)35–37.


### Molecular dynamics (MD) simulation of lead drug candidate
  MD simulations alongside MMGBSA free energy calculations were performed utilizing Desmond software v2019, which is integrated within the Schrödinger suite. To study how stable interactions between the ligands and the protein, we simulated β-sitosterol and stigmasterol phytochemicals combined with the Brachyury transcription factor TBXT for 500 nanoseconds using MD, imitating conditions close to those in the body38.

  Protein-ligand complexes were constructed utilizing the maestro protein preparation wizard from Schrödinger, which handles structure refinement by adding missing atoms or side chains, bond order assignment, and deuteration or protonation at physiological pH39. Employing the System Builder tool, all complexes were utilized in an orthorhombic box filled with TIP3P water, maintaining a 0.15 M NaCl concentration. Counterions were added to neutralize the system. Simulations were applied for the NPT ensemble with a 300 K temperature, 1 atm pressure, as well as OPLS_2005 force field40,41.

  Every simulation commenced with a predefined energy minimization and equilibration step, after which the trajectories were kept at intervals of 100 ps. As part of this study, we looked at RMSD, RMSF, intramolecular hydrogen bonding (intraHB), solvent accessible surface area (SASA), radius of gyration (rGyr), molecular surface area (MolSA), as well as we did principal component analysis (PCA) to examine how the structure, movement, along with stability of the ligands changed. Dynamic Cross-Correlation Matrix (DCCM) assessment was also performed to evaluate the correlation of motion between different residues in the TBXT complex.

  Binding free energies were computed following the molecular dynamics (MD) simulation performing the MMGBSA approach, which integrates molecular mechanics energies with those of solvents20. The frames were collected at 5 ns intervals, resulting in 11 frames for each simulation. ### Pharmacophore characterization of lead drug candidate
  The pharmacophore characteristics of the AI-generated lead drug candidates were assessed with the Pharmit web server (accessible at: https://pharmit.csb.pitt.edu/)42,43. The server provides the client with the means for interaction with chemical space and structure-based pharmacophore modeling—and enables the user to upload three-dimensional files of the selected ligands. Furthermore, the server performed characterization of the uploaded ligands automatically detecting hydrogen bond donors and acceptors, aromatic ring systems, hydrophobic regions as well as numerous other pharmacophoric elements. The server provided a visualization of the mapping as well as quantitative estimations within the framework of molecules which facilitated the search for the possible interactions within the defined binding pocket of the target protein. It assisted in verifying the AI-generated pharmacophores by showing that the active site residues of the TBXT protein were indeed complementary to the AI-generated TBXT pharmacophores.


### Density functional theory (DFT) of lead drug candidate
  To understand the electronic features of the lead phytochemical compound, we performed a Density Functional Theory (DFT) investigation with the Gaussian 09 W software44. We first optimized the compound’s molecular structure and then calculated its electronic properties employing the B3LYP functional on the 6-31G(d) basis set. To evaluate how the compound reacts chemically and how stable it is, we calculated the energy difference between the Highest Occupied Molecular Orbital (HOMO) as well as the Lowest Unoccupied Molecular Orbital (LUMO), known as the HOMO-LUMO energy gap (ΔEgap).

  The value of ΔEgap is crucial to define the limits of molecular softness and hardness, so the lower, the better, since it indicates a greater tendency for a reaction to occur. As for the biological interaction, a smaller ΔEgap increases the possibility of interaction. In addition to looking at frontier orbitals, we examined how charge is spread out and the compound’s polarity using MEP maps, dipole moment calculations, and analysis of frontier orbitals and their surroundings. These quantum descriptors not only showed that the ligand could be a good candidate for therapy but also explained how it interacts with and binds to the TBXT receptor.


### ADMET studies of lead drug candidate
  In the improvement of any new drug, it is mandatory that the important pharmacokinetic properties of lead ligands are assessed in terms of their usefulness to the therapy. The SwissADME tool (accessible at: http://www.swissadme.ch/) was used to analyze the primary pharmacokinetic aspects of the AI-based lead ligand45,46. The results of the analysis looked at various factors such as Total Polar Surface Area (TPSA), how well the compound mixes with fats, how well it dissolves in water, its ability to cross the blood-brain barrier (BBB), how well it is absorbed in the gastrointestinal (GI) tract, and its overall drug-like qualities, which are very important for estimating how the drug will behave in the body. These properties help to determine the level of oral bioavailability and circulation that can be expected of the compound.

  Pharmacokinetics aside, the safety assessment of clinical candidates also depends on the toxicity of the candidate compounds. With the intention of estimating the toxicological profile of the lead drug considering the TBXT protein, Protox 3.0 (accessible at: https://tox.charite.de/) was utilized47. This server predicts toxicity by relying on deep learning, accessing more than 2 million small molecules, and leveraging molecular similarity and multiple machine learning algorithms to estimate different toxicity endpoints like organ toxicity, acute toxicity, along with other adverse outcomes. Such estimates of safety are crucial in order to make decisions regarding the selection of the most suitable candidates with acceptable therapeutic windows for advanced stages of the drug development program.


## Results

### PPI, pathway retrieval and gene ontology of TBXT protein
  We created a protein-protein interaction (PPI) network with a score of 0.7 to demonstrate the relationship among the chosen proteins (Fig. 1A). The network showed a group of proteins that work together in signaling and transcription, which are important for early embryo development, mesoderm formation, and later cell specialization.

  As a network, we can consider the highlighted, significant proteins transcribed above as key nodes. These include T and TBX6 T-box transcription factors, which are proteins that control genes important for the development of the middle layer of cells, their specialization, and the fate of stem cells along the body’s axis, along with Notch signaling. TBX6 helps shape the left-right body pattern. Bone morphogenetic protein 4 (BMP4) is another one; it is a member of the TGF-beta superfamily as well as is essential for cartilage and bone precursors as well as other organogenetic growth factors. SNAI1 zinc finger proteins are parts of the most distinctive epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) regulatory circuitry. SNAI1 regulates mesodermal cell migration and programmed cell death during embryological development. Eomesodermin (EOMES) is a protein that helps control the development of certain cells and plays a function in the immune response of CD8 + T-cells when they are forming and activating their genes. POU5F1 (OCT4), along with SOX2, sustains critical developmental genes and proves to be the master regulator of control over pluripotency and early embryonic development.

  FOXH1 (Forkhead Box Protein 1) is known to mediate TGF-beta/activin signaling, and it is known to bind directly to goosecoid (GSC) TARE elements. NOTO (homeobox protein)—Located downstream of FOXA2 and T, NOTO regulates the development of the notochord and cilia, which are crucial for left–right asymmetry. The protein MIXL1 directs the development of blood and heart cells from early stem cells and helps form the body’s basic structures. Centromere protein T (CENPT) helps maintain structural order in the kinetochore during mitotic processes involving cell division and chromosome segregation. Delta-like 1 (DLL1) also acts as a Notch ligand, meaning it helps activate Notch signaling and allows the Notch intracellular domain to move into the nucleus.

  These relationships describe the unity of a transcriptional and signaling system in patterning the embryonic axis, discerning cell identity, and adhering to lineages. The transcriptional regulators T, TBX6, EOMES, OCT4, SNAI1, FOXH1, NOTO, and MIXL1, along with the signaling molecules BMP4 and DLL1, work together in a way that is important for making key decisions during development. The STRING enrichment analysis provided evidence of statistically significant PPI enrichment (p-value < 1.0e-16), which demonstrates that these proteins have non-random interactions, which suggests biological significance. This strengthens the rationale for the selection of candidate genes as part of a defined, integrated, cohesive pathway (Fig. 2B).

These proteins interconnect in TGF-beta signaling, Notch signaling, and BMP signaling, cross-cutting TGF-beta signaling early embryonic development. However, not having enough control or having too much control over these pathways is closely linked to birth defects, various developmental disorders, and ciliopathies, showing how important these pathways are in understanding diseases.

### Molecular dynamics (MD) Docking and interactions studies of AI-Generated ligands with TBXT protein
  Binding affinity alongside the interaction stability of the TBXT (Brachyury) transcription factor and AI-generated ligands of β-Sitosterol and stigmasterol derivatives using molecular docking was performed on three platforms: AutoDock Vina, PyRx, and MOE. Across all tested platforms, AI-generated Ligand 2 of β-sitosterol exhibited the most consistent and favorable binding. It also possessed the most stable pose in AutoDock Vina with − 5.7 kcal/mol binding energy and 0.00 RMSD, which translates to zero deviation in docking conformation. Between 1.6 Å and 3.2 Å, the Root Mean Square Fluctuation analysis showed variable movement of TBXT protein residues distributed primarily in protein loop regions that indicate structural core stability throughout ligand binding (Fig. 8B).

  The persistent development of hydrogen bonds as well as hydrophobic contact between the compound and the active site became evident through analysis of the protein-ligand interaction histogram during the entire simulation period (Fig. 9A). The radius of gyration values showed a steady pattern between 4.2 and 5.4 Å as the simulation reached 300 ns. This pattern reflected both a tight protein conformation and a stable binding complex structure. Along the simulation trajectory, both Molecular Surface Area (MolSA) measured 380–400 Å² and Solvent Accessible Surface Area (SASA) monitored 400–600 Å², which indicated consistent surface exposure and folding stability. The simulation results showed a consistent 60–70 Å² stability pattern, which represents maintained polar features that facilitate sustained solubility potential. Throughout the 500ns simulation period, researchers observed persistent intramolecular hydrogen bonds that enhanced the ligand’s internal stability. ### Density functional theory (DFT) of lead drug candidate
  The quantum chemical evaluation of the primary drug candidate showed the DFT-computed LUMO-HOMO gap energy as 0.23289 eV, suggesting low reactivity coupled with high molecular stability. Highly reactive molecules will typically have energy gaps below 0.1 eV, while moderately reactive molecules fall within the range of 0.1–0.2 eV.

### ADMET studies of lead drug candidate
  The study found that the AI-generated compound 2 has better drug-like qualities than β-sitosterol. The AI-enhanced ligand’s oral bioavailability and central nervous system (CNS)-targeted drug design suitability are stronger than those of β-sitosterol based on several pharmacokinetic, drug-likeness, and AI-derived parameters. This difference can be seen in Boiled Egg diagram (Fig. 13).

  The AI-generated Ligand 2’s molecular weight also lowers to 369.54 g/mol, giving it an advantage over β-Sitosterol’s 414.71 g/mol in terms of absorption and distribution. The AI compound’s capability to act as an H-bond acceptor increases to 2 while sitting at 1 for H-bond donors, making the total number 3 and improving interactions with the target receptor through hydrogen bonding. Furthermore, the AI compound’s topological polar surface area (TPSA) of 38.33 Å² surpasses β-sitosterol’s 20.23 Å², marking an increase in polarity and physiological solubility and permeability. The AI-enhanced ligand shows much better water solubility with a Log S (ESOL) of -5.09, which means it is moderately soluble. β-Sitosterol is poorly soluble with a Log S of -7.90. Although the AI compound has a lower Log P, indicating lower lipophilicity, this trait favors better bioavailability and reduced toxicity due to lower accumulation in lipid-rich tissues.

  In contrast to the low- absorption β-sitosterol, the AI ligand demonstrates high gastrointestinal absorption. Furthermore, this absence property of β-sitosterol allows the AI compound to have potential CNS activity as it is BBB permeant. Neither compound is a P-glycoprotein substrate or CYP450 enzyme, which reduces the risk of efflux and metabolic interplay. In addition, these compounds do not inhibit efflux. The skin permeation of the AI ligand, however, is less than the β-sitosterol at Log − 4.81 cm/s and − 2.20 cm/s, respectively. This suggests a restricted ability to diffuse across the skin but is an acceptable tradeoff considering its unparalleled oral pharmacokinetics. While β-sitosterol violates one rule due to excessive lipophilicity, the AI ligand violates none, complying along with Lipinski’s Rule of Five. Moreover, the AI-enhanced ligand surpasses Ghose, Veber, and Egan filters, demonstrating their enhanced drug-like properties. ## Discussion
  People develop chordoma at a rate of 0.3–0.84 cases per million annually, making it one of the rarest malignant bone tumors known to humans. The five-year survival rate sits near 50% and demonstrates both its severe nature as well as the challenging possibility of its return48. The slow growth rate of chordoma makes complete surgical removal both challenging and results in substantial neural and vascular complications at critical brain, clod, and spine locations4. The standard surgical approach combined with high-dose radiation therapy remains the gold standard for treatment, yet surgery often leaves behind cancer cells, and standard chemotherapy is ineffective because of tumor resistance49,50. A complete FDA-approved targeted therapy solution does not exist. The results from recent small clinical trials testing imatinib (overall PDGFR action) and erlotinib (specifically EGFR action) demonstrated limited therapeutic response despite having PDGFR and EGFR as targets51,52. New clinical trials for immunological treatments alongside advanced targeted inhibitor developments test to remedy these treatment boundaries, but present standard therapeutic outcomes remain ineffective.

  Recent advances have begun to erode the long-held view of TBXT as “undruggable.” Transcriptional CDK inhibitors (CDK7/12/13 as well as CDK9) reduce brachyury levels and suppress chordoma growth, thereby indirectly targeting TBXT dependency in preclinical models53. More importantly, direct chemical modulators of TBXT have now been described: afatinib forms a covalent adduct with brachyury at Cys122 (co-crystal PDB 6ZU8) and guided the design of DHC-156, which binds TBXT while sparing EGFR kinase activity and post-translationally downmodulates brachyury in chordoma cells (crystallographic and cellular evidence)54. In parallel, brachyury-targeting vaccines (GI-6301; BN-Brachyury) have shown acceptable safety with modest clinical signals but no regulatory approvals to date55. Innovative targeted-degradation approaches (TRAFTAC/oligoTRAFTAC) further demonstrate that TBXT can be selectively removed in cells, underscoring its tractability as a therapeutic node56,57. Within this landscape, our AI-optimized, non-covalent phytochemical analog displays docking/MM-GBSA metrics consistent with early-stage lead-like binders; while these values are not directly comparable to covalent TBXT binders, they motivate orthogonal experimental validation (e.g., brachyury level modulation, reporter assays, CETSA/SPR) to establish functional engagement and complement existing covalent and transcriptional strategies.

  The computational workflow guided by artificial intelligence combined pharmacophore modeling with multi-tool docking and DFT analysis and ADMET testing and 500ns MD simulations produced AI-Generated Ligand 2 as an effective β‑Sitosterol inhibitor with improved binding affinity and stability properties58. Computational methods utilized to screen natural phytochemicals led to successful findings for treating both uncommon diseases and multiple difficult-to-treat conditions. Computational studies of spinocerebellar ataxia type 3 through phytochemical optimization developed chamanetin analogs that showed powerful ATXN3 binding properties and favorable toxicity outcomes proving phytochemicals to be reliable treatment options for neurological diseases46.

  The AI-based modification of curcumin and ascorbic acid led to essential ADME property improvements and stability stability increases through deployment in MD packaging to counteract buprofezin toxicity32. A pharmacophore design model based on artificial intelligence developed gamma-tocotrienol analogs with − 7.49 kcal/mol binding affinity to BCR‑ABL1 oncoprotein indicating better tolerance than asciminib and demonstrating translational potentials of AI-generated natural product derivatives for cancer therapy59. Our lead compound structured its pharmacological features to establish equilibrium between 3 hydrophobic elements and 2 hydrogen bond receptor sites and 1 donor functionality and a single aromatic structure compared to β‑Sitosterol which displayed unbalanced 9 hydrophobic elements and 1 acceptor-linkage and donor58. The experimental data verified the dynamic properties observed through MD simulation. The TBXT–ligand system reached simulation time stability at 300 ns when the ligand stabilized after 200 ns and RMSF measurements showed protein backbone stability between 1.6 and 3.2 Å. The active site participated in continuous molecular contacts and hydrogen bonding during 500 ns modeling which verified the results of previous artificial intelligence-driven analyses59,60.

  The WADDAICA framework offers several advantages compared to traditional in-silico pipelines. First, by combining molecular docking, molecular dynamics, AI-driven pharmacophore optimization, and ADMET/toxicity predictions in a weighted consensus, it reduces bias arising from reliance on any single computational method. Second, the integration of AI modules enables efficient feature extraction and chemical optimization, thereby improving hit-to-lead prioritization. Third, the multi-layer validation (docking, dynamics, free energy and final ADMET) enhances the robustness of candidate selection compared to standalone approaches31. Finally, WADDAICA provides a generalizable and modular platform that can be adapted to other “undruggable” protein targets beyond TBXT. However, several limitations must also be acknowledged. The weighting scheme in WADDAICA, while rational, remains heuristic and could be further optimized using larger benchmarking datasets. In addition, all evaluations are currently computational; without experimental validation, the predictive power remains provisional. Moreover, AI-based optimization is based on the quality and representativeness of the training data, which can introduce hidden biases. Finally, the complexity of integrating multiple computational layers also increases computational cost and may reduce reproducibility for groups without access to high-performance computing facilities. WADDAICA represents a powerful consensus-based in-silico strategy, but its predictions should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating until complemented by experimental studies.

  The binding site exhibited critical coordinated motions through Principal Component Analysis (PCA) while dynamic cross-correlation matrix data (DCCM) displayed synchronized movement of residues in that region37. Multiple methods used before to examine ligand-induced conformational changes have been documented in previous studies45. SwissADME analyzed the AI lead compound and showed high gastrointestinal absorption along with blood–brain barrier permeability along with no Lipinski violations which could speed up future in vivo applications as seen with natural product success models. Performing toxicity tests on the simulated compound resulted in an LD₅₀ value of 800 mg/kg giving it a class IV rating because the compound failed to show responses in essential neuro‑ and immunotoxicity tests59. MMGBSA analysis of the optimized complex showed a dynamic shift in free energy from − 49.91 kcal/mol at zero time to -81.10 kcal/mol during 500 ns simulation time which matched published energetic measurements of enhanced natural product analogs41.

  Comparable AI-driven pharmacophore optimization strategies have recently yielded promising leads for example, gamma-tocotrienol analogs generated through an AI-assisted pharmacophore platform demonstrated improved docking affinity and toxicity profiles relative to established therapeutic comparators targeting leukemia59. This alignment with our strategy underscores the potential utility of using AI-mediated optimization for phytochemical scaffolds. Moreover, β-sitosterol is widely reported to exhibit anticancer activity anti-proliferative, pro-apoptotic, anti-metastatic, as well as chemo sensitizing impacts have been confirmed in silico and in vitro across various tumor types, from breast to pancreatic cancers60,61. Our optimization builds upon this biologically active natural compound, enhancing its drug-like properties through targeted AI-guided modifications. Experimental results show that the AI-Generated Ligand 2 possesses exceptional potency and structural durability while exhibiting safety validations in blocking TBXT oncoprotein in chordoma cells. This confirms AI’s potential for assisting natural compound development against difficult therapeutic targets. While our integrative WADDAICA framework provides robust computational evidence of β-sitosterol derivatives binding to TBXT, it is important to acknowledge that the study remains predictive. The absence of in vitro along with in vivo assays limits the immediate translational value of the findings. Therefore, the present results should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating rather than definitive proof of therapeutic potential. Future experimental studies are required to validate ligand binding, assess downstream biological effects, and evaluate toxicity in relevant models.

  While the in-silico pipeline validates strong potential, it is important to acknowledge certain limitations. All results presented here are predictive and based exclusively on computational modeling. Although the AI-optimized β-sitosterol analog (Ligand 2) showed improved binding affinity, stability, and pharmacokinetic/toxicity properties, these findings need experimental validation. Furthermore, future work must involve in vitro assays (e.g., binding studies, cytotoxicity assays in chordoma cell lines) to confirm biological activity, followed by in vivo models to assess pharmacodynamics, bioavailability, and systemic safety. Ultimately, only through this progression can the therapeutic promise of AI-Ligand 2 against TBXT in chordoma be established and advanced toward clinical development.


## Conclusion
  This study identified β-sitosterol derivatives with enhanced predicted binding affinity, stability, and pharmacokinetic properties against TBXT, an undruggable oncogenic driver in chordoma. Using a combination of molecular docking, pharmacophore modeling, molecular dynamics simulations, density functional theory (DFT), along with ADMET analyses, we proved that rational computational optimization could generate derivatives with promising drug-like characteristics compared to the native compound. The results highlight the potential of integrating multiple computational approaches to accelerate early anticancer drug discovery and provide structural insights into targeting TBXT. Nevertheless, all findings are preliminary and computational in nature. The identified β-sitosterol derivatives should therefore be regarded as computationally promising candidates for future experimental validation, rather than confirmed therapeutics. In conclusion, this work contributes to the development of new strategies for addressing traditionally undruggable proteins and offers testable molecular leads that warrant in vitro and in vivo evaluation to confirm efficacy and safety.
