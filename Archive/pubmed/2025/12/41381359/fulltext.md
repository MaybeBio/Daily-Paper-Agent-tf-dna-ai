Retinoid X receptor alpha subtype (RXRalpha) is a multifunctional and essential transcription factor, regulating vital physiological processes. Exploring the (ant)agonistic activities of emerging contaminants by targeting RXRalpha would provide a new perspective for evaluating their toxicological effects. Considering the limitations of existing methods for rexinoid search, a genetically stable cell line of COS7-RXRalpha was developed in the present study by dual transduction of the receptor plasmid of pCMV-RXRA and reporter vector of pRXRE-TA-LUC2 in the COS7 cells. After optimization, this COS7-RXRalpha cell-based assay was validated for its high efficiency in testing RXRalpha modulatory effects by the S-shaped and inverted S-shaped dose-response curves of the known agonists and antagonists, respectively. Screening the effects of three types of emerging contaminants on RXRalpha activation showed that butyltins and phenyltins exhibited different extents of agonistic activities, while polyfluorinated iodine alkanes (PFIs) exerted varying degrees of antagonistic potentials. The molecular docking analysis confirmed their interactions with RXRalpha via the formation of hydrophobic and hydrogen bonds. This developed genetically stable COS7-RXRalpha cell model would provide a sensitive and robust tool for the high-throughput screening of emerging contaminants with RXRalpha modulatory effects, expanding new knowledge of their potential endocrine-disrupting effects by targeting RXRalpha.
