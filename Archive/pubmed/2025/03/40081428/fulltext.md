Phenylnaphthalene-type lignans have been recognized as the major anti-inflammatory constituents in V. negundo seeds, among which vitedoamine A (VA) was the first discovered lignan alkaloid bearing a gamma-lactam. However, the protective effects and specific target of VA against rheumatoid arthritis (RA) have not been explored yet. Herein, our study revealed that VA could inhibit the transcriptional activity of NF-kappaB, and suppress the production of NO and reduce the expressions of inflammatory cytokines (IL-1beta, IL-6, and TNF-alpha) in several inflammatory cell models, mainly via inhibiting the phosphorylation of IKKalpha/beta and p65, and prevented the degradation of IkappaBalpha, thus restraining NF-kappaB activation. Meanwhile, VA considerably down-regulated the phosphorylation of IKKalpha/beta and p65, and inhibited the degradation of IkappaBalpha in RANKL-induced osteoclasts formation and differentiation, suggesting that VA may impede osteoclastogenesis and relieve joint damage in RA. Furthermore, VA interfered IKK/IkappaBalpha/NF-kappaB pathway and decreased the expressions of inflammatory cytokines in IL-1beta stimulated fibroblast-like synoviocytes (FLSs), suggesting that VA possessed promising in vitro anti-RA capacity, probably by direct targeting IKKbeta and inhibiting its activity (IC(50) value: 39 muM). In addition, molecular docking displayed that VA could bind with residues Cys99 and Asp103 in IKKbeta via hydrogen bonds, thus preventing ATP from binding with IKKbeta to inhibit the activity of IKKbeta. Taken together, VA directly targets IKKbeta and significantly inhibits the IKK/IkappaBalpha/NF-kappaB pathway, thus inhibiting inflammatory responses in FLSs and mitigating joint damage related to osteoclastogenesis, displaying great potential in treating RA.
