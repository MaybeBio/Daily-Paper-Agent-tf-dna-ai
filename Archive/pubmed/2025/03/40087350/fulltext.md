# Comprehensive analysis of bioinformatics identification TST, SQOR and NRDC is mitochondria-related biomarkers of ischemic cerebral apoplexy


## Abstract
  Mitochondria are an important organelle affecting the occurrence and development of ischemic stroke (IS). Although the role of mitochondria in IS has been paid attention to, the relevant biomarkers have not been identified, and the targeted treatment is still lacking. To further solve these problems, in this study, we combined and standardized GSE16561 and GSE58294 datasets in the GEO database as the test set, and GSE22255 as the training set. The mitochondria-related gene set was obtained from MitoCarta3.0 for study. R language was used to screen differentially expressed genes (DEGs), and weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) was used to obtain the intersection genes. KEGG pathway enrichment and gene ontology (GO) analysis were performed. LASSO and Logistic methods were used to determine the diagnostic markers of mitochondrial-related IS. The correlation between it and the main immune cells and immune-related factors was analysed. The ROC curve was used to determine the diagnostic efficacy. The protein co-expression network, transcription factor, and miRNA prediction, and drug prediction by cMAP were performed against these markers. Molecular docking tested the binding energy. Transmission electron microscopy was used to observe the structure and morphology of mitochondria in cerebral microvascular endothelial cells of MCAO/R rats. Biochemical kits were used to detect the levels of MDA and SOD in blood and tissues. Elisa was used to detect the changes of the above targets in blood and tissues. In this study, a total of 38 intersection genes were obtained, and a diagnostic model composed of 3 genes (TST, SQOR, NRDC) was further established. KEGG and GO analysis showed that these genes were highly related to immunity and were involved in the immune activities related to neutrophils, CD4+T cells, CD8+T cells, and macrophages. In addition, a total of 42 proteins, 601 transcription factors, and 99 miRNAs related to TST, SQOR, and NRDC were predicted, and the interaction map was constructed. cMAP predicted 5 potential small molecule drugs and molecular docking suggested that W.13 had the best binding energy. In MCAO/R rats, the mitochondria in BMECs were severely damaged and fragmented, accompanied by a decrease in SOD activity and an increase in MDA level. In addition, the levels of TST and SQOR in blood and tissues were increased, and the levels of NRDC were decreased. Our study provides new insights into further understanding of IS from a mitochondrial perspective.


## Introduction
  Ischaemic stroke (IS) is one of the most common causes of death in the world, with the characteristics of high morbidity, high disability, and high mortality1. Patients will have a variety of sequelae that cause different degrees of damage to the body’s motor function, resulting in temporary or permanent movement disorders. Ischaemic stroke is the main type of stroke, accounting for 80% of the total cases2. According to the latest statistics, the number of ischaemic stroke patients worldwide increased from 2.04 million in 1990 to 3.29 million in 2019 and IS is expected to reach 4.9 million by 20303. The increasing number of IS patients will burden the medical system with a heavy economic burden. At present, the first-line treatment for ischaemic stroke is intravenous injection of recombinant tissue plasminogen activator (tPA). Timely intravenous thrombolysis and endovascular thrombectomy can restore blood flow to improve the prognosis of neurological function. However, due to time constraints, contraindications, and the possibility of haemorrhagic transformation, thrombolytic therapy is only effective for a minority of patients, and most patients have to choose other alternative therapies4,5. In patients with successful thrombolysis, restoring blood flow to the ischaemic area may worsen brain injury. In this context, it is necessary to update treatment methods and treatment concepts and find potential therapeutic targets.

  Tissue ischemia caused by vascular occlusion, energy metabolism disorders caused by hypoxia and cascade damage are the most direct causes promoting the development of IS6,7. In the past, numerous attempts have been made to screen biomarkers related to ischaemia and hypoxia and construct corresponding prediction models, which provide a new perspective and insight for the treatment and diagnosis of IS8,9. However, there are also some shortcomings. As the main site involved in cellular respiration and energy metabolism and the most sensitive organelle to Ischaemia and hypoxia changes, the role of mitochondria in IS still needs to be further improved10. Mitochondria-derived ATP is widely involved in the electrical activity of neurons, and normal mitochondrial metabolism is a prerequisite for neuronal survival and maintenance of excitability11. Mitochondrial-derived ROS can also act as a regulator to participate in the regulation of a variety of potential pathogenesis of IS, such as apoptosis, autophagy, ferroptosis, and so on10,12,13. In addition, mitochondrial function has also been confirmed to be closely related to inflammatory response and immune abnormalities, another key feature of IS14. Recent studies have shown that mitochondrial dysfunction IS the main regulator of NLRP3 inflammatory activation and further activates innate immunity and pyroptosis, which aggravates IS through proinflammatory cytokines and caspase-115. It IS also observed that ischemia-induced abnormal changes of succinate accumulation and mitochondrial NADH in IS tissues, which promote ROS generation through the activity of ubiquinone oxidoreductase that promotes reverse electron transfer (RET) and aggravates brain damage. These research facts provide hope for the development of mitochondrial function-related IS biomarkers and thus potential IS therapeutic drugs. However, relevant studies are still lacking, and further studies are needed to explore.

  The rapid development of bioinformatics technology has provided great help for the development and prediction of biomarkers and therapeutic targets16. Although there is much experimental and clinical evidence that mitochondrial dysfunction can aggravate the progression of IS, few studies have systematically investigated mitochondrial-related IS biomarkers using comprehensive bioinformatics methods. In this work, we obtained mitochondrial-related genes from MitoCarta 3.0 database17, used differential expression analysis and machine learning to screen characteristic mitochondrial-related genes as diagnostic biomarkers for IS, explored their relationship with immune cell infiltration, and targeted regulatory mechanisms and drug prediction. The binding was verified by molecular docking, and in addition, the expression of diagnostic genes was verified by animal experiments. These works provide a new perspective to better understand the underlying molecular mechanisms of IS pathogenesis and help to identify promising biomarkers and therapeutic targets.


## Methods

### Data preparation and processing
  From Gene expression Omnibus (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo) IS the patient’s transcriptome map database, which contains the mRNA expression patterns and clinical information, with “Ischemic stroke” for key words search, Whole blood or peripheral blood gene expression profiles of IS patients or Control samples were included. The inclusion criteria for the dataset in this study included the following: (a) the dataset needed to contain genome-wide mRNA expression profiles. (b) The source of data should be peripheral blood samples from individuals in the IS group and control samples from healthy people without drug stimulation and transfection. (c) The dataset must be original and complete and have been used in other IS bioinformatics analyses. (d) The dataset type is specifically specified as “array Expression analysis”. The GSE16561 dataset (GPL6883, Illumina HumanRef-8 v3.0 expression bead chip, array, Homo sapiens, included 39 controls and 24 patients with ischemic stroke. The GSE58294 dataset (GPL570, Afymetrix Human Genome U133 Plus 2.0 Array, Homo sapiens, contained a total of 23 control samples and 69 ischemic stroke samples. The GSE22255 dataset (GPL570, Affymetrix Human Genome U133 Plus 2.0 Array, array, Homo sapiens,) included 20 controls and 20 patients with ischemic stroke18–20. ### Data processing and differential gene analysis
  The “hgu133plus2.db” annotation file and the R package “tidyverse” and “AnnoProbe” packages were used for identifier conversion of the expression profile arrays of GSE16561 versus GSE37587. After that, the expression profiles were integrated using the Combat function of the “sva” package to eliminate batch differences, and named Merge,. Finally, the preprocessed data were examined in depth by principal component analysis (PCA). Limma(linear models for microarray data) is a differential expression screening method based on generalized linear models. Differential analysis was performed using the R package “limma” with P < 0.05 and Fold Change > 1.2 as thresholds to obtain differentially expressed genes between different IS and controls. In addition, the R package “pheatmap” was used to create a clustered heatmap of the top 50 differential genes.


### Weighted correlation network analysis (WGCNA)
  Use WGCNA to screen for core module genes in Merge. Firstly, the “goodSamplesGenes” of the R software package WGCNA was used to remove outlier genes and samples, and further construct the scale-free co-expression network. In order to ensure the construction of scale-free networks, a suitable soft threshold is selected to transform the adjacency into a topological overlap matrix (TOM), which can measure the network connectivity of a gene. In order to classify genes with similar expression into different gene modules, the minimum size of the gene tree is 30 as the standard, and modules with a distance of less than 0.25 are merged, each module is represented by a different color. The grey module is considered to be a set of genes that cannot be assigned to any module. Gene significance (GS) and module membership (MM) were calculated to correlate modules with clinical traits. Finally, highly correlated modules are analyzed to explore their core genes and potential roles using module signature genes to assess the association between modules and phenotypes. The module with the largest absolute value of correlation coefficient is selected as the key module for further analysis.


### Identification of hub genes and functional enrichment analysis
  In order to obtain the hub genes related to both mitochondrial metabolic disorders and IS, we used the “VennDiagram” package in R software to obtain the intersection of genes obtained by deg and WGCNA and genes in the metabolic gene set of mitochondria related genes, and used violin diagram to show the expression of these genes in the data set.T-test was used if the data were normally distributed, Mann-Whitney U-test was used if the data were not normally distributed, and significance was defined as P < 0.05. To further explore the biological functions of hub genes, the software packages “clusterProfiler”, “org.Hs.eg.db” and “ggplot2” in R software were used. GO Gene enrichment analysis and visualization were performed for hub genes, P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.


### Screening and modeling of diagnostic markers
  Correlation analysis of target genes was performed, and the correlation heat map was drawn using the R package “corrplot”. The minimum absolute contraction selection operator (LASSO) logistic regression was used to reduce the number of genes in the model and solve the multicollinearity problem in regression analysis21. The feature selection and screening of IS diagnostic markers were determined by LASSO algorithm using “glmnet” software package. We then use Logistic regression to represent the IS sample when the response variable is 1 and the Control sample when the response variable is 0. Stepwise regression analysis was used to simplify the model and logistic regression was used to fit the relationship between these significant factors and response variables. Finally, receiver operating characteristic curve (ROC) and area under ROC curve were used to evaluate the diagnostic effectiveness of the model. These analyses are performed using the “stats” and “pROC” packages in the R software.


### GSEA analysis of hub genes
  According to the expression level of hub genes, the samples were divided into high expression group and low expression group. The subsets of c2.cp.kegg and c5.go.bp were downloaded from the Molecular Signatures Database(https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/msigdb) to evaluate related pathways and biological processes. P < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.


### Analysis of immune infiltration and immune-related factors
  CIBERSORT was run using SangerBox to assess immune cell infiltration in a microenvironment containing 547 biomarkers and 22 human immune cells, including plasma, B cells, T cells, and bone marrow cell subsets22. This tool is based on the principle of linear support vector regression to perform deconvolution analysis of the expression matrix of immune cells. This study used Merge expression data and quantified the relative proportions of 22 immune cells in each sample. Spearman Spearman correlation analysis was performed between Hub gene, immune infiltration and immune factors. The analysis is performed using the “psych” package in the R software, and the results are displayed as a heat map. In addition, we further analyzed the relationship between Hub gene and immune-related factors.


### Potential Regulatory mechanisms of Key genes and drug prediction
  Associations between hub genes were first calculated. Then GeneMANIA database (http://genemania.org/) was used to construct the co-expression network of key genes and the top 20 genes with the highest correlation with key genes23. Transcription factors (TFS) targeting key genes were predicted using the Cistrome DB database (http://cistrome.org/db/)24, The miRNAs targeting key genes were predicted from the miRWalk database (https://mirwalk.umm.uni-heidelberg.de/)25, and the TF-mRNA network and miRNA-mRNA network were constructed by Cytoscape.

  CMap (connection Map) is an open database (https://cmap.ihmc.us/) whose role is to screen potential drugs based on differential genes in IS patients26. And to identify links between potential drugs that share the same chemicals, physiological processes, and mechanisms of action.


### Molecular docking analysis
  The obtained small molecule drugs with potential effects on mitochondrial function in IS patients were docked with three diagnostic gene proteins. Molecular docking was performed using AutoDockTools and AutoDock Vina. Firstly, the core compound structure files (mol2 format) were downloaded from PubChem database. Then the target crystal structure was obtained from the PDB database (https://www.pdb.org/x) and imported into PyMOL (https://pymol.org/2/x) for dehydration and hydrogenation to separate the ligand. Docking grid boxes were subsequently constructed in AutoDockTools at the active site of each target protein and then saved in pdbqt format. Molecular docking of hypothetical targets and active compounds was performed using AutoDock Vina and free binding energies were evaluated.


### Preparation of animals
  Ten male Sprague-Dawley rats, weighing 220–250 g, were purchased from Beijing Weitong Lihua Laboratory Animal Technology Co., LTD., license number SCXK(Beijing)2021-0011. All animal handling procedures were performed in accordance with the National Institutes of Health Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals, 1996, and were approved by the Animal Experiment Ethics Committee of Hebei University of Traditional Chinese Medicine, approval number: DWLL202303004. The rearing temperature was 24 ± °C on a 12 h/ dark cycle with regular feeding and water collection.


### Establishment of animal and MCAO/R model
  Experiments were conducted in accordance with ARRIVE guidelines in this study. IS was simulated using MCAO/R. In brief, mice were anesthetized with 5% isoflurane. The left common carotid artery (CCA), external carotid artery (ECA), and internal carotid artery (ICA) are shown sequentially. We then tightened the ICA and inserted the silicone covered nylon monofilament. The top of the nylon monofilament reached the MCA. According to our previous findings, 2 h of ischemia followed by 72 h of reperfusion is an ideal MCAO/R model27. Mice in the sham-operated group underwent the same procedure without monofilaments. Longa’s neurobehavioral score was evaluated by double-blind method. The score was 0 for no neurological symptoms, 1 for inability to fully extend the contralateral forepaw, 2 for turning to the paralyzed side, 3 for tipping to the contralateral side, 4 for inability to walk spontaneously, 4 for loss of consciousness, and 0 and 4 for failure of modeling. The blood flow of the rats in the model group was monitored by laser speckle blood flow(PERIMED, PeriCam PSI Zoom). When the cerebral blood flow was observed to decrease to 60% or more and the score was between 2 and 3 the rats were included in the study. In this study, the animals were excluded from the scope of the experiment if the nylon rope between the silicon sides slipped at or between the exits or if the animals died prematurely.


### Modified neurological severity score
  The neurological deficit of MCAO/R model rats was observed, and the animal motor test, sensory test, reflex loss and abnormal movement test and balance beam test were evaluated. The sensory test included position perception test and body placement test with 2 points each. The loss of reflex and abnormal movement test included auricle, cornea, panic reflex and dystonia test with 1 point each. The balance beam test was evaluated according to the limb fall and balance time. A score of 18 was considered as severe neurological impairment.


### Preparation of blood samples and tissue
  All rats were weighed and anesthetized by intraperitoneal injection of 50 mg/ kg− 1 of pentobarbital sodium. After the animals lost consciousness, their whisks were cut off, and blood was collected from the inner canthus using microsampling vessels. Part of the rat blood was stored in a vacuum tube and allowed to rest at 4°C for 20 min. After that, the supernatant was collected at 4°C and 3000 r·min− 1 for 15 min to obtain serum. Another part of the blood was stored in a collection tube containing EDTA anticoagulant and treated under the same conditions to obtain plasma. After that, the brain tissue of the rats was obtained, and the cerebral cortex of the ischemic side was obtained.


### TEM observation
  Tissues from each group of rats were cut into 1 mm3 pieces and fixed in 2.5% glutaraldehyde for 4 h, washed three times in 0.1 mol L− 1 PBS (15 min each time), fixed in 1% osmic acid for 2 h, and washed three times in PBS (15 min each time). The samples were routinely dehydrated in graded acetone, embedded in epon812 at room temperature, oven cured, ultrathin sections with a thickness of 50–60 nm, double stained with 3% uranyl acetate lead citrate for 20 min, washed, oven dried, and finally observed and photographed by TEM (JEOL, JEM1400).


### Determination of SOD and MDA levels
  Biochemical reagents detected the levels of SOD and MDA in brain tissue and serum. All kits were purchased from Nanjing Jiancheng BioEngineering Institute (Nanjing, China) and analyzed according to the manufacturer’s instructions, the item numbers are A001-3-2 and A003-1-2 respectively. Briefly, serum and tissue homogenates were prepared, working solutions were prepared according to the manufacturer’s instructions, absorbance was measured, and absorbance values were calculated at the recommended wavelengths of 450 nm and 532 nm, respectively.


### Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (Elisa)
  ELisa kits were purchased from ElisaBiotech under the product number Cat. # EIA-3699-Rat, Cat. # EIA-2595-Rat, and Cat. # EIA-3700-Rat. The levels of TST, NRDC, and SQOR in plasma and cortex were measured. All operations were performed according to the instructions. In short, standard, blank, and sample holes were set in the microporous enzyme label plate successively and samples were added. OD values of each hole were measured at 450 nm wavelength after incubation, and the concentration was calculated.


### q RT-PCR experiment
  The ischemic penumbra area of the cortex was ground with a cryogenic tissue instrument, and the RNA was extracted with a total RNA extraction kit(promega, LS1040), and the RNA concentration was detected with an ultra-microspectrophotometer. 1 μg of total RNA was reverse transcribed with gDNA Eraser according to PrimeScriptTMRT revert KIT(promega, LS2052). Primers synthesized from Beijing set the prosperous biological technology co., LTD., details are shown in Table 1. The cDNA samples were diluted in a 10-fold gradient, and 0.8 µL of primers were used in the upstream and downstream of the reaction. The total reaction system was 20 μL, according to the TB GreenTM Premium EX TapTMII instructions(promega, A6001). 2−△△CT was used to detect the relative expression of target genes.

### Statistical analysis
  Bioinformatics analyses were performed with the use of R software, version 4.3.0. Statistical analysis of biological experiments was performed using GraphPad Prism9.5. Data were analyzed using Student’s t test or one-way analysis of variance. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

### Identification of IS and mitochondria-related genes (MRGs)

Using FoldChange > 1.2 and P < 0.05 as the condition, we analyzed the differentially expressed genes in Merge, and a total of 2857 differentially expressed genes were obtained, including 2203 up-regulated and 654 down-regulated genes.These differentially expressed genes are represented by a volcano map, and the top 50 genes were used to form a clustering heatmap using the R package “pheatmap”, showing significant genetic differences between the combined samples (Fig. 2C, D).

### Functional enrichment analysis of MRGs
  To further elucidate the biological roles of these genes, GO enrichment analysis was performed on these 38 genes. KEGG results suggested that the main metabolic process affected by these genes was cofactor synthesis, and the main pathways affecting the body were sphingolipid signaling pathway and PPAR signaling pathway, and were closely related to reactive oxygen species production, diabetic cardiomyopathy and alcoholic fatty liver, as shown in Fig. 5.

  As shown in Fig. 6A, correlation analysis of 38 MRGS and correlation heat maps using the R package “corplot” revealed a strong correlation between these genes. To identify diagnostic genes, LASSO analysis was first used to narrow down the range of possible diagnostic biomarkers. LASSO model contains 38 genes. First of all, based on the lambda identified 14 genes, and the result is shown in Fig. 6B, C. logistic regression analysis was then performed. We used a logistic regression with two response variables, where a response variable of 1 represents the IS sample and a response variable of 0 represents the control sample. Using stepwise regression analysis, eliminating the response variable is not a significant factor, simplified model, the resulting three diagnostic genes TST, SQOR, and NRDC, as shown in Fig. 6D. The diagnostic performance of the model was evaluated using the receiver operating characteristic curve (ROC) and the area under the ROC curve in Merge and GSE22255, as shown in Fig. 6E, F. The Merge and GSE22255 joint diagnosis models of AUC are 91.897, and 81.250, respectively. The diagnostic effect of combined detection is better than that of single-gene detection.

### Construction and evaluation of the model
  We constructed the MCAO/R model with 2 h ischemia followed by 72 h reperfusion based on our previous experience. As shown in Fig. 11A, the left cerebral blood flow was significantly reduced before and after insertion of the nylon wire, and the blood flow gradually recovered after removal, suggesting that the model was successfully prepared. Neurological function scores were then counted from day 1 to day 3, and as shown in Fig. 11B, there was a progressive increase in neurological impairment during the first 1–3 days after 2 h of ischemia, peaking at day 3.

### The mitochondrial damage of MCAO/R rats was observed by TEM
  Since the GEO dataset we analyzed was from the blood of IS patients and control patients, and blood-brain barrier damage IS a typical pathological injury in IS, we identified the observation target as one of the components of circulating blood in direct contact with the BBB, mainly cerebral microvascular endothelial cells. Using transmission electron microscopy to observe the morphology and structure of mitochondria in BMECs, as shown in Fig. 12, we found that compared with the Sham group, the number of mitochondria in the endothelial cells of MCAO/R rats increased significantly, showing a trend of fragmentation, and some mitochondria were even in a state of division. In addition, the structure of mitochondria was significantly damaged, mitochondrial cristae was reduced or disappeared, and the membrane density was thickened to varying degrees.

  Fig. 12Ultrastructure of BMECs in each group (TEM, × 4000 times, scale = 1.0 μm).


### Abnormal MDA and SOD levels were observed in MCAO/R rats
  MDA is one of the major toxic products produced by lipid peroxidation imbalance. SOD is an important antioxidant enzyme in the body, which protects mitochondria by scavenging superoxide free radicals in mitochondria. Their comprehensive level reflects the function of mitochondria to a certain extent. As shown in Fig. 13, we found that MDA levels in the circulating blood and the cortex of the ischemic side were sharply increased in MCAO/R rats compared with Sham rats, corresponding to a decrease in SOD content. These results suggest mitochondrial dysfunction in MCAO/R rats.

### Abnormal TST, NRDC and SQOR levels in blood and ischemic cortex of MCAO/R rats
  To determine the levels of these biomarkers, we measured the contents of TST, NRDC, and SQOR in the blood and cortex of MCAO/R rats using Elisa, and their RNA levels in the cortex using real-time qRT-PCR. As shown in Fig. 14, the levels of TST and SQOR were increased and the levels of NRDC were decreased in the blood and ischemic cortex of MCAO/R rats compared with the Sham group. ## Discussion
  With the deepening of the research on mitochondria and IS, more and more experimental and clinical evidence shows that mitochondrial function IS closely related to the progression of IS28,29. Since the dataset we analyzed was derived from the blood of patients, we chose the BBB components in direct contact with the blood for observation, and based on this idea, BMECs became our target for observation. We observed numerous fragmented mitochondria in the BMECs of the ischemic cortex of MCAO/R rats, accompanied by abnormal changes in SOD and MDA levels, which suggested the dysfunction of mitochondrial structure and function after IS. Combined with previous research evidence, it IS crucial to find mitochondrial-related IS markers.

  The MitoCarta 3.0 database was published in January 2021 and is one of the most frequently used databases for mitochondrial research30. In this study, a comprehensive bioinformatics method was used for the first time to screen 38 mitochondrial-related IS genes combined with the MitoCarta 3.0 database, and three characteristic genes TST, SQOR, and NRDC were identified as biomarkers for constructing the IS diagnostic model. ROC was used to evaluate the effect of multiple indicators on the classification and diagnosis of the testee31,32. The AUC results showed that the combined diagnostic model composed of TST, SQOR, and NRDC performed better in the test set Merge and the validation set GSE22255.

  According to the annotation of MitoPathways in MitoCarta3.0, TST, SQOR, and NRDC all belong to “metabolic” pathways, but the specific ways in which they participate in metabolic functions are quite different. TST is a cyanide sulfur transferase, also known as rhodanese, which is mapped to 22q12.3 in the chromosome and belongs to the eukaryotic sulfur transferase family33. The highest concentration of TST was found in the liver and significant amounts were found in the kidney, adrenal gland, and thyroid gland. The highest TST activity was observed in the kidney, lung, and brain.It has been reported that TST exerts its detoxification effect mainly by mediating the transfer of sulfur from thiosulfate to cyanide through a double-shift reaction34. They also participate in the process of sulfur-selenium transport, mitochondrial import of 5SrRNA, and the formation of iron-sulfur clusters either alone or in complex with proteins33,35. In the present study, both data analysis and animal experiments showed that TST levels were increased in IS patients, however, the results of recent studies are somewhat different from the present analysis. Yang’s results suggest that TST deficiency is associated with altered ROS and peroxiredoxin (mainly polysulfide) production in the cerebral cortex, as well as altered reactive species interactions due to mitochondrial OXPHOS remodelling. These changes were accompanied by abnormal expression of Nrf2-Keap1 pathway and sulfhydryl-dependent antioxidant dysfunction36. Our results seem to suggest that the protective effect of TST may be lost in ischemic encephalopathy. It has been reported that TST interferes with the oxidation/antioxidant balance by forming glutathione persulfide (GSSH) and reducing it to thioredoxin (TRX)33,34. Therefore, increased TST activity will affect ROS scavenging, but the ability of TST to catalyze forward and reverse sulfur transfer may also be determined by the phosphorylation state of the enzyme. Due to the imbalance in activity or availability of TST, and the lack of conclusive evidence of a direct link between TST activity and TRX, the TST-dominated antioxidant response may be altered during the process of IS36, and the test results of SOD and MDA levels seem to confirm this conjecture. Our results suggest that TST may be a potential biomarker of IS, but the specific physiological role and function of TST in IS still need a lot of experimental and clinical research.

  SQOR, a membrane-bound flavoprotein of the glutathione reductase family, is a key enzyme in the oxidation and detoxification of sulfide37. In vivo, SQOR participates in the oxidation of hydrogen sulfide through the assistance of quinones (such as ubiquinone-10) to gradually produce thiosulfate and thioane (molecular sulfur) atoms38. SQOR is reported to be highly expressed in the liver, lung, heart, and other tissues, but its expression level is low in most mammalian brains. Therefore, the brain is extremely sensitive to the side effects caused by sulfide accumulation. Because of the close relationship between sulfide catabolism and brain energy homeostasis, the effect of sulfide catabolism on brain bioenergetics during acute hypoxia has gradually become a hot topic in IS39. In the animal experiment, SQOR level was decreased in MCAO/R group, which was consistent with some reported experimental data. For example, Eizo et al. found that SQOR silencing would increase brain sensitivity to hypoxia, while neuron-specific SQOR expression prevented hypoxia-induced sulfide accumulation, bioenergetic exhaustion, and ischemic brain damage40. In addition, Cai suggested that targeting the SYVN1-SQOR signaling axis can improve acute kidney injury by interfering with ferroptosis, which IS also one of the pathogenesis of IS that has received much attention, which seems to provide indirect evidence for the role of SQOR in brain protection41. However, the results of data analysis were in contrast to animal experiments, where SQOR levels were significantly increased in IS patients compared with controls. Hypoxia has been reported to act as a stimulus to induce upregulation of SQOR expression in rectal cancer cells and to aggravate cell toxicity by biological stimulation with H2S. In addition, the increase of H2S can also promote the phagocytosis of erythrocytes by microglia in intracerebral hemorrhage and play a protective role. Therefore, whether hypoxia acts as a stimulus to promote increased SQOR levels and the downstream mechanisms remain unclear in IS patients. In view of the close biological function relationship between TST and SQOR, we speculate that there may be some abnormal changes in the sulfide oxidation process in IS that have not yet been identified. Although the specific mechanism and process of the changes still need to be further studied, this provides some new perspectives for the in-depth understanding of IS.

  NRDC is a metalloproteinase belonging to the invalzincin/M16 family of Metallo endopeptidases42. It can cleave peptides at the N-terminus of arginine and lysine residues in the double-base moiety and interacts closely with a variety of biomolecules such as tumor necrosis factor convertase and neuregulin 1. At the same time, it is involved in the process of extracellular domain abscission of neuregulin mediated by BACE1 and ADAM17, axon maturation, and myelination, and is widely involved in the normal process of brain function43. It is closely related to Alzheimer’s disease, schizophrenia, and other diseases. NRDC is highly expressed in the heart and skeletal muscle but at a lower level in the rest of the tissues. It is reported that in the early stages of mouse development, high levels of NRDC are almost exclusively expressed in neural tissues, which suggests the potential of NRDC as a neurodevelopmental enzyme. Studies have found that neuron-specific overexpression of NRDC can promote α-secretase activity in the non-amyloid metabolic pathway of amyloid precursor protein in AD to reduce Aβ deposition, and promote the cleavage of α-secretase to produce protective sAPPα release to play A neuroprotective role44,45. However, The role of NRDC in ischemic and hypoxic diseases has not been recognized until recently. Chen and Miao et al. successively found that NRDC levels were decreased in the infarct area in patients with myocardial infarction and angina pectoris, NRDC was proved to be a potential biomarker for ACS, and increased serum NRDC levels were also associated with worse long-term outcomes in ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction46,47 These results provide evidence for the involvement of NRDC in hypoxic-ischemic diseases, but the relationship between NRDC and IS remains unclear. In the present study, data analysis and animal experiments showed that the level of NRDC was increased in IS patients, which seemed to confirm the link between NRDC and ischemia and hypoxia. These three diagnostic genes are closely related to mitochondrial metabolic pathways, and experimental evidence in recent years has also gradually confirmed the close link between mitochondrial function and IS progression, these facts further provide reliability for our study.

  Mitochondria have a close relationship with immune function. On the one hand, mitochondria-derived damage-associated molecular patterns (mtDAMPs), such as mitochondrial DNA, can act as ligands for pattern recognition receptors (PRRs) to promote inflammation48. On the other hand, regulated cell death-related changes in mitochondrial outer membrane permeability also trigger cascade cellular responses such as autophagy to aggravate inflammatory processes49. In IS, the infiltration of peripheral immune cells due to the breakdown of the blood-brain barrier (BBB) is one of the most important hallmark events, and the inflammatory response due to vascular occlusion begins immediately in the intravascular compartment. Subsequent hypoxia, changes in shear stress, and reactive oxygen species (ROS) lead to the activation of complement, platelet, and endothelial cells, as well as the invasion of peripheral immune cells50. To further understand the process of immune infiltration and the role of these mitochondrial diagnostic genes in IS, we performed single gene GSEA analysis, immune infiltration analysis, and immune correlation analysis for Merge. Studies have found that in addition to the mitochondrial metabolic processes mentioned above, the three genes are also related to immune pathways such as the Toll-like receptor pathway and chemokine pathway. In immune infiltration analysis, the proportion of some innate immune cells such as monocytes, neutrophils, M0 macrophages, and activated mast cells in IS was significantly higher than that in controls, while the proportion of some adaptive immune cells such as CD8+T cells and naive CD4+T cells was significantly lower than that in controls. Further studies suggested that TST was negatively correlated with CD8+T cells and positively correlated with naive CD4+T cells, M0 macrophages, and neutrophils, similar to the results of Tang and Wang51, who found evidence that TST homologs participate in innate immunity and bind to blood peripheral monocytes, respectively. SQOR was negatively correlated with immature B cells, plasma cells, regulatory T cells, M0-type macrophages, and activated dendritic cells. H2S, which is closely related to SQOR, has been reported to have potential therapeutic potential to alleviate the pro-inflammatory macrophage phenotype52, and accordingly, SQOR may have a similar relationship with macrophages, which is similar to our experimental results. NRDC was negatively correlated with memory B cells, plasma cells, γδT cells, and activated NK cells, and positively correlated with M0 macrophages and neutrophils. It has been reported that defects in T cell costimulatory activation are associated with many autoimmune diseases. V-domain inhibitor of T cell activation 1(VTCN1) has been shown to promote autoimmune diabetes, systemic active vitiligo, and other immune diseases, although there is no direct evidence of an association between NRDCS and T cells53. However, NRDC as an important factor that clears membrane-tethered VTCN1 and leads to the shedding of soluble VTCN1 may play an important role in this process, and our analysis provides indirect evidence for these experiments.

  In order to clarify the regulatory mechanisms of these diagnostic genes and potential drugs, we performed protein co-expression network, transcription factor, and miRNA prediction, and predicted five targeted small molecule drugs based on the data expression matrix. At the same time, we used molecular docking to test the binding degree between the ligand and the receptor. Protein co-expression network analysis analysed the proteins that co-localized with diagnostic genes and interacted physically and genetically. These proteins were not only involved in mitochondrial sulfur metabolism, but also widely involved in oxidative phosphorylation, apoptosis, MAPK signaling pathway, VEGF signaling pathway, and other processes closely related to IS54. In the prediction of transcription factors and miRNAs, we found that POLR2A, BCL3, FOXA2, NFE2, BRD4, EP300, SPI1, MYC, ZBTB48, NR3C1, CEBPB, and AR were involved in TST, SQOR, and were involved in the prediction of TST. In the miRNA prediction, has-miR-1972 was involved in the co-regulation of NRDC and SQOR, suggesting that these transcription factors and miRNAs may play common biological functions through target genes. Finally, we predict the 5 kinds of targeted small molecule drugs, respectively butein, MK. 886, w. 13, mercaptopurine and MS. 275. Among them, W.13 had the best binding performance with the three diagnostic genes. W.13, also known as 7 “-O-Methylsciadopitysin, is a small molecule compound present in Cryptomeria japonica and Dacrydium cupressinum. However, little other information about this compound is available. More studies are needed to investigate the effects of W.13 on mitochondrial function and its role in IS.

  Despite the proposed new IS diagnostic model, there are some limitations to this study. First, we selected GSE58294 from patients with cardiogenic stroke, considering that cardiogenic cerebral artery embolism IS one of the most important practical causes of IS occurrence55. Secondly, this analysis is a secondary mining and analysis based on previous data sets, and a large number of experimental and clinical studies are still needed to verify the performance of the model. In addition, despite data pooling, the sample size between IS patients and healthy controls is still small, and it is necessary to expand the participant cohort in future studies. Finally, due to individual heterogeneity, IS patients and healthy controls do not all show the same performance in mitochondrial dysfunction, and future studies will include subgroups based on more clinical characteristics for analysis.

  In summary, this study proposed the combination of TST, SQOR, and NRDC as a diagnostic model for IS, and predicted potential regulatory mechanisms and drugs based on this model. This study provides a new perspective on the potential pathogenesis of IS, and provides new ideas for the selection and development of IS drugs.


## Conclusion
  Our findings identified TST, SQOR, and NRDC as the key MRGs in IS, and animal models were constructed to validate the expression of these genes. We targeted these key genes for immune association analysis, and constructed protein co-expression networks, transcription factors, miRNA, and drug prediction, among which W.13 was the best binding small molecule compound. We will expand the IS cohort and further investigate these genes in the future. This study provides additional evidence for potential biomarkers related to energy metabolism, immune regulation, and treatment of IS.
