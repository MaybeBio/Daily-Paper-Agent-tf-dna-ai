## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, results, discussion, methods, conclusions)
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, experimental design, data interpretation, and claims as presented in the manuscript
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report that noradrenaline (NA) synergistically enhances Porphyromonas gingivalis (Pg) lipopolysaccharide (LPS) and outer membrane vesicle (OMV)-induced IL-1β production in BV-2 microglia, and propose that this synergy occurs through distinct mechanisms: for Pg LPS, via NF-κB p65 and AP-1 c-Fos cross-coupling; for OMVs, via a TLR9/p52/RelB-mediated pathway potentially enhanced by Aβ2R/Epac signaling.
- **Visible evidence base** Figures 1–8 (reported in text), methods sections 4.1–4.10, statistical analyses
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Raw data files, full figure panels with statistical annotations, uncropped immunoblot images, AlphaFold model confidence metrics (pLDDT, PAE), and detailed experimental replicates are not provided

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript addresses a potentially interesting question regarding the interaction between stress-related noradrenergic signaling and Pg virulence factors in microglial IL-1β production. The core observation of synergistic enhancement is clearly presented and supported by dose-response and time-course data. However, the mechanistic claims, particularly the proposed cross-coupling of NF-κB p65 and AP-1 c-Fos, and the differential pathway usage between LPS and OMVs, are insufficiently supported by the evidence provided. The co-immunoprecipitation data are presented without adequate controls or quantification, and the AlphaFold modeling is used in a speculative manner without experimental validation. The manuscript would benefit from additional experiments to directly test the proposed mechanisms and from a more cautious interpretation of the structural predictions.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying neuroinflammation, microglial biology, periodontitis-systemic disease links, and stress-immune interactions would find these results relevant. The potential connection between stress, noradrenaline, and periodontal pathogen-driven neuroinflammation could interest those working on Alzheimer's disease pathogenesis and depression-associated inflammation.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses a clinically relevant question with potential implications for understanding stress-related exacerbation of neuroinflammation. The dose-response and time-course experiments are well designed. The use of both Pg LPS and OMVs allows comparison of different virulence factor preparations. The pharmacological inhibitor approach is systematic.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Mechanistic claim
  - **Claim pointer** "Co-immunoprecipitation combined with Western blotting and the structural models generated by AlphaFold2 suggested that cross-coupling of NF-κB p65 and AP-1 c-Fos transcription factors enhances the binding of NF-κB p65 to the IκB site, resulting in the synergistic augmentation of the IL-1β promoter activity."
  - **Evidence pointer** Section 2.6, Figure 7; Section 2.7, Figure 8
  - **Concern** The co-immunoprecipitation data are presented without proper controls (e.g., no input controls, no IgG control for the c-Fos blot, no quantification of the interaction). The authors acknowledge the c-Fos antibody detects multiple bands, but the interpretation of a specific p65-c-Fos interaction is not convincingly established. The AlphaFold2 model is used to support a direct interaction, but no experimental validation (e.g., mutagenesis, competition assays) is provided. The claim that c-Fos binding "enhances the binding of NF-κB p65 to the IκB site" is not directly tested; the authors only show increased promoter activity, which could be explained by other mechanisms.
  - **Why it matters** The proposed cross-coupling mechanism is central to the manuscript's claim of a distinct synergistic mechanism for Pg LPS. Without rigorous evidence for the physical interaction and its functional consequence, the mechanistic conclusion is not established.
  - **Resolution test** Provide quantified co-immunoprecipitation data with appropriate controls (input, IgG, and knockout/knockdown of c-Fos or p65). Perform electrophoretic mobility shift assays or chromatin immunoprecipitation to directly test whether c-Fos enhances p65 binding to the κB site. Validate the AlphaFold model with mutagenesis of predicted interface residues and functional assays.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Mechanistic claim
  - **Claim pointer** "The Aβ2R/Epac-mediated pathway, which promotes phagosome maturation, may be responsible for the synergistic effect of NA on the OMV-induced production of IL-1β in BV-2 microglia."
  - **Evidence pointer** Section 2.5, Figure 6E,F
  - **Concern** The claim that the Aβ2R/Epac pathway promotes phagosome maturation is not directly tested. The authors show that ICI-118551 (Aβ2R inhibitor) suppresses the synergistic effect, and that TLR9 and p52 inhibitors suppress OMV-induced activity, but no experiments directly link Epac signaling to phagosome maturation. The statement is speculative and not supported by the data presented.
  - **Why it matters** The differential mechanism claim for OMVs versus LPS rests on this proposed pathway. Without direct evidence for phagosome maturation enhancement, the mechanistic distinction is not established.
  - **Resolution test** Directly measure phagosome maturation (e.g., using fluorescently labeled OMVs and lysosomal markers) in the presence and absence of NA or Epac agonists/antagonists. Alternatively, use Epac-specific inhibitors or siRNA to demonstrate the requirement for Epac in the synergistic effect.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Data interpretation
  - **Claim pointer** "NA (30–1000 nM) significantly enhanced the mRNA level, promoter activity, and protein level of IL-1β up to 20-fold in BV-2 microglia following treatment with Pg LPS (10 μg/mL) and OMVs (150 μg of protein/mL) in a dose-dependent manner."
  - **Evidence pointer** Sections 2.1–2.3, Figures 1–3
  - **Concern** The fold-enhancement values are reported inconsistently across the manuscript. The abstract states "up to 20-fold" for both LPS and OMVs, but Section 2.1 reports 9.0-fold for mRNA and Section 2.2 reports 22.3-fold and 29.7-fold for promoter activity. The relationship between these different measurements (mRNA, promoter activity, protein) is not clearly reconciled. Additionally, the protein data in Figure 3C,D are presented as representative blots without quantification across replicates.
  - **Why it matters** The magnitude of the synergistic effect is a key finding, and inconsistent reporting undermines confidence in the quantitative claims.
  - **Resolution test** Provide a consistent summary of fold-enhancement values across all assays, with clear definitions of what is being compared. Include quantification of immunoblots with statistical analysis across at least three independent experiments.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M4
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Experimental model
  - **Claim pointer** "The present findings highlight that NA synergistically enhances the production of IL-1β in response to Pg LPS and OMVs through distinct mechanisms."
  - **Evidence pointer** Sections 2.1–2.7, Figures 1–8
  - **Concern** All experiments were performed in a single cell line (BV-2 microglia). The authors acknowledge this limitation in the Discussion, but the claim of a general mechanism for microglial responses would be strengthened by validation in primary microglia or in vivo models. Additionally, the concentrations of Pg LPS (10–30 μg/mL) and OMVs (150 μg protein/mL) are high, and the physiological relevance of these concentrations is not discussed.
  - **Why it matters** The translational relevance of the findings depends on whether the observed effects occur at physiologically relevant concentrations and in more representative cell types.
  - **Resolution test** Repeat key experiments in primary microglia cultures. Perform dose-response experiments with lower concentrations of Pg LPS and OMVs to determine the minimum effective concentrations. If possible, include an in vivo model of stress and Pg infection.

- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** The co-immunoprecipitation data lack proper controls and quantification (R1-M1). The link between Epac signaling and phagosome maturation is not experimentally tested (R1-M2). The AlphaFold model is presented without confidence metrics or experimental validation (R1-M1). The fold-enhancement values are reported inconsistently across assays (R1-M3).
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - Originality: The concept of NA synergizing with Pg virulence factors in microglia is relatively novel, though the individual components (NA effects on microglia, Pg LPS/OMV effects) have been studied. The proposed differential mechanisms add a layer of novelty, but the evidence is not yet convincing.
  - Scientific importance: The potential link between stress, noradrenaline, and periodontal pathogen-driven neuroinflammation is clinically relevant, particularly for Alzheimer's disease. However, the study is limited to in vitro experiments in a single cell line, which limits the immediate impact.
  - Interdisciplinary readership: The topic bridges microbiology, neuroimmunology, and stress biology, which could attract readers from multiple fields. However, the mechanistic depth is currently insufficient to appeal to a broad audience.
  - Technical soundness: The pharmacological inhibitor approach is reasonable, but the lack of proper controls in the co-immunoprecipitation experiments and the speculative use of AlphaFold modeling weaken the technical rigor.
  - Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is generally well-written and accessible, though some sections (e.g., the AlphaFold modeling) may be challenging for readers without structural biology background.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The core observation of synergistic enhancement is potentially interesting, but the mechanistic claims require substantial additional evidence. The authors should provide rigorous co-immunoprecipitation data with controls and quantification, directly test the proposed Epac-phagosome maturation link, and either validate the AlphaFold predictions experimentally or present them with appropriate caveats.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that c-Fos binding to p65 enhances p65 binding to the IκB site is not directly tested and is only inferred from inhibitor studies and a speculative structural model.
- The claim that the Aβ2R/Epac pathway promotes phagosome maturation is not experimentally supported.
- The statement that "this is the first report" of NA synergizing with Pg virulence factors is difficult to verify and should be softened or supported with a more comprehensive literature search.
- The physiological relevance of the concentrations used (Pg LPS 10–30 μg/mL, OMVs 150 μg protein/mL) is not discussed.
- The claim that the synergistic effect is "up to 20-fold" is inconsistently reported across different assays and should be reconciled.
