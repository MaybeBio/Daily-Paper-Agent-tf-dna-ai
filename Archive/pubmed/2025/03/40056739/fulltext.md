NPR1 (Nonexpressor pathogenesis-related genes 1) is regulated by multisite phosphorylation and SUMOylation, serving as a master switch for effector-triggered plant immunity through a transcriptional activator (TGA3) and repressor (WRKY70) are experimentally well studied. However, the conformational relationship between the various phosphorylation, un-phosphorylation states, and SUMOylation's role in the functional switch remains unclear. Using deep learning-based molecular modeling, docking, and multi-nanosecond simulations (totaling 2 mus) with end-state free energy calculations, we unveil how different phosphorylation states impact the dynamic stability of NPR1's four phospho-serine residues (Ser11, Ser15, Ser55, & Ser59) and binding of the TGA3-WRKY70 over SUMOylation. Results from our simulations show that the salicylic-acid induced (P-Ser11/15)NPR1-SUMO3 stabilizes helices and the flexible activation loop ((alpha22)Lys423 - (alpha1)Arg50 & (L35)Asp467-Arg51alpha(5)1, and Gly27(L3)), thereby switching association with TGA3. The inter-helix salt-bridge formed ((L10)Arg99-Glu323(alpha9) and (alpha14)Glu280-Pro264(L6)) between the phosphorylated NPR1-SUMO3-TGA3 engage in tight control of conformational regulation were disengaged in the unphosphorylated system. The (P-Ser55/59)NPR1-SUMO3-WRKY70 reorients itself and forms an electrostatic and hydrogen bond with Lys145(alpha7) - (L2)Asp26, (L6)Arg99 - Leu293(L18) and Lys262(L15) - Glu241(L15), (alpha13)Val239 (alpha3(10)), & (L17)Leu267 keeps complex stable and quiescent compare to unphosphorylated NPR1-WRKY70. Subsequently, the essential dynamic and secondary structural analysis reveals that the phosphorylation inhibits the alpha5(16) (long helix) formation and reduces the communication space between the 460(alpha25-betaturn3-alpha30-L42)590 (NPR1) and 90(L9-L10)107 (SUMO3), making the binding more suitable for TGA3 (260(betaturn-L6)270) and WRKY70 (230(L15-L16)265) via activation loop. These findings, which reveal the atomic and structural details of the NPR1's post-translational modification, will illuminate future investigations into enhancing plant immunity.
