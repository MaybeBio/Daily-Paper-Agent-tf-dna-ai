## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text including abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, and figure legends
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, experimental design, data interpretation, and claims as presented in the provided manuscript text
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors propose that Connexin46 (Cx46), traditionally a gap junction protein, localizes to the nucleus of cancer cells and acts as a transcriptional modulator. They report Cx46 mRNA upregulation across multiple tumor types, enhanced proliferation, invasion, and stemness in Cx46-expressing HeLa cells, nuclear localization of Cx46, ChIP-seq evidence of DNA binding at promoter regions, and a 30 kDa isoform (GJA3-30k) generated by alternative translation that may form DNA-binding dimers resembling RAD50.
- **Visible evidence base** Results sections describing TCGA expression analysis (Fig. 1), HeLa cell functional assays (Fig. 2), confocal microscopy and Western blot (Fig. 3), ChIP-seq and RNA-seq data (Figs. 4 and 5), mutagenesis and molecular dynamics simulations (Fig. 6), and multi-cell line nuclear detection (Fig. 7). Methods sections for cell culture, transfection, protein purification, Western blot, immunofluorescence, ChIP-seq, RNA-seq, and computational modeling.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Raw ChIP-seq and RNA-seq datasets, peak calling statistics, quality control metrics, replicate information, statistical details for functional assays, uncropped Western blots, antibody validation data, and the supplementary figures referenced but not provided in the text

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript addresses an interesting and potentially novel role for Cx46 in cancer cell nuclei. The combination of functional assays, localization studies, and genomic approaches is commendable. However, several technical concerns and interpretive gaps currently weaken the central claim that Cx46 acts as a direct transcriptional modulator. The evidence for direct DNA binding is indirect, the ChIP-seq analysis lacks critical quality metrics, and the molecular dynamics simulations are presented without sufficient validation. The manuscript would benefit from additional experiments to strengthen the mechanistic link between nuclear Cx46 and transcriptional regulation.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying connexin biology, particularly non-canonical functions of gap junction proteins. Cancer biologists interested in novel transcriptional regulators and therapeutic targets. The potential tissue-restricted expression of Cx46 makes it an attractive candidate for targeted therapy, which would interest translational researchers. The proposed alternative translation mechanism and DNA-binding dimer structure would also appeal to structural biologists and those studying protein-DNA interactions.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses a gap in understanding Cx46 function beyond its canonical channel role. The use of multiple complementary approaches (TCGA data, functional assays, localization studies, ChIP-seq, RNA-seq, and computational modeling) provides a broad perspective. The inclusion of both gain-of-function (HeLa) and loss-of-function (Sk-Mel-2) experiments strengthens the phenotypic observations. The identification of a specific 30 kDa isoform and its potential DNA-binding properties offers a testable mechanistic hypothesis.
- **Major Concerns** See detailed items below.
- **Minor Comments** See detailed items below.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 (ChIP-seq quality and controls), R1-M2 (direct DNA binding evidence), R1-M3 (molecular dynamics simulation validation), R1-M4 (Western blot quantification and controls)
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality is moderate to high, as the nuclear role of Cx46 as a transcriptional modulator has not been previously reported. Scientific importance is potentially significant given the cancer relevance and tissue-restricted expression. Interdisciplinary readership is plausible, spanning cancer biology, connexin research, and transcriptional regulation. Technical soundness is currently insufficient due to the concerns detailed below. Readability for nonspecialists is adequate, though some sections assume familiarity with genomic and computational methods.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The phenotypic observations are interesting, but the central mechanistic claim of transcriptional modulation requires stronger experimental support. Supportive if technical concerns are resolved.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Technical soundness of ChIP-seq
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that ChIP-seq experiments revealed Cx46 binding to DNA at intergenic and promoter regions, leading to activation of oncogenic pathways.
- **Evidence pointer** Results section "Cx46 binds to DNA, primarily in promoter regions", Fig. 4
- **Concern** The ChIP-seq experiment is described using an antibody against the C-terminal region of Cx46, but no validation of antibody specificity for ChIP is provided. The manuscript lacks critical quality metrics including the number of biological replicates, peak counts, false discovery rates, input normalization details, and enrichment scores. The claim that 47.29% of precipitated fragments correspond to promoter regions is presented without statistical context or comparison to background. Additionally, the ChIP-seq was performed only in HeLa cells overexpressing Cx46, and no endogenous ChIP-seq or control experiment with a non-specific antibody is described.
- **Why it matters** ChIP-seq is prone to artifacts from antibody cross-reactivity and non-specific pull-down. Without proper controls and quality metrics, the reported DNA binding could reflect technical noise rather than genuine Cx46-DNA interactions. This is the central evidence for the transcriptional modulator claim, so its reliability is critical.
- **Resolution test** Provide ChIP-seq quality metrics (number of replicates, peak counts, FDR thresholds, FRiP scores), validate the antibody by ChIP-Western or knockdown controls, include input and IgG controls in the analysis, and demonstrate that peaks are reproducible across replicates. Ideally, perform ChIP-seq in cells with endogenous Cx46 expression and in Cx46-knockdown cells to confirm specificity.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Direct DNA binding evidence
- **Claim** The authors claim that Cx46 binds to DNA and functions as a transcriptional regulator.
- **Evidence pointer** Results sections "Cx46 binds to DNA, primarily in promoter regions" and "Cx46 functions as both an enhancer and a repressor of genes linked to cancer aggressiveness", Figs. 4 and 5
- **Concern** The manuscript does not provide direct biochemical evidence for Cx46-DNA interaction, such as electrophoretic mobility shift assays, DNA pull-down, or surface plasmon resonance. The ChIP-seq data, even if technically sound, only demonstrates that Cx46 is crosslinked to DNA, which could occur indirectly through protein-protein interactions with other DNA-binding factors. The molecular dynamics simulations are purely computational and do not experimentally validate DNA binding. The claim that Cx46 acts as a transcriptional regulator is further weakened by the lack of reporter assays or targeted gene expression experiments demonstrating direct transcriptional control.
- **Why it matters** The central novelty of the manuscript is the proposal that Cx46 directly modulates transcription. Without direct biochemical evidence of DNA binding and functional demonstration of transcriptional regulation, the claim remains speculative.
- **Resolution test** Perform in vitro DNA binding assays with purified Cx46 or GJA3-30k protein, use reporter constructs driven by Cx46-bound promoters to demonstrate transcriptional modulation, and show that mutations in the proposed DNA-binding region abolish both binding and transcriptional effects.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Molecular dynamics simulation validity
- **Claim** The authors claim that molecular dynamics simulations suggest GJA3-30k dimerizes in a RAD50-like structure, forming stable DNA complexes.
- **Evidence pointer** Results section "Alternative translation produces the 30 kDa GJA3-30k isoform, potentially involved in DNA interactions", Fig. 6
- **Concern** The molecular dynamics simulations are described in minimal detail. The methods for model construction, force field parameters, simulation length, and analysis are not fully described. The claim of structural similarity to RAD50 is based on the Dali server, but the statistical significance of this similarity (Z-score) is not reported. The simulation was performed for 500 ns, but convergence is only assessed via RMSD, which is insufficient. No experimental validation of the predicted dimer structure or DNA-binding interface is provided.
- **Why it matters** Computational predictions require rigorous validation and clear methodological reporting to be interpretable. Without these, the proposed structural model remains speculative and cannot support the mechanistic claims.
- **Resolution test** Provide full simulation parameters, report Dali Z-scores and structural alignment statistics, show multiple independent simulations with convergence analysis, and experimentally validate the predicted dimerization and DNA binding through mutagenesis and biochemical assays.

- **Concern ID** R1-M4
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** No
- **Axis** Western blot quantification and controls
- **Claim** The authors claim that Cx46 is detected in nuclear fractions of multiple cancer cell lines, with a 30 kDa isoform present in melanoma cells.
- **Evidence pointer** Results section "Both Cx46 and the GJA3-30k isoform are present in the nuclei of prostate, melanoma and breast cancer cell lines", Fig. 7
- **Concern** The Western blot data in Fig. 7 is described qualitatively. No quantification of band intensities is provided, and the statement that GJA3-30k is "clearly present" in melanoma but "we cannot discharge low levels in the other cell lines" is vague. The purity of nuclear fractions is assessed using Lamin B1, but no cytoplasmic marker contamination assessment is shown for the nuclear fractions. The antibody used detects multiple bands, and no peptide competition or knockdown control is shown to confirm specificity.
- **Why it matters** The nuclear localization of Cx46 and its isoforms is a key observation. Without rigorous Western blot quantification and fraction purity controls, the conclusion that Cx46 is present in the nucleus across multiple cell lines is not firmly established.
- **Resolution test** Provide densitometric quantification of bands, show cytoplasmic marker blots for nuclear fractions, include a Cx46-knockdown or peptide competition control to confirm antibody specificity, and present data from at least three independent biological replicates.

### Minor Comments

- **Concern ID** R1-m1
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Statistical reporting
- **Affected element** Functional assays in Fig. 2
- **Evidence pointer** Results section "The presence of Cx46 enhances proliferation, invasion and self-renewal capacity in HeLa cells", Fig. 2
- **Issue** The statistical tests used for the proliferation, invasion, colony formation, and spheroid assays are not described in the methods or figure legends. The number of independent experiments and replicates is not consistently reported.
- **Required correction** Specify the statistical tests used, report the number of independent experiments and technical replicates, and include effect sizes or confidence intervals where appropriate.

- **Concern ID** R1-m2
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Data availability
- **Affected element** ChIP-seq and RNA-seq datasets
- **Evidence pointer** Methods sections "DNA sequencing" and "RNA sequencing"
- **Issue** The manuscript does not state where the ChIP-seq and RNA-seq data have been deposited. Accession numbers for public repositories are not provided.
- **Required correction** Deposit the raw and processed sequencing data in a public repository such as GEO or SRA and provide accession numbers in the manuscript.

- **Required correction** Expand figure legends to include sample sizes, definition of error bars, statistical tests used, and any normalization procedures.

- **Axis** Language and clarity

- **Issue** The sentence "we van not discharge low levels in the other cell lines" contains a typographical error and unclear phrasing.

- **Concern ID** R1-m5
- **Severity** Minor

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that Cx46 acts as a direct transcriptional regulator is not supported by direct biochemical evidence of DNA binding or functional transcriptional assays.
- The claim that GJA3-30k forms a RAD50-like dimer that binds DNA is based solely on computational predictions without experimental validation.
- The claim that Cx46 binding to promoter regions leads to activation of oncogenic pathways is inferred from ChIP-seq and RNA-seq correlation but lacks causal demonstration.
- The statement that Cx46 is upregulated in multiple tumor types relies on TCGA data analysis without clear description of the statistical methods or multiple testing corrections.
- The suggestion that nuclear Cx46 localization correlates with aggressive phenotype is speculative and not directly tested in patient samples.
- The claim that Cx46 may serve as a therapeutic target with minimal side effects due to restricted expression is premature without in vivo validation.
