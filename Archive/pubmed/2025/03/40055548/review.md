## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text, including abstract, introduction, results, discussion, methods, and supplementary figure/table references
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, technical soundness, structural and functional claims, methodological rigor, and consistency with the stated conclusions
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report crystal structures of the monomeric Type IIS restriction endonuclease BsmI in complex with cognate DNA, including an inactive mutant (N0.BsmI), a top-strand nicking variant (Nt*.BsmI), a bottom-strand nicking variant (Nb.BsmI), and the wild-type enzyme. They propose a sequential cleavage mechanism in which bottom-strand cleavage precedes top-strand cleavage, governed by a mobile "insulator loop" that controls active site accessibility. They also report that AlphaFold3 fails to correctly predict the protein-DNA complex.
- **Visible evidence base** X-ray crystallographic structures (four deposited PDB entries), analytical ultracentrifugation, activity assays with fluorescently labeled DNA probes, phosphorothioate stereospecificity experiments, normal mode analysis, sequence conservation analysis, and structural comparisons
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Supplementary figures and tables referenced in the text (Figures S1, S3, S4, S5, S6, S7, S8; Tables S1, S2, S3) were not provided. Supplementary Movies SM1 and SM2 were not provided. The reporting summary was not provided. Raw gel images and detailed refinement validation metrics (e.g., Ramachandran statistics per residue, ligand geometry validation) were not available for independent assessment.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a substantial structural and functional characterization of BsmI, a monomeric Type IIS restriction enzyme with two active sites. The crystallographic work appears technically sound, with four structures at reasonable resolutions, and the functional assays support the proposed sequential cleavage model. The identification of the insulator loop as a potential regulatory element is interesting and potentially significant. However, several concerns limit the strength of the conclusions as currently presented. The mechanistic model relies heavily on comparisons between structures obtained under different conditions (different ions, different mutants), and the causal role of the insulator loop in gating sequential cleavage is inferred rather than directly demonstrated. The AlphaFold3 comparison, while potentially useful, is presented in a limited manner. The manuscript would benefit from additional functional validation of the proposed mechanism and clearer presentation of some structural analyses.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in the fields of restriction-modification systems, DNA-protein interactions, enzyme mechanism, and protein engineering would find this work relevant. The structural characterization of a monomeric Type IIS enzyme with two active sites addresses a gap in the field, and the proposed mechanism of coordinated sequential cleavage has implications for understanding how such enzymes achieve specificity and control. The demonstration that AlphaFold3 fails on this protein-DNA complex is also of interest to the structural biology community, particularly those using predictive methods. Biotechnologists working with nicking enzymes or seeking to engineer restriction enzymes with altered specificity would also benefit from the detailed structural information.
- **Major strengths** The manuscript provides the first high-resolution structures of a monomeric Type IIS restriction enzyme with two active sites, filling a clear gap in the field. The combination of structural, biochemical, and biophysical data is commendable. The identification of the insulator loop and its potential role in gating sequential cleavage is a novel and interesting finding. The functional assays, including the use of pre-nicked substrates and phosphorothioate stereospecificity, are well designed to test the proposed mechanism. The authors are appropriately cautious in some interpretations, such as the caveat about the movie not necessarily representing an actual movement during catalysis.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Mechanistic claim support
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that the insulator loop "governs sequential active site accessibility" and that its movement is responsible for the ordered cleavage of the two DNA strands.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results sections "Crystal structures of fully active wt.BsmI and the position of the divalent metal ions" and "Structures of Nt*.BsmI and Nb.BsmI mutants give an insight on the cleavage mechanism"; Figures 4 and 8; Discussion
  - **Concern** The causal role of the insulator loop in gating sequential cleavage is inferred from static structures. The loop is in a "closed" position in the wt.BsmI structure and in a "retracted" position in the mutant structures. However, the wt.BsmI structure was obtained with Ca2+ while the N0.BsmI structure was obtained with Mg2+, and the Nt*.BsmI and Nb.BsmI structures were obtained with Ca2+ and Mg2+ respectively. This confounds the comparison: the observed conformational difference could be due to the different ions, the mutations, or a combination of both. The normal mode analysis provides a plausible model for how the loop could move, but it is not direct evidence of the mechanism. The functional data show that Nt*.BsmI is inactive on intact DNA but active on bottom-nicked DNA, which is consistent with the model, but does not directly demonstrate that the insulator loop is the structural element responsible for this behavior.
  - **Why it matters** The proposed mechanism is a central claim of the manuscript. If the conformational difference is due to the different ions or mutations rather than representing a physiologically relevant conformational change, the mechanistic model would be weakened. The manuscript would be strengthened by a more direct test of the role of the insulator loop, such as mutagenesis of loop residues followed by functional analysis.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should either (a) obtain a structure of wt.BsmI with Mg2+ (if feasible) to rule out ion-dependent conformational differences, (b) perform mutagenesis of insulator loop residues and test the effect on the order and efficiency of cleavage, or (c) provide additional evidence, such as solution-based studies (e.g., FRET, HDX-MS), that directly link loop movement to the cleavage mechanism. Alternatively, the authors could temper the claim and present the loop movement as a hypothesis rather than a demonstrated mechanism.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Structural interpretation and validation
  - **Claim pointer** The authors state that "the models predicted by Alphafold3 are incorrect" and that AlphaFold3 "fails to accurately capture the essence of the interaction specificity between BsmI and its substrate."
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "DNA recognition mechanism"; Figure S5 (not provided); Discussion
  - **Concern** The AlphaFold3 comparison is presented as a single statement with limited detail. The authors do not describe the parameters used for the AlphaFold3 prediction, the version of the software, the input sequences, or the criteria used to judge the prediction as "incorrect." The supplementary figure S5, which presumably shows the comparison, was not provided for assessment. Without this information, it is difficult to evaluate the validity of this claim. Furthermore, the statement that AlphaFold3 "could not predict any complex" when divalent ions were not included, but "the ions were correctly predicted but the DNA was positioned in the wrong orientation" when ions were included, is somewhat ambiguous. It is unclear what "correctly predicted" means for the ions if the overall complex is wrong.
  - **Why it matters** The claim about AlphaFold3 failure is presented as a notable finding, but it is not rigorously supported in the current text. If the authors wish to make this claim, they need to provide sufficient detail for readers to assess it. This is particularly important given the widespread use of AlphaFold3 and the potential impact of such a claim on the community.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should provide a detailed description of the AlphaFold3 prediction setup, including the version, input sequences, and any custom parameters. They should also provide a quantitative or at least clearly defined qualitative comparison between the predicted and experimental structures (e.g., RMSD values for the protein and DNA, contact maps, or a clear figure showing the incorrect placement of the DNA). If the claim is to be retained, it should be supported by this evidence.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Functional validation of the sequential mechanism
  - **Claim pointer** The authors propose a "sequential and concerted cleavage mechanism" in which bottom-strand cleavage is a prerequisite for top-strand cleavage, based on the activity of Nt*.BsmI on intact versus pre-nicked substrates.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "Activity tests on BsmI mutants reveal a sequential and concerted cleavage mechanism"; Figure 6
  - **Concern** The functional data are presented as a single gel image (Figure 6D-F) with limited quantification. The authors state that Nt*.BsmI is "completely inactive" on intact DNA, but no quantitative data (e.g., percent cleavage over time, kinetic parameters) are provided. The time point shown (10 min) may not capture the full time course of the reaction. Additionally, the authors do not test whether the top-strand cleavage by Nt*.BsmI on the pre-nicked substrate occurs at the correct position, which would be important to confirm that the observed activity is specific. The claim of a "concerted" mechanism implies a coordinated process, but the data presented are endpoint assays that do not demonstrate coordination beyond the order of events.
  - **Why it matters** The sequential mechanism is a key conclusion of the manuscript. The current data are consistent with the model but do not strongly establish it. A more detailed kinetic analysis would strengthen the claim and provide a more complete picture of the mechanism.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should provide time-course experiments for the cleavage of intact and pre-nicked substrates by the different enzyme variants, with quantification of cleavage products. They should also confirm the cleavage position on the pre-nicked substrate (e.g., by comparing product sizes to markers or by sequencing). If possible, single-turnover experiments would provide more definitive evidence for the order of events.
- **Minor Comments**
  - **Concern ID** R1-m1
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Clarity and completeness of methods
  - **Affected element** Activity assays
  - **Evidence pointer** Methods section "Activity assays"
  - **Issue** The methods for the activity assays are described briefly. The concentration of enzyme and DNA, the specific buffer conditions, and the method of quantification (e.g., how the gel images were analyzed) are not fully specified. The authors state that reactions contained 1 µM DNA and 1.5 µg/mL enzyme, but the stoichiometry and the specific activity of the enzyme are not discussed.
  - **Required correction** Provide more detail on the assay conditions, including the enzyme-to-substrate ratio, the reaction volume, the method of stopping the reaction, and how the cleavage products were quantified. If possible, include a representative time course or at least a statement about the reproducibility of the results across replicates.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m2
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Presentation of structural data
  - **Affected element** Figure 3 and Table 1
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "Crystal structures of fully active wt.BsmI and the position of the divalent metal ions"; Figure 3; Table 1
  - **Issue** The resolution of the wt.BsmI structure is 2.85 Å, which is relatively modest. The authors state that the electron density for the ions is clear, but the B-factors for the ions and the coordination geometry are not discussed in detail. The figure legend for Figure 3 mentions "electron density was contoured at 1 sigma," but it is unclear which map (2Fo-Fc or Fo-Fc) is shown and for which ions.
  - **Required correction** Clarify which electron density map is shown in Figure 3 and at what contour level. Provide B-factors for the ions and discuss the coordination geometry in the text. If the wt.BsmI structure is at lower resolution, acknowledge the limitations in interpreting ion occupancy and coordination.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m3
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Terminology and consistency
  - **Affected element** Nomenclature of mutants
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "Design of the inactive mutant N0.BsmI and structure of the complex with its cognate DNA"; Figure 6
  - **Issue** The manuscript uses several names for the mutants: N0.BsmI, Nt*.BsmI, Nb.BsmI, and wt.BsmI. The naming convention is not immediately intuitive, and the asterisk in Nt*.BsmI is not explained. The authors should define these names clearly at first use and ensure consistent usage throughout.
  - **Required correction** Add a brief explanation of the naming scheme (e.g., Nt for top-strand nicking, Nb for bottom-strand nicking, N0 for no nicking) and explain the asterisk in Nt*.BsmI. Ensure that the names are used consistently in the text, figures, and tables.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m4
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Discussion of limitations
  - **Affected element** Discussion section
  - **Evidence pointer** Discussion
  - **Issue** The discussion does not explicitly address the limitations of the study, such as the use of Ca2+ as a surrogate for Mg2+, the moderate resolution of the wt.BsmI structure, and the lack of direct evidence for the dynamic behavior of the insulator loop.
  - **Required correction** Add a paragraph acknowledging these limitations and discussing how they might affect the interpretation of the results. This would strengthen the manuscript by providing a balanced view of the findings.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** The primary technical concern is the confounding of ion identity and mutation status in the structural comparisons that support the insulator loop mechanism. The wt.BsmI structure was obtained with Ca2+ while the N0.BsmI structure was obtained with Mg2+, and the Nt*.BsmI and Nb.BsmI structures were obtained with Ca2+ and Mg2+ respectively. This makes it difficult to attribute the observed conformational differences specifically to the mutations or to the loop dynamics. Additionally, the lack of detailed kinetic data for the cleavage assays limits the strength of the sequential mechanism claim. The AlphaFold3 comparison is presented without sufficient methodological detail to be properly evaluated.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**  
  *Originality*: The structural characterization of a monomeric Type IIS restriction enzyme with two active sites is novel and addresses a gap in the field. The identification of the insulator loop as a potential regulatory element is an original contribution.  
  *Scientific importance*: The findings have implications for understanding the mechanism of Type IIS restriction enzymes and for the engineering of nicking enzymes. The work is likely to be of interest to a specialized but significant community.  
  *Interdisciplinary readership*: The manuscript is primarily of interest to structural biologists, enzymologists, and protein engineers. The broader significance to a general biological audience is limited, though the AlphaFold3 comparison may attract attention from computational biologists.  
  *Technical soundness*: The crystallographic work appears technically sound, with reasonable resolutions and R-factors. The functional assays are appropriate but would benefit from more detailed kinetic analysis. The normal mode analysis is a useful complement but is not a substitute for experimental validation of dynamics.  
  *Readability for nonspecialists*: The manuscript is generally well-written and accessible, though some sections (e.g., the detailed structural comparisons) may be challenging for nonspecialists. The abstract and introduction are clear, and the figures are informative.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The manuscript presents a valuable structural and functional characterization of BsmI, but the central mechanistic claim regarding the insulator loop requires stronger support. The confounding of ion identity and mutation status in the structural comparisons should be addressed, and the functional data should be strengthened with quantitative kinetic analysis. The AlphaFold3 comparison should be either substantiated with more detail or tempered.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that the insulator loop "governs sequential active site accessibility" is not directly supported by the data, as the conformational difference between wt.BsmI and the mutants could be due to the different ions used in crystallization.
- The claim that AlphaFold3 "fails to accurately capture the essence of the interaction specificity" is not sufficiently supported, as the details of the prediction and comparison are not provided.
- The statement that the Nt*.BsmI is "completely inactive" on intact DNA is based on a single endpoint assay without quantification or time-course analysis.
- The claim of a "concerted" cleavage mechanism implies coordination beyond simple ordering, which is not demonstrated by the data.
- The suggestion that the crystals could be used for time-resolved crystallography is speculative and not supported by any preliminary data.
