# HHQG ameliorates acute liver injury (ALI) by inhibiting NLRP3 activation through RASD1-mediated regulation of the PKCδ-NF-κB signaling pathway


## Supplementary Information
  The online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-24410-z.


## Introduction
  Acute liver injury (ALI) is a common hepatic disease that poses a serious threat to life and health, and its incidence has gradually risen in recent years, thus becoming a crucial research focus in liver diseases. This disease typically manifests as extensive cellular necrosis and apoptosis, accompanied by severe inflammatory responses1,2. ALI can be triggered by many factors, including viral infections, drug abuse, and alcohol consumption3,4. According to statistics, the incidence and mortality rates of ALI vary significantly among different populations, which is closely related to epidemiological factors and patients’ underlying conditions. However, despite various treatment modalities available in clinical practice, the management of ALI faces certain limitations, including insufficient specificity and potential adverse effects5–7. This makes the identification of novel and effective therapeutic targets both a research hotspot and a significant challenge.

  Liver injury is often accompanied by intense inflammatory responses, and macrophages play an important role as key initiators and modulators of inflammatory signals in this process8. When drugs or their metabolites induce liver injury, macrophages are swiftly activated, releasing substantial inflammatory mediators (such as TNF-α and IL-6). These mediators not only damage hepatocytes directly but also recruit other immune cells to the site of injury, thus further amplifying the inflammatory response9. Also, macrophages can modulate the intensity and duration of inflammation by secreting anti-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-10, thereby maintaining hepatic homeostasis. These characteristics make macrophages an important cellular model for investigating ALI and its associated inflammatory responses.

  Within macrophages, the NLRP3 inflammasome works as a critical mechanism regulating inflammatory responses. NLRP3 can recognize different pathogen and cellular damage signals and promote the release of inflammatory mediators10, thus playing an important role in the progression of ALI. Upon activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome, the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines further contributes to the body’s immune modulation, which is essential for the occurrence and progression of pyroptosis and inflammation11–14. However, the specific role of NLRP3 in ALI and its relationship with existing therapeutic approaches still require extensive investigation.

  Currently, while various hepatoprotective agents have been employed in diagnostic and therapeutic practice, traditional indigenous medicines are particularly noteworthy and demonstrate unique therapeutic potential. The Mongolian medicine formula—Honghua Qinggan 13 Flavor Pills (HHQG)—has achieved remarkable therapeutic efficacy in treating various liver diseases, although its specific mechanisms remain to be investigated. Studies have shown that the hepatoprotective effects of HHQG may be associated with its antioxidant activity, inhibition of the JNK/c-Jun signaling pathway, and regulation of the FasL/Fas signaling pathway for cell apoptosis15. In addition, HHQG may exert further protective effects by regulating the balance between matrix metalloproteinase (MMP-1) and its inhibitor (TIMP-1), suppressing lipid peroxidation, and modulating liver fibrosis16. Preliminary investigations have indicated that HHQG significantly inhibited the infiltration of monocytes/macrophages following CCl4-induced liver injury17, demonstrating its potential to mitigate inflammatory responses.

  In recent years, research on the ras-related dexamethasone induced 1 (RASD1) has attracted increasing attention. Located on chromosome 17p11.2, RASD1 is an activator of G protein signaling that has been demonstrated to be associated with metabolic disorders such as insulin resistance and metabolic associated fatty liver disease (MAFLD)18,19. However, the precise role of RASD1 in liver injury, particularly its regulatory mechanism in NLRP3 inflammasome activation and subsequent cell death, remains unclear. Therefore, an in-depth investigation into how RASD1 promotes NLRP3-mediated pyroptosis, as well as its potential impact on ALI, will provide new insights for treating hepatic diseases.

  Given the pressing need to explore more effective therapeutic strategies, this study aims to systematically evaluate the impact of RASD1 on NLRP3-mediated inflammatory responses, which subsequently exacerbate liver injury. In addition, we employ a multifaceted approach combining network pharmacology, molecular docking, as well as cellular and animal experiments to thoroughly investigate the mechanisms underlying HHQG’s mechanism of action in this process. This research seeks to provide new theoretical foundations and potential clinical solutions for the prevention and treatment of ALI. This study is expected to offer fresh perspectives on the application of ethnic medicine in modern medical practice and pave the way for developing innovative hepatoprotective agents.

The formula for HHQG comprises 13 ingredients: safflower, cloves, lotus seeds, ophiopogon, costus root, chebulic myrobalan, Szechwan Chinaberry Fruit, gardenia, red sandalwood, musk, water buffalo horn, bezoar and vermilion. After processing, the medicinal materials are pulverized, sieved, and mixed, then musk is added for blending. The amount of powder is calculated according to the mold. The power is rolled into pills after moistening, then dried to 0.2 g per pill, completing the water-milled pill preparation. Finally, with the vermilion prepared, the pills are coated, polished, and stored.

## Methods

### Target prediction and network pharmacology analysis of chemical components in HHQG
  To evaluate the pharmacological effects of HHQG in treating liver injury and explore its potential targets, we employed network pharmacology analysis to predict targets for 747 chemical components isolated in our previous research (Wang et al., 2022). We then screened for potential targets associated with “liver injury” and “inflammation” to reveal its mechanism of action.


### Target gene screening
  In network pharmacology, accurately identifying disease-related target genes is fundamental for network construction and exploring potential mechanisms. This study employed a retrieval strategy combining different databases to ensure the comprehensiveness and reliability of target genes. Specifically, GeneCards, DrugBank, and OMIM were used to screen for potential target genes associated with liver injury and inflammation. In order to construct a complete and non-redundant dataset of target genes, these data were integrated and deduplicated. The obtained sets of target genes laid the foundation for subsequent analysis and contributed to understanding the mechanism of action of HHQG in treating these diseases.


### Screening of overlapping targets
  By combining the targets of HHQG with those related to liver injury and inflammation, we imported them into the software for analyzing the Venn diagram. The intersection analysis of the Venn diagram can help identify co-expressed hub targets between HHQG and liver injury and inflammation. These overlapping targets may represent important therapeutic targets for the effects of HHQG.


### Constructing the network of the drug, active components, and targets
  In order to investigate the interactions among active components, targets, and genes, Cytoscape 3.9 was used to construct a visualized network diagram. In addition, network characteristics were analyzed to identify key components related to liver injury and inflammation. The degree values of the components were ranked using the Network Analyzer function, and components with higher degree values are considered potential key active components.


### Molecular docking
  This study employed molecular docking to evaluate the binding affinity between active components in HHQG and targets. Based on the integration of the protein data bank (PDB) and ligand database (PubChem), analysis was conducted using PyMol, Chem3D, and Autodock. The crystal structure of RASD1 was selected from the PDB database, and ligands of key active components in HHQG were chosen from PubChem. PyMol was used for processing target proteins, while Chem3D was used to optimize the 3D structure of ligands, remove hydrogen atoms, and generate suitable file formats for docking. Subsequently, Autodock 4.4.6 was used for further preprocessing of targets, and AutoDock Vina was employed to calculate the binding energy between each target protein and ligand. The docking pose with the lowest binding energy and optimal mode was selected. Finally, PyMol was used to visually analyze the binding modes and interaction interfaces between ligands and target proteins, and ligands that could potentially serve as effective targets for treating liver injury and inflammation were selected.


### Animal grouping and modeling
  45 C57BL/6 mice (male, 6–8 weeks old, 20–22 g) were purchased from Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co., Ltd. (China). The license number is SCXK (Beijing) 2021–0006, and the certificate number of animal quality is 110,011,241,106,067,472. The mice were housed in the Animal Center of Inner Mongolia Medical University under conditions set at 23 ± 3 °C, humidity of 60 ± 10 RH%, air exchange rate of 40.1 ACH, pressure difference of 10 Pa, and a 12/12 light/dark cycle. This study was approved by the Animal Research Ethics Committee with the approval number 2022DW258, and animal care and handling followed the guidelines issued by the Ethics Committee of Inner Mongolia Medical University.

  After one week of acclimatization, the mice were randomly divided into control group, model group, and HHQG (treatment) group, with 15 mice in each group. The HHQG group was administered HHQG (0.6165 g/kg) by gavage once daily for 7 consecutive days 17. In addition, the control and model groups were given an equal volume of saline. Four hours after the gavage on the last day, the model group and HHQG group received a single intraperitoneal injection of CCl4 mixture (olive oil : CCl4 = 4:1) at a dose of 0.1 mL/(10 g·BW), while the control group received an intraperitoneal injection of the same dose of olive oil. On day 2, day 5, and day 7 after CCl4 injection, 5 mice from each group were euthanized, and samples were collected and stored at -80 °C. Before euthanasia is carried out, C57BL/6 mice are first anesthetized to ensure complete loss of consciousness. C57BL/6 mice are anesthetized by intraperitoneal injection of 1.25% tribromoethanol at a dose of 0.3 ml per 10 g of body weight. After confirming deep anesthesia, euthanasia is performed by cervical dislocation in accordance with the guidelines approved by the Ethics Committee of Inner Mongolia Medical University and international animal welfare standards, to minimize pain and discomfort.


### In vitro cell grouping and modeling
  RAW264.7 cells were cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum in an incubator (37 °C and 5% CO2). When the confluency reached over 80%, cells were digested and evenly plated. The control group received no treatment, the model group was treated with 1000 ng/ml LPS for 24 h, and the HHQG group was treated with 100 μg/ml HHQG for 12 h, followed by 1000 ng/ml LPS for 24 h. For siRNA and miRNA groups, knockdown and the transfections for overexpression were performed first. After 24 h, the CON siRNA and CON miRNA groups received no further treatment, the model siRNA and model miRNA groups were treated with 1000 ng/ml LPS for 24 h, and the HHQG siRNA and HHQG miRNA groups were first treated with 100 μg/ml HHQG for 12 h, and then followed by 1000 ng/ml LPS for 24 h. For all groups, supernatants were collected, and RNA was extracted 24 h after treatment, while proteins were extracted and preserved 48 h after treatment for analysis.


### RASD1 overexpression/constructing knockout system
  RAW264.7 macrophages were seeded in 6-well plates and cultured for 24 h at 37 °C with 5% CO₂. Following standard transfection protocols, cells were transfected with RASD1-specific siRNA or RASD1 overexpression plasmid.


### ALT and AST test and histopathological staining
  Mouse blood was centrifuged at 3,000 rpm for 15 min at 4 °C to obtain serum. Commercial AST/GOT activity kit and ALT/GPT activity kit were used to detect ALT and AST activity according to the instructions.

  Liver tissues were fixed overnight in 4% paraformaldehyde, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned at 4 μm. H&E staining was used to illustrate the degree of injury. Masson’s trichrome staining was used to demonstrate how severe collagen deposition is.


### Proteomic analysis
  Liver tissues from the control, model, and HHQG groups were collected and ground in liquid nitrogen to maintain sample stability. The ground samples were separately added to appropriate amounts of SDT lysis buffer (sodium dodecyl sulfate-detergent) and transferred to centrifuge tubes (EP tubes). Samples were treated in a boiling water bath for 3 min, then sonicated at 4 °C for 2 min using an ultrasonic disruptor. Centrifugation was performed at 16,000 g for 20 min, and the supernatant was collected for subsequent analysis. The BCA assay was used to determine the total protein concentration in each sample. Protein samples were sent to Shanghai Bioprofile for DIA proteomics. Spectronaut software was used to integrate mass spectrometry data, perform database searches, and conduct DIA quantitative analysis of proteins. The database used was Uniprot Mus musculus (Mouse)[10090]-87552 20,240,325.fasta, available at https://www.uniprot.org/10090. Differentially expressed proteins (DEPs) were determined based on the criterion of |FC|≥ 1.2. DAVID was used for GO and pathway analysis of DEPs, and the parameters were set to count = 2 and ease = 0.05. For continuous data, the test for homogeneity of variance was performed. If data met homogeneity of variance, ANOVA was used for comparison; if not, non-parametric tests were employed. Differences were considered significant if p < 0.05.


### Immunohistochemistry
  Liver tissues were fixed overnight in 4% paraformaldehyde, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned at 4 μm thickness. Sections were baked at 65 °C for over 2 h to enhance tissue adhesion. After rehydration, sections were washed three times with PBS buffer for 5 min each, then subjected to heat-induced antigen retrieval for 4 min. Subsequently, these sections underwent natural cooling at room temperature and were washed again three times with PBS buffer (as above). After antigen retrieval, endogenous peroxidase activity was blocked with 3% H2O2 for 15 min and followed by 3 PBS buffer washes (as above). After that, sections were blocked with 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA) at room temperature for 45 min to reduce non-specific binding. Primary antibodies against NLRP3 (1:200), GSDMD (1:500), RASD1 (1:200), and PKCδ (1:2000) were then applied and incubated overnight at 4 °C, followed by 3 PBS buffer washes (as above). Sections were incubated with a ready-to-use IHC kit for 15 min, followed by 3 PBS buffer washes (as above). After color development using a DAB substrate kit, sections were counterstained, mounted with neutral balsam, and observed and photographed under a microscope.


### RT-qPCR
  Total RNA was extracted from liver tissues and RAW264.7 cells using Trizol, then reverse transcribed to cDNA using the FastKing One-Step gDNA Removal and cDNA Synthesis Kit. PCR was performed using the FastReal qPCR kit and StepOnePlus Real-Time PCR Systems. All procedures followed the manufacturer’s instructions, and β-actin was used as the internal reference gene. Relative changes in gene expression were calculated using the 2 − ΔΔCT method. ### Western blotting
  Total protein was extracted from liver tissues and RAW264.7 cells, separated by SDS-PAGE, and transferred to PVDF membranes. Membranes were blocked with TBS solution containing 5% skimmed milk powder at room temperature for 90 min. Primary antibodies (i.e., NLRP3 1:200, GSDMD 1:500, RASD1 1:100, IκB-α 1:500, PKCδ 1:1000, and internal reference β-actin 1:1000) were diluted in TBS solution containing 1% skimmed milk powder. Membranes were incubated with diluted primary antibody solutions overnight at 4 °C. HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies were diluted (1:1000) in TBS solution containing 1% skimmed milk powder and incubated at room temperature for 1 h. Subsequently, DAB chromogen was used for color development.After color development was completed, the blot strips were photographed with a cell phone and subsequently image processed using Photoshop 24.0.0 software.


### CCK8 assay
  RAW264.7 cells were cultured in 96-well plates at 5,000 cells/well for 12 h and then treated with different concentrations of HHQG/LPS (10 μl) for 12 h/24 h. After that, CCK8 reagent (10 μl) was added, and OD values at 450 nm were measured after 4 h/2 h.


### ELISA assay
  Cell supernatants were collected, and commercial ELISA kits were used to test the expression of inflammatory factors IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α.


### Statistical analysis
  GraphPad Prism 9 (GraphPad Software, CA, USA) was used for correlation and statistical analysis. p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Data are presented as mean ± SD of three experimental replicates.


## Results

### Protective effects of HHQG on liver-injured mice
  Weight gain in the model group (with CCl4-induced injury) was significantly lower than in the control group (p < 0.01), while weight gain in the HHQG treatment group was significantly higher than that in the model group (p < 0.01) (Fig. 1A). Pathological analysis revealed significant damage to the liver lobule in the model group after 2 days accompanied by massive inflammatory cell infiltration and collagen deposition. As the injury progressed to day 5 and day 7, inflammation and collagen deposition were somewhat alleviated. The HHQG group significantly inhibited liver injury as early as day 2 after treatment, and liver injury essentially recovered by day 7, indicating no signs of inflammatory response (Figs. 1B, C). Serological analysis indicated that ALT and AST activity increased significantly 2 days after CCl4 treatment (p < 0.001), while HHQG significantly reduced these two indicators (p < 0.05). Although AST levels continued to rise in the model group 5 and 7 days post-injury, no significant changes were observed in the HHQG group, indicating that HHQG’s preventive effect was optimal in the early stages of liver injury (Figs. 1D, E). In addition, levels of inflammatory factors, such as CCL2, IL-6, and TNF-α, significantly increased 2 days after CCl4 injury (p < 0.01) and continued to increase at day 5. Although they decreased at day 7, CCL2 and TNF-α remained higher than the control group (p < 0.05).   RASD1 expression in the model group significantly increased on day 2 after injury and remained elevated through day 5 and day 7 (p < 0.001). In contrast, RASD1 expression in the HHQG group was significantly lower than the model group at the same time points (p < 0.05) and further decreased by day 7, suggesting that the repair of liver injury was near completion (Fig. 1I). These findings indicate that HHQG demonstrates significant preventive effects in the early stages of liver injury (day 2), and it possibly exerts its protective effects against liver injury by modulating inflammatory responses through RASD1.

#### Constructing RAW264.7 cell models with silenced and overexpressed RASD1
  To investigate the role of RASD1 in NLRP3 inflammasome and its mediated inflammatory response, we constructed RAW264.7 cell models with silenced and overexpressed RASD1. Target gene expression was determined by qPCR. Results indicated that in the RASD1-silencing group, RASD1 expression at site 149 was significantly reduced (p < 0.01), thus confirming this site as an effective gene-silencing target. Conversely, in the RASD1 overexpression group, RASD1 expression was significantly increased (Figs. 4D, E).

## Discussion
  This study systematically explored the mechanisms by which Mongolian medicine HHQG inhibits inflammation and alleviates liver injury, with a focus on analyzing the promoting effect of RASD1 on the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome and the inhibitory effect of HHQG on it. Through network pharmacology, the main active components of HHQG were screened, and 184 related targets were identified (among which 5 were candidate active components). Combining molecular docking and experimental verification, we revealed that HHQG exerts a liver—protecting effect by interfering with RASD1 and its downstream inflammatory pathways, providing theoretical and experimental bases for the modern research of ethnic medicines and the development of new liver-protecting drugs.

  Regarding the molecular mechanism, this study found that the expression of RASD1 (AGS1/Dexras1) was significantly up—regulated in liver injury and showed a significant positive linear correlation with the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome. Overexpression of RASD1 promoted NLRP3 activation and up—regulated the expression of genes related to pyroptosis, suggesting that RASD1 plays an important pro—inflammatory role in inflammatory signal transduction20,21. Correspondingly, HHQG could significantly down—regulate the expression of RASD1 and NLRP3, indicating that it may inhibit the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome by suppressing RASD1, thereby alleviating pyroptosis and exerting a liver—protecting effect22,23. The results of network pharmacology and molecular docking supported the possibility that the candidate components in HHQG could bind to RASD1, suggesting the possibility of direct interaction between components and targets. However, the specific contributions and synergistic mechanisms of each component still need further analysis.

  Previous studies have suggested that RASD1 can negatively regulate PKCδ by interfering with its autophosphorylation and affecting PKCδ activity through an isoprenoid—dependent membrane—localization mechanism18. The activation of PKCδ is closely related to the expression of pro—inflammatory genes mediated by NF—κB (such as TNF—α, IL—6, IL—1β) and has been confirmed to be involved in the pathological processes in both CCl4—induced liver injury and LPS—induced inflammation models24–26. This study showed that the mRNA levels of PKCδ and NF—κB were significantly increased on the 2nd, 5th, and 7th days after injury and reached a peak on the 7th day, suggesting that this pathway plays a key role in the progression of liver inflammation. We also found that RASD1 regulates the PKCδ—NF—κB signal: knockdown of RASD1 could reduce the expression of PKCδ and NF—κB, while overexpression enhanced their expression. HHQG could effectively inhibit the activation of PKCδ—NF—κB, further supporting the hypothesis that HHQG alleviates inflammation by regulating RASD1 and thus affecting the PKCδ—NF—κB axis.

  In addition, starting from Mongolian medical theory, this study attempted to integrate the traditional concepts of “holism” and "treatment based on syndrome differentiation" with modern molecular mechanisms. According to Mongolian medical theory, the liver is the main circulation channel of “Xila” and has hematopoietic functions. Liver diseases often manifest as "liver—heat syndrome". HHQG is mainly composed of safflower, combined with bezoar, gardenia, and myrobalan, etc. It is clinically used for clearing heat, soothing the liver, detoxifying, and regulating the balance of the “Three Roots”, emphasizing holistic treatment. Within this framework, this study further partially explained the “holistic” mechanism of action of HHQG from the perspective of RASD1 and NLRP3 inflammasome activation, supplementing and expanding the previous evidence on the protective mechanisms of HHQG, such as antioxidant effects, inhibition of JNK/c—Jun, regulation of the FasL/Fas and MMP—1/TIMP—1 balance, and inhibition of lipid peroxidation15, as well as our previous observation that HHQG inhibits CCl4—induced infiltration of monocytes/macrophages17.

  Although this study has obtained multi—faceted evidence on the mechanism, there are still several limitations. Firstly, HHQG is a multi—component compound, and the interactions among its components and their relative contributions to the RASD1/PKCδ/NLRP3 pathway are not yet clear. Secondly, this study is mainly based on animal/cell models, and the clinical relevance and dose—response relationship need to be verified in more clinical or translational studies. Finally, the precise molecular mechanism by which RASD1 regulates the inflammasome (such as whether it involves specific upstream receptors, ubiquitination, or phosphorylation modifications) still requires in—depth molecular biology research (including protein interactomics and signal transduction kinetics analysis).

  In conclusion, this study shows that HHQG may inhibit the activation of the PKCδ—NF—κB and NLRP3 inflammasomes by suppressing RASD1, thereby alleviating the inflammatory response and liver injury. This finding provides a molecular basis for the modern pharmacological research of Mongolian medical prescriptions and offers new ideas for liver—protecting strategies targeting RASD1. Future research should focus on analyzing the action spectrum and synergistic effects of monomer components, clarifying the molecular downstream mechanisms of RASD1, and conducting pre—clinical/clinical translational studies to evaluate its therapeutic potential.


## Conclusion
  Our research provides preliminary evidence suggesting that the Mongolian medicine compound HHQG can down-regulate RASD1 and the associated PKCδ-NF-κB signaling pathway in the experimental models used, accompanied by a reduction in NLRP3-related inflammatory responses and an improvement in liver injury phenotype.
