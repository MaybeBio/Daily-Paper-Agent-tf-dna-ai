## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a potentially useful structure-function analysis of RsbW, identifying three residues with proposed roles in dimerization, ligand binding, and kinase activity. However, the evidence base is entirely descriptive at the abstract level, with no quantitative data, statistical support, or methodological detail. The claim that these residues are "indispensable" is not supported by the visible evidence, which only suggests partial effects on some functions. The computational and experimental results are presented as concordant, but the abstract does not provide sufficient detail to assess the rigor of either approach. The work may interest researchers in bacterial stress responses and anti-staphylococcal drug design, but the current abstract does not establish the central claim.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying bacterial alternative sigma factor regulation, stress response pathways in *Staphylococcus aureus*, and anti-sigma factor structure-function relationships. Those interested in targeting RsbW for antimicrobial development may also find the residue-level information useful, though the abstract does not demonstrate therapeutic relevance.
- **Major strengths** The study combines computational prediction with experimental validation, which is a reasonable approach for probing residue function. The abstract identifies specific residues and links them to multiple biochemical activities, providing a focused hypothesis. The use of alanine substitution is an appropriate strategy for assessing side-chain contributions.
- **Major Concerns** The abstract lacks quantitative data, statistical analysis, and methodological detail, making it impossible to evaluate the robustness of the claims. The term "indispensable" is contradicted by the abstract's own statement that dimerization was only "partly affected" by some substitutions. The relationship between computational predictions and experimental results is described qualitatively, with no metrics for agreement or discrepancy. The abstract does not specify how structure, binding affinity, and phosphorylation activity were measured, nor does it provide effect sizes or error estimates.
- **Minor Comments** The abstract would benefit from stating the number of replicates and statistical tests used. The phrase "various tools" is vague and should be replaced with specific techniques. The relevance to drug design is speculative and not supported by any data in the abstract. The abstract does not clarify whether the three residues act independently or cooperatively.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** No quantitative evidence is provided for any claim. The experimental characterization is described only in qualitative terms, and the computational results are not accompanied by validation metrics. The abstract does not demonstrate that the observed effects are specific to the introduced mutations rather than due to global structural disruption.

Scientific importance: limited, as the abstract does not establish broader implications for sigma factor biology or therapeutic development. Interdisciplinary readership: narrow, primarily structural biologists and microbiologists. Technical soundness: not assessable from the abstract alone, as no methods or data are shown. Readability for nonspecialists: the abstract is accessible but uses field-specific terms without sufficient context.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The abstract suggests a plausible structure-function study, but the absence of quantitative data and methodological detail prevents assessment of the central claim. A revised abstract with specific results and statistical support could change this assessment.
