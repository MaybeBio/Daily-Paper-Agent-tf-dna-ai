## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a plausible methodological pipeline for DNA-binding protein prediction, combining established components (ESM C embeddings, attention, capsule networks) in a novel arrangement. However, the abstract lacks quantitative evidence, making the central claims of superior performance and robustness unverifiable from the supplied material. The methodological novelty relative to existing ESM-based predictors is not clearly articulated. The case studies are mentioned but not described, limiting assessment of interpretability claims.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Computational biologists and bioinformaticians working on protein function prediction, particularly those developing deep learning methods for DNA-binding protein identification. Researchers applying protein language models to biological sequence classification tasks would also find the approach relevant. The potential clinical relevance to disease mechanisms may attract a broader biomedical audience, though this is not substantiated in the abstract.
- **Major strengths** The method integrates a pre-trained protein language model with attention-augmented convolution and capsule networks, which is a reasonable architectural choice for capturing both local and global sequence features. The use of a dynamic learning rate scheduler to address premature convergence is a thoughtful training optimization. The inclusion of independent dataset validation and case studies indicates an intention to assess generalizability and interpretability beyond a single benchmark.
