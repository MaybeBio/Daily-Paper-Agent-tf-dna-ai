# Multi-omics evidence supports PROC as a potential predictor of VTE risk: A Mendelian randomization study


## Abstract
  Venous thromboembolism (VTE) is a common cardiovascular condition whose etiology is closely linked to a range of factors, including trauma, immobilization, and environmental influences. The application of multi-omics Mendelian randomization may prove to be a valuable approach in clarifying the fundamental pathogenic mechanisms associated with VTE. Based on multi-omics data of mQTL, eQTL and pQTL, our study identifies core drug targets and pharmacological associations of VTE. Summary-level data from multi-omics exposure were obtained from studies focusing on quantitative trait loci (QTL) across 3 dimensions: gene methylation, gene expression, and protein levels. Genetic association data pertaining to VTE outcomes were subsequently sourced from the FinnGen database for discovery purposes and the UK Biobank for replication. Subsequently, summary-data-based Mendelian randomization (SMR) and co-localization analyses were conducted to evaluate the causal relationships between the 3 QTL levels and the risk of VTE. Through the application of xQTL and xQTL SMR analyses, we have identified 1 gene with a high causal impact, PROC, alongside 2 genes with lower causal significance, DPY19L1P1 and MAF BZIP transcription factor F (MAFF). Our results indicate that, at both the mQTL and eQTL levels, an increased risk of VTE is correlated with elevated levels of DPY19L1P1, while the causal effect associated with MAFF is inversely related. Furthermore, our analysis revealed an inverse causal relationship between gene methylation and VTE for PROC (cg06038358), with an odds ratio of 1.06 (95% CI = 1.04–1.09). Notably, PROC was identified as a protective factor against the disease at the pQTL level, with an odds ratio of 0.7 (95% CI = 0.6–0.82). Comprehensive phenotype scanning demonstrated a causal relationship between the target gene PROC and 12 distinct phenotypes. Finally, drug prediction and molecular docking identified 3 pharmacokinetic associations of drug target genes for VTE. In summary, the current research clarified the causal connections among DPY19L1P1, MAFF, and PROC in relation to VTE at the levels of methylation, expression, and protein. In addition, phenome-wide association analysis and molecular docking confirmed the functional role associations of the target genes.


## 1. Introduction
  Venous thromboembolism (VTE) is recognized as a significant vascular disorder, ranking as the third most common condition after ischemic heart disease and stroke. This highlights its considerable importance within the range of cardiovascular diseases.[1] VTE comprises 2 major types: DVT and PE, both posing serious, potentially life-threatening health risks.[2] As the global population continues to age and the burden of disease increases, the incidence of VTE has risen to approximately 1 to 2 cases per 1000 population per year.[3] Geographical disparities in disease incidence are evident, with more significant variations noted among populations of European descent in comparison to those of Asian descent.[4] The incidence of VTE is influenced by a multitude of factors, notably trauma, extended periods of immobility, obesity, pharmacological treatments, genetic predispositions, and environmental conditions. Due to the significant mortality, high recurrence rates, and detrimental effects on quality of life linked to VTE, the early detection and prevention of this condition are of paramount importance.[5] We propose the integration of advanced multi-omics methodologies to identify early biomarkers associated with the development of VTE and to discover potential pharmacological targets for therapeutic intervention. This approach represents a departure from conventional strategies aimed at reducing morbidity risks, which typically focus on weight management and smoking cessation.

  The development of high-throughput and various sequencing technologies has positioned genome-wide association studies (GWAS) as essential methodologies for identifying phenotype-specific causative genes, elucidating mechanisms of pathogenesis, discovering biomarkers, and promoting the advancement of personalized medicine.[6] Numerous extensive meta-analyses of GWAS have identified several genetic loci associated with susceptibility to VTE. These loci are predominantly implicated in the regulation of the coagulation cascade and platelet function.[7–9] Concurrently, our attention has transitioned to the analysis of molecular quantitative trait loci (QTL) data, with the objective of elucidating potential relationships among the 3 dimensions of methylation, gene expression, and protein abundance in relation to vitamin K-dependent coagulation factors (VTE). The fundamental challenge lies in establishing a methodology for prioritizing predictors of disease outcomes through the integrated analysis of pooled data from QTL studies and GWAS alongside omics data.[10] Mendelian randomization functions as an epidemiological methodology aimed at elucidating potential causal associations between histological biomarkers and particular disease outcomes, utilizing genetic variation as an instrumental variable.[11]

  Mendelian randomization (MR) offers distinct advantages over observational studies. MR methodologies leverage genetic variation to simulate natural randomization, thereby effectively minimizing the influence of confounding variables and alleviating the potential for reverse causality.[12] Consequently, they provide a more robust level of evidence to elucidate potential correlations between QTL levels and VTE disease. Considering the varied roles of proteins in biological processes, including cell signaling, metabolic regulation, and structural integrity, they are integral to the initiation and advancement of diseases.[13] Therefore, we leveraged protein quantitative trait loci (pQTL) as a cornerstone for a comprehensive evaluation of VTE risk, integrating expression quantitative trait loci (eQTL) and methylation quantitative trait loci (mQTL) analyses to enhance our understanding of how genetic variation influences various molecular levels in organisms.[14] Unlike randomized controlled trials, which require significant resources, Mendelian randomization techniques facilitate a swift examination of aggregated genetic variant data linked to particular exposures and outcomes. This approach is instrumental in the identification of potential early biomarkers and drug targets.[15] Moreover, the simulation of the therapeutic effects of approved pharmaceuticals through the utilization of high-risk genetic variants presents new opportunities for the repurposing of existing medications and the investigation of alternative therapeutic applications.

  In summary, this research elucidates the possible relationship between pQTL, eQTL, and mQTL in relation to the risk of VTE through the application of two-sample Mendelian randomization. This analysis employed a variety of statistical techniques, including assessments of heterogeneity, sensitivity, and co-localization. Furthermore, the results were corroborated in an independent multi-group study cohort.


## 2. Methods

### 2.1. Study design
  The provided flowchart outlines the multi-omics causal association analysis of MR-based VTE (Fig. 1). GWAS pooled data on disease outcomes for this summary-data-based Mendelian randomization (SMR) study were sourced from the FinnGen (Discovery) and the UK Biobank (validation) study. Additionally, instrumental variables strongly correlated with QTL exposure were acquired from McRae for mQTL, Ferkingstad for pQTL, and the eQTLGen Consortium and GTEx eQTL summary data (https://www.eqtlgen.org/cis-eqtls.html) for eQTL.[16,17] In this study, instrumental variables were identified from methylation, gene expression and protein levels and co-localization analysis was applied to strengthen causal associations. By integrating the results obtained from these 3 different levels of SMR analysis, we identified causal candidate genes.


### 2.2. Data sources of methylation, expression, and protein quantitative trait loci
  QTLs serve to illustrate the association between single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) and the levels of DNA methylation, gene expression, and protein abundance, thereby uncovering potential mechanisms underlying VTE pathogenesis via the integration of multi-omics data.

  Proteins, being the primary drivers of cellular functions, are selected as the focal point of this study due to their ability to directly mirror the physiological and pathological states of cells and organisms.[18] Pooled statistics for pQTL were obtained from the widely utilized GWAS study conducted by Ferkingstad et al, which examined 4179 plasma protein levels in 35,559 Icelanders using 4907 aptamers.[17]

  Attention has also been directed towards the role of genes in pathogenesis, whereby mutations or variations in genes can influence the structure, function, or expression levels of proteins, thereby exerting a considerable impact on the physiological and pathological processes of an organism.[19] In terms of selecting blood eQTL datasets, we specifically extracted cis-eQTL sections from a cohort of 31,684 individuals sourced from the eQTLGen consortium (https://www.eqtlgen.org/cis-eqtls.html).

  DNA methylation plays a crucial role in regulating transcription and can elucidate the association between specific SNPs and disease.[20] Data for mQTL were obtained from the Brisbane Systems Genetics Study (n = 624) and the Lothian Birth Cohorts (n = 1366) of 1921 and 1936, with 52,916 cis mQTL replicated after measuring whole blood methylation using the Illumina HumanMethylation450 array.[16]


### 2.3. VTE outcome datasets
  The VTE disease outcomes in this study were initially identified using data from the Finnish database as the discovery set, with subsequent validation carried out using the UK Biobank data. The diagnoses of VTE in this study adhere to the definitions provided by the International Classification of Diseases (ICD). The publicly available R10 data from the FinnGen study comprised 4620 patients with VTE and 356,574 healthy controls.[21] In contrast, the VTE-related GWAS data from the UK Biobank, obtained from Neale labs, consisted of 4620 cases and 356,574 controls, encompassing 11,901,177 SNPs, and utilizing the disease code ukb-d-I9_VTE (https://gwas.mrcieu.ac.uk/).

  The populations selected for the exposure and outcome data in the study were of European origin, and precautions were taken to prevent any population overlap, thereby bolstering the robustness of the results.


### 2.4. Summary-data-based MR and co-localization analysis
  SMR and co-localization analyses serve to elucidate the association between GWAS data and specific QTL molecules, allowing for the inference of causal relationships.

  SMR demonstrates greater statistical power in illustrating the correlation between gene methylation, expression, and protein abundance with VTE risk compared to conventional MR methods.[22] We primarily selected cis-QTLs, which are more likely to directly influence gene expression levels, providing a more straightforward and plausible explanation for the relationship between genotype and phenotype.[23] We conducted a rigorous screening for highly correlated cis-QTLs by implementing stringent threshold settings, which involved a window centered on the corresponding genes (±1000 kb) and a P-value cutoff of 5E−08, while also ensuring that the allele frequency difference remained below the threshold of 0.2. The HEIDI test, as a heterogeneity assessment, considers the interlocking relationships between SNPs and serves as a supplementary measure to the SMR findings. It employs a P-value threshold typically set at >0.01 to filter out SNPs exhibiting a high degree of interlocking effects. False discovery rate (FDR) was used for multiple test correction of SMR results, and results with P-FDR < 0.05 were used to perform further co-localization analysis.[24]

  Co-localization analysis methods ascertain whether various traits or diseases stem from shared genetic variants by examining the genomic locations of these variants. In line with prior research, the co-localization region windows were set to ± 1000 kb, ±1000 kb, and ± 500 kb for pQTL-GWAS, eQTL-GWAS, and mQTL-GWAS, respectively.[25–27] In Bayesian co-localization, the posterior probability of homogeneity is utilized to gauge the extent of co-localization among genetic variants across traits or diseases. PPH4 assesses the probability that 2 loci are on the same chromosome, and a value of H4 (PPH4) > .60 is commonly considered indicative of co-localization support.[28]


### 2.5. Multi-omics and externally validated concordance analyses
  To comprehensively define the target genes of the multi-omics screen, we integrated overlapping gene and probe information. Proteins serve as important mediators for the performance of organismal functions, so it is considered a high priority to have causal effects at the protein level. Based on this principle, we classified causal candidate genes into high/low causal association according to the following criteria: high causal association genes were defined as having association with VTE at the protein and other abundance levels (FDR-corrected P-value < .05) with co-localized PPH4 > .6; low causal association genes were defined as having association with VTE at the gene and methylation abundance levels (FDR corrected P-value < .05) and co-localized PPH4 > .6. Additionally, we examined positive QTLs in the UKBB validation group that closely overlapped with FinnGen’s findings, offering support for alternate approaches to early prevention and drug targeting of VTE.


### 2.6. Phenome-wide association analysis identifies phenome associations for target genes
  To identify possible causal associations of target genes with other phenotypes, we performed a phenome-wide association scan of target genes using the ExPheWas browser. The ExPheWas browser showcases the results of PheWAS using an approach that models the joint effect of variants at protein coding genes. PheWAS analyses are based on genotype and health outcome data from the UK Biobank and examine phenome associations of genes with high causal associations to identify possible associated diseases. The ExPheWas browser phenotype is defined as follows:362 Self-reported diseases, 21 Manually defined cardiovascular endpoints, 1280 Phecodes, 83 Continuous variables (standardized laboratory and anthropomorphic measurements)(https://exphewas.statgen.org/).[29]


### 2.7. Drug prediction and molecular docking
  In this study, we used AutoDock Vina 1.1.2 software for semiflexible molecular docking. We utilized PyMol 2.4 for protein preparation, removing water and excess ligands, and adding hydrogen atoms. PDBQT files for docking simulations were created with AutoDock Tools 1.5.6. Small molecule ligands were characterized from PubChem (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/), while target protein data were sourced from the RCSB database (https://www.rcsb.org/). We then visualized and analyzed the ligand–receptor binding and complex stability using PLIP and PyMol 2.4.


## 3. Results

### 3.1. Gene methylation and VTE
  The outcomes regarding the causal relationship between gene methylation and VTE are outlined in Figure 2. Under strict criteria, 103 CpG sites proximal to 42 key genes were identified, with thresholds set at P-HEIDI > 0.01 and P-FDR < 0.05. Among the 42 loci demonstrating co-localization evidence (PPH4 > 0.60) in the final screen, 39 CpG sites corresponding to 18 genes were identified (Table S2, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/Q712). These genes include LINC01057 (cg21436544), MEF2D (cg14087168, cg08943375, cg26121730), BIRC6 (cg12233946), PROC (cg06038358), SLC12A8 (cg09866143), XXYLT1 (cg22986662), DPY19L1P1 (cg02148426), CENPP (cg13798575), FADS1 (cg07689907, cg14725641, cg03735013, cg25326896, cg12517394, cg19610905), P2RY6 (cg09115116, cg21124714, cg03622371), AQP11 (cg06022507), UBASH3B (cg22806934), VWF (cg15866021), PTPRR (cg21481937), TC2N (cg22815785), SMAD6 (cg17232357), SMAD3 (cg24032190), HSD3B7 (cg05624853), STX1B (cg05787209), ZFPM1 (cg04387347, cg22791932, cg16559361, cg26884787), MYT1 (cg00133136), PLA2G69 (cg14247180, cg03162506) and MAF BZIP transcription factor F (MAFF) (cg25457927, cg00159439, cg16866941, cg09146183). In our findings, it was observed that certain genes exhibited opposite directions of effect across different CpG sites. These consist of 12 methylation sites across 4 genes, specifically MEF2D (cg14087168, cg08943375, cg26121730), P2RY6 (cg09115116, cg21124714, cg03622371), ZFPM1 (cg04387347, cg22791932, cg16559361, cg26884787), and PLA2G69 (cg14247180, cg03162506). The CpG sites identified in the discovery set, including CCDC181 (cg09671955), SLC19A2 (cg24541193), F11-AS1 (cg07770049), and FAM83C (cg26821293, cg20920419), were successfully replicated in the UK Biobank (Table S3, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/Q712).


### 3.2. Gene expression and VTE
  The causal effects of gene expression on VTE are presented in Table S4 (Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/Q712). Overall, 46 gene expressions were found to be significantly associated with VTE risk after correcting for FDR. After co-localization analysis, elevated levels of MARS2 gene expression were positively associated with VTE risk, along with several other genes including MAFF, SETD1A, ITGA4, ATP13A2, IWS1, PSMD9, MGAT3, CLNS1A, DPY19L1P1, ZNF787, and PLA2G6 (Fig. 3). Among those with PPH4 > 0.9 were MAFF (OR = 1.46, 95% CI = 1.25–1.72), SETD1A (OR = 1.56, 95% CI = 1.3–1.86), ITGA4 (OR = 1.09, 95% CI = 1.05–1.14) and ATP13A2 (OR = 1.29, 95% CI = 1.14–1.46). In contrast, elevated levels of KIAA2013 (OR = 0.94, 95% CI = 0.91–0.97), IPPK (OR = 0.53, 95% CI = 0.39–0.7), PROCR (OR = 0.59, 95% CI = 0.49–0.71), CEP68 (OR = 0.87, 95% CI = 0.81–0.93) were found to reduce the risk of developing VTE, along with several other genes, including CRIM1, HLA-DRA, PLOD1, MIIP, MFN2, WDR66, CC2D2B, TCTN3, ENTPD1, RSF1, RP11-91P24.5, RP11-91P24.6, UNC13D, and RP11-91P24.1. Furthermore, PROCR and ACSS2 were replicated in the UK Biobank (Table S5, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/Q712), providing additional support for their association with VTE risk.


### 3.3. pQTL and VTE
  Similarly, following FDR correction and the HEIDI test, we identified a total of 18 pQTL strongly correlated with the risk of VTE (Fig. 4). The 8 pQTLs (PLCG2, KNG1, ASPN, SERPINE2, LRP12, PROC, OGN and GP6) were found to co-localize, providing evidence for association (Table S6, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/Q712). Similarly, PLCG2 (OR = 1.59, 95% CI = 1.34–1.89) and KNG1 (OR = 1.15, 95% CI = 1.09–1.22), along with elevated ASPN (OR = 1.15, 95% CI = 1.08–1.21) levels, exhibited a positive correlation trend with disease risk when satisfying PPH4 > 0.9. However, the protein expression level of SERPINE2 (OR = 0.94, 95% CI = 0.91–0.97) demonstrates an opposite direction of effect. ANGPT1 and PLCG2 were identified in both the discovery and replication sets (Table S7, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/Q712).


### 3.4. Multi-omics concordance analyses
  After elaborating the multi-omics results, we identified 1 gene with high causal association, PROC (Fig. 5), and 2 genes with low causal association, DPY19L1P1 and MAFF. In the target gene-VTE relationships replicated in the UK Biobank study, although not all associations were corrected by FDR, the causal associations maintained a consistent direction, which is consistent with our observations in the discovery phase. The SMR results of mQTL-eQTL and mQTL-pQTL (Pqtl-qtl < .05) further validated the multilevel association of the target genes (Table 1).


### 3.5. Phenome association of target genes
  By scanning the whole phenome of target genes, we identified 12 phenotypes associated with PROC: mainly containing respiratory function-related phenotypes (forced vital capacity Z-score, Forced expiratory volume in 1-second (FEV1) Z-score) and lipid-related phenotypes (HDL cholesterol, Apolipoprotein A, Cholesterol), among others (Table S8, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/Q712).


### 3.6. Drug prediction and molecular docking
  We determined the likely acting drugs for the core drug target gene PROC by using the Drugbank drug prediction website. Specifically, we docked Cupric Chloride (PubChem CID: 24014), Kappadione (PubChem CID: 8556), and Menadione (PubChem CID: 4055) to the protein PROC (Uniprot ID: P04070). The docking results were set to output the 9 best docking positions, and the conformation with the lowest binding energy and highest clustering frequency was identified as the most potential binding mode between the ligand and the protein (Fig. 6).


## 4. Discussion
  After evaluating the MR framework and complementary analyses such as co-localization, we identified multi-omics evidence linking gene methylation, expression, and protein levels to venous thromboembolism (VTE) risk. At both mQTL and eQTL levels, increased DPY19L1P1 expression was associated with higher VTE incidence, while MAFF showed inconsistent causal effects. At the protein level, PROC appeared protective, whereas its methylation suggested a risk-enhancing role. Notably, elevated PROCR expression was associated with reduced VTE risk (Fig. 7).


### 4.1. PROC and PROCR: evidence-based associations
  Among the identified genes, PROC and PROCR have the strongest evidence. Protein C (PROC) was protective at the protein level (OR = 0.70, 95% CI = 0.60–0.82) but risk-enhancing after methylation (OR = 1.06, 95% CI = 1.04–1.09). PROCR expression also showed a robust protective effect (OR = 0.59, 95% CI = 0.49–0.71). These findings are consistent with prior studies linking protein C deficiency to thrombophilia and pregnancy-associated VTE.[30–32] In a comprehensive assessment of pregnancy, thrombophilia, and the risk of initial venous thrombosis, protein C deficiency (prepregnancy: 3.2%, range 0.6 to 8.2%; postpregnancy: 5.4%, range 0.9 to 13.8%) was deemed to pose an inherent risk.[33] Protein C (PROC) is a plasma protease that relies on vitamin K for its activity and is encoded by the PROC gene. Likewise, in a prospective cohort investigation concerning the absolute risk of venous thromboembolism and the prognostic utility of screening asymptomatic relatives of individuals with hereditary deficiencies in protein S, protein C, or antithrombin, protein C deficiency was associated with an elevated absolute risk of VTE.[34] Acting as an antithrombin, it regulates coagulation by inhibiting the activity of coagulation factors Va and VIIIa during the process of blood clotting.[35] The protein C anticoagulation pathway is a crucial system for regulating thrombosis, restraining inflammatory responses, and possibly decreasing inflammatory cytokines and apoptosis of endothelial cells induced by ischemia.[33] PROCR (protein C receptor) acts as a receptor for protein C, amplifying its anticoagulant effects by binding to it and guiding it to the activated thrombin-platelet regulatory protein complex, thereby enhancing protein C activation.[36] Furthermore, Stacey et al showcased through genetic analysis, genotypic human recall, and in vitro experiments that the PROCR-219Gly variant enhances (activates) plasma protein C levels by inducing shedding of the extracellular structural domain of the endothelial protein C receptor (EPCR) in endothelial cells. This shedding process helps mitigate leukocyte-endothelial cell adhesion and vascular inflammation.[37] Activated protein C (APC) bound to PROCR also exerts cytoprotective effects, including anti-inflammatory and anti-apoptotic activity.[38] Our MR findings further suggest that methylation at cg06038358 may downregulate PROC expression, potentially lowering protein C levels and increasing VTE risk. In phenome-wide analyses, PROC was linked to traits such as C-reactive protein, lipid metabolism, lung function, and leukocyte counts, all previously associated with thrombosis.[39–41]Collectively, these results reinforce the central role of PROC/PROCR in VTE biology and suggest that genetic screening of this pathway may inform perioperative anticoagulation strategies.


### 4.2. MAFF: preliminary evidence requiring validation
  MAFF emerged as a candidate gene, with increased expression and methylation at cg09146183 associated with elevated VTE risk. MAFF encodes a bZIP transcription factor involved in cellular stress responses.[42] While von Scheidt et al identified the transcription factor MAFF as a key regulator in the hepatic network, it’s noteworthy that the liver also plays a pivotal role in maintaining coagulation homeostasis and blood flow.[43] Furthermore, MAFF has frequently sparked interest in tumor progression, with Moon et al demonstrating that the hypoxia-inducible factor target MAFF fosters tumor invasion and metastasis via IL11 and STAT3 signaling pathways.[44] Cancer and venous thromboembolism share well-defined mechanisms: tumors express proteins that modulate the host system, affecting factors such as platelet and leukocyte levels, and they release procoagulant proteins into the circulation.[45] On the protein levels, The bZIP transcription factor pivotal for DNA binding and protein-protein interactions, regulating diverse cellular processes such as growth, development, and stress response by modulating gene transcription.[46] Overall, whether MAFF directly influences the initiation and progression of VTE through modulation of gene expression pathways linked to thrombosis requires experimental validation. Given the role of endothelial dysfunction in VTE pathogenesis, particularly after surgery, MAFF dysregulation may contribute to a pro-thrombotic environment. Our findings suggest MAFF could serve as a predictive biomarker of endothelial vulnerability, with possible implications for postoperative surveillance and vascular protection strategies.


### 4.3. DPY19L1P1: novel but exploratory signal
  We also identified DPY19L1P1, a pseudogene, as a potential novel factor in VTE. Our MR results suggest that methylation of cg02148426 may upregulate DPY19L1P1 expression, thereby increasing risk. Although pseudogenes are often nonfunctional, DPY19L1P1 has been implicated in immune-related pathways such as interleukin and p53 signaling.[47] The inflammatory state of the immune system can lead to vascular endothelial damage and the release of inflammatory mediators, while immune cells can also induce thrombosis by releasing tissue factor. The inflammatory state of the immune system can lead to vascular endothelial damage and the release of inflammatory mediators, while immune cells can also induce thrombosis by releasing tissue factor.[48] Given the lack of prior research, this association should be considered hypothesis-generating. Future studies should clarify whether DPY19L1P1 acts directly or through regulation of nearby thrombosis-related genes or microRNAs.

  Below, we analyze the strengths and weaknesses of this study. This study pioneers the analysis of potential causal relationships with the risk of VTE at the mQTL, eQTL, and pQTL levels using an MR approach, thereby furnishing evidence for the selection of biomarkers and drug targets in the early stages of the disease. Firstly, in selecting the methodology, the combination of MR with co-localization serves to mitigate the impact of confounding factors and reverse causation on the study results. Additionally, it minimizes potential biases stemming from chain imbalances, thereby ensuring the robustness and scientific validity of the findings. Regarding the limitations of this study: Firstly, our study primarily focused on the cis part of QTL selection, potentially overlooking the regulatory impact of the trans part on the disease. Secondly, constrained by data sources, we analyzed only a subset of existing QTLs, potentially overlooking other pivotal PQLs crucial to VTE pathogenesis. Thirdly, to mitigate the impact of population divergence on outcomes, our study exclusively utilized data from European populations, necessitating additional validation across diverse demographics for broader applicability. We update the results of MR analysis of pqtl in Asian populations for validating the ubiquity of the 3 target genes (Table S9, Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/Q712).


## 5. Conclusions
  In conclusion, this study unveiled the causal links between DPY19L1P1, MAFF, and PROC with VTE across methylation, expression, and protein levels, shedding light on the underlying pathogenesis of VTE from a multi-omics perspective. The findings hold promise for identifying early biomarkers and therapeutic targets, thereby advancing the prevention and clinical management of VTE.
