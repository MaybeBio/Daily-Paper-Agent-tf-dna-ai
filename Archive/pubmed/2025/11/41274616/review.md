- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no figures, tables, methods, or supplementary data provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full methods, experimental data (expression levels, knockdown or overexpression results, promoter reporter assays), docking parameters and validation, statistical analyses, and any supplementary figures or tables

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a plausible but incompletely supported narrative linking PtHSF to Ptcrustin1 regulation. The structural characterization and tissue expression data are descriptive and likely sound, but the central regulatory claim rests on correlative or in silico evidence that cannot be evaluated from the abstract alone. The study may be of interest to crustacean immunologists, but the case for a specific regulatory mechanism is not established from the provided material.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying innate immunity in crustaceans, particularly antimicrobial peptide regulation and transcription factor function in decapods. The work may also appeal to those interested in aquaculture disease management, as the authors frame the findings as relevant to disease control in P. trituberculatus.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses a relatively underexplored area, namely transcription factor control of crustacean antimicrobial peptides. The identification of a full-length HSF with conserved domains is a useful contribution. The combination of expression analysis and molecular docking provides a hypothesis-generating framework for future functional studies.
- **Major Concerns** The abstract does not provide functional evidence, such as RNA interference, overexpression, or promoter reporter assays, to support the claim that PtHSF "specifically regulates" Ptcrustin1. The docking results are presented as evidence of binding, but without experimental validation (e.g., electrophoretic mobility shift assay or chromatin immunoprecipitation), the interaction remains speculative. The lack of a significant transcriptional response to Vibrio challenge in hemocytes is not reconciled with the proposed regulatory role, which weakens the biological logic.
- **Minor Comments** The abstract would benefit from specifying the number of biological replicates and statistical tests used. The phrase "specifically regulates" is strong and should be tempered unless direct evidence is provided. The tissue expression data are described only for muscle and hemocytes; clarification of other tissues would improve context.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** No functional validation of PtHSF-mediated regulation of Ptcrustin1 is described. The docking predictions are not experimentally confirmed. The relationship between the lack of PtHSF induction by Vibrio and its proposed regulatory function is not explained.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: moderate, as HSF regulation of antimicrobial peptides in crustaceans is not widely studied, but the approach is not novel. Scientific importance: limited, as the findings are incremental and lack mechanistic depth. Interdisciplinary readership: narrow, primarily relevant to crustacean immunology and aquaculture. Technical soundness: cannot be fully assessed from the abstract, but the absence of functional data is a significant gap. Readability for nonspecialists: the abstract is clear but assumes familiarity with transcription factor biology and antimicrobial peptide nomenclature.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The study may be publishable in a specialized journal if functional validation is added, but the abstract alone does not support the central regulatory claim.
