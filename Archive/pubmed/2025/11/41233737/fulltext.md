# E0703 targets ERβ to facilitate the upregulation of GLI3, thereby alleviating irradiation-induced DNA damage on lymphocytes


## Background
  Radiotherapy for malignant tumor treatment and irradiation (IR)-related diagnosis damage lymphocytes, which inevitably suppresses immunity and leads to unwanted clinical outcomes. However, a few agents have been approved by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) to alleviate IR-induced injury. Here, the radioprotective effect and underlying mechanism of a new steroidal compound optimized from estradiol (E0703) were investigated.


## Methods
  Mice were exposed to γ-ray IR to establish an in vivo model of radiation injury, and human peripheral blood B lymphocytes (AHH-1) were employed to investigate injury in lymphocytes. Protein level changes in cell and tissue samples were detected by western blot and immunofluorescence. DNA damage was assessed by the comet assay and γH2AX staining. RNA sequencing was used to screen the critical genes mediating the radioprotective effect of E0703. To determine the direct target of E0703, cellular thermal shift (CETSA), drug affinity responsive target stability (DARTS), molecular docking, and surface plasmon resonance (SPR) assays were adopted. GLI3 transactivation by estrogen receptor β (ERβ) was determined by the chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) assay, while protein interactions were detected by coimmunoprecipitation (Co-IP). IP products were subjected to label-free proteomics assay to screen GLI3 conjugates.


## Results
  E0703 significantly improved survival and tissue injury in mice exposed to IR damage. In lymphocytes, IR-induced DNA damage was ameliorated with E0703 in an ataxia-telangiectasia mutated protein (ATM)-checkpoint kinase 2 (CHK2)-dependent manner. ERβ but not ERα was a direct target of E0703, wherein ERβ enhancement on the promoter region of GLI3 triggered by E0703 could sustain its protein expression. The interaction between GLI3 and eIF4G1 favored by E0703 was critical for the formation of the eIF4F translation–initiation complex. eIF4F assembly was indispensable for the stimulation of ATM-CHK2 signaling involved in DNA damage repair.


## Conclusions
  E0703 alleviated IR-induced DNA damage in lymphocytes by selectively targeting ERβ. The formation of the eIF4F complex in a GLI3-dependent manner was critical for ATM-CHK2 activation triggered by E0703. Our study provides an alternative countermeasure to alleviate IR-induced lymphopenia in individuals undergoing radiotherapy or IR-related diagnosis.

## Background
  The rate of patients with cancer is increasing around the world, and more than half of the affected individuals are administered radiotherapy to improve their disease condition [1]. As for solid tumors, including colorectal cancer, esophageal cancer, non-small cell lung cancer, hepatocellular carcinoma, glioblastoma, among others, lymphopenia induced by irradiation (IR) is an adverse effect during the radiotherapy process, which may lead to a decrease in lymphocyte-dependent immunity that is correlated with poor prognosis, local recurrence, or even tumor metastasis [2, 3]. Given that combined treatment with local radiotherapy and immunotherapy was demonstrated to significantly improve the survival rate in patients with cancer, radiation-induced lymphopenia (RIL) inevitably favors the evasion of cancer cells from antitumor immunity, resulting in suboptimal therapeutic effects [4, 5]. Moreover, IR employed for diagnostic purposes (interventional radiology, computed tomography, cardiology, and nuclear medicine) significantly increases the exposure of the general population to IR damage [6], and the collective dose can also induce toxicity in lymphocytes.

  The injury caused by IR, whether unintended or administered for therapeutic purposes, can be alleviated by radioprotectors/mitigators. Although there are several promising radioprotectors under development, agents approved by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) are rare. The only radiomitigators approved by the FDA for clinical use are aminoradiothiol amifostine and palifermin [7]. Amifostine is a selective radioprotectant of healthy tissues during the radiotherapy process, but its short half-life and dose-dependent side effects (neurotoxicity, nausea, nephrotoxicity, vomiting, allergic reactions, diarrhea, among others) limit its clinical application [8]. Other radiomitigators approved by the FDA include natural granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF) and recombinant granulocyte macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF), which effectively improve IR-induced hematopoietic injury [9]. However, the radiomitigative efficacy of G-CSF was found to be dramatically abolished in the absence of full supportive care, and it may cause delayed acute respiratory distress syndrome [10, 11]. Besides, the antagonists of arginase-1 (CB1158) and phosphodiesterase-5 (tadalafil) were recently reported to abrogate IR-induced lymphopenia, but these agents are just countermeasures under development, and their effectiveness in clinical application remains unclear [5]. Thus, there is an urgent need to develop alternative strategies to alleviate IR-induced lymphocyte injury.

  A previous study demonstrated that radiosensitivity exhibits sex specificity, with male mice being more susceptible to IR exposure [12]. As early as 1965, pretreatment with estradiol was demonstrated to dramatically improve the survival rate of mice exposed to a lethal dose of IR damage [13, 14]. Since then, several estrogen-related agents have demonstrated potential as radioprotectors. For example, pretreatment of rats with estradiol could favor recovery of spermatogenesis post-IR damage [15], while 17-β-estradiol alleviated cataractogenesis caused by low-linear energy transfer (LET) radiation or cerebellar disorganization/motor abnormalities induced by γ-rays [16, 17], and 2-methoxyestradiol could inhibit IR-induced skin injuries prior to and post X-ray [18]. Previous studies by our group have demonstrated that E0703, a new steroidal compound optimized from estradiol, could trigger the proliferation of hematopoietic cells (leucocytes, granulocytes, neutrophils, hematopoietic progenitor cells) upon IR damage, with excellent pharmacokinetic characteristics [19, 20]. However, previous studies solely focused on the pharmacokinetics of E0703, and its exact radioprotective effect on lymphocytes and the underlying mechanism remain unclear. Besides, the proliferative effect on hematopoietic progenitor cells treated with E0703 seems to contrast previous findings that estrogen reduces the number of stem cells forming new hematopoietic colonies [13], indicating a novel target of E0703 in protection against IR injury.

  In the current study, we aimed to further verify the radioprotective effect of E0703 on lymphocytes and explore the underlying mechanism. This study demonstrated that E0703 is a selective agonist of ERβ, which alleviates DNA double-strand breaks (DSBs) in lymphocytes in an ATM-CHK2 dependent manner. The formation of the eIF4F translation–initiation complex mediated by GLI3 is critical for ATM-CHK2 activation triggered by E0703. These findings might facilitate the treatment of IR-induced injury on lymphocytes for cases undergoing radiotherapy or IR-related diagnostic process.


## Methods

### Cell culture and treatments
  Human peripheral blood B lymphocytes (AHH-1) were obtained from Biovector NTCC Inc. (no. 3,617,188, Beijing, China) and cultured in RPMI-1640 containing 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin–streptomycin. Human T lymphocytes (H9 cell line that is a clonal derivative of the Hut 78 cell line, no. CL-0499) and the human natural killer (NK) cell line (KHYG-1, no. CL-0903) were purchased from Procell Life Science&Technology Co., Ltd. (Wuhan, China) and cultured in media provided by the manufacturer. Cells treated with E0703 (dissolved in dimethyl sulfoxide [DMSO]) or DMSO were subjected to γ-ray IR damage with a Co-60 source at a dosage of 5 Gy. To measure cell viability, CCK-8 (Shanghai Topscience Co., Ltd.) and Calcein AM/propidium iodide (PI) Double Staining Kit (DOJINDO, Japan) were employed according to the manufacturers’ instructions. Signals in the Calcein AM/PI Double Staining assay were visualized with a fluorescence microscope or quantified with a flow cytometer. KU-55933 (ATM antagonist), BML-277 (CHK2 antagonist), AZD9496 (ERα antagonist), PHTPP (ERβ antagonist), and SBI0640756 (eIF4G1 antagonist) were all purchased from MedChemExpress to inhibit the corresponding signaling pathways.


### Animal model of radiation injury
  All animal experiments were approved by the animal laboratory of Laboratory Animal Center of Beijing Institute of Radiation Medicine (IACUC-DWZX-2021-557, 3 March 2021). C57BL/6J mice obtained from Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co., Ltd. were orally administered E0703 dissolved in soybean oil 24 h prior to IR injury. As for IR-induced acute injury, mice were exposed to a dose of 8 Gy, and survival was monitored for 30 days (Fig. 1A). Subsequently, 5 days after IR exposure, femur, spleen, and testis were collected for pathological analysis.   Tissues fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde were paraffin embedded and sectioned into 3-μm thick slides. Pathological changes were detected by staining with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E). Terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase dUTP nick end labeling (TUNEL) staining was used to assess cell apoptosis in spleen: The TUNEL reagent was added to slides and incubated at 37 ℃ for 1 h followed by DAPI co-staining. To detect the corresponding proteins in spleen, antibodies against ATM (no. ab201022, Abcam, 1:100), CHK2 (no. ab199031, Abcam, 1:100), GLI3 (no. 28272-1-AP, Proteintech, 1:100), ERβ (no. The signals were observed and photographed with a fluorescence microscope.

### Immunofluorescence assay of cells
  Cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and then permeabilized with 0.2% Triton X-100 in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). Primary antibodies against γH2AX (no. ab26350, Abcam, 1:100), GLI3 (no. 28272-1-AP, Proteintech, 1:100), ERβ (no. After incubation with primary antibodies overnight at 4 ℃, secondary anti-rabbit IgG (H + L), F(ab′)2 Fragment (Alexa Fluor® 488 conjugate) was employed. Fluorescence intensity was measured with a ZEISS LSM980 laser scanning confocal microscope (Carl Zeiss, Germany).


### Comet assay
  The comet assay was performed to evaluate DNA strand breaks in AHH-1 cells post IR exposure. Cells were seeded on agarose-coated slides and exposed to 5 Gy of γ-ray. The treated cells were harvested, embedded in low-melting point agarose, and subjected to electrophoresis. Slides were stained with the comet electrophoresis kit for cell damage detection (KeyGEN BioTECH, KGA1302-20) and visualized under a fluorescence microscope. DNA damage was quantified by measuring the tail moment using the Comet Assay Software Project (CASP).


### RNA sequencing
  Total RNA was extracted with TRIzol reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific) and enriched with magnetic beads containing Oligo(dT). After treatment with the fragmentation buffer, cDNA was synthesized and enriched by polymerase chain reaction (PCR) to construct a cDNA library. The MGISEQ-T7 platform was employed to sequence the library, and raw data in fastq format were filtered with PERL SCRIPT. HISAT2 was used to map to the cell genome (GRCm39/mm39) with clean reads. The Fragments Per Kilobase of exon model per Million mapped fragments (FPKM) was calculated with FeatureCounts, and DESeq2 was employed for differential expression analysis, where |log2FC|> 1 and Padj < 0.05 was considered significant. The R software was used for gene ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analyses.


### Lentivirus transfection
  A lentivirus for GLI3 knockdown in AHH-1 cells was generated by Shanghai Zorin Biological Technology Co., Ltd. The target sequences were: (1) 5′-GCATATGACTTCCGCCTTATC-3′ (shGLI3#1), (2) 5′-GCAGATGCTTGGGCAGATTAG-3′ (shGLI3#2), (3) 5′-GGAAGAAAGCAAATTCCTTGC-3′ (shGLI3#3), (4) 5′-TTCTCCGAACGTGTCACGT-3′ (shScramble). The lentivirus used for GLI3 overexpression in AHH-1 cells was provided by Haixing Biosciences, where the coding sequence of full length GLI3 was cloned into the pHAGE-FLAG vector. AHH-1 cells were infected with the lentivirus, and GLI3 protein expression was detected by western blot.


### Cellular thermal shift (CETSA) assay
  The CETSA was used to assess the binding of E0703 to its target protein in AHH-1 cells. Five freeze–thaw cycles using liquid nitrogen were employed to obtain cell lysate for the CETSA experiment, and lysates were subsequently divided into two aliquots: the E0703 (160 nM) treatment group and the control group (equal volume of solvent). The aliquots were heated at indicated temperatures (46, 49, 52, 55, 58, 61, 64, and 67 ℃) for 3 min and then subjected to western blotting to determine the expression levels of ERβ and ERα.


### Drug affinity responsive target stability (DARTS) assay
  Cell lysates for the DARTS experiment were obtained with five freeze–thaw cycles using liquid nitrogen. After treatment with E0703 (0, 40, 80, 160, 320, and 640 nM) for 1 h, proteinase (MedChemExpress, HY-B2228) was added to cell lysates and then incubated at room temperature for 15 min. The protein levels of ERβ and ERα were determined by western blot.


### Molecular docking
  To predict the binding site of E0703 and affinity between E0703 and ERβ or ERα, the molecular docking assay was performed. The molecular structure of E0703 was obtained from a previous study [20], and the protein structures of ERβ [21] and ERα [22] were downloaded from the PDB database. The above structures were aligned into pdb format and were then subjected to docking with AutoDock Vina. PyMOL (version 2.2.0) was employed for visualizing the docking results.


### Surface plasmon resonance (SPR)
  Surface plasmon resonance (SPR) experiments were performed with Biacore T200 (Cytiva) to study the binding kinetics of E0703 with ERβ. Recombinant ERβ was bound to a CM5 sensor chip (Cytiva). E0703 was diluted with running buffer for five concentrations and then injected over the immobilized surface in a continuous flow of running buffer (Cytiva) at 25 ℃ (flow rate, 50 μl/min; association time, 60 s; dissociation time, 60 s). The association rate constant (Kon), dissociation rate constant (Koff), and equilibrium dissociation constant (Kd) were calculated.


### Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) assay
  The Pierce Agarose ChIP Kit (no. 26156, Thermo Fisher Scientific) was employed to determine whether GLI3 transcription could be activated by ERβ. After crosslinking with 1% formaldehyde, cells were neutralized with glycine solution. The micrococcal nuclease digestion reagent was used to obtain digested chromatin. Then, antibodies against ERβ (no. GTX70174, GeneTex) were used for immunoprecipitation (IP) of the digested chromatin. Then, 20 mg/mL proteinase K was used to digest the histone of the IP product to obtain DNA. The purified DNA was submitted to quantitative (q)PCR detection. ### Immunoprecipitation (IP)
  Protein–protein interactions were identified using the Pierce Crosslink Immunoprecipitation Kit (no. 26147, Thermo Fisher Scientific). In brief, antibodies against GLI3 (no. ab307714, Abcam), eIF4G1 (no. 15704-1-AP, Proteintech), eIF4A1/A2/A3 (no. A24293, Abclonal), and eIF4E (no. Cell lysates from AHH-1 cells treated with E0703 and DMSO upon IR exposure were incubated with the resin. The IP product was obtained with elution buffer and then subjected to western blotting or label-free proteomics assay.


### Label-free proteomics
  Label-free proteomics was performed by Beijing Qinglian Biotech Co., Ltd. on a ORBITRAP ECLIPSE mass spectrometer (equipped with AIMS Pro™ Interface) with the full scanning range (350–2000 m/z). The resolution of primary mass spectrometry was set to 120,000 (200 m/z), and secondary mass spectrometry was 15,000 (200 m/z). Proteome Discoverer 2.4 was employed to search the raw files, and the R package vsn (version 3.68.0) was used to analyze the variance stabilizing transformation of filtered expression matrix. DEP (version 1.22.0), limma (version 3.56.2), and fdrtool (version 1.2.17) in R were used for differential expression analysis of proteins, and protein-wise linear models combined with empirical Bayes statistics were used to identify significant proteins. Proteins with an absolute fold change (FC) greater than 2 and a false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05 were considered differentially expressed proteins (DEPs).


### Statistical analysis
  All statistical analyses were performed with the GraphPad Prism software version 7.0 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). At least three independent experiments were conducted, and the Student’s t-test was employed to determine significance between two groups. P < 0.05 was the threshold value to determine significance.


## Results

### E0703 significantly alleviates IR-induced damage to lymphocytes
  We first assessed the radioprotective effect of E0703 and found that pretreatment with E0703 significantly improved the survival rate of mice exposed to 8 Gy IR damage (Fig. 1A, 1). Femur, spleen, and testis were subjected to H&E staining 5 days post IR, and E0703 alleviated IR-induced damage in these organs, where the decreased level of femur karyocytes, atrophy of white pulp in spleen, and destruction of testicular seminiferous tubules were alleviated (Fig. 1C). Peripheral blood was collected 21 and 30 days after IR exposure, and white blood cell (WBC) counts were measured; E0703 accelerated the recovery of WBC levels (Fig. 1E) but not hemoglobin (HGB), blood platelet (PLT), and red blood cell (RBC) levels (Supplementary Fig. 1 A, B). Then, spleen, the major lymphoid organ, was subjected to H&E staining. The results showed that the superficial area and number of the white pulp (mainly composed of lymphocytes) were significantly decreased upon IR injury, whereas E0703 could alleviate spleen injury (Fig. 1F, 1). Besides, E0703 also inhibited IR-induced cell apoptosis detected by TUNEL staining (Fig. 1H).

  Next, AHH-1 cells were employed to determine whether E0703 could protect lymphocytes against IR-induced injury. In the CCK8 assay, 80–640 nM E0703 significantly improved the cell viability of AHH-1 upon IR damage (Fig. 2A), so further experiments were conducted using E0703 at 320 nM. The radioprotective effect of E0703 on AHH-1 was further confirmed by Calcein AM/propidium iodide staining and flow cytometry (Fig. 2B, 2). Intriguingly, in human T lymphocytes (H9) and NK cells (KHYG-1), E0703 provided no protection against IR damage (Supplementary Fig. 1C–F), indicating that the radioprotective effect of E0703 was specific to B lymphocytes. As shown in our previous study, E0703 inhibits IR-induced cell apoptosis in spleen, and in vitro experiments also found that apoptosis in AHH-1 cells could be inhibited by E0703 (Fig. 2D). Since DNA damage is a critical initiator of cell apoptosis, we next assessed whether E0703 could ease IR-induced DNA damage in AHH-1 cells. In the comet assay, DNA fragments caused by IR damage were dramatically alleviated by E0703 (Fig. 2E). Further investigation showed that γH2AX protein upregulation (a marker of DNA double-strand breaks) was inhibited by E0703 (Fig. 2F–H). ### ATM-CHK2 signaling is critical for the radioprotective effect of E0703
  We then detected the canonical pathways of DNA damage response (DDR), which include ataxia-telangiectasia and Rad3-related proteins (ATR)-CHK1 and ATM-CHK2 and DNA-dependent protein kinase catalytic subunit (DNA-PKcs)-ku70/ku80 signaling. The results showed that E0703 selectively induced ATM-CHK2 signaling after IR damage, with the decreased levels of p‐CHK2/CHK2 and p‐ATM/ATM induced by IR significantly rescued by E0703, but the levels of p‐CHK1/CHK1, p‐ATR/ATR, p‐DNA-PKcs/DNA-PKcs, and other critical components of the DDR pathway were not restored by E0703 (Fig. 3A–D). Similar results were obtained by immunofluorescence staining of ATM and CHK2 in spleen tissue samples, where IR-induced downregulation of ATM and CHK2 in spleen was significantly rescued by E0703 (Fig. 3E, 3; Supplementary Fig. 2 A, B). We then determined the essential role of ATM-CHK2 in mediating the radioprotective effect of E0703, using the selective antagonists of ATM (KU-55933) and CHK2 (BML-277). Using Calcein AM/propidium iodide staining and flow cytometry, the viability of AHH-1 cells improved by E0703 post IR was abolished with KU-55933 or BML-277 (Fig. 3G, 3; Supplementary Fig. 2 C, E). Besides, the effect of E0703 on IR-induced DNA damage was counteracted by KU-55933 and BML-277 (Fig. 3H; Supplementary Fig. 2D). ### The activation of ATM-CHK2 signaling triggered by E0703 is mediated by GLI3
  To clarify how E0703 activates ATM-CHK2 signaling, thereby ameliorating IR-induced DNA damage in lymphocytes, AHH-1 cells treated or not with E0703 upon IR exposure were subjected to RNA sequencing, where the gene encoding the transcriptional activator GLI3 was regulated dramatically (Fig. 4A). Immunofluorescent staining of GLI3 on AHH-1 cells and spleen tissues was performed (Fig. 4C, 4), and total protein was extracted from AHH-1 cells, spleen, or peripheral blood leukocytes for western blot analysis (Fig. 4B–F). The results showed that GLI3 protein downregulation was rescued by E0703 treatment. To investigate whether GLI3 was involved in the DNA damage repair process mediated by ATM-CHK2 signaling upon E0703 treatment, lentivirus was constructed to knock down GLI3 in AHH-1 cells (Fig. 4G). The results showed that GLI3 depletion dramatically abrogated the radioprotective effect of E0703, where cell death and DNA damage rescued by E0703 were abolished in GLI3-depleted cells (Fig. 4H, 4). Concurrently, activation of ATM-CHK2 signaling (ratios of p-ATM/ATM and p-CHK2/CHK2) triggered by E0703 post-IR damage was also abrogated when GLI3 was knocked down (Fig. 4J). To further clarify the vital role of GLI3 in ATM-CHK2 signaling stimulation, a lentivirus was constructed to overexpress GLI3 in AHH-1 cells (Fig. 4K). ### E0703 directly targets ERβ, thereby favoring the transcriptional upregulation of GLI3
  As E0703 is a steroidal compound designed from estradiol [19, 20], we investigated whether estrogen receptors were involved in the radioprotective effect of E0703. In CETSA and DARTS assays, E0703 inhibited the degradation of ERβ caused by heat or protease treatment but had minor effects on thermal and protease-degraded stabilization of ERα (Fig. 5A, 5). Consistently, molecular docking showed that E0703 bound to aspartic acid (ASP, 2.2 Å) of ERβ with hydrogen bond (Fig. 5C). The binding energy was −8.4 kcal/mol, but there was no binding site between E0703 and ERα (Fig. 5D). Then, recombinant protein of ERβ was employed for the SPR assay, and the results demonstrated that E0703 directly bound to ERβ with the determined equilibrium dissociation constant (Kd) of 5.441 μM (Fig. 5E). Besides, E0703 treatment significantly increased ERβ protein levels in AHH-1 cells upon IR damage (Fig. 5F, 5). ## Discussion
  The loss of circulating lymphocytes induced by IR is considered a significant concern [3], but promising countermeasures to alleviate IR-induced lymphocyte injury are still lacking. In the current study, the novel steroidal compound E0703 was demonstrated to be a potent agent against IR-induced lymphocyte injury via selective targeting of ERβ. To date, the US FDA has only approved ten agents for IR-associated injuries, of which four are radiation decorporation agents, and the remaining six (growth factors or cytokines) are radiomitigators for hematopoietic acute radiation syndrome (H-ARS) [11, 23, 24]. However, strict storage conditions and the method of administration make their use inconvenient in practical application. Besides, although amifostine is classified as a radioprotector for clinical practice, poor toxicity and safety profiles have limited its application [25]. As a novel steroidal compound, E0703 has excellent pharmacokinetic characteristics and can protect against IR-induced damage when administered orally. In addition, although estrogen-related agents have been demonstrated to be potential radioprotectors, continuous activation of ERα inevitably leads to estrogen activity, mimicking female characteristics and causing reddening and swelling of skin, among others. [26]. As E0703 selectively targets ERβ but not ERα, this study highlights a potential radioprotector with characteristics of convenient administration and low toxicity.

  As the main cell components in circulating WBCs and the white pulp of spleen [27, 28], accumulation of DNA damage in lymphocytes is involved in lymphopenia [29, 30]. The nucleus houses the genome that is the vital reproductive machinery and considered the primary target of IR damage. High-energy γ-rays can directly destroy or stimulate the production of highly reactive and toxic free radicals, thereby targeting the genome [23, 31]. DSBs represent one of the most deleterious types of DNA damage induced by IR, and eucaryotic organisms have evolved a broad network to activate DDR to repair DSBs [32], with homologous recombination (HR) and non-homologous DNA end joining (NHEJ) constituting the two major repair pathways [33]. Signaling pathways initiated by ATM, ATR, and DNA-PKcs are the three canonical groups required for DNA end processing, including excision, addition of nucleotides, and modification [34]. Our results showed that E0703 significantly inhibited DSBs in AHH-1 cells by stimulating ATM-CHK2 signaling but not ATR-CHK1 or DNA-PKcs-ku70/ku80 signaling (Fig. 3A–D). ATM is an early responder to DSBs, and CHK2 kinase can be stimulated by phosphorylated‐ATM via phosphorylation at multiple sites, including T68, which then facilitates p53 phosphorylation to induce G1/S arrest and the subsequent DNA repair [35]. Intriguingly, E0703 was found to sustain the activity of ATM-CHK2 signaling via ERβ. Previous studies have shown that activation of ERβ aggravated DNA damage in cancer cells [36, 37], but the critical role of ERβ in modulating DDR pathways in normal cells or tissues has not been examined. Our study reveals the feasibility of alleviating DNA damage by activating ATM-CHK2 signaling via ERβ.

  ERβ is an intracellular transcription factor that resides in the cytoplasm or nucleus of target cells in the absence of hormones; the main endogenous estrogens include estrone (E1), estradiol (E2), and estriol (E3) [38]. Upon stimulation with a ligand, ERβ translocates into the nucleus to facilitate the transcription of target genes [39]. Previously, ERβ stimulation has been demonstrated to be useful for the treatment of cancer, cardiovascular disease, multiple sclerosis, and Alzheimer’s disease [40], as well as for the development of B lymphocytes at all stages [41]. Besides, phytoestrogen genistein was found to mitigate IR-induced injury in the hematopoietic system and ovary in an ERβ-dependent manner [42, 43], but the intrinsic mechanism mediating the radioprotective activity of ERβ is far from elucidation. The current study demonstrated that ERβ significantly transactivated the expression of GLI3, which is indispensable for ATM-CHK2 signaling activation (Fig. 5). GLI3 is a negative regulator of the Hedgehog (Hh) signaling pathway, a major regulator of developmental tissue [44, 45]. Previous studies have shown that GLI3 is critical for thymocyte and B cell development [46, 47], with GLI3 deletion reducing the differentiation of CD4+ CD8− single-positive (SP4) cells from CD4+ CD8+ [48] or impairing B-lineage commitment [47]. Besides, an essential role for GLI3 in regulating DNA damage was also proposed; indeed, transfection of the GLI3 repressor (GLI3R) inhibited protein expression of GLI1/GLI2 and resulted in the accumulation of DNA damage and cell death [49]. Our results showed that GLI3 knockdown significantly abrogated the stimulation of ATM-CHK2 signaling, whereas GLI3 overexpression rescued the activity of this signaling (Fig. 4). DNA damage can induce CHK2 via phosphorylation on threonine 68 in an ATM-dependent manner [49], and previous studies have demonstrated various mechanisms modulating CHK2 activity: Ablation of FAM122A could suppress CHK2 signaling, thereby aggravating DNA damage [50], c-Rel-dependent Chk2 signaling was indispensable for hepatocellular carcinoma growth [51], inhibition of TBK1 kinase activity reduced cGAMP-induced phosphorylation of CHK2 [52], etc. Nevertheless, the essential role of GLI3 in mediating CHK2 activation has not been reported to date, and our study firstly reveals a connection between GLI3 upregulation triggered by ERβ and ATM-CHK2 signaling upon IR damage.

  Our further investigation showed that eIF4G1 was a key conjugate of GLI3 upon E0703 treatment (Fig. 6). eIF4G1 is a vital component of the eukaryotic translation initiation factor 4 F (eIF4F), serving as a large scaffolding protein complexing with eIF4E and eIF4A to assemble eIF4F [53]. Previously, interruption of the interaction between eIF4G1 and other components of the eIF4F complex has been demonstrated to stimulate DNA damage and cell death [54, 55], and downregulated eIF4G1 was accompanied by suppression of ATM [56]. Meanwhile, overexpression of eIF4G1 selectively triggered the translation of mRNAs in response to DNA damage, including ATM, ATR, CHK1, and CHK2 [57]. However, the intrinsic mechanism mediating the assembly of the eIF4F complex in response to DNA damage is far from elucidation. In the current study, GLI3 was confirmed to be a positive regulator for eIF4F complex assembly upon IR exposure. As a eukaryotic translation initiation factor, the eIF4F complex is critical for the translation of mRNAs that counteract DNA damage. For example, Scd6 represses the translation of SRS2 mRNA in an eIF4G1-independent manner to regulate NHEJ activity upon genotoxic stress [58], and translation of mRNAs involved in DNA damage response and repair was found to be reprogrammed by eIF4G1 in response to IR [57]. Consistently, with a selective antagonist of eIF4G1 (SBI-0640756) that can impair eIF4F complex assembly [59], the ATM-CHK2 signaling stimulation and DNA damage inhibition rescued by E0703 were significantly abrogated in this study (Fig. 7E–G). These results indicate that E0703 might favor the assembly of the eIF4F complex for ATM/CHK2 mRNA translation, thereby alleviating DNA damage upon IR exposure.


## Conclusions
  This study reveals E0703 as a potent radioprotector acting via selective targeting of ERβ but not ERα. Transactivation of GLI3 triggered by ERβ favors the assembly of the eIF4F translation–initiation complex, which ultimately facilitates ATM-CHK2 signaling activation to counteract IR-induced DSBs in lymphocytes. Our investigation highlights a novel radioprotective agent for cases with lymphopenia caused by radiotherapy or IR-related diagnostic processes.
