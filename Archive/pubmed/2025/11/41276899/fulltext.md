# Identification of common genetic features and pathways for osteoporosis with frozen shoulder by integrated bioinformatics analysis and machine learning


## Abstract
  Frozen shoulder (FS) and osteoporosis (OP) are common age-related degenerative diseases, occurring more frequently in females, which suggests potential molecular links between them. This study aimed to identify shared genetic features and pathways of OP and FS using bioinformatics and machine learning approaches. Gene expression data for OP and FS were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus database. Common differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified. Functional enrichment analysis, protein-protein interaction (PPI) networks construction, and machine learning algorithms were applied to screen key genes. Diagnostic value was evaluated by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. Immune infiltration and regulatory networks involving transcription factors and miRNAs were explored. Potential therapeutic compounds were also predicted. A total of 111 common DEGs were identified, enriched in pathways related to neurological development, cellular signaling, and immune regulation. PPI analysis revealed 14 hub genes, with SDC1 and ELN identified as key diagnostic markers by machine learning. ROC curves confirmed their diagnostic efficacy for both OP and FS. Immune infiltration analysis revealed distinct immune cell patterns in OP, correlating with the expression of key gene. Regulatory network analysis demonstrated complex transcriptional regulation of SDC1 and ELN. Drug prediction identified five candidate small molecules targeting these genes. This study uncovered shared genetic features of FS and OP through comprehensive bioinformatics analysis, enhancing understanding of their co-morbidity mechanisms. These findings provide a theoretical basis for identifying novel diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets, facilitating the development of precise diagnostic strategies for OP with FS.


## INTRODUCTION
  Osteoporosis (OP) is a degenerative disease most common in older adults, characterized by reduced bone mass and degradation of bone microarchitecture. These changes significantly increase bone fragility and fracture risk [1]. OP is prevalent worldwide, especially among postmenopausal women, the elderly, and individuals with small bone structures [2,3]. Fractures related to OP, especially in the hip, spine, and wrist, not only seriously affect patients' quality of life, but also impose substantial medical and economic burdens [4,5]. With the aging of the population, the incidence of OP continues to rise and has become an important challenge in public health [6,7]. Therefore, early identification of high-risk groups, along with effective prevention and treatment strategies, is important for reducing fracture risk and improving patient outcomes.

  Frozen shoulder (FS), also known as adhesive capsulitis, is a common shoulder disorder affecting older adults, characterized by shoulder pain and restricted range of motion [8]. The peak incidence occurs between 40 and 60 years of ages, with a slightly higher prevalence in women than in men, and patients with underlying conditions, such as diabetes mellitus, are at increased risk of developing FS [9-11]. Clinically, patients with FS often experience three phases: pain, stiffness, and recovery, and the pain is mostly aggravated at night, which seriously affects patients' sleep and quality of life [12-14]. In addition, FS can cause not only shoulder joint dysfunction but also muscle atrophy and chronic pain around the shoulder, leading to long-term physical and psychological burdens [15]. Therefore, early diagnosis and appropriate rehabilitation are crucial for symptom relief and functional recovery of the shoulder joint.

  OP and FS, as two common geriatric diseases that seriously affect patients' quality of life, have a potential pathologic association. OP is a systemic metabolic disorder characterized by an imbalance between bone resorption and formation, influenced by factors such as genetics, hormone levels, nutrition, and lifestyle [16,17]. In contrast, FS, involves chronic inflammation of the soft tissues surrounding the shoulder joint, leading to shoulder pain, limited mobility, muscle atrophy and joint dysfunction [10,18]. Its pathology mainly includes chronic inflammation and fibrosis of periarticular soft tissues, resulting in thickening, adhesion, and stiffness that restrict normal shoulder movement [19-21]. Currently, it has been suggested that there may be a potential pathologic link between patients with OP and FS, especially in postmenopausal women [22]. Shared pathological mechanisms such as inflammation, oxidative stress, and immune regulation play important roles in both diseases [23-25]. Inflammation disrupts the immune system, causing the release of pro-inflammatory factors like IL-1β and IL-18 through key pathways such as NF-κB and NLRP3 inflammasomes. This worsens bone metabolism problems and causes fibrosis and soft tissue changes around the shoulder joint in FS [26,27]. Hormonal changes, especially in oestrogen levels, also significantly affect both FS and OP by influencing inflammation and tissue repair, playing a key role in their shared disease mechanisms [28,29]. In addition, while OP causes systemic bone fragility, patients with FS may develop localized OP due to prolonged restriction of shoulder movement [30-32]. However, the molecular mechanisms underlying the coexistence of OP with FS and their common pathological features remain unclear. Therefore, revealing their common genetic features and signaling pathways is essential for improving the understanding of their association and advancing combined diagnostic strategies.

  In recent years, rapid advancements in high-throughput sequencing technologies and large-scale bioinformatics databases have made integrated bioinformatics analyses a key approach to uncover the mechanisms underlying complex diseases [33-35]. In this study, we combined gene expression data from OP and FS with machine learning algorithms to identify shared gene features and key signaling pathways. This approach not only enhances the understanding of the molecular mechanisms driving OP with FS but also provides a theoretical foundation and novel candidate biomarkers for clinical diagnosis and personalized treatment strategies.


## METHODS

### Data acquisition
  The OP-related microarray dataset GSE35958 was downloaded from the Gene Expression Omnibus (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) database, including a total of 9 samples, consisting of 5 OP cases and 4 controls. FS-related microarray dataset GSE238053 contained 8 samples, with 4 FS cases and 4 controls. In addition, the GSE35956, GSE56116, and GSE230665 for OP and the GSE140731 dataset for FS were downloaded as independent test sets. GSE35956 comprised 10 samples (5 OP cases and 5 controls), GSE56116 included 13 samples (10 OP cases and 3 controls), and GSE230665 contained 15 samples (12 OP cases and 3 controls). The FS dataset consisted of 48 samples, including 22 FS cases and 26 controls.


### Differential expression analysis
  Differential expression analysis between disease and control groups for both OP and FS was performed using the “limma” package in R. The screening threshold was p-value < 0.05 and |log fold change (FC)| > 1. Afterwards, the differential expressed genes (DEGs) of OP and FS were intersected to obtain the common DEGs. The intersected genes were subjected to Gene Ontology (GO) functional enrichment using the “clusterProfiler” package.


### Creation of protein-protein interaction (PPI) network and identification of key modules
  The intersected genes were imported into the STRING database (https://www.string-db.org/) to predict PPI, using a confidence threshold > 0.15 to construct the PPI network. Subsequently, the above network was imported into Cytoscape v3.8.0 software for visualization. Functional modules were identified with the MCODE plug-in (parameters: degree cutoff = 2, node score cutoff = 0.2, K-core = 2, and max depth = 100). The module with the highest scores was defined as the key modules, and the genes within this module were considered pivotal genes. GO enrichment analysis of these hub genes was performed using the “clusterProfiler” package.


### Machine learning screening for diagnostic genes
  To further identify key genes, two machine learning algorithms were used: Random Forest (RF) and Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression. RF and LASSO analyses were performed using the “randomForest” and “glmnet” packages, respectively, to select important feature genes. The intersection of the results from both methods was taken to obtain candidate diagnostic genes with potential diagnostic value. RF employed 10-fold cross-validation to partition and validate the data, ensuring the model's generalization ability and stability. Genes that significantly contributed to classification were selected by calculating their feature importance metrics (Mean Decrease Gini). For LASSO regression analysis, the optimal regularization parameter λ (lambda) was automatically selected via 10-fold cross-validation by minimizing the cross-validation error (lambda.min). LASSO used L1 norm regularization to effectively control model complexity, perform variable selection, remove redundant features, and retain genes that contributed most to classification performance.


### Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis and diagnostic model construction and evaluation
  In the GSE35958 and GSE238053 datasets, ROC curves for the candidate genes were generated using the “pROC” package. Genes with an area under the curve (AUC) greater than 0.7 were considered to have significant diagnostic value. Based on these selected diagnostic genes, models for OP and FS were constructed. The clinical utility of these models was evaluated using Decision Curve Analysis (DCA), and nomograms were drawn with the “rmda” package. Model validation was performed using independent datasets GSE35956, GSE56116, GSE230665, and GSE140731. In addition, boxplots illustrating the expression levels of diagnostic genes in both disease and control groups for OP and FS were generated, accompanied by single-gene enrichment analyses.


### Immune microenvironment analysis
  In order to further characterize the immune microenvironment in OP, the ssGSEA algorithm in the “GSVA” package was used to calculate the enrichment scores of 29 immune cells. In addition, the absolute abundance of immune cells was estimated using the MCP-counter algorithm form the “iobr” package. Meanwhile, the Pearson correlation analysis was then performed to assess the relationship between immune cell infiltration levels and the expression of diagnostic genes.


### Regulatory network construction and small molecule drug prediction
  MicroRNAs (miRNAs) targeting the diagnostic genes were predicted using miRNet (https://www.mirnet.ca/) database, and miRNA-gene regulatory network was constructed. Upstream transcription factors (TFs) of diagnostic genes were predicted via NetworkAnalyst (https://www.networkanalyst.ca/) to construct TFs-gene regulatory networks. All networks were visualized using Cytoscape software. In addition, potential small-molecule drug candidates targeting the diagnostic genes were predicted based on the DSigDB (http://dsigdb.tanlab.org/DSigDBv1.0/) database combined with the Enrichr tool (https://maayanlab.cloud/modEnrichr/).


### Identification of OP subtypes
  In order to further investigate the role of characterized genes in OP, OP samples from datasets GSE35958, GSE35956, GSE56116 and GSE230665 were combined, and the ComBat method in the “sva” package was used to correct for batch effects. Based on the diagnostic genes obtained from the screening, the “ConsensusClusterPlus” package was used for consistent clustering analysis to classify OP subtypes. The clustering results were validated using Principal Component Analysis (PCA) analysis. At the same time, the expression boxplots of the diagnostic genes among different subtypes were generated. In addition, immune cell infiltration differences among subtypes were analyzed using the ssGSEA and CIBERSORT algorithms. Finally, differential expression analysis between subtypes (p-value < 0.05, |log(FC)| > 0.585) was performed, and the obtained DEGs were subjected for GO and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analyses.


### Statistical analysis
  All analyses were performed using R (v4.4.1) and relevant packages. The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used to compare differences between two groups. ROC curves were generated using the “timeROC” package. Data visualization was primarily performed with the “ggplot2” package. Pearson's correlation coefficient was used to assess associations between variables. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05. p-values are denoted as follows: ***, p < 0.001; **, 0.001 < p < 0.01; *, 0.01 < p < 0.05; ns indicates no significant difference.


## RESULTS

### Identification of common differential genes in OP and FS
  Differential expression analyses were performed separately for the OP and FS disease groups compared to their respective controls. In the OP group, 2,197 DEGs were identified in total (Fig. 1A). In the FS group, 1,081 DEGs were identified in total (Fig. 1B). The intersection of the DEGs in the two groups was further taken and 111 common DEGs were obtained (Fig. 1C). GO enrichment analysis was performed on this intersection of genes. The results showed that these genes were significantly enriched in pathways related to, for example, nervous system development and synaptic regulation, suggesting that these genes may play a role in embryonic organ branching, nervous system development, and homeostatic regulation (Fig. 1D).


### PPI network analysis and module identification
  The intersecting genes obtained above were imported into the STRING database to construct a PPI network using a confidence threshold greater than 0.15 (Fig. 2A). Subsequently, the MCODE plugin in Cytoscape was used to perform module segmentation of the network, and five functional modules were identified (Fig. 2B-F, Supplementary Table 1). Among them, the module shown in Fig. 2B had the highest score and was defined as a key module, containing 14 hub genes. GO enrichment analysis of the hub genes was performed (Fig. 2G), and GO bioprocess analysis showed that these genes were significantly enriched in pathways related to the regulation of enzyme activities, reflecting their important roles in cell-cycle regulation, signaling, and maintenance of cellular function and homeostasis. Cellular composition analysis showed that the proteins encoded by these genes were mainly localized in elastic fibers, the Golgi lumen, and the PCNA-p21 complex, suggesting that they play a central role in maintaining the structural integrity of tissues, protein processing and transport, as well as in key cellular functions such as cell cycle regulation and DNA repair. In addition, molecular functional analyses revealed that these genes participate in the development and functional maintenance of the nervous system, as well as in the normal development and regulation of the reproductive system.


### Machine learning to identify feature genes
  For the 14 pivotal genes within the key modules, two machine learning algorithms were applied to further identify important feature variables. Using the MeanDecreaseGini index (threshold > 1) for RF and the optimal penalty parameter (lambda.min) for LASSO regression, 7 and 3 key variables were selected, respectively (Fig. 3A-D). Subsequently, the intersection of these results yielded 2 overlapping key feature genes: SDC1 and ELN (Fig. 3E). The expression levels of these 2 genes in OP and FS were counted, and it was found that both SDC1 and ELN were highly expressed in OP and FS (Fig. 3F). In addition, single gene enrichment analysis was performed for SDC1 and ELN (Fig. 3G, H). The results indicated that SDC1 plays important roles in host-pathogen interactions and immune defense, potentially regulating immune recognition, signaling and effector mechanisms. In contrast, ELN is mainly involved in regulating cellular signaling networks, promoting tissue and organ morphogenesis, and maintaining cellular homeostasis, revealing its relevance to developmental biology and cellular communication.


### ROC curve analysis and diagnostic model construction and validation
  In the GSE35958 and GSE238053 datasets, we performed ROC curve analysis to evaluate the diagnostic performance of the characterized genes SDC1 and ELN (Fig. 4A, D). The results showed that the AUC of SDC1 and ELN exceeded 0.7 in both OP and FS patients, indicating a strong and consistent association with disease status and good diagnostic potential as biomarkers. Based on these two genes, we constructed diagnostic models for OP and FS, respectively. In the OP training set GSE35958, the AUC of the model reached 1, showing extremely high diagnostic accuracy (Fig. 4A). Fig. 4B illustrates the nomogram of the OP-related diagnostic model. The DCA curve of the model further validated its excellent potential for clinical application (Fig. 4C). Similarly, in the FS training set GSE238053, the diagnostic model reached an AUC of 0.938. The nomogram and DCA curves are shown in Fig. 4E, F, respectively, consistently supporting the validity of the model. In addition, we evaluated the generalization ability of the model in several independent validation sets. These include three validation sets for OP (GSE35956, GSE56116, and GSE230665) and one validation set for FS (GSE140731). The validation results showed that the AUCs of the models in all datasets were higher than 0.7, further demonstrating the robustness and utility of SDC1 and ELN as diagnostic genes in OP and FS (Fig. 4G, H). In summary, SDC1 and ELN are not only closely associated with OP and FS, but also possess strong disease recognition ability and have the potential to become clinical diagnostic biomarkers.


### Immune-related analysis
  To comprehensively explore the immune microenvironment characteristics in OP patients, we applied the ssGSEA method to calculate enrichment scores of 29 immune cell types and immune functions based on gene expression data. The results showed that the levels of immune infiltration of plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDCs), T helper cells, antigen-presenting cell co-stimulation (APC co-stimulation), and chemotactic receptor CCR-associated pathway were significantly elevated in OP patients compared with controls (Fig. 5A, B). Subsequently, the differences in immune cell infiltration were further validated using the MCP-counter algorithm, which revealed significantly higher levels of CD8+ T cells and fibroblasts infiltration in OP patients (Fig. 5C). Multimethod immune analysis showed important changes in immune cells and pathways in OP, indicating that immune cells are key in its development. In addition, we calculated the Pearson correlation coefficients between the levels of immune cell infiltration and the key diagnostic genes SDC1 and ELN. The results showed that SDC1 expression was significantly positively correlated with the infiltration of pDCs and T helper cells, while it was significantly negatively correlated with the infiltration of immature dendritic cells (iDCs) (Fig. 5D). ELN expression, on the other hand, was significantly positively correlated with the infiltration levels of chemokine receptors (CCRs), neutrophils and pDCs (Fig. 5E). These results suggest that SDC1 and ELN may play an important role in regulating the OP immune microenvironment.


### Potential TFs and miRNA target gene regulatory network construction and small molecule drug prediction
  In order to predict the upstream regulators of the diagnostic genes SDC1 and ELN, we first screened the TFs potentially regulating these genes using the NetworkAnalyst, identifying a total of 11 candidate TFs. Among them, GATA2 and FOXC1 were found to regulate both SDC1 and ELN, suggesting that they might play a key role in the regulation of the expression of these two genes. Fig. 6A demonstrates the constructed TFs-gene regulatory network. Meanwhile, miRNAs targeting SDC1 and ELN were predicted using miRNet, resulting in 32 candidate miRNAs, with 10 targeting both genes. Fig. 6B presents the corresponding miRNA-gene regulatory network, revealing the complex non-coding RNA regulatory mechanism. In addition, based on the DSigDB database, we further predicted potential small molecule drugs that might target SDC1 and ELN. The top five candidate drugs are listed in Table 1, and detailed results for all predicted drugs are provided in Supplementary Table 2. These drugs provide important references for subsequent functional validation and clinical applications.


### Subtype identification in OP
  To further explore the role of diagnostic genes in OP, we integrated samples from four OP-related datasets (GSE35958, GSE35956, GSE56116 and GSE230665). Fig. 7A, B demonstrate the distribution of samples before and after removing the batch effect, respectively. The results indicate that the batch effect has been effectively eliminated, ensuring the reliability of the subsequent analysis. Based on the expression of the diagnostic genes SDC1 and ELN, the combined OP samples were classified into subtypes using the consistency clustering algorithm with the “ConsensusClusterPlus” package (Fig. 7C-E). The consensus matrix heatmap showed clear boundaries and strong stability at k = 2. The cumulative distribution function curve’s area increment was largest at k = 2 and noticeably decreased at k = 3, indicating diminishing returns in cluster stability improvement. Choosing k = 2 also avoided overly dispersed samples caused by too many clusters, which could have reduced statistical efficiency in subsequent analyses. The validity of clustering and the distinct separation of subtypes were confirmed by PCA (Fig. 7F). Further analysis showed that the expression levels of SDC1 and ELN were significantly higher in subtype 1 than subtype 2 (Fig. 7G). In order to explore the immune characteristics of different subtypes, we used the ssGSEA method to calculate the level of immune cell infiltration, which showed that the infiltration of activated dendritic cells and follicular helper T cells was significantly higher in subtype 1 than subtype 2 (Fig. 7H). These findings suggest that subtype 1 has stronger antigen presentation and T cell-helper activity, which may lead to significant differences in its immune microenvironment characteristics. Further analysis using the CIBERSORT algorithm revealed that resting natural killer cells (NK cells resting) had higher levels of infiltration in subtype 2 (Fig. 7I), which may reflect a relatively weaker cytotoxic immune response in this subtype. Immune infiltration analysis showed that the two subtypes have clearly different immune profiles, suggesting they have different immune regulation that could affect disease development and treatment outcomes. Finally, we analyzed the gene expression differences between the two subtypes, identified a total of 345 DEGs, and performed GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses based on the DEGs. The results showed that these DEGs were significantly enriched in the biological processes of protease inhibitor activity, hyaluronic acid binding, heme binding, and hormone binding, suggesting that these genes may be involved in the regulation of protease activity, extracellular matrix interactions, redox metabolism, and hormone signaling (Fig. 7J). KEGG pathway analysis showed that the DEGs were enriched in staphylococcus aureus infection and complement and coagulation cascades, suggesting that these genes may play a key role in the immune response to infection and the regulation of inflammation (Fig. 7K).


## DISCUSSION
  FS and OP often co-occur, especially in postmenopausal women [32,36]. This study used bioinformatics to identify common molecular features and pathways linking OP and FS. Machine learning found two key diagnostic biomarkers, SDC1 and ELN, which are highly expressed in both diseases and have diagnostic value. We built regulatory networks and predicted potential drugs to understand their roles. Immune microenvironment and OP subtype analyses revealed disease heterogeneity and immune mechanisms. In summary, this study offers molecular evidence of OP and FS comorbidity and guides future diagnosis and treatment development.

  Our study identified common DEGs in OP and FS and analyzed their functions. These genes were enriched in neurodevelopment, cell signaling, and immune regulation pathways. Neurodevelopmental involvement highlights the role of pain transmission and neuromodulation in both diseases [37,38]. FS and other musculoskeletal disorders cause central nervous system remodeling in pain-processing brain areas, worsening chronic pain and motor dysfunction [39,40]. OP’s pathogenesis also links to nervous system dysfunction and neuropsychiatric factors like stress, affecting bone metabolism [41,42]. Common DEGs related to nerve pathways provide a molecular basis for pain and motor issues in OP and FS. Both diseases involve complex signaling, inflammation, and immune regulation. OP mainly results from imbalance between osteoblasts and osteoclasts, regulated by pathways like RANKL/RANK/OPG, Wnt/β-catenin, and JAK/STAT, influenced by cytokines such as IL-6, TNF-α, and IL-17 [43-48]. FS, a chronic inflammatory disease, features joint inflammation and fibrosis driven by NF-κB and MAPK pathways, causing tissue adhesion and movement limitation [27,49,50]. FS patients often have local OP, indicating cross-talk between inflammation and bone metabolism [22,30]. In summary, signaling and immune pathways intertwine in FS and OP, driving disease progression and their comorbidity. Additionally, we also identified two OP molecular subtypes. GO analysis showed the subtype DEGs involved protease inhibition, hyaluronan, haem, and hormone binding, all linked to bone metabolism and tissue homeostasis, highlighting differences in extracellular matrix and signaling [51]. KEGG analysis found DEGs enriched in Staphylococcus aureus infection and complement/coagulation pathways, suggesting infection-related immunity and inflammation play roles in OP subtypes. Inflammation and immune dysregulation are known to impact OP development, especially disrupting bone remodeling balance [52]. These findings underscore key biological differences between OP subtypes and offer molecular targets for future therapies.

  Our study identified two key diagnostic biomarkers, SDC1 and ELN. SDC1 is a cell surface protein involved in cell adhesion, signaling, immune defense, and bone metabolism, contributing to OP and FS development [53-56]. It is linked to osteoclastogenesis, bone destruction, and fibrotic remodeling in FS [57,58]. ELN encodes elastin, essential for tissue elasticity and cell communication [59-61]. Abnormal ELN expression may cause fibrosis and alter bone structure, playing a key role in OP and FS progression [62-66]. Both genes are highly expressed in OP and FS, and ROC analysis shows they have strong diagnostic value, making them promising biomarkers and therapeutic targets.

  To understand the characteristics of the immune microenvironment in OP patients, we performed immune infiltration analysis. The results showed significantly elevated infiltration levels of various immune cells in OP patients, consistent with the classification of OP as an “immune-sparing disorder” [25,67]. The results showed that, compared to controls, OP patients had higher activity of pDCs, T helper cells, APC co-stimulation, and CCR-related pathways, suggesting an active immune role in bone surveillance and inflammation. Further analysis with the MCP-counter algorithm confirmed a significant increase in CD8+ T cells and fibroblasts in OP patients. For example, CD8+ T cells and fibroblasts were significantly increased. CD8+ T cells, as effector cells, may be involved in immunosurveillance and inflammatory regulation of bone tissue, while the increase in fibroblasts suggests an active remodelling of the bone matrix and fibrosis, which may affect the structural stability of the bone tissue [68-70]. The multi-method immune infiltration analysis showed dynamic changes in key immune cells and pathways in OP, highlighting the important role of immune cells and their dysfunction in OP development. We also calculated the Pearson correlation coefficients between immune cell infiltration levels and the expression of SDC1 and ELN. SDC1 expression was positively correlated with pDCs and T helper cells, but negatively correlated with iDCs, suggesting that SDC1 may modulate the direction and intensity of immune responses by regulating specific dendritic cell subsets [71]. Conversely, ELN expression showed significant positive correlations with CCRs, neutrophils, and pDCs infiltration levels, indicating an important role for ELN in regulating immune cell migration and shaping the inflammatory microenvironment [72-74].

  We predicted upstream regulators of SDC1 and ELN, focusing on TFs and miRNAs. GATA2 and FOXC1 may co-regulate both genes. GATA2 is involved in cell differentiation, proliferation, immune regulation, and bone metabolism [75,76]. FOXC1 regulates embryonic development, tissue homeostasis, extracellular matrix, and fibrosis [77-79]. Their shared regulation suggests similar transcriptional control in OP and FS pathology. We also identified miRNAs targeting SDC1 and ELN’s 3’ untranslated regions, modulating their expression [80,81]. These findings reveal complex regulation mechanisms and offer potential targets for future validation and therapies. In addition, we predicted small molecule drugs that may target SDC1 and ELN. Metronidazole targets both by disrupting pathogen DNA [82]. Though its direct effects on OP and FS are rare, its anti-inflammatory properties may influence related diseases [83]. Thalidomide has immunomodulatory and anti-inflammatory effects, inhibits TNF-α, and regulates the extracellular matrix [84], with evidence suggesting bone protection and OP treatment potential [85]. Melphalan, lenalidomide, and D-penicillamine also show potential, indicating multi-target drugs may benefit FS and OP, but clinical trials are needed to confirm safety and efficacy.

  In summary, this study advanced understanding of OP and FS comorbidity and identified two key diagnostic biomarkers, SDC1 and ELN, offering new targets for diagnosis and treatment. However, limitations include reliance on public databases with a small sample size, raising risks of overfitting despite cross-validation. Future work should validate findings in larger, independent cohorts. The study focused on common genes and pathways, but specific mechanisms remain unclear. Also, using p < 0.05 without multiple testing correction may increase false positives. Functional validation with cellular and animal experiments is needed. Despite these limits, the study provides valuable insights into the molecular link between OP and FS with clinical significance.

  In conclusion, this study reveals common molecular features of OP and FS, improving understanding of their comorbidity. SDC1 and ELN serve as biomarkers for early diagnosis and risk assessment, and guide targeted therapy development. The findings provide potential targets and interventions to advance precision diagnosis and treatment of OP and FS.
