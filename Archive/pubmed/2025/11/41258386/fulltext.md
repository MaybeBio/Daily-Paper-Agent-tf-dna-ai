# High ORC6 expression is a prognostic indicator of poor survival in glioma patients


## Abstract
  Precision therapy for glioma remains a major challenge due to tumor heterogeneity. The Origin Recognition Complex Subunit 6 (ORC6) is a crucial regulator of DNA replication initiation. This study aims to investigate the expression of ORC6 in gliomas and its relationship with survival rates and malignancy, while screening potential drugs targeting its functional network. By integrating multiple bioinformatics approaches with structure-based virtual screening, retrospective RNA sequencing data analysis was performed using patients from the Chinese Glioma Genome Atlas (CGGA) and The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) databases. A protein-protein interaction (PPI) network was constructed from ORC6-coexpressed genes to identify core hubs. Molecular docking was employed to screen a library of natural compounds and known drugs against these hub targets. Research has revealed that ORC6 is significantly upregulated in high-grade gliomas, with its elevated expression associated with poor survival outcomes and immune inflammatory responses. Network analysis identified five core hub genes (ORC1, ORC2, MCM2, MCM6, CDC45) central to DNA replication. Molecular docking revealed that several compounds, including the natural flavonoid Baicalein and the FDA-approved drug Palbociclib, exhibited high binding affinity to these hub targets. ORC6 represents a highly promising novel target for precision therapy in glioma. Potential approaches to target this pathway include disrupting the ORC6-replication axis using existing drugs (such as palbociclib) or natural products (such as baicalin).


## Introduction
  Gliomas are the most common primary malignant tumors of the central nervous system, with their malignancy closely related to pathological grading1. According to the World Health Organization (WHO) classification, grade III (anaplastic gliomas) and grade IV(glioblastomas) are classified as high-grade gliomas, accounting for more than 60% of all glioma cases. High rates of recurrence and invasiveness are characteristics of these cancers2,3. According to clinical statistics, patients with grade III gliomas have a median survival time of roughly three to four years, while for grade IV patients, survival is significantly shorter, ranging from 1 to 2 years, with some cases surviving less than six months4,5. Although current treatment strategies, primarily consisting of surgical resection combined with radiotherapy and chemotherapy, can extend survival in some patients, the long-term prognosis remains poor due to high tumor heterogeneity, strong infiltration into normal brain tissue, and the development of resistance to conventional therapies6,7.

  In recent years, the progress in molecular biology techniques has led to the inclusion of more molecular markers in the diagnosis and classification of gliomas. For example, molecular characteristics such as isocitrate dehydrogenase (IDH) mutations and 1p/19q co-deletion have been included in the WHO glioma classification system to more accurately predict patient prognosis8,9. However, research on proteins involved in DNA replication regulation remains relatively limited. ORC6, a key regulatory factor in DNA replication initiation, plays a central role in cell cycle progression10. Studies have shown that ORC family proteins are abnormally overexpressed in various solid tumors and may drive malignant progression by promoting tumor cell proliferation and inhibiting apoptosis11–13.

  A study has demonstrated that ORC6 is overexpressed in glioma, promotes tumor cell growth both in vitro and in vivo, and is transcriptionally regulated by RBPJ, establishing it as a promising oncotarget14. Building upon this important finding and the broader context of DNA replication proteins in cancer, we aimed to conduct a comprehensive bioinformatic and experimental analysis of ORC6 to independently validate its clinical significance and explore its unexplored potential as a prognostic biomarker and a node for targeted therapy in glioma. Using data from CGGA and TCGA, we analyzed ORC6 expression across glioma subtypes and assessed its association with clinicopathological features and survival. PPI network analysis identified co-expressed genes, and molecular docking was used to screen potential targeting compounds. Our study aims to firmly establish ORC6 as a key player in glioma malignancy and to advance its translational potential in precision oncology.


## Methods

### Data acquisition and processing
  For this study, the sequencing and clinical follow-up details of the patients were sourced from the CGGA-325 cohort (https://www.cgga.org.cn/), which is available to the public. The validation cohort’s clinical and follow-up information was downloaded from a separate public database(https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/).The specimens in this study were retrospectively collected with approval from the Ethics Committee of Tianjin Huanhu Hospital. The grading of each sample was diagnosed by two neuropathologists.


### Variance analysis
  To investigate the connection between ORC6 expression and several glioma clinical features, we used the ggplot2 package in R to analyze the pertinence and differential enrichment of ORC6 expression across various WHO grades, IDH status, MGMT status, and 1p/19q status. The results were presented as box plots. Additionally, ROC curves were developed with the help of the pROC package in R, calculating the specificity, sensitivity, and area under the curve (AUC) of ORC6 as an indicator for WHO grading.


### Functional enrichment analysis
  To delve the mechanisms by which ORC6 expression affects tumor prognosis, we explored the signaling pathways associated with ORC6. The analysis involved uploading relevant gene lists and cell clusters to the Database for Annotation, Visualization, and Integrated Discovery.Analysis of the obtained results was conducted using Gene Ontology (GO) and the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG).Sort the terms with p < 0.05 in ascending order, select the top 5 items, and use the ggplot2 package in R to generate a bubble chart.


### Gene set variation analysis (GSVA)
  The gene list was obtained via the GSEA portal (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/index.jsp). For each glioma sample, we determined the functional enrichment scores using R’s “GSVA” package(Gene Set Variation Analysis ‌1.42.0‌ https://www.bioconductor.org/). The results were visualized as a heatmap using the pheatmap package (packageVersion“pheatmap”1.0.12 https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/pheatmap/index.html). Additionally, The association between ORC6 expression and specific biological processes was evaluated using Pearson correlation analysis.


### Immunohistochemistry
  The glioma tissue was fixed in neutral formalin, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned. After dewaxing, the sections were washed with a buffer solution and incubated with hydrogen peroxide for blocking. The sections were then incubated overnight with ORC6 antibody (1:200). Following this, the sections were incubated with a 1:500 dilution of secondary antibody at room temperature for 30 min. DAB staining solution was applied for 20 min, and the slides were subsequently rinsed thoroughly with tap water. Counterstaining and dehydration were performed, and the slides were mounted for examination. Microscopic analysis was carried out, and the results were interpreted using image acquisition software (ImageJ Java 1.8.0_322 http://jmage.org).


### Network pharmacology and molecular docking analysis
  Using the results obtained from the above steps, co-expressed genes of ORC6 were identified through Pearson correlation analysis. Genes were considered statistically significant when their FDR-corrected p-value was < 0.05 and their correlation coefficient (r) was > 0.3. To further focus on biologically relevant pathways highlighted in the initial findings, these genes were cross-analyzed with gene sets from the KEGG database related to ‘Cell Cycle’ and ‘Immune/Inflammatory Response’. This process identified a core target gene set for subsequent network pharmacology analysis. To ensure comprehensive coverage of potential targets, we combined the union of core genes identified from both the TCGA and CGGA databases for subsequent protein-protein interaction (PPI) network construction.

  Integrate core target genes from TCGA and CGGA for protein-protein interaction network construction. Core genes were obtained through three approaches for molecular docking. Literature mining in PubMed using the keywords (glioma OR glioblastoma) AND (natural compound OR phytochemical OR traditional medicine) AND (apoptosis OR proliferation) identified natural compounds proven effective in in vitro and in vivo experiments to inhibit glioma cell proliferation and induce apoptosis. Subsequently, molecular docking analysis was performed using AutoDock Vina software to evaluate the binding potential of these compounds with the core genes.


## Result

### ORC6 expression in gliomas correlates with the degree of malignancy
  There is a strong correlation between the clinicopathological features of glioma patients and the expression level of ORC6. In both the CGGA and TCGA databases, increased ORC6 expression was linked to an asymmetric distribution of MGMT promoter methylation status, 1p/19q codeletion status, IDH mutation status, WHO grade, and histological diagnosis (Figs. 1A and 2A). Analysis of different subgroups in the CGGA database revealed that higher WHO grades and 1p/19q non-codeletion gliomas exhibited higher ORC6 enrichment (Fig. 1B–E). This trend was further validated in the TCGA database (Fig. 2B–E). Additionally, IDH-wildtype gliomas showed greater ORC6 enrichment in both databases, although this trend did not reach statistical significance in the CGGA database. Moreover, in the TCGA database, patients without MGMT promoter methylation exhibited higher ORC6 expression (Fig. 2D).

  Overall, ORC6 expression was elevated in more aggressive gliomas. Since WHO grading is associated with tumor malignancy, ROC curve analysis was conducted to assess the specificity and sensitivity of ORC6 in distinguishing different glioma grades. According to the findings, ORC6’s area under the curve (AUC) in the CGGA database was 70.5%, while in the TCGA database, the AUC reached 85.9%, indicating that ORC6 is a potential biomarker for higher-grade, more malignant gliomas (Fig. 3).

### Biological function and signaling pathway analysis of ORC6 in glioma pathogenesis
  To examine further the part ORC6 plays in the pathogenesis of glioma, we screened ORC6-associated biological functions using publicly available databases (CGGA and TCGA). The selection criteria were based on Pearson correlation analysis. The identified biological functions were then subjected to GO and KEGG analysis. In the CGGA database, ORC6 was primarily associated with biological functions such as mRNA splicing via spliceosome, DNA replication, chromosome segregation, DNA repair, and cell division. The most relevant cellular component was the nucleus, while protein binding was the most enriched molecular function (Fig. 4A–C). The spliceosome, DNA replication, and cell cycle signaling pathways were the ones most strongly associated with ORC6 Fig. 4D). In the TCGA database, the biological function most correlated with ORC6 was regulation of transcription by RNA polymerase II, while nucleoplasm was identified as the most relevant cellular component. ORC6-related molecular functions included metal ion binding and identical protein binding (Fig. 4E–H). These observations imply that ORC6 is essential for DNA replication, cell proliferation, and cell cycle management in glioma cells, emphasizing its potential role in glioma development.

### ORC6 positively correlates with the cell cycle during glioma development
  Abnormal DNA replication, cell proliferation, and cell cycle dysregulation play a crucial role in tumor development and progression. To assess the impact of ORC6 on DNA replication and the cell cycle, we analyzed data from the CGGA and TCGA databases. GSVA was used to obtain enrichment scores for related genes. According to the findings, ORC6 had a positive correlation with the majority of cell cycle-related processes. These findings indicate a strong association between ORC6 and key processes in glioma cell proliferation, including DNA replication and RNA transcription (Fig. 5).

### High expression of ORC6 positively correlates with the immune-inflammatory response of tumor cells
  Tumor immunity plays a crucial role in glioma treatment. We investigated the relationship between ORC6, key immune checkpoints, and KI67. The selected immune checkpoints included PD-2 and TIGIT (Fig. 6A). To assess immune status, we selected five immune-related metagene clusters as markers, including IgG, interferon, major histocompatibility complex (MHC), and signal transducer and activator of transcription 1/2 (STAT1/2)15–17. The findings indicated that ORC6 and these immune-related markers were positively correlated (Fig. 6B–C). Additionally, ORC6 expression was strongly associated with inflammatory responses, further highlighting its potential role in glioma immunity.

### ORC6 is a standalone prognostic marker for the overall survival of individuals with glioma
  We used the Cox proportional hazards model and Kaplan-Meier survival curves to examine data from public databases in order to assess the prognostic significance of ORC6 expression in gliomas (Fig. 7). Patients with low ORC6 expression showed a considerably higher (median survival:1409 days) than those with high ORC6 expression (median survival: 424 days). The TCGA database similarly supported this finding. Furthermore, Cox analysis identified ORC6 expression as an independent prognostic factor, regardless of other variables include MGMT promoter methylation, 1p/19q co-deletion, IDH mutation, WHO grade, and age upon diagnosis (Tables 1 and 2).

### Identification of potential therapeutic compounds by targeting ORC6-Related hub genes
  To translate the ORC6-associated gene signature into therapeutic strategies, we first constructed a protein-protein interaction network from the 95 genes most closely correlated with ORC6 expression. Using the STRING database and Cytoscape, we generated a robust network comprising 91 nodes and 506 edges, wherein the color intensity of each node was proportional to its degree of connectivity (Fig. 9). Topological analysis using three distinct algorithms (MCC, MNC, and Degree) identified 15 potential core genes within this network.

  Fig. 9PPI network diagram of core genes closely related to ORC6. The color intensity of the node is proportional to its degree of connectivity, with darker red indicating a higher degree.

  Our bioinformatic analysis revealed that ORC6 expression is tightly co-regulated with a core set of genes exclusively involved in DNA replication initiation. To therapeutically target this vulnerability, we focused on the most critical and non-redundant hubs representing key functional components of the replication machinery: ORC1 and ORC2 (representing the origin recognition complex), MCM2 and MCM6 (representing the core helicase engine), and CDC45 (representing the activation switch of the helicase).

  Subsequently, molecular docking was performed to evaluate the binding potential of seven distinct drug candidates to the protein structures of these five core hubs. The predicted binding energies (Vina scores) for all compound-target pairs ranged from − 9.4 to − 5.3 kcal/mol, with several interactions demonstrating strong binding affinity (≤ − 8.5 kcal/mol) (Fig. 10).

  Fig. 10Binding energy between candidate compounds and core genes. Values represent the predicted binding energy (Vina score, kcal/mol).Color is associated with binding energy. The darker the color, the stronger the binding energy.

  Notably, the classic chemotherapeutic agent Etoposide exhibited the highest binding affinity among all pairs, with a score of − 9.4 kcal/mol for ORC1 (Fig. 11A). Intriguingly, the FDA-approved CDK4/6 inhibitor Palbociclib demonstrated strong binding potential to ORC2 (− 8.7 kcal/mol) (Fig. 11B). Among the natural compounds screened, Baicalein emerged as a top candidate, showing a high-affinity binding to CDC45 (− 9.3 kcal/mol) (Fig. 11C). Additionally, the ginsenoside 20(S)-Rg3 also showed promising binding to ORC2 (− 8.6 kcal/mol) (Fig. 11D).

## Dicussion
  In the central nervous system, gliomas are the most aggressive primary malignant tumors, making up 40% to 50% of intracranial tumors. Of them, glioblastoma (GBM, WHO grade IV) has a five-year survival rate of fewer than 10% and a median survival of about 12 to 15 months18. Despite advancements in conventional treatments like surgical procedures, radiotherapy, and chemo, glioma remains highly recurrent and treatment-resistant due to molecular heterogeneity, diffuse growth characteristics, and the blood-brain barrier19. For example, complete tumor resection is achievable in only 60% of cases, and the efficacy of temozolomide (TMZ) is largely dependent on MGMT promoter methylation status, with nearly half of the patients showing no response20. Therefore, the development of targeted therapies based on glioma molecular characteristics and the identification of novel therapeutic targets have become key strategies for overcoming current treatment limitations.

  ORC6 is a key component of the Origin Recognition Complex and a core regulatory factor in the initiation of DNA replication21. During mitosis, the CDK-dependent phosphorylation of ORC6 influences its binding affinity with the ORC and may hinder replication licensing during the S phase. Beyond its crucial role in DNA replication initiation, ORC6 is also involved in cell cycle progression and regulates the mitotic spindle checkpoint signaling pathway22.

  The expression patterns and clinical importance of ORC6 in various glioma grades and subtypes were extensively examined in this work using sequencing data from public sources. The findings demonstrated that high-grade gliomas have a considerable ORC6 enrichment, awith the highest expression observed in WHO grade IV gliomas. Additionally, ORC6 exhibited specific high expression in 1p/19q non-codeleted (no-codel) gliomas. Functional enrichment analysis (GO and GSVA) revealed a strong positive correlation between ORC6 and cell cycle-related biological processes, including DNA replication and repair, as well as RNA biosynthesis, which is consistent with previous research findings. Based on the current analysis, ORC6 may play a crucial role in glioma cell proliferation and survival by regulating the cell cycle and DNA repair mechanisms. Additionally, pathways related to RNA biosynthesis might further promote the growth and metastatic potential of tumor cells. By integrating the results from GO and KEGG analyses, we further speculate that ORC6 not only plays a key role in initiating and maintaining the cell cycle, but may also promote glioma cell proliferation through its involvement in RNA synthesis and repair mechanisms. This insight will enhance our understanding of ORC6’s function in gliomas and provide a theoretical foundation for future research. Notably, ORC6 expression was significantly positively correlated with key cell cycle regulatory genes (CCNA2, CCNB2, TOP2A), the proliferation marker Ki-67, and immune checkpoint molecules (such as TIGIT). Further analysis revealed that tumors with high ORC6 expression exhibited a pro-inflammatory microenvironment similar to that associated with these immune checkpoints. Clinical prognosis analysis demonstrated that patients with high ORC6 expression had significantly shorter overall survival, suggesting that ORC6 may serve as a biomarker for glioma malignancy and progression.

  ORC6 plays a central role in DNA replication and cell cycle regulation, and its aberrant expression can lead to genomic instability, promoting tumor progression in various malignancies. As a key component of the DNA replication initiation complex, ORC6 orchestrates the assembly of the replication initiation machinery, directly impacting cell proliferation22. Studies have shown that dysregulated ORC6 expression disrupts DNA replication, thereby driving tumorigenesis. In breast cancer, ORC6 overexpression is associated with increased tumor invasiveness and a higher incidence of poor clinical outcomes. Similarly, in lung cancer, ORC6 is significantly upregulated, promoting tumor progression and serving as a potential prognostic biomarker for poor outcomes23,24. In colorectal cancer, ORC6 has been identified as a key player in tumor initiation and progression, with its expression level considered a potential diagnostic and prognostic marker25. Furthermore, in hepatocellular carcinoma, ORC6 overexpression is related to negative prognosis, underscoring its critical role in cancer initiation and growth26. The role of ORC6 in these cancers highlights its significance not only in DNA replication but also in the broader context of cancer biology, making it a viable subject for upcoming studies and treatment initiatives.

  Previous studies have shown that ORC6 may activate PI3K/AKT in other cancers. This pathway is often disrupted in many cancers, promoting increased tumor invasiveness27. Beyond its role in the PI3K/AKT pathway, ORC6 also participates in the regulation of multiple signaling pathways and cellular processes. Studies have shown that ORC6 can influence G2/M checkpoint control through the NF-κB pathway, as well as regulate key oncogenic pathways such as E2F targets, IL6-JAK-STAT3, and TNF-α signaling, further driving tumor progression28. These findings suggest that ORC6 functions as a central regulatory hub in multiple oncogenic pathways, making it a promising target for therapeutic intervention.

  Both our work and prior studies confirm ORC6 as a robust prognostic biomarker in glioma14. However, as a complex structure, ORC6 is not an ideal drug target.Therefore, this study breaks through conventional thinking frameworks to explore targeted therapeutic strategies for genes closely associated with ORC6. Utilizing network pharmacology and molecular docking techniques, we identified multiple highly promising candidate drugs, offering potential for therapeutic translation.

  First, we validated the strategy using known chemotherapeutic agents. The traditional chemotherapeutic agent etoposide plays a crucial role in glioma treatment. Previous studies have demonstrated a strong association between etoposide and TOP2A, which was further confirmed by docking analysis (binding energy: -9.4, not shown). In this study, we observed a strong binding energy between etoposide and ORC1, suggesting a potential secondary mechanism that may enhance its efficacy in killing highly proliferative cells. Second, exploration of FDA-approved drugs (such as the CDK4/6 inhibitor palbociclib) revealed robust binding to ORC2. Although this drug primarily targets cyclin-dependent kinases, this finding suggests potential applications in glioma therapy. Finally, natural compounds also represent an important aspect of glioma treatment. In this study, baicalin demonstrated binding affinity to CDC45, a key loading protein for replication helicase, comparable to traditional chemotherapeutic agents. Ginsenoside 20(S)-Rg3 exhibits significant binding affinity to the ORC2 protein, reinforcing the potential of traditional Chinese medicine active ingredients for glioma treatment. This not only provides new support for its positioning in anticancer therapy but also positions it as a crucial component in future research and development of natural medicines targeting glioma.

  A significant correlation between ORC6 and the immunosuppressive microenvironment was found in this study, which was characterized by the upregulation of immune checkpoints and inflammatory genes.Although this finding is significant, this correlation may not mean that ORC6 has a direct immunomodulatory function.This correlation may be the result of rapid proliferation triggered by ORC6 overexpression. Previous studies have found that rapidly proliferating tumors usually generate an immunosuppressive niche to evade immune surveillance29.Therefore, this situation may be changed by directly targeting ORC6 associated genes. Destroy the core mechanism of rapid proliferation, induce the death of relevant cells (such as immunogenic cells), reduce the secretion of immunosuppressive factors, and change the tumor immune microenvironment30. The effect of reducing tumor proliferation and reprogramming the immune environment may be achieved simultaneously through one of the ways.

  Overall, the evidence highlights the critical role of ORC6 in glioma progression, where it participates in key signaling pathways, regulates cell cycle and proliferation-related genes, and it is related to prognosis and survival. Further research into ORC6 as a potential therapeutic target may open up new avenues for the development of effective glioma treatment methods.The candidates identified herein, particularly the natural compound Baicalein, offer a compelling starting point for developing novel strategies to target the replication addiction of glioma, addressing a significant unmet clinical need.
