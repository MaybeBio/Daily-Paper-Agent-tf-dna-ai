## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (Background, Methods, Results, Discussion, Limitations) as provided
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, methodological soundness, and support of claims based solely on the supplied material
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors propose that the POLD1 Ser605del variant, causative of MDPL syndrome, not only disrupts the catalytic active site and nucleotide binding but also creates a novel short linear motif (FSLYP) that mediates an aberrant, stronger interaction with the telomeric protein TRF1. This interaction, alongside dysregulated PARP1 expression, is suggested to contribute to telomere dysfunction and premature aging phenotypes in MDPL.
- **Visible evidence base** Computational predictions (docking, MD simulations, MM-GBSA, HADDOCK, GBPM), sequence motif analysis (ELM, PROSITE), and in vitro experiments (RT-qPCR, Western blot, immunoprecipitation) on fibroblasts from three MDPL patients and controls
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Supplementary figures (S1-S3) and tables referenced but not provided; full Western blot images and immunoprecipitation raw data not shown; primer sequences stated as "available upon request" rather than provided; no structural coordinates or simulation trajectory files; no statistical details for computational comparisons beyond mean values

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript addresses a clinically relevant and mechanistically underexplored question in a rare progeroid syndrome. The integration of computational structural biology with cellular validation is commendable and the hypothesis of a gain-of-interaction with TRF1 is novel. However, the evidence presented is insufficient to firmly establish the central claims. The computational pipeline has several methodological gaps, the in vitro data are based on a very small number of patients with limited statistical power, and the functional consequence of the proposed POLD1-TRF1 interaction on telomere biology is not directly demonstrated. The manuscript is promising but requires substantial revision and additional experimentation to support its conclusions.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in the fields of rare genetic disorders, progeria syndromes, DNA replication and repair, telomere biology, and computational structural biology. The proposed link between a DNA polymerase mutation and telomere-associated protein interactions could be of interest to those studying genome stability and aging mechanisms.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses a clear gap in understanding MDPL pathogenesis beyond the known catalytic defect. The combination of multiple computational approaches (docking, MD, MM-GBSA, HADDOCK, GBPM) provides a multi-layered structural perspective. The use of patient-derived fibroblasts at different passages and ages is a valuable resource. The identification of a specific novel motif (FSLYP) is a testable and concrete hypothesis.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness of computational methods
  - **Claim pointer** The claim that Ser605del "affects the DNA-binding site, impairing dTTP binding" and that the mutant shows "significantly less favorable" binding free energy.
  - **Evidence pointer** Methods: Docking and molecular dynamics; Results: Figure 1B, Figure S2, Figure S3
  - **Concern** The computational workflow has several critical limitations. First, the mutant structure was generated by simple deletion of Ser605 and energy minimization, without explicit consideration of the conformational rearrangement that might occur. Second, the docking protocol used Glide SP, which is not designed for highly flexible or induced-fit binding events, and the grid was centered on Ser604, which may bias the results. Third, the MM-GBSA calculations are based on a single 100 ns trajectory per system, which is generally considered too short for converged free energy estimates, and no error bars or replicate simulations are shown. The claim of a ~15 kcal/mol difference in ΔGbind is presented without statistical validation. Fourth, the RMSD analysis (Figure S3) is referenced but not shown, making it impossible to assess simulation stability. The conclusion that the mutant impairs dTTP binding is plausible but not rigorously established by the data presented.
  - **Why it matters** The entire structural rationale for the downstream PPI hypothesis rests on the reliability of these simulations. If the binding free energy difference is not robust, the premise of a functionally significant active-site disruption is weakened.
  - **Resolution test** Provide evidence of simulation convergence (e.g., RMSD and RMSF plots over time), run multiple independent simulations (at least 3 replicates) with error bars for ΔGbind, and validate the docking results with an alternative protocol (e.g., flexible docking or ensemble docking). Show that the difference in ΔGbind is statistically significant.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Support for the central PPI claim
  - **Claim pointer** The claim that the Ser605del variant "exhibits a favorable binding interface with TRF1" and that the interaction is "stronger" in the mutant, as supported by HADDOCK and GBPM analyses.
  - **Evidence pointer** Methods: Protein-protein interaction; Results: Table 1, Table 2, Figure 6
  - **Concern** The HADDOCK docking was performed using the TRFH domain of TRF1 and the full p125 protein. The authors do not specify whether the FSLYP motif was used as an active residue constraint, which would be a circular argument. The HADDOCK score difference between WT and mutant (-107.9 vs -94.4) is modest and the standard deviations overlap, yet the authors claim a "stronger theoretical binding affinity." The GBPM analysis identifies different residue sets for WT and mutant, but the biological significance of these differences is not explained. Most critically, the in vitro immunoprecipitation (Figure 5) shows a qualitative interaction but does not provide quantitative binding affinity measurements. The claim of a "stronger" interaction in MDPL cells is based on densitometry from a single representative experiment (n=3 stated but no error bars or statistics shown in the figure legend).
  - **Why it matters** The central novel finding of this manuscript is the gain of a TRF1 interaction. Without quantitative binding data (e.g., surface plasmon resonance, isothermal titration calorimetry, or at least quantitative co-immunoprecipitation with proper replicates and statistics), the claim of a stronger interaction is not supported.
  - **Resolution test** Provide quantitative binding affinity measurements (e.g., KD values) for WT and mutant p125 with TRF1. For the immunoprecipitation, show full blots, include all replicates with error bars, and perform statistical testing. Clarify whether the FSLYP motif was used as a constraint in HADDOCK and, if so, justify this approach.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Statistical power and interpretation of patient data
  - **Claim pointer** The claim that POLD1 and TRF1 expression levels are dysregulated in an age-dependent manner in MDPL patients, and that PARP1 is downregulated.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results: Figures 3, 4, 7
  - **Concern** The study uses fibroblasts from only three MDPL patients (ages 8, 14, 21) compared to two age-matched controls. This is an extremely small sample size. The statistical analysis appears to compare each patient individually to a single control, which is not a valid approach for population-level inference. The authors report significant differences in some comparisons but not others, and the pattern is inconsistent (e.g., POLD1 mRNA increased in the youngest patient at p13 but protein decreased in older patients). The claim of an "age-dependent manner" is based on three data points and cannot be generalized. Furthermore, the passage number (p13 vs p20) is used as a proxy for aging, but this is a cell culture artifact and not equivalent to organismal aging.
  - **Why it matters** The manuscript makes claims about age-dependent effects and disease mechanisms that are not supported by the statistical power of the study. Overinterpretation of underpowered data can mislead the field.
  - **Resolution test** Increase the number of patients and controls, or clearly frame the results as preliminary observations from a small cohort. Use appropriate statistical tests that account for the small sample size (e.g., non-parametric tests) and report effect sizes. Avoid claims of age-dependence without longitudinal data or a larger cross-sectional cohort.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M4
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Functional significance of the POLD1-TRF1 interaction
  - **Claim pointer** The claim that the stronger POLD1-TRF1 interaction contributes to telomere dysfunction and premature aging in MDPL.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results: Figure 5, Figure 8; Discussion
  - **Concern** The manuscript demonstrates an interaction between POLD1 and TRF1 and shows expression changes, but does not provide any functional data linking this interaction to telomere length, telomere stability, or cellular senescence. The X-ray irradiation experiments (Figure 8) show expression changes but do not directly test the functional consequence of the POLD1-TRF1 interaction on DNA repair or telomere maintenance. The discussion speculates about a "dual telomere surveillance mechanism" but this is not tested.
  - **Why it matters** The title and abstract imply a functional impact on telomere biology, but the data are correlative. Without functional assays (e.g., telomere length measurement, senescence assays, or rescue experiments), the mechanistic claim is not established.
  - **Resolution test** Perform functional assays such as telomere length analysis (e.g., qFISH or Southern blot), senescence-associated beta-galactosidase staining, or measurement of telomere dysfunction-induced foci in MDPL versus control cells. Knockdown or overexpression of TRF1 could test the dependency of the observed phenotype on the POLD1-TRF1 interaction.

- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**
  - **Originality** The hypothesis of a gain-of-interaction between a mutant DNA polymerase and a telomere-binding protein is novel and not previously described. This represents a creative conceptual advance.
  - **Scientific importance** The work has potential importance for understanding MDPL pathogenesis and could have broader implications for aging-related telomere dysfunction. However, the importance is currently limited by the lack of functional validation.
  - **Interdisciplinary readership** The topic bridges structural biology, DNA replication, telomere biology, and clinical genetics, which could attract a broad readership. The computational-to-experimental pipeline is a strength.
  - **Technical soundness** The computational methods have significant limitations as detailed in R1-M1. The in vitro experiments are underpowered and lack quantitative rigor. The technical soundness is currently insufficient to support the central claims.
  - **Readability for nonspecialists** The manuscript is generally well-written and accessible. The background and introduction are clear. However, some computational details may be challenging for non-specialists, and the figures could be better annotated.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The manuscript presents a novel and interesting hypothesis, but the computational and experimental evidence is insufficient to support the central claims. Major revisions and additional experiments are required.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that Ser605del "impairs dTTP binding" with a specific ΔGbind difference of ~15 kcal/mol is not statistically validated and relies on a single short MD simulation.
- The claim of a "stronger" POLD1-TRF1 interaction in the mutant is not quantitatively supported by the immunoprecipitation data, which lack replicates and statistics.
- The claim of an "age-dependent" effect on POLD1, TRF1, and PARP1 expression is based on only three patients and two controls and is not statistically robust.
- The proposed "dual telomere surveillance mechanism" involving POLD1 and PARP1 is purely speculative and not tested experimentally.
- The statement that the Ser605del variant "leads to the acquisition of a telomere-specific interaction motif" is based solely on sequence analysis and in silico docking, without direct experimental validation of the motif's functionality.
- The functional consequence of the POLD1-TRF1 interaction on telomere length or stability is not demonstrated, making the title's implication of "functional impact" unsupported.
