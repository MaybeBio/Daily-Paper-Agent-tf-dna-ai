## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a potentially interesting characterization of a cyanobacterial FeSBCP, a relatively underexplored class within the cryptochrome/photolyase family. The combination of in silico and in vitro approaches is appropriate for the stated goals. However, the abstract-level evidence is insufficient to evaluate the robustness of the binding and repair claims, and the speculative FeS-FAD-DNA triangle is not supported by direct experimental data. The work may be of interest to specialists in DNA repair photobiology, but the current evidence base does not establish the central functional claims.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in photobiology, DNA repair mechanisms, and cryptochrome/photolyase structure-function studies would be interested. The identification of a cyanobacterial FeSBCP with altered electron-transfer residues and apparent (6-4) photolesion repair activity could inform evolutionary and mechanistic understanding of this protein family. Those studying FeS cluster roles in DNA-processing enzymes may also find the speculative triangle hypothesis conceptually useful.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses a relatively undercharacterized protein class. The use of multiple complementary approaches (sequence analysis, modeling, docking, EMSA, spectroscopy) is methodologically sound in principle. The focus on a cyanobacterial representative broadens phylogenetic coverage of FeSBCPs.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Functional claim  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract states that "efficient binding and repair of CyPhrB to damaged single/double-stranded substrates containing 6-4 photolesion" is supported by EMSA and spectroscopic measurements.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
- **Concern** The claim of "repair" is not supported by any direct assay described in the abstract. Spectroscopic measurements and EMSA can demonstrate binding, but they do not demonstrate catalytic repair of a (6-4) photolesion. No repair assay (e.g., HPLC-based lesion quantification, gel-based product analysis, or enzymatic activity measurement) is mentioned.  
- **Why it matters** The central functional claim of the paper is that CyPhrB repairs (6-4) photolesions. Without direct evidence of repair, the conclusion overreaches the data described. This is the core claim that would justify the paper's significance.  
- **Resolution test** Provide a direct repair assay with appropriate positive and negative controls, showing lesion conversion or product formation as a function of protein concentration and light exposure, with quantitative results and statistical analysis.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence quality  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract claims "efficient binding" of CyPhrB to damaged substrates.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract does not provide quantitative binding data (e.g., dissociation constants, binding affinities) or specificity controls. EMSA results are mentioned but no details on protein concentration ranges, competitor DNA, or binding stoichiometry are given. "Efficient" is a qualitative term without numerical support.  
- **Why it matters** Binding affinity and specificity are essential to distinguish a bona fide DNA repair protein from a non-specific DNA-binding protein. Without quantitative or controlled data, the binding claim is not established.  
- **Resolution test** Provide EMSA data with a titration series, calculate apparent Kd values, include undamaged DNA and non-specific competitor controls, and show that binding is specific to the (6-4) lesion.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Mechanistic claim  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract speculates on "the role of FeS clusters in FeSBCPs and existence of FeS-FAD-DNA triangle towards efficient electron transfer."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
- **Concern** The FeS cluster is invoked as a mechanistic component, but no experimental evidence is presented that CyPhrB actually contains a [4Fe4S] cluster. No UV-visible absorption features, EPR data, iron-sulfur quantification, or cluster reconstitution experiments are described. The "triangle" hypothesis is purely speculative.  
- **Why it matters** The FeS cluster is a defining feature of FeSBCPs and is central to the proposed electron-transfer model. If the cluster is not experimentally verified in CyPhrB, the mechanistic speculation is unsupported and potentially misleading.  
- **Resolution test** Provide spectroscopic evidence (e.g., UV-visible absorption at 410 nm, EPR signature) or biochemical quantification confirming the presence of a [4Fe4S] cluster, and ideally show that cluster integrity is required for DNA binding or repair activity.
