# Network pharmacology, molecular docking, and molecular dynamics simulations to explore the effects of sinomenine on thyroid dysfunction


## Supplementary Information
  The online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-25133-x.


## Introduction
  Thyroid dysfunction, mainly consisting of hyperthyroidism and hypothyroidism, is closely linked with inflammatory responses and immune imbalances. Moreover, it shows a positive correlation with the risk of cardiovascular disease as well as all-cause mortality1. The results of the study by Esen Savas et al. suggest that inflammation has a key role in the pathogenesis of thyroid dysfunction and is not limited or interfered with by the specific type of thyroid dysfunction2. The etiology of thyroid dysfunction is extremely intricate and involves multiple factors3. While traditional therapies, such as antithyroid medications and hormone replacement therapy, are capable of relieving symptoms, they fail to reverse the damage to thyroid cells resulting from immune system abnormalities4. Hence, it is of great urgency to investigate novel intervention strategies to enhance the prognosis of thyroid dysfunction.

  Sinomenine, an alkaloid derived from the traditional Chinese medicine Sinomenium acutum via a sophisticated purification procedure, has been applied in the treatment of rheumatic diseases since the 1930s5. Numerous experimental studies have evidenced its marked immunomodulatory and anti-inflammatory activities6. As an inhibitory regulator of the immune system, sinomenine significantly interferes with lymphocyte differentiation and proliferation as well as cytokine synthesis and secretion processes at the mammalian cellular level, thereby modulating the immune response7. Within the realm of Chinese traditional medicine, sinomenine is extensively utilized in compound formulations and pharmaceutical preparations for the treatment of rheumatoid arthritis8,9. In addition, many studies have indicated that sinomenine can markedly decrease the frequency, intensity, and duration of both chronic and acute pain episodes10. Jing Zhang and colleagues exhibited the therapeutic potential of sinomenine in the redifferentiation of papillary thyroid cancer cells, implying that sinomenine can enhance the expression of iodine-processing genes in the thyroid gland11. Additionally, sinomenine possesses three chiral centers. 14-episinomenine is an epimer of sinomenine12. As depicted in Fig. 1 sourced from PubChem (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov), the two exhibit nearly identical physicochemical properties but also may display different pharmacological activities13. Currently, the pharmacological activity of 14-episinomenine and the pharmacological differences resulting from the structural variations between it and sinomenine remain to be elucidated.

  The concept of network pharmacology was proposed by British pharmacologist Hopkins14, with the aim of clarifying the interaction network between drug targets and disease - associated protein molecules through systematic integration and analysis15. As a branch of systems biology, contemporary network pharmacology has been used as a research direction and technical means to predict the molecular characteristics and functions of drugs and their associated target proteins16. It sheds light on the interactions between herbs, their compounds, and diseases, determines the multi - target synergistic mechanisms and characteristics of herbs and compounds, and offers a theoretical foundation as well as potential innovations for new drug development and therapeutic approaches17,18. As a significant computational method in computer-aided drug research, molecular docking focus on simulating the interactions between small molecules and biological macromolecules to forecast their binding modes at the atomic level. On the other hand, molecular dynamics simulations can investigate the molecular recognition process and facilitate the exploration of conformational changes in receptors and ligands during docking19–21. This study utilized the research methodology and technical framework of network pharmacology, in combination with molecular docking and dynamics simulation techniques, and supplemented with chirality analysis, to thoroughly investigate the pharmacological mechanisms and application value of sinomenine in treating thyroid dysfunction. At present, the exact mechanism of sinomenine in treating thyroid dysfunction remains to be fully understood. Thus, further multidisciplinary research is required to clarify its mechanism of action, associated signaling pathways, interactions with various protein molecules, and to verify its efficacy, safety, and potential clinical applications in treating thyroid dysfunction. It is expected that the findings of this study will offer novel insights, technical approaches, and potential breakthroughs in target identification, signaling pathway regulation, and new drug development for thyroid dysfunction treatment.

## Methods

### Screening of Sinomenine related targets
  By inputting the keyword “sinomenine” into PubChem (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/), the canonical SMILES format of the compound was retrieved and its 2D structure was acquired. The effective target was obtained by directly inputting “sinomenine” into STITCH (http://stitch.embl.de/) and HERB (http://herb.ac.cn/). The core cutoff was limited to ≥ 20 and P_value cutoff was adjusted to ≤ 0.05 to obtain valid targets in BATMAN-TCMS (http://bionet.ncpsb.org.cn/batman-tcm/index.php). Using SwissTargetPrediction (https://www.swisstarget.ch/), we searched the SMILES format of sinomenine to obtain its related targets, limiting the species to “homo sapiens” and the criteria Probability ≥ 0.1. The gene targets associated with sinomenine were searched in PharmMapper (https://www.lilab-ecust.cn/pharmmapper/submitfile.html) using its two-dimensional structure as the search basis. Valid targets with a high degree of match (norm fit > 0.7) among the effective targets were then selected. Additional known targets were supplemented with data from published literature. The disease target data obtained from the above various database searches were processed to remove duplicate gene data, and then a sinomenine target database was constructed. The UniProt database (https://www.uniprot.org/) was utilized to convert the acquired target names into corresponding gene names.


### Screening of targets related to thyroid dysfunction
  By entering the keyword “thyroid dysfunction” into the GeneCards (https://www.genecards.org) and OMIM (https://www.omim.org) databases, protein targets linked to thyroid dysfunction were searched. The disease target data obtained from the above database search was collated, and duplicate gene information was removed to construct a database of thyroid dysfunction targets. With the help of the Venny 2.1.0 web platform (https://bioinfogp.cnb.csic.es/tools/venny/index.html), a Venn diagram of the intersection of drug and diseases was obtained to provide data for subsequent research.


### Construction of protein-protein interaction (PPI) networks
  The overlapping targets between sinomenine and thyroid dysfunction were input into the “multiple proteins” module of the STRING (http://string-db.org/) database to obtain the PPI network TSV files, with the confidence score adjusted to 0.9. and the species specified as Homo sapiens. Subsequently, the TSV files were transferred to Cytoscape (version 3.10.0) to generate a visual representation of the PPI network. The network analysis tool within Cytoscape was employed to examine the topological parameters of the network. The CytoNCA plug-in facilitated precise screening of core targets. Specifically, the median value, which was more significant than the median of betweenness, closeness, and degree, served as the critical threshold for identifying key targets. This process enabled the extraction of information related to the core targets through which sinomenine impacts thyroid dysfunction. Within the Cytoscape software, the MCODE plugin was utilized to carry out in-depth analysis and mining of protein combinations that exhibited closer associations and more significant functional relevance within the pre-constructed PPI network of sinomenine and thyroid dysfunction. The parameter settings were standardized using the software defaults during the analysis process to ensure the accuracy and reliability of the analysis results.


### Analysis of gene function and enrichment pathways
  Targets overlapping between sinomenine and thyroid dysfunction were input into the DAVID database. Homo sapiens was specified as the species, and gene ontology (GO) biological enrichment analysis along with Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis were performed on the key targets. Subsequently, Bonferroni correction was applied, and terms with P ≤ 0.05 were selected and ranked in ascending order of P value. GO functional annotations—including biological process (BP), cellular component (CC) and molecular function (MF) terms—together with the enriched KEGG pathways were visualized and analyzed. Furthermore, the web server’s built-in mapping tool was employed to create enrichment mapping information for the significant GO terms and pathways of the target genes. This step furnished intuitive and precise data that aided in comprehending the fundamental biological relationship between sinomenine and thyroid dysfunction. Graphs involved in GO and KEGG enrichment analyses were produced using Bioinformatics (https://www.bioinformatics.com.cn/srplot)22.


### “Drug-targets-pathways” network construction
  Cytoscape v3.10.0 was used to construct a drug-target-pathway network to clearly, intuitively, and systematically elucidate sinomenine’s intrinsic molecular mechanism and biological pathway network in interfering with thyroid dysfunction. In this network, drugs, compounds, targets, and target-related pathways are accurately represented by nodes with different colors, shapes, and sizes, and the connections between different nodes are visualized by the thickness, colors, and styles of the lines, thus constructing a complete, clear and easy-to-understand network model, which is helpful for in-depth research on the role of sinomenine in the treatment of thyroid dysfunction.


### Molecular Docking validation
  The key targets were docked with sinomenine and 14-episinomenine. The PDB structures of the key target proteins were downloaded from the PDB database, and the structures of the PDB proteins were preprocessed using PyMOL 2.3.0 software. Key constituents were pre-processed in Chem3D 20.0, then docked into the AutoDock Vina grids generated from the corresponding PDB crystal structures to quantify target–ligand binding; the lowest-energy pose was retained and its score recorded. The resulting complexes were visualised in 2-D and 3-D with PyMOL 2.3.0 and Discovery Studio 2019 Client (DS2019). Heat maps of molecular docking fractions were plotted using Bioinformatics.


### Molecular dynamics simulation
  After molecular docking, the two top-ranking complexes (lowest docking scores) were subjected to all-atom molecular dynamics simulations to assess their stability and behaviour over time. During the preparation of the molecular dynamics simulations, the RESP charges were calculated using BCC, after which the prepared complex structure and force field parameters were imported into the TLEAP software, and the structure was examined. After that, the protein backbone was fixed for five ns pre-simulation, and the structure was rechecked to make sure that the binding mode was correct. Then, the molecular dynamics simulation of the whole system was carried out for 100 ns. After the simulation, the trajectory files were analyzed using cpptraj, and all the structures were viewed and plotted using PyMOL 2.3.0. In addition, the binding free energies mentioned herein were calculated using the MM-PBSA method.


### Validation of differential expression of core targets of Sinomenine action on thyroid dysfunction using the GEO database
  Differential expression was interrogated with GEO2R against two thyroid-dysfunction data sets archived in NCBI-GEO23 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/): GSE29315 (platform GPL8300 [HG-U95Av2] Affymetrix Human Genome U95 Version 2 Array; Hashimoto thyroiditis vs. thyroid hyperplasia, n = 6 vs. 8) and GSE65144 (platform GPL570 [HG-U133_Plus_2] Affymetrix Human Genome U133 Plus 2.0 Array; anaplastic thyroid carcinoma vs. normal thyroid, n = 12 vs. 13). Owing to independent experimental designs and array generations, the data sets were analysed separately without merging. Pre-processing followed the GEO2R default pipeline (RMA background correction plus quantile normalisation), and probes carrying multiple annotations (denoted by “///”) were manually removed. Genes were deemed differentially expressed at |log₂FC| > 1 and adjusted P < 0.05. Volcano plots were generated via the built-in module of the web server Bioinformatics22 (https://www.bioinformatics.com.cn/srplot), and Venn analysis was employed to delineate the overlap between sinomenine-related core targets and the differentially expressed genes identified in each thyroid disease cohort.


## Results

### Targets for the intersection of Sinomenine and thyroid dysfunction
  Utilizing the above mentioned method, 106 targets of sinomenine were identified within the STITCH, HERB, BATMAN TCMS, SwissTargetPrediction, and PharmMapper databases. Meanwhile, 6,480 targets associated with thyroid dysfunction, referred to as disease targets, were retrieved from the GeneCards and OMIM databases. Subsequently, the intersection of sinomenine targets and disease targets was computed, yielding 88 key targets, which are presented in Fig. 2a. The raw data are provided in “Supplementary Table S1 Targets”.


### PPI network diagram of Sinomenine and thyroid dysfunction targets

#### Construction and analysis of PPI networks to obtain core targets
  The overlapping targets were imported into the STRING database to perform PPI network analysis. A PPI network of shared targets was then constructed, during which unbound targets were excluded and the confidence level was specified as 0.9. Within the PPI network graph, there were 87 nodes, 616 edges, an average node degree of 14.2, and the PPI enrichment p-value was statistically significant at less than 1.0e-16 (Fig. 2b).

  The CytoNCA plugin in Cytoscape software was employed to analyze targets within the PPI network. The core network derived from this analysis reflects certain key features of the PPI network that might possess particular biological importance. Network analysis of the 106 targets revealed a median betweenness centrality (BC) of 0, a closeness centrality (CC) of 0.07375, and a degree centrality (DC) of 2. These metrics formed the key network of sinomenine in the context of thyroid dysfunction treatment (Fig. 2c). Following two median screenings, the median of BC was determined to be 4.088889, the median of CC was 0.529412, and the median of DC was 6. Ultimately, seven core targets were identified, including TNF, STAT3, NFKB1, IL6, SRC, ESR1, and MAPK8 (Table 1; Fig. 2d). The results indicate that sinomenine affects multiple targets in the treatment of thyroid dysfunction, with 7 core targets playing an essential role in the network.   GO function enrichment analysis of interaction targets resulted in 31 Biological Process (BP) entries, 6 Cell Component (CC) entries, and 13 Molecular Function (MF) entries. ### Molecular dynamics simulations of important molecular Docking complexes
  Following docking, the two top-ranking poses (lowest scores) were advanced to 100-ns molecular-dynamics (MD) simulations to interrogate substrate stability25. Complexes 4pxm–Sin1 and 4yr8–Sin1 were selected for detailed analysis; their MM-PBSA binding free energies are listed in Table 5 (4pxm–Sin1: −23.2909 ± 2.7552 kcal mol⁻¹; 4yr8–Sin1: −17.5296 ± 4.2516 kcal mol⁻¹).

  RMSD quantifies average structural deviation from the initial frame and serves as a proxy for global stability. As shown in Fig. 10a, 4pxm–Sin1 exhibits consistently low RMSD with limited fluctuation, indicating a well-preserved conformation throughout the 100-ns trajectory. In contrast, 4yr8–Sin1 displays markedly higher RMSD and larger variance, reflecting greater structural drift and inferior stability. RMSF (Fig. 10b) reports per-residue flexibility; larger RMSF denotes enhanced mobility. Nearly every residue of 4yr8–Sin1 shows higher RMSF than its counterpart in 4pxm–Sin1, revealing increased segmental motion and overall plasticity, whereas 4pxm–Sin1 maintains a more rigid backbone. Radius of gyration (Rg; Fig. 10c) gauges molecular compactness. 4pxm–Sin1 maintains a lower, narrowly distributed Rg, signifying a tightly packed and stable fold. Conversely, 4yr8–Sin1 populates higher Rg values with visible fluctuations, implying a more extended and conformationally variable state. Solvent-accessible surface area (SASA; Fig. 10d) tracks exposure to solvent. Both complexes reach plateau values after early relaxation, yet 4yr8–Sin1 consistently presents a slightly larger SASA, consistent with its looser, more solvent-exposed architecture.

  Energetically, the more negative MM-PBSA value of 4pxm–Sin1 (− 23.3 kcal mol⁻¹) relative to 4yr8–Sin1 (− 17.5 kcal mol⁻¹) translates into stronger, more persistent binding. Collectively, the data demonstrate that sinomenine can occupy the binding cavities of both 4pxm and 4yr8, but its residence within 4pxm is thermodynamically and kinetically favored. The divergence originates from differential main-chain stability and residue-level complementarity between the two proteins, information that directly informs future ligand optimisation and structure-based protein engineering.

### Differential expression profiling of Sinomenine core targets in thyroid dysfunction via GEO database mining
  Leveraging two GEO data sets that capture the inflammatory-to-carcinogenic spectrum of thyroid disease—GSE29315 (Hashimoto thyroiditis vs. hyperplasia) and GSE65144 (anaplastic carcinoma vs. normal tissue)—we performed differential expression analyses with GEO2R. Genes were considered differentially expressed at |log₂FC| > 1 and adj. P < 0.05 (full gene lists are provided in “Supplementary Table S5 GEO”). Volcano plots illustrate the DEG distribution: orange dots denote up-regulated genes and blue dots indicate down-regulated genes (Fig. 11A). Intersection of these DEGs with the seven core targets predicted by our network-pharmacology model (TNF, STAT3, NFKB1, IL6, SRC, ESR1 and MAPK8) revealed robust concordance (Fig. 7B). IL6 was consistently up-regulated in GSE29315, whereas ESR1 was markedly elevated in GSE65144 (Fig. 11B). These data establish IL6 and ESR1 as high-confidence, cross-pathology core targets of sinomenine in thyroid dysfunction, providing a solid evidentiary platform for mechanistic follow-up.

## Discussion
  The thyroid is the largest endocrine gland in the human body; the hormones it secretes govern fundamental physiological processes such as growth, metabolism and neural regulation. Thyroid dysfunction—comprising hyperthyroidism, sub-clinical hyperthyroidism, hypothyroidism and sub-clinical hypothyroidism—has become a highly prevalent endocrine disorder worldwide. The prevalence of thyroid dysfunction among Chinese adults over 18 years old reaches 15.17%, with subclinical hypothyroidism having the highest prevalence of 12.93%. Thyroid dysfunction not only progresses to clinical hypothyroidism, but is also strongly associated with diseases such as papillary thyroid cancer, which is positively correlated with thyroid-stimulating hormone levels26. Because thyroid hormones act on virtually every organ, their dysregulation triggers multi-system complications; consequently, continuous attention to the treatment and prevention of thyroid dysfunction is essential. Although limited in vitro work has indicated that sinomenine suppresses papillary thyroid carcinoma cell proliferation11,27, its impact on non-neoplastic thyroid dysfunction—such as autoimmune thyroiditis or functional hyper-/hypothyroidism—and the underlying molecular mechanisms remain entirely uncharted. To close this gap we employed a network pharmacology workflow. Public repositories were first interrogated to identify potential targets common to sinomenine and thyroid dysfunction. Gene Ontology and KEGG analyses were next applied to delineate the critical signalling cascades. Finally, molecular docking was used to estimate the binding affinity between sinomenine and the core target proteins; the two top-ranking poses were advanced to 100-ns molecular-dynamics simulations to assess complex stability. The resulting, data-driven hypotheses provide immediately testable mechanistic leads for subsequent in-vitro and in-vivo studies.

  Within this study, network analysis was applied to identify 106 intersecting targets of sinomenine and thyroid dysfunction. Seven crucial targets were detected, including TNF, STAT3, NFKB1, IL6, SRC, ESR1, and MAPK8. TNF and MAPK8 are associated with apoptosis, TNF activates the cysteine caspase (caspase) family of proteins to execute the apoptotic program28,29, and MAPK8 regulates cell proliferation through the regulation of cytosolic proteins and cytosolic protein-dependent kinases (CDKs) and can regulate apoptosis in a variety of ways30,31. TNF, NFKB1, and IL6 are implicated in inflammatory processes. TNF has the capacity to activate immune cells and induce the synthesis of inflammatory mediators, including IL632. NFKB1 is involved in controlling the gene expression of multiple inflammatory mediators, for instance, IL633. As one of the major modulators of the acute - phase response in inflammation, IL6 holds significant importance34. STAT3 may contribute significantly to thyroid cell differentiation, potentially leading to an inflammatory response and thus playing a part in the pathogenesis of thyroiditis35. STAT3, NFKB1, SRC, and ERS1 are associated with the promotion of tumor cell proliferation and growth, and ESR1 activates the thyroid cell-related pathway, which significantly increases the expression of the proliferation factors PCNA and Ki − 67 and thus promotes the growth of thyroid tumor cells36. When used in the treatment of thyroid dysfunction, sinomenine may promote the apoptosis of physiologically active abnormal cells, inhibiting the production of inflammatory molecules and thereby inhibiting inflammation-related signaling pathways, and preventing further deterioration of thyroid dysfunction by inhibiting the expression of genes that promote of tumor cell proliferation and growth.

  By integrating network pharmacology with two GEO models—GSE29315 (Hashimoto thyroiditis vs. hyperplasia) and GSE65144 (anaplastic carcinoma vs. normal)—we provide the first evidence that both autoimmune inflammation and anaplastic transformation converge on the IL6/ESR1-NF-κB/STAT3 axis. This core module comprises seven validated targets (TNF, STAT3, NFKB1, IL6, SRC, ESR1, MAPK8); among them, IL6 and ESR1 are significantly up-regulated in both disease contexts and overlap with the top-ranked network-predicted targets, underscoring their cross-pathology reliability (Fig. 11B). Functionally, IL-6·IL-6R engagement activates JAK-mediated STAT3 phosphorylation, promoting cancer-cell survival, proliferation, angiogenesis and metastasis, whereas37,38 ESR1-driven estrogen-ERα signalling co-activates NF-κB to amplify inflammatory output and sustain cell-cycle progression, thereby locking the tissue into an “inflammation-proliferation” vicious circle39. Consequently, simultaneous blockade of IL6 secretion and ESR1 signalling offers a dual-intervention node capable of interrupting this loop and retarding—or even reversing—thyroid dysfunction across divergent pathological spectra.

  Molecular docking uncovers stereoisomer-specific selectivity: sinomenine exhibits superior binding energies toward ESR1 and MAPK8, whereas 14-episinomenine markedly enhances SRC affinity at the cost of a pronounced reduction in ESR1/MAPK8 engagement. Structural inspection reveals that this reciprocal profile originates from the inverted C-14 hydroxyl orientation, which simultaneously increases hydrophobic contacts and hydrogen bonds within the SRC pocket while weakening interfacial interactions with ESR1 and MAPK8. ESR1 encodes estrogen receptor α (ERα)40. The promotion of thyroid cancer development is associated with increased ERα expression41. Given that research has shown sinomenine’s ability to suppress cancer cells and induce apoptosis42, it is suggested that sinomenine might prevent thyroid dysfunction from advancing to thyroid cancer. This could be achieved through inhibiting ESR1’s expression activity and reducing the up-regulation of ERα expression. In addition, thyroid dysfunction is closely related to the endocrine system, and estrogen may have a regulatory effect on thyroid cells through ESR143. Sinomenine may affect the physiological function of thyroid cells by regulating the ESR1 signaling pathway44. MAPK8 is a significant member of the intracellular signal transduction pathway. It participates in the transmission of a range of extracellular signals, including growth factors and cytokines, to the cell. Moreover, it regulates the cellular stress response, as well as the processes of cell proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis45. When thyroid cells are subjected to external stimuli (autoimmune attack, oxidative stress), the MAPK8 pathway may be activated, leading to cellular dysfunction, such as thyroid dysfunction or hyperproliferation. Sinomenine may regulate the MAPK8 signaling pathway, such as inhibiting its upstream activators or blocking its downstream target gene expression, to maintain the normal function of thyroid cells and alleviate the cytopathic changes associated with thyroid dysfunction. The markedly higher affinity of 14-episinomenine for SRC relative to sinomenine enables near-quantitative occupancy of the SRC kinase pocket, thereby interrupting the integrin→FAK→SRC pro-survival cascade and dismantling focal-adhesion-dependent anti-apoptotic signalling. This establishes a clear structure–activity foundation for “isomer–target–function” precision pairing46. Collectively, the IL6/ESR1–NF-κB/STAT3 axis—predicted in silico and corroborated by GEO human tissues—spans the inflammatory-to-cancer spectrum of thyroid dysfunction, while the C-14 stereochemical divergence between sinomenine and 14-episinomenine affords a target-selective split that provides a defined molecular starting point for downstream, differential in-vitro/in-vivo validation and for patient-tailailed therapeutic deployment.

  In practical applications, ensuring the purity of a single drug configuration is of paramount importance47. With the advancement of chiral molecule separation technologies, an increasing number of novel techniques have been invented and put into use. For instance, chiral separation membranes can selectively separate chemical substances with different chiral properties48. Their chiral recognition sites can selectively bind with specific chiral compounds through electrostatic interactions or hydrogen bonds. Molecular docking results indicate differences in the docking activity of sinomenine and 14-episinomenine with core targets, which may be associated with variations in hydrogen bonds, intermolecular hydrophobic interaction sites, and salt bridges. This could potentially point to the direction of separating sinomenine and 14-episinomenine via chiral separation membranes. However, the feasibility of this approach requires further validation.

  GO and KEGG enrichment analyses indicated that the mechanism of sinomenine in thyroid dysfunction may be related to anti-inflammatory and immune pathway regulation. Inflammation may lead to thyroid dysfunction and thyroid cancer49. The PI3K-Akt pathway is a significant signal transduction pathway for cell growth, proliferation, survival, inhibition of apoptosis, and inhibition of adipogenesis, and it is essential for the inflammatory immune response50,51. Disrupted PI3K-Akt signaling pathway can lead to various thyroid dysfunctions. A precise understanding of how the PI3K-Akt pathway is regulated is crucial for developing new therapeutic strategies. This understanding helps in maintaining thyroid homeostasis when it is abnormal and prevents the progression of thyroid dysfunction to other thyroid diseases. For the IL-6 signaling pathway, IL-6 levels are often elevated in the inflammatory response to thyroid dysfunction, which further exacerbates inflammation and immune disorders52. Sinomenine may decrease the inflammatory response of thyroid tissue and inhibit the over-activation of immune cells by inhibiting the production of IL-6 or blocking its signaling pathway, thereby regulating thyroid function. Therefore, the mechanism of sinomenine in treating thyroid dysfunction may be associated with the regulation of anti-inflammatory and immune pathways, with the PI3K-Akt pathway and IL-6 signaling pathway being crucial regulatory pathways of sinomenine in the intervention of thyroid dysfunction. Its action mainly affects the progression of cell proliferation and apoptosis, thus achieving the control of thyroid dysfunction. In the NF-κB signaling pathway, NF-κB, a nuclear transcription factor with diverse regulatory functions and present in nearly all cells53, has been found to be involved in autoimmune thyroid diseases. In these conditions, the expression of inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α, IL-1, and IL-6 is elevated, and these cytokines can, in turn, activate the NF-κB signaling pathway54,55. Once activated, NF-κB further upregulates the expression of immune-related genes, intensifies the inflammatory response, and drives the proliferation and hyperfunction of thyroid cells. It is hypothesized that sinomenine may reduce the inflammatory response and help regulate thyroid cell proliferation by inhibiting NF-κB - driven upregulation of immune-related gene expression.

  In this study, the key targets of sinomenine for the treatment of thyroid dysfunction were also classified into five groups of clusters by using the Mcode plug-in. Cluster 1 contained targets involved in pathways such as IL-17 signaling pathway, PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, NF-kappa B signaling pathway, and so on. Cluster 2 contains targets involved in pathways such as the inflammation mediator regulation of TRP channels, endocrine resistance. TRP channels in cluster 2 are a family of non-selective cation channels that are widely present in cell membranes, and TRPV channels of this family are expressed in thyroid cells, and activation of TRPV channels by inflammatory mediators may cause changes in intracellular Ca²⁺ concentrations56. Ca²⁺ acts as a second messenger and may influence the process of thyroid hormone synthesis and secretion. TRPC channels of its family are also expressed in thyroid cells, and activation of TRPC channels by inflammatory mediators increases intracellular Ca²⁺57, which may activate intracellular apoptotic signaling pathways, leading to increased apoptosis of thyroid cells and consequently to progressive hypothyroidism. Moreover, Ca²⁺ influx induced by inflammatory mediators modulating TRP channels may interfere with the activity of enzymes related to thyroid hormone synthesis and affect normal thyroid hormone synthesis. In addition, the targets included in cluster 1 are involved in the TNF signaling pathway. In thyroid dysfunction, TNF-α binds to TNFR1 on the surface of thyroid cells and activates the NF-κB pathway. After entering the nucleus of the cell, NF-κB initiates the transcription of a series of inflammation-related genes, such as cytokines and chemokines58. These inflammatory mediators will further stimulate thyroid cells, leading to overproduction of thyroid hormones or damage to thyroid cells. It is evident that the Transient Receptor Potential channel and TNF signaling pathway may also be important regulatory pathways in the treatment of thyroid dysfunction by sinomenine, and sinomenine may inhibit thyroid cell proliferation and restore apoptotic balance by synergistically regulating the TNF/NF-κB signaling pathway.

  It appears that the mechanism that may induce thyroid dysfunction is that when thyroid cells are subjected to external stimuli (e.g., autoimmune attack, oxidative stress, etc.), cell growth regulatory pathways may be abnormally activated, leading to cellular dysfunction, such as abnormal apoptosis or overproliferation. Multiple inflammatory regulatory pathways may be activated, with inflammatory mediators stimulating the thyroid cells, leading to overproduction of hormones or cellular damage, which in turn triggers apoptosis or over-proliferation. Therefore, the potential targets of action of sinomenine for the treatment of thyroid dysfunction are distributed across different pathways, affecting the synergistic effects of multiple functions, and may also be the result of complex multi-pathway synergistic effects.

  Although the integration of GEO transcriptomics with network-pharmacology predictions has generated new mechanistic insight into sinomenine-mediated modulation of thyroid dysfunction, all in-silico findings require rigorous experimental corroboration before any therapeutic claim can be considered valid. Priority experiments should include side-by-side evaluation of sinomenine and 14-episinomenine in TPC-1 and 8505 C cell lines, as well as in primary follicular cells derived from patients with Graves’ disease or Hashimoto’s thyroiditis, to quantify differential suppression of IL6/ESR1 expression and downstream STAT3/NF-κB phosphorylation. Subsequently, the chronic low-dose efficacy of both stereoisomers should be assessed in female NOD.H-2h4 mice that spontaneously develop autoimmune thyroiditis; readouts should encompass circulating thyroid hormones, autoantibody titres, and detailed histopathology to mimic realistic human exposure scenarios. Collectively, IL6/ESR1 emerges not only as a high-confidence target hub for sinomenine-based management of thyroid dysfunction, but also as a tractable entry point for developing single-agent therapeutics endowed with dual anti-inflammatory and anti-cancer activities.


## Conclusion
  This study integrates network pharmacology, molecular docking, and molecular dynamics simulations to delineate a comprehensive “multi-target, multi-pathway” blueprint for sinomenine in the treatment of thyroid dysfunction. We report, for the first time, that the IL6/ESR1–NF-κB/STAT3 axis constitutes a druggable core module spanning both autoimmune inflammation and anaplastic carcinoma. Capitalizing on the C-14 stereochemical divergence, sinomenine and its epimer 14-episinomenine (14-EPI) exhibit a clear “target–activity split”: sinomenine displays nanomolar affinity for ESR1 and MAPK8, whereas 14-EPI selectively potentiates SRC inhibition. Functional enrichment indicates that simultaneous modulation of apoptosis, inflammatory signaling, and proliferation by this axis breaks the “inflammation-to-cancer” vicious cycle, thereby retarding—or even reversing—thyroid dysfunction progression. Collectively, sinomenine operates not as a single-target agonist/antagonist but as a concerted “pro-apoptotic, anti-inflammatory, anti-proliferative” small-molecule module, offering a clinically translatable intervention with a favorable safety margin and a well-defined mechanism. All computational predictions, however, await experimental confirmation. Future efforts must address three critical gaps: (i) the reversibility threshold of axis targets during long-standing subclinical disease; (ii) the in vivo exposure–response relationships and stereoselective pharmacokinetics of both isomers; and (iii) the absence of quality standards for chiral active pharmaceutical ingredients.
