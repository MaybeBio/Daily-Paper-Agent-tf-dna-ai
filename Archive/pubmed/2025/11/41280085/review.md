## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text, including abstract, introduction, results, discussion, materials and methods, and data availability statements.
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific validity, methodological soundness, strength of claims, and significance of findings as presented in the provided manuscript. No assessment of editorial fit or publication policy.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors present K-PROB, a computational tool that uses k-mer-based Bayesian variable selection to identify cis-regulatory elements (CREs) from plant pan-genome and pan-transcriptome data. They apply it to maize and soybean, identify highly variable pangenes enriched for environmental response functions, and validate several predicted CREs using promoter-luciferase reporter assays in Nicotiana benthamiana.
- **Visible evidence base** Main text figures (Fig 1, 2, 3, 4), supplementary figures (Fig S1-S7), supplementary tables (Table S1, S2), and supplementary datasets (Dataset S1-S5) are referenced. Methods are described in detail.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Supplementary figures, tables, and datasets were not provided for review. Raw data and code are available via external repositories but were not examined. Specific statistical outputs (e.g., effect sizes, credible intervals) for individual k-mer clusters are not fully reported in the main text.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a novel and potentially valuable computational framework for identifying cis-regulatory elements from pan-genomic data. The core idea of using k-mers to capture regulatory variation without reference-genome alignment is timely and well-motivated. The Bayesian variable selection approach is statistically rigorous and appropriate for the high-dimensional problem. The application to maize and soybean demonstrates generalizability, and the experimental validation of several predicted CREs strengthens the claims. However, several technical aspects require clarification and additional analysis to fully establish the method's validity and the strength of the biological conclusions. The statistical model's assumptions, the interpretation of effect sizes, and the robustness of the functional filtering step need further scrutiny. The experimental validation, while supportive, is limited in scope and would benefit from additional controls and replication details.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Plant molecular biologists and geneticists studying gene regulation, particularly those working on crop species with complex pangenomes. Researchers developing computational methods for regulatory genomics and pangenome analysis. Breeders interested in identifying natural variants for precision breeding and engineering gene expression. The approach could also be of interest to the broader genomics community working on non-model organisms with high structural variation.
- **Major strengths**
  1. The conceptual advance of using k-mers to directly link promoter sequence variation to expression variation across a pangenome is significant and addresses a clear gap in existing methods.
  2. The Bayesian variable selection framework is a principled approach for high-dimensional regression and is well-suited for prioritizing causal variants over correlated markers.
  3. The demonstration that highly variable pangenes are enriched for environmental response functions in two major crop species is a biologically interesting and potentially important finding.
  4. The inclusion of experimental validation for a subset of predicted CREs, particularly those within structural variants, substantially strengthens the claim that the method identifies functional elements.
  5. The manuscript is generally well-written and the methods are described in sufficient detail to be reproducible.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **R1-M1** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes, Axis: Statistical validity)
    - **Claim pointer** The claim that K-PROB's Bayesian variable selection model "prioritizes causative CRE identification" and that identified k-mers "directly represent TF binding sequences" is central to the paper.
    - **Evidence pointer** Methods section "Overview of K-PROB functions", Results section "K-PROB identifies significant k-mer clusters enriched for transcription factor binding motifs".
    - **Concern** The statistical model assumes a linear relationship between k-mer cluster counts and gene expression. This is a strong assumption, as regulatory effects are often non-linear and context-dependent. More critically, the model does not appear to account for population structure or relatedness among the inbred lines. If the 26 maize lines are structured, the model could identify spurious associations driven by population stratification rather than true regulatory effects. The cross-validation by "inbred lines" (Fig 2B) partially addresses this, but it does not fully control for relatedness in the training set. The authors should either incorporate a kinship matrix or principal components as covariates, or explicitly justify why this is not necessary.
    - **Why it matters** If the model is confounded by population structure, the identified k-mer clusters may not be causal, undermining the central claim of the method. The biological interpretations and experimental validation would then be based on potentially spurious associations.
    - **Resolution test** The authors should re-run the analysis including principal components or a kinship matrix as fixed effects in the model. If the significant k-mer clusters remain largely unchanged, this would alleviate the concern. Alternatively, they could perform a permutation test that breaks the genotype-phenotype relationship while preserving the correlation structure within the data.
  - **R1-M2** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes, Axis: Methodological robustness)
    - **Claim pointer** The claim that the functional filtering step identifies "functional regulatory sites" and that retained k-mer-pangene pairs represent "true regulatory sites with greater confidence."
    - **Evidence pointer** Methods section "Overview of K-PROB functions", Results section "K-PROB further filtered k-mer occurrences for functional regulatory sites".
    - **Concern** The functional filtering step performs a per-pangene linear regression between k-mer occurrence and expression. This is a post-hoc analysis on the same data used to identify the k-mer clusters, which introduces a selection bias. The significance threshold (FDR-corrected p < 0.05) is applied without accounting for the fact that the k-mer clusters were already selected based on their association with expression. This could lead to an inflated number of "functional" sites. The authors should use a more rigorous approach, such as a holdout set of pangenes for the filtering step, or a joint model that accounts for the selection process.
    - **Why it matters** The functional filtering is a key step in the pipeline that distinguishes K-PROB from simple association methods. If this step is statistically flawed, the final list of "functional" CREs may contain many false positives, and the experimental validation results would be less compelling.
    - **Resolution test** The authors should validate the functional filtering step using a split-sample approach: identify k-mer clusters in a training set of pangenes, and then test the filtering in a held-out set. They should also report the number of k-mer-pangene pairs that pass the filtering and the distribution of effect sizes to assess the potential for selection bias.
  - **R1-M3** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No, Axis: Experimental validation)
    - **Claim pointer** The claim that "K-PROB successfully pinpointed causal CREs within large SVs" based on the luciferase reporter assays.
    - **Evidence pointer** Results section "Newly identified causal CRE variations are supported by experimental validation", Figure 4.
    - **Concern** The experimental validation is limited to five k-mer clusters and six sites. While the results are supportive, the number of validated sites is small, and the manuscript does not report the full details of the validation, such as the number of biological replicates, the statistical test used, and the variability between replicates. Furthermore, the validation is performed in a heterologous system (N. benthamiana), which may not fully recapitulate the native regulatory context. The authors should provide more detailed experimental data and discuss the limitations of the heterologous system.
    - **Why it matters** The experimental validation is a key strength of the paper, but its limited scope and lack of detail make it difficult to fully assess the robustness of the findings. A more comprehensive validation, including additional sites and a more detailed statistical analysis, would significantly strengthen the claims.
    - **Resolution test** The authors should provide the full dataset for the luciferase assays, including all replicates and the statistical analysis. They should also consider validating a negative control (a k-mer cluster not predicted to be functional) to demonstrate the specificity of their approach.
  - **R1-M4** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No, Axis: Biological interpretation)
    - **Claim pointer** The claim that "highly variable (hv) pangenes are enriched for environmental response functions" and the implication that this variation is adaptive.
    - **Evidence pointer** Results section "Pangenes with highly variable promoter and expression are enriched for environmental response", Figure 1B, 1C.
    - **Concern** The enrichment analysis is based on a threshold-based definition of "highly variable" (top 30%). This is arbitrary, and the results may be sensitive to the choice of threshold. The authors should perform a sensitivity analysis using different thresholds to ensure the enrichment is robust. Additionally, the interpretation that this variation is adaptive is speculative. The observed variation could be due to neutral processes or genetic drift, particularly in a small sample of 26 lines.
    - **Why it matters** The biological interpretation of the results is a major part of the paper's significance. If the enrichment is not robust to threshold choices, or if the variation is not adaptive, the conclusions would be weakened.
    - **Resolution test** The authors should perform a sensitivity analysis with different thresholds (e.g., top 10%, 20%, 40%) and report whether the enrichment results are consistent. They should also temper their language regarding adaptation and discuss alternative explanations for the observed variation.
- **Minor Comments**
  - **R1-m1** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Clarity, Affected Element: Figure 2B, Evidence Pointer: Results section "Bayesian joint modeling of intraspecific cis-regulatory variation using k-mers")
    - **Issue** The cross-validation results in Figure 2B are described as showing "predicted accuracies" but the y-axis label is not specified in the text. It is unclear if this is R², Pearson correlation, or another metric.
    - **Required correction** Please clearly label the y-axis in Figure 2B and define the metric in the figure legend.
  - **R1-m2** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Reproducibility, Affected Element: Methods, Evidence Pointer: Methods section "Overview of K-PROB functions")
    - **Issue** The Gibbs sampler is described as "in-house" but no details are provided on the number of iterations, burn-in, or convergence diagnostics beyond the Geweke test.
    - **Required correction** Please provide the full details of the MCMC settings, including the number of chains, iterations, and the criteria used for the Geweke test.
  - **R1-m3** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Statistical reporting, Affected Element: Effect sizes, Evidence Pointer: Results section "K-PROB identifies significant k-mer clusters enriched for transcription factor binding motifs")
    - **Issue** The effect sizes for the significant k-mer clusters are described as ranging from 0.056 to 0.31 for activators and -0.079 to -0.24 for repressors, but no confidence intervals or measures of uncertainty are provided.
    - **Required correction** Please report 95% credible intervals or standard errors for the effect sizes of the significant k-mer clusters.
  - **R1-m4** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Data presentation, Affected Element: Figure 3, Evidence Pointer: Results section "K-PROB further filtered k-mer occurrences for functional regulatory sites")
    - **Issue** The heatmap in Figure 3A is described as showing "estimated effects" but it is unclear how the k-mer clusters are ordered and whether the heatmap shows all significant clusters or a subset.
    - **Required correction** Please clarify the ordering of the rows and columns in the heatmap and state whether all significant clusters are shown.
  - **R1-m5** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Literature context, Affected Element: Discussion, Evidence Pointer: Discussion section)
    - **Issue** The discussion of the advantages of K-PROB over existing methods is somewhat brief. A more detailed comparison with recent deep learning approaches (e.g., DeepCRE, PhytoExpr) and other pangenome-based methods would be helpful.
    - **Required correction** Please expand the discussion to include a more thorough comparison with existing methods, highlighting the specific advantages and limitations of K-PROB.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established**
  - R1-M1: Lack of control for population structure in the Bayesian model.
  - R1-M2: Potential selection bias in the functional filtering step.
  - R1-M3: Limited and under-reported experimental validation.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**
  - **Originality** High. The use of k-mers to directly link promoter variation to expression in a pangenome context is a novel approach that addresses a clear gap in the field.
  - **Scientific importance** High. The ability to identify causal cis-regulatory variants in highly variable regions is of significant importance for understanding gene regulation and for crop improvement.
  - **Interdisciplinary readership** Moderate. The work is primarily of interest to plant molecular biologists, geneticists, and computational biologists. The methods and findings may not be immediately accessible to a broader audience.
  - **Technical soundness** Moderate. The statistical framework is sound in principle, but the lack of control for population structure and the potential selection bias in the filtering step are significant concerns that need to be addressed.
  - **Readability for nonspecialists** Good. The manuscript is generally well-written and the concepts are explained clearly. The use of figures helps to illustrate the key findings.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The core idea is novel and promising, and the experimental validation is a strong point. However, the statistical concerns regarding population structure and selection bias must be addressed before the claims of causal identification can be fully accepted. The authors should also provide more detail on the experimental validation and perform sensitivity analyses to strengthen the biological conclusions.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that K-PROB "prioritizes causative CRE identification" is not fully supported without addressing the potential confounding effect of population structure.
- The claim that the functional filtering step identifies "true regulatory sites with greater confidence" is not fully supported due to the potential selection bias in the post-hoc analysis.
- The claim that the identified k-mers "directly represent TF binding sequences" is an overstatement, as the k-mers are associated with expression and show homology to known motifs, but direct binding is not demonstrated.
- The biological interpretation that the observed variation in hv pangenes is adaptive is speculative and not directly tested.
- The generalizability of the method to other species is claimed but only demonstrated in two species (maize and soybean).
