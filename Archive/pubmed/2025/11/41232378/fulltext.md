BACKGROUND: Multidrug-resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae (MDR-KP) has become a critical global health threat, challenging the management of infectious diseases. Anti-termination protein RfaH is crucial in promoting virulence across Gram-negative pathogens, including Klebsiella pneumoniae (KP). RfaH is a key anti-terminator protein involved in transcription regulation that directly interacts with RNA polymerase and ribosomes, activating operons associated with capsule, cell wall, and pilus biosynthesis. METHODS: This study aimed to investigate the repurposing potential of rifaximin, a well-established antibiotic, against (KP) by targeting RfaH. We employed an integrated computational approach of molecular docking and molecular dynamics (MD) simulations to obtain atomistic insights into the binding mechanism of rifaximin with RfaH. The computational findings were further validated through experimental assays, including fluorescence-based binding studies, antibacterial activity tests, and evaluation of capsule production in KP. RESULTS: Fluorescence studies showed excellent binding affinity between rifaximin and RfaH (Ka = 7.38 x 10(6) M(-1)). Notably, rifaximin treatment results in a significant reduction in capsule production in KP compared to untreated controls, highlighting its inhibitory effect on RfaH activity. The minimum inhibitory concentration for Rifaximin was 100 muM, and a minimum bactericidal concentration of 200 muM was determined against KP (ATCC 700603 strain). Docking and MD simulation studies provided detailed atomic insights into the Rifaximin binding to RfaH and structural dynamics of the RfaH-Rifaximin complex. CONCLUSIONS: These multifaceted findings collectively investigated the potential of rifaximin as a repurposed antibiotic for the treatment of KP. Finally, a strong interaction of RfaH with rifaximin and subsequent inhibition of the growth of KP provides a novel avenue for antimicrobial development to address the persistent global challenge of antibiotic-resistant infections.
