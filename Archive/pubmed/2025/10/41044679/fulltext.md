# Identification of methylation-related genes and the potential regulatory mechanism of SLAMF6 in CMS4 colorectal cancer


## Backgrounds
  Consensus molecular subtype 4 (CMS4) of colorectal cancer (CRC) is characterized by TGF-β activation, and generally accompanied with metastasis and recurrence. Nevertheless, molecular biomarkers and regulatory mechanisms underlying CMS4 CRC remain elusive. This study investigated methylation sites in CMS4 CRC and explored the mechanistic role of SLAMF6 in CRC.


## Methods
  Random forest and LASSO regression identified significant methylation sites, and Kaplan Meier analysis pinpointed prognosis-related sites. The mRNA and protein expression levels of SLAMF6 were identified by qPCR, western blot, immunohistochemistry and public database. Bisulfite sequencing PCR and methylation specific PCR were employed to validate SLAMF6 promoter methylation. Western blot was applied to study the specific mechanism underlying TGF-β regulating SLAMF6 methylation. Chromatin immunoprecipitation and electrophoretic mobility shift assay were conducted to evaluate the relationship between SMAD3 and DNMT1. The anti-tumor effect of methyltransferase inhibitor (5-Aza) and PD-L1 antibody was assessed in tumor bearing mice.


## Results
  We detected 726 differentially methylated sites between CMS4 and CMS1-3 subtypes (p < 0.05), with CMS4 showing prominently elevated methylation. Machine learning approaches refined to 43 methylation sites and 8 methylation sites were involved with significant prognostic value. The methylation level of SLAMF6 in CMS4 was much higher than other subtypes. Diminished SLAMF6 expression was firstly authenticated in CRC compared with normal colon tissues. It was more emphatic in CRC with metastasis and related with worse prognosis. Mechanically, p-SMAD2/3 and DNMT1 were acknowledged as the downstream effector of TGF-β activation. Next, p-SMAD3 was demonstrated to bound to DNMT1 promoter inducing SLAMF6 hypermethylation. Most interestingly, SLAMF6 was positively correlated with multiple immune cells and the combination of 5-Aza and PD-L1 antibody impeded tumor growth and upregulated CD8+ T cells and CD4+ T cells.


## Conclusions
  Together, our study illustrated a novel epigenetic mechanism mediated by TGF-β/SMAD3/DNMT1, and suggested a potential target for CRC prognosis and immunotherapy.

## Introduction
  Colorectal cancer (CRC) is a major malignancy of gastrointestinal cancer and its incidence rate and mortality rate rank third and second in malignant tumors [1]. CRC is an extremely heterogenous disease, which responds differentially to therapy and has a range of outcomes. Accumulated evidence has promulgated a critical role of molecular typing systems for improving cancer diagnosis, treatment and prognosis [2–4]. The consensus molecular subtypes (CMSs) issued by an international multicenter consortium divided CRC into four molecular subtypes: CMS1 (immunological), CMS2 (canonical), CMS3 (metabolic), and CMS4 (mesenchymal) [5]. These four CMS subtypes exhibit distinct molecular characteristics, for example, CMS1 is linked to hypermutation, microsatellite instability (MSI), and immune response, CMS2 is associated with activation of WNT signaling pathways, CMS3 exhibits metabolic pathway abnormalities, while CMS4 is characterized by TGF-β signal activation, lack of CD8+ T lymphocytes infiltration, high risk of metastasis and recurrence [5, 6]. In recent years, researchers have found signaling lymphocyte activation molecules family 6 (SLAMF6) is an immune cell activating receptor expressed on a wide variety of hematopoietic cells. Activation of SLAMF6 not only impacts T cell functions, but also regulates anti-tumor immune response, indicating its potential effects as a target for tumor immunotherapy [7, 8]. Activated CD3+ lymphocytes crosslinked with SLAMF6 antibody induced T cell proliferation and increased IFN-γ release, indicating that SLAMF6 is a co-stimulatory receptor [9]. However, the exact role of SLAMF6 in T cell signaling is still controversial, and studies on enhancing or dampening T cell activity have been reported [10, 11]. In addition, few studies have addressed the regulatory mechanism of SLAMF6 expression in solid tumors.

  DNA methylation is a stable and inheritable epigenetics modification, which is widely found in most eukaryotes. DNA methylation is an enzyme catalyzed modification process, which suppresses the transcription process through breaking up the interaction between DNA elements and transcription factors [12]. There are three kinds of DNA methyltransferases (DNMTs) found in mammals, which mainly consisted of DNMT1 maintaining DNA continuous methylation state, and DNMT3A, DNMT3B participating in de-novo synthesis of DNA methylation [13]. Abnormal DNA methylation is a crucial event implicated in tumor initiation and progression. Hypermethylation of miRNA-200b/c identified tumors belonging to the mesenchymal subtype and was predictive of disease-free survival (DFS) in CRC patients [14]. Microarray and high-throughput technologies have provided a technical platform for large-scale DNA methylation profiling. The Human Methylation 450 K database was used to measure DNA methylation data from over 12,000 samples across 34 cancer types in The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA). Numerous studies have employed the 450 K methylation array to identify methylation sites as biomarkers for early cancer diagnosis and prognostic evaluation [15, 16].

  Transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β) is a superfamily of peptide cytokines with various biological activities, what is closely related to occurrence and development of tumors [17]. TGF-β mainly inhibits cell proliferation and induces cell apoptosis, thereby inhibiting tumor growth in the early stages of tumor formation. During the progress of malignancy, TGF-β converts to a tumor promoting factor by immune suppression, angiogenesis and fibrosis and other mechanisms [18–20]. SMADs (mothers against decapentaplegic homologs) is a kind of downstream signaling transduction molecules in TGF-β signaling. TGF-β binds to its receptors, inducing phosphorylation and activating receptor-regulated SMADs like SMAD2 and SMAD3, which subsequently combining with their partner SMAD4, translocating into the nucleus and activating downstream target genes [17, 21, 22]. Koh et al. [23] reported TGF-β increased DNMT1 and DNMT3A expression through distinct post-transcriptional mechanism in lung fibroblasts. However, the specific mechanism underlying TGF-β as a tumor promoting factor in CMS4 and the potential regulatory mechanism of SLAMF6 in cancer have not been identified. In this study, we aimed at determining the mechanistic link between TGF-β and SLAMF6 in the context of CMS4 CRC and exploring the suppressor role of SLAMF6 in CRC. To this end, we defined that TGF-β activated SMAD3-DNMT1-SLAMF6 pathway in CRC.


## Materials and methods

### TCGA data collection and processing
  The RNA sequence data, 450 k methylation data and clinical information of TCGA database were retrieved from the UCSC XENA (https://xenabrowser.net/datapages/). Then, we evaluated the differential expression of SLAMF6 gene in CRC by extracting the expression data of SLAMF6 gene (ENSG00000162739.14) from each CRC sample with complete transcriptome data. Prognostic analysis was conducted using cox regression analysis, and the survival package was used for proportional risk hypothesis testing and fitted survival regression. The results were visualized using the survival package and ggplot2 package. All data included in this study are available in the public database.


### Differential methylation sites (DMSs) analysis
  KS tests revealed differential methylation between CMS4 and CMS1-3 (p < 0.05), with heatmaps visualization showing distinct clustering patterns. Random forest and LASSO regression identified the most predictive methylation markers.


### Random forest and LASSO regression
  Random forest is an extremely flexible machine learning algorithm that can be used for modeling or predicting patients'disease risk and susceptibility. We performed feature selection through iterative modeling. The importance of each variable was predicted based on the modeling results, and variables with zero importance were eliminated. The remaining variables were then subjected to another round of random forest modeling, repeating this process until all retained variables demonstrated non-zero importance. The final set of variables served as potential methylation biomarkers. From the 726 differentially methylated sites, we conducted 300 rounds of potential biomarker screening, recording the frequency and importance of each methylation site. After eliminating sites with zero importance, we generated a heatmap to analyze the clustering patterns of significant methylation sites between the two sample groups. LASSO regression constructs a penalty function to produce a more refined model. It compresses certain coefficients while setting others to zero, preserving the advantages of subset shrinkage. As a biased estimation method for handling multicollinear data, it achieved variable selection during parameter estimation, effectively addressing multicollinearity issues in regression analysis. We analyzed the trajectory of each independent variable and determined the optimal lambda value through threefold cross-validation (CV). The final optimal penalty coefficient was set at lambda = 0.01417414, yielding 43 selected features (methylation sites).


### Immunohistochemical (IHC)
  IHC assay was performed on formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded sections of CRC samples. Briefly, after deparaffinized with xylene, rehydrated in ethanol and antigen retrieval in citric acid at pH 6.0, tissue sections were incubated with anti-SLAMF6, anti-Ki67, anti-CD3 and anti-CD8 at 4 °C overnight, labeled by HRP (rabbit) second antibody at room temperature for 30min. Finally, sections were stained with DAB solution. The primary antibodies used in the experiments included: anti-SLAMF6 antibody (1:100, Atlas antibodies, HPA051903, Sweden), anti-SLAMF6 antibody (1:100, Abcam, ab213690), anti-CD3 antibody (1:100, Abcam, ab16669), anti-CD8 antibody (1:500, Cell Signaling Technology, #98,941, Danvers, MA, USA).


### Bisulfite sequencing PCR
  The total genome DNA was extracted from tissues using QIAamp DNA mini kit (Qiagen, USA). Bisulfite conversion was then performed using an EpiTect Bisulfite kit (Qiagen). Primers were designed from the 2000bp sites in the first exon, with relatively enriched CpG sites. The PCR products were purified and cloned into the PMD18-T vector (Invitrogen). This sequence contains a total of 9 CpG sites, the positions in the sequence are as follows: 21, 23, 50, 98, 151, 193, 205, 254, 275.

  Six cloned were randomly selected from each sample and sequenced. The details PCR reaction system and reaction conditions were shown in Supplementary file 1. The primer sequences were shown in Supplementary Table S1.


### Methylation-specific PCR
  The total genome DNA extracting and bisulfite conversion were performed as above described. The converted DNA was used as the template for PCR. The primer specific for methylated DNA was SLAMF6-M and the primer specific for unmethylated DNA was SLAMF6-U. After PCR reaction, the product was subjected to agarose gel electrophoresis. The details PCR reaction system and reaction conditions were shown in Supplementary file 1. The primer sequences were shown in Supplementary Table S1.


### Chromatin immunoprecipitation assay (CHIP assay)
  The CHIP experiment was used to verify the binding of transcription factor SMAD3 at the promoter of DNMT1 gene. The assay was performed according to the protocol based on SimpleChIP Enzymatic Chromatin IP Kit (cell signaling technology, cat. No. 9002S). Firstly, SW620 cells were fixed with formaldehyde and lysed, and chromatin is fragmented by partial digestion with Micrococcal Nuclease to obtain chromatin fragments of 1–5 nucleosomes. Secondly, the cell lysate was combined with CHIP-validated target protein antibodies (anti-SMAD3) and dilute at 1:50 overnight, then incubating with the CHIP-Grade Protein G Agarose Beads at 4 °C overnight. Thirdly, the binding of DNA protein mixture was reversed after several washes. After reversal of protein-DNA cross-links, the DNA is purified using DNA purification spin columns, allowing for easy and efficient recovery of DNA and removal of protein contaminants without the need for phenol/chloroform extractions and ethanol precipitations. The enrichment of special DNA sequences during immunoprecipitation can be analyzed by qPCR. The SimpleChIP Kit also provides important controls to ensure a successful CHIP experiment. The kit contains a positive control Histone H3 Antibody, a negative control Normal Rabbit IgG Antibody and primer sets for PCR detection of the human and mouse ribosomal protein L30 (RPL30) genes. The PCR expansion products were further separated on 2% agarose gel and visualized on a UV transilluminator. Further measurement of immunoprecipitated DNA using qPCR. Primers used for the amplification of the precipitated DNA are listed in Supplementary file 1.


### Electrophoretic mobility shift assay (EMSA)
  SW620 cells were collected on DMEM at a density of 1.0–1.5 × 107 in the presence of 10 ng/ml PMA, and collected after 2 h. The nuclear protein was extracted and determined the protein concentration through Bradford method. EMSA experiment was conducted using a biotin labeled EMSA assay kit, according to the manufacture’s manual. The EMSA reaction is mainly divided into four groups: negative control group, sample group, probe cold competition group, and mutation probe cold competition group. Nuclear protein samples (3 ug) were incubated with the biotin labeled probes (1 ul) and poly (dI:dC, 1 ug/ul) in a binding reaction buffer at room temperature for 20 min. The incubation mixture was then separated by electrophoresis on a 6.5% nondenaturing polyacrylamide gel (PAGE) in 0.25 Tris–EDTA buffer at 100 V for 1 h. Finally, the bands were visualized by Viagne Coo1Imager (Viagene Biotech Co., China).


### Tumor infiltrating immune cells
  We used the CIBERSORT algorithm to calculate the relationship between the expression of SLAMF6 in colorectal cancer and the infiltration of 22 immune cells. After grouping the expression of SLAMF6 into high and low groups, visualized the data using ggplot2 package. Calculating the matrix and immune scores in the colorectal cancer microenvironment using R package stimate (Yoshihara et al. 2013), and perform correlation analysis with the expression of SLAMF6.


### Tumor xenografts in nude mice
  All experiments involving in animals were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Nantong University, China. Five-week-old C57BL/6 mice were randomly divided into five groups (n = 4 per group). A total of 16 mice were used in our research. Mice were housed four mice per cage in a specific pathogen-free room with a 12 h light/dark schedules at 25 °C ± 1 °C and were fed an autoclaved chow diet and water ad libitum. MC38 cells were subcutaneously injected into the mice (1.0 × 107 cells/mouse) for the formation of the subcutaneous model. For the drugs treatment, 5-Aza (10 mg/kg), PD-L1 antibody (50 mg/kg), combined with 5-Aza (10 mg/kg) and PD-L1 antibody (50 mg/kg) or saline as control were intraperitoneally injected into mice, upon the formation of subcutaneous model. Tumor growth was monitored every 4 days and tumor volumes were calculated as (a × b2)/2, a and b were the longest and shortest diameters of the tumors. The mice were sacrificed at a time-defined endpoint and tumor weights and volumes were assessed by double-blinded evaluation. Paraffin embedding and sectioning of mice tumor tissue for subsequent immunohistochemistry and hematoxylin–eosin staining analysis.


### Statistical analysis
  Student’s t test was used to analyze the relative expression of CRC tissues between two groups, and one-way ANOVA was used to analyze multiple groups. Pearson correlation test was employed for clinicopathological feature correlation analysis and Kaplan–Meier method was used for prognostic analysis. Significant differences were considered where p < 0.05 represented as *, p < 0.01 represented as ** and p < 0.001 represented as ***. The figures were drawn using Graphpad prism 7.0.


## Results

### Subtype-specific differential methylation analysis in TCGA CRC
  Firstly, we analyzed progression-free survival (PFS) differences among the four CMS subtypes. The results demonstrated that CMS4 CRC exhibited the shortest PFS and highest degree of malignancy, suggesting the most aggressive clinical behavior (p < 0.05) (Supplementary Fig. S1A, B). Subsequently, we pinpointed the differential methylated sites (DMSs) between CMS4 and CMS1-3. At the thresholds of FDR < 0.05 and p < 0.05, we identified 726 significant DMSs (Fig. 1A). Cluster analysis unveiled distinct methylation patterns within CMS4, with most sites exhibiting notably higher methylation levels compared to other subtypes (Fig. 1B). Functional enrichment analysis of these DMSs highlighted their involvement in immune system and transcription factor, suggesting that altered promoter methylation affects tumor immune microenvironment and transcription factor binding (Fig. 1C, D). Analysis of these methylation sites localization showed that only a minority of DMSs were located within CpG islands, most methylation sites are distributed in the open sea region, while the rest located in the N-shelf, N-shore, S-shelf, and S-shore (Fig. 1E). Next, random forest and lasso regression were performed to evaluate the importance of these DMSs, ultimately identifying 43 significantly important methylation sites (Fig. 1F, G). Kaplan Meier analysis revealed 8 methylation sites with significant prognostic value (p < 0.05) (Fig. 1H). Here are the 8 methylation sites, cg10671066, cg06827038, cg05859264, cg01262913, cg18833140, cg25095814, cg26530341, cg27248887, respectively. ### The recognition of SLAMF6 methylation status in CMS4 CRC
  The general changes of DNA methylation landscapes in tumors are complex and diversified. Much of the hypo-methylation is concentrated in broad repeat-rich intergenic domains, leading to genomic instability, while hyper-methylation in a subset of CpG islands generally leads to transcriptional inhibition of tumor suppressor genes [24]. Previous researches indicated that CpG sites located in promoter mainly distributed in island, shore, shelf and open sea. Although the promoter of SLAMF6 does not contain CpG island, we selected the first exon and the pre-2000bp sites where CpG dinucleotide relative enrichment region for designing primers (Fig. 3A, B). The methylation status of SLAMF6 was verified by Bisulfite Sequencing PCR (BSP). The sequence data revealed there were total 9 methylation sites located in SLAMF6 promoter region and the proportion of SLAMF6 methylation sites in NCM460, DLD1, SW1116, HT29, SW480, SW620 and HCT116 cells were 58.9%, 36.7%, 51.1%, 55.6%, 80%, 93.3% and 87.8%, respectively (Fig. 3C, D). Methylation Specific PCR (MSP) was also employed to confirm SLAMF6 methylation status in CRC cells. The electrophoretic pattern manifested the promoter methylation (M) status of SLAMF6 gene across SW480, SW620, and HCT116 was more notable than un-methylation (U) status (Fig. 3E). Furthermore, we also conducted MSP for detecting 4 cases metastatic CRC tissues and 3 cases non-metastatic CRC tissues, and the results demonstrated that the methylation (M) status was significantly higher in metastatic CRC tissues than non-metastatic CRC tissues (Fig. 3F). ### Evaluation of SLAMF6 expression levels and biological function in CRC
  To validate the expression levels of SLAMF6 in CRC, qPCR, western blot and immunohistochemistry were used to detect SLAMF6 expression in both mRNA and protein levels. Firstly, differences analysis was conducted on the expression of SLAMF6 in different stages and metastasis of CRC, which revealed SLAMF6 was much lower in stage IV and metastatic tissues (normal: n = 51, 2.82 ± 0.66; M0: n = 475, 2.17 ± 0.99; M1: n = 89, 1.95 ± 0.94) (Fig. 4A, B). SLAMF6 expression in the four CMS subtypes was significantly lower than in normal tissues. Moreover, the expression level of SLAMF6 was relatively higher in CMS1 subtype and lower in CMS2 subtype. This phenomenon may be attributed to SLAMF6 expressed on various cells in tumor microenvironment, such as tumor associated immune cells and stromal cells (Fig. 4C). Clinicopathological data analysis displayed that SLAMF6 low expression was related to the pathological stage (p = 0.015) and pathological M stage of CRC patients (p = 0.004) (Table 2). Logistic regression analysis also indicated that SLAMF6 was significantly correlated with pathological M stage (OR, 95% CI: 0.501(0.312–0.805), p = 0.004) (Table 3). Prognostic analysis demonstrated that patients with high SLAMF6 expression exhibited better progression-free survival (PFS) than SLAMF6 lower expression patients (HR: 0.65 (0.46–0.93, p = 0.019)) (Fig. 4D). Additionally, the expression of SLAMF6 in CRC cells was demonstrated that DLD1 had remarkably higher levels, which was belonging to the CMS1 subtype (immunological), however, other kinds of CRC cells, especially CMS4 (mesenchymal) cells, such as SW480, SW620, HCT116 were holding relatively low expression of SLAMF6 (Fig. 4E). Immunohistochemistry indicated SLAMF6 was mainly expressed in tumor cell membrane and extracellular microenvironment. It presented multiple patterns, patients with CRC metastasis showed negative SLAMF6 expression, while microsatellite instability (MSI) patients possessed obviously high expression of SLAMF6. In addition, SLAMF6 was also overexpressed in CRC tumor immune microenvironment (Fig. 4F). The expression of SLAMF6 in DLD1 transfected with siRNA and SLAMF6 in SW480 cells transfected with SLAMF6 full-length plasmid were confirmed using western blot (Supplementary Fig. S3A, B). Colony formation demonstrated that siRNA remarkably promoted cell proliferation compared with control group in DLD1, whereas SLAMF6 overexpression reduced cell proliferation in SW480 cells (Fig. 4G). Trans well chamber displayed that knockdown of SLAMF6 enhanced the migration ability of DLD1 cells, while overexpression of SLAMF6 inhibited the migration ability of SW480 (Fig. 4H). ### TGF-β/SMAD3/DNMT1 regulated the expression of SLAMF6
  The previous research conclusion suggested that the hypermethylation of SLAMF6 in CMS4 may be due to the catalytic formation of methyltransferase. Then we stimulated CMS4 cells with different concentrations of methyltransferase inhibitors (5-aza-2-deoxycytidine, 5-Aza). The results showed that 5-Aza upregulated the expression of SLAMF6 (Fig. 5A). Further, we inhibited DNMT1, DNMT3A, DNMT3B in CMS4 cells, and the results showed knockdown of DNMTs also upregulated SLAMF6 expression (Fig. 5B). Given the hallmark of TGF-β signaling activation in CMS4 CRC, we further explored the potential function of TGF-β in modulating SLAMF6 expression. Western blot demonstrated that TGF-β counteracted the increase of SLAMF6 expression triggered by siDNMT1 or 5-Aza, this funding suggested a suppressive role of TGF-β on SLAMF6 expression (Fig. 5C). Previous studies have reported that transcription factors SMAD2/3 administrated an important part in mesenchyme activation [25–27]. An increase of p-SMAD2/3 and DNMT1 in CMS4 cells with different concentrations of TGF-β stimulation was demonstrated by western blot (Fig. 5D), indicating the accumulation entry of transcription factors into the nucleus. To further investigate whether the regulatory of TGF-β on DNMT1 expression was dependent on SMAD2/3, we knocked down SMAD3 in CMS4 cells and western blot showed the upregulation of DNMT1 expression induced by TGF-β was significantly inhibited (Fig. 5E, F). These observations suggested that TGF-β activated p-SMAD2/3 and induced high expression of DNMT1. Deeply, we performed potential prediction of transcription factors binding sites on DNMT1 promoter according to JASPAR database, in which SMAD3 was predicted to be a potential transcriptional enhancer (Fig. 5G). CHIP assay was applied to verify whether SMAD3 interacted with DNMT1. The results indicated the transcriptional enhancer SMAD3 was mainly bound at sites −1345 to −1529 on the 3’–5’ strands of the DNMT1 promoter (Fig. 5H, J). Furthermore, EMSA was also used to determine the effect of nuclear protein-DNA binding activity. As shown in Fig. 5J, SW620 cells with 5 ng/ml and 10 ng/ml TGF-β-treated groups showed obvious binding complex of SMAD3 to the probe sequence when compared with the control group. ## Discussions
  Colorectal cancer (CRC) is a highly heterogeneous malignant tumor, where patients with the same pathological type and clinical stage exhibit significant prognostic differences. To elucidate the pathogenesis and molecular mechanisms of CRC, the international Colorectal Cancer Subtyping Consortium utilized a cohort of 4,151 CRC patients and integrated multiple computational algorithms to establish the Consensus Molecular Subtypes (CMS) classification system. This system categorized patients into four distinct subtypes based on molecular characteristics and prognostic outcomes, with CMS4 exhibiting the poorest prognosis and highest propensity for recurrence and metastasis. In this study, we performed CMS classification of CRC patients from TCGA database and compared differentially methylated sites (DMSs) between CMS4 and CMS1-3 subtypes. Our analysis revealed significantly higher methylation levels in CMS4 CRC compared to other subtypes. Among 726 DMSs, we applied random forest and LASSO regression analyses for feature selection, combined with progression-free survival (PFS), ultimately identifying 8 prognostic methylation markers. Correlation analysis demonstrated significantly negative associations between methylation values of these sites and their corresponding mRNA expressions. CMS4 CRC is mainly characterized by TGF-β activation in tumor microenvironment, which promotes the formation of various tumor features, including sustained angiogenesis, immune evasion, epithelial mesenchymal transition (EMT), and metastasis. The latest researches showed TGF-β mediated tumor invasiveness and increased the expression of DNMTs [28, 29]. These studies suggested the expression of methylation genes may be an important reason for TGF-β mediated tumor progression.

  Extensive studies have highlighted an important link between abnormalities in DNA methylation and CRC initiation or progress [30, 31]. However, there are still few reports on the regulatory relationship between DNA methylation and CMS4 CRC. In this study, we identified that SLAMF6 was hypermethylated in CMS4 CRC from TCGA database. The hypermethylation status of SLAMF6 in CRC cells was further validated by MSP and BSP, and a total of 9 CpG sites located in SLAMF6 promoter regions. We detected a significant decrease of SLAMF6 expression in CRC, especially in advanced CRC. Therefore, we speculated the decrease of SLAMF6 expression in CMS4 was mediated by methylation modifications in its promoter region. DNA methyltransferases (DNMTs) shares with a meaningful role in epigenetic regulation of tumor cell proliferation and survival. Previous studies demonstrated that elevated DNMT1 expression was presented in CRC, which frequently contributing to inhibition of tumor suppressor genes [32, 33]. Wang et al. reported DNMT1 mediated miR-152-3p promoter hypermethylation induced upregulation of target gene TMSB10. Silencing DNMT1 inhibited the methylation level of miR-152-3p, thereby inhibiting CRC progress [34]. Suppression of DNMT1 expression attenuated tumor growth via regulation of FOXO3a/FOXM1/SOX2 signaling in breast cancer. Mechanistically, DNMT1 maintained FOXO3a promoter hypermethylation, which was functionally associated with the inhibition of FOXM1/SOX2 signaling pathway and resulted in the decline of breast cancer stem cells properties and tumorigenicity [35]. In this study, we inhibited the expression of DNA methyltransferases (transfected with siRNAs or stimulated with 5-Aza) in CMS4 CRC cells. Western blot demonstrated that suppressing DNMT1 significantly upregulated SLAMF6 expression levels.

  Growth factors in tumor environments are critical regulators of cell survival and metastasis. Aberrant activation of TGF-β signaling acts as a prominent function in tumor progression and metastasis. However, whether and how TGF-β regulates SLAMF6 expression in CMS4 cells are still not clear. Hence, we applied western blot for investigating the regulatory mechanism of SLAMF6 methylation in CMS4 cells induced by TGF-β signaling activation. Results showed the expression of DNMT1 was increased, on the contrary, the expression of upregulated SLAMF6 induced by siDNMT1 or 5-Aza was reversed based on TGF-β stimulation. It suggested the decrease of SLAMF6 expression induced by TGF-β depended on DNMT1. By investigating the upstream regulatory mechanisms of SLAMF6, we demonstrated that its expression was related to the hypermethylation led by TGF-β in a DNMT1-dependent manner. Previous studies have shown TGF-β signal transduction strongly drives SMAD transcription activation, and the different biological roles of TGF-β signals in cancer may partially depend on the molecules interacting with SMAD, thereby regulating the function of SMAD and altering the expression profile of TGF-β target genes. Tian et al. identified a kind of newly binding partner of DNMT3b, named as PHF14, which recruited DNMT3b to the SMAD7 gene locus, leading to SMAD7 methylation, transcriptional suppression and TGF-β signaling hyperactivation. And inhibitory of DNA methylation, which breaking the interaction between PHF14 and DNMT3B, may be a potential strategy for TGF-β driven lung adenocarcinoma progression and metastasis [36]. We speculated that SMAD2/3 also regulated DNMT1 expression in response to TGF-β stimulation in CRC cells. Treatment of TGF-β in CMS4 cells enhanced the phosphorylation of SMAD2 and SMAD3. Inhibition of SMAD3 not only reduced the expression level of DNMT1 in TGF-β (+) group than TGF-β (−) group. Moreover, CHIP assay and EMSA assay confirmed that SMAD3 transcription factor was binding with DNMT1 promoter region (−1345 to −1529). Based on these data, we validated the low expression of SLAMF6 in CMS4 CRC and TGF-β activation induced hypermethylation of SLAMF6 promoter via TGF-β/SMAD3/DNMT1 pathway. However, the potential mechanism of SLAMF6 low expression in CRC remained to be explored.

  In our study, the expression levels of SLAMF6 displayed different patterns in CRC, especially in advanced CRC with much low expression and CMS1 CRC with dramatically high expression. CIBERSORT analysis showed multiple types of immune cells, such as DC cells, B cells, CD8 T cells, Macrophages, Mase cells, Neutrophils, NK cells, Th1 cells, Th2 cells and Treg cells, were highly enriched in CRC patients with SLAMF6 high expression, while CRC patients with SLAMF6 low expression were accompanied with low infiltration of immune cells. Meanwhile, SLAMF6 expression was positively correlated with immune score and matrix score in tumor microenvironment. These data were revealing a positive relevance between SLAMF6 and immune cells infiltration. DNA methylation of RUNX3 played a critical role in CD8+ T cell infiltration and differentiation during decitabine primed PD-1 antibody immunotherapy, which provided a supporting mechanism for the essential role in immunotherapy. DNA methylation reprogramming RUNX3 was a crucial mediator of CD8+ T cell function [37]. Luo et al. [38] reported 5-Aza enhanced IFN–γ response via upregulating the expression of MHC-I molecules in breast cancer. DNMT1 inhibitors, also known as hypomethylating agents, such as 5-azacytidine (5-Aza) and 5-aza-2'-deoxycytidine, are incorporated into DNA where they covalently bind DNMTs [39]. In this study, we evaluated the anti-cancer effect of 5-Aza combined with PD-L1 antibody on tumors by constructing mice bearing tumor model. The animal experiment displayed combined treatment of 5-Aza and PD-L1 antibody significantly inhibited the volumes and weights of mice bearing tumors and IHC also demonstrated the expression levels of CD3, CD8 and SLAMF6 were upregulated upon combination treatment of 5-Aza and PD-L1 antibody than 5-Aza or PD-L1 antibody treatment alone. These data implied 5-Aza reversed the methylation status of SLAMF6 promoter region, thus upregulating SLAMF6 expression and enhancing the therapeutic effect of PD-1 antibody.


## Conclusions
  We demonstrated the crucial role of SLAMF6 methylation modification induced by TGF-β in CMS4 CRC and outlined the mechanism of the TGF-β-SMAD3-DNMT1-SLAMF6 pathway. SLAMF6 expression was involved in the immune infiltration of CRC microenvironment. Therefore, SLAMF6 can be explored as a novel therapeutic approach to improve clinical outcomes in patients with CRC.
