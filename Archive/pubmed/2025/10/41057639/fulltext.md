# Elucidating the mycochemistry and anticancer efficacy of F5 fraction of ethanolic extract of Daldinia eschscholtzii


## Abstract
  Fungi, or mushrooms, are a source of bioactive compounds that have valuable health effects. The column chromatography of ethanolic extract of Daldinia eschscholtzii (DEE) was performed, 10 fractions were collected, and out of them the F5 fraction demonstrated the greatest antioxidant activity by DPPH (1,1 diphenyl-2-picrylhydrazyl) with an EC50 of 0.94 ± 0.05a mg/mL. Mycochemistry analysis by liquid chromatography mass spectrum (LC-MS) showed that F5 was a mixture of four compounds, like myricetin, tricin, eudesmin, and (-)-curcumol. Cytotoxicity assay exhibited anti-proliferation activity with IC50 values of 125.66 ± 6.34, 96.22 ± 1.92, and 76.91 ± 2.05 µg/mL of F5 against A549 lung cancer cell line at 24, 48, and 72 h, respectively. The mechanism of anticancer effect of F5 (DEE) on the cancer cell line (A549) included the induction of apoptosis, reduction of mitochondrial potentiality, and change of gene expression levels of TCASPASE 3, TCASPASE 9, TP53, and TBCL2 of the cell line. The qPCR study showed that TCASPASE 3, 9, TBAX, and TP53 were up-regulated significantly, whereas TBCL2 was down-regulated significantly after the treatment with F5 (50 and 150 µg/mL) for 24 h. It had an anti-migration activity, which was justified by downregulation of TMMP 2 & 9, and TVEGF genes. The physicochemical, pharmacokinetic, and medicinal properties of all four compounds in F5 were screened by three web predictors (SwissADME, pkCSM, and AdmetSAR), and among them, curcumol, tricin, and eudesmin compounds fulfilled all the criteria of different rules or filters (Lipinski’s rules, Ghose filter, Veber filter, Egan filter, and Muegge filter) for drug development. Molecular docking studies of the compounds like tricin, curcumol, and eudesmin with receptor proteins like Caspase 3, p53, and BCL2 revealed that tricin had the highest binding energy towards Caspase 3 and p53, allowing upregulation of these genes, while eudesmin showed the highest binding energy (-7.6 kcal/mol) with BCL2, suggesting inactivation of BCL2. In conclusion, F5 of DEE had both anticancer and anti-metastasis properties.


## Introduction
  One of the leading causes of death worldwide is cancer. About 20 million cases of cancer and 9.7 million cancer-related deaths occurred worldwide in 2022, according to the American Cancer Society’s (ACS) Global Cancer Statistics, 20241. Scientists estimated that this disease will afflict 35 million people across 185 nations by 2050. In 2025, it was estimated that there would be 2,041,910 new cases of cancer and 618,120 cancer-related deaths in the USA2. Current Global Cancer Statistics-2025 demonstrated that approximately 10 million people die owing to cancer every year. In 2024 alone, an estimated 20 million new cases were diagnosed (https://soulivity.com/health/world-cancer-day-2025-advancing-the-fight-against-cancer). Lung cancer was the most common cancer worldwide in 2020, followed by breast cancer. At the same time, cervical cancer ranked 8th worldwide3–5 but Cancer Statistics-2025 showed the same thing. Furthermore, due to a lack of early identification and treatment, 70% of cancer deaths occurred in low- and middle-income nations. An inadequate diet, obesity, alcohol usage, and smoking are responsible for about 40% of cancer cases (https://soulivity.com/health/world-cancer-day-2025-advancing-the-fight-against-cancer). Chemotherapy, surgery, radiotherapy, immunotherapy, and photodynamic therapy are among the treatments that have been used for a long time to treat cancer6. However, they are causing several side effects and complications, including the emergence of drug resistance in cancer patients7which emphasizes the urgent need for new, effective, and less-toxic therapeutic approaches8. In both men and women, lung cancer is the primary cause of cancer-related death in more developed countries around the world9,10. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), lung cancer is responsible for one in six deaths, making it the second most common cause of death11. The prognosis of non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) is poor; only 15% of cases survive for 5 years12. Thus, the development of new, potent, and low-toxicity anticancer agents is urgent, and in this context, natural compounds have been emphasized as potential anticancer drugs7. Plant-based preparations are currently categorized as “dietary supplements” in the United States by the Dietary Supplement Health and Education Act (DSHEA). Cancer sufferers use these supplements to alleviate symptoms and prevent the disease from returning. Many patients take plant-based supplements in addition to chemotherapy or other cancer therapies13. Fungal fruit bodies or mushrooms are “mini-factories” of bioactive compounds that have valuable health effects without any known negative side effects and can be used as significant therapeutic products against cancer14–16. Certain naturally occurring mushroom compounds have been found to show target-specific activity against aberrantly activated signaling pathways in cancer cells. In addition, these compounds have also been reported to have a negative effect on specific molecular targets that are involved in angiogenesis, cell survival, and proliferation7,17. Globally, the number of fungal species was estimated as 2–11 million18,19 while the number of described species was 150,60020, (http://www.speciesfungorum.org/; December 2021). The use of medicinal fungi from the phylum Ascomycota in the treatment of type 2 diabetes has already been recorded by Jiang et al.21. There are some poisonous mushrooms or fungi; among them, almost all species of Amanita are deadly poisonous except Amanita caesarea. A few poisonous species of this genus are Amanita virosa, Amanita phalloides, and Amanita verna. So, before eating any wild mushroom, consumers must consult with a mycologist or fungal expert22. Daldinia eschscholtzii is known as a saprophytic or endophytic fungus, belonging to the phylum Ascomycota, class Sordariomycetes, order Xylariales, and family Xylariaceae. On a global scale, little research has been conducted into Daldinia eschscholtzii’s pharmacological qualities23. It was found that Daldinia concentrica extract showed some estrogen-like activities in vivo24. According to Chutulo and Chalannavar25 the solvent extract of Daldinia eschscholtzii has shown antibacterial, antioxidant, and cytotoxic properties. Similarly, in India, relatively few works have been done on this fungus25,26 most probably because it is a purely wild fungus or mushroom with no culinary importance and is not readily available everywhere at all times. So, a proper investigation related to a medically important or an application is very limited. Keeping this in mind, our objectives for this study were to explore the anticancer activity of the F5 fraction of the ethanolic extract of Daldinia eschscholtzii (DEE) on the A549 lung cancer cell line, to identify its bioactive compounds using LC-MS, to assess drug-likeness of the selected compounds and to investigate the molecular interactions of selected compounds from F5 with two apoptotic proteins (Caspase 3 and p53) and one anti-apoptotic protein (BCL2) using molecular docking tools. Practically, fractionization of ethanolic extract, antioxidant activity, and anticancer potentiality against lung cancer cell line A549, along with in silico investigation of compounds of F5 with receptors like Caspase 3 and BCL2, were not done before this study.


## Results

### Fractionization of DEE by column chromatography
  A total of 10 fractions like F1-F10 were collected, and the color of F1-F3 was off white, F4-F7 were yellowish, while F8-F10 were whitish. Each fraction was later subjected to analysis of antioxidant content and activity.


### Study of antioxidant content and activity of fractions (F1-F10)
  The results (Table 1) showed that fraction F5 showed the highest phenolic content (22.33 ± 1.45a mg GAE/g), followed by F4 (13.56 ± 1.22b mg GAE/g) and F6 (11.32 ± 1.75bc mg GAE/g). F6 exhibited the highest flavonoid content (6.34 ± 0.49a mg QE/g dry weight), followed by F5 (5.23 ± 0.45 mg QE/g dry weight). Whereas, among all fractions, F5 demonstrated the lowest EC50 value (0.94 ± 0.05 mg/mL), which implied the strongest DPPH radical scavenging activity. Fraction F5 was chosen as the most promising fraction based on its superior performance concerning two parameters (TPC and EC50 of DPPH scavenging activity).

## Anticancer activity of F5 of DEE

### Cytotoxicity/antiproliferative assay/MTT assay
  In this study, the cytotoxic/antiproliferative effect of F5 of DEE against the lung cancer cell line A549 was evaluated by MTT assay. As a measure of cell viability, the cytotoxicity was dose-dependent. The result showed that the proliferation of the cells was gradually reduced when the F5 of DEE dosage was gradually increased from 10 to 150 µg/mL at 24, 48, and 72 h. The percentages of cell growth inhibition at 24 h for 10, 50, 100, and 150 µg/mL dosages of F5 were 16.22 ± 3.32, 33.08 ± 4.79, 47.99 ± 1.30, and 52.57 ± 1.24%, respectively. In contrast, at 48 h and at the same dosages, the percentages of cell growth inhibition were 19.55 ± 5.86, 33.13 ± 2.33, 51.67 ± 0.85, and 57.76 ± 2.22%, respectively, while at 72 h, the percentages were 22.27 ± 2.04, 43.97 ± 1.70, 57.79 ± 0.75, and 66.11 ± 0.78% for the same dosages, respectively. The IC50 values of F5 of DEE at 24, 48, and 72 h were 125.66 ± 6.34, 96.22 ± 1.92, and 76.91 ± 2.05 µg/mL, respectively (Fig. 2). Tukey’s multiple comparisons test showed that the relationship between IC50 values at different hours (24 vs. 48, 24 vs. 72, and 48 vs. 72 h) was statistically significant (p < 0.05) (Fig. 3). For positive control (PC) (Adriamycin), the percentages of inhibition against A549 cells were 45.11 ± 1.39, 66.53 ± 2.26, and 77.90 ± 5.01% at 1, 3, and 5 µg/mL, respectively for 24 h. The IC50 for adriamycin was 1.69 ± 0.23 µg/mL at 24 h. DMSO (0.1%) was used as a vehicle control, and it showed no inhibition against the cancer cell lines. To determine whether F5 had toxicity towards the normal cell line, HEK 293T was exposed to a 150 to 500 µg/mL dose of F5 for 24, 48, and 72 h, and results exhibited that the percentages of growth inhibition were 0.11 ± 0.001 to 1.13 ± 0.15, 0.23 ± 0.002 to 2.21 ± 0.56 and 0.50 ± 0.001 to 3.10 ± 0.60% at 24, 48, and 72 h, respectively (Fig. 2). The Pearson correlation coefficient test was used to check the relationship among the concentrations of F5 of DEE and the percentages of inhibition. The result was statistically significant (p < 0.05).

### Study of cell morphology changes by F5 under the bright field microscope
  In this study, after treatment with different doses of F5 of DEE (50, 100, and 150 µg/mL), the changes in A549 cell morphology from normal, elongated to irregular or round shapes were seen under the bright field microscope, and cell confluence levels were also reduced. The result showed that untreated control (negative control) cells exhibited a normal elongated shape (Fig. 4A), and confluence reached 90% after 24 h of culture. Cell confluence decreased and cells lost their attachment when cells were treated with 50 µg/mL of F5 (the lowest dosage) (Fig. 4B). At 100 µg/mL, cells became smaller in size and lost their shape, and some cells floated (Fig. 4C). At 150 µg/mL of F5 (highest dose), cells became round or irregularly shaped, shrunken, the plasma membrane blebbed, cell confluence was noticeably lower, and cell debris and floating dead cells were seen (Fig. 4D).

(C, D) Cells became round-shaped, plasma membrane blebbed when treated with 100 and 150 µg/mL of F5, respectively (at 10 × magnification; Olympus Corporation, Tokyo, Japan).


### Study of nuclear morphology changes under fluorescence microscopy
  The result of DAPI staining procedure showed that the negative control exhibited normal nuclei of A549 cells (Fig. 5A), while the changes in nuclear morphology like distorted or apoptotic nuclei (chromatin condensed, fragmented nuclei) after treatment with 50, 100, and 150 µg/mL of F5 of DEE (Fig. 5B, C, and D) at 24 h, were noticed. It may be the consequence of F5’s capacity to damage the nuclear envelope membrane. As a result, during F5 treatment, the changes in the structure of the nucleus trigger the apoptosis progression in A549 lung cancer cells.

### Determination of apoptosis through flow cytometer
  The Annexin V-FITC/PI assay results (Fig. 6) showed that after 24 h of F5 administration, there was a considerable induction of apoptosis in comparison to the control (4.9%) (A). Specifically, the percentages of late apoptotic cells at concentrations of 50 and 150 µg/mL of F5 were 11.0% (B) and 33.6% (C), respectively. Tukey’s multiple comparisons test showed that the comparisons of the late apoptotic cell percentages with the concentrations of F5 (Control vs. 50 µg/mL; Control vs. 150 µg/mL and 50 µg/mL vs. 150 µg/mL) were statistically significant (p < 0.0001).

### Effect of F5 of DEE on MMP (mitochondrial membrane potential) of A549 cell
  The results (Fig. 7) indicated that, compared to the negative control (A), there was a significant decrease in cells emitting red fluorescence and a marked increase in those emitting green fluorescence after treatment with F5 for 24 h. This disruption suggested MMP disruption. Following treatment with F5 concentrations of 50 and 150 µg/mL, the percentages of cells emitting green fluorescence were 12.5% (B) and 37.1% (C), respectively, whereas control cells exhibited 0.9% green fluorescence. These findings suggest that MMP disruption likely contributes to F5-induced apoptosis.

### Study of gene expression related to apoptosis by qPCR
  After 24 h of treatment with F5 of DEE (50 and 150 µg/mL), we observed up-regulation of TCASPASE 9, TCASPASE 3, TBAX, and TP53 mRNA expressions (Fig. 8Ai); whereas TBCL2 mRNA expression was down-regulated in F5-treated A549 cells compared to control (Fig. 8Aii). These findings strongly support that F5 induces apoptosis in A549 lung cancer cells by up-regulating TCASPASE 9, TCASPASE 3, TBAX, and TP53, while down-regulating TBCL2. All results were statistically significant (***p < 0.001, and *p < 0.05). Interestingly, our flow cytometric study for apoptosis revealed that F5 was able to induce apoptosis, and this qPCR result justified the flow cytometric result.


### Study of gene expression related to migration by qPCR
  The result showed that, after the treatment of F5 of DEE (50 and 150 µg/mL) at 24 h, the mRNA expression levels of TMMP 2 and 9 were significantly down-regulated compared to untreated A549 cells (Control) (Fig. 8B). All results were statistically significant (***p < 0.001, **p < 0.01, and *p < 0.05). Perhaps the result showed that F5 was more effective on TMMP 2 than TMMP 9. So, the result indicated that F5 had anti-migration activity.


### Study of gene expression related to angiogenesis by qPCR
  The result showed that, after the treatment of F5 of DEE with two concentrations (50 and 150 µg/mL) at 24 h, the mRNA expression level of TVEGF was significantly down-regulated compared to untreated A549 cells (Control). So, F5 had an anti-angiogenic effect as the expression level of TVEGF was significantly decreased by F5 (Fig. 8C). The results were statistically significant (***p < 0.001, **p < 0.01).

### Physicochemical, pharmacokinetic properties, and medicinal chemistry of compounds of F5 of DEE detected by LC-MS
  Based on SwissADME, AdmetSAR, and pkCSM prediction web servers, the physicochemical, lipophilicity, water solubility, pharmacokinetics, druglikeness, and medicinal chemistry properties of some compounds or ligands were tabulated in Table 3. The results (Table 3) showed that, among the four bioactive compounds, curcumol, tricin, and eudesmin fulfilled all the criteria of the different filters above (Lipinski’s rules, Ghose filter, Veber filter, Egan filter, and Muegge filter). Based on the SwissADME result, it was observed that these compounds were low molecular weight compounds with high gastrointestinal (GI) absorption. (-)-Curcumol was a water-soluble compound, and tricin and eudesmin were moderately water-soluble compounds. Tricin was able to inhibit CYP1A2 protein (overexpressed in tumor cells than normal cells). By the pkCSM predictor, bioactive compounds of F5 were evaluated for their toxicity based on the parameters like AMES toxicity, maximum tolerated dose (human) (log mg/kg/day), hERG I inhibitor, hERG II inhibitor, oral rat acute toxicity (LD50) (mol/kg), hepatotoxicity, skin sensitisation, T. pyriformis toxicity (log ug/L), etc. (Table 3). Hepatotoxicity, skin sensitization activity, hERG I and hERG II inhibition activity were not found in eudesmin and (-) - curcumol through this pkCSM predictor. Tricin has skin sensitization activity, but it showed no hepatotoxicity, and it had no inhibitory activity to hERG I and hERG II. From the result of chemical property prediction by the SwissADME predictor and pkCSM predictor, it was found that (-)-curcumol, tricin, and eudesmin were fitted for oral drug preparation. So, these compounds were selected for further study,like molecular docking against Caspase 3, p53, and BCL2 proteins. In addition, five cytochrome (CYP) subtypes, which played a key role in drug excretion from the cell, were analyzed. We found that tricin was an inhibitor to CYP1A2, CYP2C9, CYP2D6, and CYP3A4, while eudesmin was an inhibitor to CYP2D6 and CYP3A4, and myricetin was also an inhibitor to CYP1A2 and CYP3A4, but curcumol had no inhibitory power to any cytochrome subtypes.

### Molecular docking of some selected bioactive compounds with Caspase 3, p53, and BCL2
  To study the binding interaction, energy, and stability of selected compounds ((-)-curcumol, tricin, and eudesmin) with different amino acids of the active site of Caspase 3, p53, and BCL2, the above compounds were docked with the above proteins [Caspase 3 (Fig. 9), p53 (Fig. 10), and BCL2 (Fig. 11)]. The result showed that tricin had the highest binding energy towards Caspase 3 and p53 (−6.2 and − 7.6 kcal/mol, respectively), and (-)-curcumol had the lowest binding energy towards Caspase 3 and p53 (−5.1 and − 5.7 kcal/mol, respectively) (Table 4). Whereas in the case of BCL2, eudesmin showed the highest binding energy (−7.6 kcal/mol), and (-)-curcumol showed the lowest binding energy (−6.0 kcal/mol) towards BCL2 (Table 4). Glu 124, Gly 125, Leu 136, Thr 140, Phe 158, and Arg 164 amino acids of Caspase 3 receptor were engaged in interaction with the tricin ligand, where three hydrogen bonds were involved (Fig. 9A). Similarly, Lys 154, Lys 156, and Phe 158, along with 2 hydrogen bonds, were involved in interaction with eudesmin (Fig. 9B), but when curcumol interacted with Caspase 3, Cys 148, Arg 149, And Ser 150 amino acids, with 3 hydrogen bonds were engaged in the interacting pocket (Fig. 9C; Table 4). The amino acids of other receptors involved in interaction with other ligands and interacting hydrogen bonds were also presented in Table 4; Figs. 10 and 11.

## Discussion
  Fruit bodies of fungi, or mushrooms, have been identified as high in antioxidant content, and they also possess antioxidant characteristics. Because of these features, the majority of scientists select them as research samples for drug development, disease prevention, and health and well-being for future generations. Very recently, Zade et al.14 and Bao et al.45 have reviewed bioactive secondary metabolites from fungi or mushrooms for our health benefits, especially targeting cancer management. For an extended period, scientists have been studying the antioxidant content and activity of various solvent extracts (such as ethanolic, methanolic, and hexane) produced from different fungal fruit bodies. They have also identified the anticancer properties of these extracts in various human cancer cell lines46–50. Very recently, we50 analyzed the antioxidant content and activity of the ethanolic extract of D. eschscholtzii (DEE) and found significant amounts of total flavonoids (TFC), total phenolic content (TPC), total antioxidant capacity (TAC), and antioxidant activity. In this study, we isolated the F5 fraction of DEE, which was fractioned by column chromatography, and it also had a satisfactory and better total content of phenolic, flavonoid, and TAC among the other nine fractions. Ghosh et al.49 fractionized AAEAE (Astraeus asiaticus ethyl acetate extract) using column chromatography, yielding 20 (F1-F20) fractions, while F12 exhibited the highest antioxidant activity of the 20 fractions. Similarly, different solvent extracts of different spp of Pleurotus showed a varied range of antioxidant contents and activity46,51,52. There was a positive correlation among Fe2+ scavenging, total phenolic content, and total antioxidant capacity53. In our F5 fraction, phenolic compounds like myricetin and flavonoid compounds like tricin, along with sesquiterpenoid (curcumol) and lignin (eudesmin), were detected, and they attributed the antioxidant property of F5. Reports of the antiproliferative or cytotoxic property of Daldinia sp. were very few. Boua et al.54 reported cytotoxicity of ethyl acetate extract of D. concentrica against carcinoma vulvar squamous CA 431 cells, and the IC50 value of this extract was 0.46 mg/mL at 24 h, which was much higher than our result. Mishra et al.55 showed the anticancer effect of the crude extract of Daldinia eschscholtzii against the HeLa (cervical cancer) and MCF-7 (breast cancer) cell lines. They reported cytotoxicity in a dose-dependent manner. The cytotoxicity and antioxidant potentials of related genera of this fungus, such as Xylaria and Tuber aestivum within the Ascomycota phylum, demonstrate anti-inflammatory and anti-angiogenic activity, as well as a correlation with the compounds present56,57. When HeLa, A549, and MCF-7 cells were treated with F12 of the ethyl acetate extract of A. asiaticus, their nucleus morphology became condensed as well as irregular and fragmented, and apoptosis ensued49. Similar cases were noted in our present study. The changes in cancer cell morphology after exposure to any chemical are a major indication of the chemical’s anticancer properties58. Anticancer drugs primarily inhibit cancer cell proliferation by triggering apoptosis. Another recognized irreversible step in the death cascade is the depletion of mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP), which is triggered by various stress signals59. In the mitochondrial apoptotic pathway, the loss of MMP is considered a pivotal event60. Pandey et al.61 reported that PFEE (Pleurotus florida ethanolic extract) altered the mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP) of the HeLa cell line in a dose-dependent manner after 12 h of treatment. LBE (Lenzities betulina ethanolic extract) changed the mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP) in HeLa cells at concentrations of 250 and 500 µg/mL after 6 and 12 h of treatment62. Similarly, in this study, we found that F5 reduced mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP).

  In this study, we investigated how F5 of DEE affects A549 cells and elucidated its underlying mechanism by examining mRNA expression changes in genes associated with apoptosis. Real-time PCR (qPCR) is widely employed to measure intracellular mRNA levels and differential gene expression in various cells and tissues due to its sensitivity, efficiency, accuracy, and automation capabilities63,64. In our present experiment, the mRNA expression of pro-apoptotic (TCASPASE 9, TCASPASE 3, TBAX, and TP53) and anti-apoptotic (TBCL2) genes in A549 cells showed statistically significant differences in mRNA expression fold changes of TCASPASE 9, TCASPASE 3, TBAX, TP53, and TBCL2 in F5-treated A549 cells compared to untreated cells, as determined by qPCR (p < 0.05). Similarly, Kiddane et al.65 found through an RT-qPCR study that the crude extract of Ganoderma applanatum significantly enhanced the expression of TCASPASE 3 & 9, and TBAX genes, indicating a dose-dependent induction of apoptosis. Angiogenesis is a key phenomenon for tumor growth and metastasis, and the vascular endothelial growth factor receptor (VEGF) promotes tumor-driven angiogenesis and is typically overexpressed in solid tumors. In the present study, F5 downregulated the TVEGF gene, as supported by Ruma et al.66 using Cordyceps militaris extract. Another key component for migration of cancer cells, or metastasis, is an up-regulated expression of TMMP 2 & 9 (matrix metallopeptidase 2 & 9), which leads to the degradation of basement membrane materials like interstitial collagens I, III, and collagen IV67–69and more distant migration of cancer cells, or metastasis70. In this respect, our F5 was able to downregulate the TMMP 2 & 9 genes, which was supported by the finding of Haque et al.71where the mRNA expression of TMMP 9 was significantly decreased by the purified PEF-III fraction of Pleurotus highking. One of the novel targets for the treatment of metastatic cancers is downregulation of TMMP 2 & 9 genes to arrest migration of cancer cells.

  In our previous work50we found 28 compounds in DEE using LC-MS analysis; however, in this experiment, F5 of DEE of D. eschscholtzii contains only four compounds, like eudesmin, tricin, (-)-curcumol, and myricetin, all of which are responsible for antioxidant activity, anticancer potential, anti-angiogenesis, and anti-metastasis against the A549 cell line. Among the four compounds, tricin has already been considered a chemopreventive agent72–74 and an important nutraceutical, as it exhibits antioxidant75and anticancer74,76 properties. Eudesmin, on the other hand, has several biological activities, including cytotoxic, anti-inflammatory, immunostimulant, and inhibitory effects on TNF-α (tumor necrosis factor-alpha) production77–80. Similarly, curcumol promoted growth inhibition and apoptosis in human osteosarcoma cell lines81 human non-small cell lung cancer82 H1650 lung cancer cell line83 and lung adenocarcinoma84. It caused anti-metastasis via inactivation of PI3K/AKT and Wnt/-catenin pathway84. The mechanism of curcumol as an anti-breast cancer was noted via down-regulation of the expression of BCL2 protein85and also by activating p73 and PUMA in p53 mutant MDA-MB 231 cells (triple negative breast cancer)86. Liu87 noted that curcumol inhibited the invasion and migration of ovarian cancer cells by targeting PAX8. Fang et al.88 revealed that curcumol arrested the growth of xenograft tumors in mice. Several flavonoids have been recognized as nutraceuticals, and myricetin is a good example. Myricetin demonstrated anticancer potential by inducing cell cycle arrest and apoptosis in lung cancer cells (A549)89. This compound was an inhibitor of migration, invasion, and adhesion and could reduce the MMP 2/9 activity and mRNA levels of ST6GALNAC5 (ST6 N-Acetylgalactosaminide Alpha‐2,6‐Sialyltransferase 5) genes in MDA‐MB‐231Br cells and also in animals90. Researchers exhibited changing levels of apoptotic proteins (BAD, BCL2, and BAX) in mice91. This compound also exhibited antiproliferative activity in human cancer cells (A2780/CP70, OVCAR‐3), which showed myricetin inhibited angiogenesis. This anticancer activity was due to decreased production of the main angiogenesis factor, like VEGF, and reduced levels of p‐Akt, p‐70S6K, and hypoxia‐inducible factor‐1α (HIF‐1α) proteins in A2780/CP70 and OVCAR‐3 cells. The chemical stability of myricetin is dependent on pH and temperature92.

  The physicochemical, pharmacokinetic, and medicinal properties of these four compounds were chalked out by three web predictors, like SwissADME34AdmetSAR35and pkCSM36for drug-like characters. Physicochemical characteristics are closely related to intestinal permeability and solubility in the early stages of oral bioavailability. If a molecule violates the law more than twice, it cannot be classified as a drug93. Lipinski37 created and introduced an easy scientific rule, “Rule of 5” (RO5), as the four parameter cut-off values all contained 5’s (https://www.pharmainformatic.com/html/rule_of_5.html). RO5 is used to determine the drug-likeness, which is the consistency of orally active drugs in humans37,93. To be potentially used as a drug, the bioactive compounds must not violate more than one parameter of these rules. The absorption or permeation of a drug will be poor if molecular weight (MW) > 500 g/mol, number of H-bond acceptors > 10, number of H-bond donors > 5, and MlogP > 4.1537. The four compounds of F5 had passed by RO5 and filters38–41. The compounds of F5 acted as anti-drug resistant by inactivating or inhibiting cytochrome subtypes, which were responsible for drug efflux from cancer cells. In our present experiment, we observed that tricin, eudesmin, and curcumol of F5 had good binding energy with the target proteins like Caspase 3, p53, and BCL2, which implied that these compounds regulated the expression of Caspase 3, p53, and BCL2. Very recently, Ghosh et al.49 demonstrated the molecular docking binding energy, binding interaction amino acids, and bonds between ligands (Hexadecanoic acid, 9,12-Octadecadienoic acid, 3,4,5,6 Tetramethyl octane of F12 of A. asiaticus mushroom) and receptors (Caspase 3 and BCL2). Ralte et al.44 reported the binding energy of some selected compounds of a plant extract (Parkia timoriana) against the BCL2 protein. Kumar et al.42 demonstrated the binding energy of four major phytoconstituents against Caspase 3. Bioactive compounds from Moringa oleifera showed good affinity towards p5394. Our wet lab experiment, i.e., the qPCR study of mRNA expression, showed that the level of pro- and anti-apoptotic gene expressions was changed by F5; it was able to up-regulate the pro-apoptotic gene (TCASPASE 3, TP53, and TBAX) and down-regulate the anti-apoptotic gene (TBCL2), which justified this result. Additionally, the chemical properties of these compounds, as predicted by SwissADME, pkCSM, and AdmetSAR predictors, enabled their use in drug-like preparations, as they were soluble, had low molecular weight, high GIT absorption, and no toxicity.

  Some limitations are found in every research work. In our work, the limitations include (a) lack of biochemical analysis of F5, like TLC, GC-MS, HPTLC, etc., and (b) lack of in vivo work (mouse model) for detecting its anticancer activity and toxicity. Our present work consistently made an inroad for further work.


## Conclusion
  After critical discussion, we came to conclude that F5 exhibited notable cytotoxic effects against the A549 lung cancer cell line in a dose-dependent manner by inducing apoptosis through downregulating TBCL2 and upregulating TCASPASE 3 & TP53, and anti-metastasis by downregulating TVEGF and TMMP 2 & 9 genes. Moreover, F5 demonstrated potent anti-migration and anti-angiogenic activities by downregulating TVEGF and TMMP 2 & 9 genes. Three bioactive compounds (eudesmin, tricin, and (-)-curcumol) in F5 of DEE had drug-like and pharmacokinetic features for drug development as judged by web predictors and high binding energy with target proteins like the pro-apoptotic (Caspase 3 and p53) and anti-apoptotic (BCL2). These findings collectively highlight F5 of DEE as a promising natural resource for developing future anticancer therapies. Further investigation is also needed for its clinical applications. As this fungus has anticancer properties, therefore, its conservation by artificial cultivation is very much needed, and its sustainable harvesting from nature is immediately warranted.

### Fungal fruit body and Daldinia ethanolic extract (DEE)
  Previously, the fruit body of this fungus was collected from fallen logs of trees at Doperia Village (Bandipur), Khardaha, North 24 Parganas, West Bengal, India, in September 2020, carried to our laboratory, characterized, and identified by morpho-anatomically and molecularly (ITSs marker), and the nucleotide sequence was submitted to NCBI GenBank and published with the accession number (PP024880.1) as Daldinia eschscholtzii strain SKGTBON1950. This fruitbody of Daldinia eschscholtzii was extracted in ethanol solvent and named as Daldinia ethanolic extract (DEE), and its antioxidant content and activity were performed50. We have taken this DEE sample in this work for further study.


### Fractionation of DEE through column chromatography
  For the separation or fractionation of DEE, column chromatography was employed. Briefly, the column was initially packed with silica gel (mesh size 200–400, Merck) using petroleum benzene (PB). The sample was then introduced into the column by forming slurry. Elution was carried out stepwise using solvents of varying concentrations: 100% PB, followed by PB and ethyl acetate mixtures of 98:2, 95:5, 90:10, and finally, ethyl acetate and methanol mixtures of 98:2 And 95:5. Ten fractions were collected in a 2 mL Eppendorf tube by an automatic fraction collector attached to the column. This system was repeated twenty times, and each fraction (like F1 from the 1st time to 20th time) was pooled and concentrated by rotary evaporator and lyophilizer sequentially and converted into powder form, and weighed. These fraction powders were screened for antioxidant content and activity, and the best one was selected for the anticancer study.


### Antioxidant content of F1-F10

#### Methods for total phenolic content (TPC)
  TPC was evaluated according to the method of Ghosh et al.49 and Bera et al.50. Briefly, 0.5 mL (from the stock of 1 mg/mL) of the each fraction (F1-F10) of DEE was added to 0.5 mL of distilled water and 1 mL of FC reagent (ten times diluted with distilled water). After keeping it at room temperature (RT) (27 ± 2 °C) for 5 min, 2 mL of 700 mM of sodium carbonate was mixed to each test tube properly. Test tubes were then kept in the dark at RT for 45 min. After the incubation period, the absorbance was measured at 765 nm by a UV-Vis spectrophotometer (UV-1800, SHIMADZU, UV SPECTROPHOTOMETER). A standard curve was done using a solution of gallic acid (standard) (concentration; 10–80 µg/mL). From this curve, the TPC of the each fraction (F1-F10) of DEE was determined and expressed as mg gallic acid equivalent (GAE) per g of the each fraction (F1-F10) of DEE. The experiment was conducted in triplicate, and the mean value was taken.


#### Method for total flavonoid content (TFC)
  TFC of fractions (F1-F10) was determined according to the method described by Ghosh et al.49 and Bera et al.50. In a nutshell, 1 mL of the each fraction (F1-F10) of DEE sample (from 1 mg/mL stock of F5) was mixed with 0.2 mL AlCl3 solution in methanol (10%, w/v), 0.2 mL of 1 M of potassium acetate, and 5.6 mL distilled water. The mixture was then subjected to dark incubation for 30 min at RT. Absorbance was then measured at 415 nm wavelength against a reagent blank by a UV-Vis spectrophotometer. The TFC of the fractions (F1-F10) of DEE was measured using a standard curve of quercetin (concentration; 10–400 µg/mL). The data (total flavonoid content) was expressed as mg quercetin equivalent (QE) per g of the fraction (F1-F10) of DEE.

#### LC-MS study of F5 of DEE for identification of bioactive compounds
  The F5 was analyzed using LC–MS following the protocol outlined by Hajji et al.95. ### Cell morphology under bright field microscopy
  The changes of cell morphology after using F5 of DEE at different concentrations were investigated by an inverted fluorescence microscope under bright field. In brief, 2× 105 A549 cells/well were seeded into 6-well plates overnight at 37 °C in an atmosphere of 5% CO2 in a CO2 incubator, and at 80–90% confluence, the cells were treated with various concentrations of the F5 of DEE (50, 100, and 150 µg/mL). DMEM was used as a negative control. After 24 h of treatment, the cell images were observed under a microscope and were captured by a Canon camera fitted in the microscope (at 10×magnification; inverted fluorescence microscope, Olympus Corporation, Tokyo, Japan).


### Nuclear morphology by DAPI staining under an inverted fluorescence microscope
  Alterations in the nuclear morphology (chromatin condensation) of A549 cells induced by F5 of DEE were assessed using DAPI (4’,6-Diamidino-2-phenylindole) (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Inc.) staining, following the method98. Briefly, 2 × 105 A549 cells/well were plated onto a 6-well plate and allowed to incubate for 24 h. Once the confluence reached 80–90%, the cells were treated with varying concentrations of F5 samples (50, 100, and 150 µg/mL) in DMEM and incubated for an additional 24 h. DMEM was used as the vehicle control. After the 24h incubation, the cells were washed two times with PBS (1x). Subsequently, the cells were stained with DAPI dye (5 µg/mL) and incubated at room temperature in the dark for 10 min. Following this, the cells were washed thrice with PBS, and the stained nuclei were observed under an inverted fluorescence microscope (Olympus Corporation) at magnification of ×10.


### Determination of apoptosis by flow cytometer
  The Apoptosis-mediated cell death of A549 cells was measured by AnnexinV-FITC (Fluorescein isothiocyanate)/PI (propidium iodide) apoptosis detection kit (Santacruze Biotechnology, USA) according to the manufacturer’s protocol and the method previously described by Wlodkowic et al.99. Briefly, 2 × 105 density of A549 cells treated for 24 h with two concentrations of F5 of DEE, one was below the IC50 value (50 µg/mL) and other was above the IC50 value (150 µg/mL) at 24 h and non-treated cells were harvested using trypsin/EDTA, washed with cold PBS and centrifuged for 5 min at 2000 rpm. The cells were then re-suspended in 195 µL binding buffer. After mixing the buffer with 5 µL Annexin V-FITC, the sample was incubated for 15 min at 37°C in the dark and then centrifuged for 5 min at 2000 rpm. After removing the supernatant, the pellets were further re-suspended in 190 µL binding buffer, and 5 µL of propidium iodide (PI) was added for the detection of apoptosis. For the determination of apoptosis induced by F5, a minimum of 1 × 106 cells/mL were analyzed using a BD accuri C6 plus flow cytometer (Becton Dickinson, CA, USA) within 30 min after supravital staining.


### Mitochondrial membrane potential determination
  A crucial measure of mitochondrial function and a sign of healthy physiological activity is the mitochondrial transmembrane potential (ΔΨm). Alternation in the potential of the mitochondrial membrane can be used to measure the extent of apoptosis in treated cells. Using the Mito Probe™ JC-1 Assay Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific), mitochondrial membrane potential was measured by flow cytometry. In short, A549 cells were seeded onto a 6-well plate and treated with two concentrations of F5 (50 and 150 µg/mL) and incubated for 24 h at 37 °C in an atmosphere of 5% CO2 in a CO2 incubator. Following incubation, the cells were washed two times with PBS and incubated with 10 µL of 200 µM JC1 for 30 min at 37 °C in a CO2 incubator. After that, cells were given a single wash by adding 2 mL of warmed phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). The cell suspension was subjected to centrifugation for pellet formation, and then the tube was gently shaken to resuspend the cells. Then 500 µL PBS was poured into each tube, and the cell suspension was loaded into a flow cytometer with 488 nm excitation using emission filters that were appropriate for it. The gating of the cell, excluding debris, was done in a flow cytometer. The cells that had reduced fluorescence were considered as they had lost the mitochondrial membrane potential. In addition, we checked the function of F5 of DEE under inverted fluorescence microscopy (Olympus, Japan) by JC1 retention as compared with negative control counterparts, and it exhibited that treatment with F5 significantly reduced JC1 fluorescence in A549 cells, indicating that the mitochondrial pathway was responsible for F5-induced apoptosis100.

#### Prediction of physicochemical properties, lipophilicity, water solubility, pharmacokinetics, drug-likeness characters, and medicinal chemistry of the compounds
  In our study, four bioactive compounds (myricetin, (-)-curcumol, tricin, and eudesmin) from the LC-MS result of F5 of DEE were selected because they have anticancer properties, according to several researchers28,29. For searching physicochemical, lipophilicity, water solubility, pharmacokinetics, drug-likeness characters, and medicinal chemistry of four compounds of F5, SwissADME34 and pkCSM36 and AdmetSAR predictors35 were used. Lipinski’s “Rule of 5” (RO5) was used to determine the drug-likeness, which was the consistency of orally active drugs in humans37,93 (https://www.pharmainformatic.com/html/rule_of_5.html). To be potentially used as a drug, the bioactive compounds must be applied RO5 for oral administration. The absorption or permeation of a drug will be poor if molecular weight (MW) > 500 g/mol, number of H-bond acceptors > 10, number of H-bond donors > 5, MlogP > 4.1537. The drug score is used to select the bioactive compounds. The greater drug score value of the bioactive compounds denotes better drug candidates101. Along with Lipinski’s Rule of Five, other filters of different researchers like Ghose filter38Veber filter39Egan filter40 and Muegge filter41 were used to evaluate the drug like characters of these four compounds and then molecular docking of those compounds (ligands) that had no violations or single violation of RO5 with receptors (Caspase 3, p53, and BCL2) were performed.


### Molecular docking study of F5 of DEE bioactive compound
  For the molecular docking study of F5 of DEE bioactive compound, protein selection, protein preparation, ligand selection, ligand preparation, grid generation, and docking calculations were done.


### Receptor preparation
  The crystal form human Caspase 3 (PDB:3GJQ)42, p53 (PDB:2× 0W)43 and BCL2 (PDB:1K3K)44 were downloaded from PDB and saved (http://www.rcsb.org/). AutoDoc Vina (https://vina.scripps.edu/) software was used to remove all imported foreign substances like water and heteroatoms, but polar hydrogens and Kollman charges were added, and amino acid sequences, if missing, were recovered by this software. Images of prepared receptors (Caspase 3, p53, and BCL2) were visualized in Chimera 1.16 and saved. The target protein was then imported into SwissDoc (http//www.swissdock.ch/) software for molecular docking purposes.


### Ligand preparation
  Tricin, (-)-curcumol, and eudesmin were selected for ligand preparation based on chemical properties determined by SwissADME predictor34 and pkCSM predictor36and Literature search for their functions. They were selected as Ligands for molecular docking against Caspase 3, p53, and BCL2 receptors. They obtained in their stable conformation from PubChem, and then they were converted from SDF to a mol2 file using Open Babel software before being imported into SwissDock for molecular docking.


### Docking of receptors and ligand
  The automated docking tool SwissDock was used for the molecular docking study. The three-dimensional protein structure was enhanced with polar hydrogen atoms and Gasteiger partial charges. Before molecular docking, the protein structures were saved in pdb files. Pre-calculation of atomic affinities was done using AutoGrid. For the docking simulation, grid box for Caspase 3, p53, and BCL2 was integrated as 20(x) × 20(y) × 20(z). The pocket of the active site of Caspase 3 was centered at 33(x)×35(y)×43(z), 13(x)×35(y)×38(z), and 33(x)×35(y)×43(z) against tricin, (-)-curcumol, and eudesmin, respectively. For p53, the pocket of the active site was centered at 116(x) ×84(y) ×−34(z), 121(x) ×97(y) ×−39(z) and 116(x) ×84(y) ×−34(z) and for BCL2 the pocket of the active site was centered at 218(x) ×19(y) ×123(z), 225(x) ×37(y) ×123(z), and 225(x) ×37(y) ×123(z) against tricin, (-)-curcumol, and eudesmin respectively. The Protein-ligand dock complexes were analyzed by SwissDock software for post-docking studies, and visualized by the Discovery Studio Visualizer 4.1 client, and saved as images102.


### Statistical analysis
  Data were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). The Pearson correlation coefficient test was also used to check the relationship between concentrations of F5 of DEE and the percentage of inhibition of A549 cells. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to compare concentration differences, and Duncan multiple range test (DMRT)103 was also used. A statistically significant difference was analyzed at p < 0.05 level. Tukey’s multiple comparisons test was performed to understand the statistically significant variation (p < 0.05) between the IC50 values of F5 of DEE at 24, 48, and 72 h. Again, Tukey’s multiple comparisons test was accomplished to check the statistically significant variation between the late apoptotic cell percentages (after 24 h treatment) between the different concentrations of F5 of DEE (50 and 150 µg/mL, respectively) and the control treatment. The statistical analysis and all graphs were developed using GraphPad Prism 9 software.
