# Geniposide alleviates kidney fibrosis by targeting STAT3-HK2-mediated glycolysis


## Background
  Geniposide (GP), which is extracted from the traditional Chinese herb Gardenia jasminoides, has extensive pharmacological effects. Recently, studies have shown its protective effects on kidney diseases. However, whether GP can alleviate unilateral ureteral obstruction (UUO)-induced renal interstitial fibrosis is unknown. In addition, its potential protective mechanism against kidney fibrosis is worthy of further study. We investigated the effect of GP on renal interstitial fibrosis induced by UUO in vivo and on HK-2 cells treated with transforming growth factor β1 (TGF-β1) in vitro and explored the underlying mechanisms involved.


## Materials and methods
  A UUO model was constructed, and the mice were treated with GP (50 mg/kg/day) for 10 days. The kidney injury score was used to evaluate the degree of kidney injury based on HE staining, and Masson staining and immunohistochemistry were used to evaluate kidney fibrosis. High-throughput sequencing analysis of animal samples was subsequently conducted, and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) functional and pathway enrichment analyses were subsequently performed to determine the possible mechanism, which were subsequently validated in vitro and in vivo. Western blotting was used to detect the signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3)/hexokinase 2 (HK2) pathway, and molecular docking analysis was used to evaluate the binding ability of GP and STAT3. STAT3 was also overexpressed in HK-2 cells.


## Results
  GP alleviated renal fibrosis, regulated the glycolysis-related protein HK2 and inhibited glycolysis by binding to STAT3. In vitro experiments revealed that GP regulated glycolysis-related proteins and prevented cell epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) during TGF-β1 treatment in HK-2 cells. Furthermore, the phosphorylation of STAT3 was drastically reduced in response to GP stimulation, leading to attenuated STAT3 nuclear translocation. Molecular docking analysis revealed a stable conformation between GP and STAT3 at Tyr705. The effects of GP were blocked by overexpression of STAT3.


## Conclusion
  Our results showed that GP protected against renal fibrosis, possibly through inhibiting STAT3/HK2 signaling-mediated glucose metabolism in UUO mouse kidneys.

## Introduction
  Kidney fibrosis is a common pathological characteristic of many kidney diseases. In the process of kidney fibrosis, excessive pathological accumulation of extracellular matrix (ECM) occurs [1]. In the clinic, slowing the prognosis by controlling risk factors that exacerbate the deterioration of renal function and renal fibrosis is currently insufficient. The availability of effective pharmacological therapies targeting renal fibrosis is limited by the inherent characteristics of the illness, its diverse etiologies, and the intricate mechanisms underlying tissue injury. Therefore, the exploration of novel strategies for the development of prospective treatments for renal fibrosis holds substantial clinical importance.

  The intricate pathophysiology of renal fibrosis has been the subject of extensive research over an extended period. However, it has predominantly remained an area that has not been thoroughly investigated. Epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) in tubular cells was detected as renal fibrosis progressed [2]. This phenomenon has been recognized as a responsive mechanism that occurs in response to a lack of nutrients, injury to tissues, and an increase in proinflammatory cytokines [3]. Moreover, recent studies have demonstrated a growing incidence of the EMT process, which has been linked to impaired metabolic pathways due to a shift from oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) to glycolytic metabolism [4, 5]. Hypoxia stimulates glycolysis, while the presence of oxygen induces an aberrant variant known as aerobic glycolysis, commonly referred to as the Warburg effect. This process is a complex metabolic pathway that occurs in response to the absorption of glucose. It involves numerous processes, including enhanced glycolysis and impaired oxidative phosphorylation. Through the action of enzymes, these steps facilitate the conversion of glucose into pyruvate molecules, which are then utilized for the generation of cellular energy [6]. Hexokinase 2 (HK2) is an isoenzyme of hexokinase that plays a crucial role in the initial stage of glycolysis. Recent studies have highlighted their significant involvement in glycolysis, particularly in the progression of renal fibrosis [7, 8].

  Geniposide (GP), a naturally occurring compound from Eucommia ulmoides, has a diverse range of biological activities. The pharmacological benefits of GP that have gained significant recognition include its anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties. Previous studies have shown that GP alleviates VEGF-stimulated angiogenesis in rheumatoid arthritis [9]. It also has hepatoprotective [10], anti-neurodegenerative disorder [11], and anti-myocardial dysfunction effects [12]. Furthermore, GP exhibits renoprotective effects [13–15], especially in diabetic nephropathy (DN) [16, 17].

  Although it has been established that GP has the potential to improve oxidative stress and inflammatory responses associated with renal disease, the precise mechanisms through which it operates remain uncertain. Hence, the primary objective of this study was to enhance the understanding of the protective impact of GP on renal fibrosis by conducting in vivo and in vitro experiments and to investigate the potential mechanisms underlying this effect.


## Materials and methods

### Animal model and tissue Preparation
  C57BL/6J mice (males, 8 weeks old) were purchased from the Animal Center of Nanjing University (Nanjing, China). The environment in which the animals were kept was carefully managed (12 h of Light and darkness, 24 °C, and 55–60% humidity). All mice had free access to food and water and were acclimated for 1 week before surgery. Male C57BL/6J mice Weighing 22–24 g were randomly assigned to four groups with 8 mice in each group as follows: (1) sham-operated mice with normal saline (sham), (2) UUO mice with normal saline (UUO), and (3) UUO mice treated with GP (50 mg/kg). (4) Sham-operated mice treated with GP (50 mg/kg). The UUO model was established as follows: (1) The mice were anesthetized with sodium pentobarbital (50 mg/kg, intraperitoneally), sterilized, and depilated. (2) The left ureter was exposed via a left flank incision, Ligated with 4− 0 silk at two points, and cut between the 2 ligatures. The sham-operated group had no ligation. GP was administered (50 mg/kg/day) [16, 18, 19] by daily gavage from the day of surgery until the end of the experiment. The mice were sacrificed under anesthesia by cervical dislocation, and the kidneys and blood were collected at 10 days after surgery. The Animal Care and Use Committee of Anhui Medical University (Hefei, China) approved the animal experimental protocol under reference number LLSC20210354. The study was performed in compliance with the Experimental Animal Administration regulations issued by the State Committee of Science and Technology of the People’s Republic of China. The mouse care procedures were conducted in compliance with the institutional guidelines for animal research.

### Cell culture and treatments
  The human tubular epithelial cell line HK-2 was obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC) and cultured in DMEM/F12 supplemented with 10% FBS and 100 U/ml 1% penicillin and streptomycin at 37 °C with 5% CO2 in an incubator. HK-2 cells were subjected to serum starvation for 12 h, followed by treatment with 10 ng/ml TGF-β1 for 24 h. Moreover, HK-2 cells were treated with GP (100 µM) to investigate their effects on fibrosis.


### MTT assay for the analysis of HK-2 cell viability
  To evaluate the cytotoxic or cytoprotective effects of GP, mitochondrial dehydrogenase activity was quantified through a modified MTT [3-(4,5-dimethyldiazol-2-yl)−2,5-diphenyl tetrazolium bromide] method according to the manufacturer’s instructions (CAS: 298-93-1, Sigma‒Aldrich). Briefly, HK-2 cells were seeded in 96-well plates (1 × 104 cells/well). At approximately 80% confluence, HK-2 cells were mixed with the corresponding mixture at the following concentrations: control, GP, or GP (1, 2, 5, 10, 25, 50, 100 µM). After cocultivation for 48 h, the viability of the HK-2 cells was determined. Cell viability was determined by calculating the percentage of MTT reduction. The absorption of solubilized formazan was measured at a wavelength of 490 nm using an ELISA plate reader. The absorbance of the HK2 cells was used as the reference and was set at 100%.


### RNA extraction and quantitative real-time PCR
  Total RNA was extracted from cells or kidney tissues using an RNA-Quick Purification Kit (RN001, ES Science Biotech, Shanghai) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The resulting RNA was reverse transcribed to cDNA using HiScript III All-in-one RT SuperMix (Vazyme, Nanjing). qRT‒PCR was performed using SYBR® Green PCR Master Mix (Vazyme, Nanjing). We calculated the results via the 2−ΔΔCt method. The primer sequences are Listed in Supplementary Table 1.


### Western blot (WB) analysis
  Following the collection of tissues or cells through in vivo or in vitro investigations, the kidney tissues were subjected to homogenization, while the cells were lysed in a buffer solution with protease and phosphatase inhibitor cocktails. Subsequently, the tissue and cell lysates were centrifuged, and the resulting supernatants were assessed for protein concentration with a Bicinchoninic Acid Protein Kit. (Cat# P0012S, Beyotime). The protein samples were separated on 10–15% sodium dodecyl sulfate‒polyacrylamide (SDS‒PAGE) gels and transferred to polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes (Cat: ISEQ00010, Millipore), followed by blocking with 5% milk and incubation with antibodies against α-SMA (1:2000), collagen Ⅰ (1:1000), α-tubulin (1:10000), HK2 (1:6000), STAT3 (1:2000), and p-STAT3 (1:2000) overnight at 4 °C. After washing with TBST for 10 min, the PVDF membrane was incubated with an HRP-conjugated secondary antibody at room temperature for 1 h. Signals were developed with ECL Western blotting Substrate (Cat: Termo Fisher Scientific) and detected by a cooling CCD imaging apparatus (Tanon-X6, Shanghai, China). ImageJ V1.8.0 was used to analyze the intensity of the protein bands.


### Transcriptomic analysis of mouse samples
  To elucidate how GP improved kidney fibrosis, the UUO group and GP group were selected for transcriptome sequencing analysis. Three biological replicates were used for each group. The sequencing company used was Gene Denovo Biotechnology Co. (Guangzhou, China), and the sequencing platform used was the Illumina HiSeqTM 4000 platform. We processed the obtained fragments per kilobase of transcript per million mapped reads (FPKMs) sample data with the statistical software R Studio. Log transformation and data normalization were first performed using the sva package. The limma package was used to evaluate the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between the model group and the GP group, and a |log2(fold change) | >1 and a corrected P value < 0.05 were used to determine the genes with significant differences in expression. Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis of the DEGs was conducted using the “clusterProfiler” and “dose” packages and visualized with the “ggplot2” package in R. The raw sequence readings were submitted to the NCBI database as a BioProject with the accession number PRJNA1056261.


### Histological examination
  The obtained kidneys were first fixed with 4% polyoxymethylene for 24 h and then paraffin-embedded. After transverse sectioning into 5-m sections, hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining was performed on the kidney to evaluate renal injury, and Masson’s trichrome was used to evaluate histological changes and fibrosis. The samples were observed under a light microscope (Nikon, Tokyo, Japan). In summary, the pathologist employed a random selection process to choose 10 distinct and separate places within the cortical portion of each slice. Subsequently, the pathologist assessed the extent of renal tubular injury in each of these areas, taking into account factors such as tubular dilatation and atrophy, intratubular cast formation, inflammatory cell infiltration, and interstitial widening. The scoring of lesions was conducted using a scale ranging from 0 to 4. The numerical scale used to classify the extent of cortical involvement in a certain condition is as follows: 0 represents the absence of participation, 1 indicates mild involvement affecting less than 25% of the cortex, 2 signifies moderate involvement affecting 25–50% of the cortex, 3 denotes severe involvement affecting 50–75% of the cortex, and 4 indicates involvement of more than 75% of the cortex.

  Following the application of Masson staining in accordance with established protocols, five distinct regions within the renal cortex were chosen at random. Subsequently, the areas exhibiting collagen positivity were quantitatively assessed utilizing ImageJ V 1.8.0.

  The tissue slices were deparafinized and rehydrated, after which they were subjected to antigen retrieval through treatment with a 0.01 M citrate buffer solution at a pH of 6.0. Subsequently, the sections were subjected to blocking using goat serum at a concentration of 10% at ambient temperature for 30 min. Subsequently, the sections were subjected to overnight incubation at 4 °C with α-SMA (1:200), collagen Ι (1:200), p-STAT3 (1:200), and HK2 (1:200). The immunoreactivity of the tissue slices was evaluated using an HRP-DAB system (ZSGBBIO, Beijing, China) and subsequently counterstained with hematoxylin. Following the sealing and subsequent dehydration procedures, the tissue slices were removed using a Zeiss microscope. The staining analysis was conducted by two independent observers using a blinded approach. A total of 5 distinct fields of view, each with a magnification of 400x, were randomly chosen for image acquisition in each section. The acquired images were subsequently subjected to quantitative assessment of their staining intensity using ImageJ V 1.8.0.


### Immunofluorescence (IF) microscopy
  The cells were placed onto cover-slips within a 6-well plate and allowed to adhere overnight. After treatment with TGF-β and/or GP for 24 h, the cells were fixed and permeabilized. Then, the cells were subjected to overnight incubation at 4 °C in the presence of primary antibodies. Subsequently, the cells were treated with secondary antibodies conjugated with Alexa 568 for 1 h at room temperature. Following the staining of cellular nuclei with DAPI, the cover slips were affixed onto glass slides using Prolong Gold Antifade reagent. The cells that had been stained were observed using a laser scanning confocal fluorescence microscope.

  Kidney sections were deparaffinized at 70 °C and underwent antigen retrieval at 97 °C for 20 min using a microwave oven. Non-specific antigen blocking was performed with serum at room temperature for 30 min. The sections were then incubated overnight at 4 °C with specific antibodies, including HK2 (Abcam, UK, ab209847), p-STAT3 (Abcam, UK, ab76315). Immunofluorescence secondary antibodies and LTL (fluorescein labeled Lotus Tetragonolobus Lectin, a marker of the proximal tubule, FL-1321, Vector Labs) were used for labeling. Cell nuclei were stained with DAPI. Ten fields were randomly selected and quantified on each slide by two experienced renal pathologists using blind assessment, and data analysis was performed using Image J.


### Lactate production
  Lactate production was determined using a lactate assay kit (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China). Briefly, kidney tissue was perfused with cold PBS to remove red blood cells. The homogenized tissue at 0.10 g/mL was fixed in cold lactate assay buffer. Afterward, the sample was centrifuged at 12,000 × g for five minutes at 4 °C. Subsequently, the supernatant was used for the lactate assay. The level of lactate was normalized to the protein concentration.

  The superannuate from the treated cells was collected and stored at − 80 °C. Lactate production was quantified with a Lactate Assay Kit. The levels were normalized to the number of viable cells.


### Measurement of extracellular acidification rate
  Seahorse Extracellular Flux (XF96) Analyzer (Seahorse Bioscience Inc. North Billerica, MA, USA) was used to measure the extracellular acidification rate. The extracellular acidification rates (ECAR) were measured in real-time using a glycolysis stress test kit, according to manufacturer’s instructions. Sequential compound injections, including glucose (25 mM), oligomycin A (1 µM) and 2-DG (50 mM), were applied on the microplate to test the glycolytic activity.


### Plasmid transfection
  pEX-3 STAT3 and pEX-3 empty vectors were constructed by GenePharma Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). For a single well of a six-well plate, 3.0 µg of plasmid and 6 µL of Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen, USA) were added to 500 µL of DMEM/F-12 reaction buffer, mixed thoroughly, and incubated at room temperature for 20 min to generate a transfection mixture. For transfection, the culture medium was replaced with antibiotic-free DMEM/F12, and the transfection mixture was added. The plate was incubated at 37 °C for 6 h, and the transfection complexes were removed and replaced with fresh media.


### Molecular docking analysis
  We examined whether GP and STAT3 could interact directly via a molecular docking method and precisely determined the binding position and binding strength. The crystal structure of STAT3 was downloaded from the Protein Data Bank (PDB ID: 415I, 5BTR). Molecular docking was performed using AutoTools and AutoDock Vina. Finally, the molecular docking results were analyzed.


### Determination of serum creatinine and urea nitrogen levels
  Serum was used to determine creatinine and urea levels by an automatic biochemical analyzer (Model 7020; Hitachi, Tokyo, Japan).


### Scratch assay
  For the scratch assays, 2 × 105 HK-2 cells were seeded in 6-well plates in complete medium. The next day, the monolayer was disrupted with a sterile 200 µl pipette tip. Then, the medium was changed to medium containing 0.5% FBS after the cells were washed twice with medium. After serum starvation for 12–16 h, 100 µM GP and 10 ng/ml TGF-β1 were added to 0.5% FBS medium for 12 h at 37 °C. Then, the leading edge of the cells was measured and quantified.


### Statistical analyses
  Qualitative data, including immunoblots and cell/tissue images, are representative of at least 3 experiments. The quantitative data are expressed as the means ± SDs. Statistical analysis was conducted using GraphPad Prism 9. Statistical differences between two groups were determined by 2-tailed, unpaired Student’s t tests. Significant differences among multiple groups were determined by one-way or two-way ANOVA with multiple comparisons. P values are indicated in the figures. P < 0.05 was considered to indicate a significant difference.

As shown in Fig. 1C, the differences between the UUO group and the sham group were statistically significant in terms of serum creatinine rather than blood urea nitrogen because kidney function can also maintain normal conditions. The information presented above suggests that GP is effective at reducing renal pathological injury and fibrosis caused by UUO. (Figure 2A and D). Consistently, the changes in the mRNA levels of collagen Ι and α-SMA were consistent with the changes in the protein levels (Fig. 2B and C).

### GP ameliorates renal fibrosis by meditating Glycolysis
  The six samples used for transcriptomic analysis were named UUO 1–3 and GP 1–3. We normalized the fragments per kilobase of exon model per million mapped fragments (FPKM) data obtained for the two groups of samples. Subsequently, the two groups of samples were screened for differentially expressed genes (DEGs). We then performed Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analyses of the DEGs in the two groups of samples (Fig. 3A). Table S2 lists specific information related to the pathways. KEGG analysis revealed that genes are associated with metabolic pathways, pyruvate metabolism, carbon metabolism, glyoxylate and dicarboxylate metabolism, and glycolysis/gluconeogenesis; thus, we focused on metabolic pathways, especially glycolysis. Subsequently, lactate generation, which is the result of glycolysis, was assessed using a lactate assay kit. As depicted in Fig. 3B, there was a significant reduction in lactate generation following the administration of GP to UUO mice compared to that in the untreated group. Subsequently, an examination was conducted to assess the impact of GP on the expression of several glycolytic enzymes. RT‒PCR was performed to detect the relative expression of glycolytic genes, including PFKM, HK2 and PKM2. Expression data of HK2, PFKM, and PKM2 genes in the transcriptome sequencing results of 6 mice were listed in Table 3. GP treatment led to a reduction in HK2 and PFKM mRNA levels in UUO + GP group and an increase in the PKM2 mRNA level in UUO + GP group compared with those in the UUO group (Fig. 3C). Given that HK2 increased at a high level, we selected HK2 for further study. Research has demonstrated that STAT3 is crucial for transactivating HK2 [20, 21]. Thus, we hypothesized that the increase in HK2 expression caused by GP could be due to the suppression of STAT3 signaling. Molecular docking analysis was used to visualize the interactions between GP and STAT3 (Fig. 3D). The binding site was Tyr705, and the binding energy was − 8.38 kJ mol−1, indicating that the docking activity was strong and that GP and STAT3 could bind stably. The phosphorylation of STAT3 at Tyr705 promoted its nuclear translocation and induced STAT3-dependent gene transcription. However, GP binds to STAT3 at Tyr705 and inhibits its activation. IHC and WB analysis revealed that p-STAT3 levels were increased in the UUO model group, and p-STAT3 levels were decreased after GP treatment. Consistent with the mRNA results, WB analysis and IHC analysis suggested that HK2 expression was increased in the UUO model. In response to GP administration, HK2 expression was significantly reduced (Fig. 3E and F).

This suggests that Geniposide alleviated renal fibrosis primarily in proximal tubular epithelial cells (PTECs) following obstruction-induced injury. Subsequently, to verify the effect of GP on renal fibrosis in vitro, we used human renal cortex proximal tubule epithelial cells (HK-2 cells). The MTT assay was used to determine the appropriate concentration of GP for HK-2 cells, and 100 µM GP was used (Fig. S1). As shown in Fig. 4C, cell migration experiments demonstrated that GP can decrease the migration of HK-2 cells triggered by TGF-β1. Moreover, TGF-β1 treatment upregulated the protein and mRNA levels of α-SMA, collagen Ι, vimentin and GP treatment alleviated these increases. In addition, TGF-β1 treatment downregulated the protein and mRNA levels of E-cadherin expression, GP treatment increased E-cadherin expression (Fig. 4D, E and F). Immunofluorescence also show that GP administration reduced the vimentin expression and increased the E-cadherin expression (Fig. S2).The results of the cell experiments were in accordance with our findings in animal experiments that GP alleviates renal tubular cell EMT in kidney fibrosis.

## Discussion
  Kidney fibrosis, which is characterized by the excessive deposition of extracellular matrix resulting in the formation of scar tissue, is a prominent feature observed in several progressive chronic kidney diseases (CKDs). The investigation of therapeutic targets and the advancement of novel pharmaceutical agents are imperative for the treatment of kidney fibrosis. Metabolic reprogramming is a hallmark of renal fibrosis and is characterized by the dysregulation of numerous metabolic pathways, including aerobic glycolysis, which has been observed in kidney fibrosis [5, 22]. In this study, we first reported that GP inhibit glycolysis and protect renal tissue from fibrosis by inhibiting the STAT3-HK2 signaling pathway in vivo and in vitro. In our study, we first investigated the effects of GP on UUO-induced kidney fibrosis.

  The UUO model is a classical model for studying the molecular mechanisms underlying renal fibrosis [23]. GP reduced plasma creatinine and tubular injury in the UUO model. Subsequently, GP attenuated interstitial fibrosis by decreasing collagen Ι accumulation in UUO kidneys. To further understand how GP improve kidney fibrosis, we conducted a transcriptomic analysis of kidneys from the UUO group and UUO + GP group. Interestingly, in the KEGG analysis, the DEGs were enriched in metabolic pathways, of which glycolysis attracted our interest. Then, we detected lactate production and found that GP can regulate glycolysis and inhibit lactate production. In addition, it was verified that GP can suppress the expression of the glycolysis-related protein HK2 by binding to STAT3. Molecular docking showed that GP binds to STAT3 at Tyr705. In previous studies, it has been observed that GP can enhance lipolysis [24], suppress lipogenesis [25], and modulate lipid transport [26]. With respect to glucose metabolism, GP has been found to enhance glucose consumption [27] and stimulate insulin production [28]. Additionally, it has been shown to provide protection to pancreatic islet β cells [29] and to suppress gluconeogenesis [30]. Furthermore, we first demonstrated that GP modulate glycolysis in kidney fibrosis.

  Although the involvement of myofibroblasts in the progression of kidney fibrosis is well established, recent studies have suggested that the renal tubules are the primary location of damage onset, disease progression, and the most effective target for long-term resolution. Tubular epithelial cells (TECs) exhibit a high level of metabolic activity and perform crucial physiological processes, such as reabsorption, maintenance of water-electrolyte homeostasis, and regulation of acid‒base balance. To fulfill their energy requirements, healthy TECs depend on the use of fatty acids. Nevertheless, in the presence of certain stimuli, such as ischemia, obstruction, and medications, there is a malfunction in the process of fatty acid oxidation in TECs [31]. Fatty acid oxidation dysfunction is commonly characterized by intracellular lipid accumulation and a change in cellular metabolism toward aerobic glycolysis [32]. While aerobic glycolysis is responsible for supplying energy and substrates that support the proliferation of TECs, it also contributes to the progression of renal fibrosis. As anticipated, our findings indicate a large upregulation of the gene and protein levels of enzymes associated with glycolysis in murine fibrotic kidneys. Additionally, we observed a notable increase in lactate content, confirming a metabolic shift toward aerobic glycolysis in renal fibrosis.

  TGF-β1 is a highly influential factor that promotes fibrosis, regulates metabolism, and activates glycolysis. It achieves this by directly stimulating the production of key glycolytic enzymes, including hexokinase (HK), pyruvate kinase muscle isoform 2 (PKM2) and phosphofructokinase (PFK) [33–35]. Furthermore, TGF-β1 promotes autophagy, subsequently upregulating aerobic glycolysis and monocarboxylate transporter 4 expression [36]. Consequently, TGF-β1 indirectly facilitates glycolysis. Previous research has demonstrated that TGF-β1 elicits the process of glycolysis in TECs. Consistent with the aforementioned research, our findings indicate that TGF-β1 facilitates the upregulation of HK2 expression and leads to a notable increase in lactate content in HK-2 cells. Significantly, GP mitigated glycolysis in TECs both in vivo and in vitro.

  Metabolic reprogramming is involved in the activation of myofibroblasts, which also play an important role in the progression of renal fibrosis [37]. The antifibrotic effects of GP may not be limited to renal tubular epithelial cells, as fibroblasts and macrophage could also be targets. Future research can further explore the mechanisms by which GP affects fibroblasts and macrophag.

  Several studies have elucidated the significant involvement of STAT3 in energy metabolism through its ability to induce the expression of HK2 in many types of cells [21, 38]. STAT3 is activated via the phosphorylation of tyrosine 705 on STAT3, leading to dimerization initiation, translocation into the nucleus, and subsequent transcriptional activation. The current study provides the first evidence that GP-mediated glycolysis is dependent on STAT3 signaling. In addition, we further investigated the precise mechanisms through which GP mediated STAT3-dependent HK2 transactivation. As expected, the phosphorylation of STAT3 was largely blocked in HK-2 cells in response to GP stimulation, leading to the inhibition of STAT3 nuclear translocation. In addition, the forced expression of STAT3 is sufficient to drive an increase in HK2 expression in HK-2 cells treated with GP stimulation, leading to lactate production.

  There are several limitations in our study. First, we only used a single concentration to determine the effect of GP on renal fibrosis, and we lacked a dose‒response relationship assessment. Drug effects are often dose dependent, with varying responses observed across different concentration ranges. Failing to assess this relationship hinders our understanding of optimal dosing regimens. Second, although we focused on the function of HK2, a key kinase for the first step of glycolysis, there must be other glycolytic enzymes, such as PKMs and PFKs, that synergistically govern the metabolic shift during the EMT of HK-2 cells. Therefore, more comprehensive studies are required to analyze the correlation between HK-2 cell glycolysis and GP in the future.


## Conclusion
  In summary, this study demonstrated the alteration of glycolysis in kidney fibrosis. Geniposide suppressed HK2-mediated glycolysis by inhibiting STAT3 signaling, leading to decreased lactate production and thereby attenuating TEC EMT. These findings suggest that HK2 and STAT3 are novel potential molecular targets for the prevention and treatment of kidney fibrosis.
