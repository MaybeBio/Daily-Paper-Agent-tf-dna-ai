# The role of TEAD4 gene in the Hippo signaling pathway in triple-negative breast cancer and targeted therapy strategies


## Background
  Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) is an aggressive subtype with poor prognosis and limited targeted therapies. The Hippo signaling pathway, critical in tumor progression, may harbor key genes influencing TNBC behavior. However, the specific genes and their clinical significance remain unclear.


## Methods
  We analyzed the GSE45827 dataset from the GEO database using differential gene expression analysis (DEG) and weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) to identify TNBC-related genes. We enriched Hippo pathway-related genes from the MsigDB database and literature to identify candidate genes (HRGs) that may affect TNBC progression. The Boruta algorithm further screened for core genes, which were validated by immunohistochemistry in TNBC and other breast cancer tissues. Finally, we explored the biological and pharmacological significance of the target through drug prediction, molecular docking, molecular dynamics simulations, and in vitro experiments.


## Results
  DEG analysis identified 2,888 differentially expressed genes, and WGCNA yielded 276 TNBC-associated genes. Intersection analysis with 70 hypoxia-related genes (HRGs) revealed four key genes: TEAD4, WWTR1, AREG, and SOX11. TEAD4 was confirmed as the central gene influencing TNBC progression. Immunohistochemical results showed strong TEAD4 expression in TNBC tissues, with negligible expression in adjacent normal tissues or other breast cancer subtypes. Drug prediction and molecular docking identified irinotecan as a potential TEAD4-targeting agent. Molecular dynamics simulations confirmed the stable binding and favorable dynamics of the irinotecan-TEAD4 complex. Both TEAD4 knockdown and irinotecan treatment significantly suppressed TNBC cell migration and invasion. The combination of TEAD4 knockout and irinotecan produced a more pronounced inhibitory effect, underscoring the therapeutic potential of targeting TEAD4 in TNBC.


## Conclusions
  Through comprehensive analysis, we identified TEAD4 as a key gene in TNBC with high expression specificity. Irinotecan may be a potential targeted drug for TEAD4, offering a new therapeutic strategy for TNBC and potentially improving patient outcomes. Further experimental verification is required.

## Introduction
  Breast cancer is one of the most common malignant tumors among women globally, with persistently high incidence and mortality rates [1]. Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), a unique subtype of breast cancer, has garnered significant attention due to its distinct biological characteristics and clinical manifestations [2, 3]. TNBC is defined by the absence of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) expression in tumor cells. This feature poses substantial challenges for treatment, as TNBC lacks clear molecular targets and cannot benefit from endocrine therapy or anti-HER2 targeted treatments [4]. Consequently, TNBC primarily relies on traditional therapeutic approaches such as surgery, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy. However, TNBC is highly aggressive, prone to early recurrence and distant metastasis, and is associated with a poor prognosis and significantly lower 5-year survival rates compared to other breast cancer subtypes [5, 6]. Therefore, elucidating the molecular mechanisms of TNBC and identifying new therapeutic targets are of paramount clinical significance for improving patient outcomes.

  The Hippo signaling pathway is an evolutionarily conserved regulator of organ size and tissue homeostasis, whose dysregulation has been firmly implicated in a wide range of cancers [7, 8]. A notable study by Wu et al. identified the Hippo/TEAD4 axis as a potential therapeutic target in breast cancer, underscoring its broader oncogenic relevance [9]. However, despite this significant discovery, the clinical and mechanistic role of TEAD4 specifically in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) remains insufficiently elucidated. Key unanswered questions include whether TEAD4 serves as the central effector of Hippo-mediated oncogenesis in TNBC, what molecular mechanisms govern its activity, and how it may be therapeutically targeted in this aggressive subtype. To address these gaps, our study employs an unbiased bioinformatics approach to validate TEAD4 as a critical prognostic driver in TNBC and investigates its pharmacological targeting potential through computational drug repositioning strategies.

  With the advent of high-throughput sequencing technologies and advanced bioinformatics methods, multi-omics analysis has become a powerful tool for elucidating the molecular mechanisms of complex diseases [10]. By analyzing gene expression data from public databases, we can employ differential gene expression analysis (DEG), weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA), and pathway enrichment analysis to systematically identify key genes associated with diseases. DEG analysis can pinpoint genes with significant expression differences between TNBC and normal tissues, providing initial clues for further investigation. WGCNA can further uncover correlations among genes and identify gene modules closely related to TNBC progression [11, 12]. Enriching for Hippo signaling pathway-related genes allows us to focus on those that may significantly impact TNBC biology. Machine learning algorithms, such as the Boruta algorithm, can then be used to screen for core genes, which can be validated through experimental methods like immunohistochemistry, potentially offering new targets for the diagnosis and treatment of TNBC.

  This study aims to systematically identify and characterize key genes in the Hippo signaling pathway that influence TNBC progression through multi-omics analysis and to explore their potential value as therapeutic targets. By analyzing the GSE45827 dataset from the GEO database and integrating information from the MsigDB database and literature reviews, we identified Hippo pathway-related genes associated with TNBC (HRGs) and further pinpointed core genes using the Boruta algorithm. We validated their expression in TNBC tissues and other breast cancer subtypes through immunohistochemistry and explored the mechanisms of potential targeted drugs via drug prediction, molecular docking, and molecular dynamics simulations. The detailed workflow of this study is shown in Fig. 1. This study not only enhances our understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying TNBC but also provides new strategies and directions for targeted therapies, potentially improving the prognosis of TNBC patients.

## Methods

### Data source and preprocessing
  We retrieved and downloaded the GSE45827 dataset from the official website of the GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). This dataset includes a breast cancer cohort with various subtypes, comprising 41 TNBC samples, 30 HER2-positive breast cancer samples, 29 Luminal A breast cancer samples, 30 Luminal B breast cancer samples, 11 normal tissue samples, and 14 cell lines. During the data preprocessing phase, we conducted comprehensive quality control of the raw data using the R package Seurat (v4.3.0), which included removing low-quality samples, correcting batch effects with the Harmony package (v0.1.1), and normalizing the gene expression data via the sctransform method. These steps established a solid foundation for the accuracy and reliability of subsequent analyses. To identify genes with significant expression differences in TNBC, we performed differential gene expression analysis using the DESeq2 package (v1.38.3) in R [13]. By comparing gene expression levels between TNBC tissues and adjacent normal tissues, we calculated the expression differences for each gene and screened for differentially expressed genes (DEGs) based on predefined thresholds (P value < 0.05, |log2Fold Change| >2). These DEGs provided a crucial basis for further gene selection.


### Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA)
  To further uncover gene modules closely related to TNBC progression, we employed Weighted Gene Co-expression Network Analysis (WGCNA) using the WGCNA R package (v1.72-1). WGCNA constructs scale-free gene co-expression networks to identify highly correlated gene modules and evaluate their associations with clinical traits. The analysis was performed on the selected DEGs according to the following pipeline: first, a similarity matrix was generated by calculating pairwise Pearson correlations between genes. Subsequently, a signed adjacency matrix was constructed by raising the similarity matrix to a soft-power threshold (β = 18), which was selected based on the scale-free topology criterion (scale-free R² >0.85). The adjacency matrix was then transformed into a topological overlap matrix (TOM) to minimize spurious connections, and a dissimilarity measure (1 - TOM) was used for hierarchical clustering. Gene modules were identified using dynamic tree cutting with a minimum module size of 30 genes and a merge cut height of 0.25. Module eigengenes (MEs) were calculated as the first principal component of each module and correlated with TNBC progression traits. Modules with the highest absolute correlation values and significant p-values (< 0.05) were considered highly associated with TNBC progression. Genes within these key modules were subsequently regarded as potential drivers of TNBC progression. Parameter selection was guided by established practices from the original WGCNA methodology [14].


### Hippo signaling pathway gene enrichment
  To investigate the role of the Hippo signaling pathway in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), we collected genes related to the Hippo signaling pathway (HRGs) from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB), a repository of well-annotated gene sets widely used for pathway enrichment analysis. Specifically, we retrieved and downloaded four well-established gene sets related to the Hippo signaling pathway, including GO_HIPPO_SIGNALING, KEGG_HIPPO_SIGNALING_PATHWAY, REACTOME_SIGNALING_BY_HIPPO, and WP_HIPPOYAP_SIGNALING_PATHWAY [15]. These gene sets are derived from extensive and authoritative sources, such as pathway databases, biomedical literature, and contributions from experts across various fields (Table 1). After downloading the gene sets, we integrated them with our existing gene expression profile data and performed rigorous quality control measures, including the removal of duplicate probes and imputation of missing data, to ensure the reliability and accuracy of the data. Subsequently, we intersected the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and the gene modules identified through Weighted Gene Co-expression Network Analysis (WGCNA) with the HRGs to focus on Hippo pathway genes that may significantly impact TNBC progression. The intersected genes were regarded as key candidate genes for TNBC progression.

### Core gene identification using the Boruta algorithm
  To pinpoint the core genes with the greatest impact on TNBC progression, we utilized the Boruta algorithm, a robust wrapper feature selection method built around the Random Forest classifier. The analysis was performed using the Boruta R package (version 4.3.2). The key parameters were set as follows: maxRuns = 500 to ensure the algorithm had sufficient iterations to converge, and the p value threshold for the statistical test of importance was set to the default value of 0.01. The Random Forest model itself was built using ntree = 500 trees. All genes classified as ‘Confirmed’ by the algorithm after these runs were retained as core drivers of TNBC progression. Boruta is a feature selection algorithm based on random forests that effectively identifies genes significantly influencing disease progression [16]. We applied the Boruta algorithm to the enriched HRGs to screen for core genes that most influence TNBC progression. In addition, to enhance the credibility of the research findings, this study incorporated the GSE53752, GSE65194, and GSE38959 datasets to further validate the differential expression of core gene in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) tissues compared with normal tissues.


### Immunohistochemical validation
  To verify the protein-level expression of TEAD4 in TNBC tissues, immunohistochemical (IHC) analysis was conducted using a validated rabbit monoclonal anti-TEAD4 antibody (Clone D3H8; Cell Signaling Technology, Cat# 13295, RRID: AB_2650486) at an optimized dilution of 1:200. Antigen retrieval was performed using citrate buffer (pH 6.0), and specific binding was detected using a horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibody followed by 3,3′-diaminobenzidine (DAB) chromogenic development. All stained sections were evaluated independently by two pathologists blinded to the sample groups. This study aimed to assess the expression differences of the core genes in TNBC, adjacent non-tumor tissues, HER2-positive breast cancer, Luminal A, and Luminal B breast cancers. To ensure the reliability and comparability of the study results, we strictly controlled the baseline characteristics of the patients in the study design to minimize the impact of confounding factors. All samples were obtained from breast cancer patients who underwent surgical treatment at our hospital.

  According to pathological diagnosis, patients were divided into the following five groups: TNBC, adjacent non-tumor tissue, HER2-positive breast cancer, Luminal A breast cancer, and Luminal B breast cancer. A minimum of five samples were meticulously selected for each group, thereby ensuring adequate statistical power for robust analysis.

  To ensure comparability between groups, we strictly matched the following baseline characteristics: age (the age distribution of patients in each group was similar, with a mean age ranging from 32 to 55 years), sex (all patients were female), ethnicity (all patients were of Han ethnicity), tumor stage (all breast cancer patients were stage II), pathological grade (all breast cancer patients were moderately differentiated), treatment status (all patients had surgical resection specimens and did not receive any preoperative treatment), hormone receptor status (TNBC and HER2-positive breast cancer were ER(-) PR(-), while Luminal A and Luminal B were ER(+) PR(+)), HER2 status (TNBC and Luminal A were HER2(-), while HER2-positive breast cancer and Luminal B were HER2(+)), comorbidities (all patients had no other significant comorbidities), sample collection time (sample collection work was carried out successively from August 2020 to August 2021), fixation time and preservation conditions (all samples were fixed within 24 h after surgery, using 10% neutral formalin fixative, and preserved at 4 °C).

  Data collection included the baseline characteristics of all patients, such as age, sex, ethnicity, tumor stage, pathological grade, treatment status, hormone receptor status, HER2 status, comorbidities, sample collection time, fixation time, and preservation conditions. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 26.0 software. For continuous variables (e.g., age), one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) or Kruskal-Wallis test was used, while for categorical variables (e.g., sex, ethnicity, tumor stage, pathological grade), chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test was applied. In all tests, a P value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Ethical guidelines were strictly followed throughout the study. All patients were provided with comprehensive information about the study and voluntarily signed the informed consent form. The study adhered to the ethical guidelines of the World Medical Association and was approved by the Ethics Committee of Gansu Provincial Academy of Medical Sciences (approval number: [A202005140025]).


### Drug prediction and molecular docking
  To explore potential targeted drugs for the core genes, we employed an integrated computational approach combining drug prediction with molecular docking. Initially, we utilized the Drug Signature Database (DSigDB; http://dsigdb.tanlab.org/DSigDBv1.0/), a comprehensive resource containing 22,527 gene sets and 17,389 compounds associated with 19,531 genes, to predict drugs targeting the core genes [17]. Subsequently, molecular docking was performed to simulate binding interactions between the predicted drugs and core gene proteins. The two-dimensional structures of these drugs were retrieved from PubChem (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov), while protein crystal structures were obtained from the Protein Data Bank (PDB; https://www.rcsb.org/). Docking simulations were carried out using CB-Dock2 (https://cadd.labshare.cn/cb-dock2/php/index.php), a blind docking tool that incorporates cavity detection, structure-based docking, and template-based docking to evaluate binding affinity and stability for computer-aided drug discovery [18].


### Molecular dynamics simulation
  To further verify the binding stability and reliability between the candidate drug and the core gene protein, we conducted molecular dynamics (MD) simulations using Gromacs2022 software [19]. The simulation system was constructed by integrating the protein and small molecule ligand structures, employing the GAFF force field for the ligand and the AMBER14SB force field with the TIP3P water model for the protein. All bond lengths involving hydrogen atoms were constrained with the LINCS algorithm, using a 2 fs time step, under periodic boundary conditions at constant temperature (298 K, maintained with the V-rescale method) and pressure (1 bar, controlled with the Berendsen method). Electrostatic interactions were treated with the Particle Mesh Ewald (PME) method using a 1.2 nm cutoff, and non-bonded interactions were updated every 10 steps with a 10 Å cutoff. The system underwent equilibration with 100 ps of NVT and 100 ps of NPT simulations, followed by a production run of 100 ns, saving conformations every 10 ps. Trajectories were visualized and analyzed in VMD and PyMOL, and the binding free energy was computed using the g_mmpbsa tool with the MMPBSA method, confirming robust binding affinity and supporting the potential reliability of the drug candidate.


### Cell transfection and functional assays (CCK-8, scratch assay, transwell assay)

#### Cell culture and transfection
  The human TNBC cell line MDA-MB-231 was obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC). Cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM, HyClone, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Gibco, USA) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (Beyotime, China) at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO₂.

  For TEAD4 knockdown, cells were transfected with a specific small interfering RNA (siRNA) targeting TEAD4 (si-TEAD4) or a non-targeting control siRNA (si-NC), which were synthesized by GenePharma (China). The siRNA sequence was as follows:si-TEAD4: 5’-GGACUUGGUGGAGAUCAUUTT-3’si-NC: 5’-UUCUCCGAACGUGUCACGUTT-3’

  Transfection was performed using Lipofectamine 3000 reagent (Invitrogen, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Subsequent experiments were conducted 48 h after transfection to ensure efficient gene knockdown.


#### Experimental grouping and drug treatment
  The transfected cells were divided into the following four experimental groups:

  NC Group: Cells transfected with negative control siRNA (si-NC) and treated with 0.1% DMSO (Sigma-Aldrich, USA) as a vehicle control.Irinotecan Group: Cells transfected with si-NC and treated with 10 µM Irinotecan hydrochloride (MedChemExpress, USA) for the indicated durations.si-TEAD4 Group: Cells transfected with si-TEAD4 and treated with the vehicle control (0.1% DMSO).si-TEAD4 + Irinotecan Group: Cells transfected with si-TEAD4 and co-treated with 10 µM Irinotecan.


#### Cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8) proliferation assay
  Cell proliferation was assessed using a CCK-8 assay kit (Dojindo, Japan). Briefly, approximately 3 × 10³ cells per well were seeded into 96-well plates. After the respective treatments, 10 µL of CCK-8 reagent was added to each well at the designated time points (0, 24, 48, and 72 h). The plates were then incubated for another 2 h at 37 °C. The optical density (OD) at 450 nm was measured using a Synergy H1 microplate reader (BioTek, USA). The experiment was repeated three times independently.


#### Scratch wound healing assay
  Cell migration ability was evaluated using the scratch wound healing assay. Cells were seeded into 6-well plates and grown to 90–100% confluence. A straight scratch was created in the cell monolayer using a sterile 200 µL pipette tip. The detached cells were washed away with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), and fresh medium containing the respective treatments (DMSO or Irinotecan) was added. Images of the same scratch areas were captured at 0 h and 24 h under an inverted CKX53 microscope (Olympus, Japan). The migration rate was calculated by measuring the change in the scratch area using ImageJ software (NIH, USA).


#### Transwell invasion assay
  Cell invasion was assessed using Corning Transwell chambers (24-well insert, 8 μm pore size; Corning, USA) pre-coated with Matrigel (BD Biosciences, USA). After the respective treatments, 5 × 10⁴ cells in serum-free medium were seeded into the upper chamber. Medium containing 20% FBS was added to the lower chamber as a chemoattractant. After incubation for 36 h at 37 °C, the non-invading cells on the upper membrane surface were carefully removed with a cotton swab. The invaded cells on the lower surface were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, stained with 0.1% crystal violet (Beyotime, China), and photographed under a microscope. The number of invaded cells was counted in five random fields per well.


### Statistical analysis
  In this study, data processing and analysis were conducted using Perl (version 5.30) and R (version 4.1.2) to ensure efficient and accurate data manipulation. Data organization and visualization were performed using GraphPad Prism (version 9.0) and ImageJ (version 1.53k) to clearly and intuitively present the research findings. A significance level of P < 0.05 was used for all statistical analyses.

### Identification of Hippo signaling pathway-related genes
  To explore the role of the Hippo signaling pathway in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), we constructed a set of Hippo-related genes (HRGs). We identified 70 HRGs with distinct characteristics and further narrowed down to four candidate HRGs that may significantly impact TNBC progression: TEAD4, WWTR1, AREG, and SOX11 (Fig. 3E). This discovery provides a solid foundation for further investigation into the role of the Hippo signaling pathway in TNBC.


### Identification of core genes using the Boruta algorithm
  To further elucidate the roles of the candidate genes in disease progression, we assessed their importance using the Boruta algorithm. The variance inflation factor (VIF) analysis showed that the VIF values of all genes were within the acceptable range of 1 to 5 (Fig. 4A), indicating no significant multicollinearity among these genes and ensuring the reliability of the subsequent analysis. The Boruta algorithm analysis revealed that all four candidate genes were deemed important. In terms of importance ranking, TEAD4 was the most significant, followed by AREG, SOX11, and WWTR1 (Figs. 4B-C). We further confirmed the reliability of our analysis results using the GSE53752, GSE65194, and GSE38959 datasets. TEAD4 and AREG were found to be significantly upregulated in all three independent datasets (P < 0.01). WWTR1 also exhibited a significant upward trend in multiple cohorts. Only the expression of SOX11 was inconsistent, suggesting that its role may be more context-specific (Supplementary Fig. 1). This finding provides robust evidence for the pivotal roles of these genes in disease progression and lays a solid foundation for subsequent mechanistic studies and clinical applications.

### Molecular dynamics simulation
  To verify the stability of Irinotecan binding to TEAD4, we performed molecular dynamics (MD) simulations and analyzed several key parameters. The Root Mean Square Deviation (RMSD) of the complex and the protein stabilized over the simulation period (Fig. 7A), indicating a stable system. The Radius of Gyration (Rg) of the complex also stabilized (Fig. 7B), further confirming the stability of the complex structure. The Root Mean Square Fluctuation (RMSF) values provided insights into the dynamic behavior of the protein backbone (Fig. 7C). Distance analysis revealed that the small molecule stably bound to the initial binding site on the protein, with minor fluctuations observed around 70 ns that remained within a stable range (Fig. 7D). The Buried Surface Area (Buried SASA) remained stable (Fig. 7E), indicating a constant contact area and stable binding state. The binding energy components showed that van der Waals forces (VDW) were more stable than electrostatic interactions (ELE) (Fig. 7F), with VDW playing a dominant role in binding. The number of hydrogen bonds fluctuated between 1 and 3 (Fig. 7G), indicating dynamic but stable interactions. The free energy landscape revealed two low-energy states (Fig. 7H), corresponding to stable conformations before 50 ns and after 70 ns, with van der Waals forces providing the main stabilization. These results collectively demonstrate the stable binding of Irinotecan to TEAD4, highlighting the pivotal role of van der Waals forces in maintaining this stability.

### Cell transfection and functional assays (CCK-8, scratch assay, transwell assay)

#### Knockdown of TEAD4 and irinotecan treatment suppress TNBC cell migration
  To investigate the role of TEAD4 in TNBC cell migration, a scratch wound healing assay was performed in MDA-MB-231 cells under four conditions: negative control (NC), irinotecan alone, TEAD4 knockdown (siTEAD4), and the combination of irinotecan and siTEAD4. As illustrated in Fig. 8A, both irinotecan treatment and TEAD4 knockdown significantly inhibited cell migration compared to the NC group at 24 h. Quantitative analysis revealed that the wound healing percentage was markedly reduced in the irinotecan group and siTEAD4 group. Most notably, the combination of irinotecan and siTEAD4 resulted in the most substantial suppression of migration (Fig. 8A), indicating a synergistic inhibitory effect.

#### Combined inhibition of TEAD4 and irinotecan significantly reduces cell viability
  The effect of TEAD4 knockdown and irinotecan on cell proliferation was evaluated using the CCK-8 assay. As shown in Fig. 8C, irinotecan treatment alone led to a moderate reduction in cell viability at 24 and 48 h. TEAD4 knockdown also suppressed proliferation, though to a lesser extent compared to irinotecan. However, the combination of irinotecan and siTEAD4 resulted in the most pronounced decrease in optical density (OD) values, reflecting significantly inhibited cell growth over time. These results suggest that co-targeting TEAD4 and irinotecan has a stronger anti-proliferative effect than either treatment alone.


#### TEAD4 knockdown and irinotecan cooperate to inhibit cell invasion
  Cell invasion was assessed using a Transwell assay with Matrigel. Consistent with the migration and viability results, both irinotecan monotherapy and TEAD4 knockdown reduced the number of invading cells compared to the NC group (Fig. 8B). The combined treatment group (siTEAD4 + irinotecan) exhibited the greatest reduction in invasive capacity, further supporting the notion that dual targeting of TEAD4 and irinotecan may provide a potent therapeutic strategy for suppressing TNBC aggressiveness.


## Discussion
  This study identified TEAD4 as a critically upregulated gene in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) and validated its close association with disease progression through integrated multi-omics analyses. By combining differential gene expression analysis, co-expression network construction, and cross-referencing with Hippo pathway-related gene sets, TEAD4 was established as a core regulatory gene in TNBC. Immunohistochemical results demonstrated specific high expression of TEAD4 in TNBC tissues, with notably lower expression in adjacent normal tissues and other breast cancer subtypes. Molecular docking and dynamics simulations suggested irinotecan as a potential TEAD4-targeting agent, a finding further supported by in vitro experiments: both TEAD4 knockdown and irinotecan treatment significantly inhibited the migration and invasion capabilities of TNBC cells, with combined intervention yielding enhanced inhibitory effects.

  From a mechanistic perspective, TEAD4 likely functions as a transcriptional effector within the Hippo signaling pathway, promoting the expression of genes related to epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), cell proliferation, and metastasis, thereby driving TNBC malignancy [20–22]. Irinotecan may inhibit tumor phenotypes by interfering with the interaction between TEAD4 and its DNA-binding domain, thereby disrupting its downstream transcriptional activation function [23, 24]. The synergistic effect observed with combined treatment further suggests that irinotecan may possess TEAD4-specific inhibitory activity, although its precise binding mode and mechanism of action require further experimental validation.

  These findings are consistent with multiple recent reports on the role of TEAD4 in cancer. For example, Chen et al. indicated that TEAD4 promotes TNBC tumor growth and metastasis by interacting with YAP to activate EGFR signaling [25, 26]. Wang et al. also found in a study of TNBC patients that high TEAD4 expression was significantly associated with poor prognosis and chemotherapy resistance [27]. Furthermore, the central role of TEAD4 in the Hippo pathway and its regulation of EMT were supported by studies such as those by Wang and Liu, who revealed that the TEAD4–YAP complex acts as a master regulator of EMT in breast cancer stem cells [28, 29]. Notably, consistent with our findings, Wang et al. recently reported that components of the Hippo pathway, including TEAD4, are more specifically highly expressed in TNBC compared to Luminal or HER2-positive subtypes [30]. While previous studies have largely focused on genetic or transcriptional mechanisms, our study advances the translational perspective by proposing irinotecan as a potential targeted therapeutic agent against TEAD4, offering new avenues for clinical intervention.

  This study carries significant biological and clinical implications. Theoretically, TEAD4 as a key molecule in TNBC pathogenesis provides new insights into the role of the Hippo pathway in tumor invasion and metastasis. Clinically, irinotecan—an FDA-approved chemotherapeutic agent for other indications—offers a repurposing strategy for treating TEAD4-high TNBC patients, demonstrating considerable feasibility and translational potential. Moreover, TEAD4 expression levels may serve as a biomarker for predicting irinotecan sensitivity, facilitating stratified and precision medicine for TNBC.

  However, this study has several limitations. First, the gene screening was based on public microarray datasets, which may not fully capture the high heterogeneity of TNBC. Second, although in vitro experiments preliminarily validated TEAD4 function and the effects of its targeting, further verification in models that more closely resemble human disease states—such as patient-derived xenograft (PDX) models or organoids—is necessary. Additionally, the specific molecular mechanism underlying the interaction between irinotecan and TEAD4—whether it involves direct binding, allosteric modulation, or indirect pathway interference—requires further investigation through methods such as chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) or reporter gene assays. Finally, the findings of this study remain at the preclinical stage, and subsequent clinical trials are urgently needed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of irinotecan in TEAD4-high TNBC patients.


## Conclusions
  In this study, we employed an integrated bioinformatics and computational screening approach to identify TEAD4 as a key upregulated gene that is strongly associated with poor prognosis in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC). Through molecular docking and drug repositioning, irinotecan was selected as a high-affinity agent targeting TEAD4, suggesting its potential for repurposing in TEAD4-high TNBC subtypes. These findings, supported by completed in vitro experiments, propose TEAD4 inhibition as a promising therapeutic strategy and warrant further preclinical and clinical investigation.
