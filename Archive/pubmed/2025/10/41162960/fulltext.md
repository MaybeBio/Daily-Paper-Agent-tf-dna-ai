# Comprehensive computational analysis of PKCδ non-synonymous variants identifies rs1703863535 as a potential breast cancer biomarker


## Supplementary Information
  The online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-025-15194-6.


## Background
  Genetic variants are differences in the genome observed among individuals within a population. They significantly influence disease susceptibility, treatment responses, and therapeutic outcomes [1]. These variants can alter genes and chromosomes, leading to changed phenotypes and contributing to disease conditions, including cancer [2]. The human genome is estimated to contain 3 to 5 million variants at various frequencies, with approximately 1. 1.5 million being single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs), occurring roughly once every 300 base pairs with a frequency of >1%. These SNPs can be further classified as intronic, untranslated region, and exonic SNPs, with densities of 8. 21, 7. 51, and 7. 51, respectively [3]. Half of all SNPs in coding regions are silent, while the remaining half are non- synonymous, which alter amino acid residues and directly affect protein structure, function, and interactions [4]. SNPs in non-coding regions (UTRs and promoter sequences) influence gene expression. Consequently, SNPs are regarded as potential biomarkers for disease pre- diagnosis and prognosis, making it essential to identify risk- associated SNPs [5]. Advances in bioinformatics and computational biology have greatly improved the screening and identification of SNPs involved in various diseases. Several in silico tools exist to interpret the effects of variants by predicting whether a specific SNP is damaging to the protein, based on sequence conservation, the electrochemical properties of wild- type and variant residues, and protein structural features [6, 7]. SNP data can be obtained from multiple databases containing curated SNP information, and through structural and functional analysis tools, the impact of SNPs on proteins and their potential damaging effects can be assessed, which can subsequently be validated experimentally [8, 9]. A comprehensive computational assessment of human ATM kinase non- synonymous SNPs was conducted to thoroughly evaluate their effects on protein stability, function, and disease association [10].

  The PKC family proteins have been extensively studied for their role in tumor development and progression. The PKC family includes three main sub-families, Conventional PKCs (cPKC), Novel PKC (nPKC), and Atypical PKCs (aPKC), based on their different activation requirement [1]. The PRKCD gene is located on chromosome 3p21.1 and encodes PKCδ protein, a member of the novel subclass of the PKC. Human PKCδ is a 676 amino acid serine-threonine kinase, expressed ubiquitously in several tissues and organs and is involved in various cellular processes, including proliferation, survival, migration, and apoptosis. PKCδ is a multifunctional protein implicated in various ailments, such as autoimmune, metabolic, cardiovascular diseases, and cancers [2, 3]. Studies have shown that altered expression of PKCδ enhance carcinogenic processes in liver, breast, colorectal, lung, and pancreatic cancers [4, 5]. PKCδ is comprised of four major domains that coordinate in sophisticated manner to confer stability to protein structure. The two regulatory domains include C2, C1a/C1b and the pseudo-substrate region are primarily involved in protein activation. The protein kinase domain contains C3, C4, and AGC kinase C-terminal tail regions which are highly conserved and involved in the catalytic activity of the protein. Hence, any mutations occurring in these regions may alter the activity of PKCδ which can increase the likelihood and susceptibility of breast cancer development and progression. Hence, it is crucial to thoroughly investigate non-synonymous SNPs in this gene and their impact on protein structure, stability, intermolecular interactions, and tumor association through extensive bioinformatics analyses. Therefore, this in silico exploratory study explicitly aimed to identify the non-synonymous SNPs in PKCδ that might have impact on protein structure and dynamics, that can cause alterations in its biological functions, by analyzing the variants impact on protein activity, stability, and evolutionary conservation, and protein-protein interactions with STAT3 through docking interaction analysis, through multiple computational tools. The tumor-associated non-synonymous SNPs were further validated through molecular dynamics simulation analysis. The information attained from these sophisticated analyses will help in the rapid and efficient screening of oncogenic non-synonymous SNPs in PRKCD. Moreover, the study further aimed at determining the association of PRKCD non-synonymous variant rs1703863535 with breast cancer which can serve a potential genetic marker for breast cancer.

### Pathogenicity prediction of non-synonymous SNPs
  The consequence of a single nucleotide variation can be analyzed by two basic techniques: sequence-based SNP analysis and structure-based SNP analysis. The sequence-based techniques are established on structural homology and evolutionary conservation, while the structure-based techniques consider the effect of amino acid variation on protein phenotype. Therefore, the methods that cover both approaches include a wide range of features of the SNPs. A combination of two techniques was used in this study to find the most deleterious non-synonymous PRKCD variants. For this purpose, various bioinformatics algorithms were used, which are described below.

  PolyPhen predicts variant damaging effects based on the structural and evolutionary characteristics of the amino acids [9]. This tool provides scores ranging from 0 to 1, with scores ranging from 0 to 1. REVEL predicts the pathogenicity of a non-synonymous variant by combining the scores for a particular variant from various individual bioinformatics tools [10]. REVEL scores for individual variants range from 0 to 1, with higher values depicting an increased chance that the variant is deleterious. CADD predicts the deleteriousness of the variant by integrating the functional and sequence conservation information into a single metric [11]. CADD scores for variants range from 1 to 99. Scores below 30 are regarded as likely benign, and scores above 30 as likely deleterious. SIFT predicts the damaging potential of the variant through the physical properties of the amino acid and the sequence homology [12]. This tool also scores the variant from 0 to 1, with scores < 0.5 called deleterious and >0.5 are tolerated. Mutation Assessor mines alignment information from a large number of protein families and searches the 3D structures of the homologous sequences [13]. This tool predicts the functional implications of the amino acid variations in the protein by evaluating the evolutionary conservation of the modified amino acid in protein homologs. Mutation Assessor scores the variants in the range of 0–1 and categorizes them into high (>0.9), medium (0.5–0.9), low (0.2–0.4), and neutral (0.0–0.1.0.1) groups. MetaLR utilizes logistic regression to merge the allelic frequency and deleteriousness scores of independent variants to predict the pathogenicity of the non-synonymous variants [14]. It scores the variants from 0 to 1 and groups them as tolerated or damaging. The biological function of the variants was predicted using PROVEAN [15], which analyzes the impact of amino acid alteration on protein and assigns scores to the variant. PROVEAN has a threshold score of −2.5, and a variant with scores lower than the threshold value is regarded as deleterious, while if the predicted score is higher than the threshold, it is considered neutral.

  The selected deleterious SNPs were subjected to further analyses using various bioinformatics applications. PANTHER was used to evaluate the effect of SNPs on protein functions based on their evolutionary preservation timescale [16]. This tool analyzes the preservation time frame of the particular amino acids in the specific protein. Meta-SNP is a random forest-based binary classifier algorithm that can identify pathogenic non-synonymous SNPs [17]. Meta-SNP incorporates scores from various online tools that help it detect deleterious SNPs more effectively. SNAP analyzes the protein sequence and the properties of the variant amino acid to differentiate between the variants with no consequence and variants with consequence [18]. It generates scores between 0 and 100%, with higher scores indicating the higher likelihood that a particular SNP will disrupt the functions of the wildtype protein. PHD-SNP was also used to predict the effect of SNPs on protein function [19]. It is a support vector machine (SVM) based classifier that can differentiate the damaging and neutral SNPs based on the scores. If the SNP has a probability score >0.5, then it is considered damaging otherwise neutral. PredictSNP is an online variant classifier that combines the six most effective tools to predict the consequences of SNPs on the functions of proteins [20]. This software color codes the predicted effects of SNPs: deleterious SNPs are coded with red, while neutral SNPs are green. SuSPect was used to analyze the deleterious and neutral SNPs in the protein [21]. This tool utilizes the disease-propensity method that compares the observed numbers with the random expected numbers using the binomial test. It makes its predictions using the amino acid sequence and the protein central chain as the input. SuSPect also uses the structural, sequence, and system biological properties to evaluate the effect of SNPs. It scores the variants in the range of 0 (neutral) to 100 (disease-associated), with a threshold value of 50.


### Oncogenic potential of non-synonymous SNPs in PRKCD
  Cancer association of the pathogenic non-synonymous SNPs was also analyzed. For this purpose, FATHMM, a web-based tool, was used to predict the cancer-causing potential of the variants [22]. The FATHMM prediction is capable of differentiating between the passenger and cancer-driving polymorphisms. The variants were also analyzed through CScape, a web-based tool that predicts the oncogenic potential of the variants [23]. This tool can categorize the non-synonymous SNPs as either cancer-driving or neutral based on the P-value scores ranging from 0 to 1. Scores below 0.5 indicate that a particular SNP is benign, and scores above 0.5 indicate that SNP is oncogenic.


### Align-GVGD analysis
  Align-GVGD is an online program that analyzes the biophysical features of the protein sequence alignment and amino acid residues to determine into which spectrum a non-synonymous SNP is falling, ranging from class 0 to class 65, where class 0 indicates that a particular SNP is less likely to affect the biophysical features of the protein [24]. This tool receives a list of SNPs and multiple sequence alignment as input and processes this information to classify SNPs as neutral or deleterious.


### Modelling and validation of protein 3D structures
  The determination of 3D structures promotes various structural and functional analyses of proteins. PKCδ 3D structure was predicted through AlphaFold [25] (https://alphafold.ebi.ac.uk), the most reliable protein 3D structure prediction tool. It is an artificial intelligence program that uses neural network-based models to predict the 3D conformation of protein. Models predicted by AlphaFold also include per-residue model confidence score (pLDDT) ranging from 0 to 100, with scores above 70 showing high confidence of amino acid locations in the 3D structure. Furthermore, the 3D structures of PKCδ variants were attained by in silico mutagenesis using the mutagenesis plugin entrenched in PyMol v4.0.4 [26]. The refinement in the 3D structures of both native and variant proteins was performed using ModRefiner, which is an atomic-level protein structure refinement software [27]. This program has been developed to refine the structures of protein based on the two-step atomic level energy minimization from the alpha carbon traces of the protein. The main chain is refined from the initial trances of Cα, while the side chains of the protein are then constructed or refined using knowledge-based and physics-based principles [27]. PROCHECK was used to validate the protein structures by evaluating the Ramachandran plots for the native and variant proteins and the quality assessment of the refined protein models [28]. This tool analyzes the stereochemical quality of the protein structure and produces the plots evaluating the overall geometry of the protein structure residue by residue. Lastly, the information regarding the domain, motifs, and other critical functional sites of PKCδ was attained from InterProScan [29], which is an online package that can scan the protein sequences against the InterPro repository signatures. The identified domains and motifs were then highlighted in PyMol.


### Conservation analysis
  Understanding the conservation of amino acids in proteins is essential for comprehending their role in protein activity and evolution. Alterations in the amino acids present in the evolutionary conserved regions in protein can disturb its structure and functions. ConSurf server was used to evaluate the evolutionary conservation of amino acid residues. This tool utilizes multiple sequence alignment to analyze the conservation of the amino acids in protein by analyzing the phylogenetic association among the homologs [30]. This tool scores every amino acid residue ranging from 1 to 9, where low scores indicate no evolutionary conservation, and higher scores indicate that the residues are highly conserved. Moreover, highly conserved and exposed amino acids were predicted to be functional residues, while those amino acids that were highly conserved and buried were predicted to be structural residues.


### Stability and flexibility analyses of PKCδ non-synonymous variants
  The amino acid substitutions can either increase or reduce the structural stability of the protein structure. The impact of the non-synonymous variant on protein stability and flexibility was also analyzed and compared with the wild-type structure. The stability of the protein structure is determined through ΔΔG. For this purpose, several reputed silico tools were employed.

  mCSM utilizes a graph-based approach to evaluate the effects of non-synonymous alterations on the stability of the protein structure. This tool has been trained in a particular environment based on the patterns of atomic distances of various amino acid residues. This tool has a specific cutoff value for evaluating the stability of the protein. This tool categorizes the variant as stabilizing when the predicted value of Gibbs free energy is above zero, while it categorizes a variant as destabilizing when the predicted value of Gibbs free energy is less than zero [31].

  MUpro predicts the impact of non-synonymous SNPs on the stability of protein using a set of machine learning systems. It performs analysis using two different machine learning approaches, neural networks, and support vector machines (SVM). This tool also analyzes the impact of SNPs on the stability of the protein via determining the values of change in Gibbs free energy and also predicts the direction of change in energy. Moreover, it predicts the change in protein stability based on the provided sequence instead of the tertiary structure of protein [32].

  I-Mutant is also another machine learning based tool that regards the spatial surroundings of the altered residue with respect to the types of surrounding residues and the surface accessibility. This tool has been trained to perform two major tasks. (a) It predicts the direction of changes in the stability of protein due to the occurrence of mutations, (b) and it also predicts the value of Gibbs free energy due to the change in amino acid in the protein [33].

  DynaMut2 is another tool to predict the stability of the protein structure. This tool includes the Normal Mode Analysis (NMA) technique to attain the motion of protein and represents the wildtype environment using graph-based signatures. For the prediction of stability upon the non-synonymous alteration, various methods including NMA, wildtype residue environment, contact potential score, substitution propensities, graph-based signatures, and interatomic interactions, are combined and used to train the machine learning algorithm. This tool can provide stability prediction for single as well as multiple alterations [34].

  DUET is a web tool for predicting the impact of non-synonymous SNPs on the stability of the specific protein. It is an integrated computational approach, and it evaluates the protein stability based on the secondary structure of the protein. This tool includes the data set of experimentally obtained thermodynamics data from the ProTherm database. This tool scores the variant as Gibbs free energy, and negative values of DDG indicates that a variant has destabilization effect [35].

  The flexibility of the protein structures plays a crucial role in their biological activities, such as stability, interactions with other proteins, and general protein functions. The flexibility of the native and variants was determined through a deep learning-based protein flexibility prediction program MEDUSA [36]. It uses physiochemical and evolutionary information of protein sequences for the neural networks to determine the flexibility class of each protein sequence position.


### Structural effect of non-synonymous SNPs on PKCδ
  The impact of non-synonymous SNPs on PKCδ structure and function was analyzed using Project HOPE [37]. This tool was designed to describe the impact of variations in the human proteins on the molecular basis of disease-associated phenotypes. HOPE gathers information from various sources including the ‘WHAT IF’ calculations on the homology model built by YASARA or the PDB file, annotations from UniProt, DAS-server generated sequence-based predictions, and HSSP conservation score. This information is used to determine protein sequence annotation, predict 3-dimensional coordinates of the protein, predict the impact of the amino acid variant on the protein’s structure and physio-chemical properties exerted by the altered amino acid [38].

  Lastly, to evaluate the implications of variants on PKCδ functions and disease association, MutPred2 was used, which is a machine learning software that screens the non-synonymous SNPs and delineates the underlying molecular processes that disrupt the protein functions and cause pathogenicity [39]. This tool provides scores depicting the probability of the disease-associated amino acid substitution. This tool scores (g-score) the variants in the range of 0–1 based on the probability that a particular amino acid variant is disease-causing or not. The g-score is the average of all the scores from the MutPred2 neural networks. A probability score >0.5 suggests that a particular SNP is pathogenic, and a score < 0.5 indicates that a SNP is non-pathogenic. It has also been estimated that a score of 0.68 generates 10% of the positive rates, while a score of 0.80 yields 5% to false positive results.


### Protein-protein docking interaction analysis
  Molecular docking interaction of wildtype and deleterious non-synonymous variants of PKCδ with its interacting proteins was performed through HADDOCK (High Ambiguity Driven Protein–Protein Docking) [40]. It is a web based tool to evaluate the intermolecular interactions among the proteins in a binding complex. Protein-protein docking was performed with default settings of all HADDOCK parameters and cluster with lowest Z-value was selected from each docking simulation. The 3D protein structures were predicted (predicted and minimized as described in section Modelling and validation of protein 3D structures) through AlphaFold and validated through Ramachandran plots. Furthermore, structural refinement and energy minimization was performed prior to docking using ModRefiner. To analyze and visualize the intermolecular interactions among protein-protein docking complexes, PDBsum server was used which provides the statistics of interactions as well as highlights the interacting residues in complex [41]. Moreover, this tool also provides information about the types of bonds present in protein interaction complexes including hydrogen bonds, salt-bridges, disulfide bonds, and non-bonded interactions.


### Molecular dynamics simulations
  Molecular dynamics (MD) analyses of native and non-synonymous variant structures as well as the docked complexes were performed with the help of the GROMACS package using CHARMM36 force field [42, 43]. GROMACS is the most widely used and freely available software, and it is primarily used to perform simulations [44]. MD analysis of protein structures is essential to gain deeper insight into their inter-molecular and intra-molecular interactions, confirmational alterations, proteins’ stability, and functions. The protein structure was solvated in water using the spc216.gro solvent model in a cubic box, where the structure was placed at a distance of 1 nm from the edge of the cubic box. The ionization of the amino acid residues of PKCδ was done at pH7. The solvated system was then neutralized by adding 0.15 M KCl, negatively charged (Cl−) and positively charged (K+) ions, into the cubic box. The neutralized solvated system was then subjected to energy minimization to avoid any inappropriate configurations and steric clashes. The steepest-descent minimization technique was used, and energy minimization was achieved in 5000 steps. Before initiating protein dynamics, equilibration of the energy-minimized system is essential to attain the optimal temperature and orientation of the protein, without which the system can collapse. For this purpose, the temperature of 300k and pressure of 1 bar were used [45]. 300 K is the most suitable temperature for protein folding for all systems. The Particle-Mesh Ewald (PME) technique was used for the electrostatic interactions, and the protein was equilibrated for volume and pressure in NVT and NPT, respectively. MD was run on the well-equilibrated system for 100ns on variant and native protein structures was performed at 2 fs time step. Lastly, Lastly, H-bonds length and angle cutoff were set at 2.8–3.1Å and 30° between the hydrogen donor and acceptor, respectively. And only those H-bonds that follow this cutoff were considered. Upon the completion of MD simulations, the trajectory was analyzed by entering the command: gmx trjconv -s md_0_1.tpr -f md_0_1.xtc -o md_0_1_noPBC.xtc -pbc mol -center to generate md_0_1_noPBC.xtc file. This trajectory file was used to perform further analyses. For the analysis of root mean square deviations (RMSD) rmsd.xvg file was produced, root mean square fluctuations produced rmsf.xvg file, radius of gyration (Rg) was analyzed through gyrate.xvg, solvent accessibility surface area (SASA) was analyzed through sasa.xvg. Lastly, intra-protein hydrogen bonds were analyzed through hydrogen2-bond-intra-protein.xvg.


### Experimental analysis

#### Sample acquisition
  Blood samples were attained from the 360 female patients with confirmed diagnosis of breast cancer, while the control group included blood samples from 363 healthy females. The sample population was recruited after confirmed histological diagnosis. The blood samples were collected from the Combined Military Hospital (CMH) and District Headquarter Hospital (DHQ), Rawalpindi. The clinical characteristics of breast cancer patients including metastasis status, tumor stage, family history of cancer, and molecular subtype were determined from patient history Performa. Pregnant and nursing patients and patients with other types of malignancies were excluded. Research was conducted after the institutional review board (IRB) of Atta-ur-Rahman School of Applied Bioscience (ASAB), National University of Sciences and Technology (NUST), Ref: No: IRB No. 2024-IRB-A-16/16, according to the protocols of the Helinski Declaration [46]. Blood samples obtained from the participants were stored in anticoagulating EDTA tubes for genotyping analysis. DNA from blood samples was isolated using organic extraction protocol.

Two sets of primers were obtained: inner forward and inner reverse, and outer forward and outer reverse. The inner primers are specific to the allele and used to detect the specific genetic variant present in particular samples. The primers were then validated using web-based tools OligoAnalyser (https://www.idtdna.com/pages/tools/oligoanalyzer) and OligoCalc (http://biotools.nubic.northwestern.edu/OligoCalc.html).

#### Statistical analysis
  Statistical analysis on the attained data was performed using GraphPad prism 9.0 software (California, USA). Fisher exact test was applied to find the association of variant rs1703863535 with breast cancer. Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium (HWE) was analyzed. Genotypic associations using different genetic models (codominant, dominant, and recessive) were also analyzed. The analysis of Odds ratios and relative risk was also performed, and their respective 95% confidence-intervals were also determined. The p-value ≤ 0.05 was regarded as statistically significant.


## Results

### Retrieval of SNPs
  The genetic variants of the human PRKCD gene were retrieved from the Ensembl, gnomAD, and COSMIC databases, and a total of 15,135 SNPs were retrieved. Out of this reported number of SNPs, 613 were unique non-synonymous variants, 281 were coding region variants, 240 were 3`UTR variants, 217 were 5` UTR variants, 48 were frameshift variants, 277 were splice site variants, 12,160 were intronic variants, and the remaining SNPs belonged to several other variant classes. It is evident from Fig. 2a that PRKCD variants are extensively distributed into various categories, with highest percentage of variants occurring in the intronic region, followed by non-synonymous variants, synonymous variants, splice site variants, UTR variants, and frameshift variants. Non-synonymous SNPs impact the protein stability and function by altering their structures, which can cause changes in the biological activities of the protein; therefore, only non-synonymous SNPs were selected for this study. The detailed catalog of non-synonymous SNPs retrieved from the three genome repositories is given in supplementary table 1a. Out of a total of 676 amino acid residues in PRKCD encoded protein, 409 residues were found to have non-synonymous SNPs, depicting that non-synonymous SNPs were present in 60% residues of the protein (Fig. 2b, supplementary table 1b). Furthermore, the exons of PRKCD were also determined, and their corresponding amino acids were identified, and the frequency of non-synonymous SNPs corresponding to each amino acid residue was also determined (Fig. 2c). It can be seen that exons 3,4, 6, 13, 16, and 19 have a high frequency (> 40%) of occurrence of non-synonymous SNPs.

### Identification of deleterious SNPs
  The selected unique 613 non-synonymous SNPs were analyzed to evaluate their potential impact on the biological functions of the protein. Out of 613 non-synonymous SNPs, 126 were found to have incomplete information. Therefore, only 487 non-synonymous variants with complete information and rsIDs were selected for further analysis. It was found that out of 487 non-synonymous SNPs, 336 were predicted to be deleterious, and 151 were predicted to be tolerated by SIFT; PolyPhen predicted 299 SNPs as benign, and 188 SNPs were predicted as damaging; CADD categorized 458 SNPs as likely benign, and 27 SNPs were pointed as likely deleterious; 339 SNPs were regarded as likely benign, and 144 SNP were considered likely disease-causing by REVEL; Meta-LR categorized 339 SNPs as tolerated and 144 as damaging; Mutation Accessor classified 156 SNPs as neutral, 166 as low, and 161 as high deleterious SNPs, and lastly the PROVEAN categorized 354 SNPs as neutral and 133 SNPs as deleterious (Fig. 3).

### Molecular dynamics simulation analysis
  This study analyzed the impact of selected variants on the structural dynamics, fluctuations in the amino acid residues, intramolecular hydrogen bonds, alteration in the radius of gyration, and the analysis of solvent accessibility surface area of native and variant PKCδ proteins. For this purpose, 100 ns MD simulations were performed, and structural data was obtained which is described below.


### Root mean square deviation
  RMSD analysis was done to analyze the deviation in the amino acid residues in the protein models over the period of 100 ns simulations. The analysis revealed that RMSD for the wildtype PKCδ was increased from 0 to 20 ns and achieved the value of 1.0 nm at 15 ns and declined to 0.8 nm at 20 ns. At 30 ns, RMSD for native PKCδ was found to be 1.05 nm. During the period of 40–80 ns, native PKCδ RMSD was reduced, and it was at its lowest value, i.e., 0.77 nm at 80 ns. However, during the last 20 ns of simulation, RMSD for the native protein was increased to achieve a maximum value of 1.17 nm at 100 ns. Analysis of V114G revealed that from 0 to 10 ns, RMSD was increased to 0.8 nm, however, RMSD was reduced dramatically and at 20 ns RMSD of 0.4 nm was reported. RMSD values for this variant fluctuated between 0.5 and 0.7 nm and at 100 ns, its value was reported to be 0.59 nm. Variants C189R and C189Y showed considerably different trends in their RMSD values. RMSD for variant C189R was increased to 0.89 nm, whereas for variant C189Y, it was 0.6 nm. During 20–60 ns, RMSD was increased to 1.1 nm for C189R and 0.79 nm for C189Y. At the time frame of 70–80 ns, RMSD for C189R and C189Y was at the maximum value of 1.3 nm and 0.8 nm, respectively, and during the last 20 ns, the RMSD for C189R decreased to 0.8 nm and 0.6 nm for C189Y. These results suggested that C198R caused an increased structural deviation in the protein, while C189Y tends to have a stabilizing role. Lastly, RMSD for variant W608R was evaluated. This variant showed an overall decreased value in RMSD compared to the native PKCδ. Its RMSD value increased from 0 to 0.5 nm at 20 ns, and after that, its RMSD values for this variant remained largely constant throughout the 100 ns simulation. This result suggested that W608R resulted in protein structural consistency (Fig. 10a).


### Root mean square fluctuation
  The alterations in the structural flexibility of amino acid residues in the PKCδ native and variants were assessed through RMSF analysis. Wildtype and the four variants V114G, C189R, C189Y, and W608R showed increased fluctuation at various residue locations. Wildtype protein had high fluctuation peaks at amino acids Glu-20, Arg-40, Gln-129, and Ala-323. Regulatory domain variants V114G, C189R, and C189Y were found to have elevated fluctuation in the residues present in the regulatory region of the proteins. These variants resulted in increased flexibility in 20, 40, 78, 111, 130, 132, 220, 239, 255, 317, and 321 amino acid residues. The native protein had the highest fluctuation value of 1.28 nm, its average RMSF value was 0.25 nm, and the lowest RMSF was reported to be 0.088 nm. The variants V144G, C189R, and C189Y had the highest RMSF values of 1.09 nm, 1.3 nm, and 1.2 nm; the average RMSF values for these variants were calculated to be 0.26 nm, 0.30 nm, and 0.25 nm during the course of 100 ns simulations. The AGC-kinase variant W608R showed a similar pattern of residual flexibility compared to the wildtype; however, it showed more fluctuations within the residues located within or in close vicinity of CTT, such as residues 502, 524, 552, 625, and 676. The average RMSF for W608R was estimated to be 0.26 nm, while the highest and lowest values were calculated to be 1.02 nm and 0.08 nm, respectively (Fig. 10b).


### Radius of gyration
  The radius of gyration was also determined for the native and variant proteins. Rg quantifies the overall compactness of the proteins and predicts their structural behaviors as it determines the atomic distribution around the axis of the protein [50]. In this study, the structural compactness of the native and the variant PKCδ proteins were also determined by analyzing the Rg values throughout the 100 ns period of simulations. The Rg values ranged from the highest values to the lowest in the order: 3.11177 nm–2.91324 nm, 3.1448 nm–2.82997 nm, 3.20406 nm–2.90165 nm, 3.12018 nm–2.86464 nm, 3.23576 nm–3.00179 nm, 3.152 nm–2.85367 nm, and 3.10479 nm–2.89519 nm for the native and four non-synonymous variants V114G, C189R, C189Y, and W608R, respectively. The mean values for Rg of the native and variants were 2.998 nm, 3.003 nm, 3.036 nm, 2.976 nm, and 2.9989 nm, respectively. It was shown that all variants increased the compactness of the protein due to their tight packaging, as indicated by the decreased values of Rg compared to the native protein structure (Fig. 10c).


### Solvent accessible surface area analysis
  SASA studies the accessibility behavior of amino acid residues in protein to the solvent, thereby analyzing the hydrophobic and hydrophilic regions present in the protein molecule while also evaluating the impact of the nonsynonymous variants on the compactness of the PKCδ protein hydrophobic core. The SASA values for the native and four variants are shown in Fig. 10d. The average SASA value for the native protein structure was 377.8 nm2, whereas for the variants V114G, C189R, C189Y, and W608R were 384.5 nm2, 386.8 nm2, 387.4 nm2, and 380.3 nm2, respectively. Increased values of SASA indicate that the protein structure has expanded, suggesting that the variant proteins have increased structural expansion compared to the native counterpart.


### Hydrogen bonds

The analysis of Hydrogen bond numbers can provide insight into the molecular characteristics of the proteins, such as their structural intramolecular associations, structural stability, and their interactions with their binding molecules. During the period of 100 ns, all proteins formed various hydrogen bonds ranging from the highest to lowest values 488 − 399, 469 − 387, 480 − 402, 465 − 388, 472 − 389, 477 − 396, and 490 − 408, with an average of 446.5, 427.6, 441.0, 429.2, and 446.4 for wildtype, V114G, C189R, C189Y, and W608R, respectively (Fig. 10e).

## Discussion
  PKCδ is a member of the PKC family, which is a member of highly conserved AGC kinase proteins. The major PKC isoforms include PRKCA, PRKCBI, PRKCBII, PRKCG, PRKCD (gene that encode PKCδ), PRKCE, PRKCQ, PRKCH, PRKCI and PRKCZ. Among them PKCδ is of critical importance due to its role in various cellular processes such as cell proliferation, cell cycle regulation, immune hemostasis and metabolism [51]. Analysis of the PKCδ variants presented the opportunity to evaluate the multi-functional and potentially pathogenic amino acid variants in the protein that is known to be involved in various diseases in human including cancers [52–56]. However, there is no data available that describes the association of PKCδ variants with diseases, including cancers. The experimental analysis of the gene variants can be quite extensive and costly; however, pre-estimation of the potentially pathogenic genetic variants through computational tools can orient the scientific investigation in the right direction and help save resources and time [57]. Hence, the aim of this study was to explore the non-synonymous SNPs in PRKCD, evaluate their potential damaging impact, and analyze their effect on the structural, functional, and biophysical characteristics of the protein. A total of 613 non-synonymous variants among 15,135 SNPs were retrieved from various repositories. This massive number of variants indicates how a significant amount of the variants can alter the structure of PKCδ protein. The non-synonymous variants were then analyzed for the precise filtration of the most pathogenic SNPs through various computational approaches. These analyses led to the identification of ten most highly deleterious variants. These functionally important variants were then analyzed for their cancer-causing potential, and it was predicted that V114G, C189R, C189Y, and W608R were cancer drivers while the rest of the variants were passengers. These variants were present in various domains of the protein. Regulatory SNPs that stabilize the protein structure can lock the protein in a constitutively active state, which can lead to its increased oncogenic potential. Such phenomena have been previously investigated in the KRAS proteins [58, 59]. Previous reports have shown that alterations in the regulatory regions can weaken the interactions of the auto-inhibitory region with the catalytic site, resulting in the increased enzymatic activity of the proteins [60]. Also, the mutation in the C2 regulatory region of the PKCβ was shown to inactivate the protein by forming intramolecular interactions with the catalytic region [61]. On the other hand, the kinase region variants can directly hamper the catalytic activity of the protein and disrupt its enzymatic potential. These variants can modify the formation of enzyme-substrate complex, which can eventually cause alterations in the downstream signaling pathways of the protein [59, 62]. The CTT of PKCδ has been shown to play critical roles in its nuclear localization, structural stability, and activation by the phosphorylation of important residues. Non-synonymous polymorphism in this region can impact the protein stability and its activation [1].

  Stability analyses showed that selected non-synonymous SNPs decreased the protein stability. The stability of the protein plays a key role in the maintenance of various biological activities and functions of the proteins [63, 64]. The pathogenic non-synonymous variants can significantly alter the protein stability by affecting its physicochemical properties, such as the substitution of polar and non-polar residues, loss of intramolecular disulfide bonds or hydrogen bonds, or perturbing its structural constraints, such as the substitution of bigger residue with a smaller one and vice versa, resulting in strain in the protein backbone [65, 66]. Furthermore, the structural analysis through Project HOPE revealed critical details on the impact of variants on protein structure. It was shown that the substitution with larger residues resulted in increased electrostatic repulsion and gain of surface alterations in the protein structure. Differences in charges of the native and variant amino acid residues resulted in altered protein charge and disrupted the intramolecular interactions in the protein structure. The change in the charges of amino acids can alter the net charge on protein which can cause disruption of the protein structure [67]. The substitution of charged amino acid with other uncharged residues can affect the binding of the protein’s active site with the substrate due to the loss of the intermolecular interactions.

  The substitution of a native amino acid with an altered one can cause confirmational misalignment in the protein structure [68]. The presence of new residues in improper confirmation can result in the disruption of the intramolecular bonds that were previously present between the native residue and the neighboring amino acids [69–71]. The presence of the non-synonymous variants in the amino acid residues that have been evolutionarily conserved can have serious consequences compared to the variants occurring in non-conserved regions [72]. The conservation status of the variant residues was determined through ConSurf, which showed that all four variants were evolutionarily conserved. Variants V114G, C189R, C189Y, and W608R were buried residues with structural implications. It has been implicated that when the conserved residues are mutated, they can directly impact the structure, stability, and biophysical properties of the protein [73, 74]. The protein domains are functionally active regions, and variations in these sites can significantly alter the protein activity [75]. Therefore, these four variant proteins might have damaging effects at the supra optimal level in the genome. MD simulation is a practical approach for the structural analysis of the proteins [76]. MD simulation analysis was performed to analyze the impact of these SNPs on protein structural deviation, residual fluctuations, accessibility of solvent surface area, degree of compactness, and number of intramolecular hydrogen bonds. Consistent fluctuations in the RMSD values of four variants suggest that these variants cause instability in the structure of the protein. RMSF evaluates the amino acid fluctuations in the wildtype and corresponding variant protein structures. It was found that the variant proteins showed a high degree of fluctuations compared to the native protein structure. Increased RMSD and RMSF indicates that the protein has higher structural deviation and flexibility [77, 78]. RG values of variants were higher compared to the wildtype protein. The altered residues caused the distribution of atoms around the protein axis in such a way that resulted in decreased structural compactness.

  To determine the impact of these non-synonymous variants on the intermolecular interactions of PKCδ with its binding partners, molecular docking and interaction dynamics simulations were performed between STAT3 and native/variants PKCδ. Studies have shown that STAT3 is phosphorylated at ser727 by PKCδ [79], which leads to its non-canonical activation. Any alterations in the molecular interactions between PKCδ and STAT3 can affect the activation of STAT3 at post translational levels. Hence, to evaluate the possible impact of PKCδ non-synonymous SNPs on interactions with STAT3 and its subsequent activation various in silico analyses were performed. It was revealed that V114G and W608R complexes were interacting more stably and with high intermolecular interaction energies compared to that of native complex. The amino acid substitution, particularly in the AGC kinase domain can alter the molecular interactions of PKCδ. Interaction dynamics and docking analysis showed that AGC kinase domain variant can alter the molecular interactions and may impart more stability to complex compared to the wildtype counterpart. Protein stability plays critical role in cell cycle progression and cancer. Overstabilization of proteins can constitutively render them with active confirmation which can activate various signaling cascades active in cancer [80]. A stabilized PKCδ-STAT3 complex can lead to increased activation of STAT3, which may translocate to the nucleus where it performs its transcription factor activity, resulting in the increased transcriptional activation of various genes, which may lead to cancer development and progression [60, 81]. Furthermore, to understand the role of PRKCD non-synonymous variants in breast cancer, the genotypic association analysis of variant rs1703863535 was also conducted in this study. Genotyping analysis revealed that genotype TT was associated with breast cancer, while heterozygous genotype TC was shown to have a protective effect. Moreover, genotype CC showed no statistical association in this regard. The results of the present study are coherent with the previously reported studies, indicating the role of the variant alleles in the development of diseases [82]. Furthermore, genotype TT was found to impart a protective effect in patients with hereditary breast cancer. Studies have reported that certain genetic polymorphisms can be risk factors for the development of hereditary tumors, while other may impart protective effect in this regard [83]. Hence, the identification of such variants is necessary as they can serve as potential genetic markers for pre-screening of breast cancer. Furthermore, genetic association analysis also revealed that variant rs1703863535 was significantly associated with receptor status among the breast cancer patients. Certain SNPs have been identified which have shown association with various receptor status particularly, Luminal subtypes and Her2 + subtypes. A study showed that SNP rs584298 in DEAR1 was associated with increased risk of Her2 + breast cancer [84]. Hence, the identification of such genetic variants may provide a way forward for the targeted treatment and improved therapeutic outcomes in breast cancer patients.

  Previous reports have shown that various other PKC isoforms are widely associated with several diseases, including cancers [85]. Non-synonymous variants in epsilon, gamma, and iota PKC are associated with various types of cancers, including breast cancer, cervical cancer, ovarian cancer, and liver cancer, and prostate cancer [59, 60, 86–89]. Therefore, investigating the role of non-synonymous SNPs in PKCδ in various types of cancers will help in understanding the molecular basis of disease, which can subsequently help in the development of new genetic markers for disease diagnosis and targeted treatment. This study has given an initial insight into the consequences of PKCδ non-synonymous variants on its molecular activities and structure. In future, MD simulations for extended time scale and in replicates can be performed for complete conformational sampling to deepen our understanding on the variant impact on protein confirmation. Moreover, further extensive functional investigations are essential to analyze clinical pathogenicity of PKCδ non-synonymous SNPs and their impact on its molecular properties and intermolecular interactions to reveal the underlying molecular mechanisms of PKCδ in regulating various cancer-associated signaling pathways.


## Conclusion
  PKCδ plays an important role in various cellular processes, and it has also been shown to play a role in diseases such as cancers. The comprehensive computational analysis of non-synonymous SNPs in PKCδ revealed four SNPs as highly pathogenic and caused destabilization of the protein structure. Furthermore, they also altered the biological functions of the normal protein and confer pathogenic features to it. It was also shown that missense variants in PKCδ alter it molecular interactions with STAT3, leading to its non-canonical activation. The association of PRKCD deleterious variant rs1703863535 with breast cancer was also determined through the Tetra ARMS PCR analysis, indicating that this variant might serve as a potential genetic marker for breast cancer. This study has established a foundation regarding the understanding the role of PKCδ non-synonymous SNPs on protein structure and molecular functions. The data provided in this study should be validated through extensive experimental analysis so that these variants can be used for specific diagnosis and efficient therapeutics targeting for the treatment of breast cancer.
