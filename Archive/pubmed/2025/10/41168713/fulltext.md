# A novel prediction method for protein-DNA binding sites based on protein language model fusion features with SE-connection pyramidal network and ensemble learning


## Abstract
  Protein-DNA interactions are crucial in life processes such as gene expression and regulation. Therefore, the accurate prediction of DNA-binding sites on proteins is highly important for the advancement of scientific understanding in the field of biological activities. In this work, we propose a protein-DNA binding site prediction framework, termed Evolutionary Scale Modeling-SE-Connection Pyramidal (ESM-SECP), which integrates a sequence-feature-based prediction method with a sequence-homology-based predictor via ensemble learning. The sequence-feature-based prediction method is built on two types of input features: ESM-2 protein language model embeddings and evolutionary conservation information computed by PSI-BLAST. These features are fused by a multi-head attention mechanism and processed through the newly proposed SE-Connection Pyramidal(SECP) network for prediction. The sequence-template method, based on sequence homology, serves as a complementary approach to predict DNA-binding residues. The two predictors are combined via ensemble learning to improve overall model performance. Through the experimental validation of the TE46 and TE129 datasets, ESM-SECP outperforms the traditional methods in several evaluation indices, demonstrating its outstanding performance in Protein-DNA binding site prediction.


## Introduction
  Protein-DNA interactions play key roles in processes such as gene expression and regulation, DNA replication, and the pathogenesis of various human diseases. Therefore, accurately identifying protein-DNA binding sites is particularly important for exploring complex regulatory networks in cells and developing new drugs [1]. Traditional experimental methods can identify these binding sites by solving protein structures; however, the high economic costs and time consumption make wide application difficult. Compared with experimental methods, computational approaches based on computer technology and artificial intelligence take advantage of rapid prediction and high accuracy, which makes them more competitive when facing massive amounts of protein sequence information [2].

  The computational prediction methods for Protein-DNA binding sites can be broadly divided into structure-based and sequence-based strategies. Structure-based methods typically rely on experimentally determined three-dimensional structures of protein-DNA complexes and employ techniques such as molecular docking and energy scoring to identify binding sites [3]. As of June 3, 2025, the Protein Data Bank comprised 237,777 released structures, of which approximately 44,300 were DNA binding proteins still representing approximately 18.6% of all deposited protein structures. However, since the available structural information for protein-DNA complexes remains relatively limited, structure-based approaches face inherent restrictions in practical applications [4]. In contrast, sequence-based methods do not depend on experimentally resolved 3D structures. Instead, they predict DNA-binding residues directly from protein primary sequences. Owing to the far greater abundance of sequence data, sequence-based predictors exhibit superior generalizability compared with their structure-based counterparts.

  Sequence-based predictors have demonstrated excellent performance in identifying protein-DNA binding sites. Early machine learning approaches relied on handcrafted sequence encodings. For example, position-specific scoring matrices (PSSMs) capture evolutionary conservation and can markedly improve the accuracy of interaction site predictions [5]. Ahmad et al. [6] proposed a prediction method based on a back-propagation neural network using PSSM-derived features extracted from protein sequences. Their results underscore the critical importance of evolutionary information in protein-DNA binding prediction. Wang et al. [7] introduced the BindN model, which extracts physicochemical properties such as side-chain pKa values, hydrophobicity, and molecular weight as input features and employs a support vector machine to achieve strong predictive performance.

  With the development of deep learning, methods based on neural networks such as convolutional neural networks (CNNs) [8] have been widely used in the field of bioinformatics [9]. Zhou et al. [10] proposed the CNN model, which first captures sequence-based features from the sequence around the target amino acid and then uses CNNsite as a classifier to predict the Protein-DNA binding sites, which achieves better results. Song et al. [11] proposed a deep convolutional neural network, Se-Residual-Inception, which is based on channel attention. The attention block in Se-Residual-Inception gave higher weights for more distinguished features, which demonstrated that the distinguished sequence features with implicit information are crucial for Protein-DNA binding sites prediction.

  With AlphaFold2 [12] achieving breakthroughs in protein structure prediction, its outstanding performance has been widely used in bioinformatics. Yuan et al. [13] proposed the GraphSite model, which first uses AlphaFold2 to predict the three-dimensional structure of proteins and combines the predicted structural features with sequence evolution information. Finally, they used the Graph Transformer model to predict protein-DNA binding sites, which achieved promising results.

  In recent years, pretrained protein language models with significant embedding capabilities for protein sequences have been introduced for binding site prediction. Compared with handcrafted features extracted from sequence analysis tools, protein language models extract distinguished features automatically from protein primary sequences, which is more convenient and efficient [14]. Liu et al. [15]proposed an end-to-end prediction framework called CLAPE-DB, which encodes input sequences into 1024-dimensional feature vectors using ProtBert, a large-scale pretrained protein language model. And then processes the embedding vectors through a four-layer one-dimensional CNN for the prediction of protein-DNA binding sites, with good generalizability. Huang et al. [16]proposed a method called iProtDNA-SMOTE, which utilizes non-equilibrium graph neural networks along with pre-trained protein language models to predict DNA binding residues. This approach effectively addresses the class imbalance issue in predicting protein-DNA binding sites by leveraging unbalanced graph data, thus enhancing model’s generalization and specificity.

  Existing approaches have made pioneering contributions to the computational prediction of Protein-DNA binding sites. However, most of these methods rely solely on handcrafted features or language-embedding representations, employ relatively simple classifier architectures that yield unsatisfactory performance, and typically predict binding sites from only a single perspective. To address these limitations, we propose an ensemble-learning framework that combines a sequence-feature-based predictor with a sequence-homology-based predictor. The sequence-feature branch integrates protein language-model embeddings and PSSM-derived evolutionary features via a multi-head attention mechanism. The sequence-homology branch offers complementary information by identifying DNA-binding sites through homologous templates. These complementary predictors are fused by ensemble learning to improve identification of DNA-binding residues in protein sequences. Specifically, we first extract residue embeddings from protein sequences using the ESM-2_t33_650M_UR50D version of the ESM-2 language model [17] and compute PSSM evolutionary features via PSI-BLAST [18]. Then, we fuse these two features using a multi-head attention mechanism and feed the combined representation into the SECP network to predict binding sites. In parallel, the sequence homology template method identifies binding sites by searching for homologous sequences using Hhblits [19]. Finally, the outputs of the two methods are combined for more accurate identification of protein-DNA-binding sites across a broader range.


## Materials and methods
  Our prediction framework comprises two predictors, a sequence-feature-based predictor with SE-Connection Pyramidal (SECP) network and a sequence-homology-based predictor, which are combined via ensemble learning. Firstly, the datasets used in this work are described and the necessary components in our prediction method are explained. Then, the framework of sequence-feature-based predictor and sequence-homology-based predictor are demonstrated respectively. Finally, how the two predictors are integrated by ensemble learning method for final prediction is described.


### Dataset description
  The present study is conducted on two widely used benchmark datasets, whose training and testing sets are prefixed “TR” and “TE”, respectively. To ensure non-redundancy, CD-HIT (Li and Godzik, 2006) was applied to cluster sequences at a 30% identity threshold. In both datasets, binding residues are defined as those whose minimum distance to any atom of DNA is less than the sum of their van der Waals radii plus 0.5 Å.


#### TE46 and TR646
  The datasets TE46 and TR646 were obtained from the DBPred study [20]. The independent testing set TE46 comprises 46 protein sequences, containing 965 DNA-binding residues and 9,911 nonbinding residues, whereas the training set TR646 includes 646 protein sequences, comprising 15,636 DNA-binding residues and 298,503 nonbinding residues.


#### TE129 and TR573
  The datasets TE129 and TR573 were obtained from the CLAPE-DB study [15]. The independent testing set TE129 comprises 129 protein sequences, containing 2,240 DNA-binding residues and 35,275 nonbinding residues; the training set TR573 comprises 573 protein sequences, containing 14,479 DNA-binding residues and 145,404 nonbinding residues.


### Protein sequence embedding
  ESM-2 is a family of transformer-based protein language models [21] trained in an unsupervised fashion on large sequence repositories such as UniRef90 and UniRef50 [22] using masked language modelling [23]. By leveraging multiple layers of self-attention, ESM-2 captures long-range dependencies between amino acids. Model sizes range from tens of millions to 1.5 billion parameters and support both end-to-end atomic-level structure prediction and extraction of high-dimensional residue embeddings. In this work, we employ the ESM-2_t33_650M_UR50D model, which comprises 33 transformer layers total approximately 650 million parameters and was pretrained on the redundancy UniRef50 dataset. We extract the output of the final transformer layer to generate a 1280-dimensional embedding vector for each residue.


### PSSM profile
  The position-specific scoring matrix (PSSM) is widely used in bioinformatics for characterizing the evolutionary conservation of amino acids in a protein sequence. By assigning a score to each amino acid at each position-reflecting its frequency of occurrence in related sequences-PSSM captures residue-level conservation. In this study, we use PSI-BLAST to perform multiple iterations of sequence alignment between the query protein and the Swiss-Prot database [24], thereby generating the PSSM. Then the first 20 columns of this matrix are extracted as features. Since the magnitude and scale of the PSSM features differ substantially from those of the protein-language-model embeddings, normalization and a sliding-window transformation are applied to accelerate training and improve prediction accuracy. The sliding-window size is evaluated from 5 to 25. Experimental results indicate that the model achieves optimal performance with a sliding-window size of 17 (see Fig. 1). ### Multi-Head attention
  Multi Head Attention [25] projects the input vectors in parallel into multiple query, key and value subspaces. It computes attention weights independently within each subspace to yield weighted aggregates of the value vectors, and concatenates the outputs of all attention heads and applies a linear transformation to restore the original dimensionality. This mechanism thus enables simultaneous modelling of diverse relational patterns across subspaces, thereby bolstering both representational richness and generalizability capacity. In our framework, the 1280-dimensional residue embeddings produced by ESM-2 and the 340-dimensional PSSM features are processed by two separate 1D CNN branches with identical architecture, each producing a 1024-dimensional feature map. We define dₕ as the dimensionality of Query/Key/Value in a single attention head. These feature maps are then independently linearly projected into the same 8×dₕ subspace to form the Query and Key/Value tensors. For each attention head, the ESM-2 embedding serves as Query, while the PSSM representation serves as both Key and Value, and scaled dot product attention is performed to capture their deep complementary information. Finally, the eight attention head outputs are concatenated and linearly mapped back to 1024 dimensions and fused with the original Query via a residual connection, thus effectively integrating structural and evolutionary features.


### SE-connection pyramid (SECP) network
  The Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) block [26] is a channel-attention mechanism designed to increase the representational power of convolutional neural networks by adaptively recalibrating channel-wise feature responses. It consists of two stages: “squeeze” and “excitation.” In the squeeze stage, global average pooling condenses each channel’s spatial information into a single descriptor, capturing its global context. In the excitation stage, this descriptor is processed by a two-layer fully connected network: first, ReLU activation [27] introduces nonlinearity, and then a sigmoid function [28] generates channel-wise weights. These weights are multiplied back into the original feature map, amplifying informative channels while suppressing less relevant channels, thereby improving the network’s focus on critical features and boosting classification performance.

  Pyramidal Net [29] is a convolutional architecture that incrementally increases the number of feature channels while reducing spatial resolution in a “pyramid” fashion, enabling multi-scale feature extraction suited for both image and sequence data tasks. After each convolutional layer, batch normalization [30] and an activation function are applied, and the normalized output is added in a residual style to the layer’s input, which aids training stability and convergence.

  In our SE-connection pyramid (SECP) architecture, the backbone combines one-dimensional pyramidal network convolutions with SE blocks to form a channel-attentive, pyramid-structured network termed the SE-CNN block. Each SE-CNN consists of Conv1D, BatchNorm1d and a Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) module. Batch normalization ensures that each mini-batch output has zero mean and unit variance, thereby mitigating internal covariate shift and accelerating convergence. We then apply the SE block to dynamically recalibrate channel weights, enabling the model to emphasize channels that contribute most positively to DNA-binding-site prediction and to adaptively refine feature selection during forward propagation. Specifically, in the SECP network the number of channels doubles after every four SE-CNN units, producing a hierarchical feature pyramid. The multi-scale features processed by the SE-CNNs are passed through fully connected (FC) layers and dropout and then mapped to logits. This design both guarantees that each convolutional unit undergoes channel-wise adaptive re-weighting enhancing key channels at fine granularity, and leverages pyramidal channel growth to capture global and local information at different abstraction levels, which is well suited to the DNA-binding site prediction task. ### Prediction method based on SECP network with sequence fusion features
  The prediction method based on SECP network with sequence fusion features consists of feature extraction and model prediction. In the feature extraction stage, features are divided into those based on sequence evolutionary information and those produced by protein language models. For the feature extraction based on sequence evolutionary information, we first perform iterative searches of the FASTA sequences with PSI-BLAST, i.e., conduct BLAST comparisons against the Swiss-Prot database to identify a set of homologous sequences, compute position-specific frequency distributions, and generate 20-dimensional PSSM files for each residue. The 20-dimensional PSSM features are then subjected to a sliding-window operation of size 17 to obtain 340-dimensional PSSM features. The feature extraction based on protein language models employs the Transformer and self-attention-based ESM-2_t33_650M_UR50D model to extract features from the FASTA sequences. This model comprises 33 transformer layers. We extract the final-layer hidden representations to generate 1280-dimensional features for each residue in the sequence. After feature extraction, the two types of features are each processed by a 1D-CNN to produce 1024-dimensional PSSM and ESM features, which are then fused via a multi-head attention mechanism to generate a 1024-dimensional fusion feature for each residue. Finally, the per-residue features, ordered according to their sequence positions, are input sequentially into the SECP network to yield the predicted probability that a residue is a binding site. ### Prediction methods based on sequence homology
  Since protein-DNA binding site prediction is a classical imbalanced learning problem, which means that the number of DNA-binding sites is far less than the number of nonbinding residues, accurately identifying more true binding sites is critical to overall performance. Inspired by ensemble learning [31], we introduce a sequence homology-based template prediction method as a supplement to our sequence-feature-driven predictor. When a query protein shares significant homology with a protein that has a known structure or function, conserved segments often carry equivalent roles. Thus, homologous sequences with known structures can serve as templates for functional inference. The supplementary template method is conducted as follows: first, we format the training set as a HHblits-compatible MSA database [19]. Next, for each query sequence, we execute Hhsearch [32] against this database to identify candidate homologous templates. We then evaluate template quality using the HHsearch metrics Sum_probs and confidence, discarding any hits below predefined thresholds. For retained templates, local alignment results pinpoint high-similarity fragments: if these fragments contain annotated DNA-binding residues, we map and transfer those labels onto the corresponding positions in the query sequence; otherwise, the template prediction is aborted. Because our training and testing sets are clustered at ≤ 30% sequence identity, this template selection remains independent of downstream evaluation. By sweeping various threshold combinations, we find that imposing Sum_probs ≥ 30 and confidence ≥ 8 yields the highest predictive accuracy. Under these criteria, our template module predicts 104 binding sites on the TE46 dataset. 73 of which are correctly identified; on the TE129 dataset. It predicts 147 binding sites. 99 of which are correct. The structure of the template method is shown in Fig. 4.

### Overall architecture
  In this work, we propose an end-to-end deep learning framework, Evolutionary Scale Modeling-SE-Connection Pyramidal (ESM-SECP), for protein-DNA binding site prediction. The framework consists of two prediction methods which are sequence-feature-based predictor and sequence-homology-based predictor. The sequence-feature-based predictor first adapts 1280-dimensional ESM-2 embeddings and 340-dimensional PSSM features to 1024-dimensional data shapes via a 1DCNN. The processed ESM-2 embeddings and PSSM features are subsequently fused through the multi-head attention mechanism to form 1024-dimensional fusion feature; Finally, the deep learning model SECP, which combines pyramid convolution and SE-Block, is adopted for prediction.

  For sequence-homology-based predictor, we introduce a template prediction method based on sequence homology, retain some of the binding sites in the template prediction results, and integrate them with the prediction results from the sequence-feature-based predictor to improve the ability to identify DNA-binding sites and enhance the overall performance.   The ROC curve plots the false-positive rate (FPR) on the x-axis and the true positive rate (TPR) on the y-axis; points closer to the top-left corner indicate higher predictive accuracy, whereas points closer to the bottom-right corner indicate lower accuracy. The AUC (area under the curve) is the area under the ROC curve and ranges from 0 to 1. An AUC closer to 1 signifies better model performance; an AUC of 0.5 corresponds to random guessing; and an AUC below 0.5 indicates performance worse than random. The PR curve plots recall on the x-axis and precision on the y-axis; a curve that lies closer to the upper-right corner indicates better model performance, whereas a curve nearer the lower-left or hugging the axes indicates poorer performance. The AUPRC is the area under the PR curve and ranges from 0 to 1; values closer to 1 signify a stronger ability to identify the positive class. Because these metrics reflect performance at a specific decision threshold and to ensure a fair comparison with other sequence-based predictors, we use the same threshold-selection procedure as in previous studies. We sweep thresholds from 0.01 to 0.99 in steps of 0.01, choose the threshold that maximizes the MCC, and report results at that optimal threshold.

  Since DNA-binding sites are functional sites that exhibit high evolutionary conservation, PSSM feature which reflects the evolutionary conservation of residues has been widely applied to protein-DNA binding site prediction. However, Table 1 shows that the use of manually extracted PSSM features alone cannot guarantee robust predictive performance. ### Comparative analysis of feature fusion methods
  To determine the optimal feature fusion strategy, we evaluated common fusion methods: simple concatenation, a 1D-CNN-based fusion, and a multi-head attention-based fusion. The detailed fusion strategies are described below:

  For simple feature concatenation fusion, the 340-dimensional PSSM features and the 1280-dimensional ESM features are concatenated to form a 1620-dimensional fused feature, which is then fed into the SECP network to predict binding sites.For 1D-CNN fusion method, the 340-dimensional PSSM features and the 1280-dimensional ESM features are first projected to 1024 dimensions, respectively, through 1D convolutional layers. The resulting 1024-dimensional representations are concatenated into a 2048-dimensional vector, which is further refined by a 1D convolution followed by a 1 × 1 Conv1D projection to obtain a 1024-dimensional fused representation. Finally, the fused features are fed into the SECP backbone for prediction.For the multi-head attention-fusion approach, the 340-dimensional PSSM features and the 1280-dimensional ESM features are first projected to 1024 dimensions each by one-dimensional convolutional layers. The projected representations are then passed into a multi-head attention module, which produces a 1024-dimensional fused feature; this fused feature is finally fed into the SECP network for prediction, see Sect. 2.4 for a detailed description. Feature fusion based on multi-head attention adaptively weights pairwise interactions between positions and uses multiple parallel heads to learn diverse relationship patterns, thereby better capturing the complex complementary information between ESM and PSSM features. This gives it an advantage over CNN-based fusion and simple feature concatenation.

  As shown in Tables 3 and 4, the SECP network with multi-head attention achieves superior performance compared with the other two methods, with improvements in the primary evaluation metrics of AUC. This improvement derives from the capacity of the multi-head attention mechanism to model long-range dependencies and multiscale features [36]. Whereas traditional convolutional networks are adept at capturing the local context, they struggle to account for interactions among residues that are far apart in the sequence. In contrast, multi-head attention employs several parallel attention heads to compute weighted associations between queries, keys, and values in different subspaces, thereby adaptively allocating focus. Moreover, by concatenating and linearly projecting the outputs of each head, the model retains the detailed information captured by local convolutional features while integrating global semantic relationships, leading to enhanced predictive accuracy in the complex scenario of protein-DNA interactions.

Therefore, it is ideally suited to complement the relatively low‐sensitivity results of deep learning-based approaches. ## Conclusions
  Protein-DNA binding site is crucial for biological activities. In this study, we formulate a novel prediction method named ESM-SECP for protein-DNA binding sites, which is constructed by two complementary predictors. The first predictor is a sequence-feature-based model, the SE-Connection Pyramidal (SECP) predictor, which itself relies on two types of input features: contextual sequence embeddings produced by the ESM-2 protein language model and evolutionary conservation information computed via PSI-BLAST (PSSM). These two feature streams are fused by a multi-head attention mechanism that captures long-range dependencies and adaptively emphasizes informative residues, and the fused representation is processed by the proposed SECP architecture, a pyramidal convolutional backbone augmented with squeeze-and-excitation modules, to integrate multi-scale and global context information for residue-level prediction. The second predictor is a template-based sequence-homology method that predicts protein-DNA binding sites by identifying homologous fragments in templates, serving as a complementary approach to identify DNA-binding residues. Finally, the two predictors are integrated through ensemble learning to leverage both learned sequence representations and explicit template evidence, yielding improved positive-sample detection and overall performance. The experimental results demonstrate that ESM-SECP outperforms existing methods that are based on single feature sets or traditional network architectures, which shows its promising application prospect in identifying protein-DNA binding sites.
