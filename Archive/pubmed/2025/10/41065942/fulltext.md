# A novel bispecific antibody targeting TNF-α and IL-6 receptor as a potent immunotherapeutic agent for inflammation


## Abstract
  Tumor necrosis factor-alpha and interleukin-6 are proinflammatory cytokines involved in several autoimmune diseases, including rheumatoid arthritis. Although monoclonal antibodies targeting these cytokines or their receptors are successful treatment approaches for autoimmune diseases, approximately 30% of treated patients fail to respond. Therefore, the designation of more effective and versatile therapeutics for simultaneously targeting multiple inflammatory pathways is pivotal. This study aimed to design a recombinant bispecific antibody (BisAb) targeting TNF-α and IL-6 receptor based on the complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) of adalimumab and tocilizumab antibodies. The physicochemical properties and tertiary structure of the modeled bispecific antibody proteins were studied through bioinformatics. The interaction of the designed bispecific antibody with human TNF-α and IL-6R was computed by molecular docking and molecular dynamics. The recombinant BisAb (60kDa) was expressed in Escherichia coli. The ELISA results demonstrated that the affinity of BisAb and its Ada-scFv moiety to TNF-α was quite similar with Kaff values of 7.7 and 9.4 × 10–13 M, respectively. The recombinant BisAb and Toci-scFv also represented similar affinities towards IL-6R (Kaff values of 1.65 and 1.87 × 10–12 M), closely comparable with the parental tocilizumab antibody. A dose-dependent neutralization of TNF-mediated cytotoxicity was also observed on L929 cells. Decreased phosphorylation of signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) was determined through western blotting with specific antibodies, indicating the blockade of IL-6 receptors before the preincubation with IL-6. The designed BisAb, a minimized biotherapeutic molecule, could successfully target two main ligands (IL-6R and TNF-α) involved in rheumatoid arthritis and many inflammatory/infectious diseases.


## Key points
  • A new bispecific antibody was designed to target TNF-α and IL-6R.

  • In silico studies confirmed the physicochemical and structural properties of BisAb.

  • The best-modeled protein was recombinantly expressed in Escherichia coli.

  • BisAb significantly inhibited TNF-α-mediated cytotoxicity.

  • BisAb could efficiently suppress the IL-6-related phosphorylation of STAT3 protein.

## Introduction
  Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a chronic, inflammatory autoimmune disease characterized by joint deformities and destroyed cartilage and bone structures (Ostrowska et al. 2018). The pathogenesis of RA is characterized by persistent synovitis, vascular opacity formation, and bone erosion (Guo et al. 2018). The prevalence of arthritis is estimated to be approximately 1% of the worldwide population, and 2 to 3 times more frequent among women than men (Venetsanopoulou et al. 2023).

  Due to the RA multifactorial nature, patients usually fail to be treated with the first-line treatment options, including nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) and synthetic conventional disease-modifying anti-rheumatic drugs (DMARDs). Therefore, biological DMARDs such as TNF inhibitors are usually administered in disease progression and failure of chemical DMARD therapies (Arnell et al. 2021). The biological DMARDs specifically target an antigen/receptor or a pathway involved in immunological reactions. These substances are polypeptides, including monoclonal antibodies (Burmester and Pope 2017), bispecific antibodies (Brinkmann and Kontermann 2017), or fusion proteins (Van Roy et al. 2013).

  TNF-α, a major inflammatory cytokine in RA pathogenesis, is involved in the activation and survival of leukocytes, synoviocytes, and endothelial cells as well as cytokine and chemokine amplification, and angiogenesis (Elshabrawy et al. 2015; Jang et al. 2021). It is produced by B and T cells, monocytes, and macrophages, and its interaction with the specific receptors eventually induces cell death pathways (Sedger and McDermott 2014; Xu et al. 2017). Activation of TNF-α-associated T cells induces their infiltration and neoangiogenesis, leading to joint destruction and osteoclast formation (Kim et al. 2016). Although anti-TNF biologics are currently considered the gold standard treatment regimens for RA, 30 to 40% of the patients do not effectively respond (Taylor and Feldmann 2009; Rubbert-Roth et al. 2019).

  Interleukin-6 (IL-6) is a pleiotropic and pro-inflammatory cytokine secreted by fibroblasts, monocytes, and macrophages and involved in inflammatory immune responses, bone metabolism, and pathogenesis of RA (Srirangan and Choy 2010; Hashizume and Mihara 2011). The biological activity of IL-6 begins with its binding to the specific IL-6Rα as well as a membrane-bound glycoprotein (gp130) to transduce Janus kinase and the signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (JAK/STAT3) pathway. Synovial overexpression of IL-6 develops chronic synovitis, proliferation of fibroblast-like synoviocytes, angiogenesis promotion, and cartilage degradation in the synovium (Bartok and Firestein 2010; Ogata et al. 2019), indicating the role of IL-6 inhibitors such as tocilizumab and sarilumab in RA management (Yip and Yim 2021).

  Bispecific antibodies (BisAbs) are biological therapeutics characterized by the simultaneous detection of two targets. This technology is considered a successful approach in cancer and autoimmune disease treatments, especially when single-targeted therapies cannot completely manage the disease (Drosos et al. 2019). In the field of RA, several BisAbs are currently being developed against different cytokines and growth factors, including IL-1β, IL-17A (Li et al. 2014), VEGF, and TNF-α (Zhang et al. 2024).

  In the present study, a novel bispecific antibody has been developed consisting of two single-chain variable fragments (scFv) against TNF-α and IL-6R, originating from the variable regions of commercially available adalimumab and tocilizumab monoclonal antibodies (mAbs). The biological activity of the recombinant BisAb was assessed in in vitro assays.


## Materials and methods
  The amino acid sequences of the two mAbs, adalimumab and tocilizumab, were retrieved from Drug Bank (https://go.drugbank.com). G4S and G2S peptide linkers were analyzed to connect the variable fragments of heavy and light chains. Within the designed proteins, the N- or C-terminal location of the histidine tag and the order of variable heavy (VH) and variable light (VL) domains of each scFv molecule were investigated through bioinformatics.


### Homology modeling
  The secondary structure of designed BisAb proteins was evaluated by the PSIPRED (http://bioinf.cs.ucl.ac.uk/psipred/) and VADAR web servers (http://vadar.wishartlab.com/) (Willard et al. 2003).

  The tertiary structure of each designed protein was assessed using the Alphafold2 web server (https://neurosnap.ai/service/AlphaFold2) (Yang et al. 2023), which employs fast and accurate artificial intelligence methods to predict tertiary structures. The quality of the models was evaluated by different web tools such as Procheck (Laskowski et al. 2012), ERRAT (Colovos and Yeates 1993), and Verify3D (Lüthy et al. 1992) modules in the SAVES web server (https://saves.mbi.ucla.edu/) (Laskowski et al. 1993). Procheck analyzes the stereochemical quality of a protein structure by Ramachandran (Laskowski et al. 2012). The higher percentage located in the core and allowed regions indicates better quality of the modeled protein. Verify3D evaluates protein quality by assessing the compatibility between one- and three-dimensional atomic models. Molprobity was applied to measure all-atom clash score in each model (Williams et al. 2018). This score combines the clashscore, rotamer, and Ramachandran calculations into a single score, which is normalized to be on the same scale as X-ray resolution. The Molprobity score percentiles higher than 66 are interpreted as good-quality protein structures. ProSA-web compares the protein models with all available protein structures determined by X-ray and NMR (nuclear magnetic resonance) techniques (Wiederstein and Sippl 2007).


### Assessment of physicochemical properties
  The physicochemical properties of each model were defined by Protparam (Walker 2005), which computes half-life, instability, aliphatic, and Gravy index. It calculates protein half-life based on the N-rule method and the effective role of N-terminal amino acid residues of the protein (Varshavsky 1997). The instability index compares the stability of the protein with different dipeptides in both stable and unstable states. The aliphatic index is an indicator of protein thermostability, based on the relative volume of aliphatic amino acid side chains (Ikai 1980). Gravy calculates the hydrophobicity values of the amino acids (Kyte and Doolittle 1982).


### Study of protein solubility and aggregation
  The solubility of the designed proteins was measured by different web servers based on protein sequence and structure. The protein-sol estimates the solubility of proteins based on their amino acid sequence (Hebditch et al. 2017). SoluProt web application utilizes a machine learning algorithm to employ 96 protein features to predict the solubility of Escherichia coli–expressed proteins (Hon et al. 2021). SoDope (Soluble Domain for Protein Expression) is a web tool for the prediction of protein solubility by applying the Solubility-Weighted Index (SWI), which is derived from crystallographic B-factors (Bhandari et al. 2020). It uses the amino acid sequences and designates the solubility scores. The higher scores show more solubility properties of proteins. The Camsol server could also predict the solubility of proteins through their structure with three different algorithms (Sormanni et al. 2017). NetSolP-1.0 calculates the solubility of a purified recombinant protein using a language model (Thumuluri et al. 2022). This web server indicates the solubility of protein by solubility and usability values. Aggregation is one of the main challenges in the manufacturing of therapeutic proteins. In the present study, the Aggrescan3D server predicted the potential aggregation of the modeled structures (Kuriata et al. 2019).


### Molecular docking
  To illustrate the ability of the BisAb and its scFv fragments to interact with TNF-α and IL-6R, molecular docking was performed by the HADDOCK web server (https://wenmr.science.uu.nl/), which is an information-driven flexible docking approach for modeling of bimolecular complexes (Honorato et al. 2021). Protein–protein complexes with lower HADDOCK scores and higher cluster sizes were selected for further analysis using LigPlot software (Wallace et al. 1995), signifying the amino acids involved in protein–protein interactions and the type of these interactions (hydrogen or hydrophobic bonds). The binding affinity of each protein complex was identified by the PRODIGY (PROtein binDIng enerGY prediction) server (Xue et al. 2016), and the outputs were presented as dissociation constant (KD) and binding affinity (ΔG). The protein–protein complexes with lower KD and ΔG values represent higher binding affinities.


### Molecular dynamics simulation
  Molecular dynamics simulation (MD) was done by the Gromacs package (v. 2022.7) (Astuti and Mutiara 2009) to check the stability of interactions between the selected modeled BisAb protein with TNF-α and IL-6R during the simulation time. The OPLSAA force field was applied for the MD simulation. The simulation box consists of a cubic box with a 1-nm distance from the protein complex, which was filled with SPCE water type. The simulation system was neutralized with Na+ and Cl− ions. The system was energy minimized by steepest descent algorithms with 500,000 steps and a minimized energy tolerance of 100 kJ/mol/nm. The particle mesh Ewald (PME) algorithm was used to calculate the electrostatic interaction with a 1.0-nm radius cut-off and a grid spacing of 0.16 nm. The system was equilibrated according to the constant volume and temperature (NVT) and NPT (constant pressure and temperature) protocols. The balanced equilibrated system enters the molecular dynamics simulation for 100 ns. The MD trajectory was analyzed by calculating root mean square deviation (RMSD), root mean square fluctuation (RMSF), and radius of gyration (Rg) plots (Abraham et al. 2015).


### Expression of bispecific antibody
  NcoI and HindIII restriction sites were incorporated at the 5′ and 3′ ends of the gene fragment encoding the selected bispecific antibody (BisAb). The designed construct was synthesized after E. coli codon optimization (Biomatik, Cambridge, ON, Canada) and subcloned into the pET28a expression vector (Novagen, Madison, WI, USA). The expression vector was confirmed by DNA sequencing and restriction digestion. E. coli BL21 (DE3) (Novagen, Madison, USA) cells were transformed with the expression vector. Recombinant colonies were cultivated in LB (Luria Bertani) broth medium supplemented with kanamycin at 37°C. Protein expression was induced with 0.5 mM IPTG (isopropyl β-d−1-thiogalactopyranoside) when the optical density (OD) of the medium reached 0.5 at 600 nm. Protein expression continued for a further 4 h and was evaluated using 12% sodium dodecyl sulfate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE).

  To optimize the expression level of r-BisAb protein, three important parameters, including incubation temperature, IPTG concentration, and post-induction time, were tested in 17 experiments using Design-Expert software (v.13) (Stat-Ease Inc., MN, USA) (Supplementary Table S1) through response surface methodology (RSM) in three levels (low, middle, and high). The expression level of BisAb was evaluated following densitometry analysis of the corresponding protein band on SDS-PAGE, and the optimal condition was selected.


### Expression of scFv proteins
  Specific primer pairs were designed based on the BisAb construct to amplify the anti-TNF-α and anti-IL-6R scFv genes harboring NcoI and XhoI restriction sites within F and R primers (Supplementary Table S2). The amplified gene fragments were double-digested with the two enzymes and separately subcloned into the digested pET28a expression vector. The recombinant vectors were transformed into E. coli BL21 (DE3) cells (Novagen, Madison, WI, USA) and expression of two scFv proteins was induced at 37 °C with 0.5 mM IPTG at OD600 nm of 0.5 and evaluated on 12% SDS-PAGE.


### Purification of BisAb and scFv proteins
  After large-scale expression (500 ml), purification of the recombinant proteins was performed. Primary studies revealed that all three recombinant antibody fragments are expressed as inclusion bodies (IBs). After centrifugation of culture medium at 6,000 rpm (20 min at 4 °C), the bacterial pellet was resuspended in lysis buffer (50 mM Tris, 300 mM NaCl, 1% Triton X-100, 1 mM ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA); pH 8.0) and incubated on ice for 1 h. The cells were sonicated through nine pulses (30-s sonication with the same rest intervals at 25% amplitude). The suspension was centrifuged at 6,000 rpm (20 min at 4 °C), and the pellet was washed twice with the washing buffer (50 mM Tris, 300 mM NaCl, 1 mM EDTA, 1% Triton X-100, 0.5 mM urea; pH 8.0). The pellet was dissolved in solubilization/equilibration buffer (50 mM Tris, 300 mM NaCl, 5 mM 2-mercaptoethanol (2-ME), 8 M urea; pH 8.0) and placed on ice for 1 h, then centrifuged at 10,000 rpm (30 min, 4 °C). The supernatant was loaded on the equilibrated nickel affinity column after filtration through 0.45-μm syringe filters. The column was washed (50 mM Tris, 300 mM NaCl, 8 M urea, 5 mM 2-ME, 30 mM imidazole; pH 8.0), and r-protein was eluted with the elution buffer (50 mM Tris, 300 mM NaCl, 5 mM 2-ME, 8 M urea, 250 mM imidazole; pH 8.0). The purity of the eluted protein was evaluated on 12% SDS-PAGE.

  The eluted proteins were dialyzed to reduce urea concentration. In brief, in the first step, proteins were dialyzed against buffer I (50 mM Tris 50, 300 mM NaCl, 6 M urea, 200 mM imidazole; pH 8.0), buffer II (50 mM Tris, 300 mM NaCl, 4 M urea, 150 mM imidazole; pH 8.0), and buffer III (50 mM Tris, 300 mM NaCl, 3 M urea, 100 mM imidazole; pH 8.0) for 24 h. Final protein refolding was done using an Econo-Pac 10DG column (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA) to remove residual imidazole and urea. Protein concentration of refolded proteins was measured using the BCA kit (Parstous, Mashhad, Iran).


### Evaluation of affinity towards TNF-α
  Beatty’s method evaluated the affinity of purified BisAb and Ada-scFv proteins against TNF-α protein (Beatty et al. 1987). In brief, two different concentrations of TNF-α (5 and 10 µg/ml) were coated in 96-well ELISA plates (4°C, 16 h). After blocking the wells with 1% bovine serum albumin (BSA) for 2 h, serial dilutions of purified BisAb and Ada-scFv proteins were added and incubated at room temperature (RT) for 2 h. Antibody/antigen binding was evaluated by using an HRP-conjugated anti-His antibody as the secondary antibody (1:750 dilution). The optical absorbance of the wells was measured at 450 nm. Commercially available adalimumab biosimilar antibody (anti-TNF-α antibody) (CinnoRA, CinnaGen, Tehran, Iran) was used as a positive control, and its secondary antibody was HRP-conjugated rabbit anti-human IgG. ### Inhibition of TNF-α cytotoxicity by MTT assay
  The L929 cells (mouse fibroblasts) (purchased from the Cell Bank Department of Pasteur Institute of Iran) were cultured in DMEM medium (BIO-IDEA, Tehran, Iran) (2 × 104 cells/well) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) within 96-well plates and incubated at 37 °C for 24 h. For the MTT assay, the purified BisAb and Ada-scFv proteins were first diluted in culture medium supplemented with 1 μg/ml actinomycin D (ActD) and 5 ng/ml TNF-α and incubated for 2 h (RT). The mixture was then added to the cells, and incubation continued for a further 24 h. Subsequently, the cells were treated with 3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)−2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide (MTT) reagent for 4 h, followed by overnight incubation with SDS reagent. The optical absorbance of the wells was measured at 550 nm, and EC50 (half maximal effective concentration) values of the antibody fragments were calculated. ActD/TNF-α-treated cells and cells treated with Cinnora® (CinnaGen, Tehran, Iran) were considered as controls.


### IL-6R binding assay
  IL-6R binding was evaluated through a cell-based ELISA assay. Briefly, 4 × 104 and 8 × 104 cells/ml of human monocytic THP1 cells (purchased from the Cell Bank Department of Pasteur Institute of Iran) were seeded in 96-well plates within RPMI 1640 culture medium (BIO-IDEA, Tehran, Iran) supplemented with 10% FBS and incubated at 37 °C for 24 h. Following the removal of the culture medium, the cells were fixed for 30 min (RT) using 8% formaldehyde and washed (three times) with PBS containing 0.1% Tween 20. Then, the wells were blocked by 2% BSA (2 h). The purified primary antibodies (BisAb and Toci-scFv) were serially diluted in PBS and added to the wells for 24 h at 4°C. After the washing step, HRP-conjugated anti-His antibody (1:750 dilution) was added to the wells for 1 h (RT). TMB (3,3′,5,5′-tetramethylbenzidine) substrate was added to the wells after the final washing step, and the optical densities were measured at 450 nm. Commercially available Tocilizumab biosimilar antibody (Temziva, AryoGen Pharmed, Tehran, Iran) was used as the positive control, where rabbit HRP-conjugated antibody against human IgG served as its secondary antibody. Kaff of tested antibodies was calculated using the above-mentioned Beatty’s equations.


### Assessment of IL-6 neutralizing effect
  1.5 × 106 cells/well of the THP1 cells were cultured in RPMI medium within 12-well plates supplemented with 10% FBS (24 h at 37 °C). The cells were then incubated with serum-free RPMI medium for a further 4 h, followed by recombinant antibody pretreatment at two different concentrations (20 and 80 µg/ml) for 24 h at 37°C. The cells were then treated with 10 ng/ml of IL-6 and incubated for 40 min at 37°C. The cell pellet of each experiment was resuspended in cold Pro-PRETM cell lysis solution (iNtRON Biotechnology, Seongnam-Si, Korea) after centrifugation at 1,100 rpm for 10 min and lysed using a homogenizer. After centrifugation at 13,000 rpm (15 min, 4 °C), 20 µg of each supernatant was subjected to 12% SDS-PAGE and then transferred to polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membrane (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA). The membrane was then blocked with 5% BSA (Sigma, Burlington, VT, USA) in Tris-buffered saline with 0.1% Tween (TBST) 20 for 1 h. The membranes were incubated with anti-STAT3 (Abcam, Cambridge, UK), anti-p-STAT3 (phospho Y705; Abcam, Cambridge, UK), and anti-β-actin (Abcam, Cambridge, UK) antibodies for 1 h (RT). After three washes with TBST buffer, the membranes were incubated with goat HRP conjugated anti-rabbit IgG H&L antibody (Abcam, Cambridge, UK) as the secondary antibody. Enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) was used as the developer reagent. Protein expression was normalized against β-actin protein. Densitometry of protein bands was performed using the gel analyzer software (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA). The percentage area under the curve of each band was divided by the percentage area under the curve of its corresponding β-actin band, and the calculated values were compared.


### Statistical analysis
  The obtained data is presented as the mean value of triplicate experiments with the standard deviation (SD) of means. The mean values were compared using the student’s t-test or one-way ANOVA. P values below 0.05 were considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses and figure creation were performed using GraphPad Prism software (v.8.0) (GraphPad Software, Boston, MA, USA).


## Results

### Designation of protein models
  To design the most convenient model for the expression of recombinant bispecific antibody, all possible conditions were assumed, and ultimately, eight different proteins were designed (Fig. 1).Fig. 1The schematic view of the designed proteins. Two peptide linkers, (G4S)3 or (G2S)4, were tested between VL and VH of each scFv molecule. The G4S tag has also been used as the constant linker between the two scFv fragments in all designed proteins

  Analysis of generated secondary structures by PSIPRED (Supplementary Fig. S1) and VADAR (Supplementary Table S3) did not represent significant alterations based on the location of the histidine tag, the type of peptide linker, and the order of VH and VL of the two scFv molecules.

  Alphafold2 outputs represented the correct folding of the two Ada- and Toci-scFvs in each designed model compared to the Fab structures of the two original mAbs (Supplementary Fig. S2), indicating that the observed different structures in the modeled proteins are due to the orientation and folding in the tertiary structures.

  Stereochemical quality assessments by Ramachandran plot analysis revealed the high quality of the proposed models. Alongside, the ERRAT values (around 95%) warranted the high resolution and quality of the models based on non-bonded interactions between different atom types. The calculated Verify3D values (> 80%) were associated with high compatibility of 1D and 3D structures of each model (Supplementary Table S4). The Molprobity scores revealed the best quality of coded proteins # 1 and 3 compared to the experimentally identified protein structures. The ProSA analysis showed nearly the same z-scores for all designed protein models, indicating moderately similar qualities (Supplementary Fig. S3).


### Physicochemical properties of designed proteins
  All designed proteins had almost the same physicochemical properties (Supplementary Table S5). According to the calculated instability indexes, all modeled polypeptides represented similar degrees of instability compared to adalimumab and tocilizumab monoclonal antibodies. The same pattern was also observed in the case of the aliphatic index, representing similar thermostability of the modeled proteins, lower than the original mAb molecules. The Gravy index of the models was also the same, indicating all designed proteins had hydrophilic characteristics. The estimated protein half-lives indicated different patterns within different expression systems, representing the mammalian cells as the ideal expression system for the expression of the designed BisAb protein.

### Molecular dynamics (MD) simulation
  The molecular dynamics (MD) simulation of modeled protein #1 during 100 ns showed a stable interaction with both IL-6R and TNF-α (Fig. 3). The root mean square deviation (RMSD) plot represented stable fluctuation of the protein structure during simulation, with an average value of 0.73 nm, indicating the effective interaction of this protein with IL-6R and TNF-α proteins (Fig. 3A). A slight reduction of the Rg value from 4.5 to 3.5 nm indicates the relaxation of this protein structure during simulation (Fig. 3B).Fig. 3Molecular dynamics of the selected modeled bispecific protein (Code #1) with IL-6R and TNF-α during 100 ns of simulation. A RMSD plot. B Rg plot. C RMSF plot

  The RMSF plot which represents the fluctuation of individual amino acids during simulation showed a low/slight fluctuation ranging from 0.1 to 0.2 nm, with an exception of IL-6R region covering 25–80 amino acids having an RMSF around 0.4 nm, which were not involved in binding to the BisAb (Fig. 3C). Alignment of protein complexes before and after MD simulation revealed no significant structural changes (Supplementary Fig. S4).

  The force field (FF)–based molecular mechanical/generalized born surface area (MM/GBSA) approach is a powerful tool for the prediction of the binding free energy within protein–protein complexes. The lower binding free energy values predict greater binding affinities. The calculated binding free energy of −60 kcal/mol for the selected BisAb protein model suggested an effective interaction with both IL-6R and TNF-α ligands (Supplementary Table S6). Besides, it was illustrated that the electrostatic potential is the strongest binding type energy contributing to the interaction of code #1 BisAb and the two antigens (IL-6R and TNF-α).

## Discussion
  Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a global public health challenge with increasing rates of age-standardized point prevalence and incidence that affects approximately 17.6 million people worldwide (Black et al. 2023). RA is a chronic autoimmune disease mediated by a complex network of inflammatory factors, including immune effector cells and cytokines, which result in inflammation and joint destruction (Zwerina et al. 2005). Due to this complexity, treatment approaches against a single target cannot successfully control the disease in RA patients. Therefore, combined blockage of key pro-inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α, IL-5, IL-6, and IL-13 may hopefully increase the efficacy of the treatment (Brzustewics and Bryl 2015); Woś and Tabarkiewics 2021).

  TNF-α is an important cytokine playing a pivotal role in the regulation of inflammatory reactions of RA, including synovial lesions and cartilage matrix degradation (Jang et al. 2021). Therefore, inhibiting its binding to the specific surface receptors could significantly reduce the severity of the inflammatory reactions of RA.

  IL-6 is a pleiotropic pro-inflammatory cytokine that plays an important role in several pathological conditions, including tumorigenesis, rheumatic diseases, autoimmune diseases, infections, and inflammatory disorders (Liu et al. 2022). Hence, it seems that IL-6, its surface receptor, and associated molecular pathways are ideal targets for undergoing therapeutic interventions.

  Despite the basic role of chemical therapeutic reagents such as methotrexate (MTX) in the treatment of rheumatoid arthritis, up to 30% of RA patients usually complain about its adverse effects, which result in its discontinuation. Consequently, there is a need to provide effective therapeutic alternatives for these patients. It has been documented that tocilizumab (TCZ) monotherapy outperforms MTX, adalimumab, or etanercept monotherapies in terms of efficacy (Gabay et al. 2013). In contrast, Bombardieri et al. (2007) demonstrated that adalimumab was effective in a large group of 899 moderate to severe RA patients who had experienced a failed treatment with infliximab, etanercept, or both TNF antagonists.

  The aim of designing bispecific antibodies was to target two different targets, including epitopes or antigens, and block different signaling pathways to overcome the limitations of monospecific targeted therapies in efficacy and cost (Kontermann 2012). With this approach, the first FDA (U.S. Food and Drug Administration)-approved bispecific antibody was blinatumomab, which targets CD19 and CD3 antigens in the treatment of acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL) (Burt et al. 2019). In the field of RA, there are several BisAbs in preclinical and clinical evaluations. In one preclinical study, Liu et al. (2014) designed a novel bispecific antibody (BisAb) for targeting both TNF-α and ED-B fibronectin in the collagen-induced arthritis (CIA) mouse model. This study demonstrated that the designed antibody could inhibit the progression of arthritis and reduce synovial inflammation. In another preclinical study, the efficacy of three different bispecific antibodies was investigated against IL-1β and IL-17A pro-inflammatory cytokines, in which the inflammation was effectively reduced (Li et al. 2014). Ozoralizumab is also a recently approved bispecific protein that contains two anti-TNF-α and one anti-human serum albumin nanobodies to enhance binding avidity towards TNF-α and neutralizing activity against this cytokine (Ishiwatari-Ogata et al. 2022).

  It has been well-documented that computational methodologies are valuable tools in the design of new therapeutic molecules through dynamics of antibody-antigen interactions by molecular dynamics simulation (Hummer et al. 2022). In the present study, eight different proteins were designed to evaluate the type and length of different peptide linkers and the orientation of VH and VL fragments of the two scFv antibodies. To study the interaction of modeled proteins with IL-6R and TNF-α, they were subjected to molecular docking, and the preferred model with the highest binding affinity and cluster size with the lowest KD and delta G values was selected for molecular dynamics simulation and cloning. Wang et al. (2019) reported two lead molecules, ZINC19701771 and ZINC06576501, as potential candidates for inhibition of human TNF-α and IL-6 receptors in in silico molecular simulation. In this approach, Aldossari et al. (2023) employed molecular docking studies to simulate the interaction between the candidate compounds (staurosporine and withanoside L) and the binding sites of TNF-α and IL-6 and showed that withanolide L could effectively interact with TNF-α and IL-6.

  In the present study, the results indicated that the correct folding of the two scFv proteins, originating from adalimumab and tocilizumab, was not affected by the histidine tag location, the type of peptide linker, and the order of VH and VL of these two scFv molecules. Taken together, based on calculated aggregation scores and higher affinity towards the two target proteins (IL-6R, TNF-α) obtained from molecular docking, the first modeled structure (Code #1) was selected for molecular dynamics simulation, representing effective and stable interactions with both TNF-α and IL-6R during 100 ns.

  The recombinant bispecific antibody (BisAb) was successfully expressed as cytoplasmic inclusion bodies (IBs) within E. coli cells, which were then purified using nickel affinity chromatography under denaturing conditions. The expression level of the recombinant BisAb was optimized using response surface methodology based on three vital parameters (37°C, 1 mM IPTG, and 2 h post-induction).

  In vitro functional studies demonstrated that the recombinant BisAb could effectively detect and bind to human TNF-α and IL-6R. The measured affinities were quite similar to the properties of each scFv moiety when expressed as single chains against TNF-α or IL-6R. The binding affinity (Kaff) of BisAb to TNF-α was 7.7 × 10–13 M, which was quite similar to that of Ada-scFv (9.4 × 10–13 M), suggesting the unchanged 3D conformation of the Ada-scFv moiety in the BisAb structure. On the other hand, the calculated Kaff for the biosimilar form of adalimumab (Cinnora) (7.5 × 10–15 M) was significantly higher than recombinant antibody fragments, which can be explained by its properties in simultaneous binding to two TNF-α molecules. These high affinities were associated with lower KD values calculated through bioinformatics studies for both BisAb (3 × 10–9 M) and adalimumab (2.3 × 10–9 M), indicating the reliability of in vitro studies in affinity measurements. These findings confirm the reported Kd values for adalimumab, ranging from 1.32 × 10 −10 M (Liao et al. 2021) to 5.9 × 10 −12 M (Daub et al. 2020).

  Regarding the affinity towards IL-6 receptor, the obtained Kaff values were again quite similar between BisAb and Toci-scFv (1.65 × 10 −12 M vs. 1.87 × 10 −12 M), which were in the range of the calculated Kaff of the biosimilar form of tocilizumab (7.13 × 10 −12 M) and greater than reported Kd values of this monoclonal antibody (Mihara et al. 2011) and those KD values calculated in bioinformatics studies, showcasing the variability between the in silico and in vitro studies.

  The designed BisAb could effectively rescue L929 cells from TNF-α-mediated cytotoxicity with an EC50 value fairly similar to its scFv moiety (Ada-scFv) (16.48 vs. 12.27 nM) and far from the parental mAb (0.6 nM). This increased EC50 concentration is likely due to the presence of only one anti-TNF binding site/arm in these recombinant proteins, resulting in reduced interactions and potential binding.

  The role of phosphorylated STAT3 in TNF-α and IL-6-mediated pathways and disease progression has been well documented (De Simone et al. 2015; Chung et al. 2017). Therefore, inhibition of STAT3 phosphorylation is a practical approach to determine the efficacy of TNF-α or IL-6 targeting molecules. Preincubation of THP-1 cells with BisAb, Toci-scFv, or the commercially available tocilizumab biosimilar (Temziva) before the addition of IL-6 could significantly reduce the phosphorylation rate of STAT3 induced by IL-6 in comparison to the cells treated with IL-6 alone.

  In conclusion, the present study highlights the ability of E. coli cells in the expression of potent bispecific antibodies against TNF-α protein and IL-6 receptor, similar to its scFv antibody moieties. Although we have shown that the recombinant antibody fragments could significantly detect the corresponding antigens and effectively neutralize their downstream pathways in cytotoxicity and STAT3 phosphorylation, more aspects should be studied to prove the therapeutic benefits of the simultaneous detection of these two important inflammatory cytokines. We would need to confirm the obtained results through more in vitro and in vivo experimental models before making such claims. The use of such BisAb molecules in inflammatory and infectious diseases may result in efficient activities toward the two distinct ligands, increased safety, and reduced healthcare costs.
