# Investigating the prognostic role of citrullination-related genes in breast cancer by combining transcriptomics, single-cell analysis and verification


## Abstract
  Citrullination, a process linked to autoimmune diseases, has been implicated in cancer, but its role in breast cancer (BRCA) remains unclear. This study aimed to identify citrullination-related genes (CRGs) associated with prognosis in BRCA. We utilized datasets from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA-BRCA) and Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO), pinpointing candidate genes by intersecting differentially expressed genes (DEGs) from TCGA-BRCA with known CRGs. Prognostic CRGs were selected using univariate Cox regression and Lasso regression analyses, and a predictive nomogram was created based on independent prognostic factors identified through multivariate Cox regression. Our findings revealed that SEZ6, S100B, SPIB, and TFF1 were key CRGs used to construct a risk model. High-risk BRCA patients exhibited immune-suppressive tumor microenvironments (TMEs) with low CD8+ T cell mutation and enrichment in C1/C4 immune subtypes. In contrast, low-risk patients displayed high immune infiltration and enrichment in C2/C3/C6 subtypes. High expression of S100B, SPIB, and TFF1 was associated with increased immune infiltration by NK cells and T cells, while high SEZ6 expression was linked to neutrophil mutations, PD-L1 upregulation, and low CD8+ T cell infiltration. Molecular docking studies explored the interactions of these CRGs with entinostat. In conclusion, SEZ6, S100B, SPIB, and TFF1 were identified as significant prognostic genes in BRCA, providing insights into BRCA pathogenesis and potential personalized treatment strategies.

## Introduction
  Breast cancer (BRCA) is a heterogeneous disease that includes activation of human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER 2, encoded by ERBB 2), activation or mutation of hormone receptors (estrogen receptor and progesterone receptor) [1,2]. Despite advances in early detection and treatment, the prognosis for advanced-stage BRCA remains poor, The incidence of BRCA increases with increased potentially modifiable risk factors, such as obesity, increased body mass index, increased age at menopause, and menopause [3,4]. Current therapeutic strategies, including surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and targeted therapy, often face limitations such as resistance to treatment and adverse side effects [5]. The molecular mechanisms of breast cancer involve multiple levels and factors of metabolic reprogramming, tumor microenvironment, gene mutations, protein changes and so on.

  Citrullination is a post-translational modification that is catalyzed by enzymes known as peptidylarginine deiminases (PADs) [6,7]. This process plays a significant role in regulating tumor progression, as well as autoimmune diseases and other related conditions [[8], [9], [10], [11], [12], [13], [14]]. Specifically, the promotion of tumor growth through citrullination is mediated by the enzymes PAD2 and PAD4 [8,[15], [16], [17], [18]]. Epigenetically, citrullination impacts histone-DNA and histone-histone interactions by reducing the positive charge on histones through the conversion of arginine to citrulline [18]. Integrating citrullination expression profiles with these bioelectronic read-outs could link transcriptional dysregulation to measurable changes in membrane potential and extracellular metabolite flux [19]. Metabolically, arginine, synthesized in the arginine-citrulline cycle, is crucial for the accelerated metabolism of BRCA cells and is linked to a poor prognosis, and this process involves histone citrullination, arginine deprivation strategy (arginine residue diamine catalysis to citrulline), etc [[20], [21], [22], [23], [24]]. Moreover, citrullination plays a crucial role in the tumor microenvironment, particularly in the formation of neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) [25,26]. The key enzyme responsible for citrullination also serves as a tumor suppressor, highlighting its importance in enabling the immune system to effectively inhibit tumor growth [27,28]. Nevertheless, there is a paucity of comprehensive research examining the correlation between citrullination-related genes (CRGs) and BRCA. Therefore, further research is needed to fully elucidate the role and molecular mechanisms of CRGs in BRCA.

  In this study, we aimed to identify and characterize CRGs associated with prognosis in BRCA. To achieve this, we utilized comprehensive datasets from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA-BRCA) and the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO). By intersecting differentially expressed genes (DEGs) from TCGA-BRCA with known CRGs, we pinpointed candidate genes for further analysis. We employed a combination of univariate Cox regression, Lasso regression, and machine learning methods to select prognostic CRGs. These genes were then used to create a predictive nomogram, which can help stratify patients into high-risk and low-risk groups based on their gene expression profiles. Furthermore, we explored the biological functions and immune correlations of these prognostic CRGs. Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data were utilized to identify key cell subsets involved in the tumor microenvironment (TME). Our findings revealed distinct immune landscapes between high-risk and low-risk patients, with high-risk patients exhibiting immune-suppressive TMEs and low mutation rates in CD8+ T cells. In contrast, low-risk patients displayed high immune infiltration and enrichment in specific immune subtypes. Additionally, we conducted molecular docking studies to explore the interactions between the identified CRGs and potential therapeutic agents, such as entinostat.

  This study provides a comprehensive analysis of citrullination-related genes in breast cancer, identifying SEZ6, S100B, SPIB, and TFF1 as significant prognostic biomarkers. Our findings not only enhance the understanding of BRCA pathogenesis but also offer insights into personalized treatment strategies, potentially improving patient outcomes.


## Materials and methods

### Differential expression analysis of CRGs in breast cancer
  In this study, breast cancer (BRCA) -related datasets were obtained from the Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database. Among them, the TCGA-BRCA training set contained a total of 1217 breast tissue samples, where 01A and 11A samples were included. A total of 1171 samples were retained, with BRCA: Control = 1072:99. Besides, 1050 of these samples with survival information were used for risk model construction. After that, GSE20685 (platform of GPL570) included 327 BRCA tumour tissue samples with survival information. The single-cell dataset GSE176078 (platform of GPL18573) consisted of 26 tumour tissue samples. These two datasets were obtained from GEO database. Furthermore, citrullination-related genes (CRGs) were obtained from the literature for this study [29]. We used “PAD2 and PAD4” as a search term in GeneCards (https://www.genecards.org/) to find related genes based on the standard of |log2 change (FC)|>2 and p.adj<0.05, a total of 291 genes were selected.

  Firstly, differential expression analysis was performed on TCGA-BRCA using the “DESeq2” R package (v 1.40.2.0) to identify differentially expressed genes between the cancer and control groups [30], based on |log2 Fold Change (FC)|> 1 and p.adj <0.05 criteria. A volcano was generated using the “pheatmap” R package (v 1.0.12). Subsequently, using CRGs as the background gene set with 271 CRGs. A heatmap was generated using the “pheatmap” R package (v 1.0.12) to visualize differentially expressed CRGs in BRCA. Based on 1050 samples with survival information in the training set, the CRGs with prognostic significance in BRCA were selected by the “survminer” R package (v 0.5.0) through univariate Cox analysis (HR ≠ 1, p-value <0.05). Subsequently, the associated of prognostic CRGs were further investigated using GeneMANIA where gene-gene interaction network (GGI) were generated.


### Functional analysis of CRGs
  To explore the regulation mechanism of prognostic CRGs, GSEA was conducted via “clusterProfiler” R package (v 4.8.3) with p.adj <0.05 as filter condition [31], where the GO (c5.go.Hs.symbols.gmt) and KEGG background gene set (c2.cp.kegg.v2023.1.Hs.symbols) was derived from the GSEA database. Subsequently, the biological functions and signaling pathways of prognostic CRGs were further explored through the “cluster-Profiler” R package (v 4.8.3) (p-value <0.05) [31], based on GO and KEGG database.


### Identification of key prognostic CRGs signature in BRCA
  To find key genes, the data of TCGA-BRCA with prognostic CRGs was trained by least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) using “glmnet” R package (v 4.1–8), multivariate COX (HR ≠ 1, p-value <0.01) was further employed to screen for prognostic genes. Subsequently, Forest plot visualizes the risk ratio (Hazard Ratio, HR) and its 95 % confidence interval (Confidence Interval, CI) of prognostic CRGs signature in BRCA. Afterwards, both univariate and multivariate COX regression analyses were conducted to identify potential independent prognostic factors (HR ≠ 1, p-value <0.05). Based on this result, we constructed a nomogram to predict patient survival through the “rms” R package (v 6.7.1) [32].


### Construction and evaluation of the nomogram containing key CRGs signature in BRCA
  The effectiveness of the key CRGs-based risk models was substantiated through additional analyses. Patients were stratified into high-risk and low-risk categories based on their risk scores, and the model's performance was evaluated in an independent cohort from TCGA-BRCA using ROC curves, Kaplan-Meier curves, and risk curves. The model was also validated in the GSE20685 dataset. The effectiveness of the risk model was evaluated by plotting a ROC curve via “survivalROC” R package (v 1.0.3.1) [33]. The ssGSEA algorithm of “GSVA” R package (v 1.42.0) was employed to calculate the scores of these genes in training set of 1050 samples with survival information [34], and these samples were stratified into high and low score groups. The survival differences between the score groups were then explored using Kaplan–Meier curves. Meanwhile, the survival differences between the different risk groups and the survival status of the patients were further explained by the risk curve, respectively. The accuracy and validity of the nomogram were then reflected in the calibration curve and ROC curve, respectively.


### Regulatory network analysis of key prognostic CRGs in BRCA
  Subsequently, functions associated with the key prognostic CRGes were further investigated using STRING database, where Protein-Protein Interaction (PPI) of the key prognostic CRGs in BRCA were generated. The miRDB database was employed to predict the miRNA (Target score >60). After that, lncRNAs corresponding to miRNA were also mined in ENCORI with clipExpNum >30 as condition. Finally, the lncRNA-miRNA-prognostic gene network was constructed.


### Tumor microenvironment analysis of key prognostic CRGs in BRCA
  In this study, we employed the Single-Sample Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (ssGSEA) algorithm from the “GSVA” R package (version 1.48.3) to calculate the scores of 28 immune cell types for each sample and compared their differences among various risk groups [34]. Subsequently, we used the “psych” R package (version 2.3.9) to explore the correlations between differentially expressed immune cells and between these immune cells and prognostic genes [35]. Additionally, we calculated ImmuneScore, StromalScore, and EstimateScore for different risk groups using the “estimate” R package (version 1.0.13) [36], The differences in these scores between risk groups were compared using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. The TISIDB database provides a comprehensive resource for studying the prognostic CRGs interplay between tumors and the immune system.


### Single-cell analysis of key prognostic CRGs in BRCA
  Based on the expression matrix of 26 BRCA samples from a single cell, the data were filtered by the “Seurat” R package (v 5.0.0), which removed clusters with less than 3 cells and cells with less than 200 genes. Also, cells with nFeature_RNA ≧ 7000, nCount_RNA >50000, and percent.mt > 10 % were removed. Besides, the genes with relatively large coefficients of variation between cells were extracted using the vst method after normalising the data. After principal component analysis (PCA) analysis, the appropriate principal components (PCs) were selected. Meanwhile, the cells were applied for unsupervised cluster analysis via FindNeighbors and FindClusters functions, which then were classified into different cell clusters by uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP) method. Based on this result, the cell clusters were annotated into different cell types according to Refs. [[37], [38], [39]] and CellMarker 2.0. Afterwards, prognostic genes expression in different cell types was visualised, where cells with higher expression levels of all prognostic genes were defined as critical cells. Moreover, cellular communication and interactions were analyzed using the “CellChat” R package (v 1.6.1) [40]. Eventually, the “Monocle” R package (v 2.30.0) was used to perform pseudo-temporal analysis on key cells to visualize the differentiation trajectories of key cells.


### Expression validation of key prognostic CRGs in BRCA
  In this study, the expression levels of key prognostic CRGs were further validated via quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR). Specifically, a total of 5 pairs samples from the First Affiliated Hospital of University of South China were utilized for RT-qPCR, including 5 BRCA samples and 5 normal samples. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of University of South China. All patients had signed an informed consent form.

  First, the total RNA of the frozen sample was extracted a by TRIzol reagent (Ambion, China). Then, an equal amount of mRNA was reverse transcribed to synthesise cDNA. Next, the qPCR reaction was performed with 2 x Universal Blue SYBR Green qPCR Master Mix kit (Servicebio, China) according to the reaction system. Finally, the gene expression level was calculated by the 2-ΔΔCT method. Primer sequences for the genes can be seen in Supplementary Table seq.xlsx

  To elucidate the functional roles of the key CRGs in BRCA, we incorporated data from The Human Protein Atlas, Enter the name of the protein (e.g., SEZ6, S100B, SPIB, and TFF) in the search bar click“pathology”, Select the “Breast cancer” column under “Protein expression”, Scroll through the available IHC images. The HPA is a valuable resource that provides comprehensive proteomic information, including subcellular localization, tissue-specific expression patterns, and protein-protein interactions [41].


### Molecular docking of key prognostic CRGs in BRCA
  Utilizing the “pRRophetic” R package (version 0.5), we conducted a drug sensitivity analysis on CRGs that have prognostic significance. For this purpose, we downloaded the necessary drug files from PubChem and procured molecular files for the prognostic CRGs from RCSB. Furthermore, molecular docking was performed using the CADD (Computer-Aided Drug Design) platform from LabShare to analyze the interactions between the drugs and the target molecules.


### Statistical analysis
  In this study, all bioinformatics analyses were conducted using R software (v4.2.2). A p-value of less than 0.05 was deemed to indicate a significant difference (∗p < 0.05; ∗∗p < 0.01; ∗∗∗p < 0.001).


## Results

### The identification of CRGs in BRCA
  In the differential expression analysis, we screened 1262 genes from TCGA-BRCA, including 688 up-regulated and 574 down-regulated genes (TCGA-BRCA-DIFF) (Fig. 1A). We intersected 291 citrullination-related genes (CRGs) with the differentially expressed genes from the TCGA-BRCA dataset, identifying a total of 86 genes. This subset included 55 upregulated and 31 downregulated genes. To illustrate the expression dynamics of these genes, we generated a heatmap (Fig. 1B) that depicts the top 50 genes with the highest log fold change (logFC) and the bottom 50 genes with the lowest logFC. In the univariate Cox regression analysis, we initially identified 10 CRGs associated with BRCA. A forest plot was utilized to visualize the hazard ratios (HR) and their corresponding 95 % confidence intervals (CI), along with the associated p-values, for each of these CRGs in relation to prognosis. The plot reveals that genes ACTG2, S100B, SEZ6, SFRP1, SLC27A2, and TFF1 have hazard ratios less than 1, which are associated with a poorer prognosis. Conversely, genes B4GALNT2, POU3F2, SPIB, and XKR7 exhibit hazard ratios greater than 1, suggesting a better prognosis. ### Functional analysis of CRGs in BRCA
  In the Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis, 10 CRGs associated with BRCA were enriched to a total of 335 functional entries, which were categorized into three main domains: 21 Cellular Components (CC), 56 Molecular Functions (MF), and 258 Biological Processes (BP), of which the top 30 entries included modulation of chemical synaptic transmission, positive regulation of secretion, and so on (Fig. 2A). In addition, the candidate genes were involved in a total of 15 signaling pathways, including neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction, estrogen signaling pathway and so on (Fig. 2B). To further elucidate the molecular distinctions between high-risk and low-risk breast cancer (BRCA) patients based on the classification of citrullination-related genes (CRGs), we performed comprehensive Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analyses. These analyses uncovered distinct functional and pathway enrichments that characterize the two risk groups. The high-risk group exhibited significant enrichment in RNA splicing and spliceosomal complex organization, as well as pathways associated with autoimmune diseases. In contrast, the low-risk group showed strong enrichment in adaptive immune response, immune cell signaling, and immune complex formation. These findings suggest that the high-risk group may be characterized by dysregulated RNA processing mechanisms, while the low-risk group is distinguished by a more robust immune response. These results provide valuable insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying the differences in prognosis between these two groups and highlight potential therapeutic targets for personalized treatment strategies (Fig. 2C and D).Fig. 2Functional enrichment analysis of CRGs in BRCA. ### SEZ6, S100B, SPIB, and TFF1 were applied for constructing risk model
  To furtherly identify key prognostic CRGs, we employed Lasso regression method and pinpointed nine genes—SEZ6, S100B, SPIB, TFF1, SFRP1, PADI3, B4GALNT2, PCDH10, and NCCRP1—as the most significant predictors of patient outcomes (Fig. 3A). Further analysis using multivariate analysis refined this list to SEZ6, S100B, SPIB, and TFF1 (Fig. 3B). We then constructed a risk score formula based on CRGs: riskScore = (0.224 × SEZ6) + (−0.164 × S100B) + (−0.246 × SPIB) + (−0.1 × TFF1). After that, we incorporated five clinical features along with the riskScore into the univariate Cox analysis of BRCA samples from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA-BRCA) dataset (HR ≠ 1, p-value <0.05). The results indicated that stage, M, N, riskScore, Age, and T were all significant prognostic factors (all p-values <0.05). Specifically, the hazard ratio (HR) for stage was 2.13 (95 % CI: 1.686–2.69), for M it was 6.425 (95 % CI: 3.608–11.44), for N it was 1.644 (95 % CI: 1.372–1.972), for riskScore it was 2.606 (95 % CI: 1.818–3.736), for Age it was 1.035 (95 % CI: 1.021–1.05), and for T it was 1.563 (95 % CI: 1.263–1.935) (Fig. 3C). In multivariate Cox analyses, age (p-value: 2.893e-05, HR: 1.032, 95 % confidence interval (CI): 1.017–1.047), M (p-value: 0.002836, HR: 2.824, 95 % CI: 1.428–5.584), N (p-value: 0.0001042, HR: 1.492, 95 % CI: 1.219–1.825), and riskScore (p-value: 1.184e-05, HR: 2.346, 95 % CI: 1.602–3.436) emerged as independent prognostic factors for BRCA (Fig. 3D). Ultimately, we developed a nomogram based on CRGs in BRCA to predict the 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year survival rates for individual patients (Fig. 3E). ### Construction and evaluation of the nomogram containing key CRGs signature in BRCA
  We used the TCGA-BRCA data set as the training group (Fig. 4A) and GSE20685 as the validation group (Fig. 4B). The area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves (AUC) for all analyses surpassed 0.6, signifying a commendable level of discriminatory power. The Kaplan-Meier survival analysis demonstrated a notably poorer prognosis for patients classified in the high-risk group as opposed to those in the low-risk group. This observation was corroborated by the risk curve, which showed a higher incidence of mortality among high-risk patients compared to their low-risk counterparts. These findings collectively highlight the dependability and precision of the risk model in forecasting patient outcomes. The Kaplan-Meier survival analysis revealed a significantly poorer prognosis for patients with high SEZ6 expression compared to those in the low-expression group. Conversely, for S100B, SPIB, and TFF1, the results indicated better outcomes for the high-expression groups (Fig. 4C). These findings are consistent with the risk scores assigned to each patient. In terms of calibration, the slope of the calibration curve was approximately 1, and the AUC value of the ROC curve was above 0.7 (Fig. 4D). These metrics indicate the high accuracy and efficacy of the nomogram in making prognostic predictions. ### Expression of SEZ6, S100B, SPIB, and TFF1
  In this study, we further validated the expression of prognostic genes. ## Discussion
  The discovery of citrullination's involvement in BRCA provides a novel perspective on the disease's molecular landscape and treatment strategy [45]. The current understanding of breast cancer includes its biology, biomarkers, and treatment methods such as antibody-drug conjugate systems, nanoparticles, and immunotherapies targeting breast cancer stem cells [46,47]. While anti-Peptidyl arginine deiminase 4(PADI4) has been found to inhibit BRCA by suppressing the citrullination of fibronectin in the tumor microenvironment [48]. This process is significant as it modulates the tumor's supportive structure and can potentially halt cancer progression [16]. Additionally, the citrullination mediated by PAD2 has been identified to block the widely prevalent Wnt/-catenin signaling pathway in malignant tumors, which is a critical mechanism in cancer development and spread [49]. Based on this, this study combined RNA-seq and scRNA-seq analysis to identify four prognostic genes related to citrullinination in BRCA: SEZ6, S100B, SPIB, TFF1, and constructed a prognostic risk model of BRCA, which can effectively predict the prognosis of BRCA patients. Meanwhile, we constructed a nomogram containing clinical information and prognostic score to extend the clinical value of this model and verify it in the calibration curve and ROC curve. Finally, T cells were identified as key cells, enabling a more comprehensive understanding of the complexity of the tumor microenvironment. Therefore, these studies suggest that prognostic models based on CRGs may have potential prognostic significance in BRCA and could provide theoretical guidance for personalized treatment of patients.

  The present research has delineated a complex interplay among genes in BRCA, specifically focusing on four prognostic genes related to citrullination: SEZ6, S100B, SPIB, and TFF1. They influence various biological pathways, including cell proliferation and apoptosis, cellular differentiation, tumorigenesis and tumor progression, neurodevelopment, function, metabolic regulation, and immune responses. For instance, SEZ6, associated with neurological disorders, may influence BRCA through its effects on synaptic function and cell proliferation [50,51]. Similarly, S100B, a calcium-binding protein, is often overexpressed in cancers and could contribute to BRCA by modulating cell growth and metabolism [52,53]. SPIB, a transcription factor, might regulate genes involved in cell adhesion and migration, thus affecting BRCA progression [54,55]. TFF1, known for its role in gastrointestinal repair, could be linked to BRCA through its interactions with the tumor microenvironment [56,57]. For example, studies have shown that the circular RNA has-circ-0061825 (Circ-TFF 1) mediates BRCA progression by targeting the miR-326/TFF 1 axis [58]. Furthermore, the RUNX transcriptional co-regulator core binding factor β (CBF β) inhibited the migration of estrogen receptor-positive BRCA cells by inhibiting the estrogen receptor α-mediated expression of the migration factor TFF1 [59]. The dysregulation of these genes might disrupt normal cellular processes, leading to uncontrolled cell growth and tumorigenesis in BRCA. However, further research is needed to fully elucidate their mechanisms in BRCA and their clinical utility.

  In this study, GSEA analysis has identified key KEGG pathways significantly enriched in the low risk group, particularly the “cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction”, “T cell receptor signaling pathway” and “chemokine signaling pathway”, which are crucial for intermolecular communication in the tumor microenvironment. Existing studies have focused on the role of cytokines and chemokines in cancer therapy, emphasizing the challenges and potential of using these molecules for treatment [60]. For example, the oleanolic acid 4T1-induced mouse BRCA model suppresses tumor progression by regulating lactic acid bacteria through the cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction pathway [61]. CCL 20 activates the differentiation of granulocytic-monocytic progenitors (GMPs) through its receptor C-C motif chemokine receptor 6 (CCR 6), leading to the expansion of PMN-MDSCs to promote breast cancer cell stemness [62]. T cells, comprising 41.9 % of all cells, play a central role in these interactions, with macrophages and neutrophils also exhibiting strong interactions, suggesting that citrullination-related genes can influence macrophages and T cells in the immune microenvironment [[63], [64], [65]]. For instance, tumor-associated macrophages promote epithelial-stromal transformation and tumor stem cell properties in triple-negative breast cancer via the CCL 2/AKT/β -catenin signaling pathway [66]. These pathways’ enrichment correlates with the proportion of specific cell subsets in cancer samples, highlighting distinct gene expression patterns of T cells and tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) in the tumor microenvironment [[67], [68], [69]]. Elucidating the regulatory mechanisms of these pathways could enhance our understanding of BRCA and potentially uncover shared pathobiological processes across other diseases.

  The scRNA-seq analysis showed that T cells was the most proportion (41.9 %), and all four prognostic genes were expressed in T cells, so they were defined as key cells. T cells are important immune cells to enhance antitumor immunity, and inhibiting the glycolysis of CD8 + T cells can promote the formation of long-term memory and antitumor function of CD8 + T cells [70]. The nest-losing apoptosis gene TJP 3 promotes chemoresistance, cell migration and immune escape in BRCA T cells by regulating epithelial mal transformation and PD-L1 expression [71]. Aiduqing formula Can inhibit BRCA metastasis by inhibiting regulatory T cell differentiation and infiltration induced by tumor-associated macrophages (TAM)/chemokine CXCL1 [72]. Therefore, these data can reveal the diversity of immune cells and their functional status around the tumor, help to understand the mechanism of immune escape and tumor response to immunotherapy and better understand the complexity of the tumor, laying the foundation for the realization of BRCA precision medicine.

  Based on the close association between CRGs expression and metabolic reprogramming, nanoscale electrochemical biosensors can be developed to intraoperatively monitor metabolic changes in the tumor microenvironment in real time [19,73]. Single-cell electrical analysis techniques such as microfluidic impedance flow cytometry can elucidate the regulation of CRGs on immune cell membrane capacitance and potential [74], Meanwhile, atomic force microscopy-conductance measurements can visually characterize the effects of CRG-mediated epigenetic modifications on surface charge distribution at the single-cell level [75]. Establishing a correlation between CRGs expression profiles and electrophysiological characteristics to construct a complete technical chain of intraoperative real-time diagnosis - personalized scheme optimization - therapeutic efficacy closed-loop monitoring, thereby providing new bioelectronics support for precision diagnosis and treatment of breast cancer.

  The study has identified citrullination-related prognostic genes in BRCA and explored their underlying mechanisms, offering new insights into disease pathology. These findings pave the way for future research, including RT-qPCR, gene knockouts, and cellular assays, to confirm the clinical application of these genes. Several limitations should be acknowledged in our current investigation. Our findings may be constrained by sample size and diversity limitations, necessitating validation through larger-scale, multi-center prospective cohorts to confirm their applicability across diverse populations. While we have identified relevant genes, their specific functional mechanisms in breast cancer remain incompletely characterized, with a notable absence of direct evidence from in vitro and in vivo functional experiments. The translation from gene discovery to clinical application presents significant challenges, requiring the development of reliable detection methods and standardized clinical evaluation protocols. Additionally, we lack longitudinal data on the dynamic expression changes of these genes during disease progression and treatment. Furthermore, the interactions between these citrullination-related genes and other established breast cancer driver genes and signaling pathways remain inadequately explored. Although potential therapeutic targets have been identified, concrete treatment strategies and specific drug development pathways targeting these molecules have not yet been established. Finally, the stability of these genes as biomarkers requires further evaluation across different detection platforms and varying sample processing conditions to ensure their reliability in clinical settings. We acknowledge the limitations of our study, but we are committed to continuing our investigation into the roles of these genes in BRCA. Our work sets the foundation for potential therapeutic targets and diagnostic markers in the fight against BRCA.


## Conclusions
  A total of 4 genes (SEZ6, S100B, SPIB, and TFF1) associated with CRGs in BRCA were identified as prognostic genes, which provided a basis for understanding BRCA pathogenesis.
