# The ferroptosis-related gene MAFG screened by machine learning is associated with the diagnosis and prognosis of sepsis


## Supplementary Information
  The online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s10238-025-01880-z.


## Introduction
  Sepsis is a systemic inflammatory response syndrome caused by an infection that poses a serious threat to the lives of patients, particularly critically ill patients in the ICU, and is responsible for 19.7% of all fatalities globally [1]. However, only a small proportion (8.3%) of them was considered potentially preventable [2]. Consequently, there is an urgent clinical need to identify reliable sepsis biomarkers to improve diagnostic and prognostic accuracy. In patients with sepsis, dysregulated activation of the coagulation system leads to the progressive formation and accumulation of thrombus, which is associated with disseminated intravascular coagulation [3], and immunosuppression and excessive inflammation are both regarded as significant factors in sepsis mortality [4]. A meta-analysis suggested that in high-income countries, the incidence of sepsis reached 437 per 100,000 person-years, compared with 270 per 100,000 person-years in severe sepsis [5]. Sepsis involves complicated pathophysiological processes and gene polymorphisms, but the exact cause has remained indistinct. Therefore, in order to reduce the burden of disease, it is vital to explore the mechanism of sepsis associated with survival prognosis. Platelet activation is an important process in the occurrence of DIC. Promoting the death of platelets can effectively inhibit platelet activation, thus alleviating DIC. Studies have shown that platelets can undergo ferroptosis through ROS-induced lipid peroxidation in addition to the classical mitochondrial apoptosis pathway [6, 7]. Furthermore, existing studies have confirmed that immune cells can both induce ferroptosis and cause it. Therefore, ferroptosis can affect the immune response and its imbalance in diseases [8]. Based on the above findings, this study aims to explore the important role of ferroptosis in the progression of sepsis and its correlation with the survival prognosis of patients with sepsis.

  Ferroptosis is a novel form of iron-dependent programmed cell death that is closely related to membrane lipid peroxidation and decreased glutathione peroxidase GPX4 expression [9]. Ferrous ions are involved in inducing the formation of reactive oxygen species (ROS). Peroxidation products of polyunsaturated fatty acids cause damage to cell membranes, while GPX4 can eliminate peroxides through GSH, protecting cell membranes from destruction, and thereby inhibiting ferroptosis [10]. There are various ferroptosis-related genes (FRGs) that play a critical role in multiple diseases, including sepsis. For instance, ferritinophagy-mediated ferroptosis participates in sepsis-induced cardiac injury [11]. Studies showed that METTL3-induced m6A modification of GPX4 could activate ferroptosis and regulate sepsis-associated acute lung injury [12], which was confirmed to be relieved by the modulation of NRF2 and ATF3 [13]. The expression of ferroptosis-related gene GPX4 is upregulated in patients with sepsis [14], and activated ferroptosis can enhance sepsis-mediated tissue damage, organ dysfunction and death [15]. The expression of NRF2 in sepsis patients with poor prognosis is usually low. On the one hand, when NRF2 activity is low, the release of very low density lipoprotein (VLDL) is reduced, the protective effect on organs is weakened, and the organ damage is aggravated [16]. On the other hand, NRF2 is an important transcription factor that can alleviate ROS and ROS-induced ferroptosis, maintain redox balance, and play an antioxidant protective role in sepsis [17]. Therefore, the increased ferroptosis caused by the dysregulation of ferroptosis-related genes may be one of the important reasons for the poor prognosis of patients with sepsis. Additionally, this process involves different signaling pathways and the release of large amounts of cytokines and chemokines that trigger an immune response [18]. Neutrophils and macrophages are firmly associated with the progression of ferroptosis, such as macrophage extracellular traps, which potentially exacerbate ferroptosis-related hepatic ischemia/reperfusion injury [19]. The polarization of macrophages and the release of chemokines by inflammatory immune cells such as neutrophils are important mechanisms for the occurrence and progression of sepsis [20]. Ferroptosis can affect the immune landscape of diseases by regulating the recruitment and polarization of macrophages and neutrophil infiltration [18]. It can be seen that ferroptosis plays an important role in immune dysregulation and the progression of sepsis.

  Machine learning has become a technical tool widely used in the diagnosis or prognosis of diseases [21]. We can classify the molecular subtypes of sepsis more effectively and accurately [22], as well as predict the severity of sepsis at the onset of the first clinical symptoms [23]. Machine learning models demonstrate significant advantages in sepsis, a disease characterized by high heterogeneity and complex gene interactions. Algorithms like random forest can identify nonlinear gene interactions through collaborative decision trees, while unsupervised methods reveal clinically undefined subtypes in sepsis patients. Additionally, machine learning enables feature importance ranking to help pinpoint potential therapeutic targets. These highlight the power of machine learning in deciphering sepsis pathogenesis and advancing precision medicine. Nevertheless, current research lacks integration between ferroptosis studies and patient-derived transcriptomics. To address this gap, we employed bulk RNA-sequencing (bulk RNA-seq) and single-cell RNA-sequencing (scRNA-seq) analyses to systematically investigate hub FRGs in sepsis. We constructed a random forest model to identify hub FRGs that play a role in sepsis diagnosis and screened by survival analysis to obtain FRGs associated with the prognosis of sepsis. The random forest model was applied to reduce dimensionality and improve prediction stability, and scRNA-seq was leveraged to resolve cell-type-specific expression patterns of FRGs identified in bulk data. Meanwhile, the targets of hub FRG were predicted by databases, and external datasets and experiments were used to verify their expression alteration. The identification of ferroptosis-related prognostic targets in this study facilitates the innovation and clinical translation of precision medicine for sepsis. Our research may highlight MAFG as a novel potential target for diagnostic and therapeutic approaches in sepsis.


## Materials and methods

### Data collection and preprocessing
  We downloaded transcriptional RNA expression profiling from the Gene Expression Omnibus database (GEO) (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) for analysis. GSE65682 was performed as the training set, GSE75065 as the validation set for sepsis diagnosis, and GSE185263 as the validation set for sepsis prognosis. In this study, pediatric samples, non-whole blood samples, and datasets with too small a sample size (samples < 50) were excluded from the selection session. GSE65682 contained a total of 802 samples, with 479 septic patients remaining and 42 healthy controls after excluding unidentified samples. GSE57065 consisted of 28 septic patients and 25 healthy controls. GSE185263 consisted of 348 septic patients and 44 healthy controls. Both GSE65682 and GSE185263 were clinical data sources for survival analysis. We normalized the microarray sequencing data to enable comparability between the two groups. Probes with missing gene symbols were removed. Prior to formal analysis, we performed batch effect correction on the data using the normalizeBetweenArrays function from the limma package to minimize its impact on the research findings.


### Screening the differentially expressed FRGs between sepsis and control
  The 621 ferroptosis-related genes (FRGs) were acquired from the FerrDb V2 database (http://www.zhounan.org/ferrdb/current/), including 264 drivers, 238 suppressors, 9 markers, and 110 unclassified genes, for a total of 564 unduplicated FRGs [24]. These FRGs from FerrDb were selected for subsequent analysis because they are regulatory factors to play roles in ferroptosis as evidenced by prior literature, with curated relevance or biological roles in ferroptosis. We conducted differential analysis of the data between the sepsis and control for GSE65682 via the limma package in R software (v 4.2.1) to obtain differentially expressed genes (DEGs), applying thresholds of p.adj < 0.05 and |log2FC|> 1, which were subsequently intersected with 564 unduplicated FRGs to filter differentially expressed FRGs. The Venn diagram allows the intersection of the two parts to be observed [25]. We can observe the upregulated and downregulated changes of the FRGs by using the heatmap and volcano map.


### Functional enrichment analyses and protein–protein interaction (PPI) network analysis of differentially expressed FRGs
  Gene ontology (GO) analysis and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis revealed the enriched signaling pathways of the genes and their associated regulatory molecules by clusterProfiler package. GO terms comprise biological process (BP), cellular component (CC), and molecular function (MF). PPI plays a role in observing the complex interaction of proteins with each other. The FRGs were uploaded to the STRING website (www.string-db.org/) to predict the contact between genes, and the minimum interaction score was set to a low confidence level of 0.15. The PPI network was plotted by Cytoscape software (v.3.9.0), using betweenness centrality (BC) as a measure of relative importance, which demonstrates the interconnections and significance ranking within the differentially expressed FRGs.


### Immune infiltration analysis
  We visualized and compared the relative proportions of 22 immune cells in the sepsis and control groups, and in the survivor and non-survivor groups of septic patients, using the CIBERSORT algorithm. Differential expressions of immune cells between comparative groups could be remarked. By Spearman correlation analysis, it is possible to quantify whether there is a relationship between key FRGs and immune cells and the intensity of the correlation.


### Construction of random forest model to identify hub FRGs
  Several studies have shown the application of machine learning models to predict diseases with FRGs, such as ulcerative colitis [26]. Based on the FRGs yielded from the previous analysis, GSE65682 was randomly divided into a training set and a testing set, consisting of 312 and 209 samples, respectively. The training set is intended to construct a random forest model for prediction, and the testing set is applied to verify the accuracy and robustness of the model. A series of indicators, including accuracy, balanced accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and Kappa are calculated to assess the excellence of the model’s prediction effectiveness. After determining the parameters (mtry = 3, ntree = 500), the diagnostic performance of the model can be informed by the classification ROC. Meanwhile, the most important genes are obtained according to the MeanDecreaseGini, and the top five hub FRGs are selected for validation with GSE57065.


### Survival analysis
  We performed univariate Cox proportional hazards regression on the survival data of GSE65682 and obtained differentially expressed prognosis-related FRGs by taking the intersection of screened genes with differentially expressed FRGs. This was followed by a log-rank test, and Kaplan–Meier survival curves were plotted. The remaining hub FRGs were validated for their association with sepsis prognosis by GSE185263, illustrating the predictive performance of FRGs that were statistically significant between the survivor and non-survivor groups by ROC.


### Nomogram establishment
  The clinical data of GSE65682 such as gender, age, and FRGs gained from the survival analysis were used as variables of the nomogram to observe the contribution of different features on the 7-day and 14-day survival probability of sepsis by the rms package. Each variable value is assigned a score, and the total score of all scores summed for a given patient corresponds to the 7-day and 14-day survival probability. The calibration curve reflects how well the predicted condition fits the actual condition and enables evaluation of the model’s accuracy.


### Target gene prediction of the transcription factor
  We used GTRD, ChIP-Atlas, and GTEx database websites to predict the target genes of the transcription factor MAFG, and then intersected them with differentially expressed FRGs in GSE65682 and verified the expression differences of the screened genes in normal and sepsis samples by RT-qPCR. Meanwhile, the interaction network between MAFG and its target genes in GTRD and ChIP-Atlas databases was presented via TF Target Finder software (https://jingle.shinyapps.io/TF_Target_Finder/), according to the criteria of p-value < 0.05 and |log2FC|> 1.


### Analysis of scRNA-seq data of sepsis
  We analyzed scRNA-seq data from GSE175453 containing samples from five healthy participants and four sepsis patients using the Seurat package in R software. For doublets removal and quality control, we filtered low-quality cells based on the criteria of nFeature_RNA > 200, nFeature_RNA < 6000, and nCount_RNA > 1000, percent.mt < 10%, and percent.HB < 3%. This was followed by normalization, the selection of 2000 VariableFeatures, and the standardization of gene expression. The data integration and correction of batch effects were performed by Harmony. Furthermore, we used UMAP to cluster and visualize the data in the high-dimensional space for dimensionality reduction, and then manually annotated the clusters with the markers corresponding to the specific cell type previously reported in the literature. We ultimately annotated monocytes (CD14, LYZ, S100A9), CD4 + T cells (CD3D, CD4), NK cells (KLRC1, FCGR3A, GNLY), platelets (ITGA2B, GP1BA), macrophages (CD68, CD163), CD8 + T cells (CD3D, CD8A), NKT cells (CD3, NKG7), B cells (CD79A, MS4A1), neutrophils (MPO, CD66B), and plasma cells (TNFRSF17, SDC1). Using the FindAllMarkers function and setting min.pct = 0.1, logfc.threshold = 0.25, we obtained all the marker genes for each cluster.


#### Predicting potential functions of MAFG through virtual knockout
  We utilized the R package scTenifoldKnk to analyze the single-cell dataset GSE175453 for predicting potential gene functions through virtual knockout [27]. This method enables prediction of whole-genome expression profile changes when a specific gene is knocked down in a cell population. First, we constructed a denoised gene regulatory network from the sepsis sample expression matrix, and we set the regulatory edges corresponding to the target KO gene MAFG to zero to generate a simulated knockout network. Next, we employed manifold alignment to embed both the original network and the simulated KO network into a latent space. By calculating expression differences of genes between the two networks, we identified differentially regulated genes sensitive to the knockout. Finally, we performed GO enrichment analysis on these genes to infer potential functions of MAFG based on the results.


#### Sample collection
  We collected 10 whole blood samples from normal individuals and 7 whole blood samples diagnosed with sepsis in the Department of Laboratory Medicine, the Third Xiangya Hospital of Central South University, and placed them in anticoagulation tubes for subsequent experiments. The procedure was reviewed by the Ethics Committee of the Third Xiangya Hospital of Central South University (No. 25034) on January 13, 2025. The whole process was operated according to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.


#### PBMC extraction
  The whole blood samples were diluted in sterile, enzyme-free PBS and slowly added to Ficoll-Paque PLUS (17,144,003–1, Cytiva, USA), and the mononuclear cell layer was separated by centrifugation at 300 g for 30 min. PBS was added for washing, and the supernatant was discarded after centrifugation at 300 g for 10 min, which was repeated twice to obtain ultimate peripheral blood mononuclear cells.


#### RNA extraction and RT-qPCR
  Total RNA from the cells was extracted by adding 500 μl of Trizol reagent (15596018CN, Invitrogen, USA) to each sample, and the concentration and purity of each RNA sample were determined by a NanoDrop®ND-1000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher, USA). Next, cDNA was synthesized by reverse transcription from 1 µg RNA (RK20433, ABclonal, China) and amplified by qPCR using the SYBR Green Mix Kit (RK21219, ABclonal, China). The relative expression of target genes was then estimated using Ct values detected by a real-time fluorescent quantitative PCR system (LightCycler480, Roche, USA). GAPDH was used as an internal reference gene for normalization, and 2−ΔΔCT was applied to assess relative gene expression. The primer sequences (Sangon Biotech, China) synthesized were as follows: MAFG: Forward: TCAGATTTCAGAGGAATACCCAGCAG, Reserve: TGATCACCAGTCAGAAGTGTACACAC. TXN: Forward: AGACTCCAGCAGCCAAGATG, Reserve: AGCAACATCATGAAAGAAAGGCT. GAPDH: Forward: AATGGGCAGCCGTTAGGAAA, Reserve: GCGCCCAATACGACCAAATC.


#### Statistical methods
  All statistical analyses were completed using R software (v 4.2.1) and SPSS 25.0. The univariate Cox regression analysis and the log-rank test were conducted to filter the genes related to prognosis by the p-value and hazard ratios. The ROC demonstrates the efficacy of diagnosing or predicting prognosis in sepsis. Normally distributed data and non-normally distributed data were compared between the two groups by Student’s t-test and Mann–Whitney U test, respectively. The correlation analysis was evaluated by the Spearman correlation coefficient. The p < 0.05 (two-sided) was considered to be statistically significant.

## Discussions
  Sepsis is defined as a dysregulated host response to infection that leads to life-threatening organ dysfunction syndrome, often occurring alongside other diseases and injuries, which still has a high mortality rate currently [28]. Septic shock is a severe status of sepsis that imposes a substantial strain on affected individuals and medical resources. Meanwhile, the major focus of treatment is on addressing organ injuries related to sepsis, particularly concerning the liver, kidneys, heart, lungs, and other organs [29]. The initial immune homeostasis is disturbed by the increased release of inflammatory cytokines and the activation of complement and coagulations, resulting in excessive inflammation. Furthermore, the upregulation of anti-inflammatory cytokines and negative co-stimulatory molecules, reduced HLA-DR expression, T cell exhaustion, and expansion of immunoregulatory cells exacerbate the procedure of immunosuppression [4], accompanying mitochondrial damage, endoplasmic reticulum stress, and neuroendocrine-immune network abnormalities [30]. Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA), one of the clinical diagnostic criteria for sepsis, makes it difficult to obtain comprehensive data based on non-ICU patients. On the other hand, Systemic Inflammatory Response Syndrome (SIRS) and Quick Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (qSOFA) have decent sensitivity but poor specificity, which is prone to overdiagnosis [31], so joint application of multiple indicators is necessary. The keys to grasping the therapeutic approach to sepsis are infection control, hemodynamic management, and modulation of the host response, with specific measurements including the administration of antibiotics, fluid resuscitation, vasopressors, and steroids [32]. Immunotherapy that targets immune checkpoints such as PD-1, PD-L1, CTLA4, TIM3, and LAG-3 can potentially lower sepsis mortality and improve prognostic prediction [33, 34]. By identifying the immune endotype of sepsis, the fields of omics technology and systems medicine have made it possible to apply patient stratification for sophisticated immunotherapy [35]. As research deepens into the signaling pathways and target genes involved in the pathogenesis of sepsis, it has been shown that Tim-3 plays a regulatory role in sepsis-induced immunosuppression by inhibiting the NF-κB signaling pathway in CD4 T cells [36]. Another important gene, STING, engages in interactions with NCOA4 to intensify sepsis through the modulation of ferroptosis and inflammatory responses in macrophages [37], which can be activated by redox homeostasis maintained by the ferroptosis-related gene GPX4 [38]. Research showed that transcriptional profiling obtained through sequencing, when combined with explainable artificial intelligence and machine learning models, can be utilized to screen for disease-associated genes, thereby providing potential insights for disease diagnosis and treatment [39].

  As a form of cell death that relies on phospholipid peroxidation and ferrous ions, ferroptosis exerts its impacts by resisting the antioxidant system, particularly the SLC7A11-GSH-GPX4 axis of system xc− [9]. The occurrence of the Fenton reaction and elevated levels of ROS can activate ferroptosis, which leads to the peroxidation of the main substrate polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs) and produces peroxidation products, such as phospholipid hydroperoxides (PLOOHs), thus damaging cell membranes [40]. Ferroptosis can be initiated through two major pathways: the extrinsic or transporter-dependent pathway, which mainly refers to the iron and amino acid transportation, and the intrinsic or enzyme-regulated pathway including key enzymes that mediate antioxidant activity such as GPX4 and lipid peroxidation such as ACSL4, LPCAT3, ALOX15 [41, 42]. It can be induced by classic reagents erastin and RSL3 and inhibited by ferrostatin-1 and liproxstatin-1 [43]. Meanwhile, numerous genes are involved, such as activation of the p62-Keap1-NRF2 pathway that prevents ferroptosis in hepatocellular carcinoma cells [44], while NCOA4-mediated ferritinophagy is required for ionizing radiation-induced ferroptosis in intestinal epithelial cells [45]. As we know, these ferroptosis-related genes play an integral role in many diseases. It is reported that ferroptosis-related neurodegeneration is triggered by NOX1 in Parkinson’s disease [46]. In addition, Zhou et al. showed that PTGS2 is identified as a hub gene for coronary atherosclerosis, and its expression is positively correlated with the severity of the disease [47]. Cancer is also one of the major diseases in which ferroptosis regulates its progression. In recent research, CST1 inhibits ferroptosis by recruiting OTUB1 to alleviate GPX4 ubiquitination, improve GPX4 protein stability, and reduce intracellular ROS, thereby promoting gastric cancer metastasis [48]. The promotion of ferroptosis by inactivation or blockade of the interaction between TRPML1 and ARL8B has been reported to be inhibitory for AKT-driven tumorigenesis and cancer therapeutic resistance [49]. It is shown that modulation of the SMURF2/GSTP1 axis or inhibition of the catalytic activity of GSTP1 sensitizes cancer cells to ferroptosis, thus serving as one of the targets of anticancer therapy [50].

  In addition, ferroptosis-related genes (FRGs) can also be modulated through various signaling pathways in sepsis. By machine learning models, we found that several FRGs have a certain predictive effect on the diagnosis and prognosis of sepsis. Among the five FRGs related to the diagnosis of sepsis screened in our study, KLF2 is a transcriptional regulator of chronic and acute inflammation, and its partial deficiency regulates in vivo responses to sepsis and skin inflammation [51]. The restoration of KLF2 induced by AMPK activation contributes to the anti-inflammatory effects of metformin in endotoxemia-induced endothelial cells [52]. In addition, the study indicated that TXN was identified as a characteristic diagnostic biomarker for maintaining endoplasmic reticulum homeostasis in sepsis, which is closely related to the immune infiltration landscape [53]. It was similarly screened as a characteristic gene for sepsis diagnosis by a machine learning model in this study as well. It has been found that by modulating TXN and TIP levels and initiating the reduction of ROS levels, sevoflurane was shown to have an ameliorating effect on septic encephalopathy [54]. We found that TXN may be a potential downstream target of MAFG, which has an interaction with MAFG and is also significantly highly expressed in sepsis validated by the external dataset and RT-qPCR. Accumulating evidence shows that, PEBP1, identified as one of the key genes associated with the occurrence and progression of sepsis [55], has been demonstrated to improve neuronal injury and inflammation through ferroptosis via the hippocampal PEBP1/15-LOX/GPX4 pathway in the treatment of sepsis-associated encephalopathy [56]. However, the relationship between gene QSOX1 and sepsis has rarely been reported so far.

  MAFG is recognized as MAF BZIP Transcription Factor G, a member of the MAF protein family, which can form heterodimers with Nrf2 and then binds to and activates the antioxidant response element (ARE) in the promoter region of DNA to regulate the transcription of the corresponding gene [57]. MAFG is involved in the development of a wide range of diseases. A recent study shows that increased MAFG expression drives astrocytes to promote CNS inflammation, that is, through GM-CSF signaling that drives the expression and cooperation of MAFG and MAT2α, which in turn promotes DNA methylation and acts to inhibit antioxidant and anti-inflammatory transcriptional programs [58]. The results of this study might serve as one of the supporting types of evidence for promising treatment strategies for multiple sclerosis. It has additionally been reported that MAFG expression can be upregulated by lithocholic acid activation in cholestatic liver injury and hepatocellular carcinoma, along with decreased MAT1A and increased MAT2A expression, which enhances the activity of the MAFG promoter. High levels of MAFG are associated with tumor progression and shorter survival time [59]. As an oncogene, MAFG can contribute to osteosarcoma cell and melanoma progression [60, 61], and also increase ROS levels to restore chemosensitivity in cisplatin-resistant non-small-cell lung cancer cells [62]. Actually, the function of MAFG in sepsis has been less reported, and MAFG was included as a progressively dysregulated gene in sepsis by bioinformatics analysis [63]. It is also demonstrated that four subgroups of sepsis were identified based on the median expression of angiogenesis and myc target v2 genes: normal, myc target v2, mixed-quiescent, and angiogenesis. Another study showed that MAFG was filtered by RNA-seq as one of the potential immune-related genes expressed in sepsis, which is at the core of the differential gene network. MAFG is increased in sepsis, and it is associated with survival in patients with sepsis and is known to localize in monocytes by scRNA-seq [64]. This evidence is consistent with the results of this study, in which we identified MAFG as a monocyte-located gene significantly related to both the diagnosis and survival rates of sepsis by machine learning and survival analysis.

  However, this study has limitations, primarily due to the retrospective and in silico nature of the results, including (1) the lack of protein-level validation, (2) absence of functional assays (e.g., knockdown or overexpression experiments), (3) the lack of external validation using independent patient cohorts, and (4) unassessed causal relationships, (5) the threshold setting may omit subtle changes related to the biology of sepsis, (6) intersecting all DEGs with the FerrDb gene set may lead to a bias toward already curated targets rather than novel associations, (7) the lack of a multivariate Cox regression analysis means that other patient factors may have interfered with the results. These limitations constrain further exploration of MAFG as a potential sepsis biomarker and the generalizability of the findings. Future studies should validate its biological functions and confirm the clinical significance of ferroptosis in sepsis pathogenesis.


## Conclusions
  In this study, we identified MAFG as a significantly upregulated FRG in sepsis, mainly localized to monocytes, and its high expression predicted a more favorable diagnosis and poorer prognosis of sepsis. Meanwhile, TXN, a potential target FRG of MAFG, is also a characteristic gene and highly expressed in sepsis, broadening new insights into the therapeutic targets of sepsis and the regulatory role of ferroptosis in sepsis.
