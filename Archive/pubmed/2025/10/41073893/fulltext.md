# Identification and tissue-level validation of ferroptosis-related genes in small intestinal neuroendocrine neoplasms based on machine learning


## Background
  Small intestinal neuroendocrine neoplasms (SI-NENs), a subgroup of neuroendocrine tumors originating from neuroendocrine cells in the small intestine, present significant therapeutic challenges, and their relationship with ferroptosis—a regulated form of cell death driven by iron-dependent lipid peroxidation—remains poorly understood. This study aimed to identify the ferroptosis-related genes in SI-NENs and evaluate their clinical relevance.


## Methods
  The RNA sequencing data of SI-NENs patients were retrieved from two public datasets (GSE65286 and GSE98894). Machine learning (LASSO regression, a regularization method for selecting key features by reducing redundant genes, and random forest, an ensemble learning algorithm that builds multiple decision trees to improve prediction accuracy) was adopted to identify the core ferroptosis-related genes in SI-NENs. An external cohort consisting of 10 health volunteers and 14 SI-NENs patients was enrolled and intestinal mucosal biopsies were obtained for validation.


## Results
  CDCA3, CDC25A, CYP4F8, and MYB were identified as the core ferroptosis-related genes in SI-NENs patients via machine learning. In the external cohort, immunofluorescence (IF) staining confirmed a significantly decreased expression of CYP4F8 and CDCA3 in patients with SI-NENs compared with the health control. Moreover, in G2/3 patients, the expression levels of CYP4F8 and CDCA3 were significantly decreased compared with G1 patients. In contrast, no significant difference in the expression of the four core ferroptosis genes was found across the different tumor locations, the age stratifications, or the abdominal pain statuses. In addition, a statically negative correlation of the expression of CYP4F8 was further detected concerning the tumor diameter and the expression of Ki67.


## Conclusion
  Four core ferroptosis-related genes (CDCA3, CDC25A, CYP4F8, and MYB) were identified in SI-NENs patients, which may aid in developing the diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets in the clinic.

## Introduction
  Neuroendocrine neoplasms (NENs) originate from specialized secretory cells within the diffuse neuroendocrine system. Small intestinal neuroendocrine neoplasms (SI-NENs) represent a distinct subgroup, which are further categorized into the functional and the non-functional subtypes based on their ability to secrete bioactive hormones and associated clinical syndromes [1]. Over the past decades, advancements in endoscopic techniques and an improved understanding of NENs have contributed to a steady rise in their reported incidence, with an overall incidence of approximately 6.98 per 100,000, of which SI-NENs account for about 1.05 per 100,000 cases [2]. Current therapeutic strategies for SI-NENs primarily include surgical resection and pharmacotherapy. Surgery remains the cornerstone of treatment, with the decision between radical and palliative approaches depending on factors such as tumor size, location, histopathological grade, and metastatic status [3]. Pharmacal treatments involve biotherapy, targeted therapy, and chemotherapy [4–6]. However, resistance to pharmacological agents may develop, and the limited efficacy along with the significant toxicity were reported [7–9]. Hence, it is necessary to investigate the molecular mechanism of SI-NENs for the development of new treatment strategy in the clinic.

  Ferroptosis is a distinct form of regulated cell death characterized by uncontrolled lipid peroxidation, ultimately leading to membrane damage and rupture. Unlike classical apoptosis or necrosis, ferroptosis is driven by iron-dependent oxidative stress [10]. Emerging evidence suggests that ferroptosis plays a critical role in the pathogenesis of various malignancies, including hepatocellular carcinomas, gastric cancer, and colorectal cancer [11–13]. Several key oncogenic pathways such as RAS, TP53, and NFE2L2 have been reported to intricately regulate ferroptosis, with tumor-specific effects that vary across cancer types. Numerous clinically approved and investigational agents, including sorafenib, sulfasalazine, statins, and artemisinin derivatives, have been shown to exert antitumor effects by modulating ferroptosis-related pathways, such as glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4) inhibition [14, 15]. Ferroptosis has dual significance in tumor treatment: on the one hand, it is an innate tumor suppressor mechanism. Tumor suppressors such as p53 can eliminate abnormal cells by inducing ferroptosis, and it is particularly sensitive to drug-resistant mesenchymal and dedifferentiated cancer cells, providing a new direction for overcoming drug resistance to traditional therapies; on the other hand, cancer cells can evade ferroptosis by upregulating antioxidant systems such as SLC7A11/GPX4, regulating iron metabolism, or utilizing the tumor microenvironment. In terms of therapeutic strategies, tumor growth can be inhibited by inducing ferroptosis, and ferroptosis inhibition can alleviate treatment side effects or protect immune cell functions, but it is necessary to balance its complex impact on immunity [16–18]. Machine learning has been widely applied in research on neuroendocrine tumors. Examples include exploring the relationship between the Ki-67 index or tumor size and recurrence in pancreatic neuroendocrine neoplasms, as well as combining radiomics to identify small pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors, among others [19, 20]. However, no study has focused on the role of ferroptosis in the incidence and progression of SI-NENs to date. In addition, the involvement of machine learning technology is accelerating the revelation of potential associations between ferroptosis and diseases, such as diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, neuroblastoma, adamantinomatous craniopharyngioma, and osteosarcoma [21–24]. These studies collectively confirm that the strategy of mining ferroptosis-related key genes in rare diseases through machine learning, combined with validation using clinical samples, can efficiently provide strong support for the discovery of diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets.

  This study aims to investigate the relationship between SI-NENs and ferroptosis by applying bioinformatic analysis and validation in an external cohort. We identified four core ferroptosis-related genes via machine learning, and the expression of the core ferroptosis-related genes was re-examined in the external cohort which consists of 10 healthy controls and 14 SI-NENs patients. The association of the core ferroptosis-related genes with the clinical pathological features of SI-NENs was investigated. Our study will enhance the understanding of SI-NENs pathogenesis and help to identify potential therapeutic targets.


## Methods

### The enrollment of the external cohort
  We collected 10 healthy volunteers and 14 patients pathologically diagnosed with SI-NENs from the First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University between January 2023 and January 2024. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Patients who met the diagnostic standards for small intestine neuroendocrine tumors (SI-NENs) as outlined in the 2022 version of the Chinese consensus. (2) Patients who underwent surgical resection or small bowel endoscopy at The First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University and were subsequently diagnosed with SI-NEN through pathological examination. We excluded patients with a history of other malignant tumors or received adjuvant therapy. The intestinal mucosal biopsies were obtained when performing enteroscope or surgical removal and immediately fixed in 4% formalin, and the medical records of each participant were retrieved to collect the basic clinicopathological information, including age, sex, BMI, smoking status, the intake of alcohol, the level of CEA and CA125. The prognosis of SI-NENs patients was evaluated via direct telephonic conversations. The diagnos is of SI-NENs was based on the UICC-TNM classification, and G1 SI-NENs was considered as Ki67 (a nuclear antigen associated with cell proliferation, reflecting tumor cell proliferative activity) ≤ 2%, G2 with Ki67 3%−20%, and G3 with Ki67 > 20%. All procedures related to human were conducted according to the criteria set by the declaration of Helsinki and the written informed consent was obtained from each donor under the supervision of the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University (LLSC20220610).


### Data acquisition
  GSE65286 (generated using Agilent-014850 Whole Human Genome Microarray 4 × 44 K G4112F) and GSE98894 (genera ted using Illumina HiSeq 2500) were retrieved from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo). The GSE65286 dataset included 10 SI-NENs cases and 10 normal controls, while the GSE98894 dataset contained 44 primary SI-NENs cases.


### Screening of differentially expressed genes (DEGs)
  Differentially expressed genes (DEGs, genes with significantly different expression levels between SI-NENs and normal tissues) were identified from the GSE65286 dataset using the “limma” package in R software, with thresholds set at |log2(FC)| >2 and P < 0.05. Volcano plots illustrating DEGs were generated using the “ggplot2” package.


### Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) of DEGs
  Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) is a bioinformatic method that determines whether a predefined set of genes is significantly enriched in SI-NENs samples compared to controls. We performed GSEA to identify the top five upregulated and downregulated pathways in SI-NENs samples, which helps clarify the biological significance of DEGs.


### Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA)
  Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) constructs gene co-expression networks based on weighted correlations, aiming to identify modules of highly co-expressed genes associated with clinical traits. We applied WGCNA to identify highly correlated gene modules, summarize inter-module relationships, and explore their associations with clinical traits. Using the “WGCNA” package in R software, gene modules most relevant to SI-NENs were identified based on the criteria as Module Membership (MM) > 0.8 and Gene Significance (GS) > 0.5. Genes from the most significant module were then intersected with DEGs and ferroptosis-related genes (obtained from the FerrDb database) to derive differentially expressed ferroptosis-related genes (DEFGs).


### Enrichment analysis
  We conducted enrichment analyses using the “clusterProfiler” package in R software, including: (1) Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis (KEGG is a database integrating gene functions with biological pathways); (2) Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis (GO annotates gene functions into biological processes [BP], cellular components [CC], and molecular functions [MF]).


### Dataset expansion and batch correction
  The original dataset was expanded by incorporating 44 primary SI-NENs samples from the GSE98894 dataset. Batch correction was performed to reduce technical variability and ensure data consistency.


### Machine learning-based hub gene screening
  LASSO logistic regression analysis was conducted using the “glmnet” package in R, with optimal penalty parameters determined via 10-fold cross-validation. Additionally, random forest analysis was implemented using the “randomForest” package. The core ferroptosis-related genes were identified by intersecting results from both algorithms. The diagnostic efficacy of these genes was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis (a plot of true positive rate vs. false positive rate), with the area under the curve (AUC, a value ranging from 0 to 1, where 1 indicates perfect diagnostic performance) used to assess predictive performance.


### Single-gene GSEA
  Single-gene GSEA was performed to explore the potential functional roles of the identified hub genes.


### Molecular docking
  Molecular docking simulations were conducted using AutoDock Vina 1.2.2 (http://autodock.scripps.edu/) to assess the binding affinity of candidate drugs from DsigDB with proteins encoded by each target gene. Drug structure data were obtained from EMBL-EBI (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/) using AlphaFold-based structural data. Prior to docking, water molecules were removed from both protein and ligand files, and polar hydrogen atoms were added. Grid boxes encompassing each protein’s structural domains were defined to allow unrestricted molecular mobility during the simulation. The docking pocket was constructed with a grid point distance of 0.05 nm and a pocket size of 30 Å × 30 Å × 30 Å. Docking results were analyzed and visualized using PyMol software.


### Immunofluorescence staining (IF), immunohistochemistry (IHC) and H&E staining
  Small intestinal tissue samples were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, embedded in paraffin and sliced into 5 μm sections. Hematoxylin and eosin (HE) staining was adopted to evaluate tissue pathological changes. The procedures of Immunohistochemistry (IHC) and Immunofluorescence (IF) are as follows: in brief, IHC uses enzyme-labeled antibodies to detect protein expression, with results visualized via chromogenic substrates. IF uses fluorescently labeled antibodies for protein detection, with results visualized under a fluorescence microscope. In brief, after heat-induced antigen retrieval, all slides were incubated with 3% BSA for 1 h at 37 ℃ to block non-specific staining and then incubated with primary anti-CDCA3 (1:200), anti-CDC25A (1:200), anti-CYP4F8 (1:100), and anti-MYB (1:400) at 4 ℃ overnight. ImageJ 1.8.0 was adopted to analyze the intensity of IF staining. Five microscopic fields were randomly selected in each slide. The average count of cells with positive staining and DAPI-positive nuclei were measured using the ImageJ plugin “Cell Counter”. Quantification of the positive area was conducted via manual segmentation of positive area using ImageJ and presented as a percentage area fraction of the whole area. To make manual segmentation as accurate and unbiased as possible, all segmentations were performed by the same experienced user in a blinded way. The primary antibody is presented in Table S1.


### Statistical analysis
  Continuous variables were expressed as means ± standard deviation (SD), or medians (interquartile range). Comparisons between different groups were analyzed in categorical variables and continuous variables using Fisher’s exact test, and Mann-Whitney U test or t-test, respectively. For statistical analysis involved the validation in external cohort, non-parametric tests were adopted with regard to the small sample size, and Benjamini-Hochberg method were applied to conduct multiple testing corrections. Spearman’s correlation test was performed to detect the correlation between the expression of the ferroptosis-related genes and the clinical pathological features of SI-NENs patients. ImageJ was adopted to analyze the intensity of IF staining. The statistical software for the GraphPad Prism 9, and R version 4.4.2 were employed in this study. All tests were 2 sided and P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.


## Results

### Bioinformatic analysis obtained the ferroptosis-related DEGs in SI-NENs
  Firstly, we identified a total of 2,543 DEGs in SI-NENs, while 1263 genes were upregulated and 1280 genes were downregulated (Fig. 1A). GSEA showed that the pathways included circadian entrainment, glycosaminoglycan biosynthesis, nicotine addiction, dopaminergic synapse, and morphine addiction were positively enriched in SI-NENs patients, and the pathways such as vitamin digestion and absorption, renin-angiotensin system, fat digestion and absorption, galactose metabolism, and primary immunodeficiency were negatively enriched (Fig. 1B).

  Next, WGCNA was performed to identify key gene modules associated with SI-NENs. Using a squared feature gene correlation coefficient (r²) of 0.8 and a soft thresholding power of 3, module-trait relationships were established. Figure 1C illustrates the clustering dendrogram for SI-NENs and control groups. Among the identified gene modules, the Medarkturquoise module demonstrated the strongest positive correlation with SI-NENs (cor = 0.95, P < 0.001) (Fig. 1D-E). Screening based on module membership (MM) and gene significance (GS) thresholds (|MM| >0.8 and |GS| >0.2) identified 2,994 genes significantly associated with SI-NENs.

  Subsequently, we performed an intersection analysis using 2543 DEGs in SI-NENs, 2994 genes that were identified by WGCNA and 484 ferroptosis-associated genes that were retrieved from the FerrDb database. A total of 23 ferroptosis-related DEGs were finally obtained (Fig. 1F) and their protein-protein interaction (PPI) network (depicting physical interactions between proteins to reveal potential functional associations) is illustrated in Fig. 1G. GO analysis showed enrichment in biological processes such as response to nutrients and regulation of protein serine/threonine kinase activity. Cellular component enrichment included the RNA polymerase II transcription regulator complex and mitochondrial outer membrane, while molecular functions were associated with transcription coregulator binding and nuclear receptor activity (Fig. 1H). KEGG pathway analysis revealed significant associations with microRNAs in cancer, PPAR signaling pathway, transcriptional misregulation in cancer, thyroid cancer, and ferroptosis (Fig. 1I).


### Four core ferroptosis-related DEGs were identified by machine learning
  We further performed two complementary machine learning approaches, including LASSO regression analysis and Random Forest model, by expanding the original dataset of 20 samples with an additional 44 primary SI-NENs cases from the GSE98894 dataset. Batch effects were corrected using the “sva” package in R (Figs. 2A-B). Six key genes with significant predictive power were identified by the LASSO regression (Figs. 2C-D), while 10 candidate genes were identified by the Random Forest model (Fig. 2E). By intersecting results from the two methods, four core ferroptosis-related genes (CDCA3, CDC25A, CYP4F8, and MYB) were identified (Fig. 2F).

### Expression and functional analysis of the core ferroptosis-related DEGs
  Differential expression analysis demonstrated the significant downregulation of CDCA3, CDC25A, CYP4F8, and MYB in SI-NENs when compared with the control group (P < 0.001 for all genes) (Figs. 3A-D). Spearman’s correlation analysis revealed a moderate correlation between CDC25A and MYB (Cor: 0.48) (Fig. 3E). ROC curve analysis showed the satisfied performance of the four core ferroptosis-related DEGs for the diagnosis of SI-NENs, with the AUC values exceeding 0.9 for all four genes (Fig. 3F).

(F) Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curves assessing the diagnostic performance of the four ferroptosis related genes (CDCA3, CDC25A, CYP4F8, and MYB)

  According to the cut-off value of each core ferroptosis-related DEGs, single-gene GSEA based on GO and KEGG gene sets was performed to identify the different biological pathways between the low- and high-expression groups. The Hedgehog Signaling Pathway and Linoleic Acid Metabolism were enriched in the CDCA3 low expression group when compared with the CDCA3 high expression group (Supplementary Fig. 1 A). Ascorbate and Aldarate Metabolism and Hematopoietic Cell Lineage were enriched in the CDC25A high expression group when compared with the CDC25A low expression group (Supplementary Fig. 1B). Compared with the CYP4F8 low expression group, DNA Replication and Proteasome were enriched in the CYP4F8 high expression group (Supplementary Fig. 1 C). Meanwhile, Hematopoietic Cell Lineage and Peroxisome were enriched in the MYB high expression group compared with the MYB low expression group (Supplementary Fig. 1D). The expression pattern of the individual core ferroptosis-related genes was further evaluated across 50 common gene sets (Supplementary Fig. 1E), and the scores of the 50 common gene sets in the health control and SI-NENs patient groups were also presents in Supplementary Fig. 1F.


### Molecular docking analysis
  Molecular docking was conducted to evaluate the binding affinities of potential drug candidates with the identified hub genes. AutoDock Vina v.1.2.2 was used to determine binding sites and interactions. Each drug candidate exhibited visible hydrogen bonds and strong electrostatic interactions with its respective protein target. Moreover, all four drug candidates successfully occupied the binding pockets of their target proteins. Among them, daunorubicin showed the lowest binding energy for CDC25A (−7.949 kcal/mol) (Figs. 4A-B), while quercetin demonstrated the strongest affinity for CDCA3 (−6.012 kcal/mol) (Figs. 4C-D). Retinoic acid was identified as the most effective ligand for MYB (−6.525 kcal/mol) (Figs. 4E-F), and for CYP4F8, the small molecule identified by PubChem ID: 15,558,489 exhibited the lowest binding energy (−9.349 kcal/mol) (Figs. 4G-H).

### Histological verification of the core ferroptosis-related DEGs in the external cohort
  By HE staining, the morphological difference of intestinal mucosal tissue between the SI-NENs and the control was observed (Fig. 5A). Tumor tissue exhibited characteristic nest-like or mass-like arrangements, with disordered cell polarity, deeply stained nuclei and obvious atypia. Increased Acyl-CoA synthase long chain family member 4 (ACSL4) expression is a characteristic of ferroptosis. IHC staining reveals the elevated expression of ACSL4 in the colonic mucosal biopsies of the SI-NENs patients when compared with the health control (Fig. 5B), suggesting ferroptosis may be involved in the incidence of SI-NENs. IF staining was performed to study the expression difference of the four core ferroptosis-related genes among the SI-NENs and the control. A significantly decreased positive area of CYP4F8 (Fig. 6A), as well as CDCA3 (Fig. 6B), was observed in the SI-NENs when compared with normal small intestinal mucosa (P < 0.001), while there was no statistical difference concerning the expressions of CDC25A or MYB (Fig. 6C-D).

### Association of the core ferroptosis-related genes with clinicopathological features in the external cohort
  Subgroup analysis was performed to investigate the association of the expression of the core ferroptosis-related genes with the clinicopathological features of SI-NENs patients. None of the four ferroptosis-related genes showed a significant expression difference across different infiltration depths (Fig. 7A-D). As for the G grade, compared with the Group A tumors, the protein expression of CYP4F8 and CDCA3 were significantly downregulated in the Group B tumors (Fig. 7E, F), while there was no significant difference in the protein expression of CDC25A between the two groups (Fig. 7G). The expression level of MYB was significantly upregulated in the Group B tumors (Fig. 7H). Spearman correlation analysis showed that the proportion of Ki67-positive cells was significantly negatively correlated with the expression of CYP4F8 (Spearman r = −0.82, P < 0.01) (Fig. 7I), while no significant correlation was observed concerning the expression of CDCA3, C-MYB or CDC25A (Fig. 7J-L). As for tumor diameter, a statically negative correlation was observed for CYP4F8, but not for CDCA3, C-MYB or CDC25A (Fig. 7M-P).

## Discussion
  With the increased incidence of SI-NENs in the clinic, current treatment options remain suboptimal, with limited objective response rates (ORR). Research on ferroptosis in SI-NENs is still in its early stages. In this study, we identified four core ferroptosis-related genes (CDC25A, CDCA3, MYB, CYP4F8) in SI-NENs patients using machine learning and an external cohort was enrolled to validate and assess the association of the expression of the four core ferroptosis-related genes with the clinical pathological features of SI-NENs patients. To our knowledge, this is the first study to report the potential involvement of ferroptosis-related genes in SI-NENs.

  Numerous studies have revealed that ferroptosis is involved in multiple neuroendocrine tumors, emerging as a potential therapeutic target. In pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors (pNETs), the highly mutated gene MEN1 encodes menin protein, which induces ferroptosis by inhibiting the mTOR-SCD1 signaling axis [25]. In BON-1 neuroendocrine tumor cells, artesunate (ART) exerts significant cytotoxic effects by regulating ferroptosis-related genes and activating classical ferroptosis pathways [26]. In SI-NENs, Chen et al. performed differential expression analysis, functional enrichment, and regulatory network construction to identify diagnostic markers for distinguishing primary and advanced stages [27]. Despite these reports, the relationship between ferroptosis and SI-NENs remains unexplored. In current study, we firstly identified 2,543 DEGs between the health control and the SI-NENs patients; WGCNA was then employed to screen 2994 SI-NENs related genes. Intersection analysis of these genes with the ferroptosis-related genes from the FerrDb database yielded differentially expressed ferroptosis-related genes. Among these genes, machine learning finally identified four core ferroptosis-related genes. Validation was subsequently conducted in an external cohort by evaluating the associations between their protein expression levels and clinicopathological characteristics (e.g., tumor grade, invasion depth).

  CYP4F8 is a member of the CYP4F subfamily, which plays a key role in prostaglandin and eicosanoid metabolism, thereby influencing inflammatory responses and cellular signaling [28]. Study focus on the role of CYP4F8 in gastrointestinal NENs is limited, while in colorectal cancer, CYP4F8 has been incorporated into a prognostic model to stratify patients into high- and low-risk groups based on distinct survival outcomes [29]. In ferroptosis, the direct involvement of CYP4F8 also remains unexplored, but its role in fatty acid metabolism suggests a potential link to lipid peroxidation, a hallmark of ferroptosis [30]. Evidence suggests that CYP4F8 may regulate lipid peroxidation by modulating the oxidative metabolism of polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs), such as arachidonic acid, thereby influencing ferroptosis susceptibility [28]. Our results found that CYP4F8 is significantly downregulated in SI-NENs and validated in an external cohort. The expression level further decreased with increasing pathological grades of SI-NENs. Correlation analysis showed that the expression of CYP4F8 is significantly correlated with the expression of KI67 as well as the tumor diameter. These results suggest that the expression of CYP4F8 could be a promising biomarker to help the evaluation of biological traits of SI-NENs in the clinic, although no statical correlation was detected with regard to the tumor infiltration which was possibly due to the small sample size.

  CDC25A and CDCA3 both play a crucial role in regulating cell proliferation [31, 32]. CDC25A overexpression has been reported in various cancers, including hepatocellular, gastric, and colorectal carcinomas, where it is associated with poor prognosis [33–35]. Interestingly, CDC25A overexpression has been shown to inhibit sorafenib-induced ferroptosis in cervical cancer cells via the PKM2/ErbB2 signaling axis, suggesting its role as a negative regulator of ferroptosis [36]. CDCA3, also known as mitotic entry trigger 1, regulates cell cycle progression by promoting the degradation of the endogenous cell cycle inhibitor WEE1, thereby facilitating mitotic entry [32]. Emerging evidence suggests that CDCA3 may play a role in ferroptosis regulation. In clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC), CDCA3 has been identified as a poor prognostic factor in a ferroptosis- and oxidative stress-related prognostic model, where elevated expression correlates with advanced TNM stage and higher tumor grade [37]. While these findings suggest a link between CDCA3 and ferroptosis pathways in RCC, direct evidence of its role in ferroptosis regulation remains elusive. c-MYB, encoded by MYB, also plays a critical role in cell cycle regulation [38]. As a transcription factor, c-MYB positively regulates CDO1 expression by binding to its promoter, modulating cysteine metabolism and glutathione (GSH) levels while inversely regulating GPX4 expression to promote ferroptosis in gastric cancer [39]. In our study, bioinformatic analysis revealed the downregulation of the four ferroptosis-related genes in SI-NENs, and the validation in an external cohort also revealed the decreased expression of CYP4F8 and CDCA3. Previous studies reported that SI-NENs exhibit distinct molecular characteristics, especially the high somatostatin receptor (SSTR) expression and the low proliferative indices (e.g., Ki-67 levels). These findings incorporated the paradoxically low expression of these genes revealed by our results reinforcing the notion that SI-NENs may rely on homeostasis maintenance rather than aggressive proliferation [40].

  The clinical value of existing biomarkers for SI-NENs, including CgA, synaptophysin, and NETest is limited. CgA has insufficient specificity as a biomarker for NENs. Non-tumor factors (such as liver/kidney dysfunction) and non-NEN malignancies (e.g., colorectal cancer, gastric cancer) can both cause false positives, and its sensitivity is limited as 30% to 50% of NEN patients have normal CgA levels [41]. In neuroendocrine carcinomas, synaptophysin often shows diffuse but faint punctate or patchy expression, prone to missed diagnosis; it cannot independently discriminate between well-differentiated neuroendocrine tumors (NET G3) and neuroendocrine carcinomas (NEC) [42, 43]. Although the NETest can assess tumor burden and predict treatment response, its high cost and technical complexity limit routine clinical application [44]. Therefore, there is an urgent need to identify new biomarkers that, when combined with traditional markers like CgA, can improve diagnostic sensitivity and specificity. Our study shows that CYP4F8 expression negatively correlates with tumor diameter and Ki67 index, reflecting tumor proliferative activity and malignant potential—attributes that markers like CgA cannot directly capture. CDCA3, which is downregulated in G2/G3 tumors, may serve as a novel prognostic indicator. While traditional markers reflect neuroendocrine differentiation, genes such as CDCA3 and CYP4F8 directly regulate ferroptosis, providing new directions for targeted therapy. Future clinical trials may evaluate combinations of ferroptosis inducers with standard treatments (e.g., somatostatin analogs). Additionally, incorporating ferroptosis genes into multi-biomarker panels could enhance diagnostic and prognostic accuracy, helping to identify patients most likely to respond to specific therapies (e.g., mTOR inhibitors).

  There are several limitations in this study. First, a small sample size of external validation is a major concern, which becomes more pronounced when performing subgroup analyses. We must acknowledge that this limitation might undermine the robustness and generalizability of the findings, although we have adopted non-parametric tests in statical analyses to minimize the bias induced by small sample size. Primary SI-NENs are inherently rare, and it is difficult to enroll sufficient patients to achieve the necessary statistical power in a single center. A study incorporated with multiply medical center to achieve a large sample size is required to validate the findings of our study. Second, prognostic value of the identified ferroptosis-related genes in SI-NENs remains unclear, which undermines the clinical value of this study. As a low invasiveness and long course disease, there was no death among the external cohort with a mean follow-up time of 44.3 months. The incidence of endpoint event also is limited in a small sample size. Hence, a multiply center study with a longitudinal follow-up time is required to evaluate the prognostic value of the identified ferroptosis-related genes. Thirdly, the role of the identified ferroptosis-related genes in the incidence and progression of SI-NENs was not investigated in this study due to lack of animal models and the unavailable SI-NENs cell line. Further experiments in vivo and vitro were required to address the function of ferroptosis in SI-NENs. Finally, we must acknowledge the potential bias induced by dataset heterogeneity. Although we adopted the ComBat method to correct batch effect and PCA plots clearly demonstrate the effectiveness of the correction, the heterogeneity in sample collection, processing, and analysis methods across different datasets still undermine the reliability of the combined analysis.


## Conclusions
  In conclusion, the current study identified four core ferroptosis-related genes (CDCA3, CDC25A, CYP4F8, and MYB) in SI-NENs through machine learning and validated in an external cohort. The findings provide novel insights into SI-NENs pathogenesis and highlight ferroptosis-related genes as potential diagnostic and therapeutic targets. Further large-scale studies with long-term follow-up are required to validate the findings and investigate the prognostic value of CDCA3, CDC25A, CYP4F8, and MYB in SI-NENs.
