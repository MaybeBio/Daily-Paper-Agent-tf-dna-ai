## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, results, methods, discussion) as provided.
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific and technical evaluation of the claims, methods, and evidence presented in the provided text. No assessment of supplementary figures, tables, or datasets beyond what is referenced in the text.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report a compendium of in vivo ChIP-Seq data for 139 E. coli transcription factors (TFs), and introduce BoltzNet, a biophysically motivated neural network that predicts TF-DNA binding energy from sequence. They claim BoltzNet provides interpretable, nucleotide-resolution predictions genome-wide, can design novel binding sites validated by Library-ChIP and biophysical assays, and offers insights into global features of TF binding including weak sites, accessory bases, clustering, and genomic background affinity.
- **Visible evidence base** Main text figures (Figures 1-6) as described in the text, equations (1-23), and references to supplementary figures, tables, and datasets. No raw data, code, or supplementary material were directly inspected.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Supplementary figures (S1-S64), supplementary datasets (1-9), source data, code availability, and detailed methods for Library-ChIP and BLI analysis. These are referenced but not provided in the submitted material.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a substantial and potentially valuable resource: a large compendium of ChIP-Seq data for 139 E. coli TFs, and a novel neural network architecture (BoltzNet) that is explicitly designed to mirror a biophysical model of TF-DNA binding. The central idea of embedding a thermodynamic model into a neural network to achieve interpretability is conceptually appealing and addresses a known limitation of black-box deep learning approaches in genomics. The validation strategy, combining in vivo ChIP-Seq, in vitro ChIP-Seq, Library-ChIP, and BLI, is thorough and appropriate for the claims made. However, the manuscript as presented has several significant gaps that prevent full assessment of the claims. The description of the BoltzNet architecture and training is incomplete, the statistical treatment of key comparisons is unclear, and the evidence for some of the broader biological conclusions (e.g., genome-wide non-specific binding) is indirect. The work is likely of high interest to the bacterial gene regulation and computational biology communities, but the current evidence base is insufficient to fully establish all claims.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in bacterial gene regulation, transcription factor biology, and systems biology would be interested in the ChIP-Seq compendium as a community resource. Computational biologists and machine learning researchers working on interpretable models for genomics would be interested in the BoltzNet architecture as a methodological contribution. Synthetic biologists may find the quantitative design of binding sites useful. The findings on weak binding sites and genomic background affinity have implications for understanding gene regulatory network evolution and function.
- **Major strengths**
  1. The scale of the ChIP-Seq compendium (139 TFs) is a significant resource for the field.
  2. The BoltzNet architecture is a novel and conceptually elegant approach that directly integrates a biophysical model into a neural network, providing interpretability that is often lacking.
  3. The multi-pronged validation strategy (in vivo, in vitro, Library-ChIP, BLI) is rigorous and provides strong evidence for the predictive power of the model.
  4. The analysis of weak binding sites and genomic background affinity offers new perspectives on TF binding landscapes.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **R1-M1** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes)
    - **Axis** Technical soundness / Reproducibility
    - **Claim pointer** The authors claim BoltzNet is a novel neural network that predicts TF binding energy and ChIP coverage from sequence, with a specific architecture mirroring a two-stage biophysical model.
    - **Evidence pointer** Methods section "BoltzNet architecture and training" and "BoltzNet architecture and implementation" (location not provided in detail).
    - **Concern** The description of the BoltzNet architecture and training procedure is insufficiently detailed to allow replication or full technical evaluation. Key hyperparameters (e.g., learning rate schedule details, regularization strength, number of training epochs used in practice) are mentioned but not specified. The exact structure of the fully connected network (number of layers, activation functions beyond Leaky ReLU) is not fully described. The procedure for selecting the 25 bp kernel width and the 140-node hidden layer is stated as a hyperparameter but the selection process is not described. The handling of the negative samples (5000 random genomic regions) and their potential overlap with positive regions is not discussed. The loss function is stated as MSE, but the target values (normalized coverage) and how they are scaled across experiments is not clear.
    - **Why it matters** Without a complete and precise description of the model architecture and training protocol, the work cannot be reproduced, and the technical soundness of the approach cannot be fully assessed. This is a core requirement for a methods-focused paper.
    - **Resolution test** Provide a complete, step-by-step description of the model architecture, training procedure, hyperparameter selection, and data preprocessing in the Methods. This should include all relevant equations, layer dimensions, activation functions, regularization parameters, and optimization settings. Ideally, provide code in a public repository.
  - **R1-M2** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes)
    - **Axis** Statistical rigor / Evidence strength
    - **Claim pointer** The authors claim strong concordance between BoltzNet predictions and experimental measurements (Library-ChIP R2 of 0.88, 0.72, 0.46; BLI R2 of 0.51-0.99).
    - **Evidence pointer** Figures 4B-D and 5B-F (location not provided).
    - **Concern** The R2 values are presented as the primary evidence of predictive accuracy, but the statistical framework is unclear. For the Library-ChIP experiments, it is not stated how many sequences were tested, how the sequences were selected (beyond "spanning a range of predicted binding affinities"), or whether the R2 is calculated on the training set or a held-out test set. For the BLI experiments, the number of sequences and replicates per TF is not stated. The range of R2 values (0.51-0.99) is very wide, and the reasons for this variability are not discussed. The comparison to Proform is also based on R2, but the specific test set and conditions are not described.
    - **Why it matters** The central claim of the paper is that BoltzNet can quantitatively predict binding affinity. The evidence for this must be statistically robust and clearly described. Without knowing the number of test sequences, the selection criteria, and the error bars on the R2 values, the strength of the correlation cannot be properly evaluated.
    - **Resolution test** Provide a detailed description of the validation experiments, including the number of sequences tested per TF, the criteria for sequence selection, the number of replicates, and the statistical significance of the reported R2 values. Show scatter plots with error bars and confidence intervals. For the comparison with Proform, ensure the comparison is fair and on the same test set.
  - **R1-M3** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No)
    - **Axis** Scientific importance / Overinterpretation
    - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that their results "reframe the discussion of weak and strong binding sites into real physical units" and that the majority of TF protein is bound non-specifically to the genome.
    - **Evidence pointer** Discussion section and Figure 6 (location not provided).
    - **Concern** The conclusion that >79% of TF protein is bound non-specifically to the genome is based on a model extrapolation from the calibrated energy parameters. This is an interesting prediction, but it is presented as a finding rather than a model-derived hypothesis. The calculation depends on assumptions about TF concentration, cell volume, and the definition of "non-specific" binding, which are not fully discussed. The claim that weak sites are "physiologically relevant" is supported by the correlation with ChIP-Seq data, but the functional significance is not directly tested.
    - **Why it matters** These are strong claims that go beyond the direct measurements. Overinterpretation of model predictions as established facts could mislead the field. The distinction between model prediction and experimental observation must be clear.
    - **Resolution test** Clearly label the genome-wide non-specific binding estimates as model predictions. Provide a sensitivity analysis showing how the >79% estimate depends on key assumptions (e.g., TF concentration, cell volume). Discuss the limitations of the model in this context. For the physiological relevance of weak sites, either provide direct functional evidence or soften the claim to a hypothesis.
  - **R1-M4** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No)
    - **Axis** Technical soundness / Benchmarking
    - **Claim pointer** The authors claim BoltzNet outperforms existing tools (Proform, BPNet, Catchitt).
    - **Evidence pointer** Section "BoltzNet outperforms existing tools" and Supplementary Table S2 (location not provided).
    - **Concern** The comparison with existing tools is incomplete and potentially unfair. For Proform, the comparison is only on the BLI test set, and the authors note Proform was designed for eukaryotic SELEX, not prokaryotic ChIP-Seq. For BPNet, the comparison is indirect, using SHAP scores as a proxy for affinity, which may not be a fair comparison. The criteria for "outperforming" are not clearly defined beyond R2 on a specific test set. The computational cost and ease of use are not compared.
    - **Why it matters** A claim of superiority over existing methods requires a rigorous and fair benchmark. An incomplete comparison could be misleading and does not provide a solid basis for the community to adopt BoltzNet.
    - **Resolution test** Provide a more comprehensive benchmark, including a clear definition of the evaluation metrics, a description of the training and test sets for each tool, and a discussion of the limitations of each comparison. Consider comparing on a common set of sequences and using multiple metrics (e.g., AUC for site prediction, correlation for affinity).

- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**
  - **Originality** The concept of a biophysically constrained neural network is a novel and creative contribution. The idea of using a single convolutional kernel with an exponential activation to directly represent a binding energy matrix is elegant and distinct from standard deep learning approaches. This is a clear strength.
  - **Scientific importance** The ChIP-Seq compendium is a valuable resource. The potential to move from cataloging binding sites to quantitatively predicting binding affinity for any sequence is an important step for the field. The findings on weak sites and background binding could have broad implications. However, the importance of the biological conclusions is currently tempered by the indirect nature of some evidence.
  - **Interdisciplinary readership** The work bridges machine learning, biophysics, and microbiology. The writing is generally clear, but the methods section is dense and assumes significant background in both neural networks and thermodynamics. The main text figures are well-designed to convey the key concepts. The paper would be accessible to a broad Nature Communications audience, but the methods would be challenging for non-experts.
  - **Technical soundness** The core idea is sound, and the multi-assay validation is a strength. However, the lack of detail in the model description and the incomplete statistical reporting are significant technical weaknesses that must be addressed. The benchmarking against other tools is not yet rigorous enough to support the claim of superiority.
  - **Readability for nonspecialists** The abstract and introduction are accessible. The results section is mostly clear, but the discussion of model architecture and energy calculations may be difficult for readers without a computational background. The figures help significantly.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The core concept and the generated resource are valuable and likely to be of significant interest. However, the manuscript in its current form does not provide sufficient technical detail or statistical rigor to fully establish the claims. The authors should be encouraged to revise the manuscript to address the major concerns, particularly the completeness of the model description and the statistical framework for validation. If these issues are resolved, the paper would be a strong contribution.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that "the majority (>79%) of protein molecules for our studied TFs are bound non-specifically to genome" is a model-derived prediction, not a direct measurement. The sensitivity of this estimate to model assumptions is not presented.
- The claim that BoltzNet "outperforms" existing tools is not fully supported by the presented benchmark, which is incomplete and potentially biased.
- The claim that weak binding sites are "physiologically relevant" is supported by correlation with ChIP-Seq data but lacks direct functional validation.
- The statement that "BoltzNet is thus a bridge between high-throughput genomics and biophysical" is a reasonable interpretation but is presented as a conclusion without direct experimental evidence for the underlying biophysical accuracy of the model beyond the validation shown.
- The generalizability of BoltzNet to other organisms or growth conditions is mentioned as a future direction but is not tested, so any implied generalizability is unsupported.
