# Ciliary IFT88 inhibits intervertebral disc degeneration under excessive mechanical stress by regulating endplate cartilage calcification


## Graphical abstract
  A novel study investigates the role of IFT88 in endplate cartilage calcification and disc degeneration for the first time. It reveals that mechanical stress enhances the regulation of IFT88 by C/EBPα, while excessive stress leads to negative feedback on C/EBPα. Notably, under abnormal stress, IFT88 in primary cilia inhibits TRPV4 activation, thereby reducing calcium influx and consequently suppressing the increase of ROS and the activation of the Wnt signaling pathway associated with the stress. Overall, this research positions IFT88 as a key factor in protecting endplate cartilage and intervertebral discs from stress-related damage.

  


## Introduction
  Low back pain (LBP) is a global medical problem affecting more than 500 million people worldwide [[1], [2], [3]], with more than 80 % of adults having suffered from LBP [4], and LBP-related problems costing more than $100 billion a year in healthcare in the United States alone [5,6]. Degenerative changes in the intervertebral discs (IVDs) lead to biomechanical and structural failures of the IVDs, resulting in disorders such as disc herniation, spondylolisthesis, and spinal stenosis [[7], [8], [9]], which have been widely recognised as the primary pathological cause of LBP [10]. Current clinical treatment regarding intervertebral disc degeneration (IDD) is limited to symptomatic relief through analgesic or surgical means [11], but fails to address the underlying pathology. Genetic, traumatic, and mechanical loading factors can exacerbate IDD [12,13], but the definitive pathological mechanisms of IDD are unknown and still need to be further explored [14].Fig. 1IFT88 expression in degenerated discs.   The IVD is composed of the nucleus pulposus, annulus fibrosus, and cartilaginous endplates [15]. The cartilaginous endplates are the intermediary of the contact between the vertebral bone and the intervertebral disc, and are involved in balancing the pressure between the intervertebral disc and the vertebral body, and the cartilaginous endplates are the key to the correct transport of passive diffusion of nutrients and the ability to discharge waste products [16]. In order to maintain the normal function of the IVD, the proper function of endplates for the cartilaginous transport function is essential [17]. Damage to the endplate cartilage is one of the hallmarks of IDD [18]. Irreversible damage to the endplate cartilage leads to a reduction in joint function and a series of pathogenic changes downstream [19]. Abnormal pathological calcification and thickening of the endplate are important causes of disc degeneration [20], but the mechanism of endplate calcification remains unclear.

  Mechanical stress can effectively regulate cellular activities such as cell proliferation, apoptosis, differentiation and autophagy [21], but abnormal mechanical loading can lead to oxidative stress, apoptosis and extracellular matrix degradation, which is an important cause of endplate cartilage calcification [22]. Primary cilia exist on chondrocytes as cellular “antennae”, which can receive mechanical and chemical stimuli from the extracellular environment, and are an important structure for the cell to feel mechanical stimuli [23], and can play a key role in regulating a variety of biological processes through various signalling pathways such as Wnt and Hedgehog [24]. It is an important structure for cells to receive mechanical stimuli from the extracellular environment. However, the effect of primary cilia on calcification has not yet been studied, and the mechanism has not yet been clarified.

  One of the earliest ciliary components defined in mammalian cells, intracilium transporter protein 88 (IFT88), is a core cis- or B-type transporter protein [25] − which transports proteins to the tip of the primary cilium and is therefore essential for cilium assembly and function [26]. As a key molecule of the ciliary machinery that transduces chondrocytes in response to mechanical stimuli, IFT88 has a significant impact on cartilage [27]. Disruption of IFT88 leads to increased catabolism in chondrocytes in vitro [28], and knockdown of IFT88 prevents cells from successfully assembling primary cilia [29]. However, we know little about the mechanism of IFT88 on cartilage calcification and further exploration is needed.

  In this study, we explored whether IFT88 could influence disc degeneration by affecting calcification. We identified a potential protective effect of IFT88 against disc degeneration. It was also surprising to find that the protective effect of IFT88 is potentially depleting and is affected by the degree of mechanical loading, and the mechanism of this depletion was hypothesised through the relationship between IFT88 and its transcription factor C/EBPα. Mechanistically, we investigated the inhibitory effect of IFT88 on the calcium ion channel TRPV4, which reduces the abnormal increase of intracellular calcium ions due to mechanical stress, and probed the alterations in the downstream Wnt signalling pathway and oxidative stress pathway. The protective mechanism of IFT88 against calcification and disc degeneration was revealed in vitro and validated in vivo in a rat model.


## Materials and methods

### Human endplate tissue collection
  To investigate the relationship between IFT88 and the degree of disc degeneration, we selected endplate cartilage tissues from 12 patients with lumbar disc herniation requiring surgical treatment, with IDD aged 43–56 years based on the Pfirrmann grading criteria for further experiments (Table S1). In our study, patients in whom endplate cartilage tissue was harvested during surgery did not have other significant complications related to IDD.


### Western blot analysis
  Cartilaginous endplate cells (CEPCs) were placed in RIPA lysis buffer containing protease inhibitors and lysed by sonication. The lysate is then centrifuged at high speed at 4 °C to remove cell debris, and the supernatant is collected. Proteins were extracted for western blotting analysis. Brands and concentrations of antibodies used are in the Supplementary Material (Table S2). Specific experimental steps were performed as described in previous experiments [30].


### RNA reverse transcription and real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT–qPCR)
  RNA was extracted using a commercial RNA extraction kit (Share-bio, China). Cells were lysed on ice by adding lysis buffer followed by thorough pipetting. An equal volume of ethanol was then added, and the mixture was transferred to a spin column. After centrifugation, the column was washed with wash buffer and centrifuged again. Following a brief standing period, elution buffer was added, and the RNA was collected by centrifugation. Using reverse transcriptase (Vazyme, China) to convert RNA to cDNA, followed by PCR amplification with specific primers and real-time monitoring of the PCR process using fluorescent dyes for quantitative analysis. The experiments were performed as we described previously, and quantitative PCR analyses were performed on a QuantStudio 7 Flex (Thermo, USA) using SYBR Green (Yeasen, China). The expression of related genes was normalised to that of GAPDH. The primers used in this study are shown in the Supplementary Table S3.


### Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) assay
  CEPCs were fixed with glutaraldehyde electron microscopy fixative for two hours at room temperature, rinsed and treated with 1 % osmium acid, dehydrated with acetone to embed thin sections for staining, and finally photographed by TEM.


### Rat tail crush model
  A total of 72 12-week-old male Sprague–Dawley rats were used. No stress intervention was applied after mounting the rats in the sham operation group. To avoid introducing degeneration caused by needling the disc, 2 µl of virus (1 × 10 6 TU) was injected into the C8-C9 endplate cartilage by intraoperative X-ray fluoroscopy of the viral groups (knockout virus LV-IFT88i group; overexpression virus LV-IFT88 group) using a microinjector with a total volume of 5 µl and a 32G needle syringe. Controls were injected with 2 µl of control virus (knockdown control virus CON-LVi group; overexpression control virus CON-LV group). The rat tail was fixed between the C8-C9 caudal vertebrae using a spring-loaded Ilizarov-type device (Fig. S1) [31]. A long screw was passed through aluminium alloy rings, and four calibrated 5 N/cm springs were fitted to each screw to ensure that the screws passed through the four side holes of the middle aluminium alloy ring without resistance. Once the instrument was mounted, axial pressure was applied equally from the distal ring to produce a calculated compressive stress of 1.3 MPa, which was then secured with nuts. Moulding was prevented from infection by intramuscular injection with veterinary penicillin for one week. Rats that could not be subjected to sustained compression due to loosening of the screw nut as well as rats with secondary infection in the caudal spine were removed (Fig. S2).


### Establishment of a lumbar spine instability (LSI) model
  Disc degeneration was induced in 10-week-old male C57 mice using the LSI model [32]. The mice were anaesthetised, and after separating the dorsal skin, the supraspinous ligament, interspinous ligament and spinous processes of lumbar 3-lumbar 5 segments were clipped, while in the control group, only the paraspinal dorsal muscles were separated. The IVDs of the mice were harvested for immunohistochemical staining at 8 and 12 weeks after surgery.


### Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and X-ray
  After anaesthetising the rats, lateral radiographs of the rat tail spine were taken using X-ray at each time point (exposure time, 0.06 s; distance, 100 cm; current, 160 mA; voltage, 50 kV). A 3.0 T MRI system was used to photograph the caudal spine of rats in the prone position (repetition time, 1,600 ms; echo time, 85 ms; field of view, 80 × 80 mm; slice thickness, 2.0 mm).


### Immunohistochemical (IHC) histology and sections
  The caudal vertebrae of rats and L3-L5 spines of mice were collected and sectioned. Then the sections were fixed in 4 % neutral formaldehyde for 24 h and decalcified on a shaker for one month. Sections were stained with haematoxylin and eosin (HE), toluidine blue (TB), and senna O-solid green (SO). As previously described, and each sample was scanned with a section scanner (Hamamatsu, Japan).


### Calcium ion detection
  After washing the CEPCs with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) according to the instructions of the Fluo-4 Calcium Ion Assay Kit (Beyotime, S1061M, China), the liquid was aspirated. One millilitre of Fluo-4 staining working solution was added to each well and incubated for 30 min at 37 ℃ in the dark. The cells were washed with PBS, and the intensity of green fluorescence was observed by fluorescence microscopy. Each group was observed at the same time after incubation.


### ROS detection
  ROS production was detected using the ROS Assay Kit (Beyotime, China). They were stained with 10 μM DCFH-DA for 20 min at 37 °C, then imaged by fluorescence microscopy and ROS levels were measured by flow cytometry.


### Plasmid and lentivirus transfection
  The Rat_Cebpα plasmid, Rat_Ift88 promoter (−2042 to −43) WT plasmid, and Rat_Ift88 promoter (−2042 to −43) MT plasmid were purchased from Shanghai Genomeditech Co. Ltd. The plasmids were used to transfect 293 T cells and CEPCs according to the manufacturer’s protocol [33]. When 30–40 % confluence was reached, CEPCs were transfected with lentivirus LV-IFT88 (GeneChem, Shanghai, China) at multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 125 and LV-IFT88-RNAi (GeneChem, Shanghai, China) at MOI of 100. After 12 h of transfection, the complete medium with puromycin was changed for screening, and when the cells reached over 95 % confluence, all the transfected cells were passaged for further experiments.


### ShRNA transfection
  While CEPCs reached 50–60 % confluence, 4  μg of shRNA (GeneChem, Shanghai, China) was added to each well of a 6-well plate. After 6 h of serum-free transfection, the medium was replaced with complete medium. Experiments were performed 48 h after transfection.


### Coimmunoprecipitation (Co-IP)
  Anti-IFT88 (Proteintech, China) antibodies, Anti-TRPV4 (Abcam, England) antibodieswere used at a dose of 4 µg of according to the instructions of the Co-IP kit (Abmart, China). Proteins were collected and analysed by western blotting [34].


### Protein structure prediction and docking
  The full-length AlphaFold 2 (DeepMind, United Kingdom) predicted structure of TRPV4_RAT (Uniprot ID: Q9ERZ8) was selected as the receptor protein and ligand protein, while the amino acid sequence of A0A8I5ZPQ6_RAT (Uniprot ID: A0A8I5ZPQ6) was selected for downloading, and the protein structure was modelled using AlphaFold 2, the software was executed on a local server in Ubuntu 18.04. In this paper, protein docking was performed through HDOCK server. The free energy of binding was calculated using MM/GBSA of HawkDock server. The binding interfaces of protein–protein complexes were fully described and systematically analysed using the PLIP interaction analysis platform. Interaction-related details were then supplemented by PyMOL (Schrödinger, United States).


### Finite element analysis simulates forces on intervertebral discs in a rat tailbone compression model
  In order to investigate the mechanical stress changes in the endplates of rats in a compression model of the tailbone, we performed finite element analysis to map the mechanical stress distribution of the endplates at normal height and under compression. We performed a finite element analysis to map, the mechanical stress distribution in the endplate of the rat tail spine at normal height as well as under compression. As described in the previous articles [[35], [36], [37]], we first assigned raw material properties to each part in ANSYS 21.0 (Table S4 in Supplementary Materials). We applied a compressive stress of 1.3 MPA to the rat caudal spine to simulate the forces on the disc in the compression model of the caudal spine.


### Ethics statement
  All surgical interventions, treatments and postoperative animal care procedures were performed in strict accordance with the requirements of the Animal Care and Use Committee of Zhongshan Hospital, Fudan University(#2023-168). Human endplate tissue collection and experiments involving human endplates were approved by the Ethics Committee of Jinshan Hospital (JIEC 2022-S77), Fudan University, and followed the guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki.


### Statistical analysis
  The results were calculated from at least three independent experiments and are expressed as the means ± Standard Deviation (SD). A one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was performed to assess the statistical significance of the results using GraphPad Prism (version 9.4) software to calculate the statistical significance between groups. The significance level was set at 0.05.


## Results

### Expression levels of IFT88 in endplate cartilage are associated with the degree of disc degeneration
  In order to reveal the expression of IFT88 in endplate cartilage, immunohistochemical staining of human discs with Pfirrmann scale II-V showed a highpercentage of IFT88-positive chondrocytes in the endplate of six grade II-III patients. In contrast, the proportion of IFT88-positive cells in grade IV-V patients was less than 10 % (Fig. 1 A and B), no gender-related differences were observed (Fig. S3). We further verified this using a mouse model of lumbar spine instability. IFT88 immunopositive cells in degenerated tissues were fewer in the LSI group compared to the Sham group at 8 and 12 weeks. The decline became more pronounced with greater degeneration, with IFT88-positive cells significantly reduced (Fig. 1 D and E). It is well documented that IFT88 plays an important role in regulating the ossification of primary cilia and protecting them from mechanical damage. ### IFT88 plays a potential protective role in endplate chondrocytes in vitro
  IFT88 is an important component of the primary cilium [38], and we hypothesise that it exerts a stress stimulus by sensing its protective role, but this protective role may be “exhausted”. We investigated changes in IFT88 under different stress interventions. Transmission electron microscopy of primary cilia structures and immunofluorescence of acetylated microtubule proteins showed that primary cilia tended to lengthen in response to short stress stimuli, but showed some shortening in response to overstretching (Fig. 2 B and C).

  At 10 % elongation, the expression of IFT88 also increased with increasing stress frequency. However, under the intervention of 0.3 HZ stress frequency, the expression of IFT88 at 15 % elongation rate was smaller than that at 10 % elongation rate (Fig. 2 D-G). To further investigate the variation of IFT88 with stress, we performed western blotting experiments, which showed that the expression of IFT88 tended to increase when the time, frequency and amplitude of stress intervention increased. However, when the stress exceeded a certain level (10 % elongation, 0.5 HZ, 24H), IFT88 levels showed a decreasing trend, and interestingly, when the frequency and amplitude of stretching were further increased (10 % elongation, 1 HZ; 15 % elongation, 0.3 HZ; 15 % elongation, 0.5 HZ; 15 % elongation, 1 HZ), IFT88 levels peaked earlier and started to show a decreasing trend (Fig. 2 D-I). This may indicate that the protective effect of primary cilia is limited and depleting.

### C/EBPα is a positively regulated transcription factor of IFT88 and is negatively feedback regulated by IFT88
  To deeply explore the upstream regulatory transcription factors of IFT88, we first predicted the promoter site of IFT88 using jaspar and demonstrated that the IFT88 promoter was positively regulated by C/EBPα using a dual luciferase reporter assay in 293 T cells (Fig. 3L and 3 M). For further validation, we transfected C/EBPα overexpression plasmid (OE) into rat primary endplate chondrocytes and found that overexpression of C/EBPα also promoted IFT88 expression in endplate chondrocytes (Fig. S5A and S5B). Interestingly, by successfully overexpressing IFT88 in endplate chondrocytes (Fig. S6) after simultaneous stress intervention with its control group, we found that C/EBPα was significantly lower in the overexpression of IFT88 group than in the control group, which could be a potential mechanism for primary cilia depletion under stress (Fig. S5C and S5D).To further investigate the degradation pathway of C/EBPα, we examined the phosphorylation sites of C/EBPα [[39], [40], [41]] (Thr230 and Ser21) by Western blotting. We found that with the overexpression of IFT88, the degradation of C/EBPα was accompanied by a decrease in phosphorylated C/EBPα. This suggests that the reduction of C/EBPα may not be due to increased phosphorylation levels, but rather other potential mechanisms (Fig. S7).


### Overexpression of IFT88, calcium antagonists, and TRPV4 inhibition alleviate overstress-induced endplate cartilage degeneration
  To investigate the effect of calcification on disc degeneration, we examined the impact of calcium ions on endplate chondrocyte degeneration under abnormal stress using the calcium antagonist BAPTA, which is commonly employed to study intracellular calcium influx. TRPV4, a mechanosensitive calcium channel [42], may play an important role in excessive calcium inward currents caused by tensile stress. The calcium antagonist BAPTA and the TRPV4 channel antagonist GSK205 (10 µM) [43] inhibited excessive calcium inward flow, but the phenomenon was exacerbated by the use of the TRPV4 channel agonist GSK101 (1 nM) [43] (Fig. 4 A). And overexpression of IFT88 similarly suppressed the abnormal increase in calcium ion concentration under abnormal tensile stress (Fig. 4 B). CEPCs were first transduced with either an IFT88-overexpressing lentivirus or a control lentivirus, followed by transduction with either TRPV4 shRNA or control shRNA, generating four experimental groups: CON-LV + CON shRNA (Movie S1), CON-LV + TRPV4 shRNA (Movie S2), LV-IFT88 + CON shRNA (Movie S3), and LV-IFT88 + TRPV4 shRNA (Movie S4). Following mechanical stretch intervention, calcium imaging was conducted for one minute. Compared with the control group, cells in the IFT88-overexpressing group exhibited a significantly faster reduction in intracellular calcium levels, as evidenced by a more rapid decline in green fluorescence intensity. Furthermore, in the LV-IFT88 + TRPV4 shRNA group, fluorescence intensity decreased even more rapidly and was nearly undetectable, indicating a synergistic effect of IFT88 overexpression and TRPV4 knockdown on calcium influx suppression (Movie S1-Movie S4). The calcium antagonist BAPTA also prevented the aberrant activation of the Wnt pathway gene β-catenin under abnormal stress and delayed the decline of the oxidative stress protection factor Nrf2 and HO-1. It also reduced the elevation of calcification-related genes such as OCN and OPN, also protected the extracellular matrix (Fig. 4 C and D). ### IFT88 protects disc structure and function in vivo by inhibiting calcification
  By observing HE staining, toluidine blue staining, and reddish green staining of the rat tailbone at 4 and 12 weeks after modelling, the LV-IFT88i group had a better histomorphology of all parts of the IVD than the experimental group at 12 weeks. The cartilage structure of the endplates was still in a standardized state, relatively smooth and intact, with normal cell numbers and good intensity of toluidine blue and saffron solid green staining. In contrast, the fibrous ring, nucleus pulposus, and endplate cartilage of the LV-IFT88i group were disorganized, and the endplate cartilage layer almost disappeared after 12 weeks (Fig. 7 B-D). Histological scoring of the rat tailbone was performed after 8 and 12 weeks, and the rats in the LV-IFT88i group scored close to 12 at 12 weeks, with severe disc damage. In contrast, the LV-IFT88 group scored only about 6 points, which was significantly lower than that of the CON-LV group, and the disc morphology was well maintained (Fig. 7 E). Based on the imaging data at 4, 8, and 12 weeks after modelling of the rat tailbone compression at four times, and subsequent MRI and X-ray imaging, the overexpression virus LV-IFT88 injection group had better disc grading, and intervertebral space height than the control group, which were significantly better than those of the knockdown virus LV-IFT88i group (Fig. 7 F-H). We found that the mechanical stress distribution of the rat caudal intervertebral disc was altered by finite element analysis, and when the disc height decreased, the maximum stress on all parts of the disc increased, and the overexpression of the IFT88 group protected the disc height and reduced the abnormal stress on all parts of the disc (Fig. 8 A). Immunohistochemistry and immunofluorescence of rat endplate cartilage showed that lentivirus overexpressing IFT88 inhibited the expression of TRPV4 calcium channel, which promoted the expression of oxidative stress protection factors (Nrf2 and HO-1), and suppressed the aberrant increase of β-catenin, an important factor of Wnt pathway, and the calcification-related genes (OCN, OPN and ALP). ## Discussion
  Primary cilia are present in most eukaryotic cells expressed and act as a mechanotransduction [44,45], Wnt signaling pathway coordination center [46]. In order to investigate the role of primary cilia in endplate cartilage, we first collected samples from clinical patients for immunohistochemical examination, and found that the positivity rate of IFT88 in endplate cartilage samples with severe disc degeneration was significantly lower than that of samples with mild degeneration [47]. However, in this experiment, the intervertebral disc endplate samples collected from patients ranged from 43 to 56 years of age, which presents a certain age span. Additionally, the experimental samples lacked a control group of normal endplate cartilage. These limitations will be addressed and further improved in subsequent experiments. Additionally, all the mice used in this study were male, which may introduce gender-related differences. Female animals, influenced by hormones, could experience distinct effects on the intervertebral disc microenvironment. Further experiments are needed to investigate the impact of IFT88 on intervertebral disc degeneration in female mice.

  This study primarily uses tensile stress to intervene in endplate chondrocytes, but various types of mechanical stress also exist in the human body. Compressive stress is one of the most common mechanical forces in the spine. Moderate compressive stress helps maintain intervertebral disc (IVD) homeostasis and promotes extracellular matrix (ECM) synthesis, while prolonged or excessive compressive stress induces cell apoptosis, matrix degradation, and ECM structural damage [[48], [49], [50]]. Shear stress has been less studied in the context of the intervertebral disc, but it is particularly important in cases of spinal instability or abnormal segmental motion. Excessive shear stress can disrupt the cytoskeletal structure of IVD cells, activate inflammatory responses, and affect mechanical signaling. In nucleus pulposus cells, shear stress may also alter cell morphology and promote the expression of degeneration markers [[51], [52], [53]]. Hydrostatic pressure is a common mechanical stimulus. Moderate and intermittent hydrostatic pressure helps maintain cell activity and ECM synthesis, while excessively high or low non-physiological hydrostatic pressure may impair cell function, inhibit ECM synthesis, and accelerate intervertebral disc degeneration [[54], [55], [56]]. The combined effects of different types, magnitudes, durations, and frequencies of mechanical loading determine whether homeostasis is maintained, or degeneration occurs. A deeper understanding of the interaction between these mechanical forces and cellular signaling pathways will provide important insights for the prevention and treatment of IDD.

  We hypothesized that primary cilia, as an important organelle in the cellular sensing of mechanical stress [57], and its important molecule, IFT88 [58], may be a protective factor for IVDs subjected to abnormal stresses. This suggests that IFT88 in primary cilia plays an important role in cartilage development and in protecting the normal function of cartilage in response to mechanical stress. The downstream effects of IFT88 in primary cilia are mechanically driven, ensuring that chondrocytes adapt to the extracellular matrix and are better able to withstand forces [59,60]. By analyzing the trend of IFT88 under different tensile stress frequencies, different tensile stress amplitudes, and different tensile stress intervention times, we found that after mild tensile stress interventions endplate chondrocytes show a tendency to increase in IFT88. But under excessive stress interventions, the expression of IFT88 decreases to a certain extent after a peak. And this peak would appear earlier when increasing the frequency of tensile stress and the magnitude of tensile stress. We believe that IFT88 is a protective factor when the IVD is subjected to abnormal stress and plays a role in inhibiting disc degeneration to a certain extent, but we found that the protective effect of the primary cilia is limited and depleting. This suggests that IFT88 may be a mechanical regulator of cartilage under stress intervention, but this regulation is limited and does not work when the stress is too high, which may be related to the “depletion” effect of primary cilia reported in other articles [47].

  Chondrocytes are orientated to a complex in vivo mechanical loading environment [61,62]. In addition to compressive loading and fluid shear, chondrocytes also experience cyclic traction in vivo, and sensing of extracellular loading signals through chondrocytes is a critical step in chondrocyte mechanotransduction [63]. To demonstrate the protective effect of IFT88 against IDD under stress-intervening conditions, we overexpressed and knocked down IFT88. We found that IFT88 overexpressing viral transfected group attenuated the abnormal stress-induced calcification-associated genes OCN, OPN, COLX, and ALP. IFT88 overexpressing viral transfected group reduced the abnormal elevation of Wnt pathway-associated genes Axin2 and β-catenin and reduced the excessive reduction of oxidative stress protection factors Nrf2 and HO-1 under abnormal stress. In contrast, the IFT88 knockdown virus group showed elevated calcium concentrations and abnormal activity of the Wnt pathway due to increased strain. Knockdown of IFT88 by lentivirus also exacerbated the depletion of Nrf2 and HO-1 oxidative stress protective factors and increased the expression of genes related to oxidative stress and calcification induced by the abnormal stress, leading to degenerative-related changes in cartilage due to osteogenesis. It has been demonstrated that deletion of total cartilage IFT88 also leads to activation of the Hedgehog signaling pathway [64,65], which results in cartilage degeneration. Specific ablation of IFT88 is also an early marker for osteoarthritis, and aberrant activation of the Hedgehog signaling pathway can worsen the phenotype of cartilage degeneration and exacerbate the progression of osteoarthritis [66].

  Previous experiments have demonstrated that inhibitors of the calcium channel TRPV4 significantly reduce calcium levels and the expression of matrix catabolic genes, such as metalloproteinases [67,68], to prevent the progression of normal cartilage to an osteoarthritic phenotype in the treatment of aged chondrocytes, and TRPV4 has been recognized as a potentially valuable therapeutic target for joint disease [69]. It has also been recently demonstrated in the literature that TRPV4 knockdown alleviates disc degeneration and that activation of TRPV4 mediates degradation of the disc matrix under abnormal stresses [70]. Although TRPV4 is indispensable for mechanotransduction in normal chondrocytes, its dysfunction in arthritic phenotypes is also an important aspect [71]. TRPV4 co-localizes with IFT88, and CO-IP as well as protein molecule docking demonstrates the interaction between the two. TRPV4 acts as a calcium ion channel and may be a potential downstream mechanism by which IFT88 affects calcification. Moreover, endplate chondrocytes did not increase TRPV4 expression after IFT88 overexpression virus infection, which resulted in its overactivation after pulling. Furthermore, through calcium imaging, we found that the overexpression of IFT88, in combination with the knockdown of TRPV4, can reduce the increase in intracellular calcium concentration caused by excessive stress intervention. TRPV4 is likely to be an important downstream target of IFT88 to affect calcium concentration.

  TRPV4 is likely to be an important downstream target of IFT88 in affecting calcium concentration. In order to verify the downstream mechanism of IFT88, we performed stress interventions on the overexpressing lentiviral group and its control group by transfecting with TRPV4 shRNA or adding TRPV4 activator and TRPV4 blocker. Under the influence of the TRPV4 inhibitor GSK205 or TRPV4 shRNA, the overexpressed viral group exhibited an increase in oxidative stress protective factors as well as a decrease in the expression of calcification-associated genes, which was accompanied by a further reduction in Wnt pathway genes. In contrast, the TRPV4 activator GSK101 reverses to some extent the favorable effects of overexpression of IFT88. Flow cytometry and ROS fluorescence observations also confirmed that the IFT88 overexpression group produced the least amount of ROS by the cells upon addition of the TRPV4 blocker GSK205. This demonstrates that TRPV4 acts as a downstream of IFT88, and that IFT88 expression inhibits TRPV4 overactivation, preventing aberrant activation of the Wnt pathway and generation of oxidative stress states caused by high intrachondral calcium ion concentrations. We validated C/EBPα as a positive transcription factor of IFT88 in 293 T cells as well as in endplate chondrocytes, respectively. Interestingly, the expression of the C/EBPα transcription factor was reduced after stretch in endplate chondrocytes overexpressing IFT88, which may be a potential depletion mechanism that still needs to be further explored. However, by assessing the phosphorylation of C/EBPα, we found that the degradation of C/EBPα induced by IFT88 overexpression was not due to an increase in its phosphorylation level. The exact mechanism underlying this process still requires further investigation.

  IFT88 may play different roles at different stages, with some studies suggesting that IFT88 deletion may thicken articular cartilage at the juvenile stage in mice [26,72]. For the regulation of cilia, some experiments suggest that IFT88 may respond to inflammatory stimuli Inflammation may induce cilia lengthening thereby modulating Hedgehog signaling to mediate inflammatory responses [73]. Calcification of cartilage may be associated with Enpp1 [74], a pyrophosphatase, which can inhibit calcification by switching to Hedgehog signaling, which is positively correlated with the expression of IFT88, and the increase in calcification in IFT88 knockout mice may be associated with IFT88. It has been proposed that in mouse joints, IFT88 in conditional knockout mice may have a mechanically driven-dependent effect, possibly because deletion of IFT88 leads to arthritis in mature mice with reduced cartilage thickness, and disruption of the primary cilia system in growth plate cartilage also leads to inappropriate cilia production and defects in columnar orientation, which affects their phenotype [75]. To further validate the role of IFT88 in the calcification of IVD endplate cartilage, we performed in vivo validation in animals using a vertebral compression model after in vivo local injection of lentivirus in the IVD. In the overexpression of IFT88 group, the disc structure and morphology were better than those of the control group, and the levels of extracellular matrix and oxidative stress protection factor were significantly higher than those of the other experimental groups, and the degree of endplate calcification was lower. In contrast, the IFT88 knockdown group had a heterogeneous disc structure and obvious degeneration.

  This study has several limitations. Firstly, normal undegenerated samples were not collected, and further more explicit exploration of the tendency of IFT88 to change in endplate cartilage samples at each level of grading based on IVD grading has been undertaken. Secondly, the protective effect of IFT88 against disc degeneration was verified in vivo in this experiment in rats, which needs to be considered when translating rat observations to the human condition. In addition, our mouse lumbar instability model of induced IDD as well as the rat caudal compression model does not fully mimic the clinical situation of human degenerative discs. In addition, this experiment was carried out in animals using local injection of the virus, other components of the IVDs were not investigated, and the experimental animal was a rat, which could be genetically knocked out or experimented in a large animal IVD in order to further improve the experiment. Finally, the therapeutic potential of treatments specifically targeting calcification for IDD has not been clinically evaluated and warrants further study.


## Conclusion
  In conclusion, IFT88 can regulate intracellular calcium concentration and inhibit the over-activation of Wnt and oxidative stress pathways by decreasing the expression of TRPV4, thereby protecting the endplate cartilage of the IVDs and alleviating the degeneration caused by abnormal stresses, and it may be an important potential target for the prevention of IDD.
