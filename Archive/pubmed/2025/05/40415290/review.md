## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and conclusions as presented in the abstract; no full-text methods, figures, or supplementary data were available for verification
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report the design, expression, and purification of fusion enzymes comprising terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase (TdT) from *B. bovis* and single-stranded DNA-binding protein (EcSSB) from *E. coli*. They claim successful purification under native conditions, improved thermal stability of the fusion proteins, prevention of aggregation, and an unexpected enzymatic activity where the fusion enzyme removes a nucleotide (TTP) from the 3'-end of the DNA substrate rather than adding nucleotides.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no figures, tables, sequence data, or experimental protocols were provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full methods, structural models, purification chromatograms, differential scanning fluorimetry and dynamic light scattering data, HPLC activity traces, statistical analyses, and any sequence or construct details

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a potentially interesting application of gene fusion to a terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase, with a clear rationale and a notable unexpected finding regarding altered enzymatic activity. However, the evidence base is limited to a summary, and several claims are either under-supported or internally inconsistent. The most significant issue is the contradiction between the predicted homotetrameric structure and the measured monomeric radius for TdT, alongside the unexplained reversal of enzymatic activity from nucleotide addition to removal. The abstract does not provide sufficient detail to assess the robustness of the methods or the validity of the conclusions. The work may be of interest to researchers in DNA synthesis and enzyme engineering, but the case is not fully established from the supplied material.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers working on enzymatic DNA synthesis, particularly those seeking cost-effective alternatives for *de novo* DNA production, would be interested in the fusion strategy and its effects on thermostability. Additionally, investigators studying DNA-binding protein fusions to polymerases or transferases, and those interested in the structural and functional consequences of protein engineering, may find the results relevant. The unexpected exonuclease-like activity could also attract attention from enzymologists studying catalytic promiscuity.
- **Major strengths** The rationale for fusing TdT with DNA-binding proteins is clearly articulated and builds on prior work with thermostable polymerases. The use of multiple biophysical and biochemical methods (differential scanning fluorimetry, dynamic light scattering, HPLC) indicates a multi-pronged approach to characterization. The report of a novel activity (nucleotide removal) is potentially significant and could open new avenues for investigation.
- **Major Concerns**  
  - R1-M1: The abstract reports a homotetrameric structure predicted *in silico* but a measured radius for TdT consistent with a monomer. This discrepancy is not explained, and the implications for the fusion proteins' quaternary structure are unclear.  
  - R1-M2: The fusion enzyme is reported to remove a nucleotide (TTP) from the 3'-end instead of adding nucleotides. This is a fundamental reversal of the expected activity, yet no mechanistic explanation or supporting data are provided.  
  - R1-M3: The claim of increased thermal stability is based on the prevention of aggregation, but the abstract does not provide quantitative data (e.g., melting temperatures) or a direct comparison of thermal stability between native and fusion proteins under identical conditions.  
  - R1-M4: The yield values are presented without context regarding purity, activity, or reproducibility, and the significance of the differences between constructs is not discussed.
- **Minor Comments**  
  - R1-m1: The abstract does not specify the linker lengths or design rationale for TdT_L1_EcSSB versus TdT_L2_EcSSB, which would be important for interpreting the results.  
  - R1-m2: The statement that the fusion "broaden the enzyme's application field" is vague and not supported by specific evidence in the abstract.  
  - R1-m3: The conclusion that the fusion "resulted in increased thermal stability and a reduced ability to add nucleotides" conflates two separate observations; the causal link between them is not established.  
  - R1-m4: The abstract mentions "molecular dynamics and homology modeling" but provides no details on the validation of the models or the confidence in the predicted tetrameric structure.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** The discrepancy between predicted and measured oligomeric states (R1-M1) and the unexplained reversal of enzymatic activity (R1-M2) are critical technical issues. Without resolution, the core claims of the manuscript cannot be considered reliable. Additionally, the lack of quantitative thermal stability data (R1-M3) weakens the central claim of improved thermostability.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**  
  - *Originality*: The concept of fusing TdT with DNA-binding proteins is not entirely novel, given prior work with polymerases, but the specific application to TdT and the observed activity reversal could be considered original. However, the abstract does not demonstrate sufficient novelty to distinguish it from existing literature.  
  - *Scientific importance*: The potential for cost-effective DNA synthesis is of broad interest, but the unexpected activity reversal raises questions about the utility of the fusion enzyme for its intended purpose. The importance is currently moderate and contingent on resolving the mechanistic issues.  
  - *Interdisciplinary readership*: The work spans protein engineering, enzymology, and DNA synthesis, which could attract a multidisciplinary audience. However, the abstract is too brief to convey the broader implications effectively.  
  - *Technical soundness*: The methods listed are appropriate, but the lack of data and the internal inconsistencies prevent an assessment of technical soundness.  
  - *Readability for nonspecialists*: The abstract is reasonably clear but uses technical terms without sufficient explanation, and the unexpected result is presented without context, which may confuse nonspecialist readers.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The abstract presents intriguing findings, but the internal inconsistencies and lack of supporting data mean that the core claims cannot be validated. The authors should be encouraged to provide a full manuscript with detailed methods, quantitative results, and a mechanistic explanation for the observed activity reversal.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that the fusion proteins form a homotetramer is unsupported by the measured data, which suggest a monomeric TdT.
- The claim of increased thermal stability is not quantitatively supported; only aggregation prevention is mentioned.
- The claim that the fusion enzyme "removed a nucleotide, specifically TTP" is presented without any mechanistic or kinetic data.
- The assertion that the fusion "broaden the enzyme's application field" is speculative and not backed by evidence in the abstract.
- The yields reported (130 mg/L, 5 mg/L, 9 mg/L, 7 mg/L) are presented without purity or activity context, making them difficult to interpret.
