# Molecular dynamics simulation and docking studies reveals inhibition of NF-kB signaling as a promising therapeutic drug target for reduction in cytokines storms


## Supplementary Information
  The online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-78411-5.


## Introduction
  Cytokine storm is a clinically relevant condition that has been associated with several life-threatening diseases. Cytokines are intricately linked to cell death mechanism in mammals and are involved in the positive feedback loop, whereby cytokine signaling or PAMP/DAMP sensing causes inflammatory cell death that facilitate further pathogenic cytokine release, culminating in the cytokine storm to drive severe life-threatening damage to host tissues and organs1. Early in the 1990s, the phrase “cytokine storm” was initially used to characterize the consequences of graft-versus-host diseases (GvHD)2,3 and later, in the infectious disease setting, during the early 2000s in studies of cytomegalovirus (CMV)4, Epstein-Barr virus (EBV)5 associated hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH), group A streptococcus (GAS)6, influenza virus7, variola virus8, and SARS-CoV1,9–12. During the 2005 “bird flu” outbreak, the cytokine storm was also mentioned in cases of human H5N1 avian influenza virus infection. Since then, interest in the concept of cytokine storm has been growing among scientists, with the continuing coronavirus diseases 2019 (Covid-19) pandemic, which is brought on by the SARS-CoV-2 virus, serving as a further catalyst.

  Nearly 30 years ago, the nuclear factor kappa-light chain enhancer of activated B cells (NF-kB) was identified. Since then, because of its multiple therapeutic uses and pathological effects, it has been utilized as an inducible transcription model13,14. Although NF-kB has a diverse range of functions, there are still questions concerning how this small set of signaling mediators combines a variety of stimuli into cell type- and stimulus-specific responses. Studies have shown that NF-kB does not work alone; instead, its crosstalk interactions with other signaling pathways orchestrates diverse NF-kB responses15,16. Furthermore, the pathogenesis of critically ill Covid-19 cases has been associated with the NF-kB signaling pathway17,18. These implications are based on the studies carried out during the severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus (SARS-CoV) and Middle East respiratory syndrome coronavirus (MERS-CoV) pandemics. Numerous viral proteins, including nucleocapsid, spike, SARS-CoV-2 non-structural proteins (nsp) 1, nsp3a and nsp7a can hyperactivate the NF-kB, which propagates cytokines storm and contributes to the multi-organ damage leading to a high fatality rate19,20. Based on the facts and current Covid-19 scenario, the inhibition of the NF-kB signaling pathway as therapeutics intervention seems to be an inevitable hypothesis for future studies.

  In mammals, the NF-kB family is composed of five related transcription factors: p50, p52, p65 (also RelA), c-Rel and RelB (Fig. 1). These transcription factors share an N-terminal.

  DNA-binding/dimerization domain, known as Rel homology domain, through which they can form homo- and heterodimers. NF-kB dimers can bind to a variety of related target DNA sequences called kB sites to modulate gene expression. RelB, c-Rel and p65 contain C-terminal transcription activation domains (TADs) that enable co-activator recruitment and target gene expression. As they lack TADs, p50 and p52 can activate transcription by forming heterodimers with p65, c-Rel, or RelB, or by recruiting other TAD-containing proteins21. However, as homodimers lacking to drive transcription, they can repress transcription on binding to DNA. The five mammalian NF-kB transcription factors are divided into two groups. The first is a complex of three mature and active proteins: c-Rel, RelA (p65) and RelB and the second consists of inactive precursor proteins: NF-kB1 (p50/p105) and NF-kB2 (p52/p100). The discovery was made that NF-κB proteins assemble in dimeric formations, of which only heterodimers possess a functional significance22. NF-kB dimers, particularly the p50/p65 heterodimer, are widely present in almost all cell types. The binding of NF-κB dimers to specific inhibitors (IκB) is what keeps them in the cytoplasm. However, there are three distinct signaling pathways that result in the activation of NF-κB. It triggers the movement of genes associated with inflammation, which are regulated by NF-κB, to the cell nucleus, where they become active23,24. Moving forward, it became apparent that a heightened activation of NF-κB results in a significant increase in the production of proinflammatory mediators, ultimately culminating in the development of a cytokine storm25.

  Significantly, investigations into the inhibition of cytokines are currently being conducted for COVID-19. For instance, glucocorticoid drugs are used in the early stages of an inflammatory cytokine storm to inhibit acute respiratory distress syndrome and protect organ function26. There are several reported potential treatments for cytokine storm, like IL-6 inhibition using Tocilizumab, NF-kB and lipocortin-1 inhibition using corticosteroids, PD-1 checkpoint inhibitor like Nivolumab, BMS-936,559, cytokine adsorption device like NCT04195126, NCT03685383, NCT03974386, Intravenous Immunoglobulin (IVIG) and Hydroxychloroquine (HCQ)27.

  A development of a good cytokine storm inhibitor is a necessity for the preparedness and response for the future pandemics associated with the viral infections. Recent, Covid-19 pandemic was the breaking point for preparedness of pandemic, during this time period for treatment of cytokine storm using Tocilizumab was judiciously used for blocking the interleukin-6 (IL-6) receptors. Tocilizumab was also suggested by WHO with the combination of Corticosteroids or baricitinib making it the only drug available for treating the cytokine storm in Covid-19. Owing to the scarcity of alternative treatments for cytokine storm, the manufacturer faced mounting pressure to satisfy the heightened demand for Tocilizumab, which had previously been prescribed for patients with rheumatoid arthritis (RA), giant cell arteritis (GCA), polyarticular juvenile idiopathic arthritis (PJIA), and systemic juvenile idiopathic arthritis (SJIA). As a result, a shortage of medication has emerged for these patients. The primary objective of the current computational study was to identify the target site and create a novel cytokine inhibitor that could prevent, modulate, or inhibit the binding of the NF-kB p50/p65 heterodimer complex to kB sites, which regulate gene transcription and initiates cytokine production.


## Results and discussion
  The outline of the experimental study was to investigate the NFkB p50/p65 heterodimer possessing a biological activity function and bind to a variety of related target DNA sequences called kB sites to modulate gene expression for cytokine storms. A four-stage strategy approach was used to investigate the modulation of NFkB p50/p65 heterodimer with kB sites. First the construction of molecular database which are the derivatives of Psoralen and Angelicin; second the screening of this database against NF-kB by means of docking procedure; third the study of the poses on NF-kB of the most interesting, selected compound; in fourth stage the top lead molecules are simulated in complex with NFkB p50/p65, kB site and lead molecules.

### Construction of the targeted chemical library
  The first step was the construction of targeted chemical libraries of Angelicin and Psolaren based small molecule database28. Two approaches were used for designing the compound one was using rational approach, and another was using WADDAICA webserver29, which uses a deep learning models for scaffold hoping of compounds to modify or design new novel drugs. The developed library of 101 compounds is shown in Supplementary Tables 3 and 4, which contained 50 rationally designed molecules and 51 from the WADDAICA webserver (Fig. 2). Rationally designed molecule contained customized functional group on the scaffolds of Angelicin and Psoralen as shown in Figs. 2A,B and 3.

### Target pockets of NF-kB and molecular docking
  The identification of binding pocket is considered as an initial step for structure-based drug discovery. Two distinct methods were used to find the pockets for the modelled structure. The first approach involved using CASTp 3.0 and trj_cavity to identify the druggable pockets. Another approach was using blind docking with Autodock4.2 with Angelicin as probe. Previously, Angelicin were reported for the inhibition of NF-kB28, this was one of the reasons for selecting Angelicin as a probe. Using these two approaches two druggable pockets were identified as A1 and A2 (Fig. 4A). Identified pocket A1 resides in the NF-kB-p50 domain, surrounded by charged residues and polar residues like Asp279, Asp278, Lys277 and Gln276 respectively (Fig. 4D). Pocket A1 surface hydrophobicity and electrostatic properties revealed that it is majorly composed of polar (Fig. 4B) and positively charged residue as it is closer to kb site (Fig. 4C). While the identified pocket A2 resides in interaction junction of NF-kB p50/p65 heterodimer. Pocket A2 surface hydrophobicity and electrostatics surface properties revealed that the identified pocket is surrounded with charged (Fig. 4C) polar residue and non-polar residues (Fig. 4B), from p65 domain residues involved were Lys221, Gln241, Asp243, Val244, Arg246, Gln247 and from p50 domain Asp273, Lys251 and Val253 (Fig. 4E).

  The molecular docking approach was followed against NF-kB heterodimer (p50/p65) for both the identified pockets using Auto dock 4.2. In Table 1 the docking results of the in-silico screening are shown for top binding affinity molecules from both the pockets in heterodimer (p50/p65) complexed with kB sites. The docking studies were carried out with Lamarckian Search and genetic algorithm (LGA) in pocket A1 and A2 independent of each other. The selection of docked compounds was based on the binding affinity values in each target. The docked compounds showing the best binding affinity values in each pocket (Supplementary Tables 3, 4) were selected among the rational and WADDAICA design. Moreover, the molecules with higher rank in more than one conformation of the heterodimer were preferred. Among the possible docking hits, three molecules (NF49, NFAI64 and NF30) were chosen based on their to selectively interacting to the heterodimer NF-kB (p50/p65) complex in individual pockets (A1 and A2) with a binding affinity greater than − 6.6 kcal/mol (Table 1).

  After this analysis, it revealed that compound NF49 (psolaren-like structure, binding energy = − 6.79 kcal/mol), the NFAI64 (diphenyl like structure, derived from Angelicin) has showed binding affinity for pocket A1 and A2 with − 6.6 and − 6.74 kcal/mol respectively. The small molecules designed with rational approach showed higher average binding affinity (− 5.79 ± 0.35 kcal/mol) in pocket A1 as compared to WADDAICA designed molecules (− 5.48 ± 0.48 kcal/mol), while in case of pocket A2 showed similar characteristics average binding affinity, rationally designed being higher (-5.59 ± 0.31 kcal/mol) and WADDAICA being lower (− 5.49 ± 0.50 kcal/mol) (Fig. 6A). ### Ligand binding modes and interactions of NF-kB
  All the individual best ranked ligand poses in heterodimeric states were analyzed with BIOVIA Discovery Studio Visualizer for interaction studies (Fig. 5). The interaction analysis of pocket A1 with molecules NFAI64 negatively charged residue Asp278 from p50 domains forms pi-anion interaction. Lys277 was majorly involved in the formation of Pi-alkyl interaction with the ring and methyl group of the molecule. Three conventional hydrogen bonding formations were observed with hydroxyl and amine group of molecules to kB site (Fig. 5A), formation of multiple conventional hydrogen bond to kB site change/restrict the rate of transcription which initiates the formation of cytokines in cells (Fig. 1). Gln308 from p50 domain forms the Pi-donor hydrogen bond to ring of the molecule while Fluorine group of the substrate forms the carbon-hydrogen bonds with the kB site. The interaction of NF49 revealed that the negatively charged residue Asp278 from p50 domains forms conventional hydrogen bonds with hydroxyl group (Fig. 5B). Lys277 was majorly involved in the formation of Pi-alkyl interaction with the ring of the molecule. Pi-anion interactions were also observed with Asp278 to ring of the substrate, in another scenario ring of the substrate formed pi-anion interaction with the Kb site (Fig. 5B). Both the molecules (NFAI64 and NF49) showed similar molecular interaction of the compound and similar preferential binding in pocket A1.

Dynamic properties of each complex were compared with the dynamic behavior of ligand free protein. From 10ns MD simulation trajectory, the RMSD and was calculated and plotted against time and residue index respectively. The molecular simulation of all top molecules revealed stable interactions in both the pockets (Supplementary Movie 1) and stayed in the pocket during complete course of simulation for both the runs. The MM-PBSA binding affinity for small molecules during complete course of simulations revealed that molecule NF30 showed highest binding energy of − 60.92 ± 1.83 kJ/mol in pocket A2 (Fig. 6C). While the binding energy of NF49, which showed highest energy in docking showed least energy in the MM-PBSA calculations, this could be the possible due to solvent exposed binding to the target region (Fig. 6C).

In pocket A1 it was observed with − 45.04 kJ/mol and for pocket A2 -44.29 ± 5.22 kJ/mol (Fig. 6C). ## Conclusions
  There are two main pathways for NF-kB signaling: the conventional or canonical pathway and the non-canonical pathway (or alternate pathway)31. The canonical pathway is primarily activated by TNF-α, IL-1, and LPS. On the other hand, the non-canonical pathway is activated by LTα/β, CD40 ligand, and Blys/BAFF, but not by TNF-α, IL-1, and LPS31. In this study, we have screened potential 3 compounds designed using rational and WADDAICA29 that can inhibit the canonical nF-kB pathway by screening a comprehensive collection of approximately 101 small molecules compounds derived from Psoralen and Angelicin28. The following compounds have been approved by regulatory agencies for human use or clinical trials: Psoralen, Angelicin, and furocoumarin derivatives. Some of these substances have been identified or are believed to inhibit NF-kB28,32 and also used in the treatment of cancer.

  The virtual screening method we adopted against NF-kB p50/p65 lead to the identification of three potential, Psoralen and Angelicin derivative that can inhibit the NF-kB/DNA complex formation. The putative protein binding modes of these substituted psoralen (NF49 and NF30) and angelicin derivative (NFAI64) have been individuated in described in both the pockets (A1 and A2), hinting new candidate molecules for future design of NF-kB activity modulators ligands and biological inhibition activity for NF-kB/DNA. Moreover, other chemical structures have been suggested as potential NF-kB inhibitors from our screening studies. Molecular simulations of screened molecules showed prominent interactions in their respective binding pockets as shown in Supplementary Movie 1. However, there were significant differences in the interaction pattern of kB site nucleotides and amino acids residues of NF-kB. The NF30 showed maximum atomic contact of ~ 89% with NF-kB amino acid residues correlating with observed binding affinity (ΔGbinding) of the molecule 60.92 ± 1.83 kJ/mol in pocket A2. In the amino acid interactions of NF-kB, charged residues like Lys, Arg, His showed prominent interactions in the case of all the screened molecules. These charged residues pockets give a potential to design further on charged based small molecules or peptides which can inhibit NF-kB binding to the kB sites, which has also negative charge characteristics and can work on the principle of competitive inhibition. The screened NF49 showed a completely different atomic interaction pattern in molecular simulation as compared to the other two molecules (NFAI64 and NF30), more than 72% of interactions were observed from nucleotides (kB sites). Various novel compounds that target different nodes in the NF-kB pathway are currently being synthesized or are in the process of being developed, and there is a substantial amount of ongoing interest in creating NF-kB inhibitors for the treatment of diseases such as cancer and cystic fibrosis. Additionally, there is an increasing trend in exploring new clinical applications for drugs that have already been approved for other indications. This is due to the lengthy, expensive, and unpredictable nature of the process involved in developing new drugs. We have discovered new putative NF-kB inhibitors in the current investigation. Our discovery of novel NF-kB inhibitors within the current pharmacopoeia sheds light on the potential binding region of recently discovered molecules as well as their mode of therapeutic action.

  As NF-κB is involved in the initiation of the cytokine storm, diseases such as Covid-19, Anaphylaxis, Graft-versus-host disease, acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), Systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS), Hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH), Chimeric antigen receptor-T (CAR-T) cell therapy, and Sepsis, which are primarily involved in the initiation of the cytokine storm, can provide a roadmap and can also be used for inhibiting the cytokine storm related to these diseases. In conclusion, NF-κB plays a crucial role in the pathogenesis of cytokine storm-associated diseases, and targeting this pathway may provide a potential therapeutic strategy for managing these conditions. Future research should focus on developing targeted therapies that can inhibit the cytokine storm in these diseases.


## Materials and methods

### Protein preparation
  Human p50/RelA(p65) complex is modelled using AlphaFold33,34 from uniport retrieved sequences Q04206 (RelA) and P19838 (NFKB1/p50). The modeled structure was superimposed with Mus musculus NFkB p50/p65 heterodimer complexed to the immunoglobulin kB sites (PDB ID: 1VKX)30. The properly folded region of p50 (residue 42–354) and p65(residue 18–293) were kept for study, while the rest unfolded structures were deleted. The modelled structure was validated using Ramachandran plot (Figure S1) using PROCHECK from SAVES v.6.0 server (https://saves.mbi.ucla.edu)35–37.


### Construction of compound library
  A potential inhibitor of 101 compounds were derived from the Angelicin and Psolaren scaffold using a rational design of the pharmacophore and using a WADDAICA29 server (https://heisenberg.ucam.edu:5000/), which uses a deep learning method for scaffold hoping of compounds to modify or design new novel drugs. In WADDICA method of novel drug generation, Angelicin and Psolaren scaffold were used.


### Identification of the NF-kB target pockets and molecular docking
  The modelled structure pockets were identified using the CASTp 3.038 server (http://sts.bioe.uic.edu/castp) and trj_cavity39 version 2.1 was used to identify the druggable pockets. While in another approach blind docking was used with Autodock4.240 as Angelicin as a probe.

  Molecular docking was performed in Auto dock version 4.240 to get the best conformation of the target-ligand interaction. The Lamarckian genetic algorithm (LGA) was applied to find the interaction pattern between protein and ligand. A grid of 60, 60, and 60 points in the x, y, and z directions, with grid spacing 0.375 Å was used around the DNA binding region. The grids were prepared reflecting the area of the DNA binding region of the protein. The maximum number of poses per ligand to pass to the grid-refinement calculation was set to 100. Only one good pose for each molecule was retained. Subsequently, all possible redundant structures were eliminated from the database. The ranking of target-ligand interaction was done based on free binding energy.


### Molecular simulations of top lead molecules
  MD simulations were carried out for NF-kB with top lead molecules using the AMBER99SB force field as implemented in GROMACS41 version 2023 with using the respective crystal structures as starting points. To prepare the system for simulations, each structure was placed in a triclinic box at 0.4 Å from the box boundary and solvated with TIP3P water model42 then neutralized with potassium ions. Electrostatics were calculated using the Particle-Mesh Ewald sums method43 with a real space cut-off of 10 Å, using order of 4 and a relative tolerance between long- and short-range energies of 10−5. Short range interactions were evaluated using a neighbor list of 10 Å, and the Lennard–Jones interactions, and the real space electrostatic interactions were truncated at 9 Å. The temperature was maintained at 300 K using V-rescale; hydrogen bonds were constrained using the LINCS algorithm. Energy minimization was carried out to reach a maximum force of no more than 10 kJ/mol using steepest descent algorithm. Mulliken charges of small molecules were calculated for structures optimized by density-functional theory at B3LYP/6-31G(d) as implemented in GAMESS version 3044. The topology of small molecules was defined with ACPYPE45. Data reported are the means of two independent simulations. Each structure was simulated twice for 10 ns, totaling 100 ns of simulation time.


### MMPBSA binding free energy
  The molecular mechanics Poisson-Boltzmann surface area (MMPBSA) method was utilized to determine the binding affinity of NF-kB-DNA-lead complexes46,47. Here a total of 200 snapshots from 20 ns of molecular dynamics were utilized to calculate the binding energy of top NF-kB-DNA-lead complexes. The per residue decomposition analysis was performed to calculate the binding energy contribution of active site residues with the ligand in NF-kB-DNA-lead complexes.
