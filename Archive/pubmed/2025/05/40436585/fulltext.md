This study aimed to prepare and identify anti-photoaging peptides from royal jelly proteins (RJP), and to investigate their protective effects and molecular mechanisms against ultraviolet B (UVB)-induced photoaging in human keratinocytes. A low-molecular-weight peptide fraction (<3 kDa, P-RJP1) exhibiting high antioxidant activity was obtained through pepsin hydrolysis of RJP followed by ultrafiltration fractionation. It was found that P-RJP1 effectively restored the viability of UVB-irradiated human keratinocytes, and alleviated oxidative stress by enhancing the activity of antioxidant enzymes and reducing intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels. Furthermore, P-RJP1 attenuated UVB-induced cell damage and inflammatory responses by inhibiting the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK)/nuclear factor kappa B (NF-kappaB) signaling pathway. Further purification of P-RJP1 by gel filtration chromatography yielded two major peptide fractions (F1 and F2). The F2 fraction, which exhibited superior antioxidant activity, was subjected to liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) for peptide sequence identification. Molecular docking revealed that the peptides FDYDFG, FDYDPKF, and FDL could establish stable hydrogen bonds with matrix metalloproteinase-1 (MMP-1) to inhibit its activity. These results indicated that P-RJP1 could effectively alleviate UVB-induced photoaging through multi-target effects.
