# Lactylation modulation identifies key biomarkers and therapeutic targets in KMT2A-rearranged AML


## Supplementary Information
  The online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-86136-2.


## Introduction
  Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML) is an aggressive hematologic malignancy characterized by the rapid proliferation of immature leukocytes in the bone marrow, disrupting normal hematopoiesis1–3. Among the various subtypes of AML, the KMT2A-rearranged subtype (KMT2Ar-AML) is notable for its poor prognosis and rapid disease progression4,5. Rearrangements of KMT2A on chromosome 11q23 have been identified in 5–10% of pediatric AML cases and are considered adverse prognostic factors6. To better understand the molecular basis of KMT2Ar-AML, we used high-throughput microarray platforms, with detailed sample information for the CN-AML (n = 70) and KMT2Ar-AML (n= 52) cohorts. These platforms identified significant changes in gene expression and epigenetic modifications that may serve as potential diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers7. In recent years, the role of epigenetic modifications, particularly lactylation, has gained increasing attention as a crucial factor in cancer biology8,9.

  In recent studies, it has been demonstrated that histone lactylation regulates gene expression and advances cancer. Researchers have found that lactylation-related enzymes, such as lactate dehydrogenase, are associated with poor outcomes in various cancers, including non-small cell lung cancer, ocular melanoma, and clear cell renal cancer10,11. This suggests that lactylation may represent a new therapeutic target for enhancing patient outcomes. Furthermore, research highlights how lactylation influences immune responses and shows promise in overcoming immunosuppression within the tumor microenvironment12,13. Notably, histone lactylation has been implicated in the regulation of key oncogenic pathways, such as the PI3K/AKT/HIF-1α signaling pathway, further emphasizing its relevance in cancer therapy14,15. These findings position lactylation as a promising focal point for developing innovative cancer treatments.

  This study examines the role of lactylation in KMT2A-rearranged acute myeloid leukemia (AML) by analyzing lactylation-dependent differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and identifying key epigenetic regulators as potential therapeutic targets. The findings offer insights into mechanisms that may affect AML progression, prognosis, and treatment response, although further experimental validation is necessary.


## Results

Comparative analysis identified 646 DEGs, with 360 upregulated and 286 downregulated. This observation highlights substantial molecular variances specific to KMT2Ar-AML, as illustrated in Fig. 2A.

### Identification and validation of diagnostic markers
  To uncover potential diagnostic markers, a multifaceted machine-learning strategy was implemented. Initially, LASSO regression analysis identified 9 significant genes from the statistically relevant univariate variables (Fig. 3A). Following this, Random Forest analysis (Fig. 3B) highlighted the top 10 genes based on their importance. Furthermore, SVM-RFE (Fig. 3C) was employed to refine the identification of significant genes. The intersection of these methodologies culminated in the identification of 6 core genes, designated as LactoKey Genes: PFN1, S100A6, CBR1, LDHB, LGALS1, and PRDX1 (Fig. 3D). We investigated the interrelationships among these specific genes, utilizing red to signify positive correlations and green to indicate negative correlations, as illustrated in Fig. 3E. In order to assess the diagnostic relevance of these genes, a receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed (Fig. 3F). This analysis highlighted the predictive efficacy of the LactoKey Genes concerning disease manifestation, yielding the following area under the curve (AUC) values: PFN1: AUC = 0.77, S100A6: AUC = 0.855, CBR1: AUC = 0.791, LDHB: AUC = 0.806, LGALS1: AUC = 0.863, and PRDX1: AUC = 0.74. The AUC values suggest that the LactoKey genes hold considerable promise for the diagnosis of KMT2Ar-AML. Notably, LGALS1 and S100A6 demonstrate the most significant diagnostic precision, with AUC values recorded at 0.863 and 0.855, respectively. These findings suggest that the LactoKey genes are not only essential for elucidating the biological mechanisms underlying KMT2Ar-AML but may also serve as valuable biomarkers to help distinguish KMT2Ar-AML from CN-AML, complementing traditional diagnostic methods like karyotyping for KMT2A rearrangements.


### Immune infiltration analysis of LactoKey genes
  The examination of immune cell infiltration indicated a positive association among diverse immune cell populations. Nevertheless, specific subsets, particularly Type 2 T helper cells, exhibited negative correlations with other immune cell types, notably Activated B cells, Type 17 helper cells, CD56dim natural killer cells, Natural killer cells, and Immature dendritic cells (refer to Supplementary Fig. 4). In samples from patients with KMT2Ar-acute myeloid leukemia (AML), there was a marked increase in the predicted proportions of several immune cell populations, which included CD56dim natural killer cells, Eosinophils, Gamma delta T cells, Myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs), Macrophages, Monocytes, Natural killer cells, and Neutrophils, when compared to control AML samples. Notably, MDSCs exhibited elevated levels in both KMT2Ar-AML and control AML, demonstrating distinct immune cell subset profiles (Fig. 4A). Correlation analysis between the LactoKey Genes (CBR1, PFN1, S100A6, LGALS1, LDHB, and PRDX1) and immune cell infiltration indicated that CBR1, PFN1, S100A6, and LGALS1 generally exhibited positive correlations with most immune cells, whereas LDHB and PRDX1 tended to show negative correlations, as illustrated in Fig. 4B-G.

### Biological functions of LactoKey genes
  Through the execution of correlation analysis and Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) of Reactome pathways pertaining to LactoKey genes, facilitated by the clusterProfiler package in R, we uncovered distinct functional relationships for each gene. This underscores their contributions to the pathogenesis of KMT2Ar-AML. Selected pathways were prioritized for KMT2Ar-AML based on gene relevance, biological function, leukemia mechanisms, and statistical significance(Reactome pathways data for LactoKey genes are provided in Supplementary Table S1). The detailed functional associations are as follows: CBR1 is implicated in several pathways, including the citric acid (TCA) cycle and respiratory electron transport, mitochondrial translation initiation and elongation, selenoamino acid metabolism, and selenocysteine synthesis (Fig. 5A, highlighted in red). This underscores its essential role in maintaining mitochondrial function, regulating cellular metabolism, and responding to oxidative stress. LDHB is involved in eukaryotic protein synthesis, encompassing fundamental processes such as Translation, Eukaryotic Translation Initiation, and Peptide chain elongation (Fig. 5B, highlighted in red). LGALS1 participates in pathways pertinent to Neutrophil degranulation, Response of EIF2AK4 (GCN2) to amino acid deficiency, Eukaryotic Translation Initiation, Cap-dependent Translation Initiation, and Nonsense Mediated Decay (NMD) independent of the Exon Junction Complex (EJC) (Fig. 5C, highlighted in red). PFN1 primarily functions in cell signaling and the regulation of protein translation, affecting processes such as SLIT-ROBO signaling and translation elongation (Fig. 5D, highlighted in red). PRDX1 contributes to mitochondrial function and energy metabolism, as well as cell cycle regulation, particularly through its involvement in respiratory electron transport and cyclin D degradation (Fig. 5E, highlighted in red). S100A6 is involved in cell signaling, inflammatory responses, and protein translation, playing distinct roles in SLIT-ROBO signaling, peptide chain elongation, and ribosomal assembly, as illustrated in Fig. 5F (highlighted in red). These in-depth characterizations enhance our comprehension of the molecular mechanisms implicated in KMT2Ar-AML and underscore the pivotal contributions of each LactoKey gene to disease advancement and prospective therapeutic interventions. This thorough examination elucidates the manner in which these genes partake in the pathogenesis of KMT2Ar-AML, as depicted in Fig. 5.

### Lactylation subtypes in KMT2Ar-AML: divergent expression profiles and pathway associations
  Our analysis identified two distinct lactylation subtypes in KMT2Ar-AML, referred to as Cluster A and Cluster B, using the ConsensusClusterPlus R package to perform consistency clustering based on key immunomodulators. Cluster A is distinguished by increased expression of PFN1 and S100A6, suggesting a unique lactylation profile. In contrast, Cluster B is defined by elevated expression of LDHB, LGALS1, and PRDX1, indicating a contrasting gene expression pattern across clusters, highlighting the heterogeneity within KMT2Ar-AML (Fig. 7A-C).

  The biological differences between these lactylation subtypes were investigated through pathway analyses. Cluster A showed significant associations with pathways such as KEGG endocytosis and Notch signaling, while Cluster B demonstrated positive correlations with pathways related to proteasome activity, Protein export, folate-mediated one carbon metabolism, and pyruvate metabolism. These findings offer valuable insights into the unique biological processes and disease associations of each subtype (Fig. 7D).

  In the Reactome pathway analysis, Cluster A demonstrated significant relationships with the modulation of cell death-related genes via FOXO-mediated transcription, the innate immune response to cytosolic DNA, and interleukin signaling pathways. In contrast, the associations identified in Cluster B suggested the suppression of Notch4 signaling, the destabilization of mRNA mediated by AUF1 HNRNP D0, the stabilization of the p53 protein, the metabolism of polyamines, and the cross-presentation of soluble exogenous antigens within endosomal compartments. These observations offer valuable insights into the molecular mechanisms that distinguish the two lactylation subtypes present in KMT2Ar-AML. (Refer to Fig. 7E).


### Drug sensitivity analysis of LactoKey genes
  In our comprehensive examination of drug sensitivity for the six LactoKey genes in the KMT2Ar-AML context, we screened 198 compounds and identified 43 with significant sensitivity profiles. To ensure a targeted and effective analysis, these compounds were chosen due to their well-characterized mechanisms of action and their potential to specifically interact with the identified LactoKey genes. Notably, among the top ten most responsive drugs were PI3K inhibitors, specifically Dactolisib and GNE-317. The top five drugs in terms of sensitivity, ranked by their inhibitory concentration values, were Sepantronium bromide, a CDK9 inhibitor, Luminespib, Dactolisib, and Epirubicin (Fig. 8A-B). We focuses on drug sensitivity profiles of the six key LactoKey genes. KMT2Ar-AML samples with high expression of the six key LactoKey genes (PFN1, S100A6, CBR1, LDHB, LGALS1, PRDX1) exhibited increased sensitivity to Dactolisib, Epirubicin, AZD7762_1022, Trametinib_1372, and Pevonedistat_1529 (Fig. 8C-H).

## Discussion
  Our study identified six key LactoKey genes—PFN1, S100A6, CBR1, LDHB, LGALS1, and PRDX1—that are strongly associated with KMT2Ar-AML, highlighting their potential as both diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets. PFN1 plays a vital role in tumor development by regulating cytoskeletal dynamics and key cellular processes16. S100A6 is crucial for CD34 + cell survival and contributes to the progression of myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS) to acute myeloid leukemia (AML)17. CBR1 supports mitochondrial function and redox homeostasis, playing a role in AML progression18. LDHB is integral to the glycolytic pathway and plays a role in the resistance to chemotherapy, exhibiting strong associations with protein biosynthesis19. LGALS1 is implicated in the progression of AML by fostering an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment, with its heightened expression correlating with unfavorable survival rates20–22. PRDX1 influences leukemic stem cells and is implicated in resistance to chemotherapy23. These results emphasize the pivotal roles of these genes in cellular metabolism, energy generation, and immune system modulation, underscoring their importance in the initiation and progression of KMT2Ar-AML.

  The investigation reveals insights into LactoKey Genes’ role in immune cell infiltration, notably in regulating myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs), which contribute to the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment in KMT2Ar-AML23–25. These genes offer a therapeutic strategy to alter the tumor microenvironment, potentially enhancing immune responses and clinical outcomes. Furthermore, the role of lactylation in immune evasion underscores its significance in disease progression.The role of LactoKey Genes in immune cell infiltration also suggests potential therapeutic targets, such as the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway, to modify the immunosuppressive milieu and enhance anti-tumor immune responses26,27.

  Regulatory network analysis suggested that hsa-miR-96 may enhance glycolysis by means of LDHB28, thereby supporting the survival of leukemia cells, while hsa-miR-361-3p provides tumor-suppressive effects29. The transcription factors CLEC5A, MYC, and TP53 were identified as key regulators of differentiation, stem cell maintenance, and metabolic pathways30–32, underscoring the regulatory complexity of KMT2Ar-AML. The distinction between lactylation subtypes A and B adds more evidence to the concept of disease diversity. Subtype A, which shows high PFN1 and S100A6 levels, is associated with Notch signaling, while Subtype B, dominated by LDHB and LGALS1, is linked to proteasome activity and pyruvate metabolism. The findings suggest that lactylation-related pathways could be involved in KMT2Ar-AML heterogeneity, but the mechanistic links are speculative and need further validation through functional assays to explore their therapeutic implications.

  The research identified 43 potential therapeutic agents, including PI3K inhibitors such as Dactolisib and GNE-317, that specifically target LactoKey gene pathways. Pharmacological agents like Epirubicin, AZD7762, and Trametinib impact cell proliferation mechanisms modulated by these genes33–35, while Pevonedistat affects protein stability with broader implications for tumor biology36. Preclinical validation is necessary to assess these compounds’ efficacy and safety, and further exploration of lactylation mechanisms in other AML subtypes is crucial for expanding therapeutic options.

  This study employs RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) and CIBERSORT to investigate immune cell infiltration, offering valuable insights. Nonetheless, bulk RNA-seq data is limited in its ability to differentiate the gene expression contributions of acute myeloid leukemia (AML) cells from those of infiltrating immune cells, potentially introducing biases in the interpretation of immune infiltration. Future research endeavors that incorporate single-cell RNA sequencing or flow cytometry could serve to validate and expand upon these findings.

  Future studies should focus on validating the role of LactoKey genes in KMT2Ar-AML through functional assays and in vivo research. Understanding how lactylation contributes to immune evasion and resistance mechanisms could enhance the effectiveness of combination therapies. Furthermore, research should aim to optimize the dosage and timing of PI3K inhibitors to improve their efficacy while minimizing side effects. Furthermore, integrating lactylation-targeted therapies with chemotherapy and immune checkpoint inhibitors could improve treatment strategies37,38. A comprehensive strategy integrating genomics, epigenetics, and immunology is essential for fully exploiting the therapeutic potential of LactoKey genes and improving patient outcomes across diverse cancer types.

  The study emphasizes lactylation’s role in KMT2Ar-AML, suggesting new biomarkers and therapeutic targets for personalized cancer treatment, although further research is necessary to validate these findings and their implications.


## Materials and methods

### Microarray data source
  A database of gene expression data (e.g., https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) can be found at the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO)39. Searching the GEO database for “Acute Myeloid Leukemia with Mixed Lineage Leukemia rearrangements”, “Homo sapiens” [porgn: txid9606], and “Expression profiling by array”. The R package sva(version 3.40.0) was used to analyze 70 KMT2Ar-AML samples, characterized by the 11q23 translocation of the KMT2A gene, and 52 control samples from wild-type AML without chromosomal abnormalities, contained in GSE1785540–42and GSE2205643. The PMID:37,242,42744, 35,761,06745, 36,092,71246 databases (Supplementary Table S2) were used to obtain 336 lactylation genes, respectively.


### Identification of immune-differentiated genes
  In order to assess the influence of immune-related gene expression levels on KMT2A, a differential gene analysis was conducted using the R package “limma.” This analysis aimed to identify genes that exhibit differential expression between diseased samples and control samples within the integrated dataset47. The criteria established for determining differentially expressed genes (DEGs) included an absolute log2 fold change (log2FC) greater than 0.5 and an adjusted P-value of less than 0.05, which indicates genes with upregulated expression. Conversely, a log2FC of less than − 0.5 alongside an adjusted P-value of less than 0.05 signifies genes with downregulated expression. Following this, DEGs associated with lactylation were analyzed. Specifically, a DEG is considered to be differentially expressed when its log2 fold change exceeds 0.5 and its adjusted P-value is below 0.05.


### Enhancement of functionality
  To ascertain the probable functions of various potential targets, Gene Ontology (GO) was utilized for the analysis. This approach specifically focused on three main categories: molecular functions (MF), biological processes (BP), and cellular components (CC)48. The Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) serves as a prominent repository for information pertaining to genomes, biological pathways, diseases, and pharmaceuticals49. In this study, the analysis of Gene Ontology (GO) annotations and the enrichment of KEGG pathways for differentially expressed genes were conducted utilizing the clusterProfiler package available in R50. A threshold of less than 0.05 for the false discovery rate was deemed to indicate statistical significance.


### Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA)
  Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was conducted utilizing the clusterProfiler package (version 4.2.2) in R. This analytical approach assesses both the statistical significance and the consistency of designated gene sets between two distinct biological states. To ascertain the adjusted p-values, the Benjamini-Hochberg method was employed, with a threshold for significance established at an adjusted p-value of less than 0.0551.


### RegNetwork Analysis
  RegNetwork serves as a comprehensive online resource that aggregates both experimentally confirmed and predicted data pertaining to gene regulation. Within this repository dedicated to regulatory networks, users have the capability to explore and identify various transcription factors (TFs), microRNAs (miRNAs), and genes, allowing for the examination of their combinatorial and synergistic regulatory interactions. The construction of a TF-miRNA-gene regulatory network was accomplished utilizing Cytoscape52.


### Identification of molecular subtypes utilizing key immunomodulators
  The consistency clustering approach is a resampling technique employed to ascertain the membership of individual components and their respective subgroup classifications, along with the validation of these clusters. The R package ConsensusClusterPlus was utilized to unveil immunological patterns predicated on notable differentially expressed immune-related genes (Imm-DEGs)53.


### Screening and validation of diagnostic markers
  In this study, novel and significant biomarkers for KMT2Ar-AML were identified through the application of three distinct machine-learning algorithms: random forests (RF)54, least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) logistic regression55, and support vector machine-recursive feature elimination (SVM-RFE)56. The random forest methodology was executed utilizing the R package “randomForest” (version 4.14). The LASSO logistic regression analysis was performed using the R package “glmnet” (version 4.4)57. A ten-fold cross-validation approach was employed to select the optimization parameters that satisfied the minimum criteria. Subsequently, genes exhibiting common characteristics across more than one of the aforementioned classification models were identified. To evaluate the predictive performance of the algorithms, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were analyzed, and the area under the curve (AUC) was computed. The statistical significance of the findings was established through a two-tailed t-test, with a P value threshold set at less than 0.05.


### Analysis of immune infiltration
  The immune microenvironment is primarily composed of various immune cells, inflammatory cells, fibroblasts, mesenchymal components, as well as cytokines and chemokines. Analyzing immune cell infiltration is crucial for predicting disease progression and the efficacy of therapeutic interventions. In this context, a method for RNA transcript analysis known as “Cell type identification by estimating relative subsets of RNA transcripts” (CIBERSORT) was utilized to quantify the infiltration of 23 distinct immune cell types58. The CIBERSORT algorithm facilitates the identification of significant subpopulations of immune cells within pathological conditions by enabling a quantitative analysis of these cell types. For visualization purposes, a box plot was employed to present the infiltration results across different groups, while a scatter plot illustrated the interrelations among various immune cells. Additionally, a lollipop plot was constructed to depict the relationship between specific characteristic genes and the infiltrating immune cell populations. Only findings with a significance level of P< 0.05 were included in the presentation of results59.


### The correlation between Gene expression and drug sensitivity
  The Drug Sensitivity Analysis of GSCA Lite has compiled data on 198 small molecules sourced from the Cancer Therapeutics Response Portal (CTRP) (https://portals.broadinstitute.org/ctrp/) and an additional 198 small molecules obtained from the Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer (GDSC) (https://www.cancerrxgene.org/)60,61. This analysis integrates cancer cell lines from both GDSC and CTRP to facilitate the assessment of drug sensitivity alongside gene expression profiling62. In this dataset, each gene’s expression is evaluated through Spearman correlation analysis in relation to the sensitivity of small molecules/drugs, quantified as the IC50 value.
