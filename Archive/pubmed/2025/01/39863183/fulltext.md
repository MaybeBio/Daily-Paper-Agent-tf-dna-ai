OBJECTIVE: The prevalence of herpes simplex virus type 1 (HSV-1) infection and the emergence of drug-resistant HSV-1 strains posts a significant global health challenge, necessitating the urgent development of effective anti-HSV-1 drugs. As one of the most prevalent molecular chaperones, heat shock protein 90 alpha (Hsp90alpha) has been extensively demonstrated to regulate a range of viral infections, thus representing a promising antiviral target. In this study, we identified JD-13 as a novel Hsp90alpha inhibitor and explored its capability in inhibiting HSV-1 infection. METHODS: The inhibitory effect of JD-13 on Hsp90alpha activity was confirmed by molecular docking, molecular dynamic stimulations, fluorescence quench titration and cellular thermal shift assay. The antiviral activity of JD-13 was examined by viral plaque assay, RT-qPCR, Western blot, flow cytometry, fluorescence microscopy and time-of-addition assay. The in vivo antiviral efficacy of JD-13 was evaluated in the HSV-1 skin infection guinea pig model by analyzing skin lesions and herpes formation. RESULTS: JD-13 significantly inhibited the infection of both normal and acyclovir-resistant HSV-1 strains. In addition, JD-13 alleviated skin damage in guinea pigs caused by cutaneous HSV-1 infection. Further studies revealed that JD-13 impaired HSV-1 early infection and suppressed the Akt/beta-catenin signalling pathway by promoting Akt degradation. Consequently, the inhibition of the Akt/beta-catenin signalling pathway restricted HSV-1 infection. CONCLUSIONS: These results suggest JD-13 as a novel HSP90alpha inhibitor with the potential to be developed as an antiviral agent for the treatment of HSV-1-related diseases.
