## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, results, discussion, conclusions, methods) as provided
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, methodological soundness, evidence quality, and internal consistency of the claims as presented in the provided text
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors integrate GWAS summary statistics, epigenomic annotations, and a deep learning framework (TREDNet) to prioritize regulatory single nucleotide variants (SNVs) in 11 cell types implicated in PCOS. They report that approximately 20% of PCOS risk variants are predicted to disrupt enhancer activity, that these variants are enriched for binding sites of transcription factors involved in androgen signaling and neuroendocrine regulation, and that prioritized variants tend to exert pleiotropic, predominantly downregulatory effects on target gene expression. The IRX3-FTO locus is presented as a case study linking obesity-associated mechanisms to PCOS pathogenesis.
- **Visible evidence base** Main text figures referenced (Figures 1A-C, 2A-D, 3A-E, 4A-B, S1-S9), supplementary tables (S1-S10), methods section, and data availability statements
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Supplementary tables and figures were not provided for inspection. Figure panels are referenced but not shown. No code or model architecture details beyond a brief methods description. No access to the deposited models or training data. No information on hyperparameter selection, model training details, or reproducibility of the DL pipeline beyond what is stated.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript addresses a timely and important problem in PCOS genetics, namely the functional interpretation of non-coding risk variants. The integration of a deep learning framework with cell-type-specific epigenomic data is a reasonable and potentially powerful approach. However, the evidence presented in the text is insufficient to fully evaluate the validity of the central claims. Several key analyses are described only at a high level, and the absence of the supplementary materials prevents verification of the statistical methods, control procedures, and the robustness of the model predictions. The case study of the IRX3-FTO locus is illustrative but relies on speculative mechanistic links that are not experimentally tested. The manuscript would benefit from clearer presentation of the model evaluation, more rigorous statistical controls, and a more cautious interpretation of the functional significance of the prioritized variants.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in the fields of complex disease genetics, regulatory genomics, and computational biology would find this work of interest. The application of deep learning to prioritize causal variants in PCOS is a novel contribution that could inform future functional studies. Clinicians and translational researchers focused on PCOS may also be interested in the proposed regulatory mechanisms, though the lack of experimental validation limits immediate clinical relevance.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses a significant gap in PCOS research by moving beyond GWAS locus identification toward mechanistic interpretation. The use of multiple cell types, including neuroendocrine and peripheral tissues, is a thoughtful approach given the pleiotropic nature of PCOS. The integration of eQTL data and TF binding site analysis provides a multi-layered view of variant effects. The case study of the IRX3-FTO locus is a useful demonstration of how the model can be applied to generate testable hypotheses.
