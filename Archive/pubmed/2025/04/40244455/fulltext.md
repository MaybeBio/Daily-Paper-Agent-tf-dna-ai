# HIF-1α/LTBP2 axis activate HSCs to promote liver fibrosis by interacting with LOXL1 via the ERK pathway


## Supplementary Information
  The online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00018-025-05682-0.


## Introduction
  Liver fibrosis (LF) is caused by excessive accumulation of extracellular matrix (ECM) in the liver and can be considered as the result of prolonged liver damage caused by various factors such as hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection, alcohol abuse and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) [1]. Abnormal or excessive fibrosis is generally considered to be the culprit for disruption of normal liver anatomy and deterioration of liver function in patients with chronic liver diseases, including cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma [2]. Unfortunately, treatment pathways for liver fibrosis remain an unsolved clinical problem worldwide. Therefore, a deeper understanding of the molecular basis of liver fibrosis is important and necessary to support the development of novel anti-fibrotic therapies.

  Despite a variety of causes, liver fibrosis is always mediated by myofibroblasts, a specialized cell lineage similar to fibroblasts and myocytes [3]. Virtually undetectable in the liver under physiological conditions, myofibroblasts emerge rapidly after liver injury occurs and produce ECM proteins to initiate the fibroblast response [4]. Hepatic stellate cells (HSCs) are one of the main non-parenchymal cell types in the liver characterized by a star-shaped morphology and a large number of cytoplasmic lipid droplets in the normal liver [5]. During liver injury, under a series of stimuli, HSCs are activated and transformed into myofibroblasts, which act as the main cell source of the ECM, leading to fiber scarring and hepatic lobular destruction [3]. HSCs has received much attention as a therapeutic target for alleviating and reversing liver fibrosis. In many preclinical studies, inhibiting the activation of HSCs and /or eliminating activated HSCs has been shown to reverse liver fibrosis. However, the mechanisms and specific markers of HSCs activation have not been fully elucidated, which hinders the research and development of liver fibrosis drugs.

  Latent transforming growth factor beta binding protein 2 (LTBP2) belongs to the family of latent transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β) binding proteins (LTBPs), which are extracellular matrix proteins with multi-domain structure [6]. LTBP2 is the only member of the LTBP family that does not bind any isoforms of TGF-β, although it may interfere with the function of other LTBPs or interact with other signaling pathways [7]. Notably, in our previous study [8], we observed significant up-regulation of LTBP2 expression based on transcriptome sequencing data obtained from samples of liver fibrosis associated with HBV. We also searched additional databases of liver fibrosis caused by HBV, HCV, MASH and alcoholic liver disease. The results revealed that LTBP2 expression was consistently increased, irrespective of the underlying cause of LF. However, the association of LTBP2 with the process of liver fibrosis and the underlying mechanisms remain to be determined.

  Epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) refers to the process by which epithelial cells undergo a series of changes, including the loss of cell-cell junctions and apical-basal polarity, reorganization of the cytoskeleton, modify the signaling programs that define cell shape, and reprogram gene expression to transform into mesenchymal cells [9]. HSCs can undergo EMT to trans-differentiation into myofibroblasts, which simultaneously accelerates HSCs proliferation [9–11] and promotes the deposition of ECM, thereby facilitating the progression of liver fibrosis [10]. Research shows that LTBP2 is upregulated in human thyroid cancer cell lines, knockdown of LTBP2 can inhibit the proliferation, invasion and EMT of thyroid cancer cells [12]. However, the role of LTBP2 in the activation of HSCs and EMT during liver fibrosis remains to be elucidated.

  Therefore, in our current work, we sought to explore the important role of LTBP2 in the progression of liver fibrosis and the underlying mechanisms involved. The expression of LTBP2 was significantly elevated in fibrotic liver tissues from human and mice. In addition, LTBP2 expression was up-regulated during the activation of HSCs. Notably, adeno-associated virus 6 (AAV6)-mediated knockdown of LTBP2 in the HSCs of mice significantly alleviated the liver fibrosis in CCl4-induced fibrotic mice. In vitro studies have shown that the accumulation of LTBP2 promotes the activation of HSCs, proliferation, migration and EMT in LX-2 cells.

  Through bioinformatics prediction, we identified HIF-1α as transcription factors for LTBP2. Protein-Protein Interaction (PPI) analysis suggests that LOXL1 may have a latent interaction with LTBP2. In addition, LTBP2 promoted the activation of HSCs and EMT, likely through the Mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling pathway. These findings provide direct experimental evidence that LTBP2 has a critical function in liver fibrosis and may be a potential target for therapeutic intervention against liver fibrosis.


## Methods

### Bioinformatics analysis
  Correlation analysis of gene expression data in human liver tissues was performed using the GEPIA2 database (http://gepia2.cancer-pku.cn/). Analysis of single-cell sequencing was performed using an online website [13] (https://shiny.igc.ed.ac.uk/livercellatlas/). We used the R package to acquire gene expression profile data from the GEO datasets (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). The GSE68001 [14] data included 3 primary human quiescent HSCs (qHSCs) samples and 3 activated HSCs (aHSCs) samples. The GSE149508 [15] for quiescent and activated HSCs from control mice and mice with CCl4-induced liver fibrosis. And the GSE84044 [16] data included 124 HBV-LF patients, categorized as follows: 43 samples in S0, 20 samples in S1, 18 samples in S2, 33 samples in S3 and 10 samples in S4.


### Histological evaluation of liver
  The liver tissue was fixed with formaldehyde solution and embedded in paraffin wax. Deparaffin and rehydrated liver sections were pathologically evaluated by hematoxylin-eosin (HE), Masson staining, staining of reticular fibers and Sirius red. The stained sections were observed with an optical microscope.


### Immunohistochemistry of human liver tissues
  The liver tissue of human was fixed with 10% formalin, embedded in paraffin, sliced into 4 ~ 6 μm and placed on a slide. After dewaxing, rehydration, and microwave antigen recovery, slides were diluted with α-Smooth Muscle Actin (α-SMA) (BM3902, Boster) antibody and LTBP2 (sc-166199, Santa) antibody at 4 °C at 1:200 overnight. Incubation of the second antibody at room temperature for 40 min, DAB substrate staining, hematoxylin reverse staining.


### Immunofluorescence of mice liver tissues
  Paraffin-embedded liver Sect. (4 μm thick) were dewaxed and rehydrated in ethanol. The slices were then incubated with 3% H2O2 for 10 min, followed by antigen retrieval in citrate buffers. Sections were sealed with goat serum and incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against α-SMA (BM3902, 1:200). Then, the slices were incubated with CY3 labeled goat anti-rabbit IgG (Servicebio, 1:300) and stained with DAPI. The staining of the slices was observed by microscope.


### Animals
  Animals are kept in a controlled environment with a light-dark cycle of 12 h. Mice at 8 weeks of age were intraperitoneally injected with CCl4, which was diluted with olive oil, and intraperitoneally injected with 0.4 mL/kg CCl4, thrice a week for 8 weeks to establish a mice fibrosis model. Two weeks after intraperitoneal injection of CCl4, these mice were administered with 100 µL of AAV6-shLTBP2 (4 × 1011/copies; OBiO Technology (Shanghai) Corp.,Ltd) or AAV6-shNC (non-targeting control) through tail vein injection and CCl4 was continued for 4 weeks (All plasmids carry GFP fluorescence). The detail protocol of AAV6-shLTBP2 injection was reported as previously described [17]. All mice were euthanized 48 h after the last injection of CCl4, blood samples and liver tissues were collected for experiments. And the detail protocol for primary HSCs isolation was displayed as previously described [18].


### Liver function test and hydroxyproline level
  The levels of serum ALT, AST and the hydroxyproline level of mice liver tissues were determined by the corresponding detection kit according to the instructions (A030-2-1, C009-2-1 and C010-2-1, Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute).


### Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay
  The serum samples of mice and LX-2 cells supernatant were kept in a -80 °C freezer until measurements. The LTBP2 levels were examined with the ELISA (ELK2129, ELK8607, ELK Biotechnology) kit following the manufacturer’s protocols.


### Cell cultural
  The human hepatic stellate cell line (LX-2 cell) was purchased from Xinyu Biotechnology (Shanghai, China), cultured with RPMI 1640 Medium with 10% fetal bovine serum (Biological Industries, Israel) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin (C100C5, NCM Biotech). Then incubated at 37 °C in 5% CO2. After 12 h of serum-free starvation, the LX-2 cells were treated with recombinant human TGF-β1 (abs04204, Absin) for 24 h to simulate the activation of hepatic stellate cells.


### Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction
  The total RNA was isolated from the LX-2 cells and liver tissues by FastPure Cell/Tissue Total RNA Isolation Kit (RC101-01, Vazyme) according to the instructions. The HiScript III All-in-one RT SuperMix Perfect for qPCR (R333-01, Vazyme) was purchased to reversely transcribe RNA. Subsequently, gene expressions were measured by real-time PCR with ABI (Applied Biosystems), to quantify the mRNA expressions. GAPDH was regarded as an endogenous reference gene. The primer sequences are listed in Supplementary Table 1.


### Western blot
  The LX-2 cells and liver tissues were lysed in RIPA buffer (WB3100, NCM Biotech) supplemented with ProtLytic Protease and Phosphatase Inhibitor Cocktail (P002, NCM Biotech). The primary antibodies against α-SMA (T55295, Abmart), Collagen I (T61022S, Abmart), LTBP2 (SC166199, Santa), E-cadherin (TA0131, Abmart), N-cadherin (T55015, Abmart), Vimentin (A19607, ABclonal), HIF-1α (36169, CST), ERK (9101, CST), p ERK (4695, CST), P38 (8690, CST), P P38(4511, CST), JNK (9252, CST), P JNK (4668, CST) and LOXL1 (sc-166632, Santa) were used. These antibodies were detected using HRP-conjugated goat anti-mouse (A21010, Abcam) or anti-rabbit (A21020, Abcam) secondary antibodies. The relative protein quantification was calculated from ImageJ software.


### CCK-8
  CCK-8 (C6005, NCM Biotech) was used according to the instructions to detect cell proliferation. About 10,000 cells were cultured in 96-well plates. At the indicated times, 10 µL CCK-8 reagent was added and incubated for 2 h, and the cell activity was measured at 450 nm.


### Cell cycle analysis
  Cell Cycle Assay Kit (40301ES50, Yeasen Biotechnology) were used to examine the cell cycles of LX-2 cells. We fixed and stained the cells according to the instructions provided by the supplier and tested them using flow cytometry (BD Biosciences, Franklin Lakes, NJ, USA).


### Wound healing
  Wound healing experiments were used to confirm cell migration. The LX-2 cells (5 × 105 / well) were added to the six-well plate and cultured until fully fused in a 37 °C culture, scratched with a 200 µL gun tip, then washed three times with phosphate-buffered saline to remove the non-adherent cells and replaced with a serum-free medium. Wound images were taken under the microscope at 0 and 24 h after scratch.


### Luciferase reporter assay
  The promoter of LTBP2 was cloned upstream of the promoter of Luciferase, and then construct the expression plasmid of HIF-1α (Miao Ling Biology). HEK 293T cells were transfected with LTBP2 promoter plasmid, HIF-1α expression plasmid and Renilla luciferase plasmid using transfection reagents (AC04L032, Life iLab) for 48 h. Promega dual luciferase kit (Promega, USA; E1910) to detect luciferase activity.


### ChIP
  The ChIP assays were performed following the established protocol [19–21]. Briefly, chromatin from cells was cross-linked with 1% formaldehyde and then incubated in a lysis buffer (150 mM NaCl, 25 mM Tris pH 7.5, 1% Triton X-100, 0.1% SDS, 0.5% deoxycholate) supplemented with protease inhibitors and PMSF. The DNA fragments were sonicated to an average size of approximately 200 bp. Each immunoprecipitation reaction utilized an equal amount of lysate containing 200 µg protein along with either anti-HIF1α (CST, 36169 S) or pre-immune IgG.


### Protein–protein docking
  First, we obtained the amino acid sequences of LTBP2 and LOXL1 proteins from the NCBI database and used the existing structural information from the AlphaFold database to predict and optimize the three-dimensional structure of LTBP2 and LOXL1. The HDOCK server predicted the interaction sites of LTBP2 and LOXL1 using a global search algorithm. Finally, we used PyMOL (https://pymol.org) to predict the protein-protein interaction and generated a protein-protein interaction map.


### Co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP)
  Samples (1.1 g of liver tissue) was ground and homogenized, and 1 ml of lysis buffer was added. The tissue was lysed at 4 °C for 30 min. The tissue lysis buffer was incubated with anti-LTBP2 antibody for 2 h. Then, protein A/G agarose beads (Beyotime, Shanghai, China, P2-197 S) were added and gently shaken overnight at 4 °C. The beads were washed with an adequate amount of lysis buffer, then boiled in 2 × SDS loading buffer and subjected to Western blot analysis.


### RNA sequencing
  Stable shLTBP2 and control LX-2 cells were generated. Total RNA was extracted using TRizol (Takara) following the manufacturer’s instructions. After assessing its concentration and integrity, the qualified RNA samples were amplified by PCR to construct a cDNA library. The RNA samples were sequenced using the lllumina NovaSec6000 (lllumina Inc., San Diego, CA, USA), with raw data analysis performed by Wekemo Tech Group Co., Ltd. (Shenzhen, China). Data analysis using online websites: https://www.bioincloud.tech/.


### Statistical analysis
  The data were processed using the SPSS 25.0 and GraphPad 7.0 software. The results are presented as the mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). Student’s t-test or the non-parametric Mann-Whitney U test was used for measurement data, while one-way ANOVA was employed for multiple comparisons involving three or more groups. A P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.


## Results

### LTBP2 was upregulated in human liver fibrotic tissues
  We first explored the potential role of LTBP2 in liver tissue using the GEPIA2 database. The analysis results showed that the mRNA expression levels of LTBP2 and α-SMA or COL1A1, important markers of fibrosis, were highly positively correlated in human liver tissue (LTBP2-ACTA2: R = 0.83, P < 0.01; LTBP2-COL1A1: R = 0.62, P < 0.01) (Supplementary Fig. 1a and 1b). Then, in the transcriptome dataset of liver fibrosis in GSE8404416, the expression levels of ACTA2 (LTBP2-ACTA2: R = 0.5093, P < 0.0001) and COL1A1 (LTBP2-COL1A1: R = 0.7999, P < 0.0001) exhibited significant positive correlations with LTBP2, as illustrated in Supplementary Fig. 1c and 1d. Subsequently, liver tissue from normal and fibrotic was used to detect the expression and distribution of LTBP2. The results of liver histological examination showed that compared with normal liver tissue, fibrotic liver tissue showed obvious collagen deposition, hepatic lobular structure disorder and fibrous spacer formation, and hepatic fibrotic injury was obvious (Fig. 1a). The immunohistochemical results of human liver tissues showed that compared with normal liver tissue, the expression levels of α-SMA and LTBP2 in fibrotic liver tissue were significantly increased, and there were obvious positive expression areas of protein accumulation. It is worth noting that the expression area of LTBP2 in fibrotic liver tissue is consistent with the positive expression area of α-SMA, suggesting that LTBP2 is localized in patients’ liver tissue and mainly distributed in the liver fibrosis lesion area (Fig. 1b). Since liver fibrosis is a multicellular reactive disease, we wanted to identify the major cell distribution types of LTBP2 by single-cell sequencing. By searching the GEO database, we obtained a single-cell transcriptome data (GSE136103) from the healthy livers and cirrhotic livers [13]. The results showed that LTBP2 was mainly expressed in the myofibroblasts of cirrhotic livers (Fig. 1c), and activated HSCs are regarded as the main precursor cells of myofibroblasts [22]. Collectively, our results indicated that the expression of LTBP2 is associated with the activation of HSCs, which suggested the LTBP2 plays critical role in liver fibrosis.

### Increased expression of LTBP2 in mice fibrotic liver and TGF-β activated LX-2 cells
  The mice liver fibrosis model was successfully established by intraperitoneal injection of CCl4, and verified by HE, Masson and Sirius Red (Fig. 2a). The results of Masson and Sirius Red staining indicated that the CCl4 model had obvious collagen deposition, fiber hyperplasia and bridge fibrosis. The contents of HYP and inflammation index (ALT and AST) in liver fibrosis group were also higher than those in control group (Fig. 2b and c). Furthermore, CCl4 upregulated LTBP2 level in fibrotic mice serum than control group (Fig. 2d). Meanwhile, we also found that the expression of LTBP2, α-SMA and Collagen I increased in CCl4-treated mice (Fig. 2e and f). We also analyzed the data of primary quiescent and activated HSCs from control mice and mice with CCl4-induced liver fibrosis (GSE149508) [15], and found that LTBP2 expression was increased in primary activated HSCs from mice with CCl4-induced liver fibrosis (Supplementary Fig. 2). Then, we explored the relationship between LTBP2 expression and HSCs activation in vitro. LX-2 cells were treated with 10 ng/mL of TGF-β, it was observed that the expression of α-SMA and LTBP2 were elevated (Supplementary Fig. 3a). At the same time, the content of LTBP2 protein was also increased in activated LX-2 cells and supernatant (Supplementary Fig. 3b and 3c). Take together, these results suggested that LTBP2 is associated with fibrosis progression and the activation of HSCs in vitro and in vivo.

### LTBP2 deficiency attenuates liver fibrosis in CCl4 induced liver fibrosis mice
  The above data suggested that LTBP2 participates in liver fibrosis and HSCs activation. To further validate the role of LTBP2 in liver fibrosis, short-hairpin RNA (shRNA) targeting LTBP2 was pack aged into Adeno-associated virus type 6 (AAV6), which has been shown to excellent targeting capability towards activated HSCs in the liver [23, 24]. We initially verified the knockdown efficacy of LTBP2 in NIH/3T3 cells (Supplementary Fig. 4a and 4b). After intraperitoneal injection of CCl4 solution for 2 weeks, we injected the AAV6-shNC and AAV6-shLTBP2 virus solutions with viral load of 4*10^¹¹ copies into the tail vein, followed by continued injection of CCl4 solution for another 4 weeks (Fig. 3a) and the results of the frozen section indicated that AAV6 was successfully expressed in the liver tissue of mice (Supplementary Fig. 4c). To further clarify the infection efficiency of AAV6 on HSCs, we performed double immunofluorescence staining of GFP and the marker of activated HSCs. Our results clearly demonstrated that AAV6 selectively and efficiently infected HSCs (Supplementary Fig. 4d). And the expression of LTBP2 in primary HSCs decreased in AAV6-shLTBP2 treated mice (Supplementary Fig. 4e). Visual inspection of the whole liver indicated less pathological changes in AAV6-shLTBP2 mice treated with CCl4 (Fig. 3b). Meanwhile, HE, Masson and Sirius red both suggested the attenuation of liver fibrosis in the AAV6-shLTBP2 mice compared with the control mice (Fig. 3b). And serum ALT and AST levels decreased in AAV6-shLTBP2 treatment groups (Fig. 3c). Meanwhile, treatment with AAV6-shLTBP2 significantly reduced the level of hydroxyproline in the liver of mice (Fig. 3d). Immunofluorescence showed deletion of LTBP2 significantly reduced the level of α-SMA, after CCl4 treatment (Fig. 3e). Quantitative RT-PCR and Western blotting also revealed that the increase in LTBP2 level after CCl4-treatment was significantly reduced by AAV6-shLTBP2 injection, accompanied by the significant reduction of the fibrosis markers α-SMA and Collagen I (Fig. 3f and g). Therefore, we conclude that LTBP2 may be essential for liver fibrosis in vivo.

### LTBP2 promoted the activation, proliferation, migration and EMT of HSCs
  The above results have proved that the increased expression of LTBP2 in hepatic fibrosis tissues and its correlation with the activation of HSCs. To further explore whether LTBP2 influences the activation of HSCs, we examined the expression of fibrosis related genes (α-SMA and Collagen I) in LX-2 cells. The experimental results indicated that the overexpression of LTBP2 in LX-2 cells was confirmed by qRT-PCR and Western blotting. Meanwhile, the expression of the HSCs activated marker α-SMA increased, along with a rise in collagen I production (Fig. 4a and b). Conversely, suppression of LTBP2 expression led to decreased expression of α-SMA and Collagen I (Fig. 4a and b). Activated HSCs exhibited characteristic feature such as proliferation and migration. To elucidate whether LTBP2 contributes to these processes, we conducted a series of experiments. The results of CCK-8 assay showed that overexpression of LTBP2 promoted the proliferation of LX-2 cells, whereas knockdown of LTBP2 inhibited it (Supplementary Fig. 5a). Additionally, to clarify the underlying mechanisms by which LTBP2 affects the proliferation of LX-2 cells, the cell cycle phase distribution was analyzed by flow cytometry. The data showed that LTBP2 overexpression decreased the proportion of cells in the G1 phase compared to the control group, and LTBP2 knockdown induced a more pronounced G1 arrest compared to the negative control group (Supplementary Fig. 5b). In addition, results from the wound healing assay showed that compared with control groups, overexpression or knockdown of LTBP2 led to a higher or lower wound closure rate in LX-2 cells, respectively (Supplementary Fig. 5c).

  Studies have reported that HSCs can differentiate into collagen-producing myofibroblasts through EMT [9, 10], which is of significant importance for understanding the mechanisms underlying liver fibrosis. To investigate whether LTBP2 plays a role in the EMT of HSCs, we conducted a series of experiments. The results revealed in LX-2 cells transfected with the LTBP2-overexpressing plasmid, the expression levels of E-cadherin, an epithelial cell marker, were downregulated, while the mesenchymal markers N-cadherin and vimentin were upregulated (Fig. 4c). Also, knockdown of LTBP2 reduced the expression of N-cadherin and vimentin, and upregulated that of E-cadherin (Fig. 4c). Furthermore, in vivo experiments showed that E-cadherin expression increased, N-cadherin and vimentin were decreased in the AAV6-shLTBP2 treated mice (Supplementary Fig. 6a). These results demonstrated that LTBP2 significantly influenced the activation, proliferation, migration and EMT of HSCs.


### HIF-1α promoted LTBP2 expression by directly binding to LTBP2 promoter
  Given the key role of LTBP2 in liver fibrosis and the activation of HSCs, the upstream genes that regulate LTBP2 are of interest to us. By searching the JASPAR database, HIF-1α may be a transcription factor for LTBP2. We searched a single-cell transcriptome data (GSE136103) from healthy livers and cirrhotic livers. The results showed that HIF-1α was mainly expressed in myofibroblasts and mesothelia of cirrhotic livers. (Supplementary Fig. 7a). It was observed that the expression of HIF-1α were elevated after the LX-2 cells were treated with 10 ng/mL of TGF-β (Supplementary Fig. 7b and 7c). Meanwhile, we also found that the expression of HIF-1α increased in CCl4-treated mice (Supplementary Fig. 7d and 7e). To explore the potential molecular mechanism of HIF-1α regulating LTBP2, HIF-1α overexpressed plasmid were transfected into LX-2 cells to increase the expression level of HIF-1α. Quantitative RT-PCR and Western blotting analysis indicated that the levels of HIF-1α and LTBP2 were up-regulated (Fig. 5a and b). Furthermore, we used COCl2 to simulate a hypoxic environment for stimulating HSCs [25] and then transfected with the shHIF-1α plasmid to achieve HIF-1α knockdown in LX-2 cells. Compared with the control group, both HIF-1α and LTBP2 levels were significantly decreased in LX-2 cells (Fig. 5c and d). Next, we searched the promoter region of the LTBP2 gene to find a consensus for the HIF-1α region. We designed specific primers based on the binding site sequences for the ChIP experiment to detect the HIF-1α transcription factor. DNA fragments potentially bound by the HIF-1α transcription factor were obtained through the ChIP experiment, purified and amplified using qPCR on the resulting DNA products. The findings indicated that ChIP DNA products in the HIF-1α group exhibited higher enrichment signals compared to those in the IgG group, suggesting the direct binding of HIF-1α to the promoter region of LTBP2 in LX-2 cell and liver fibrosis tissue of mice (Fig. 5e). Meanwhile, dual luciferase reporter gene analysis showed that co-transfection with OE-HIF1α increased the luciferase activity of LTBP2 promoter reporter gene, but the co-transfection control group did not change the luciferase activity of LTBP2 promoter (Fig. 5f). In general, we believed that HIF-1α promoted LTBP2 expression via transcriptional activation of LTBP2.

### LTBP2 interacts with the LOXL1 regulates the activation of HSCs and EMT via ERK signaling pathway
  To tackle the mechanism whereby LTBP2 may contribute to HSCs activation and liver fibrosis, the proteins that interact with LTBP2 were analyzed using the PPI database (https://cn.string-db.org/) (Fig. 6a). The prediction outcomes indicate that a latent interaction may exist between LOXL1 and LTBP2 (Fig. 6a). LOXL1, a member of the lysyl oxidase family, is a copper-dependent extracellular enzyme that plays a crucial role in ECM cross-linking during liver fibrosis [26]. The analysis results showed that the mRNA expression levels of LTBP2 and LOXL1, were positively correlated in human liver tissue (LTBP2-LOXL1: R = 0.97, P < 0.01) from GEPIA2 datasets (Fig. 6b) and in liver fibrosis (LTBP2-LOXL1: R = 0.7950, P < 0.0001) from GEO dataset (Fig. 6c). Quantitative RT-PCR and Western blotting analysis showed that the expression level of LOXL1 increased in fibrotic mice liver (Fig. 6d and e). According to the molecular simulations, stable potential binding sites between LTBP2 and LOXL1 docking were found (Fig. 6f). Considering that both LTBP2 and LOXL1 are associated with the ECM, we conduct a Co-IP experiment in liver fibrotic tissues, which confirmed the binding of LTBP2 and LOXL1 in liver fibrotic tissue of mice (Fig. 6g).

  RNA-sequencing was performed to compare the transcriptomes of HSCs from shLTBP2 and control LX-2 cells. The heat map showed that LTBP2 expression decreased significantly (Fig. 7a). Surprisingly, the MAPK signaling pathway was likewise identified in the pathway enrichment analysis in the activation process of human primary HSCs and mice primary HSCs (Supplementary Fig. 8). The MAPK signaling pathway encompasses three classical regulatory modalities: Extracellular Signal-Regulated Kinase (ERK), c-Jun N-terminal Kinase (JNK), and p38 MAPK. Our results demonstrated that LTBP2 knockdown led to a decrease in ERK1/2 phosphorylation (Fig. 7c). In contrast, no significant changes were observed in the JNK and p38 pathways (Fig. 7c). We also observed a decrease in ERK1/2 phosphorylation of AAV6-shLTBP2 treatment mice (Supplementary Fig. 6b). When we overexpressed LTBP2 in HSCs, we found that the phosphorylation level of ERK1/2 increased (Fig. 7d), while the total ERK level did not change. In contrast, using of ERK pathway inhibitors (U0126) leads to an opposite effect (Fig. 7d). These data suggest that LTBP2 participates in the ERK1/2 dependent fibrosis signaling pathway.

## Discussion
  Liver fibrosis progresses with the deposition of ECM and gradually destroys the normal structure of liver parenchyma, eventually leading to liver dysfunction, cirrhosis and even liver cancer [10, 28]. The treatment of liver fibrosis has long been a challenge, and despite promising results observed in preclinical studies, anti-fibrosis drug candidates have had limited effectiveness in clinical trials [29, 30]. Thus, more potential targets for the development of anti-fibrosis therapeutics with thoroughly explained molecular mechanisms are urgently needed. When the liver is injured, qHSCs are activated to transform into myofibroblasts, which secretes ECM and contributes to the progression of liver fibrosis. Therefore, targeting the activation of HSCs is considered as a potential therapeutic approach for managing liver fibrosis. However, the molecular mechanisms and potential therapeutic targets have not been comprehensively investigated.

  LTBP2 is a member of the LTBP family and functions as a multi-domain exocrine protein present in the ECM [6]. It plays a crucial role in microfibril formation and cell adhesion [31]. Previous studies have shown that LTBP2 may be associated with fibrosis or tissue remodeling in multiple organs, including the skin [31], heart [32], and lungs [33]. To date, no studies have reported its role and function in liver fibrosis. In our study, we observed that the expression of LTBP2 exhibits a positive correlation with fibrogenesis markers (COL1A1 and ACTA2) in GEPIA2 dataset and GEO dataset. Therefore, we propose that LTBP2 represents a promising target for further investigation in liver fibrosis.

  In this study, we found that LTBP2 was upregulated in human and mice fibrotic liver tissues. Single-cell RNA sequencing was used to identify subpopulations and functions of HSCs with remarkable resolution of single-cell transcriptome expression. Through analysis, we found that LTBP2 was expressed in myofibroblasts of cirrhosis liver. Study has indicated that activated HSCs are the major source for differentiating into myofibroblasts during the process of liver fibrosis [34], and it is indicated that LTBP2 is expressed in activated HSCs.

  Multiple studies have shown that adeno-associated virus (AAV) vectors are a promising therapeutic gene delivery platform for treating diseases [20, 35–37]. Among them, AAV6 has been shown to excellent targeting capability towards activated HSCs in the liver [17, 23, 24, 38–42]. However, it is also recognized that AAV6 can transduce a small percentage of Kupffer cells [38], which may have a minor impact. In this study, we confirmed the targeting efficiency of AAV6 in HSCs by immunofluorescent staining, and the expression of LTBP2 in primary mice HSCs was successfully inhibited by intravenous injection of AAV6-shLTBP2. And the CCl4-induced liver fibrosis models in mice were treated with intravenous injection of AAV6-shLTBP2, we found that the absence of LTBP2 in vivo effectively alleviated CCl4-induced liver fibrosis, collagen accumulation and expression of fibrogenic genes. Furthermore, when LTBP2 was suppressed, the levels of HYP in the liver and serum AST and ALT were significantly reduced. These findings strongly suggest that inhibiting the expression of LTBP2 impedes the progression of liver fibrosis. And the LTBP2 may serve as a potential target for liver fibrosis treatment.

  The activation of HSCs has been shown to be a crucial factor in promoting liver fibrosis. In our study, overexpression of LTBP2 enhanced the activation of HSCs, proliferation and migration capabilities of LX-2 cells, while inhibition of LTBP2 expression had the opposite effect. EMT is regarded as one of the mechanisms generating myofibroblasts during liver fibrosis. Research has demonstrated that HSCs can undergo transformation into myofibroblasts through the EMT process [9]. EMT is not a simple binary shift between epithelial and mesenchymal states. Instead, it represents a dynamic process in which epithelial cells transiently lose their polarity, acquire mesenchymal-like morphological features, and gain enhanced migratory capacity [43]. The epithelial cell marker E-cadherin is consistantly expressed in quiescent HSCs. However, upon activation, HSCs exhibited a marked reduction in the expression of epithelial cell markers, and instead, they express the mesenchymal cell marker N-cadherin, secrete type I collagen, and transdifferentiate into myofibroblasts to synthesize a considerable amount of ECM [9, 44]. Consequently, the occurrence of EMT in HSCs can facilitate the advancement of liver fibrosis [45]. In our research, over-expression of LTBP2 promotes EMT, leading to an increase in the levels of mesenchymal markers (N-cadherin and vimentin) in LX-2 cells, while reducing the expression of epithelial marker (E-cadherin). Knockdown of LTBP2 can suppress these effects. Therefore, we hypothesize that LTBP2 may promote liver fibrosis by regulating the activation of HSCs and EMT.

  In terms of mechanism, we first explored the mechanism of increased LTBP2 expression. Through bioinformatics prediction, we identified HIF-1α as transcription factors for LTBP2. Our results indicated that HIF-1α was significantly up-regulated in the activation of HSCs and liver fibrotic tissue of mice. Moreover, the upregulation or downregulation of HIF-1α expression similarly affects the expression levels of LTBP2. In the end, ChIP and dual-luciferase reporter gene assays demonstrate that HIF-1α binds to the LTBP2 promoter and activates the LTBP2 transcription.

  PPI analysis predicted LTBP2-binding proteins, revealing a latent interaction between LOXL1 and LTBP2. LOXL1 plays a crucial role in ECM cross-linking during liver fibrosis [26]. Correlation analysis using the GEPIA2 database and liver fibrosis tissue dataset demonstrated a significant positive correlation between LTBP2 and LOXL1 expression levels. Molecular docking revealed the binding sites between LTBP2 and LOXL1. Then the Co-IP experiment confirmed the interaction between LTBP2 and LOXL1 proteins in the liver fibrosis tissues of mice.

  Additionally, we conducted RNA-sequencing analysis on the shLTBP2 and control LX-2 cells to further explore the potential signaling pathway. The RNA-sequencing results confirmed that LTBP2 is involved in the MAPK pathway. And our results showed that ERK1/2 phosphorylation decreased after LTBP2 knockdown. Overexpression of LTBP2 upregulated the phosphorylation level of ERK1/2 and activated the ERK pathway. The use of the ERK pathway inhibitor U0126 inhibited the activation of the ERK pathway induced by LTBP2. Previous studies have demonstrated that the use of U0126 to inhibit the activation of the ERK pathway can affect the activation of HSCs and reduce the production of collagen [46]. In conclusion, LTBP2 exerts its function in liver fibrosis through the ERK pathway.

  Studies indicate that LOXL1 expression is upregulated in activated HSCs and fibrotic liver tissues [47–49]. Inhibiting LOXL1 expression in HSCs can effectively suppress the activation of HSCs and reduce collagen expression [49], findings that consistent with our experimental results. Moreover, mechanism studies indicate that LOXL1 can regulate the ERK pathway to promote the activation of HSCs and the progression of liver fibrosis [50]. Through functional rescue experiments, overexpress LTBP2 while interfering with the expression of LOXL1 inhibited the activation of HSCs, the occurrence of EMT and the activation of the ERK pathway. Therefore, we propose that LTBP2 interacts with the LOXL1 promote the activation of HSCs and EMT via the ERK pathway.

  The structural components of the ECM, particularly the crosslinking of fibrous collagen and elastin, are closely related to the progression of fibrosis and the resistance to fibrosis reversal. Although both LTBP2 and LOXL1 are closely associated with the ECM, it remains unknown whether the LTBP2/LOXL1 complex activates ERK directly within the cell or in the ECM. As such, the role of LTBP2/LOXL1 in ECM crosslinking may influence ERK activation, either directly within cells or indirectly via the ECM. To further investigate this, future studies using immunofluorescence to track ERK activation in both HSCs and ECM, along with immunoprecipitation assays to validate interactions between LTBP2, LOXL1, and key ERK pathway components, will be necessary for clarifying this mechanism. Additionally, ECM stiffness, which can be modulated by LOXL1 through crosslinking of collagen and elastin, is a key factor that can influence cellular signaling, including the ERK pathway. Therefore, further experiments focusing on how ECM stiffness influences the ERK pathway in the context of liver fibrosis are needed.

  To sum up, our study also has some limitations. Firstly, although LX-2 cells have been widely used in HSC-related studies, we acknowledge that it is crucial to replicate key experiments using primary murine or human HSCs. In future studies, we plan to incorporate primary murine or human HSCs to replicate key experiments and strengthen the robustness of our conclusions. Secondly, although we successfully demonstrated the regulatory role of LTBP2 in liver fibrosis both in vitro and in vivo, further investigation using LTBP2 knockout mice models is necessary to fully explore the biological significance of LTBP2 induced expression. Lastly, additional experiments are required to investigate whether the LTBP2/LOXL1 complex directly activates ERK signaling within cells or in the extracellular matrix.

  In summary, our study has demonstrated the functional role and potential mechanism of LTBP2 in liver fibrosis. The results show that LTBP2 is highly expressed in human and mice liver fibrosis tissues and positively correlates with the severity of liver fibrosis. In particular, our data also indicates that LTBP2 promotes the activation of HSCs and liver fibrosis both in vitro and in vivo. Mechanistically, HIF-1α binds to the promoter of LTBP2 to activate its transcription, and then LTBP2 interacts with the LOXL1 via ERK signaling pathway to promote the activation of HSCs and EMT, thereby promoting the liver fibrosis progression. Therefore, our findings contribute to the current understanding of the mechanisms of liver fibrosis and suggest LTBP2 as a potential therapeutic target for liver fibrosis.
