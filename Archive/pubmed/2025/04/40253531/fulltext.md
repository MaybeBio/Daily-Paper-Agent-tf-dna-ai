# Integrative bioinformatics analysis reveals STAT1, ORC2, and GTF2B as critical biomarkers in lupus nephritis with Monkeypox virus infection


## Supplementary Information
  The online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-97791-w.


## Introduction
  MPXV has garnered significant global attention recently, particularly in public health1–3. While MPXV typically manifests as a mild, self-limiting skin disease, the potential complications for individuals with weakened immune systems warrant further investigation. Recent research has linked MPXV infection to natural killer (NK) cell dysfunction and immune evasion, highlighting the critical role of the immune response in combating poxvirus infections4,5. LN, a serious complication of systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), involves immune complex deposition in the kidneys, causing inflammation and substantial quality of life decline for patients6. Emerging studies indicate that viral infections may play a complex role in the progression of lupus nephritis7, although the exact mechanisms require further exploration and validation.

  Viral infections can influence the progression of lupus nephritis through various mechanisms. Research indicates that SARS-CoV-2 may trigger excessive immune system activation, stimulating both innate and adaptive responses that result in the production of autoantibodies8,9. This can complicate lupus nephritis, making it harder to treat10,11. Additionally, virus infection may impair renal function by inducing cellular senescence, an irreversible state of cell cycle arrest linked to chronic inflammation and tissue damage12. Senescent cells further aggravate local inflammation and tissue fibrosis through the release of pro-inflammatory factors (the senescence-associated secretory phenotype, SASP)13, which can accelerate the progression of lupus nephritis and worsen the disease.

  Currently, there is no definitive evidence that MPXV infection worsens LN, but both involve inflammation and immune cell infiltration. The Tfh/Treg ratio is increased in patients with active LN, and Tfh1 cells infiltrate the LN14. Additionally, NK cells and dendritic cells are instrumental in combating MPXV virus5. A clinical trial found that among the 34 confirmed cases of MPXV, eight patients (24%) reported underlying medical conditions, including hepatitis C, asthma, lupus nephritis, bone marrow transplantation, and hemophilia15.In addition, Gu C et al. conducted RNA sequencing and KEGG enrichment analysis on immortalized keratinocytes infected with the MPXV virus. The results indicated that the heatmap of DEGs revealed significant changes in genes associated with the interferon pathway and the IL-17 signaling pathway. Specifically, the expression levels of the inflammation-related transcription factors JUN and FOXS were up-regulated, while the expression of the downstream interferon pathway genes GNP2 and STAT2 was significantly down-regulated. Furthermore, the KEGG analysis suggested that these DEGs are involved in several signaling pathways, with activation of the systemic lupus erythematosus pathway being further supported by GSEA16.It can be seen that MPXV virus infection can activate the transcription of genes related to systemic lupus erythematosus, and the transcription of genes related to systemic lupus erythematosus may exacerbate the progress of lupus nephritis. Follow-up research is required to confirm this association. Therefore, identifying immune genes between LN and MPXV infections is of great significance for early diagnosis and treatment.

  The connection between MPXV virus and lupus nephritis remains underexplored. We hypothesize that MPXV may worsen lupus nephritis by disrupting immune regulation. Thus, it is crucial to identify targets linked to immune dysregulation for early diagnosis and treatment. We analyzed data from a gene expression database, utilized Limma and WGCNA methods to identify key modular genes, and conducted functional enrichment analysis. We also constructed PPI networks and examined immune cell infiltration to pinpoint candidate genes. Key immunodiagnostic genes were validated through column-line graphs and ROC analysis, establishing a basis for detecting immune markers in both conditions. This study investigates the potential impact of MPXV virus infection on lupus nephritis, focusing on its relationship with immune response and cellular senescence, aiming to offer new insights for improving patient prognosis and quality of life.


## Materials and methods

### Data acquisition and processing
  We obtained 5,830 senescence-related genes (SRGs) from the GeneCards website (Access time: September 2024, https://www.genecards.org/). Additionally, we collected disease datasets from the GEO database: GSE112943, which includes kidney biopsy samples from 14 LN patients and 7 healthy controls; GSE99967, comprising peripheral blood samples from 42 LN patients and 17 healthy controls; and GSE36854, featuring 2 HL cell lines infected with the MPXV virus along with 2 controls. Detailed information is available in Supplementary Table S1. We used GSE112943 and GSE36854 as training datasets and GSE99967 as the validation dataset. The data were log-transformed to identify DEGs across the three datasets using the “limma” package17 in R software (v4.1.2 )18, applying the criteria of p < 0.05 and |log2FC| > 1.5. The study flow is summarized in Fig. 1, and DEGs were visualized using the Sangerbox platform (Access time: September 2024 ,http://vip.sangerbox.com/login.html).

  Fig. 1Workflow of the analysis.


### WGCNA and identification of clinically significant modules
  In this study, we investigated gene-phenotype associations by constructing gene co-expression networks using the “WGCNA” package in R (version 4.1.2)19. The analysis focused on the relationship between modular feature genes and LN. We identified genes in the top 75% of median absolute deviation (MAD) using “WGCNA” and evaluated them with the same tool. The goodSamplesGenes algorithm was applied to exclude abnormal samples or genes. Additionally, we created a clustering dendrogram with the hclust function to identify and exclude two outlier samples with heights exceeding 70. After setting a soft threshold of 24 and requiring at least 30 genes per module, we derived gene modules and performed a Pearson correlation analysis to explore the association of these modules with disease phenotypes.


### Functional enrichment analysis and PPI network analysis
  To explore the biological roles of the intersecting genes, we first identified the overlap among the DEGs linked to LN, module genes, and those associated with MPXV. We used the “clusterProfile” package20 in R software (v 4.1.2) to perform GO21 and KEGG22 analyses, applying a significance threshold of p < 0.05. Additionally, we analyzed PPI for the intersecting genes using GeneMANIA (Access time: September 2024, https://genemania.org/)23.


### Identifying of candidate hub genes
  We employed the LASSO regression algorithm to identify DE-SRGs. First, we intersected senescent genes with DEGs observed in both LN and MPXV. Using these genes, we conducted a LASSO analysis, which integrates variable selection and regularization to improve predictive accuracy24. This analysis was performed with the “glmnet” package25 in R software (v 4.1.2), and the results suggest candidate hub genes for diagnosis.


### Nomogram construction and assessment
  To evaluate the role of candidate genes in diagnosing LN alongside MPXV virus infection, we first analyzed their differential expression in the validation cohort. We then employed the “rms” package in R software (v 4.1.2) to develop a Nomogram with “Points” (scores for the candidate genes) and “Total Points” (cumulative scores), which are essential for predicting LN with MPXV. Additionally, we analyzed the prognostic value of the candidate genes and the generated column plot using ROC analysis on the validation dataset, reporting the results as the area under the curve (AUC). An AUC above 0.7 indicated strong diagnostic efficacy.


### Immune infiltration analysis and GSEA analysis
  We used the CIBERSORT algorithm to assess the proportions of 22 immune cell subpopulations in patients from the LN and control groups through gene expression profiling26. Moreover, we demonstrated the correlations between immune cells and relevant signaling pathways for candidate genes using heatmap analysis and GSEA27.


### miRNA-mRNA-TF network
  To identify miRNA and transcription factor (TF) linked to the overlapping genes, we employed the miRNet 2.0 online database (Access time: September 2024 ,https://www.mirnet.ca/)28. This extensive platform offers essential insights into the relationships and interactions among different miRNAs, TFs, and the targeted genes .


### Prediction of targeted drugs
  The present research employed the DGldb online resource (https://dgidb.org/, Access time: October 2024) to forecast the response of drugs regarding significant genes associated with senescence. By utilizing this open-source search platform, the study sought to uncover interactions between drugs and genes, as well as to gather information on druggable genomes for the identification of targeted therapeutic options29.


### Statistical analysis
  The data were statistically analyzed using R software (v 4.1.2) and the Sangerbox online platform. The Wilcoxon test was used for two and multiple group comparisons, while Spearman rank correlation was applied for correlation analyses, with significance set at p < 0.05.

### WGCNA and key module identification
  A scale-free co-expression network was constructed using WGCNA to identify the modules most strongly associated with LN. Following the analysis, we opted for a “soft” threshold β of 24 (Fig. 3C,D), generating clustering dendrograms for both the LNs and controls. By setting a module merging threshold at 0.25 and a minimum module size of 50, we identified a total of 19 distinct colored gene co-expression modules (Fig. 3E–G). Clinical correlation analysis revealed that the antiquewhite4 module exhibited the highest association with LN (r = 0.84, p < 0.001) (Fig. 3H), prompting us to select this module, which contains 4107 genes, for further in-depth analysis (Supplementary Table). We computed the correlation between module eigenvectors and gene expression, resulting in module eigenvalues (MM) that displayed a significant positive correlation (correlation coefficient = 0.81, p < 0.001) (Fig. 3I). These findings indicate that genes within the antiquewhite4 module are closely related to LN.


### Enrichment analysis and PPI network analysis
  To explore the biological roles of LN in conjunction with MPXV virus infection, we analyzed LN differentially DEGs, along with key modular genes and MPXV DEGs, leading to the identification of 113 shared genes (Fig. 4A; Supplementary table). The KEGG enrichment analysis indicated that these genes were primarily implicated in pathways such as “Cellular senescence,” “Human T cell leukemia virus infection,” and “Ferroptosis” (Fig. 4B). In addition, BP is mainly enriched in “lymphangiogenesis”, “lymphangiogenesis regulation, and “mRNA metabolism processes “.CC is enriched at the locations of “cytoplasm,” “nucleoplasm” and “cytoplasm.” “. MF is mainly enriched in “protein N-terminal binding” (Fig. 4C–E). The results suggest that cellular senescence significantly impacts the co-infection of LN and MPXV. Thus, we will focus our further research on genes linked to cellular senescence. Additionally, we created a protein interaction network, where the CDK7 node displayed the highest connectivity, as illustrated in Fig. 4F.

### Identifying candidate hub genes
  To investigate the role of cellular senescence-related genes in disease, we identified 47 co-expressed genes associated with disease (see Fig. 5A and Supplementary Table). LASSO analysis revealed 11 significantly differentially expressed Candidate Hub Genes (see Fig. 5B-C). ROC curve analysis indicated a high predictive accuracy for this gene model (AUC = 1, 95% CI: 1–1) (see Fig. 5D). Additionally, we created a prognostic heatmap for the 11 genes related to risk scores and clinical features, showing higher diagnostic accuracy for identifying LN, with the exceptions of PANX1, PTGS1, and ZFP36 (see Fig. 5E).

### Construction and evaluation of the prediction model
  To verify the validity of the screened 11 DE-SRGs, we analyzed them in the validation set GSE99967. The results showed that only the expression levels of STAT1, ORC2 and GTF2B were statistically different (Fig. 6A). In addition, we constructed nomograms containing these three key diagnostic genes separately (Fig. 6B) and plotted ROC maps to evaluate their diagnostic effects.The AUC values of STAT1, ORC2 and GTF2B in both the training and validation sets were all greater than 0.7.(Fig. 6C-E). These results indicated that STAT1, ORC2 and GTF2B could be used as potential biomarkers for the diagnosis of MPXV complicating LN, and the nomograms constructed from them showed good diagnostic value.

### Immune infiltration analysis
  To further investigate immune dynamics in LN, we analyzed immune infiltration between the LN and control groups. As shown in Fig. 7A, there were significant differences in the proportions of 22 immune cell types. The LN group exhibited lower levels of naïve CD8 T cells, cytotoxic T cells, nTregs, monocytes, and neutrophils compared to the control group. In contrast, Tr1 cells, Th17 cells, NKT cells, MAIT cells, dendritic cells (DCs), NK cells, and CD4 + T cells were significantly more abundant in the LN group (Fig. 7C). Correlation analysis indicated that Th1 cells were positively correlated with Th17 and NKT cells, while CD8 + T cells negatively correlated with γδ T cells. Cytotoxic T cells also showed a negative correlation with NKT cells (Fig. 7B).

  GSEA analysis of the genes STAT1, ORC2, and GTF2B from LN patients revealed their enrichment in immune pathways, including T lymphocyte receptor, B cell receptor, and Toll-like receptor signaling (Fig. 7D–G). In summary, MPXV virus seems to influence LN pathogenesis by affecting immune cell infiltration, indicating that targeted regulation of immune cell populations may offer a new therapeutic approach for managing LN.

## Discussion
  LN, the most severe form of SLE, often surfaces within the first five years following diagnosis and often presents as the initial symptom according to the literature30.The intricate pathogenesis of LN involves genetic, environmental, and hormonal factors that intertwine various inflammatory processes and cellular interactions7. Recently, MPX has garnered attention; however, its replication mechanisms within host cells and genomic characteristics remain underexplored. Recent findings suggest that MPXV infection can disrupt NK cell function, lead to lymphopenia, and facilitate immune evasion, emphasizing the significance of the immune response in combating poxviruses4. To understand and combat its implications, we obtained datasets GSE36854 of MPXV-infected HeLa cells and GSE112943, focusing on patients with LN, from the GEO database. We cross-screened 113 genes with significant differential expression and employed bioinformatic and machine learning approaches to develop a predictive model. Our analysis identified three crucial DE-SRGs, STAT1, ORC2, and GTF2B, for further investigation, given their potential diagnostic value in LN in the context of MPXV.

  Cellular senescence, a response to telomere erosion, oxidative stress, and persistent inflammation, leads to permanent growth arrest through the accumulation of cell cycle inhibitors such as p16 INK4a12. Senescent cells exhibit distinct characteristics, including increased β-galactosidase activity and secretion of pro-inflammatory and profibrotic SASP31. In renal senescence and kidney diseases, such as LN, p16 INK4a- or β-galactosidase-positive cells are associated with tissue damage and renal dysfunction12,32. However, the role of cellular senescence in MPXV virus infections remains unknown. In this study, we analyzed the co-expressed genes of LN combined with MPXV virus infection, and functional enrichment and immune infiltration analyses revealed that these genes were involved in cellular senescence “Cellular senescence” “Human T cell leukemia virus l infection” and “Ferroptosis”; BP was enriched in “lymph vessel development”; CC was enriched in cytoplasm; MF is mainly enriched in “protein N-terminal binding” protein N-terminal binding. Analysis of immune cell infiltration showed that the levels of Tr1 and other cells were high, and Th1 was positively correlated with Th17 and NKT. GSEA showed that STAT1, ORC2, and GTF2B genes were mainly enriched in immune-related pathways. In conclusion, DE-SRGs may affect the proportion of immune cells by regulating cellular senescence, which in turn affects disease development.

  Our research found that the key genes associated with MPXV infection are involved in regulating the pathogenesis of LN and are mainly enriched in immune-related pathways. Specifically, immune cell subpopulations such as Tr1, Tfh, NKT, MAIT, DC, NK and CD4 + T cells showed higher levels after the body was attacked by MPXV virus, reflecting the active response of the immune system under stress. Further analysis revealed that there is a significant positive correlation between Th1, Th17, and NKT, which supports the imbalance of the immunomodulatory network under the interference of MPXV virus. At the same time, the STAT1, ORC2, and GTF2B genes, as key regulatory factors, were found to be significantly enriched in immune-related pathways such as the T lymphocyte receptor signaling pathway, the B cell receptor signaling pathway, and the Toll-like receptor signaling pathway, highlighting their pivotal role in mediating the immune response and transmitting immune signals. Existing studies have shown that after infection with MPXV virus, immune function is disturbed. MPXV infection can trigger a series of immune responses, including abnormal immune cell function (such as lymphopenia, impaired NK cell function, immune escape, cytokine storm, etc.), changes in blood composition (increased antibodies, increased monocytes and granulocytes), and inhibition of the complement system. These changes are closely related to the occurrence and development of various diseases4.Lupus nephritis, on the other hand, is associated with the deposition of antigen-antibody complexes in kidney tissue. After the MPXV virus invades the body of patients with lupus nephritis, its viral protein can be used as an exogenous antigen to stimulate the body’s immune system to produce specific antibodies and form immune complexes. These complexes are deposited in the glomerular basement membrane of the kidney, thereby activating the complement system, recruiting inflammatory cells, and ultimately triggering the pathological process of LN.

  Signal transducer and activator of transcription 1 (STAT1) is a key transcription factor that is important in the immune-inflammatory response and is closely associated with LN and MPXV virus infection33–35. Studies have shown that STAT1 is a core gene involved in LN progression. iFN-γ activates the JAK/STAT1 pathway to promote CXCL10 expression, and inhibition of this pathway reduces lupus kidney injury34. In addition, miR-574-5p activates STAT1 signaling by triggering an immune-inflammatory response35. Single-cell sequencing has shown that STAT1 is highly active as an interferon-stimulated gene in some LN patients35. In MPXV virus infection, STAT1 deletion causes up to 100% mortality in mice36, and recombinant MPXV virus vaccine is safe and effective in STAT1 knockout mouse models37. Therefore, STAT1, as a signal transduction and transcription activator, can mediate the activation of intracellular antiviral signaling pathways and regulate the antiviral response of immune cells during MPXV virus infection. In lupus nephritis, abnormally activated STAT1 is involved in regulating the release of local inflammatory cytokines in the kidney and promoting the process of renal fibrosis.

  Origin recognition complex 2 (ORC2) is a key protein for the initiation of DNA replication38.Phosphorylation of ORC2 dissociates the replication initiation complex from chromatin and the replication start39.Deletion of ORC2 leads to defective DNA replication40, whereas co-expression of ORC1 and ORC2 prevents apoptosis and facilitates the assembly of ORC on chromatin41. This suggests that ORC2 may influence disease progression by regulating DNA replication and the cell cycle. Although the role of ORC2 in LN and MPXV virus infection is unknown, it has been shown that ORC2 is associated with other diseases. For example, bioinformatics and immunohistochemical analyses have shown that ORC2 can serve as a diagnostic prognostic gene for Barrett’s esophagus and esophageal adenocarcinoma42. In addition, overexpression of ORC2 in a mouse model of myocardial infarction improved cardiac function and reduced fibrosis and immune cell infiltration43. KEGG enrichment analysis in this study showed that LN-related genes were mainly involved in “Cellular senescence”, “Human T-cell leukemia virus infection” and “Fibrosis”. GSEA analysis further showed that ORC2 was mainly enriched in signaling pathways such as Toll-like receptor, cell cycle and T lymphocyte receptor. These results are consistent with the known functions of ORC2 in DNA replication and cell cycle. Therefore, we speculate that ORC2 plays a key role in the regulation of cell cycle initiation. MPXV virus infection may interfere with its normal function, thereby affecting the cell cycle process, and ultimately lead to abnormal immune cell function; ORC2 disorders are closely related to the loss of control of kidney inherent cell proliferation and metabolic disorders of the extracellular matrix.

  General transcription factor IIB (GTF2B) is important in the regulation of gene expression, especially in the initiation of RNA polymerase II transcription to assist in the recognition of promoters and complex formation. Research on the function of GTF2B in LN and MPXV virus infection is limited, but it is known to be related to other diseases. Elsby et al. noted that GTF2B is involved in hepatocellular carcinoma proliferation by inhibiting HBV X protein transcriptional activation44 .Cai F et al. found that GTF2B binds to AIP and promotes AIP expression to inhibit GHPA tumor development45. In addition, Zhou W et al. showed that GTF2B regulates apoptosis and invasion in invasive trophoblast cells affecting trophoblast dysfunction in preeclampsia46. Multi-omics studies have shown its involvement in the development of diseases such as heart failure47, osteogenic differentiation of MSCs48, and depression49. In summary, GSEA analysis in this study showed that ORC2 was mainly enriched in signaling pathways such as Toll-like receptor, adhesion junction, Rig I-like receptor and T lymphocyte receptor, which was consistent with the known function of GTF2B, reflecting its transcriptional signaling regulation in disease development.


## Conclusion
  This study on LN and MPXV infection highlights unique features and benefits. Rather than just focusing on isolated factors like symptoms or genes, we analyzed GEO, GeneCards, and GSEA databases to identify three crucial genes: STAT1, ORC2, and GT2B, used in diagnostic models. We emphasize the combined effects of cellular senescence and underlying pathological mechanisms to enhance early diagnosis. The analysis reveals how these genes function: STAT1 drives inflammation and fibrosis through antiviral pathways; ORC2 connects immune responses to kidney metabolism by disrupting cell cycle; and GT2B helps create a diagnostic system by influencing transcription and regulating inflammation-related gene expression. We also examine immune dysfunction and the unusual activity of immune cells, improving disease understanding, and have developed a diagnostic monogram to aid clinical decision-making. While the advantages are evident, limitations include the absence of human kidney tissue and blood samples from monkeypox-infected LN, as well as the need for further in vivo and in vitro validation of the selected genes. Consequently, follow-up studies, such as animal experiments and clinical case assessments, are crucial to confirm the clinical value of this research and enhance our understanding of the pathogenesis and accurate diagnosis.
