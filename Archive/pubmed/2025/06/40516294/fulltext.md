# Investigation of the anti-inflammatory mechanisms of fermented Chinese herbal residue solution in an APEC-Infected HD11 cell model through the PI3K/AKT and NF-κB pathways


## Abstract
  Avian pathogenic Escherichia coli (APEC) infection poses a significant challenge to the poultry industry, severely threatening poultry health and industrial development. The emergence of antibiotic resistance in conventional treatments underscores the urgent need for novel alternative therapies. Fermented Chinese herbal residue solution, as a potential substitute, is rich in bioactive components and exhibits multifaceted effects, including anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and immunomodulatory properties. This study investigates the anti-inflammatory mechanisms and key active compounds of the fermented herbal residue solution (BY3) in an APEC infection model. An in vitro APEC-infected HD11 cell model was established, with optimal infection conditions determined as a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 0.1 and an infection duration of 6 hours, based on cytotoxicity assays and qPCR analysis. Results demonstrated that BY3 intervention significantly downregulated the expression of inflammatory cytokines. APEC infection markedly upregulated the expression of key target genes in the PI3K/AKT and NF-κB pathways, whereas BY3 treatment significantly reduced their expression. Western blot analysis further confirmed that BY3 significantly decreased the phosphorylation levels of AKT, P65, and IκB proteins, as well as the total PI3K protein content. These findings suggest that BY3 mitigates APEC-induced inflammation by modulating the PI3K/AKT and NF-κB pathways. To elucidate the active components of BY3, non-targeted metabolomics sequencing, database comparison, and molecular docking were employed, identifying four key bioactive compounds: Tangeretin, Arctigenin, Rhein, and Phloretin. All tested compounds significantly reduced APEC-induced inflammatory cytokine expression. qPCR and Western blot analyses revealed differential regulatory effects on the PI3K/AKT and NF-κB pathways. In the PI3K/AKT pathway, Tangeretin exhibited the most comprehensive inhibitory effect, significantly reducing both AKT phosphorylation and total PI3K levels. In the NF-κB pathway, all compounds except Rhein markedly decreased the phosphorylation of P65 and IκB. Collectively, BY3 and its identified compounds exert protective effects against APEC-induced HD11 cell damage by regulating the PI3K/AKT and NF-κB pathways, suggesting that the fermented Chinese herbal residue solution may represent a promising therapeutic approach for APEC-related diseases in poultry.


## Introduction
  Avian pathogenic Escherichia coli (APEC) is a significant pathogen jeopardizing the poultry industry(Cui et al., 2016), causing a range of diseases including acute septicemia, colibacillosis in broilers, and peritonitis in layers, thereby posing a substantial threat to poultry health(Yuan et al., 2020). In recent years, the global prevalence of APEC infections has increased significantly, particularly in intensive farming regions, accompanied by a rapid rise in antibiotic resistance(Jamali et al., 2024). The widespread dissemination of antibiotic-resistant bacterial strains not only compromises the efficacy of conventional antibiotics but also elevates poultry mortality and diminishes production efficiency, resulting in substantial economic losses for the global poultry industry (Li et al., 2020). APEC infections predominantly affect the respiratory and intestinal systems, inducing intestinal inflammation, intestinal wall damage, and disruption of the intestinal barrier, which may progress to systemic infections(Jamali, Akrami, Bouakkaz and Dozois, 2024). In recent years, the incidence of APEC infections has risen due to the expansion of intensive farming practices and the increasing misuse of antibiotics(Tan et al., 2024), resulting in considerable economic losses to the poultry sector(Azam et al., 2020). Cellular models have proven instrumental in studying the mechanisms of infection and stress-related pathologies(Chen et al., 2024a), providing a critical platform to elucidate the interactions between pathogens or environmental factors and host cells(Qiao et al., 2024). Research has demonstrated that APEC can invade and persist within avian and human epithelial cells, a property critical to its pathogenicity (Chanteloup et al., 2011). Furthermore, chicken macrophages are widely employed to investigate APEC’s interactions with host immune cells, revealing that APEC not only survives within macrophages but also induces their apoptosis(Peng et al., 2019), a mechanism that may enable APEC to evade host immune clearance and facilitate infection spread.

  The inflammatory response constitutes a critical innate defense mechanism against APEC infection, activating the immune system to eliminate pathogens and safeguard the host. However, excessive or dysregulated inflammation can result in severe tissue damage and organ dysfunction(Peng et al., 2024b), exacerbating disease progression(Peng et al., 2019). Similarly, other poultry infectious diseases, such as nephropathic infectious bronchitis virus (NIBV), induce tissue damage by promoting proinflammatory cytokine release via the TLR7/NF-κB signaling axis, highlighting the common role of dysregulated inflammation in poultry pathogen infections(Li et al., 2022). The PI3K/AKT and NF-κB signaling pathways serve as pivotal regulatory nodes in the inflammatory response, playing essential roles in the pathological processes triggered by APEC infection(Lv et al., 2019). The PI3K/AKT pathway modulates cell survival, proliferation, and metabolic homeostasis through phosphorylation cascades, sustaining cellular functions in inflammatory environments and influencing the intensity of immune responses(Eräsalo et al., 2018). Meanwhile, the NF-κB pathway, a central transcriptional regulatory system, primarily mediates the transcription and expression of proinflammatory cytokines (e.g., IL-1β, TNF-α), thereby amplifying inflammation(Lai et al., 2017). Studies indicate that APEC infection markedly activates the PI3K/AKT and NF-κB pathways, intensifying inflammatory damage. Thus, inhibiting the activation of these pathways may offer a potential strategy to mitigate APEC-induced inflammation.

  Chinese medicine residue refers to the solid residual material remaining after the extraction of active components from Chinese medicinal herbs. It is rich in various bioactive compounds and exhibits potential effects such as anti-inflammatory, antibacterial, and anti-stress properties, making it a promising candidate as an alternative to antibiotics (Li et al., 2021). Microbial fermentation enhances its bioactivity, producing metabolites such as polyphenols, flavonoids, and polysaccharides that confer multiple benefits, including anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and immunomodulatory effects(Hsu et al., 2013). The BY3 fermentation broth used in this study is derived from the residues of Asarum sieboldii, Glycyrrhiza uralensis, Cinnamomum cassia, Angelica sinensis, Paeonia lactiflora, Ziziphus jujuba, and Akebia quinata. These herbs are valued in traditional Chinese medicine for their tonic, anti-inflammatory, and immunomodulatory properties. For example, Glycyrrhiza uralensis contains glycyrrhizin, which has anti-inflammatory and antibacterial effects(Zhu et al., 2022), while Angelica sinensis and Paeonia lactiflora alleviate inflammation(Li et al., 2024a; Sun et al., 2024). Notably, BY3 fermentation broth has demonstrated significant anti-inflammatory and antibacterial effects against porcine Klebsiella pneumoniae by inhibiting capsular polysaccharide and immune escape (Chen et al., 2024b). However, the mechanisms underlying the effects of the fermentation broth on APEC infection, as well as its key active compounds, remain incompletely understood. This study hypothesizes that the fermentation broth and its critical compounds may alleviate APEC-induced inflammatory damage by suppressing the PI3K/AKT and NF-κB signaling pathways, providing a foundation for exploring its molecular mechanisms against APEC infection.


## Materials and methods

### Strains, cells, and drugs
  The avian-derived APEC clinical isolate A1 and Lactobacillus rhamnosus PC30-2-1 were preserved by the Key Laboratory of New Veterinary Drug Development and Innovation, College of Veterinary Medicine, Jilin Agricultural University. HD11 cells were purchased from Shanghai Yaji Biotechnology Co., Ltd. The BY3 compound Chinese medicine is a formulation primarily designed for tonic effects. Based on preliminary experimental results(Chen et al., 2024b), 12 g each of Asarum sieboldii (Asarum), Glycyrrhiza uralensis (licorice), Cinnamomum cassia (cinnamon), Angelica sinensis (Chinese angelica), Paeonia lactiflora (red peony root), and Ziziphus jujuba (jujube), along with 8 g of Akebia quinata (akebia), were weighed, added to 2 L of distilled water, soaked for 30 minutes, boiled over high heat, and filtered after 1 hour of boiling to obtain the decoction. Subsequently, 2 L of distilled water was added again, and the mixture was decocted for 45 minutes, filtered, and the herbal residue was naturally air-dried for fermentation. At a solid-to-liquid ratio of 1:15, the BY3 compound herbal residue and distilled water were weighed, autoclaved at 121°C for 20 minutes to obtain the aqueous extract (AE). The Lactobacillus rhamnosus PC30-2-1 strain was inoculated into MRS liquid medium and cultured in a shaker until the logarithmic growth phase (OD600nm=0.5). It was then added to the aqueous extract at a 2 % inoculation rate and fermented at 37°C with an initial pH of 6.5 ± 0.1 for 18 hours. The fermentation endpoint was determined at 18 hours, with confirmation of an OD600nm value of 1.0 ± 0.1 and a pH decrease to 5.0 ± 0.2. After fermentation, BY3 was filtered through four layers of gauze and centrifuged (4°C, 12,000 g for 10 minutes), with the supernatant being the BY3 fermentation broth. The Chinese medicinal materials were purchased from Fuzhiyuan Agricultural Development Co., Ltd., Qiaocheng District, Bozhou City.


### Cytotoxicity assay
  HD11 cells were seeded into 96-well plates at a density of 1 × 10⁴ cells per well, with 100 μL per well, and incubated at 37°C for 2 hours. After the cells had fully adhered, the culture medium was discarded, and 100 μL of maintenance medium containing adjusted drug concentrations was added. The cells were cultured at 37°C for 24 hours. Subsequently, 10 μL of CCK8 solution (Kryptolite Biologicals, China) was added to each well, followed by incubation at 37°C for 1 hour. The absorbance at 450 nm was then measured using a multifunctional microplate reader, and cell viability was calculated.


### Effects of different multiplicities of APEC infection on the transcriptional levels of inflammatory cytokines in HD11 cells over continuous time periods
  Cells were seeded into 6-well plates at a density of 1 × 10⁶ cells per well and cultured in an incubator at 37°C for 12 hours. The culture medium was then discarded, and APEC was inoculated into the wells at multiplicities of infection (MOI) of 0.01, 0.1, 1, 10, and 100. At 2 h, 4 h, 6 h, and 8 h post-infection, the culture medium was removed, and 1 mL of Trizol was added to collect the cells. The relative expression levels of the IL-1β, IL-2, IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α genes were subsequently measured.


### Effects of BY3 fermentation broth on gene expression in APEC-infected HD11 cells
  Cells were seeded into 6-well plates at a density of 1 × 10⁶ cells per well and cultured in an incubator at 37°C for 12 hours. Subsequently, four intervention groups were treated with BY3 fermentation broth (25 mg/mL and 50 mg/mL), aqueous extract (AE) (50 mg/mL), and gentamicin (GM) (4 μg/mL), respectively. Gentamicin was selected based on its minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) for APEC A1, with 4 μg/mL representing a sub-inhibitory concentration to minimize bacterial growth suppression while evaluating its effects on gene expression. Except for the negative control (NC) group, the remaining five groups were inoculated with APEC at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 0.1. After incubation at 37°C for 6 hours, the culture medium was discarded, and 1 mL of Trizol was added to collect the cells for the detection of gene expression levels.


### Effects of natural compounds on gene expression in APEC-infected HD11 cells
  Cells were seeded into 6-well plates at a density of 1 × 10⁶ cells per well and cultured in an incubator at 37°C for 12 hours. Subsequently, four compound intervention groups were treated with Tangeretin (128 μg/mL), Phloretin (128 μg/mL), Rhein (64 μg/mL), and Arctigenin (64 μg/mL), respectively. Except for the negative control (NC) group, the remaining five groups were inoculated with APEC at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 0.1. After incubation at 37°C for 6 hours, the culture medium was discarded, and 1 mL of Trizol was added to collect the cells for the detection of gene expression levels.


### Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) assays
  The primers used for qRT-PCR were synthesised according to the literature(Wang et al., 2018; Zhang et al., 2019).RNA was extracted using RNAiso Plus (TransGen Biotech, China), and the quantity and quality of the RNA were assessed on a NanoDrop 2000c spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). cDNA was synthesised with a cDNA Synthesis Kit (ToloBio, China). The reaction system consisted of 10 μL of 2 × Q3 SYBR qPCR Master Mix, 8.2 μL of ddH2O, 1 μL of cDNA, and 0.4 μL of upstream or downstream primers. The reaction program was as follows: pre-denaturation at 95°C for 5 min, followed by 40 cycles of denaturation at 95°C for 10 s, annealing at 60°C for 15 s, and extension at 72°C for 20 s. β-actin was used as the reference gene for normalization of gene expression. The fold change in gene expression was calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method. ### Total Protein Extraction and Western Blot
  All experimental antibodies were purchased from Wuhan Proteintech Co., Ltd. Primary antibodies, including those against AKT, p-AKT, P65, p-P65, IκB, p-IκB, PI3K, and β-actin, were diluted at 1:1000, except for the β-actin antibody, which was diluted at 1:2000. HD11 cells were washed with pre-chilled PBS, and 400 μL of RIPA lysis buffer (Beyotime, China) was added. After lysis on ice for 10 minutes, the mixture was transferred to a 1.5 mL centrifuge tube and lysed on ice for an additional 30 minutes. It was then centrifuged at 4°C and 12,000 g for 15 minutes, and the supernatant was collected. The protein concentrations of all samples were determined using a BCA protein quantification kit (Beyotime, China) and normalized. Based on the size of the target protein, SDS-PAGE gels were prepared. Protein samples and markers were sequentially loaded into the wells, electrophoresed at 80 V for 20 minutes, then switched to 120 V until the loading buffer migrated to the bottom. The proteins were transferred to a PVDF membrane at 200 mA for 60 minutes and blocked with 5 % skim milk powder for 2 hours. Primary antibodies were incubated at 4°C for 12 hours, followed by secondary antibodies for 2 hours. Excess antibodies were washed off with TBST, and ECL luminescent solution (BOSTER, China) was used for development in a protein imaging system. β-actin was used as an internal reference, and ImageJ was employed for grayscale analysis to quantify the expression levels of the target proteins.


### LC-MS/MS Analysis
  Sample Preparation: Six samples each of BY3 aqueous extract and fermentation broth (12 samples total) were thawed at 4 °C and vortexed for 1 min to ensure homogeneity. An appropriate volume of each sample was accurately transferred to a 2 mL centrifuge tube, and 400 µL of methanol was added as the extraction solvent, followed by vortexing for 1 min to facilitate component extraction. The samples were centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 min at 4°C, and the resulting supernatant was transferred to a new 2 mL centrifuge tube. The supernatant was concentrated to dryness using nitrogen blowing or vacuum evaporation. The dried residue was reconstituted in 150 µL of 80 % methanol containing 4 ppm 2‑chloro-l-phenylalanine (internal standard). The reconstituted solution was filtered through a 0.22 μm membrane, and the filtrate was collected in LC-MS vials for analysis.

  Chromatographic conditions: A Thermo Vanquish ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) system was used, equipped with an ACQUITY UPLC® HSS T3 column (2.1 × 100 mm, 1.8 µm). The column temperature was maintained at 40°C, and the flow rate was set to 0.3 mL/min. The mobile phase consisted of acetonitrile with 0.1 % formic acid and water. The gradient elution started with 8 % acetonitrile, increased to 98 % over 8 minutes, maintained at 98 % until the 10th minute, then quickly dropped back to 8 %, and continued until the 12th minute.

  Mass spectrometry conditions: A Thermo Q Exactive Focus mass spectrometer was used, equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source. Data were collected in both positive and negative ion modes. The capillary temperature was set to 325°C. In full scan mode, the m/z range was 100 to 1000, with a resolution of 70,000 and a collision energy of 30 eV.


### Network pharmacology screening of key anti-inflammatory compounds in fermented herbal residue broth
  Proteins from the PI3K/AKT and NF-κB signaling pathways were selected as candidate targets, and compounds associated with these targets were identified by leveraging traditional Chinese medicine databases such as TCMSP, HERB, and ETCM. By inputting the names of the target proteins into the databases, compounds with potential interactions with these targets were screened. Additionally, RDKit was employed to perform structural comparisons between the compounds retrieved from the databases and the known components in the BY3 fermentation broth. The molecular fingerprint comparison algorithm provided by RDKit quantifies structural similarity between the two, facilitating the identification of common compounds between the BY3 fermentation broth and the Chinese medicine databases. This process enabled the screening of key components with anti-inflammatory potential, providing a foundation for subsequent experimental validation.


### Molecular docking validation of key anti-inflammatory compounds in fermented herbal residue broth
  The crystal structures of candidate protein targets were downloaded from the UniProt database (https://alphafold.ebi.ac.uk/) and the PDB database, and processed using AutoDockTools 1.5.6. This processing included the removal of ligands and water molecules, addition of hydrogen atoms, and conversion of the files into pdbqt format for storage, which were then used as receptors for molecular docking. The screened ligand compounds (potential active components in BY3 fermentation broth) were processed using RDKit to add hydrogen atoms and generate 3D coordinates for small molecules, followed by conversion to pdbqt format using Open Babel 2.4.1, serving as ligands for molecular docking. Molecular docking was performed using AutoDock Vina, and the docking results were visualized using PyMOL and Discovery Studio.


### Data processing
  All data were subjected to statistical difference analysis using SPSS 26.0 software, and graphs were generated using GraphPad Prism 9. Statistical analysis of the data was performed using a univariate analysis method, with p < 0.05 or p < 0.01 indicating significant or highly significant differences, respectively.


## Results

### Effects of APEC infection on HD11 cell viability
  The results showed that within the infection time range of 2–8 h, cell viability decreased with prolonged infection time. Concurrently, cell viability also decreased as the multiplicity of infection (MOI) increased. Based on these observations, the experimental parameters for subsequent APEC infection of HD11 cells were established as follows: the infection time range was set at 2–6 hours, while groups with APEC infection for 6 hours at MOI values of 1, 10, and 100, as well as APEC infection for 4 hours at an MOI of 100, were excluded. These excluded conditions resulted in cell viability significantly below the 70 % threshold. The remaining experimental groups were used for further screening and analysis to determine the optimal infection conditions. ### Effects of APEC infection on inflammatory cytokine gene transcription in HD11 cells
  The results showed that the transcriptional levels of inflammatory cytokines generally increased with longer infection times and higher MOI values. Specifically, the expression levels of IL-1β, IL-2, and IL-6 showed no significant difference compared to the negative control (NC) group under APEC infection for 2 hours at MOI values of 0.01 and 0.1, but were significantly upregulated under other infection conditions (p < 0.05 or p < 0.01). The transcriptional levels of IL-8 and TNF-α were significantly higher than those in the NC group across all infection conditions (p < 0.05 or p < 0.01). Notably, the highest transcriptional levels of all tested genes were observed under APEC infection for 6 hours at an MOI of 0.1. Based on these results, APEC infection for 6 hours at an MOI of 0.1 was selected as the standard infection condition for subsequent studies. ### Effects of BY3 fermentation broth on HD11 cell viability
  The results showed that after HD11 cells were treated with varying concentrations (0–100 mg/mL) of fermented herbal residue broth for 16 hours, cell viability in the 75 and 100 mg/mL concentration groups was significantly reduced compared to the NC group (p < 0.01). However, within the 0–50 mg/mL concentration range, BY3 fermentation broth exhibited no significant cytotoxicity. These results indicate that BY3 fermentation broth demonstrates good biocompatibility with HD11 cells within the 0–50 mg/mL range, providing a safe and reliable concentration range for subsequent anti-inflammatory studies. ### Effects of BY3 fermentation broth on inflammatory cytokine transcription in HD11 cells
  The results showed that compared to the NC group, the APEC infection group (IG group) significantly upregulated the transcriptional levels of IL-1β, IL-2, IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α genes in HD11 cells (p < 0.01). Compared to the IG group, the BY3 aqueous extract intervention group (AE group) showed no significant differences in the transcriptional levels of these inflammatory cytokines. However, intervention with BY3 fermentation broth significantly reduced the transcriptional levels of these cytokines (p < 0.05 or p < 0.01), as did gentamicin intervention (p < 0.01). Notably, the downregulation effect of the high-concentration fermentation broth group was comparable to or superior to that of gentamicin. ### Effects of compounds on HD11 cell viability
  The results showed that HD11 cells treated with varying concentrations (0–256 μg/mL) of compounds for 16 hours exhibited concentration-dependent cytotoxicity compared to the NC group. Similarly, Tangeretin(TAN) and Phloretin(PHL) at 256 μg/mL significantly reduced cell viability (p < 0.01), but showed no significant cytotoxicity within the 0–128 μg/mL range. Based on these results, subsequent cell experiments utilized the non-cytotoxic concentration ranges of each compound. ## Discussion
  APEC is an important pathogenic bacterium in poultry farming, inducing diseases such as colibacillosis, airsacculitis, and septicemia, which result in substantial economic losses and jeopardize poultry health(Khairullah et al., 2024). Studies have shown that APEC employs virulence factors such as lipopolysaccharide (LPS) and outer membrane proteins to invade host cells, with certain strains capable of infiltrating avian and human epithelial cells and surviving intracellularly, indicating potential cross-host infectivity(Chanteloup et al., 2011). Its epidemiology, virulence mechanisms, pathogenesis, and transmission routes have been extensively investigated, with strains like O1:K1 and O2:K1 identified as posing zoonotic risks(Ha et al., 2024). Furthermore, APEC exacerbates inflammation in HD11 cells by triggering necroptosis and NF-κB activation via RIPK1-mediated outer membrane vesicles(Li et al., 2024b). Antibiotic resistance poses a significant challenge to APEC control, as multidrug-resistant strains isolated from broilers exhibit complex patterns of resistance and virulence genes(Patel et al., 2024). Previous research has demonstrated that BY3 fermentation broth offers a novel antibacterial strategy against resistance by inhibiting bacterial immune evasion, such as in porcine Klebsiella pneumoniae(Chen et al., 2024b). This study revealed that BY3 fermentation broth significantly mitigates APEC-induced inflammatory damage in HD11 cells by modulating the PI3K/AKT and NF-κB pathways.

  This study selected HD11 cells as the APEC infection model because they are a chicken-derived macrophage cell line that robustly mimics the inflammatory responses of the poultry immune system to APEC infection, particularly in cytokine secretion and signaling pathway activation(Peng et al., 2018). They are widely used in studying poultry pathogen immune regulation(Liu et al., 2019). Compared to other cell lines, HD11 cells stably express inflammation-related genes, making them ideal for in vitro studies of APEC-induced immune responses(Sun et al., 2022). Inflammatory cytokines are central regulators of immune responses, playing a critical role in inflammation and pathogen defense(Song et al., 2022). Cytokines such as IL-1β, IL-2, IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α, secreted by activated immune cells (e.g., macrophages), facilitate cell recruitment, fever, and tissue repair, yet their overproduction can lead to pathological inflammation. Research indicates that APEC infection markedly induces these cytokines, as evidenced by caspase 3/7 activation in J774 macrophages, suggesting an apoptotic response accompanying inflammation(Bastiani et al., 2005). Infections with APEC and fecal E. coli both result in lung inflammation and macrophage apoptosis, reflecting similar respiratory damage patterns(Horn et al., 2012). Analysis of alternative splicing in APEC-infected chicken macrophages has uncovered gene regulation mechanisms that enhance cytokine expression(Li et al., 2023). To establish the APEC infection model in HD11 cells, this study tested multiplicities of infection (MOI) ranging from 0.01 to 100 and infection durations of 2–8 hours. Cell viability was assessed using CCK8 assays, and transcription levels of IL-1β, IL-2, IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α were measured by qPCR. The results showed that at an MOI of 0.1 and 6 hours, cell viability exceeded 70 %, and inflammatory cytokine transcription, as detected by qPCR, reached its peak with the most significant upregulation, indicating that this condition effectively induces inflammation without excessive cell damage, thus it was selected as the standard condition for APEC infection of HD11 cells.

  Fermentation broth derived from traditional Chinese medicine residues has garnered significant attention due to its bioactivity and low toxicity. Microbial fermentation transforms complex herbal substrates into bioactive metabolites with therapeutic potential. Studies have demonstrated that Ganoderma lucidum fermentation of ginseng residue converts ginsenoside Rd into a more bioavailable form(Hsu et al., 2013). Likewise, Ginkgo biloba extract has been shown to mitigate testicular injury in poultry by modulating SKP2 and autophagy pathways, demonstrating the therapeutic potential of natural products(Wang et al., 2024). For instance, Xiang-qi-tang, a traditional Chinese medicine extract, demonstrates anti-inflammatory potential by modulating serum TNF-α and IL-1β levels in APEC-infected chickens(Lee et al., 2023), whereas BY3 fermentation broth, derived from microbial fermentation of herbal residues, generates polyphenol and flavonoid metabolites, achieving broader anti-inflammatory effects in the HD11 cell model. Aspergillus oryzae fermentation extract exhibits inflammation alleviation in Mycoplasma pneumoniae infection, suggesting broad-spectrum anti-inflammatory properties(Lee et al., 2023). Amid the escalating challenge of antibiotic resistance, fermentation products are considered promising alternatives due to their reduced risk of resistance development(Nhung et al., 2017). This study found that BY3 fermentation broth exhibited no significant toxicity to HD11 cells at concentrations of 0-50 mg/mL, with its safe concentration range determined via CCK8 assays using a cell viability threshold of ≥70 %, and markedly suppressed APEC-induced transcription of IL-1β, IL-2, IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α, with the high-concentration group achieving anti-inflammatory effects comparable to or surpassing gentamicin, whereas the aqueous extract showed limited suppression, indicating that fermentation substantially enhances its anti-inflammatory activity.

  The PI3K/AKT and NF-κB signaling pathways are pivotal in mediating inflammation induced by bacterial infections(Xu et al., 2024). APEC activates these pathways via TLR4, resulting in AKT phosphorylation and P65 nuclear translocation, which drive the expression of proinflammatory genes(Wang et al., 2023). Shikonin suppresses inflammation and chondrocyte apoptosis in an osteoarthritis model by regulating the PI3K/AKT pathway(Fu et al., 2016). Moxibustion inhibits M1 macrophage polarization in rheumatoid arthritis through the TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB pathway, demonstrating its immunomodulatory role(Kun et al., 2024). For instance, baicalin mitigates inflammation in APEC infection by regulating the PI3K/AKT and NF-κB pathways(Cheng et al., 2022); in contrast, this study employs BY3 fermentation broth, which utilizes green transformation of traditional Chinese medicine residues for waste valorization, and assesses a broader range of PI3K/AKT and NF-κB pathway target genes, revealing a more comprehensive anti-inflammatory mechanism. This study observed that APEC infection significantly upregulated the transcription of PI3K/AKT and NF-κB target genes, along with increased phosphorylation of AKT, P65, and IκB and total PI3K protein levels; high concentrations of BY3 fermentation broth markedly downregulated the expression of target genes and proteins in these pathways, indicating that its anti-inflammatory mechanism relies on suppressing these signaling cascades.

  Natural products and their metabolites have gained considerable attention for their notable anti-inflammatory potential, offering a diverse chemical foundation for drug development. Amber extract demonstrates anti-inflammatory activity in LPS-induced RAW 264.7 macrophages by modulating inflammation-related pathways(Tian et al., 2021). RHE alleviates inflammation and oxidative stress in chronic glomerulonephritis by inhibiting NF-κB signaling(Chen et al., 2022). TAN reduces inflammation and oxidative responses in spinal cord injury by activating the Sesn2/Keap1/Nrf2 pathway(Peng et al., 2024a). PHL mitigates insulin resistance and associated inflammation by blocking the PPARγ S273-Cdk5 interaction(Kumar et al., 2023). ARC effectively prevents LPS-induced neuroinflammation and cognitive impairment(Yuan et al., 2022). Endarachne binghamiae extract ameliorates macrophage inflammatory responses by regulating MAPK, NF-κB, and PI3K/AKT pathways, and prevents acute lung injury in mice(Lee et al., 2025). This study identified ARC, RHE, TAN, and PHL as potential key anti-inflammatory compounds in BY3 fermentation broth through LC-MS analysis, network pharmacology, and molecular docking, establishing their safe concentration ranges in HD11 cells via CCK8 assays using a cell viability threshold of ≥70 %. TAN significantly downregulated all inflammatory cytokines and target genes and proteins in the PI3K/AKT and NF-κB pathways, RHE and PHL broadly inhibited most inflammatory cytokines and target genes and proteins in the PI3K/AKT and NF-κB pathways, though their inhibitory scope was slightly less comprehensive than that of TAN, and ARC was effective against only a subset of targets, confirming these compounds as the primary contributors to BY3’s anti-inflammatory activity.

  This study demonstrates that BY3 fermentation broth effectively mitigates APEC-induced inflammatory damage in HD11 cells by suppressing inflammatory cytokine transcription and the PI3K/AKT and NF-κB signaling pathways. ARC, RHE, TAN, and PHL were identified as its key active components, with TAN exhibiting the broadest inhibitory effects. These findings underscore the potential of BY3 fermentation broth as an antibiotic alternative for combating APEC-induced inflammation. Future in vivo studies are needed to further validate its therapeutic efficacy and clinical applicability.
