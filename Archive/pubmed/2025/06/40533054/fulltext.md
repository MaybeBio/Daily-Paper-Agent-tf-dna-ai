# Age-dependent elevation of Nr4a1 attenuates PI3K/AKT/GSK3β pathway and mediates tau hyperphosphorylation and cognitive impairments


## Graphical abstract
  Graphical abstract


## Introduction
  Aging is the primary contributor to cognitive decline, manifesting as either the inevitable process of healthy aging or as pathological changes in cognition, such as mild cognitive impairment (MCI) and various forms of dementia [1]. Alzheimer’s disease (AD), the most prevalent dementia, accounts for approximately 60 % to 80 % of dementia cases, affecting around 11 % of individuals over 65 years old [2]. In terms of pathologies, AD is characterized by hyperphosphorylated tau-consisted neurofibrillary tangles (NFTs) inside neurons and deposition of protein β-amyloid (Aβ) outside neurons [3]. Among these pathologies, NFTs are known to disrupt axonal transport, destabilize microtubules, and ultimately contribute to neuronal death [4,5]. Despite numerous studies conducted over the past few decades on age-related cognitive decline, the underlying molecular mechanisms and effective therapies are still limited [6].

  Nuclear receptor subfamily 4 group A member 1, commonly known as Nr4a1, or alternatively as NGFI-B/NUR77/TR3, belongs to NR4A family, consisting of early-response genes that can be swiftly and transiently activated by a variety of stimuli [7,8]. Nr4a1 functions as a transcription factor, and binds to specific DNA sequences to regulate the expression of target genes involved in neuronal circuit dysregulation [9]. Structurally, Nr4a1 is composed of three functional domains: an N-terminal region that encodes activation function 1 (AF-1), a ligand-binding domain, and a DNA-binding domain. The latter specifically recognizes and binds to responsive elements known as NBREs, located in the promoter regions of its target genes, thereby enabling its transcriptional regulatory role. In the cell, Nr4a1 was reported to shift between mitochondria, cytoplasm, and nucleus to alter metabolism [10,11], synaptic plasticity [12,13], and cognition [14,15]. Research by Odila et al. has demonstrated widespread expression of Nr4a1 across all regions of the cerebral cortex, which is a brain region particularly vulnerable in AD [16]. In another early study, Nr4a1 was increased in postmortem brain tissue of AD patients. Notably, the expression of this gene was confined to specific neuronal populations, namely the vertebral neurons and dentate granule cells located within the cortex and hippocampus [17]. SerpinA3, which is a major constituent of senile plaque and found to be implicated in the development of AD, was showed to be a Nr4a1-regulated gene [18]. These findings suggest a positive correlation between Nr4a1 and AD pathology. However, whether Nr4a1 contributes directly to AD-like pathology during aging, and the underlying mechanisms involved, remain to be further explored.

  Senescence-accelerated mouse prone 8 (SAMP8) is a naturally occurring mouse strain that exhibits an accelerated senescence phenotype. The model exhibits significant neuronal loss and pronounced neuroinflammation. In addition, SAMP8 mice show age-dependent impairment in cognitive function. Thus, this mouse strain is considered to be an appropriate model of brain aging and sporadic AD [[19], [20], [21], [22], [23]]. Hence, SAMP8 mice were used to explore Nr4a1′s effects on tau hyperphosphorylation and cognitive function in context of aging, with senescence accelerated resistant mouse 1 (SAMR1) as a healthy control. In our present study, it was showed that hippocampal Nr4a1 was increased in SAMP8 and aged mice. Hippocampal overexpression of Nr4a1 led to tau hyperphosphorylation, synaptic deficits and cognitive dysfunction in SAMR1 mice. We discovered that PI3K/AKT signaling was compromised in hippocampus of both Nr4a1-overexpressed mice and aged mice via Bulk RNA sequencing and then these phenomena were validated at protein levels. Furthermore, through in vitro study, we found that Nr4a1 interacted with catalytic region of AKT, thus inhibited its enzymatic activity, giving rise to hyperactivation of GSK-3β, which induced hyperphosphorylation of tau; pretreatment with a small molecular blocker AKT302-312 reversed above changes in primary neurons. In vivo, knockdown of Nr4a1 in the hippocampus, to some extent, alleviated AD-like pathologies including tau hyperphosphorylation, and synaptic deficits in SAMP8 mice, meanwhile, PI3K/AKT signaling was recovered. Our findings highlight Nr4a1 decreases protein level of PI3K, and simultaneously functions as an AKT inhibitor, thus impairs PI3K/AKT pathway, and ultimately mediates tau hyperphosphorylation and cognitive dysfunction. Utilize of a Nr4a1-AKT blockage peptide provides a potential intervention of AD and aging-related cognitive decline.


## Results

### Nr4a1 is elevated in the hippocampus of AD patients and aging mice, accompanied with tau hyperphosphorylation
  Considering AD as the predominant cause of age-related dementia, we first analysed Nr4a1 transcription levels in the hippocampus of AD patients using the GSE29378 dataset from Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. Our analysis indicated a notable upregulation of Nr4a1 expression in the hippocampus of AD patients compared to that observed in healthy individuals (Fig. 1a, Table S1). In another dataset, GSE28146 [24], we found that hippocampal Nr4a1 expression was markedly upregulated in patients with severe AD compared to those with moderate AD (Fig. 1b). Additionally, within the GSE26927 dataset, we observed a positive association between Nr4a1 expression and age among AD patients (Fig. 1c). Subsequently, we assessed the mRNA and protein levels of Nr4a1 in the hippocampus of aged mice. Notably, both mRNA and protein levels of Nr4a1 were significantly increased in the hippocampus of older mice compared to those of young controls (Fig. 1d–f). To further investigate, we detected hippocampal Nr4a1 expression and tau phosphorylation levels in SAMP8 and SAMR1 at 3, 6, and 8 months of age. Immunoblotting indicated a progressive elevation of Nr4a1 protein levels in the hippocampus of SAMP8 mice from 3 to 8 months of age, compared to age-matched SAMR1 controls, suggesting an age-dependent upregulation (Fig. 1g–h). Consistent with the changes in Nr4a1 expression, AT8 (a marker indicative of tau hyperphosphorylation) was also elevated in the hippocampus of SAMP8 mice compared to age-matched SAMR1 controls (Fig. 1g, i). Linear regression analysis demonstrated a significant positive correlation between Nr4a1 expression and AT8 intensity (R2 = 0.7601, p = 0.0002) (Fig. 1j).   Cellular senescence represents a stable state of cell cycle arrest in proliferating cells, that occurs across various tissues in response to diverse physiological and pathological stimuli including aging [25,26]. In our current study, we examined several senescence-associated indicators, specifically focusing on SA-β-gal activity and the protein abundance of p16 and p53. Elevated SA-β-gal activity means larger lysosomal quantity and volume which indicates cellular senescence [27]. Our findings revealed that the area stained positive for SA-β-gal in the hippocampus of 17-month-old C57BL/6 mice was markedly expanded compared to that of 2-month-old controls (Fig. S1a). Furthermore, the activation of p16 and p53 proteins is strongly correlated with cell cycle arrest during aging and senescence [28]. Immunoblot analysis of P16 and P53 showed the same trends of changes with SA-β-gal staining between aged and young mice (Fig. S1b–c). Collectively, these results suggest that hippocampus of 17-month-old C57BL/6 mice undergoes notable senescence-associated alterations in comparison to that of 2-month-old mice.


### STP-induced tau hyperphosphorylation is mediated by Nr4a1 in the primary hippocampal neurons
  Previous studies have demonstrated that both staurosporine (STP) and amyloid-β (Aβ) can induce cellular senescence [[29], [30], [31]]. To explicit the function of Nr4a1 in aging-induced tau hyperphosphorylation in vitro, we utilized STP to induce cellular senescence and employed shRNA-mediated lentiviral to knockdown Nr4a1 in primary hippocampal neurons. Firstly, to figure out optimal concentration of STP to induce neuronal senescence, after neurons were treated with gradient concentrations of STP from 0.1 μM to 1 μM, CCK8 assay was utilized to measure the cell viability (Fig. 2a). As a consequence, 0.1 μM STP was the highest non-toxic concentration, and thus, we chose 0.1 µmol STP to do the following experiments. SA-β-Gal staining showed that STP-treat group had more SA-β-Gal positive cells than control group (Fig. 2b), indicating successful induction of a senescence phenotype in neurons. Protein levels of Nr4a1 and tau phosphorylated at Ser202/Thr205 (AT8) were detected by immunoblot analysis, and the results showed that Nr4a1 and AT8 in the STP-treated group was increased compared to negative control, of which, Nr4a1 showing a more than one-fold increase; total tau (tau5) levels remained unchanged (Fig. 2c–d). Furthermore, we found Nr4a1 mRNA level was increased after STP treatment (Fig. 2e), suggesting that STP may elevate Nr4a1 levels, at least in part, by promoting its transcription. In addition, numerous studies have reported that Aβ can induce cellular senescence [[32], [33], [34], [35]]. Based on a prior research [36], we used 5 μM oligomeric Aβ to induce neuronal senescence. After treating primary neurons with DMSO, 1 μM Aβ, or 5 μM Aβ for 24 h, p16 was increased, further confirming the induction of a senescence phenotype (Fig. S2a–b). Notably, similar to the STP treatment, both Nr4a1 and tau phosphorylated at Ser202/Thr205 were elevated following Aβ treatment (Fig. S2a–b).Fig. 2STP-induced tau hyperphosphorylation was mediated by Nr4a1 in primary hippocampal neurons.   To elucidate whether Nr4a1 is a necessary factor for STP-induced tau hyperphosphorylation, we employed the most efficient shRNA-encoded lentivirus to knock down Nr4a1 in rat primary neurons (Fig. S2c), followed by 24-hour treatment with STP. Our results showed that knockdown of Nr4a1 effectively rescued STP-induced tau hyperphosphorylation at Ser202/Thr205 (AT8) (Fig. 2f–g). Additionally, we utilized adeno-associated virus (AAV) to overexpress Nr4a1 and lentivirus to knock down Nr4a1 separately in non-treated neurons. Overexpression of Nr4a1 resulted in increased tau phosphorylation, whereas knockdown of Nr4a1 reduced tau phosphorylation levels at sites of Ser202/Thr205 (AT8) and Ser396 (Fig. 2h–i, Fig. S2c). To conclude, through the upregulation and downregulations of Nr4a1 in normal and senescent neurons, we demonstrate that Nr4a1 is sufficiently and necessarily involved in the regulation of STP-induced tau hyperphosphorylation in vitro.


### Overexpression of Nr4a1 induces tau hyperphosphorylation, synaptic damage and cognitive impairments in wild-type mice
  In vitro study suggested that Nr4a1 is implicated in tau hyperphosphorylation under conditions of cellular senescence. To further figure out the role of Nr4a1 in regulations of tau hyperphosphorylation and cognitive function in vivo, we performed bilateral injections of AAV-Nr4a1-EGFP (AAV-Nr4A1) or AAV-EGFP (AAV-CON) into the CA3 region of the hippocampus using stereotactic techniques in wild-type SAMR1 mice, aiming to upregulate Nr4a1 expression (Fig. 3a). One-month post-injection, GFP fluorescence was specifically detected in the hippocampal region (Fig. 3b). Immunofluorescence staining of Nr4a1 in CA3 hippocampal area was significantly increased with injection of AAV-Nr4a1 (Fig. 3c). Open field test showed that there was no significant change in locomotor activity between the two groups of mice (Fig. 3d). However, the novel object recognition test demonstrated that AAV-Nr4a1-treated mice exhibited a significantly reduced discrimination index, indicating impaired long-term hippocampus-dependent object recognition memory (Fig. 3e). The MWM test was employed to measure hippocampus-dependent spatial learning and memory. During the fifth day of training, mice in the AAV-Nr4a1 group exhibited a longer duration to reach the escape platform compared to the control group (Fig. 3f–g). On the testing day, AAV-Nr4a1-treated mice showed fewer platform crossings and longer escape latency to the platform location (Fig. 3h, Fig. S3a), suggesting impaired spatial learning and memory. ### Bulk RNA sequencing in hippocampus of mice reveals abnormal changes in PI3K/AKT signaling in Nr4a1-overexpressed mice
  To gain deeper insights into the underlying molecular mechanism by which elevated Nr4a1 levels drive tau hyperphosphorylation, we conducted Bulk RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) to detect alterations in gene transcription linked to Nr4a1 upregulation. This analysis revealed a set of genes that exhibited significant differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in mice overexpressing Nr4a1 compared to controls (Fig. 4a). An examination of these DEGs using Gene Ontology (GO) annotation revealed that the downregulated genes were predominantly associated with signalling, localization, synaptic activity, transcription factor activity, and protein binding (Fig. 4b). Furthermore, Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis of these DEGs demonstrated notable enrichment in pathways including PI3K/AKT signalling, neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction, and cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction (Fig. 4c). PI3K/AKT signalling pathway has been implicated in the modulation of various cellular processes, encompassing growth, survival, and metabolism in the central nervous system. In terms of its effect on neurons, it plays a crucial role in neuronal development, synaptic plasticity, and neuroprotection [[39], [40], [41]]. Decreased levels of PI3K subunits as well as inactivated AKT kinase were exhibited in the AD brain [42]. Thus, we conducted the analysis of the transcriptomic profile of the hippocampus in aged mice (17-month) and young mice (2-month) using RNA sequencing and found that the DEGs were similarly enriched in PI3K/AKT signalling cascade (Fig. 4d). Furthermore, we compared the expression levels of Nr4a1 between the two groups. The results demonstrated an upregulation of Nr4a1 expression in the older mice compared to the younger ones (Fig. 4e–f). To validate the effects of Nr4a1 overexpression on the PI3K/AKT pathway, we detected the protein abundance of PI3K, phosphorylated AKT, and GSK-3β in mice injected with either AAV-Nr4a1 or a control vector. Immunoblot analysis indicated a notable decrease in the protein levels of PI3K, phosphorylated AKT, and phosphorylated GSK-3β in the AAV-Nr4a1-injected mice (Fig. 4g–h).   To determine whether Nr4a1 induce tau hyperphosphorylation through regulating PI3K/AKT/GSK3β signalling, HEK293-hTau cells were treated with 20 μM PI3K inhibitor, LY294002 [44], in the meanwhile, they were transfected with DsRed-Nr4a1 or empty vector.   Based on above data, we speculate that increased Nr4a1 induces tau hyperphosphorylation potentially through dysregulation of PI3K/AKT signaling.


### Knockdown Nr4a1 rescues STP-induced PI3K/AKT pathway downregulation and neurodegeneration in primary neurons
  To test the hypothesis that Nr4a1 induces tau hyperphosphorylation by impairing the PI3K/AKT pathway during senescence, we used lentiviral-mediated shRNA (lenti-shNr4a1) tagged with GFP to knock down Nr4a1 in primary neurons, followed by STP treatment to induce senescence. In neurons transfected with the lenti-vector and treated with STP, Nr4a1 expression was elevated, and the PI3K/AKT pathway was inhibited. However, in neurons transfected with lenti-shNr4a1 and treated with STP, PI3K/AKT signaling remained unchanged (Fig. 5a–e). Synaptic impairment is a typical AD pathology, which is characterized by reduced dendritic spines and immature dendritic spine morphology. Dendritic spines of typical morphology are characterized by extensions ranging in length from 0.5 to 5 μm, featuring a distinct neck and either a mushroom-shaped head or a stubby appearance [45], and these were assessed using GFP fluorescence. STP treatment markedly reduced both the number and length of dendritic spines, while knockdown of Nr4a1 alleviated STP-induced synaptic deficits (Fig. 5f–j). ### Blockade Nr4a1-AKT interaction reversed tau hyperphosphorylation induced by Nr4a1
  It has been previously reported that AKT is predisposed to bind to Nr4a1 [46], leading us to hypothesize that increased Nr4a1 binding to AKT may inhibit its enzymatic activity, thereby contributing to tau hyperphosphorylation in context of senescence. In order to test this hypothesis, we first predicted the potential protein–protein interaction between Nr4a1 and AKT1 using the STRING database (https://string-db.org/), which suggested a possible structural interaction between the two proteins (Fig. 6a). We then further investigated this interaction using endogenous co-immunoprecipitation and co-immunofluorescence assays (Fig. 6b–e). To determine the specific AKT region that interacts with NR4A1, we conducted molecular docking studies, revealing that AKT has a strong binding affinity with NR4A1, particularly at amino acids 307–309 (free docking energy is 23.7 Kcal/mol), which located in the catalytic region of AKT (Fig. 6d). Based on this prediction, we synthesized a peptide (AKT302-312) conjugated with FITC to competitively inhibit the Nr4a1-AKT interaction.   To evaluate the effects of AKT302-312 on blocking the Nr4a1-AKT interaction in vitro, we first performed a cell viability assay in HEK293-hTau cells treated with various concentrations of AKT302-312. No cytotoxicity was observed at concentrations ranging from 0 to 200 μM (Fig. 6f). Next, to assess the direct interaction between AKT302-312 and Nr4a1, we treated HEK293-hTau cells with 50 μM AKT302-312 followed by transfecting with EGFP-AKT and DsRed-Nr4a1. Co-immunoprecipitation and immunoblot analysis revealed that AKT302-312 effectively blocked the interaction between Nr4a1 and AKT, which was accompanied by an increase in GSK-3β phosphorylation and a reversal of Nr4a1-induced tau hyperphosphorylation (Fig. 6g–k). To figure out whether AKT302-312 would interfere the interaction of Nr4a1 with other partners which showed in Fig. 6a, we detected the interaction between Nr4a1 and p38-MAPK in HEK293-hTau cells treated with 50 μM AKT302-312 followed by transfecting with EGFP-AKT and DsRed-Nr4a1. The results exhibited that AKT302-312 didn’t change the interaction between Nr4a1 and p38-MAPK (Fig. S5b). These findings suggest that the blockade of the Nr4a1-AKT interaction by AKT302-312 can reverse Nr4a1-induced tau hyperphosphorylation, as well as rejuvenate the activity of PI3K/AKT/GSK-3β pathway.


### Knockdown of Nr4a1 alleviates tau hyperphosphorylation, synaptic deficits and cognitive impairments in a senescence-accelerated mouse model
  To investigate whether knockdown of Nr4a1 could ameliorate aging-induced cognitive dysfunction, we bilaterally injected AAV-shNr4a1 and AAV-shRNA control vectors into the hippocampus of 3-month-old SAMR1 and SAMP8 mice, respectively. Cognitive performance was then assessed in all groups one month later (Fig. 7a). Initially, we conducted open field test and observed no statistically significant variations in locomotor activity among the groups (Fig. S6a). Golgi staining showed that the AAV-shRNA-injected SAMP8 resulted in a marked deletion of dendritic spines, along with a reduction of synaptic proteins like PSD95 and GluR2, compared with AAV-shRNA-injected SAMR1. In contrast, downregulation of Nr4a1 effectively lessened the above changes (Fig. 7k–o). Presynaptic protein synaptophysin displayed no significant change in four groups (Fig. 7m, p). These findings suggest that knockdown of Nr4a1 mitigates tau hyperphosphorylation and synaptic deficits in the senescence-accelerated mouse model, SAMP8.

  Previously, we observed that Nr4a1 elevation inhibited PI3K/AKT pathway through two mechanisms, i.e., decreasing protein level of PI3K and increasing Nr4a1′s interactions with AKT. To determine whether Nr4a1 knockdown could counteract this inhibition, we evaluated the protein levels of key components within the PI3K/AKT pathway. Our findings indicated that downregulation of Nr4a1 in the hippocampus rejuvenated the activity of the PI3K/AKT pathway (Fig. 7q–r). Taken together, these data suggest that Nr4a1 knockdown alleviates tau hyperphosphorylation, synaptic deficits and cognitive dysfunction through restoring PI3K/AKT pathway in SAMP8 mice.


## Discussion
  Aging is a major risk factor of neurodegenerative diseases associated with cognitive deficits. It has been well established that hyperphosphorylation of tau, deposition of Aβ, and loss of synapses are causative factors of cognitive decline in neurodegenerative diseases, particularly in AD [[47], [48], [49]]. Therefore, identifying potential therapeutic targets to mitigate AD-like pathologies in aging is crucial. Previous studies have suggested that Nr4a1 may play a key role in the regulation of AD-like pathologies [[16], [17], [18]]. Using the GEO dataset (GSE29378, GSE28146, GSE26927), we found significant upregulation of Nr4a1 in AD patients, a finding corroborated by observations in aging mice, i.e., 17-month-old mice, and SAMP8 mouse model of accelerated aging, where hippocampal Nr4a1 elevations were also observed. These indicate that Nr4a1 production increases during aging, which is consistent with findings made by Zhao et al. in APP/PS1 mice, exhibiting significantly heightened Nr4a1 mRNA and protein levels compared to age-matched controls [50]. In our study, upregulation of hippocampal Nr4a1 mediated tau hyperphosphorylation, synaptic deficits, and cognitive decline in wild-type SAMR1 mice. In contrast, downregulation of hippocampal Nr4a1 expression ameliorated these pathological features in SAMP8 mice. In particular, the protein level of Nr4a1 in SAMP8 mice was positively correlated with the severity of tau pathology. These findings bolster the notion that Nr4a1 plays a pivotal role in promoting AD-like pathologies during aging. However, contrasting our findings, Montarolo et al. reported a decrease in NR4A1 levels in the peripheral blood of AD patients relative to healthy controls [51]. This discrepancy may be attributed to differences in Nr4a1 expression between peripheral blood and brain tissue. Furthermore, Chen et al. found that Nr4a1 reduction contributed to cognitive decline [52]. In line with Montarolo et al., Chen et al. assessed Nr4a1 mRNA levels in peripheral blood but did not examine protein levels in brain parenchyma from AD patients. In their studies, Nr4a1 knockout (KO) mice showed deficits in contextual and working memory, as well as reduced long-term potentiation (LTP), suggesting that certain level of Nr4a1 is indispensable for maintaining normal function of brain. In our study, we employed AAV-shNr4a1 to suppress Nr4a1 expression, effectively maintaining low levels of Nr4a1 in the hippocampi of both SAMR1 and SAMP8 mice after injection (Fig. 7i). These results reinforce the idea that Nr4a1 might have a deleterious effect on the acceleration of aging processes, while also highlighting its necessity for normal physiological functions, as both elevated and reduced Nr4a1 levels may impair brain function.

  PI3K/AKT signalling pathway holds a crucial position in modulating distinct cellular processes, including signal transduction, cell proliferation, apoptosis, and metabolic regulation. In AD brain tissue, the levels of PI3K subunits, AKT phosphorylation at Thr308, and AKT activity are diminished, indicating compromised PI3K/AKT signalling in AD [42,53]. In the present study, both aged mouse hippocampi and STP-induced neurons showed decreased PI3K/AKT activity, suggesting that the inactivation of AKT and its downstream signalling occurs during aging and in AD. AKT phosphorylates GSK-3β at the Ser9 site, thereby decreasing its enzymatic activity. GSK-3β is a key kinase for tau, driving tau hyperphosphorylation in AD. However, the detailed mechanisms underlying PI3K/AKT inactivation in contexts of aging and AD remain unclear.

  Previous studies have linked Nr4a1 with impaired AKT activity. In context of colorectal cancer, under hypoxic, NR4A1 interacts with tumor suppressor protein, p63, to inhibit the transcription of Dicer, and subsequently decreasing the biogenesis of let-7i-5p which targets the 3′UTR of p110α mRNA and regulates its degradation, therefore AKT was inactivated [54]. Juan Xia et al. also reported that transcriptional activation of Nr4a1 inhibits the PI3K/AKT pathway [55]. In our observations, PI3K/AKT signalling was decreased in hippocampus of Nr4a1-overexpressed mice through Bulk RNA sequencing, which is consistent with this study [55]. Notably, Croce et al. showed that AKT interacts with Nr4a1 and inactivates it by phosphorylating Ser350 [46]. Hence, we hypothesize that excessive Nr4a1 may interact with AKT and inhibit its activity. Predictions made using the STRING database suggested that Nr4a1 could structurally interact with AKT1, which was confirmed by subsequent co-immunoprecipitation analysis. To further explore the interacting domains of these proteins, molecular docking studies revealed that AKT binds strongly to Nr4a1 at amino acids 307–309, which is part of AKT's catalytic region. Excessive Nr4a1 binding to AKT at these sites likely prevents the binding of kinase PDK1 to AKT, which is required for AKT phosphorylation and activation, consequently leading to AKT inactivation. Thus, we synthesized a small molecular peptide (AKT307-309) to competitively bind to Nr4a1 and restore AKT activity. Treatment with this peptide alleviated tau hyperphosphorylation. For SAMP8 mice with knockdown of Nr4a1 in hippocampus, phosphorylation and activity of AKT were restored, suggesting that elevated Nr4a1 may inhibit AKT and lead to its inactivation in the aging brain. Further investigation is needed to explore the therapeutic potential of small molecular blockers targeting this interaction in aging animal models.


## Conclusion
  This study provides a mechanistic insight into AD-like pathology in the context of aging, revealing an elevation of Nr4a1 in the hippocampus of both AD patients and aging mice. Furthermore, increased Nr4a1 is shown to be essential for senescence-associated tau hyperphosphorylation, both in vitro and in vivo. In addition, elevated Nr4a1 expression in the hippocampus of healthy wild-type mice leads to an increase in tau phosphorylation and a decline in cognitive function. RNA sequencing of aged and Nr4a1-overexpressing mice reveals a downregulation of the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway. Our results demonstrate that either knocking down or employing a blocking peptide to disrupt the Nr4a1-AKT interaction can rejuvenate PI3K/AKT pathway activity and alleviates tau hyperphosphorylation. These results suggest that Nr4a1 inhibition and small molecular blockers may offer promising therapeutic strategies for treating AD and mitigating cognitive decline associated with aging.

### Mice
  All mice experiments were granted approval by Animal Care and Use Committee of Jianghan University. Male mice of SAMR1/SAMP8 strains, aged 3 months and C57BL/6J mice, aged 2 and 17 months, respectively, were meticulously housed under a controlled 12-hour dark-light cycle, with continuous access to sufficient food and water. SAMP8 mice strain has been used for animal models of brain aging and sporadic AD due to its shortened life span and accelerated aging process. Due to similar background to SAMP8, SAMR1 with normal aging is used as a healthy control. Both SAMP8 and SAMR1 mice were provided by the Institute of Genetics and Developmental Biology, Chinese Academy of Sciences.


### Stereotaxic surgery
  SAMR1/SAMP8 mice were anesthetized intraperitoneally with a 6 % chloral hydrate solution at a dosage of 6 ml/kg. After adequately sedated, the mice were put in a stereotaxic apparatus (RWD, China) and firmly fixed with ear bars and bite bars. Virus was bilateral injected at the following coordinates relative to bregma: M/L: ±2.1 mm, A/P: +1.9 mm, D/V: −2.1 mm [56].

  A total volume of 1 μl OE-Nr4a1 (CMV-betaGlobin-Nr4a1-EGFP-3Flag-SV40 PolyA, 1.41 × 1012 copies/ml), or sh-Nr4a1 (AAV9-Nr4a1-RNAi, U6-MCS-CAG-EGFP, 3.69 × 1012 copies/ml), or negative control virus was bilaterally injected into the CA3 region of hippocampus. The virus was administered through stereotactic injection over a period of 5 min at a rate of 0.20 μl/min, and the needle was kept in place for 10 min. The viruses utilized in this study were all constructed by Genechem (Shanghai, China). Subsequent to behavioural experiments, all mice were euthanized, and their brain tissues were collected for biochemistry and morphology analysis.


### Behavior experiments
  Open field test, designed to evaluate motor activity and anxiety levels in mice, was conducted according to a previously established methodology [57,58]. Mice are placed in the center of a 45 cm × 45 cm × 45 cm square arena to explore for 5 min. Using the ANY Maze software (Shanghai Xinran Technology Co., Ltd.) to track and analyse their movements, which measured total distance travelled in the field [59].

  Novel object recognition test, which is aimed at assessing memory retention in mice, was performed according to established protocols [60]. On the first day, mice were allowed to become accustomed to the testing environment for 5 min; they were placed in a field containing two identical objects located in adjacent corners and given 5 min to explore freely. After a 24-hour interval, their capacity to distinguish between a novel and a familiar object was tested over 5 min. Time stayed in the object area was recorded using ANY maze software (Shanghai Xinruan Technology Co, LTD). The discrimination index quantifies the mice's preference for exploring the novel object relative to the familiar one.

  Morris water maze (MWM) test, was conducted, adhering to previously established protocols [61,62]. The setup involved a circular pool with a diameter of 120 cm and a height of 75 cm, filled with water of consistent opacity and maintained at 21 °C. Over a period of five days, mice were tasked with finding a submerged, stationary platform within a 60 s timeframe, with four trials per day. The time taken by each mouse to reach the platform, referred to as escape latency, was recorded. If a mouse couldn't locate the platform within the allowed time, it was gently guided to the platform and permitted to stay there for an extra 15 s. Following the training phase, a probe test was executed by removing the platform and allowing the mice to swim freely in the pool for 60 s. Their movements during this period were recorded by video, which was subsequently analysed using the Noldus video tracking system (Ethovision, Netherlands) to assess various performance indicators, including escape latency, swimming path, speed, and the number of times crossing the platform.


### Golgi staining
  Rapid Golgi Stain Kit (PK401, FD neurotechnology) was utilized in this experiment. In brief, brain tissues were taken from mice after anesthesia and saline perfusion, and then immersed in the A + B mixture for one week. The brain tissue was then transferred to liquid C for three days of immersion. A 150 μm section of the brain was then cut, and the section was observed with an Olympus VS200 slide scanner.


## Immunoblot analysis
  Cellular lysates were prepared from primary neurons and HEK293-hTau cells by incubating them in lysis buffer containing protease inhibitors on ice for 10 min. After being homogenized, the isolated hippocampus was then lysed in lysis buffer. Subsequently, approximately 10 μg of each protein sample were resolved by SDS-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis and transferred into a nitrocellulose membrane (Amersham Biosciences, England). 5 % skim milk was used to block the unoccupied protein sites in the membranes. The membranes were then incubated with specific primary antibodies for over 12 h, followed by a 1-hour incubation with the corresponding secondary antibody. Visualization of the immunoblots was achieved using the Odyssey infrared imaging system (Li-Cor Biosciences, USA) and analyzed with Image J software (Rawak Software, Germany).


### Co-immunoprecipitation
  Transfected HEK293-hTau cells were washed with cold PBS and subsequently lysed in a mild RIPA lysis buffer (P0013D, Beyotime Biotechnology, China). After centrifugation, the supernatant was incubated with protein A + G agarose and anti-DsRed2 overnight at 4 °C. The next day, after another centrifugation step, the precipitates were resuspended in loading buffer and boiled at 95 °C for 5 min. Finally, the interacting proteins were analysed by immunoblot analysis.


### Immunofluorescence staining
  The immunofluorescence (IF) procedure was carried out as previously detailed [63]. Neurons were fixed with 4 % polyformaldehyde solution for 10 min. Prior to antibody staining, both brain sections and cellular slides underwent permeabilization and blocking, which involved treatment with a solution containing 0.5 % Triton X-100 and 5 % BSA diluted in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). Following this, brain slices and cell slides were respectively incubated overnight with primary antibody against the target protein. The next day, they were incubated with a corresponding secondary antibody (1:200; Alexa Fluor, Invitrogen) for 1 h, followed by DAPI staining for 8 min. Finally, the images were visualized using a confocal microscope (Zeiss LSM 780, Germany).


### β-Galactosidase staining
  β-Galactosidase staining of cryopreserved brain sections was conducted utilizing the Beta-Galactosidase Staining Kit (C0602, Beyotime Biotechnology, China). The protocol was as follows: Initially, the brain slices were rinsed twice with PBS. Subsequently, they were placed in a β-galactosidase staining fixation solution and incubated at room temperature for 15 min. After this, the slices underwent three thorough washes with PBS, followed by dye solution incubation overnight at 37 °C. Finally, these brain slices were photoed under a light microscope: the small blue particles indicate positive β-galactosidase signals.


### GSK-3β activity assay
  Neuronal GSK-3β activity was determined utilizing an ELISA kit provided by MEIMIAN (China). The samples were measured on the immediate day following the preparation of the neuronal lysates. Kinase assays were conducted as previously described [64].


### RT-qPCR
  Brain tissue and primary neurons were subjected to RNA extraction using TRIzol reagent (CWBIO, Wuhan). 1 µg of total RNA was reverse-transcribed to cDNA employing a kit provided by TOYOBO (USA). The resulting cDNA was then diluted and amplified using specific primers for target genes, along with a reference gene for normalization (GAPDH). Relative Nr4A1 mRNA level was calculated by 2−ΔΔCt after normalized to GAPDH. The primer sequences were as follows: mouse Nr4a1-Forward: 5′-GCATGGTGAAGGAAGTTGTACG-3′ and Reverse: 5′- GGAGGCTGCTTGGGTTTTGA-3′; mouse GAPDH-Forward:5′-TCGCTCCTGGAAGATGGTGAT-3′ and Reverse: 5′-CAGTGGCAAAGTGGAGATTGTTG-3′; rat Nr4a1-Forward: 5′-CCTGGCCTACCGATCTAAGC-3′ and Reverse:5′- AGGCAAAGGCAGGAACATCA-3′; rat GAPDH-Forward:5′-GAAGGTCGGTGTGAACGGAT-3′ and Reverse: 5′-CCCATTTGATGTTAGCGGGAT-3′.


### Primary neuron culture and treatment
  All rat experiments were approved by Animal Care and Use Committee of Jianghan University. Primary neurons were isolated and cultured according to methods detailed in a previous study [65]. Briefly, the hippocampus was removed from embryos from E18 Sprague-Dawley rats and quickly cut into small sections, the tissue was incubated with D-Hank buffer, then suspended in 0.25 % trypsin solution and digested in a cell incubator for 15 min. After centrifugation, neurons were plated onto poly-D-lysine-coated plates and then maintained in neurobasal medium supplemented with B27, Glutamax and antibiotics. Half of the medium was replaced every three days. All reagents utilized for culturing the primary neurons were sourced from Gibco, USA.

  Neurons cultured at 5th day in vitro (DIV5) were infected with AAVs or LVs. AAV9-Nr4a1 (CMV-betaGlobin- Nr4a1-EGFP-3Flag-SV40 PolyA, 1.41 × 1012 copies/ml) and lenti-shNr4a1 (hU6-MCS-CBh-gcGFP-IRES-puromycin, 2.0 × 109 copies/ml) were constructed from Genechem (Shanghai, China). After neurons were infected with the virus, a concentration of 0.1 μM STP, dissolved in DMSO, was added into the medium and allowed to incubate for a period of 24 h. Subsequently, the neurons were collected for subsequent analyses.


### Bulk RNA sequencing
  The hippocampus of mice was isolated, followed by the extraction of total RNA using TRIzol reagent. After that, Oligo (dT)-enriched magnetic beads were utilized to enrich eukaryotic mRNA. This mRNA was then fragmented using a specific buffer to generate short fragments, which served as templates for synthesizing the first cDNA strand through the use of random hexamer primers. For the synthesis of the second cDNA strand, a mixture containing dNTPs, RNase H, and DNA polymerase I was employed. The resulting cDNA was purified with a QiaQuick PCR kit and eluted in EB buffer. The cDNA ends were repaired, poly(A) tails were added, and sequencing adapters were ligated. Fragment size selection was then conducted using agarose gel electrophoresis, followed by PCR amplification. The constructed sequencing library was sequenced with Illumina HiSeq™. Sequencing the samples on the platform generates image files, which are transferred by the software on the platform to generate raw data in FASTQ format (raw data). Cluster Profiler (v3.18.1) software was used for further analysis. RNA sequencing was provided by Sangon Biotechnology (Shanghai, China).


### Analysis of Nr4a1 transcriptional level in human AD brains
  For the quantitative analysis of Nr4a1 transcriptional level and correlative analysis between Nr4a1 transcriptional level and age in the AD GEO datasets, we downloaded the original data of these datasets (GSE29378, GSE28146, GSE26927), imported the downloaded data into “R” software, converted the gene IDs in the “R” software, and got the transcriptional levels of Nr4a1 among groups. Student's T test and linear correlation were performed to analyze the data.


### HEK293-hTau cells culture, plasmid transfection and treatment
  Fresh culture medium was prepared by adding 10 % fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1 % antibiotics into DMEM for the cultivation of HEK293-hTau cells, a variant of the human embryonic kidney 293 cell line genetically engineered to stably express the full-length human tau protein. These cells were maintained in a humidified incubator set at 37 °C with an atmosphere containing 5 % CO2. Prior to performing plasmid transfection, cells were plated in a 6-well plate and cultured for 24 h. For transient transfection, a solution containing 3 μg of either human NR4A1 or AKT plasmid (or their corresponding empty vectors) and 3 μl of Lipofectamine 3000 (Thermo Fisher, USA) was prepared and utilized for transfection in each well of the 6-well dish. For the use of LY294002, 20 μM LY294002 dissolved in DMEM was treated with HEK293-hTau cells.


### Molecular docking of protein–protein interactions
  Protein-protein interaction predictions were conducted using the PRISM Webserver, in accordance with established methodologies [66,67]. Briefly, the AA sequences of NR4A1 (P22736) and AKT1 (P31749) were obtained from UniProt database. Afterwards, ligand and receptor proteins were input into the PRISM platform (https://cosbi.ku.edu.tr/prism) to facilitate the prediction of their interaction modes. This computational approach enables a detailed analysis of potential binding affinities and the interaction surfaces.


### Statistical analysis
  Statistical analysis was conducted using GraphPad Prism 8.0 software. Descriptive statistics were utilized for all variables, with results presented as means ± SEM. Comparisons between groups were made using appropriate statistical methods: t-tests for two-group comparisons and one-way ANOVA for multiple groups, with subsequent post hoc tests conducted when necessary. Statistical significance was established at a p-value threshold of 0.05 or less.
