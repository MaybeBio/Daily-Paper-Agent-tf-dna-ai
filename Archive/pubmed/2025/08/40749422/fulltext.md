Growing evidence demonstrated the involvement of ferroptosis in the pathogenesis of myocardial ischemia/reperfusion injury (MIRI). The nature compound Wedelolactone (Wed) exhibits anti-ferroptosis effects. The current study aims to explore the impact of Wed on MIRI and reveal whether Wed could protect cardiomyocytes from ferroptosis. Rats underwent cardiac IR operation, and H9c2 cardiomyocytes were subjected to oxygen-glucose deprivation/reoxygenation (OGD/R). Rats or cell in Wed-treated group were subdivided into 2 different dose regimens: low and high. Wed intervention alleviated myocardial infarction and left ventricle dysfunction, and diminished the levels of biochemical markers for cardiac injury caused by cardiac IR. The content of Fe(2 +) and MDA, 4-HNE expression, and ROS production were increased in infarct zone, as well as GSH content, GSH-Px activity, and the levels of ferroptosis-related proteins were reduced. Treatment with Wed reversed the aforementioned changes. Similar results were observed in vitro experiments. Molecular docking analysis showed good binding affinity between Wed and the target protein METTL3. In OGD/R-induced H9c2 cells, Wed downregulated METTL3 protein expression and further blocked METTL3-mediated m(6)A modification of BRD4 mRNA. Overexpression of METTL3 reversed the antagonistic effect of Wed on cardiomyocyte ferroptosis. Collectively, our findings uncovered that Wed showed anti-ferroptosis effects in cardiomyocytes through downregulating the METTL3/BRD4 axis, thereby ameliorating MIRI.
