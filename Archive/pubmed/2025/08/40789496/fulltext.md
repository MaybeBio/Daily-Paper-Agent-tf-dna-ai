Superoxide dismutases (SODs) are essential antioxidant enzymes that catalyze the dismutation of superoxide radicals, playing a crucial role in cellular defense against oxidative stress. However, most natural SODs lack the thermostability required for industrial and therapeutic applications. In this study, we identified a manganese-dependent SOD (PsSOD) from the extremophilic bacterium Pseudogracilibacillus sp., which exhibited high native activity but limited stability under high-temperature conditions. To enhance its robustness, we employed a protein language model-guided strategy that integrated PSSM, ESM-1v, and SaProt to predict thermostability-enhancing mutations. Through four rounds of rational design and combinatorial mutagenesis, we developed an optimized variant (M4: T19H/L90M/Q122K/A183I), which retained approximately 70 % of its activity after 1 h at 90  degrees C-a 1.8-fold improvement over the wild type. Moreover, its catalytic activity reached 1567.6 U/mg, representing a 2.2-fold enhancement compared to the wild-type enzyme. Structural modeling and molecular dynamics simulations revealed that enhanced hydrophobic packing and reduced conformational flexibility contributed to the improved stability. Furthermore, M4 exhibited strong radical scavenging activity in vitro, activated the Nrf2/HO-1 antioxidant pathway, and suppressed pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, TNF-alpha, IL-1beta) in LPS-stimulated HaCaT and RAW264.7 cells. These results underscore the potential of AI-assisted protein engineering to develop robust antioxidant enzymes for biomedical, cosmetic, and industrial applications.
