# Integrative bioinformatics and deep learning to identify common genetic pathways in Crohn’s disease and ischemic cardiomyopathy


## Abstract
  Crohn’s disease (CD) and ischemic cardiomyopathy (ICM) share inflammatory characteristics, yet their common genetic underpinnings remain underexplored. Using an integrative bioinformatics approach, we analyzed GEO datasets (GSE3365 and GSE9128) to identify shared genetic pathways between CD and ICM. Through differential expression analysis, we identified 60 common differentially expressed genes (CDEGs). Functional enrichment analysis revealed enrichment in inflammatory pathways, including NF-κB and TNF-α signaling, highlighting their role in disease pathogenesis. We conducted microRNA (miRNA), transcription factor (TF), and protein–protein interaction (PPI) analyses to uncover regulatory networks. Notably, hsa-miR-98-5p emerged as a key miRNA, while RELA and NFKB1 were identified as prominent TFs interacting with CDEGs. Six hub genes—IL1B, CXCL8, CXCL2, TLR2, FCGR1A, and FCGR2A—were pinpointed, demonstrating high diagnostic potential via receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis. To advance diagnostic precision, we developed AutoClass, a deep learning framework that leverages hub gene regulatory networks to classify CD patients with approximately 95 % accuracy. These hub genes and regulators likely drive neutrophil-mediated inflammation, offering insights into the molecular interplay between CD and ICM. Our findings suggest that the identified CDEGs, miRNAs, and TFs hold promise as therapeutic targets and biomarkers, paving the way for precision medicine approaches in managing CD and its cardiovascular complications. Future experimental validation and cohort expansion could further elucidate these shared mechanisms, enhancing their translational impact.


## Introduction
  Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is divided into two groups: Ulcerative colitis and Crohn’s disease (CD). CD is a multisystem auto-inflammatory bowel disease, characterized by transmural inflammation of any part of the gastrointestinal tract1. CD is a destructive and progressive process, whose prevalence is increasing worldwide2. Genetic, immunological, and epidemiological data confirms that CD is a heterogeneous disease with a multifactorial etiology, where the environmental and genetic factors interact to instigate the disorder3. Despite major progress in the pathophysiology of IBDs, molecular mechanisms, early diagnosis, and effective therapy of IBD remains a challenge4. Some investigations have indicated that the risk of cardiovascular complications increases in chronic inflammatory diseases, particularly during relapses of the IBD. There is a potential relation between an increased risk of cardiovascular disorder and inflammatory processes related to Chronic IBD, but details of pathogenic pathways are unknown5. Ischemic cardiomyopathy (ICM), leading to morbidity and mortality, is described by insufficient blood pumping due to injured myocardium and coronary artery disorder6. A set of computational techniques in bioinformatics for clustering, evaluating, and validating clinical as well as molecular biomarkers can lead to recognizing the diagnostic markers and also treatment of various diseases7. Bioinformatics databases can be used to detect the biological pathways, metabolites, expressed genes, and proteins that could pave way for identifying candidate biomarkers of the disorder8. In the past decades, the expression profile analysis of microarray data was performed to find diagnostic candidate biomarkers and various key genes, containing differentially expressed genes (DEGs) for each given microarray dataset[9], [10]. Little research has been done on the connection between CD and ICM. In addition, MicroRNAs (miRNAs), the key genes, transcription factors (TFs) contributing to the pathogenesis and progression of CD in cases with ICM and have not been evaluated.

  Deep learning is a machine learning-originated computational method with many successful applications in research and industry. In order to specifically resolve biological problems of interest, deep learning automatically learns hidden features from the fairly raw representation of inputs11. The deep learning algorithms use sequential neural networks in several layers, each computing some mathematical functions on the previous layer’s output to finally get the final outcome. Basically, the neural network layers are sub-grouped into the input, hidden, and output layers. Each layer might contain one or several neurons (units) that are the core processors of the algorithm12. The input layer receives the input data with a determined dimension and features (the factors that the algorithm trains itself on them) and passes it to the rest of the network. The hidden layers have a crucial role in the mathematical computation of this input data. Generally, they will receive the input data multiplied by some randomly set weights and added bias. Then they will use the sum product of these values to compute a function called the activation function. Finally, the output layer receives the outcomes of hidden layers (multiplied by weights and added bias) and helps the decision-making in classification problems by applying a certain activation function. The output layer might have one or several neurons depending on the type of classification (Binary or Categorical). There are very different neural network architectures, each with several advantages and disadvantages developed for specific situations. Therefore, the high computational power and mathematical operations existing deep inside a neural network make it an appropriate method for TFBS prediction and finding the relations in complex TF regulation networks.

  Therefore, the gene expression microarray datasets GSE3365 and GSE9128 for CD and ICM, respectively were used from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database to predict common pathways in these disorders. Apart from exploring molecular mechanisms between Crohn’s disease and ischemic cardiomyopathy, miRNAs, hub genes, and TFs involved in both disorders were identified using bioinformatics. We designed a deep learning approach called AutoClass that can diagnose CD patients from the expression matrix of a set of selected regulatory network.


## Methodology

### Data acquisition
  The microarray datasets related to CD and ICM were explored in the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo; retrieved on 13 January 2023). The following criteria were followed to select datasets: 1. Samples of datasets had been collected from human volunteers. 2. The tissue source of samples was peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMC). 3. Datasets had the same microarray platform and GPL. Overall, two datasets were found; The first one (ID: GSE3365) for CD, consisting of 59 patients with CD and 42 healthy controls with GPL96 [HG-U133A] Affymetrix Human Genome U133A Array. The second one (ID: GSE9128) in the case of ICM comprised 4 patients with ICM and 3 healthy controls with GPL96 [HG-U133A] Affymetrix Human Genome U133A Array. These datasets were utilized for downstream analyses.


### Analysis of datasets and DEG screening
  A linear model for microarray data (LIMMA) package in the R software was performed to obtain differentially expressed genes (DEGs) for each given microarray dataset. The screening criteria for DEGs were |log2 (fold change) | > 0.58 and adjusted p-value <0.05. Significant and insignificant DEGs from both datasets were visually represented using a volcano plot. Then, the common DEGs (CDEGs) between CD and ICM datasets were screened using a Venn diagrams.


### Functional enrichment analysis of CDEGs
  Functional enrichment analysis encompasses gene ontology (GO) and the pathways enrichment analysis was analyzed using the Enrichr database (https://maayanlab.cloud/Enrichr; retrieved on 13 January 2023). The GO analysis contains biological process (BP), cellular component (CC), and molecular function (MF) terms. The pathways analysis was assessed using the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) and MSigDB Hallmark2020. An Adjusted P-value lower than 0.05 was considered statistically significant for these analyses.


### Identifying DEG-targeting MicroRNAs and DEG-linked metabolites
  Significant influence over gene expression in eukaryotic cells is wielded by microRNAs (miRNAs), making them pivotal players in reshaping the transcriptome and impacting gene levels in various diseases. MiRTarbase 2021 database (https://amp.pharm.mssm.edu), was used to identify potential interactions between CDEGs mRNAs and miRNAs. The significance level was set at an adjusted p-value below 0.05.

  Human metabolites can contribute to diseases by influencing gene expression. Investigating significant metabolites is important when studying transcriptome changes in diseases. To address this issue, the EnrichR database, linked to the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB), was utilized. This resource was utilized to identify the significant metabolites (adj.p.val <0.05) that were associated to CDEGs.


### Evaluation TFs and gene regulatory networks associated with CDEGs
  Cis and trans-regulatory elements, including trans-regulatory elements (TRE), impact gene expression regulation through transcription factors (TFs). The investigation employed the ChEA database (https://maayanlab.cloud/chea3/) and TRUST algorithm (https://www.grnpedia.org/trrust/) to identify transcription factors controlling gene expression. ChEA integrates data from ChIP-based methods, while TRUST is a comprehensive transcriptional regulatory network database (18). The cut-off criterion was considered adjusted p-value <0.05.

  The CDEGs underwent gene regulatory network analysis using eXpression2Kinases (X2K) at https://amp.pharm.mssm.edu/X2K/. X2K, an online bioinformatics tool, predicts relationships between upstream kinase pathways, essential transcription factors, and target genes, generating a diagram illustrating collaborative influences on gene expression levels.


### Construction of the PPI network and hub gene selection
  The protein–protein (PPI) interaction network was produced based on CDEGs via STRING. Subsequently, the PPI network was visualized with Cytoscape software (v3.8.0). CytoHubba plugin of Cytoscape was utilized to determine important genes in this network as hub genes. Five algorithms of CytoHubba, namely Degree, Closeness, Betweenness, MCC, and MNC were used to estimate the top 10 hub genes. Ultimately, the results of all algorithms were intersected to acquire the final hub genes.


### Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis of hub genes
  The diagnostic performance of hub genes was assessed through ROC analysis in the primary datasets (GSE3365 and GSE9128) utilizing GraphPad Prism software (v8.0.1). The area under the ROC curve (AUC) was calculated and used to compare the diagnostic value of these hub genes.


### Deep neural network (DNN) design and evaluation
  In this study, an autoencoder-classifier-based pipeline (AutoClass) was designed to first denoise, extract important features, dimensions, and simplify the expression data of the hub-gene network. Subsequently, CD and normal samples were classified in latent space. The human protein–protein interaction network was downloaded from the Stanford Large Network Dataset Collection (SNAP, https://snap.stanford.edu/biodata/datasets/10000/10000-PP-Pathways.html). The highlighted dataset contains a list of 21,557 nodes (genes) and in total 342,353 edges (Interactions). All protein interactions were first extracted to our hub genes. Next, self-interactions and interactions with proteins outside of our expression datasets were excluded. The expression data of remaining genes were used as input to AutoClass.

  The encoder, designed to process the input data, consists of a series of densely connected layers with rectified linear unit (ReLU) activation functions, progressively reducing the input’s dimensionality to a 6-dimensional latent representation. Conversely, the decoder takes this latent representation and reverses the process, expanding it back to the original input shape. The MLP takes 6-dimensional latent space as input, passes it through hidden layers with ReLU activations and regularization, and yields a binary output using a sigmoid activation function. To train the classifier and autoencoder simultaneously, A loss to the model was introduced with the following formula:(1)CombinedLoss=0.5∗MSE+0.5∗BCEwhere MSE and BCE correspond to Mean Squared Error and Binary Cross Entropy, respectively. MSE is used to optimize autoencoder gradient decent, while BCE is utilized for classifier. The similar weight of 0.5 for each ensures that both models are trained equally and simultaneously. The model was trained for 500 epochs with Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 0.005. L2 regularization with a coefficient of 0.001 was applied to the MLP’s first layer to prevent overfitting. Early stopping was employed with a patience of 10 epochs based on validation loss to avoid overfitting. Training was performed on an NVIDIA V100 GPU.

  To train AutoClass, only the data from CD and its corresponding normal samples were considered due to few samples in ICM and the resulting overfitting and bias. The training data was split into 70 % training set, 20 % evaluation set and 10 % test set. To evaluate the model, accuracy, F1-score, precision, and recall were utilized, and the confusion matrix was drawn using the Python programming language version 3.11.


### Dimensionality reduction with UMAP
  The UMAP algorithm was applied to reduce both the original 110-dimensional gene feature space and the six-dimensional embedding vector from the deep learning model to two dimensions for visualization. Key hyperparameters – including the number of nearest neighbors (10) and minimum distance between embedded points (0.1) – were configured using the UMAP Python library. This dual application enabled comparative analysis of the high-dimensional data structures at both input and intermediate model stages.


### Feature importance analysis via SHAP
  For interpretability, the SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) Python library was employed to quantify the contribution of individual gene features to the model’s classification predictions. The analysis retained default parameters to ensure consistency with established explainable AI (XAI) benchmarking practices.

  The ROC analysis was employed to estimate the hub genes' diagnostic performance. Besides, AUC was computed for comparing values of diagnostic performance. ### AutoClass accurately classifies CD patients from healthy ones using hub genes expression network
  The study evaluated the biomarker potential of hub genes, leading to the decision to enhance their classification ability. Primarily, the hub gene regulatory networks were constructed using human protein–protein interaction data in the SNAP database. ## Discussion
  IBD and ICM have been associated positively in numerous large population studies. However, the conclusions are still controversial[13], [14]. The molecular mechanisms underlying this phenomenon are not completely understood. The primary goal of our research is to use an integrated bioinformatics approach to describe the association between CD and ICM, to find diagnostic biomarkers or possible treatment candidates. A gene microarray expression profile was retrieved and examined to demonstrate the mechanism of pathogenesis of CD and ICM. In this study, 60 overlapping DEGs between the two comparison groups were found, including 19 downregulated genes and 41 upregulated genes.

  A thorough theoretical framework for gene expression that describes the interactions and activities of genes is known as the GO model15. The GO of common genes was investigated based on three divisions (biological, cellular, and molecular). The cell component relates to the cellular form in which the gene regulates its activity, whereas the molecular concept refers to molecular processes. Pathway analysis is a new approach for investigating and revealing how physiologically or molecularly complicated disorders are linked. The pathway is the best approach to produce an organism’s reactions generated by internal changes16. Numerous mechanisms relating to several diseases have been identified by gene ontology and pathway studies previously.

  In this study, the biological process indicates multiple immune response pathways linked with both diseases, including inflammatory response, neutrophil degranulation, neutrophil activation involved in immune response, and neutrophil-mediated immunity. The secretory granule membrane, ficolin-1-rich granule, and ficolin-1-rich granule membrane were all significantly enriched (ex. over-represented) in the cell component annotations. Neutrophil granules contain and release ficolin-1, and the majority of it associates with the cell’s surface membrane17. So, we infer that the hub genes may be crucial in immune functions and inflammation, particularly via influencing neutrophil activity during the development of ICM and CD. Neutrophils are the primary immune cells gathered to an inflammatory site and also play a critical role in the effective control of inflammation. If these procedures are not properly controlled, they may start positive feedback amplification loops that boost neutrophil activation, causing significant tissue damage and the development of chronic disorders18. Moreover, by releasing neutrophil extracellular traps (NET), neutrophils may contribute to an elevated risk of thrombosis in IBD. Though not verified, neutrophils could play an important role in both gastro-intestinal bleeding and the pro-thrombotic phenotype in IBD patients19. Additionally, by releasing NETs, neutrophils can contribute to pathologic venous and arterial thrombosis20.

  DEGs were primarily enriched in pathways such as TNF-α signaling via NF-kB, inflammatory response, IL-6/JAK/STAT3 signaling, Leishmaniasis, and Staphylococcus aureus infection, according to KEGG and MSigDB pathway enrichment analysis. These pathways are most likely involved in the development of both diseases at the same time. For instance, the multifunctional transcription factor NF-κB has several immune system functions that have been preserved throughout evolution21. Through the activation of the NF-κB pathway, cytokines from the TNF family primarily promote the rapid transcription of genes controlling inflammation, cell survival, proliferation, and differentiation22. The studies have shown that prolonged NF-κB activation causes endoplasmic reticulum stress responses and cell death by evoking signals that start chronic inflammation and increase the production of cytokines such as TNF-α, IL-1, and IL-6 in ischemic myocardial injury23. Also, In CD, NF-κB activation may be crucial for the start of the relapsing inflammatory process and may also be the target of the therapeutic action of a variety of anti-inflammatory medications, including corticosteroids and aminosalicylates24. The pathophysiology of CD and ICM may be significantly influenced by NF-κB activation, but this is not likely to be their main cause.

  Additionally, TNF-α also stimulates IL-6 synthesis via NF-κB phosphorylation and JAK-mediated phosphorylation of STAT3, in addition to p38 MAP kinase and SAPK/JNK25. During myocardial ischemia–reperfusion, cardiomyocytes release IL-6, which causes neutrophils and cardiomyocytes to display CD11b/CD18 and intercellular adhesion molecule-1 (ICAM-1), resulting in myocardium injury26. Additionally, IL-6 has been demonstrated to be crucial in the etiology and physiopathology of CD27. It is significant to note that new research indicates that blocking IL-6 signaling is useful in treating experimental models of autoimmune and chronic inflammatory disorders. As a result, anti-IL-6/anti-IL-6R therapies show promise as innovative treatments for inflammatory disorders[28], [29].

  Based on the highest level of connectivity in the present research, a total of 6 hub genes that are common in both CD and ICM have been found. These include the IL1b, CXCL8, CXCL2, TLR2, FCGR1A, and FCGR2A genes. The development of ICM in CD patients may be significantly influenced by these genes. These hub genes all displayed the most remarkable association with the inflammatory process.

  Interleukin-1 (IL-1) and CXCL8 (also known as interleukin-8 (IL-8)) are cytokines concerned with the immediate innate immune response30. IL-1β levels are significantly elevated during CD, and there is a positive correlation between the severity of mucosal inflammation and IL-1β levels31. IL-1β also influences the inflammatory response during an acute myocardial infarction (AMI) and the progression to ischemic cardiomyopathy32. The first cells to be recruited to an infection or disease region are neutrophils. CXCL8, CXCL2, and CXCL1 are the three most important inflammatory mediators for such recruitment33. A member of the CXC chemokine family, CXCL8, is an essential neutrophil activator and chemoattractant and has been linked to the pathophysiology of a number of inflammatory disorders, including IBD[30], [34], [35]. By interactions with CXCR2 on neutrophils, CXCL8 accelerates the development of atherosclerosis by causing the formation of NET36.

  TLRs, particularly TLR2, have emerged as significant regulators of autoimmune-associated inflammation, such as CD, among the several molecules known to have involvement in the beginning and development of autoimmune diseases[37], [38]). Based on a study, activating the TLR signaling system with LTA (TLR2 ligand) or LPS boosted the development of NF-κB p65 nuclear translocation and dramatically elevated IL-8 production,39. Moreover, TLRs are strongly associated with a variety of cardiovascular disorders36. TLR2 and TLR4 play the most significant roles in ICM, where they can activate NF-κB and inflammatory responses, control cardiomyocyte death, and change cardiac function4. Hence, it is possible to speculate that TLR2 plays an important role in the etiology of CD and ICM. Overall, TLR2 could potentially serve as a molecular biomarker in the pathogenesis, diagnosis, and treatment of ICM in CD cases. These hub genes were determined as common hub genes for CD and ICM. It is likely that the absorption of neutrophils and other immune cells is a common mechanism in the pathogenesis of CD and ICM, as well as the development of ICM in CD.

  In our research, we identified that hsa-miR-98-5p had the maximum interactions with the hub genes among the miRNAs and can be a diagnostic biomarker for the prediction of ICM in CD. Hsa-miR-98-5p, a member of the let-7/miR-98-5p family, is found on the X chromosome40. MiR-98-5p has been identified as a stress-related miRNA that is crucial for oxidative stress, cell survival, and apoptosis in a variety of cell types and disorders41. The findings show that miR98, through targeting STAT3 and PGC-1, enhances oxidative stress, inflammation, and apoptosis in the situation of H/R in vitro42. Furthermore, miR-98-5p knockdown shifted macrophage polarization to the M2 phenotype by elevating tribbles homolog 1 (Trib1) expression, relieving IBD symptoms43. Consequently, miR-192-5p might have a critical function in the development of both disorders and can be a diagnostic biomarker for prediction of developing ICM in CD patients.

  The transcription factors RELA and NF-κB were identified to regulate most of the hub genes in our survey. NF-κB is a member of a family of inducible TFs that regulates an extensive range of genes involved in immunological and inflammatory responses18. RELA, also known as nuclear factor NF-B p65 subunit, is a protein encoded by the RELA gene in humans44, the importance of which has already been noted.

  By evaluating all of the findings, we realize the importance of the role of neutrophils in these two diseases and probably their association. While neutrophils have been evaluated in several animal models of IBD and ICM for many years, no neutrophil-targeting drugs have been employed and verified for the treatment of these disorders19. As a result, it is critical to increase our knowledge of their mode of action in these specific situations to discover new therapy options for CD and ICM in CD patients.

  Furthermore, in our current study, we introduce our neural network framework named AutoClass, aiming to evaluate its capacity to extract pivotal features from our hub gene regulatory network and employ them for patient classification. To accomplish this, we have devised an autoencoder, which serves to compress and simplify the expression matrix into lower-dimensional data while learning the reconstruction process. The advantage of employing this algorithm is our ability to work with a more streamlined dataset, focusing on the most critical features[45], [46], [47]. Subsequently, we leverage this compressed data in the latent space for the classification of CD (Crohn’s Disease) patients. Our choice to exclusively employ the GSE3365 dataset is deliberate, as the other dataset under consideration featured a limited number of samples, which could potentially lead to overfitting and unreliable results. To address this, we have optimized the autoencoder to encode data based on essential classification features by training both the classifier and autoencoder simultaneously using a combined loss function. This approach enriches the latent space with the most valuable information from the dataset, exemplified by the model’s exceptional performance. It is worth noting that the application of deep learning in medical science, particularly for the detection of CD, has been explored in prior research. However, most studies have primarily utilized imaging data[48], [49], [50], [51], [52]. Our study stands out as one of the pioneering efforts to employ expression data for CD diagnosis, expanding the horizons of deep learning’s application in the medical field.

  The identification of hub genes such as IL1B, CXCL8, CXCL2, TLR2, FCGR1A, and FCGR2A, along with their associated regulatory networks, holds significant potential for clinical applications. The identification of hub genes and their regulatory networks, combined with the high diagnostic accuracy of AutoClass, suggests their potential as biomarkers for personalized diagnostics and targeted therapies. These findings pave the way for precision medicine approaches, enabling early detection and tailored management of CD and its cardiovascular complications like ICM.

  These genes, deeply involved in inflammatory processes and neutrophil activity, could serve as novel therapeutic targets for both Crohn’s disease (CD) and ischemic cardiomyopathy (ICM). For instance, targeted inhibition of IL1B or TLR2 signaling pathways, which are critical in perpetuating chronic inflammation, may offer a dual benefit in managing CD and reducing the risk of cardiovascular complications in these patients. Moreover, the high accuracy of our AutoClass deep learning framework in classifying CD patients based on hub gene expression profiles suggests its potential as a diagnostic tool. With further validation, AutoClass could be integrated into clinical workflows to enable early detection of CD or to stratify patients at higher risk of developing ICM, thereby facilitating personalized treatment strategies. These findings underscore the translational relevance of our study, bridging bioinformatics insights with practical clinical advancements.

  This study had shortcomings. no laboratory tests were performed to confirm the main pathways and hub genes revealed in the current study. In spite of this, the credibility of this analysis is enhanced by the fact that the functions of some hub genes, including IL-1b and CXCL8, have been clarified in several preclinical and clinical trials of CD and ICM. Other genes that could potentially play roles in both disorders were missed in our study since they were not detectable by available bioinformatics assays. In the context of disease analysis, risks include potential misclassifications that could lead to incorrect diagnoses or treatment recommendations. Overfitting to the training data, especially if the dataset is not representative of diverse populations, could reduce generalizability. To mitigate these risks, we employed a robust validation strategy with 20 % of the data for validation and 10 % for testing, incorporated regularization (L2 penalty in the MLP), and propose integrating explainability tools to further enhance trust and reliability in clinical settings. Although the model could achieve the accuracy of 100 % on the test dataset and 95 % on the validation dataset, the low number of test cases (10) does not guarantee the excellent performance of the model. Thus, including more data for training and evaluation in future studies are required. In addition, we suggest involving a long-term reinforcement learning agent to be implemented in clinical environments to improve model performance in real clinical applications.


## Conclusions
  In conclusion, our bioinformatics analysis revealed IL1B, CXCL8, CXCL2, TLR2, FCGR1A, and FCGR2A as common hub genes implicated in the pathogenesis of Crohn’s disease (CD) and ischemic cardiomyopathy (ICM). These genes likely contribute to shared inflammatory mechanisms, particularly through neutrophil-mediated pathways, offering insights into the molecular interplay between these disorders. The AutoClass deep learning framework demonstrated remarkable accuracy in classifying CD patients, highlighting its potential as a diagnostic tool. By elucidating shared genetic pathways and developing AutoClass for accurate classification, our study lays the groundwork for precision medicine strategies, where hub genes and regulatory molecules could guide personalized treatment plans for CD and ICM. While our findings provide a robust foundation, future studies should focus on experimental validation of these hub genes and pathways using in vitro and in vivo models to confirm their roles. Additionally, expanding this analysis to larger cohorts, including diverse populations and other chronic inflammatory diseases, could further elucidate shared genetic mechanisms and enhance the generalizability of our results. These efforts may pave the way for novel therapeutic strategies targeting inflammation to mitigate the progression of both CD and ICM, ultimately improving patient outcomes.
