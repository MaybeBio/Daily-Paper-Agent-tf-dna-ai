Fatigue has emerged as a significant global health concern. Based on World Health Organization statistics, approximately 488 million people worldwide face risks associated with excessive fatigue. Excessive fatigue is recognized as a major risk factor for stroke and ischemic heart disease. In China, approximately 600,000 deaths are annually attributed to excessive fatigue. Ancient texts document the anti-fatigue properties of Jiu Zhi Huang Qi (JZHQ), although the precise underlying mechanisms remain unclear. This study aimed to validate the anti-fatigue efficacy of JZHQ and elucidate the underlying mechanisms of action, illuminating a novel therapeutic strategy against fatigue. Utilizing UHPLC-QE-MS and bioinformatics analysis, we identified 25 putative anti-fatigue target genes and 44 bioactive components of JZHQ. Enrichment analysis revealed that JZHQ exerts anti-fatigue effects by modulating energy metabolism, regulating protein phosphorylation, and affecting antioxidant pathways. In vivo experiments and subsequent ELISA and histopathological analysis indicated that JZHQ enhances antioxidant capacity and mitigates tissue damage. RT-PCR and Western blotting also confirmed that JZHQ activates the AMPK/PGC-1alpha signaling pathway, promotes AMPK phosphorylation and enhances energy metabolism. Molecular docking indicated favorable binding affinity between key JZHQ components and targets within the AMPK/PGC-1alpha pathway. Overall, JZHQ was shown to upregulate antioxidant enzymes and glycogen, eliminate harmful metabolites, such as malondialdehyde, activate the AMPK/PGC-1alpha pathway, enhance AMPK phosphorylation, and upregulate the expression of PGC-1alpha, thereby enhancing energy metabolism and protecting against fatigue.
