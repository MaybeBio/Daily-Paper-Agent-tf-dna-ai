They report 26 studies published between 2021 and April 2024, and claim that tokenization and transformer models enhance genomic data processing and regulatory annotation prediction, while noting challenges in accessibility and interpretability.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no methods details, search strategy, inclusion criteria, data extraction forms, or synthesis results beyond the abstract
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full text, PRISMA flow diagram, search strings, study characteristics table, risk of bias or quality assessment, detailed results, and discussion of limitations

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a timely and potentially useful scoping review topic at the intersection of NLP and genomics. However, the abstract provides insufficient methodological detail to evaluate the rigor of the review process, and the reported results are too general to support the stated conclusions. The claim that tokenization and transformer models "enhance" processing and understanding is not substantiated by specific evidence in the abstract. The review's contribution to the field cannot be adequately assessed from the supplied material.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in bioinformatics, computational genomics, and NLP applied to biology would be interested in a systematic mapping of current NLP methods for genomic data. Clinicians and translational researchers interested in personalized medicine applications may also find value, though the abstract does not yet demonstrate how the review informs practical applications.
- **Major strengths** The topic is relevant and timely given the rapid adoption of LLMs in biomedical domains. The stated use of PRISMA guidelines and multiple databases suggests an intention toward systematic methodology. The focus on tokenization, transformer models, and regulatory annotation prediction provides a clear thematic structure.
- **Major Concerns**  
  - R1-M1: The abstract does not report key methodological elements of a scoping review, including search dates, inclusion and exclusion criteria, screening process, and data extraction approach. Without these, the reproducibility and rigor of the review cannot be evaluated.  
  - R1-M2: The results section reports only the number of studies and a general thematic finding. No information is provided on the characteristics of included studies, the range of NLP methods surveyed, or the specific outcomes reported. The claim that these methods "enhance" processing is an evaluative statement not supported by data in the abstract.  
  - R1-M3: The discussion and conclusion claim that NLP and LLMs "can help streamline" processing and "have the potential to drive advancements in personalized medicine," but no evidence from the reviewed studies is presented to support these forward-looking claims. The abstract does not distinguish between demonstrated capabilities and speculative potential.
- **Minor Comments**  
  - R1-m1: The abstract states the review was conducted "without restrictions on publication date or article type," yet the results report studies published only between 2021 and April 2024. This apparent inconsistency should be clarified.  
  - R1-m2: The objectives mention "data and model accessibility" as a goal, but the results and discussion do not report findings related to accessibility. This gap should be addressed.  
  - R1-m3: The conclusion mentions "challenges remain in accessibility and interpretability," but the abstract does not describe what these challenges are or how they were identified from the reviewed literature.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1, R1-M2, R1-M3
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**  
  - Originality: The topic is not novel in itself, as other reviews of deep learning in genomics exist, but a focused scoping review on NLP-specific methods may offer a distinct angle. Originality cannot be fully assessed from the abstract.  
  - Scientific importance: The intersection of NLP and genomics is of growing importance, and a rigorous synthesis would be valuable. However, the abstract does not yet demonstrate the scientific contribution.  
  - Interdisciplinary readership: The topic bridges computational methods and biomedical applications, which could attract a broad readership. The abstract is written in accessible language for both communities.  
  - Technical soundness: Not assessable from the abstract due to missing methodological detail.  
  - Readability for nonspecialists: The abstract is clearly written and avoids excessive jargon, making it accessible to a general biomedical audience.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that tokenization and transformer models "enhance the processing and understanding of genomic data" is unsupported by specific evidence in the abstract.
- The claim that NLP and LLMs "can help streamline the processing of large-scale genomic data" is presented as a finding but is not tied to any reported result.
- The claim that the field "has the potential to drive advancements in personalized medicine" is speculative and not grounded in the abstract's reported findings.
- The statement that "challenges remain in accessibility and interpretability" is asserted without supporting detail from the reviewed studies.
