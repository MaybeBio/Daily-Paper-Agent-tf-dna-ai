# Identification of biomarkers associated with phagocytosis regulatory factors in coronary artery disease using machine learning and network analysis


## Background
  Coronary artery disease (CAD) is the leading cause of death worldwide, and aberrant phagocytosis may be involved in its development. Understanding this aspect may provide new avenues for prompt CAD diagnosis.


## Methods
  CAD-related information was obtained from Gene Expression Omnibus datasets GSE66360, GSE113079, and GSE59421. We identified 995 upregulated and 1086 downregulated differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in GSE66360. Weighted gene co-expression network analysis revealed a module of 503 genes relevant to CAD. Using clusterProfiler, we revealed 32 CAD-related PRFs. Eight candidate genes were identified in a protein-protein interaction network. Machine learning algorithms identified CAD biomarkers that underwent gene set enrichment analysis, immune cell analysis with CIBERSORT, microRNA (miRNA) prediction using the miRWalk database, transcription factor (TF) level predication through ChEA3, and drug prediction with DGIdb. Cytoscape visualized the miRNA -mRNA- TF, miRNA-single nucleotide polymorphism-mRNA, and biomarker-drug networks.


## Results
  IL1B, TLR2, FCGR2A, SYK, FCER1G, and HCK were identified as CAD biomarkers. The area under the curve of a diagnostic model based on the six biomarkers was > 0.7 for the GSE66360 and GSE113079 datasets. Gene set enrichment analysis revealed differences in their biological pathways. CIBERSORT revealed that 10 immune cell types were differentially expressed between the CAD and control groups. The TF-mRNA-miRNA network showed that has-miR-1207-5p regulates HCK and FCER1G expression and that RUNX1 and SPI may be important TFs. Ninety-five drugs were predicted, including aspirin, which influenced ILIB and FCERIG.


## Conclusion
  In this study, six biomarkers (IL1B, TLR2, FCGR2A, SYK, FCER1G, and HCK) related to CAD phagocytic regulatory factors were identified, and their expression regulatory relationships in CAD were further studied, providing a deeper understanding of the pathogenesis, diagnosis, and potential treatment strategies of CAD.

## Background
  Coronary artery disease (CAD) is one of the leading causes of death worldwide, posing a severe risk to human health (Brown et al. 2023). CAD is characterized by the formation of arterial plaques composed mainly of lipids, calcium, and inflammatory cells (Li et al. 2018). CAD is caused by partial or complete vascular blockage due to coronary atherosclerosis, resulting in a sudden reduction or interruption of the myocardial blood supply, leading to myocardial ischemic necrosis. Early diagnosis and treatment can significantly improve the prognosis of patients with CAD (Meng et al. 2022). Early diagnosis is supported by analysis of serum biomarkers, such as natriuretic peptides, soluble suppression of tumorigenicity 2 (sST2), growth differentiation factor-15 (GDF-15), high-sensitivity cardiac troponins (hs-cTn), C-reactive protein (CRP), interleukin 6 (IL-6), and myeloperoxidase with recent interest in microRNAs (Zhelankin et al. 2021).

  Phagocytosis is a cellular protection mechanism that involves the clearance of particles larger than 0.5 μm in diameter (Cockram et al. 2021). Performed by many types of cells, such as “specialized phagocytes”, including macrophages, and “non-professional phagocytes”, including fibroblasts; phagocytosis is a necessary process for tissue homeostasis (Kelley et al. 2021; Uribe-Querol et al. 2020). However, aberrant phagocytosis can lead to immune system dysfunction, abnormal protein aggregation, and developmental abnormalities (Xing et al. 2022). Macrophages are involved in atherosclerosis during CAD (Cichoń et al. 2017) and initiate and regulate the inflammatory response during myocardial infarction (Kubota et al. 2022). Three days after myocardial infarction, macrophages exhibit enhanced proliferation and phagocytosis and enhanced expression of genes related to mitochondrial function and oxidative phosphorylation (Mouton et al. 2018). Indicating that phagocytosis plays an important role in this process.

  Specialized phagocytes, such as macrophages, express several phagocytic receptors that sense target particles and activate signaling pathways, resulting in phagocytosis (Uribe-Querol and Rosales 2020). Therefore, a wide range of different proteins are involved in phagocytosis and can be considered phagocytosis regulatory factors (Rosales et al. 2017). CD47 is a glycoprotein expressed on the surface of cell membranes that has been found to prevent cancer cells from being phagocytosed (van Duijn et al. 2022). CD47 expression in plaque-associated phagocytes increases when atherosclerosis occurs, and cardiomyocytes associated with infarcted tissue also express more CD47 when myocardial infarction occurs (Kelley and Ravichandran 2021). In animal models, it has been shown that blocking CD47 by antibodies can increase phagocytosis, and blocking the CD47 axis can prevent atherosclerosis (Mueller et al. 2022). This may indicate a future direction for the prevention and treatment of CAD. However, the exact mechanism involved remains unclear. Most studies on phagocytosis regulators have focused on tumors, but few have investigated their role in CAD. Therefore, exploring phagocytosis regulators in CAD may identify new biomarkers and potential mechanisms that can help us understand the pathogenesis and molecular mechanisms of this disease and provide new targets for determining the prognosis and treatment.

  In this study, we first investigated CAD datasets from a public database for differentially expressed phagocytosis regulators. We then analyzed the functional pathways these differentially expressed phagocytosis regulators were involved with using bioinformatics and used machine learning to explore the regulatory mechanisms and the immune cell landscape to identify biomarkers and potential targets for the treatment of CAD.


## Materials and methods

### Patient and public involvement
  No patient involved.


#### Data Acquisition
  An overview of the study protocol is shown in Supplementary Fig. 1. Three independent datasets (GSE66360, GSE113079, and GSE59421) were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). Information on whole blood samples from 49 CAD patients and 50 healthy controls (HC), all of whom were male, was included in the GSE66360 dataset and used in the primary study. The GSE113079 dataset contained information on whole blood samples from 93 CAD patients and 48 HC, which was used to validate biomarkers and their expression. GSE59421 contains the information on 99 blood samples (CAD patients = 33 and HC = 63) that were selected to screen for differentially expressed miRNAs. Four hundred and ninety phagocytosis regulatory factors (PRFs) were identified from previous studies (Feng et al. 2022; Xing et al. 2022). The “limma” (Ritchie et al. 2015) R package was used to identify the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between the CAD and HC groups. Genes were considered differentially expressed when they met the following criteria|log2fold change (FC)|>0.5 and adjusted p.<0.05. The “ggplot2” R package was used to plot volcano maps and heatmaps to visualize DEGs.


#### Identification of CAD-related PRFs
  Gene modules with similar expression patterns were identified using weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) (Langfelder et al. 2008), and the relationships between the modules and particular traits were examined. The R package “WGCNA” was used to create a gene co-expression network for CAD using the GSE66360 dataset. In the analysis, the threshold was chosen based on the criterion that the constructed network should satisfy the scale-free topology assumption. Specifically, the following procedure was used. (1) Soft thresholding power selection. An analysis was performed to determine the soft-thresholding power (β) that ensures a scale-free topology by plotting scale independence and mean connectivity at different soft-thresholding powers. This analysis is a critical step in weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) to ensure that the constructed network reflects the underlying biological processes. (2) Scale-free topology criterion (R²). The power value where the scale-free topology index (R²) reaches 0.9 or higher was selected. This value indicates that the network exhibits a scale-free topology, meaning that the connectivity distribution of nodes (genes) follows a power-law distribution. The minimum number of genes per module was set to 30. The link between the clinical traits and module attributes was examined using Pearson’s correlation analysis (module eigengenes [MEs]). The module with the highest correlation was identified as the one most closely related to CAD, and the PRF DEGs related to CAD were analyzed using the genes in that module (correlation coefficient 0.65, 500 genes in total). The “VennDiagram” R package (Chen et al. 2011) was applied to visualize the differential PRFs associated with CAD.


#### Functional analysis and protein-protein interaction (PPI) network of CAD associated PRFs
  The “clusterProfiler” R package (Yu et al. 2012) was used to perform functional analysis. Gene Ontology (GO) function enrichment was carried out on the PRF DEGs related to CAD, with a threshold of p.adjust < 0.05, and the correlation was judged to be random and significant according to the P value. This identified 1351 enriched functions. Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis was also performed with the top 20 signaling pathways shown. The results were visualized using the R package “ggplot2”.

  The STRING database (http://string-db.org) was used to establish the PPI network structure. The topological properties of the PPI network were identified using the cytoHubba plugin for Cytoscape (Shannon et al. 2003). At a threshold > 0.4, 29 of the 32 genes were retained. The top 10 genes were selected to intersect through three algorithms (Degree, MCC, and Stress), and 8 biomarkers were obtained.


#### Machine learning to identify CAD biomarkers
  In the GSE66360 dataset, the two algorithms were used to screen the 32 PRF genes associated with CAD that were identified above through the R package “glmnet” (Friedman et al. 2010) for least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) and support vector machine recursive feature elimination (SVM-RFE) and to confirm the expression and diagnostic utility of biomarkers in GSE66360 and GSE113079. Cross-validation was used to optimize the models and prevent overfitting during feature selection. The glmnet R package was used to perform LASSO regression. The regularization parameter (λ) was selected using 10-fold cross-validation, which is standard in such analyses. The optimal λ was chosen based on the minimum mean cross-validated error, ensuring that the model generalizes well to unseen data. For SVM-RFE, the e1071 R package was employed to perform recursive feature elimination to select the most relevant features. The performance of the model was evaluated across different subsets, and the subset with the highest classification accuracy was selected as the final feature set. The Wilcoxon test was used to assess whether the expression trends of the biomarkers were consistent between the two datasets. The “lrm” function was applied to build diagnostic models of biomarkers.


#### Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) and correlation analysis of immune cells and biomarkers
  All genes in the GSE66360 dataset were analyzed for correlations with biomarkers. Based on a p-value < 0.05,|normalized enrichment score [NES]|>1, the functional pathways of the biomarkers were analyzed using GSEA.

  The CIBERSORT package was used to analyze the abundance of immune cells in all samples in the GSE66360 dataset. Principal component analysis (PCA) was performed on the immune cell infiltration matrix data using the “ggplot2” R package. “corrplot” was applied to visualize the correlation among the immune-infiltrating cells. The Wilcoxon test was used to examine variations in immune cells between CAD patients and HC. Spearman’s correlation coefficient was used to determine the correlation between biomarkers and significantly different immune cells.


#### Construction of transcription factor (TF)-mRNA-microRNA(miRNA) and miRNA-single-nucleotide polymorphism (SNP)-mRNA regulatory network
  The differential expression of miRNAs in the GSE9421 dataset was examined using the “limma” R package. Upstream miRNAs of CAD biomarkers were predicted using miRWalk (https://mpd.bioinf.uni-sb.de/overview.html), and target miRNAs were selected based on the intersections of the differentially expressed and predicted miRNAs. ChEA3 predicted TFs associated with regulatory biomarkers. Target miRNA-SNPs were predicted using a miRNA SNP database (http://bioinfo.life.hust.edu.cn/miRNASNP/#!/). Cytoscape software was used to visualize the miRNA-mRNA-TF and miRNA-SNP-mRNA regulatory networks.


#### Drug prediction analysis
  Potential drugs associated with biomarkers in the CAD were retrieved from the Drug-Gene Interaction Database (DGIdb [https://dgidb.org/]). Cytoscape and the “ggalluvial” R package (Qian et al. 2022) were used to visualize the “drug-biomarker” network.


## Results

### Identification of CAD-related PRFs
  A total of 2081 DEGs (upregulated = 995 DEGs and downregulated = 1086 DEGs) were screened in the CAD and HC samples of the GSE66360 dataset. (Figure 1a and b). According to the nearly scale-free topology criterion, the soft threshold was selected. Since 9 was the first number that met the standard, 9 was taken as the soft threshold (Supplementary Fig. S2). Using CAD as the trait, we identified 10 modules using the GSE66360 dataset through WGCNA (Fig. 1c). A heatmap of the module-trait relationships was obtained using Spearman’s analysis (Fig. 1d). The dark slate-blue module, which contained 500 genes, showed the strongest correlation with CAD (Fig. 1e). The Venn diagram shows 32 CAD-related PRFs (Fig. 1f).

### Functional analysis and PPI network of 32 CAD-related PRFs
  These 32 CAD-related PRFs were enriched in 1351 GO entries and 113 KEGG pathways. According to the biological process analysis, these genes were associated with myeloid leukocyte activation and regulation of phagocytosis in CAD. In the cellular component analysis, these genes were engaged in the secretory granule membrane and ficolin-1-rich granule membrane. In the molecular function analysis, these genes were involved in Toll-like receptor binding and non-membrane-spanning protein tyrosine kinase activity (Fig. 2a). These genes were significantly involved in osteoclast differentiation, C-type lectin receptor signaling pathway, and neutrophil extracellular trap (NET) formation (Fig. 2b). The final PPI network of 32 CAD-related PRF was obtained (Fig. 2c). After selection with a PPI threshold of > 0.4, 29 of the 32 genes were retained. The top 10 genes were then selected for intersection by degree, MCC, and stress algorithms. This identified eight candidate genes that were then used for subsequent analyses (Fig. 2d). Therefore, this analysis identified eight potential biomarkers for CAD.

The top 10 results were visualized with the “bar-plot” function in the enrich-plot package. The x-axis and y-axis represent the gene ratio and GO terms, respectively, and the color represents the corrected p-value. b Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment significant items for DEGs. c PPI network of CAD-related differential PRFs (score > 0.4, 32 genes retained 29 genes with correlation scores > 0.4), the darker the color, the higher the connectivity. d Venn diagram of eight candidate biomarkers


### Identification of the biomarkers
  In the GSE66360 dataset, eight genes (IL1B, TLR2, TYROBP, FCGR2A, SYK, SPI1, FCER1G, and HCK) were identified using LASSO regression analysis based on 32 CAD-related PRFs (Fig. 3a). Six genes (IL1B, HCK, FCER1G, FCGR2A, SYK, and TLR2) were also identified using SVM-RFE (Fig. 3b). These six biomarkers were significantly differentially expressed in the normal group and the CAD group and were common to both the machine learning algorithms (Fig. 3c, Supplementary Fig. 3). The subsequent diagnostic power of the model consisting of a combination of these six biomarkers was extreme (area under the curve [AUC] > 0.7) in both the GSE66360 (AUC = 0.973, CI95%=0.947–0.998) and GSE113079 (AUC = 0.797, CI95%=0.717–0.876) datasets (Fig. 3d and e).

  Fig. 3Identification of the biomarkers. a Featured genes screened using the LASSO regression analysis. b Graph of generalization error and the number of features. The red text in the figure depicts the minimum error value. c Venn diagram of six biomarkers. d, e Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve validation. The areas under the curve (AUC) were all greater than 0.7, indicating good model efficacy


### GSEA of biomarkers and correlation with Immune Cell Infiltration
  The GSEA results for each biomarker are shown in Supplementary Fig. 4. For example, FCGR2A was mainly enriched in the GO entries for neutrophil activation and in KEGG entries for the IL-17 signaling. In the GO terms, these biomarkers were mainly enriched for neutrophil activation, migration, chemotaxis, and phagocytosis. NET formation, the Toll-like receptor signaling pathway, and other pathways were in common with the enhanced KEGG biomarkers.

  PCA results showed differences in immune-infiltrating cells between the CAD and HC groups (Fig. 4a). The correlations between the 22 immune cells are shown in Fig. 4b. Ten immune cell types differed in the GSE66360 dataset. Activated dendritic cells, eosinophils, activated mast cells, monocytes, neutrophils, activated NK cells, resting NK cells, and follicular helper T cells were all increased in CAD compared to HC. Resting CD4 memory T cells and gamma delta T cells were decreased in CAD compared to HC (Fig. 4c). As shown in Fig. 4d, correlation analysis between the six biomarkers and the ten immune cell types showed that IL1B was strongly and positively correlated with mast cell activation.

### Construction of TF-mRNA-miRNA, miRNA-SNP-mRNA, and biomarker-drug regulatory networks
  A total of 3765 miRNAs were predicted as biomarkers using the miRWalk database (Supplementary Table 1). Seventy miRNAs were differentially expressed in the GSE59421 dataset. This intersection yielded seven differentially expressed miRNAs. (Fig. 5a). Fifty-one TFs related to regulatory biomarkers were predicted using ChEA3 (Supplementary Table 2). The TF-mRNA-miRNA network showed that hsa-miR-1207-5p regulates HCK and FCER1G and that RUNX1 and SPI may be important TFs (Fig. 5b). The SNP binding sites corresponding to miRNAs of 6 biomarkers were predicted by miRNA SNP database. As HCK and TLR2 did not have SNP binding sites corresponding to miRNAs, they were excluded. Thus, fourteen SNPs from seven differentially expressed miRNAs were predicted using the miRNA SNP database (Supplementary Table 3). We constructed a network of 4 biomarkers, 5 miRNAs, and 14 SNPs (Fig. 5c), finding that hsa-miR-486-3p regulated rs907156000 to affect IL1B expression. Finally, 95 drugs were predicted based on six biomarkers using the DGIdb (Fig. 5d). Aspirin influenced both IL1B and FCER1G and was the only drug that could affect FCER1G.

## Discussion
  The development of CAD involves atherosclerosis, eventually causing vessel blockage, decreasing myocardial blood supply, and leading to myocardial ischemic necrosis. As a major cause of mortality throughout the world, early detection and treatment of CAD is critical to prevent adverse outcomes. Much is currently known about CAD development. For example, platelet receptor CLEC-2 plays a role in arterial thrombosis and thrombosis inflammation, so it can be speculated that the occurrence of CAD is related to CLEC-2 (Harbi et al. 2021). It is also known that CD47 increases in the atherosclerotic area, mainly located in the core of plaque necrosis. The residual apoptotic bodies in atherosclerotic plaques treated with anti-CD47 antibodies were significantly reduced. This suggests that anti-CD47 antibodies may be a new therapeutic strategy for the treatment of CAD by promoting efferocytosis (Ryan 2016). Another area of interest is the heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1) degradation pathway, which plays a protective role in the progression of atherosclerosis. Patients with high HO-1 levels are more likely to exhibit thin-cap fibro atherosclerosis, plaque rupture, and thrombosis. The level of HO-1 in patients with acute myocardial infarction is higher than that in the control group (Kishimoto et al. 2019). However, there is an urgent need for more understanding of CAD development through the many pathways involved to identify alternative biomarkers that might help with early diagnosis of CAD and may even suggest novel treatment approaches. In recent years, several newly discovered CAD-related factors have been identified. In this study, we speculated that PRFs might be important in CAD because aberrant phagocytosis appears to be important in atherosclerosis development. Therefore, we undertook a bioinformatic analysis of DEGs in CAD to identify 32 CAD-related PRFs. Functional analysis and PPI network construction of these 32 genes suggested eight potential biomarkers. Machine learning algorithms then identified IL1B, TLR2, FCGR2A, SYK, FCER1G, and HCK as CAD-related PRFs. Therefore, these genes may play a key role in CAD etiopathogenesis.

  Phagocytosis has an important role in the development of CAD(Cichoń et al. 2017; Kubota and Frangogiannis 2022). Fc gamma R-mediated phagocytosis has been identified in previous analyses of CAD databases as being a potential pathogenic mechanism(Li et al. 2014; Yan 2018). However, very little is known about the role of PRFs in CAD. Understanding their differential expression might identify potential phagocytosis pathways that might assist with early diagnosis of CAD. A total of 32 CAD-related phagocytic regulators were identified in this study, and 1351 GO and 113 KEGG pathway entries were obtained. The analysis of the biological processes demonstrates that these genes are associated with myeloid leukocyte activation and the regulation of phagocytosis in CAD. The total leukocyte count is an independent predictor of death or myocardial infarction in patients with or at high risk of developing CAD, and the greatest risk prediction is given by the neutrophil/lymphocyte ratio (Angkananard et al. 2018). In a review article by Sun et al., it was noted that myocardial tissue-resident macrophages (cTMs) phagocytose bacteria and apoptotic cells as part of cardiac homeostasis, and after cardiac injury cTMs phagocytose dead cardiomyocytes thereby regulating cardiac progenitor cell proliferation and angiogenesis (Sun et al. 2021). These results are consistent with the CAD-related biological processes that were found to be enriched in this study.

  In terms of cellular composition, the biomarker genes are involved in the function of secretory granule membranes and ficolin-1-rich granule membranes. To date, no studies have been conducted on the role of these membranes in cardiovascular disease, indicating that this should be explored further. In terms of molecular function, the biomarker genes are involved in Toll-like receptor binding and non-membrane-spanning protein tyrosine kinase activity. It has long been known that members of the Toll-like receptor family are associated with atherosclerosis and the development of myocardial infarction (Jin et al. 2023). There are few studies on non-membrane-spanning protein tyrosine kinase activity in cardiovascular diseases; therefore, this may be a future research avenue. However, it has been shown that reduced expression of tyrosine kinase receptor B (TrkB) increases the risk of CAD, protects endothelial integrity during atherogenesis, and plays a protective role in CAD (Jiang et al. 2015).

  Our results demonstrated that all six candidate genes, IL1B, TLR2, FCGR2A, SYK, FCER1G, and HCK, were associated with CAD. IL1B mRNA levels have been shown to be higher in patients with non-calcified and/or partially calcified coronary plaques and high-risk atheromatous plaques than in patients with no apparent coronary plaques, and the presence of high-risk atheromatous plaques was significantly associated with elevated IL1B mRNA levels in the epicardial adipose tissue (Kitagawa et al. 2021), suggesting that IL1B is related to the development of CAD. TLR2 expression has been shown to be significantly higher in patients with atherosclerotic disease than in a control group, demonstrating that TLR2 is an independent risk factor for atherosclerosis (Kuwahata et al. 2010). In another report, TLR2 expression was found to be positively correlated with vascular and Gensini scores, and TLR2 expression was found to correlate with the degree of CAD (Mizoguchi et al. 2007). In a clinical study, the FCGR2A genotype has been found to be associated with acute coronary syndrome as the first manifestation of CAD (Raaz et al. 2009), reflecting its relationship with the occurrence of CAD. SYK expression increases with an increase in CAD severity and is significantly associated with CAD risk factors (Liang et al. 2019). This finding corroborates the findings of the present study, which state that SYK is a CAD biomarker. FCER1G is a critical gene in atherosclerosis (Huo et al. 2021). FCER1G expression is significantly increased in patients with acute myocardial infarction (Xiao et al. 2021). However, there are few studies on the role of FCER1G in the cardiovascular system, which may be a promising research direction. Studies suggest that a high level of HCK in atherosclerosis is a risk factor for its further development (Huo and Wang 2021). In the diagnostic model study, six of these genes (IL1B, TLR2, FCGR2A, SYK, FCER1G, and HCK) had an AUC of 0.973 under the overall ROC curve, indicating that these six signature genes are important for the diagnosis and prediction of CAD.

  To explore potential therapeutic options, we assessed immune cell infiltration in CAD. The analysis revealed differences in immune cell infiltration between the CAD and HC groups, with 10 cell types being significant: activated dendritic cells, eosinophils, activated mast cells, monocytes, neutrophils, activated natural killer (NK) cells, resting NK cells, resting memory CD4 T cells, follicular helper T cells, and γδ T cells. These findings suggest that these immune cells influence the development of CAD through direct or indirect mechanisms. In addition, we performed a correlation analysis between biomarkers and significantly different immune cells and found that the correlation between different immune cells and biomarkers was strong, and IL1B expression was most positively correlated with mast cell activation. This result suggests that IL1B may be an important gene in the pathology of CAD through phagocytosis, and the mechanism might involve the activation of immune cells. It has been reported that ILIB positively correlated with activated mast cells in patients with chronic spontaneous urticaria (Peng et al. 2022). Activation of mast cells can promote plaque instability and its corresponding rupture in atherosclerosis, and secretion of trypsin by mast cells may indicate acute coronary syndrome in patients with early diagnosis and prognostic indicators (Morici et al. 2016). Therefore, IL1B expression may affect the occurrence and development of CAD by affecting mast cells.

  miRNAs regulate many human diseases and have been widely studied in cancer, viral hepatitis C, and hematologic diseases; they have also been reported in cardiovascular diseases (Bernardo et al. 2015). We investigated differentially expressed miRNAs and biomarker-related regulatory TFs in CAD biomarkers and constructed TF-miRNA-mRNA and miRNA-SNP-mRNA networks. The results showed that hsa-miR-1207-5p regulates HCK and FCER1G, RUNX1, and SPI1 may be essential transcription factors, and hsa-miR-486-3p affects IL1B by regulating rs907156000. RUNX1 has been extensively studied in cancer and hematology research but has been less studied in cardiovascular disease. RUNX1 reportedly regulates cardiac sarcoplasmic reticulum calcium uptake and contractile function mainly through increased protein kinase A and sarcoplasmic reticulum Ca2+-ATPase activity. In animal models, whole heart RUNX1 mRNA levels were elevated 4 weeks after myocardial infarction and showed lower amplitude and slower calcium transients, thus affecting cardiac contractile function and remodeling (McCarroll et al. 2018). This finding provides a new target that can prevent poor cardiac remodeling and heart failure after myocardial infarction in patients with CAD, which is consistent with our recruitment of the RUNX1 gene as a possible transcription factor for CAD. SPI1 is significantly upregulated in the myocardial tissue of mice with myocardial infarction and hypoxia-induced HL-1 cells, mainly through a significant decrease in DNA methylation of SPI1, leading to its transcriptional activation. Upregulated SPI1 binds to the TLR4 promoter and activates the TLR4/NF-κB axis, thereby exacerbating cardiomyocyte apoptosis, inflammation, and fibrosis (Liu et al. 2022). This finding indicates that SPI1 activation plays a vital role in cardiomyocyte injury during myocardial infarction in CAD patients. SPI1 is critical to the TLR4 promoter, which also validates the functional involvement of Toll-like receptor binding molecules in CAD, as mentioned earlier. This is also the first report that hsa-miR-1207-5p regulates HCK and FCER1 in CAD, and hsa-miR-486-3p affects IL1B by regulating rs907156000, the mechanism of which is still unclear and warrants further exploration.

  The results of this analysis not only suggested biomarkers for the diagnosis of CAD but also suggested the pathways and mechanisms involved. Therefore, the next step was to explore the potential target drugs for CAD, so we conducted a drug prediction analysis. Some of the drugs identified in the analysis are already currently used in the clinical treatment and prevention of CAD. For example, aspirin, as a first-line antiplatelet drug, has been widely used in the prevention and treatment of CAD (Hennekens 2002; Ittaman et al. 2014). In this study, we found that aspirin may act on CAD through FCER1G and IL1B gene expression, suggesting that it may have an alternative mechanism of action. In addition, morphine is a potent analgesic used to relieve chest pain during the management of acute coronary syndrome (Braun et al. 2020). Canakinumab, an anti-inflammatory monoclonal antibody, has also been listed for the secondary prevention of CAD (Ortega-Paz et al. 2021). As a hormone replacement therapy, raloxifene has been shown to have a protective effect on the hearts of sterilized female patients with CAD (Mijatovic et al. 1999). Therefore, the drugs suggested in our analysis have already shown some potential in CAD therapy. In the future, this information may help develop targeted therapy for CAD further.


### Limitations
  There was a lack of clinical trial data in this study, which is a limitation of our research. The database used in this study was limited, as the sample size was small and the samples were all male; hence, there may be sex differences. We will continue to focus on these biomarkers to compensate for the lack of knowledge and understanding regarding CAD and will further enrich the sample size.


## Conclusion
  We propose that IL1B, TLR2, FCGR2A, SYK, FCER1G, and HCK are biomarkers associated with phagocytosis regulators in CAD and have diagnostic and predictive value. When exploring potential target drugs, we found that aspirin can affect FCER1G and IL1B expression; furthermore, it is the only drug that can affect FCER1G. Additionally, we propose that hsa-miR-1207-5p regulates HCK and FCER1 in CAD and that hsa-miR-486-3p affects IL1B expression by regulating rs907156000, enriching the exploration of the gene expression of CAD.
