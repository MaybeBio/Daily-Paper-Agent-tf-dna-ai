# Daidzin improves the cisplatin chemosensitivity for osteosarcoma via binding to β-catenin protein and suppressing the wnt pathway


## Abstract
  Osteosarcoma (OS) mainly occurs in adolescents and children, accompanied with chemotherapy resistance as well as unsatisfactory treatment outcome. Recent studies have discovered a series of natural products with anticancer activity, which have important translational value. Daidzin has been reported with a variety of biological activities. We aim to explore whether daidzin has therapeutic potential for OS. In this study, cell based and in vivo studies showed that daidzin can inhibit the ability of OS cells to proliferate and metastasize. Compared to single treatment arms, combined treatment of daidzin and cisplatin, a classic recommendation for OS treatment, showed a further suppression in OS cell viability, migration and invasion ability, and led to further apoptosis. Mechanistically, daidzin was found to downregulate the β-catenin expression and further inhibited the Wnt pathway. However, daidzin did not alter the transcriptional level of β-catenin. Molecular docking showed a potential interaction between daidzin and β-catenin protein, and their combination was further confirmed by thermal shift assay and MST assay. Daidzin was found to reduced protein stability of β-catenin, and only transfection of β-catenin mutant (a mutant that targets the binding sites to daidzin) at the same time as daidzin treatment could effectively attenuate the antitumor effect of daidzin. Our study suggests that daidzin has translational value as a potential adjuvant to improve treatment outcomes for OS.


## Introduction
  Osteosarcoma, a kind of pernicious bone tumor developed from stromal cell lines, mainly occurs in adolescents and children. It can occur in any bone of the body, but the most common site is the end of a long bone, such as the knee, femur, or tibia. At present, the therapeutic strategies for osteosarcoma mainly include surgical resection, chemotherapy and radiotherapy1–4. Due to continuous medical technology innovation, the 5-year survival rate of osteosarcoma is approximately 60–70%5. However, due to the prevalence of cases with significant chemotherapy resistance in clinical practice6, it is difficult to achieve satisfactory treatment outcomes. Therefore, improving the clinical efficacy of chemotherapy is urgently needed for the better disease outcome.

  Daidzin is a natural plant compound found mainly in soybean, and has multiple biological activities, including anti-inflammatory as well as protective effects on the skeleton and cardiovascular and nervous systems7–9. Current studies have shown that daidzin may have certain antitumor effects. For example, daidzin has reported to inhibit cervical cancer growth through the JAK2/STAT3 pathway10. In addition, daidzin influences MnSOD and EMT progression via the PI3K/Akt signaling in both prostate and colorectal cancer11. However, whether daidzin has therapeutic potential for OS remains unknown.

  Cisplatin (DDP), a commonly utilized chemotherapeutic drug, inhibits viability and division of tumor cells through interfering with DNA synthesis and its repair12. Meanwhile, cisplatin is the classic recommendation derived from the 2021 NCCN guidelines (2021.V1) for the osteosarcoma treatment13. However, cisplatin resistance is occasionally present in the clinic of osteosarcoma treatment, which limits the efficiency of chemotherapy and reduces the survival rate. Therefore, exploring ways to improve the sensitivity to chemotherapy drugs will result in a better outcome for patients with osteosarcoma.

  Herein, we planned to explore the potential effect and the underlying molecular mechanism of daidzin in OS. Additionally, we intended to evaluate the comprehensive synergistic effect of daidzin and cisplatin for OS treatment. Our study may provide evidence of daidzin as a potential adjuvant for better OS treatment.


## Materials and methods

### Cell culture and reagent
  Osteosarcoma cells including U2OS, HOS, MNNG/HOS, SJSA-1, 143B, as well as MG63 were gained from the ATCC organization. And the U2OS/MTX300 cell was kindly provided by Dr. M. Serra (Istituti Ortopedici Rizzoli, Bologna, Italy). The cells were cultured in high-glucose DMEM (Gibco) with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) supplemented with 1% streptomycin/penicillin (Thermo Fisher) in 37 °C, 5% CO2 incubator. Daidzin (purchased from selleck) was dissolved in Dimethyl sulfoxide, and the concentration used in the experiments was based on its IC50 value.


## Cell viability assay
  Cells were seeded in 96 well plates and treated with the desired experimental conditions. Afterward, the culture medium was aspirated, and replaced with CCK-8 reagent for 2–4 h. The absorbance of the formazan dye was measured using the spectrophotometer at a wavelength around 450 nm. The absorbance values were then normalized to a control condition to calculate relative cell viability. The combination index (CI) was measured using the Chou-Talalay method. And the interactions between daidzin and cisplatin were respectively categorized into antagonism (CI value greater than 1), synergism (CI value less than 1), and additive effect (CI value equal to 1).


## Flow cytometric apoptosis experiment
  The cells were treated with increasing concentrations of daidzin, collected, and then resuspended in binding buffer. Based on the standard instructions, the Annexin V-FITC/PI Cell Apoptosis Kit (KeyGEN) treatment was performed in the indicated cells. The cells were incubated at room temperature away from light for 20 min. The stained samples were detected by Gallios flow cytometer, and the apoptosis rate was detected.


## Colony formation experiment
  The indicated cells (500 cells/well) were seeded onto the 6-well plates containing 2 mL culture medium. The cells grew for 2 weeks to develop colonies. Colonies were fixed with paraformaldehyde, stained using crystal violet and then counted and analyzed to measure the colony formation ability.


## Western blot analysis
  Total cellular proteins were obtained using lysis buffer containing protease inhibitor. Then the concentration was measured using Bradford method. The indicated samples were separated using an SDS-PAGE gel, and subsequently transferred to a PVDF membrane at 350 mA on ice for an hour. The membrane was then blocked with skim milk for an hour at room temperature and soaked in diluent primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C. Afterward, the membrane was soaked in appropriate secondary antibodies for an hour at room temperature, and measured with ECL reagent. The antibodies used were rabbit anti-BAX (ab32503), rabbit anti-Caspase3 (ab32351), rabbit anti-Bcl-xL (ab32370), rabbit anti-β-catenin (ab223075), rabbit anti-Cleaved Caspase3 (ab32042), rabbit anti-MMP2 (ab92536), rabbit anti-MMP9 (ab76003), and rabbit anti-ACTB (ab8227) from Abcam, rabbit anti-C-Jun (AF6090) and rabbit anti-CyclinD1 (AF0931) from Affinity, and rabbit anti-C-myc (18583) form Cell Signaling Technology.


### Transwell assay
  7–10 × 104 cells were seeded in Boyden chamber containing 8 μm-pores membrane. The upper chambers were precoated with Matrigel (corning) diluted to a final concentration of 15% for cell invasion assays or uncoated for cell migration assays. 150 µL of cell suspension free of FBS was seeded in the upper chambers, and 600 µL of cultured medium containing 10% FBS was added into the lower chambers. After 8–10 h incubation, the cells adhering in the upper chambers were cleaned by swab, and the cells invading into the lower ones were fixed and stained with crystal violet to achieve visualization. The stained ones were photographed and counted (3 random fields for each well).


## RNA extraction and RT-qPCR experiment
  Cellular RNA was prepared by TRIzol cleavage based on the standard manual, and further synthesized into cDNA by the cDNA Synthesis Kit (Vazyme) based on manufacturer’s manual. The RT-qPCR assay was conducted using the Quantitative PCR Kit (Vazyme). ACTB was selected as the internal control. The Ct(2−△△CT) method was performed to normalize the indicated mRNA expression level. And the primers of CTNNB1 and ACTB were shown as follows:

  CTNNB1 sequence (5’ → 3’).

  Forward: AAAGCGGCTGTTAGTCACTGG.

  Reverse: CGAGTCATTGCATACTGTCCAT.

  ACTB sequence (5’ → 3’).

  Forward: CATGTACGTTGCTATCCAGGC.

  Reverse: CTCCTTAATGTCACGCACGAT.


## Molecular docking
  The interaction analysis between daidzin and the β-catenin protein was performed using the computational docking method. The crystal structure of the β-catenin protein was acquired from the UniProt website. The potential binding sites on the β-catenin protein that involved in the induced-fit docking of daidzin was analyzed by official Autodock software. During the docking combination, type of interaction, potential conformations, the best-fit docking configuration, binding energy values, and bond distances were calculated. The configuration which has the lowest binding energy was finally selected as the representative binding model.


### Thermal shift assay
  The thermal shift assay was performed as previously reported14. Firstly, 293T cells were treated with daidzin or DMSO. Secondly, the cell suspensions were harvested and then heated at the indicated temperature point for 3 min. Finally, protein extraction was conducted, and protein levels were measured using immunoblotting assay.


### MST assay
  β-catenin protein is labeled as a target following the instruction manual of Monolith protein Labeling Kit RED-NHS 2nd Generation Kit. And the labelling effect was evaluated by a preliminary experiment. The binding affinity detection was then conducted if the signal-to noise ratio was standard, and there was no protein aggregation. The fixed concentration labelled molecules (β-catenin protein) and gradient diluted unlabeled drug (daidzin) were incubated for 5 min. The samples were measured by MST-NT.115 instrument.


### Animal experiments
  4 weeks old male BALB/c nude mice were provided by GemPharmatech organization. The in vivo experiments were authorized by the Animal Research Committee of the Linfen central hospital. For the subcutaneous tumorigenicity experiment, the OS cell line 143B (a density of 7 × 105 cells per 100 µl saline) were subcutaneously injected into the axilla skin of mice. Till the tumor grew to a volume of approximately 50 mm3 10 days later, the mice were randomly assigned into 4 groups, and then administered with the indicated treatment. All tumors’ measurements were recorded every three days, and the mice were sacrificed after 27 days. The tumor volume was calculated according to the equation: width2 × length × π/6. The tumor weight and body weight were also recorded. For lung metastasis assays, the 143B cells (a density of 1 × 106 cells per 100 µl saline) were injected into the mice via the tail vein. One month after the injection, all the samples were collected. And the lungs were also surgically obtained, fixed, and performed hematoxylin (HE) staining. Specifically, the BALB/c nude mice were treated with the indicated drugs via intraperitoneal injection: the control group, cisplatin treatment group (every 3 days, 5 mg/kg), daidzin treatment group (every day, 20 mg/kg), and the combination treatment group [cisplatin (every 3 days, 5 mg/kg) and daidzin (every day, 20 mg/kg)]. The mice due to other death reasons (such as infection of fighting) were excluded. During the outcome analysis, the researches were blinded of the allocation of each group.


### Immunohistochemistry staining (IHC)
  The tumor tissue samples were deparaffinized, rehydrated followed by antigen retrieval. For antigen retrieval, Tris/EDTA solution (pH8.0) was used and heated for 3 min after the pressure cooker reached maximum pressure. Then, the tumor samples were blocked by using goat serum, and subsequently incubated with a Ki-67 antibody (Abcam, ab15580, 1:500) at 4 °C overnight. Next, the IHC staining was performed using horseradish peroxidase conjugates by DAB reagent detection. Two independent pathologists were assigned to evaluate the IHC staining levels. The immunostaining degrees were measured and calculated as a staining index.


### Statistical analysis
  Each experiment was performed in three biological replicates, and each biological replicate contained three technical replicates. Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 9.0. The data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation. Quantitative data were compared by the two-tailed Student’s t test. Two-way ANOVA with Tukey’s multiple comparisons test was conducted for the experiment with the second variable. When the P value was less than 0.05, the difference was considered to be statistically significant.


## Results

### Daidzin inhibits cell viability of osteosarcoma
  Daidzin is a bioactive natural product. Its molecular formula is C21H20O9, and its molecular weight is 416.38. The chemical structure is presented in Fig. 1A. To figure out whether daidzin has an antitumor effect on OS, we first treated several OS cell lines, U2OS, U2OS/MTX300, HOS, SJSA-1, MNNG/HOS, 143B as well as MG63, with increasing concentrations of daidzin for different durations. The CCK-8 assay showed significant toxic effects of daidzin on these cells. We observed that daidzin treatment inhibited the viability of these OS cells in both time- and dose-dependent manner. To be specific, the IC50 values of daidzin treatment for 48 and 72 h were 44.0 and 25.7 µM for U2OS, 75.1 and 58.7 µM for U2OS/MTX300, 76.2 and 56.9 µM for HOS, 57.3 and 33.4 µM for MNNG/HOS, 29.2 and 19.8 µM for SJSA-1, 44.0 and 26.4 µM for 143B, and 26.3 and 19.8 µM for MG63 (Fig. 1B and C). Subsequently, the colony formation experiment was conducted to further explore the biological effect of daidzin on 143B, SJSA-1 as well as U2OS cells. We found that increasing concentrations of daidzin treatment significantly suppressed the colony formation abilities of 143B, SJSA-1 as well as U2OS cells in a dose-dependent manner after daidzin treatment for 2 weeks (Fig. 1D). In conclusion, these observations elucidated that daidzin effectively inhibit the cell viability of OS.

B The cell viability assay showing the inhibitory effect of daidzin on the osteosarcoma cell lines (U2OS, U2OS/MTX300, HOS, MNNG/HOS, SJSA-1, 143B and MG63) in both dose- and time-dependent manner. The cells were treated with the indicated concentrations of daidzin for 24, 48, and 72 h. C The table shows the IC50 values of each cell line at different time points. D Daidzin inhibits the colony formation ability of osteosarcoma cells. The indicated cells were treated with increasing concentrations of daidzin for 14 days. Representative images and quantification analysis of the results are shown. The data are presented as mean ± SD. *P < 0.05; **P < 0.01, compared to the control groups.


### Daidzin promotes cell apoptosis of osteosarcoma
  After demonstrating that daidzin can inhibit OS cell viability, we investigated whether it also plays a regulatory role in the apoptosis of OS. We performed a flow cytometry assay to analyze cell apoptosis, and found that the cell proportions stained with Annexin V/PI in the 143B, SJSA-1 and U2OS cell lines were elevated gradually with increasing concentrations of daidzin compared to the control group (Fig. 2A). These data revealed that daidzin treatment promoted the cell apoptosis of OS via a dose-dependent manner. Further, in order to explain this phenomenon at the molecular level, we detected the levels of some apoptosis-related proteins, including BAX, Bcl-xL, Caspase3 and Cleaved Caspase3, in 143B, SJSA-1 as well as U2OS with daidzin treatment for 48 h. And we found that the expression levels of the proapoptotic protein BAX were dose-dependently upregulated after treatment with increasing concentrations of daidzin, while the levels of Bcl-xL, an antiapoptotic protein, were dose-dependently downregulated. Moreover, the levels of Caspase3 as well as Cleaved Caspase3 were also elevated with increasing daidzin treatment compared to the control groups (Fig. 2B-D). In summary, these data suggest that daidzin promotes cell apoptosis of osteosarcoma.

  Fig. 2Daidzin promotes the apoptosis of osteosarcoma cells. A Annexin V/PI staining of the 143B, SJSA-1 and U2OS cells treated with or without daidzin was detected by flow cytometry. Representative images and quantification analysis of the flow cytometry assay results are presented. B-D Western blot analysis showing the expression levels of the apoptosis-related protein BAX, Bcl-xL, Caspase3 and Caspase9 in the 143B(B), SJSA-1(C) and U2OS(D) cells following the 48 h treatment with daidzin. The data are presented as mean ± SD. **P < 0.01, compared to the control groups.


### Daidzin enhances osteosarcoma cell sensitivity to cisplatin
  Cisplatin resistance is an obstacle to the treatment of osteosarcoma patients. Therefore, it is particularly urgent to improve the cisplatin sensitivity of OS cells. In order to explore whether daidzin can increase the curative effect of cisplatin in OS cells, a CCK-8 experiment was performed using 143B, SJSA-1 as well as U2OS cells with increasing concentrations of cisplatin and/or daidzin treatment for 48 h. Surprisingly, the inhibitory effect of daidzin treatment combined with cisplatin was observed to be strengthened compared to that of cisplatin or daidzin treatment alone. The CI value was also calculated to evaluate the antagonistic, additive, or synergistic effects of the combination treatment. The results showed that daidzin treatment exhibited a synergistic effect combined with cisplatin treatment (CI value less than 1) on suppressing viability of the indicated OS cells (Fig. 3A). The colony formation experiments found that the co-treatment of cisplatin and daidzin showed a stronger inhibitory effect on the colony formation ability of OS than either cisplatin or daidzin treatment alone (Fig. 3B). Subsequently, we found that the protein expression of the proapoptotic proteins BAX, Caspase3 and Cleaved Caspase3 were upregulated by the co-treatment of cisplatin and daidzin compared to either reagent alone. Besides, the levels of the antiapoptotic protein Bcl-xL in the indicated cells were dramatically decreased when co-treated with cisplatin and daidzin compared to either reagent treatment alone (Fig. 3C-E). Taken together, daidzin increases the cisplatin sensitivity of OS cells.

The fraction affected-combination index plots showing the synergistic effect of daidzin when combined with cisplatin treatment. B Colony formation assay showing the colony numbers of the 143B, SJSA-1 and U2OS cells following the combination treatment of daidzin (40µM) and cisplatin (1µM), or each reagent alone. The data are presented as mean ± SD. **P < 0.01.


### Daidzin reduces β-catenin protein stability and inhibits wnt signaling pathway in OS cells
  Previous reports found that abnormal activation of the Wnt pathway may lead to the development of osteosarcoma and further regulate the disease progression15,16. Here, we wondered whether daidzin can influence the Wnt signaling in OS. As shown by western blotting, the expression level of β-catenin, a critical node in Wnt signaling, as well as its downstream targets C-Jun, CyclinD1 and C-myc were downregulated by daidzin treatment in 143B, SJSA-1 and U2OS cells. Moreover, the inhibitory effect of the co-treatment of cisplatin and daidzin on Wnt signaling was stronger than that of either daidzin or cisplatin alone (Fig. 4A-C). Surprisingly, we found that the mRNA expression level of CTNNB1 showed no difference after daidzin treatment at different concentrations, which revealed that daidzin did not regulate Wnt signaling by affecting the transcriptional events of CTNNB1 (Fig. 4D-F). To further clarify this finding, we determined the stability of β-catenin protein after treatment with daidzin. As shown in Fig. 4G-I, the stability of β-catenin protein was significantly decreased after daidzin treatment.

A-C Western blot analysis showing the expression levels of the β-catenin, C-Jun, CyclinD1 and C-myc in the 143B, SJSA-1 and U2OS cells following the combination treatment of daidzin (40µM) and cisplatin (1µM), or each reagent alone. D-F RT-qPCR assay showing the mRNA level of CTNNB1 in the 143B, SJSA-1 and U2OS cells following treatment of increasing concentrations of daidzin. G-I Western blot analysis showing the protein stability of β-catenin in the 143B, SJSA-1 and U2OS cells following daidzin treatment (40µM). L Microscale thermophoresis assay of daidzin binding to β-catenin protein. The data are presented as mean ± SD. **P < 0.01, n.s., not significant, compared to the control groups.

  Previous studies have shown that drug monomers can not only bind to specific sites of proteins to regulate their folding, stability or degradation, but also affect the structure and stability of proteins by changing the charge, hydrogen bond or hydrophobic properties on the surface of proteins. To predict the potential interaction of daidzin and β-catenin protein, we thus conducted the molecular docking by Autodock software to determine possible binding configurations between them. And the predicted binding configuration of daidzin to β-catenin protein showed an appropriate shape match, and residues Glu267 and Lys270 of β-catenin were responsible for forming hydrogen bonds with daidzin (Fig. 4J). The predicted docking energy was − 5.77 kcal/M, suggesting a good binding ability. Subsequently, the thermal shift assay showed that daidzin treatment altered β-catenin protein stability after heating (Fig. 4K), meaning the potential interaction between them. The further microscale thermophoresis (MST) assay with β-catenin protein and a dilution series of daidzin showed their dissociation constant (KD) (Fig. 4L). In conclusion, daidzin could bind to the β-catenin protein, reduce its protein stability, and further inhibit the Wnt pathway in osteosarcoma.


### Daidzin and cisplatin synergistically suppress cell migration and invasion of OS
  The malignancy of OS cells is reflected not only in their rapid proliferation rate but also their ability to easily metastasize. Therefore, we wonder whether daidzin can regulate the metastatic capacity of OS. The transwell experiments were performed and showed that daidzin obviously reduced the cell migration and invasion abilities of 143B as well as SJSA-1 cells. In addition, the co-treatment of cisplatin and daidzin showed a stronger inhibitory influence on cell migration and invasion ability than either daidzin or cisplatin treatment alone (Fig. 5A and B). Subsequently, the expression levels of MMP2 as well as MMP9 proteins were found dramatically downregulated in cells co-treated with cisplatin and daidzin for 48 h compared with either reagent alone (Fig. 5C and D). Taken together, the co-treatment of cisplatin and daidzin synergistically suppresses the metastatic capacity of OS by decreasing MMP2 and MMP9 expression.

Representative images and quantification analysis of the transwell assay results are presented. C-D Western blotting analysis showing the protein levels of MMP2 and MMP9 in the 143B(C) and SJSA-1(D) following the combination treatment of daidzin (40µM) and cisplatin (1µM), or each reagent alone.   Daidzin suppresses osteosarcoma cell viability, migration and invasion via Wnt signaling pathway.

  Since it has been shown that daidzin can regulate the Wnt signaling pathway by binding to β-catenin, we further wondered whether daidzin regulates osteosarcoma malignancy through Wnt signaling. We constructed CTNNB1 mutants (E267D, K270N) based on the binding sites between daidzin and β-catenin protein. We found that transfection of CTNNB1 MUT attenuated the inhibitory effect of daidzin on the proliferation of 143B and SJSA-1 cells, whereas transfection of CTNNB1 WT had no significant effect (Fig. 6A). Similarly, compared with the daidzin treatment group, the migration and invasion ability of 143B and SJSA-1 cells after transfection with CTNNB1 MUT was significantly increased, while there was no significant change after transfection with CTNNB1 WT (Fig. 6B). In addition, daidzin treatment with rescue of CTNNB1 MUT can restore the expression of the downstream effector molecule C-Jun in Wnt pathway, but CTNNB1 WT cannot (Fig. 6C). Therefore, we speculate that daidzin suppresses osteosarcoma cell viability, migration and invasion via Wnt signaling pathway.

### Cisplatin and daidzin synergistically suppress the viability and metastasis of OS in vivo
  We have previously shown that daidzin and cisplatin can synergistically inhibit cell viability and metastasis in vitro. To further verify this conclusion in vivo, we established the subcutaneously implanted tumor model in nude mouse. The mice were randomly assigned to 4 groups when the volume of the tumor grew about 50 mm3 after 10 days, and then administered with cisplatin (every 3 days, 5 mg/kg) and/or daidzin (every day, 20 mg/kg). The tumor volume and tumor weight were found obviously reduced in either cisplatin treatment group or daidzin treatment group compared with control one. Besides, the co-treatment of cisplatin and daidzin showed a further suppression on tumor compared to either drug used alone (Fig. 7A-C). Daidzin treatment had no effect on body weight among groups (Fig. 7D). The immunohistochemical staining of the tumor tissues revealed that the proportion of Ki-67 positive cells was obviously reduced in the co-treatment group of cisplatin and daidzin compared with either reagent used alone (Fig. 7E). Furthermore, we determined the role of co-treatment of cisplatin and daidzin in OS metastasis by establishing a mouse lung metastasis model. The co-treatment of cisplatin and daidzin exhibited a lower incidence of metastasis than either drug alone, as confirmed by the H&E staining of the lung samples (Fig. 7F). In summary, cisplatin and daidzin have synergistical inhibitory effects on the viability and metastasis of osteosarcoma in vivo.

## Discussion
  Osteosarcoma, a common bone pernicious tumor in adolescents and children, accounts for more than half of all primary bone malignancies. And malignancy of osteosarcoma lies in its easy occurrence of hematogenous metastasis in the early stage. Even if the primary lesion is surgically removed, it tends to recur or metastasize to other sites. In addition, adjuvant/neoadjuvant chemotherapy has become one of the standard therapy methods for osteosarcoma, but the effect of chemotherapy is poor because of the resistance to a variety of chemotherapy drugs17,18. In response to these problems, in-depth research is needed to explore new treatment strategies to improve the treatment effect and quality of life of patients with osteosarcoma.

  Daidzin is a natural bioflavonoid compound found in legumes, especially soybean. Recently, daidzin has been found to have potential antitumor effects. Increasing evidences have demonstrated that daidzin exhibits toxic effect on tumor cells, induces tumor cell apoptosis, and blocks tumor angiogenesis, to exert an antitumor effect10,11,19, demonstrating that daidzin may be a potential adjuvant drug for cancer treatment. However, it remains unclear whether daidzin has an inhibitory effect on OS. Herein, we demonstrated that daidzin had a significant anti-OS effect both in vivo and in vitro. Daidzin was found to inhibit the cell viability of OS, induce cell apoptosis, and further suppress cell migration as well as invasion ability. Moreover, the inhibitory effect of daidzin was both dose- and time-dependent. Mechanistically, we elucidated that daidzin inhibited the Wnt pathway, specifically, reducing the protein expression level of β-catenin, the core molecule of this pathway, and downregulating the downstream targets, including C-myc, CyclinD1 as well as MMP9.

  The β-catenin protein, a structural protein encoded by CTNNB1, serves as a critical component of the Wnt pathway. The balance of its extranuclear accumulation and nuclear translocation regulates Wnt signaling. The Wnt signaling has shown a decisive role in tumorigenesis and development. Abnormal activation of the Wnt pathway is tightly associated with a variety of malignant tumors20–23. For example, in colorectal cancer, approximately 80% patients have mutations in the adenomatous polyposis coli (APC) gene, which can lead to persistent activation of the Wnt pathway. These mutations disrupt regulation of β-catenin protein, leading to its accumulation in the nucleus and the carcinogenic effects described above24. Recent researches have also demonstrated that Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway exerts a key role not only in the development but also the malignant progression of osteosarcoma25–27. In osteosarcoma cells, abnormal Wnt signaling can lead to excessive accumulation of β-catenin and enhanced transcriptional activity, thereby promoting cell viability, migration, and anti-apoptotic ability. At the same time, Wnt signaling can also regulate the fate of stem and progenitor cells and then affect the degree of differentiation of osteosarcoma cells. Therefore, targeting the Wnt pathway has become a new promising strategy for the treatment of osteosarcoma28. In our research, we conducted molecular structure virtual docking and found the potential binding sites between daidzin and the β-catenin protein. We also found β-catenin as a bona fide target of daidzin, supported biophysically by thermal shift assay and MST assay results. In addition, daidzin treatment significantly reduced the protein stability of β-catenin. Through β-catenin rescue experiment, we found that only β-catenin transfection after mutation of the binding sites with daidzin can effectively attenuate the anti-osteosarcoma effect of daidzin. Hence, we considered that the anti-OS effect of daidzin was achieved by targeting the Wnt pathway via binding to β-catenin protein and thus affecting its protein stability. Interestingly, our group are going on another research focusing on the relationship between β-catenin expression and malignancy in osteosarcoma (results not shown here), and here we did not observe a linear correlation between daidzin sensitivity and β-catenin level in osteosarcoma cell lines. We speculate that this is because β-catenin contributes differently to the malignant behavior of different osteosarcoma cell lines.

  At present, chemotherapy performs an important role for osteosarcoma treatment, and can improve prognosis by reducing the volume of tumor, controlling metastasis, improving the effect of surgical resection, and killing residual tumor cells. Cisplatin, a commonly utilized chemotherapeutic agent during the treatment of osteosarcoma, is often combined with other chemotherapeutic agents, such as doxorubicin, cyclophosphamide and methotrexate, to form a multidrug combination chemotherapy regimen29. However, cisplatin resistance is still a common and intractable clinical problem during the treatment of osteosarcoma. It can be caused by several factors, including the following: (1) DNA repair: Some tumor cells may obtain enhanced DNA repair capacity, so as to reduce DNA damage caused by cisplatin. (2) Increased expression of transport pumps: Transport pumps on the cell surface (such as P-glycoprotein) can rapidly expel cisplatin from the cells, reducing its effective concentration and thus reducing the therapeutic effect. (3) Apoptotic escape: Some tumor cells may develop abnormal apoptotic pathways that render them insensitive to cisplatin. (4) Dysregulations in signaling pathways: Cisplatin resistance may also involve abnormal expression or mutation of the Wnt signaling pathway, DNA damage response pathway and transcription factors, thereby affecting the cellular response to cisplatin30–32. Therefore, any adjuvant that can improve the cisplatin resistance will be beneficial for osteosarcoma treatment. In our research, we demonstrated that daidzin combined with cisplatin exerted a synergistic effect, which obviously increased sensitivity to cisplatin. The co-treatment of cisplatin and daidzin had a stronger tumor-inhibitory effect on both cell viability and migration as well as invasion ability than either drug alone. In vivo experiments further revealed that the inhibitory effect of the co-treatment of cisplatin and daidzin on tumor growth and the incidence of metastasis was more obvious than that of daidzin or cisplatin used alone. Our study provides evidences that daidzin is a potential effective adjuvant to enhance the cisplatin sensitivity of OS cells.


## Conclusion
  In summary, our study demonstrates that daidzin inhibits the viability, induces cell apoptosis, and suppresses the lung metastasis of OS cells by binding to the β-catenin protein, thus reducing its protein stability and further inhibiting the Wnt signaling pathway. Furthermore, daidzin can enhance the cisplatin sensitivity of OS cells, suggesting that daidzin has translational value to be a potential adjuvant to improve treatment outcomes for OS.
