# Integrative bioinformatic approach reveals novel melatonin-related biomarkers for Alzheimer’s disease


## Supplementary Information
  The online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-80755-x.


## Introduction
  AD is a neurodegenerative disorder that mainly causes dementia and has become one of the most expensive, lethal, and burdensome diseases in the 21st century1. This disorder is characterized by tau-containing intracellular neurofibrillary tangles and amyloid-containing extracellular plaques. Most patients with AD present difficulties with short-term memory, but they may also experience problems with expressive speech, visuospatial cognition, and executive function (mental agility)2. Unfortunately, there is currently no effective treatment for AD. Therefore, an increasing number of studies have focused on the early diagnosis and treatment of AD. A study on the global epidemiological risk prediction of AD demonstrated that the number of AD patients could be reduced by over nine million in the next 40 years by delaying the onset of AD symptoms by one year3.

  Consequently, it is therefore meaningful to develop preventive intervention strategies during the early stages of the disease. Identifying individuals at a high risk for AD results in faster diagnosis, better patient classification, and higher levels of clinical research, ultimately leading to effective preventive treatment4. However, the early stages of AD are challenging to diagnose because of the atypical nature of the early symptoms of AD .Therefore, patients are often at an advanced stage of the disease when seeking medical attention. Moreover, it has been estimated that 90% − 95% of AD casesoccur in people over the age of 65 years5. AD dementia may go unnoticed when younger patients lack ‘typical’ hippocampal volume reduction or do not exhibit non-amnestic symptoms. A cohort of young-onset AD patients with neuropathological confirmation reported a misdiagnosis rate of 53% compared with 4% for patients with typical symptoms6,7. Accordingly it is imperative to identify novel biomarkers with high sensitivity and specificity for early diagnosis of AD. These findings will serve as the basis for clinical research and as a reference.

  MLT is a multifunctional neurohormone primarily secreted by the pineal gland8 and involved in the control of circadian rhythms9. Additionally, MLT is an antitumor agent, antioxidant, and immune system regulator10. MLT secretion decreases with age, particularly in AD patients11. Another study revealed that a decrease in MLT positively correlated with AD progression11,12. A study reported that plasma MLT concentrations were significantly lower in patients with AD than in controls without APOE ε4 allele13. Furthermore, an investigation demonstrated that the concentrations of MLT in the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) of patients with AD were significantly reduced compared with those in the control group. Moreover, among patients with AD, those expressing the apolipoprotein E-epsilon3/4 genotype exhibit significantly higher MLT levels than those expressing the apolipoprotein E-epsilon4/4 genotype14.

  Besides, a reduction in CSF MLT levels has been observed even in preclinical stages when patients do not display any cognitive impairment (Braak stages I-II), implying that this is an early indicator of AD15,16. Numerous studies demonstrated the therapeutic effects of MLT in patients with AD. Studies haverevealed that MLT might ameliorate the symptoms of AD by affecting the cholinergic system17–20, attenuating tau hyperphosphorylation21–24, regulating circadian rhythm20, inhibiting aging and enhancing self-healing ability25,26. Furthermore, the antioxidant properties of MLT make it a promising therapeutic candidate for AD, as it is a potent free radical scavenger16. Moreover, most in vitro and in vivo experiments have confirmed that MLT decreases the amyloid-beta (Aβ) toxicity and aggregation27–31. Another study demonstrated that MLT levels drop sharply in frail older adults, leading to nerve failure and dementia32, implying that MLT is inversely related to Aβ and tau protein. However, there is a lack of direct evidence establishing a relationship between MLT and Aβ or tau proteins in humans, and the biological roles of MRGs in AD treatment are unclear.

  Transcriptomics, single-cell sequencing, and Mendelian randomization (MR) studies are essential research methods. In recent years, transcriptome sequencing has become crucial for analyzing gene expression levels, identifying DGEs, exploring gene functions, and studying genetic evolution33,34,It has high sensitivity and canidentify low levels of molecules, including nuclear transcription factors (TFs)35. Moreover, single-cell sequencing offers significant opportunities to explore transcriptomics at the cellular level36, allowing for simultaneous study of different cell types, gene expression profiles, and communication pathways37. Furthermore, MR has been used extensively to study causal relationships. This approach uses genetic variants to assess the causality of an observed association between a risk factor and an outcome38. In MR studies, confounding bias is reduced because genetic variants are randomly assigned at birth, preventing reverse causation. This demonstrates the great potential of MR to infer causation from observational data. Compared with traditional biochemical methods, the approaches above are fast, inexpensive and efficient. Integratingthese analytical technologies has recently become more common for studying important factors involved in complex biological pathways.

  This study, explored the relationship between MRGs and AD by identifying important genes associated with AD using transcriptomics, single-cell sequencing, and MR research. Bioinformatics analysis of key genes was subsequently performed to explore immune microenvironment characteristics, regulatory mechanisms, and potential drugs, providing new research ideas for the studying and treating AD.


## Materials and methods

### Dataset access
  Single-cell mRNA expression profiling of three pairs of brain entorhinal cortexes from aged AD patients (AD1-AD6) and healthy individuals (Ct1-Ct6) in the GSE138852 (NAD=3, Ncontrol=3, Platform: GPL18573)39 dataset was performed via the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/), as well as the expression profiles of the entorhinal cortex in the GSE5281 (NAD=10, Ncontrol=13, Platform: GPL570)40 and GSE48350 (NAD=15, Ncontrol=39, Platform: GPL570) datasets41. Moreover, a total of 34 MLT-related regulators were extracted from previous studies42, as listed in Supplementary Table S1. The marker genes in each cell type were collected from public literature39 and the online website of the single-cell atlas of the entorhinal cortex in human AD (http://adsn.ddnetbio.com/) for cell annotation in GSE138852. As shown in Fig. 1, the flow chart for the study process is detailed.

  Fig. 1Workflow of this Study.


### Identification of key cell and DEGs in the single-cell RNA dataset GSE138852
  First, the quality control of the GSE138852 dataset was carried out with 300 ~ 1,500 genes (nFeature_RNA), and the proportion of mitochondrial genes (percent.mt) was less than 5% according to the “Seurat” package (version 4.1.0)43. After data standardization via the “NormalizeData” function, the top 2,000 highly variable genes were identified through the “FindVariableFeatures” function. Afterwards, principal component analysis (PCA) was performed to determine the principal components (PCs) for follow-up analysis. In the wake of dimensionality reduction, we created a uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP) plot to display the cell clusters in the AD and control groups44 (resolution = 0.1), followed by cell annotation and identification of key cells via Fisher’s test (odds ratio > 1, P < 0.05). Finally, the DEGs in key cells between the AD and control groups were obtained using the “FindMarkers” function (adj.P < 0.05, |avg_log2FoldChange|>0.5, min.pct = 0.1). The extent of cell‒cell communication in the AD and control samples was inferred with the help of the “Celltalker” package (version 0.0.7.900), and the functional analysis of key cells was further explored via the “ReactomeGSA” package (version 1.12.0)45.


### Authentication of DEGs and key modular genes related to MLT in GSE5281
  With the help of the “limma” package (version 3.54.0)46, the DEGs between the AD and control groups in GSE5281 were identified (adj.P < 0.05, |log2FoldChange|>0.5). The results of the differential expression analysis were visualized using volcano plots and heatmaps, which were generated with the “ggplot2” (version 3.4.1) and “ComplexHeatmap” (version 2.14.0) packages47, respectively. Subsequently, the single sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) algorithm in the “GSVA” package (version 1.46.0)48 was used to calculate the ssGSEA score in the AD and control samples based on 34 MLT-related regulators to ascertain the correlation between MLT-related regulators and disease. By closely following the ssGSEA score, weighted gene coexpression network analysis (WGCNA) was implemented to acquire the modular genes most strongly correlated with the ssGSEA score in GSE5281 using the “WGCNA” package (version 1.71)49. Briefly, the outlier samples were eliminated through sample clustering analysis, and then a soft threshold was used to construct a coexpression network with a maximum scale-free distribution. All genes were divided into several modules decorated with different colors, and the relevance of the ssGSEA score to these modules was estimated to determine the key modules with the highest correlation. In addition, the key modular genes were further filtered based on gene significance (GS) and module membership (MM) (MM > 0.7, GS > 0.7) and were named MRGs.


### Identification of biomarkers related to MLT in AD patients
  By means of overlapping DEGs of key cells in GSE138852, DEGs in GSE5281 and MRGs, the intersecting genes were regarded as candidate genes strongly connected with MLT in AD. To investigate the biological functions and signaling pathways involved in candidate genes, enrichment analysis was performed using the “clusterProfiler” package (version 4.2.2)50 based on Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) databases (P < 0.05)51–53. Furthermore, Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis was executed to single out key genes causally associated with AD using the “TwoSampleMR” package (version 0.5.6)54 and the inverse variance weighted (IVW) method55 based on the expression quantitative trait locus (eQTL) data of candidate genes (exposure factors) and genome-wide association study (GWAS) summary data of AD (outcome, ebi-a-GCST90027158) from the IEU OpenGWAS database (https://gwas.mrcieu.ac.uk/). MR‒Egger56, weighted median57, simple mode54 and weighted mode58 methods were also included in the causal inference analysis. SNPs significantly correlated with exposure factors were screened out as instrumental variables (P < 5 × 10− 8), which also lacked linkage disequilibrium (clump = TRUE, r2 = 0.001, kb = 60) and were irrelevant to outcome (proxies = TRUE, rsq = 0.8). Furthermore, the Steiger test and sensitivity analysis were adopted to evaluate the directionality and reliability of the MR results. In the end, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve and expression analyses were performed to identify the biomarkers for AD patients, in which the area under the curve (AUC) of the ROC curve must be greater than 0.7 and the expression trend of biomarkers between the AD and control groups must be in complete agreement coupling with statistical significance in the GSE5281 and GSE48350 datasets. Moreover, an artificial neural network (ANN) was constructed to assess the ability to distinguish patients with AD from control individuals, and ROC curve analysis was performed.


### Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) and immune infiltration analysis
  To explore the signaling pathways affected by biomarkers in AD, GSEA was performed according to the Spearman correlation of biomarkers with all the other genes (adj. P < 0.05). The KEGG gene set was defined as the background gene set, which was obtained from the Molecular Signatures Database (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/msigdb/). The five enrichment results with the strongest significance were visualized via the “enrichplot” package (version 1.18.0). Furthermore, the ssGSEA algorithm was also employed to estimate the abundance of 28 immune cells in the AD and control groups in the GSE5281 dataset, as well as the Spearman correlation between differential immune cells and biomarkers via the “psych” package (version 2.2.9), to determine which cells play an important role in the development of AD.


### Correlation analysis between biomarkers and metabolic pathways
  To explore the relationship between the incidence of AD and body metabolism, the correlations between biomarkers and metabolic pathways were assessed. The ssGSEA algorithm was used to estimate metabolic pathway-related scores based on metabolic pathway-related gene sets, including fatty acid metabolism, oxidative phosphorylation, adipogenesis, bile acid metabolism, hypoxia, heme metabolism, xenobiotic metabolism, cholesterol homeostasis and glycolysis, followed by correlation between biomarkers and differential metabolic pathways. In addition, the AlzData database (http://www.alzdata.org/) was utilized to analyze the expression levels of biomarkers in different brain tissues of AD patients and control individuals. The expression of biomarkers in key cells was also compared between the AD and control groups in the GSE138852 dataset.


### Construction of the regulatory network
  According to the NetworkAnalyst platform (https://www.networkanalyst.ca/), the transcription factors (TFs) corresponding to biomarkers were predicted based on the JASPER database. The TF-mRNA regulatory network was constructed via Cytoscape software (version 3.8.2)59. Furthermore, the DGidb (https://dgidb.org/) and DisGeNet (https://www.disgenet.org/) databases were used to predict small molecule drugs and neurological diseases corresponding to biomarkers, respectively. The prediction results were displayed using an mRNA-drug/disease network.


### Molecular docking between biomarkers and MLT
  Finally, molecular docking was adopted to research the interactions between biomarkers and MLT. This analysis was carried out based on the crystal structure of biomarkers and the three-dimensional structure of MLT, which were obtained from the Protein Data Bank (http://www.rcsb.org/) and PubChem Compound database (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/), respectively. The optimal conformation of biomarkers and MLT was obtained by the CB-Dock online molecular docking tool60, and then the results were visualized by PyMOL61.


### Statistical analysis
  R software (version 4.1.0) was used in this study (The first access to the software date was 11 October 2023, last access to the software date was 2 December 2023), Differences among variables were ascertained through the Wilcoxon test. If not specifically stated, P < 0.05 was regarded as statistically significant.


## Results

### Oligodendrocytes were regarded as key cells in GSE138852
  In the wake of quality control, a total of 12,653 cells and 10,850 genes in GSE138852 were screened out (Supplementary Figure S1A). The top 10 highly variable genes, such as ROBO2, CEMIP, and CLDN5, were labeled in the scatter diagram (Supplementary Figure S1B). With respect to PCA, the top 30 PCs were selected for follow-up analysis (Supplementary Figure S1C). Afterwards, the resulting nine cell clusters were classified into six cell types, including microglia, astrocytes, neurons, endothelial cells, oligodendrocytes and oligodendrocyte progenitor cells (OPCs) (Fig. 2A and B). The expression of marker genes in the six annotated cell lines is shown in a bubble diagram (Fig. 2C). The histogram shows the proportions of cell types in the AD and control samples, and oligodendrocyte cells were the most abundant in both groups (Fig. 2D). On the basis of the Fisher analysis, we discovered that oligodendrocytes could be key cells for lucubrating (odds ratio = 3.386, p < 0.001) (Supplementary Table S1). Importantly, there were 281 DEGs in oligodendrocyte cells between the AD and control groups, including 153 with upregulated expression and 128 with downregulated expression (Fig. 2E and F). Moreover, the cells in the AD group communicated through ligand‒receptor interactions more closely than did those in the control group (Supplementary Figure S1D), and the top 10 functional pathways with the greatest differences in oligodendrocyte cells between the two groups, such as MGMT-mediated DNA damage reversal, amino acid conjugation, and agmatine biosynthesis, are displayed in the heatmap (Supplementary Figure S1E).

### A total of 550 MRGs were acquired from the GSE5281 dataset
  A total of 3,490 DEGs were identified between the AD and control groups in GSE5281, of which 1,179 were upregulated and 2,311 were downregulated (Fig. 3A). The expression profiles of the top 10 up- and downregulated DEGs are shown via a heatmap (Fig. 3B). With regard to the ssGSEA score of MLT-related regulators in the AD and control groups, we considered the association between MPGs and AD due to the increase in the score (P < 0.001) (Fig. 3C). With respect to WGCNA, there was an outlier sample in GSE5281, namely, GSM119619 (Fig. 3D). When the optimal soft threshold (β) was 10 (R2 = 0.855), the network bordered a scale-free distribution (Fig. 3E). Then, eight modules were identified, and the blue (4,648 genes) and yellow (2,088 genes) modules were strongly correlated with AD (|Cor|≥0.79, P < 0.001) (Fig. 3F and G). After further screening, a total of 550 modular genes were identified as MRGs (Fig. 3H).

### GPRC5B, NFKBIA and RASSF4 are biomarkers related to MLT for AD
  After overlapping 281 DEGs in oligodendrocyte cells in GSE138852, 3,490 DEGs in GSE5281 and 550 MRGs, a total of 21 genes were regarded as candidate genes strongly related to MLT in AD (Fig. 4A). These candidate genes were enriched in 286 GO terms (such as myeloid cell homeostasis, neuron projection regeneration and regulation of the canonical Wnt signaling pathway) and 14 KEGG pathways (such as the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, the Apelin signaling pathway and the Toll-like receptor signaling pathway) (Fig. 4B and C). According to MR analysis between candidate genes and AD, only four genes (GPRC5B, METTL7A, NFKBIA and RASSF4) were causally related to AD based on the IVW method (P < 0.05), and these genes were risk factors for AD (hazard ratio > 1) (Fig. 4D). The sensitivity analysis and Steiger test confirmed the reliability and directionality of the MR data (Supplementary Table S2-S4&Figure S2A). We further identified three biomarkers for AD patients, namely, GPRC5B, NFKBIA and RASSF4, because of their greater than 0.88 AUC in the GSE5281 dataset (Fig. 4E) and their significantly increased expression in the AD samples in the GSE5281 and GSE48350 datasets (Fig. 4F). In addition, the ANN model accurately distinguished AD samples from control samples (Fig. 4G and H).

### Biomarkers, especially NFKBIA, play important roles in AD development
  By virtue of GSEA (Fig. 5A), we discovered that three biomarkers might be connected with Parkinson’s disease, oxidative phosphorylation and Huntington’s disease. GPRC5B and RASSF4 might participate in myocardial contraction, and NFKBIA and RASSF4 might be negatively correlated with AD development. Moreover, there was a significant difference in the enrichment score of 15 immune cells between the AD group and the control group (Fig. 5B and C). Compared with those in the control group, 14 immune cell types, such as T helper type 17 (Th17) cells, natural killer (NK) cells and immature B cells, were more abundant in the AD group, while CD56dim NK cells were less abundant. Moreover, we evaluated the correlation of these 15 differential cell lines with biomarkers (Fig. 5D). NFKBIA was most strongly correlated with natural killer cells (Cor = 0.802, P < 0.001), memory B cells (Cor = 0.797, P < 0.001) and T follicular helper cells (Cor = 0.790, P < 0.001). However, CD56dim NK cells were significantly negatively correlated with three biomarkers (|Cor|>0.447, P < 0.05).

### Biomarkers associated with brain metabolism are differentially expressed in brain tissues
  Additionally, we closely examined the expression of biomarkers in the entorhinal cortex, hippocampus, temporal cortex and frontal cortex of the brain, as well as in oligodendrocyte cells. The expression of GPRC5B, as well as that of NFKBIA, was markedly greater in the entorhinal cortex and frontal cortex of AD patients than in control individuals (Fig. 6A). Moreover, the expression levels of NFKBIA and RASSF4 in the temporal cortex were clearly greater in the AD group than in the control group. Similarly, the expression of biomarkers in oligodendrocyte cells was also markedly different between the two groups (Fig. 6B). The correlation between biomarkers and metabolic pathways revealed that three pathways, namely, oxidative phosphorylation, adipogenesis and heme metabolism, were markedly different between the AD and control groups (Fig. 6C). Oxidative phosphorylation was passively associated with three biomarkers (|Cor|>0.5, P < 0.05). Adipogenesis was positively correlated with biomarkers (Cor > 0.58, P < 0.001), and heme metabolism was positively correlated with GPRC5B and RASSF4 (Cor > 0.57, P < 0.01) (Fig. 6D).

  The TF-mRNA network included three biomarkers and 24 TFs (Fig. 7A). A total of 19 diseases corresponding to GPRC5B or NFKBIA were predicted, such as AD and hyperactive behavior for GPRC5B, cerebral ischemia, brain ischemia and others for NFKBIA. Overall, two biomarkers (GPRC5B and NFKBIA), nine drugs and 19 neurological diseases were incorporated into the mRNA-drug/disease network (Fig. 7B).

### MLT is a potential target of three biomarkers
  Finally, we assessed the interactions and binding modes of three biomarkers with MLT via molecular docking analysis. GPRC5B can bind to MLT with a binding energy of -6.2 kcal/mol through the amino acid residues TYR-195 and ARG-125 (Fig. 8A). NFKBIA might bind to MLT at a -5.0 kcal/mol affinity via the active residue D100 (Fig. 8B). RASSF4 also targeted MLT with a binding energy of -5.8 kcal/mol by interacting with the MET-137 residue (Fig. 8C).

## Discussion
  Considering that the current treatments for AD are insufficient to adequately meet clinical requirements, it is imperative to develop novel therapeutic options that are both clinically feasible and effective. MLT has demonstrated efficacy in treating AD and has been proven in numerous studies62–65. However, the therapeutic value of MRGs in AD remains unclear. Our study highlights the crucial role of oligodendrocytes in AD and reveals significant variations in their interactions with other neurocytes and intracellular pathways between AD patients and controls. These findings provide novel insights into AD pathogenesis. This study used single-cell sequencing, transcriptomics, and MR analysis to identify three key MRGs in the entorhinal cortex that may be crucial in AD. A reliable TF network of hub genes was constructed, and potential treatment chemicals were identified. This study aimed to improveour genetic understanding of MRGs in AD and offer valuable insights for treating patients.

  This study, identified three promising MLT related biomarkers (GPRC5B, NFKBIA, and RASSF4) in AD. The MR analysis revealed that all three MRGs exhibited positive slopes on the scatterplots, indicating that they are all risk factors for AD. However, studies on the relationship between these biomarkers and AD are rare. GPRC5B, an orphan receptor belonging to the G protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) family, contributes to neurogenesis66. Its neuronal enrichment is controversial at the mRNA level67,68 but consistent at the protein level, with the highest levels in the neocortex and hippocampus69. Murine GPRC5B affects synaptic formation and neurogenesis70, whereas rat GPRC5B may be involved in microglial activation71. A previous study also revealed that RNA editing of GPRC5B was associated with AD dementia, neuropathological measures and longitudinal cognitive decline72. However, research on the effect of GPRC5B in AD has rarely been reported, necessitating further investigation. NF-κBIα, another MRG member, interacts with REL dimers to inhibit the NF-κB/REL complex, which is involved in inflammation. Mutations upstream of NF-κB have previously been reported to affect NF-κB activity in patients with AD, leading to anatomical defects such as shrinkage of the entorhinal cortex and early AD limbic system73. NFKBIA degradation followed by phosphorylation is critical for NF-κB activation74. Furthermore,, NFKBIA has been reported to be upregulated in patients with AD75,76, implying that it is a potential target for AD research and treatment. RASSF4 is a member of RASSF protein family77. The function of RASSF4 remains elusive but it may be involved in tumor suppression78, early myogenic differentiation79 and cell proliferation80. However, information regarding its relationship with AD is scarce. This study suggests the potential relevance of RASSF4 in AD for the first time and warrants further investigation into the detailed mechanisms involved. Additionally, molecular docking studies demonstrated that these biomarkers bind well to MLT. However, there is no direct evidence to verify that MLT can regulate these MRGs.

  We performed a GSEA analysis to gain an in-depth understanding of these MRGs. We identified that these three key MRGs were mainly enriched in Parkinson’s disease (PD), the oxidative phosphorylation pathway, the transforming growth factor β (TGF-β) signaling pathway, the pathways involved in cancer, and cardiac muscle contraction. PD is the second most prevalent neurodegenerative disease after AD. A study investigating the comorbidity of AD and PD revealed a strong association between the two disorders81. Several studies have demonstrated that AD and PD share common pathogenic mechanisms, including the NO pathway, p62 dysfunction, neuroexcitotoxicity and oxidative stress82–85. Oxidative phosphorylation is another pathway enriched in MRGs in AD. The brain is a highly energy-consuming organ, and neurons rely mainly on the mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation system for energy86,87. In mild AD and amnestic cognitive impairment, decreased glucose metabolism due to oxidative damage impairs cognitive function and leads to synaptic dysfunction and neuronal death88.

  Furthermore, a primary pathological event in AD is mitochondrial dysfunction, characterized by alterations in mitochondrial morphology, generation of reactive oxygen species and impaired oxidative phosphorylation89–91. In AD neurodegeneration, Aβ and tau work together to inhibit the production and activity of mitochondrial respiratory complexes, leading to a dysfunctional oxidative phosphorylation system92, thereby aggravating AD. The TGF-β signaling pathway is also enriched in MRGs in AD. TGF-β is a pleiotropic cytokine crucial for embryonic development, physiological tissue homeostasis, and several pathological behaviors93. TGF-β1 is one of the three isoforms of the TGF-β superfamily and has been reported to have immunomodulatory and neuroprotective properties94. The absence of TGF-β1 promotes Aβaccumulation and contributes to the formation of neurofibrillary tangles95, underscoring its significance in AD96. Furthermore, the roles of other enriched pathways in AD, including those involved in cancer and cardiac muscle contractions, remainunclear. Therefore, further studies are required.

  Besides, we observed that oligodendrocytes may contribute to AD pathogenesis through cell population clustering analysis consistent with most existing studies97–99. In the central nervous system (CNS), the primary function of oligodendrocytes is to synthesize the myelin sheath, which accelerates nerve impulses and protects axons100. However, oligodendrocytes are susceptible to ischemic and proinflammatory changes in their environment, and their mortality provokes further demyelination far beyond the initial injury site, resulting in sensory, motor, cognitive, and autonomic nerve impairments101. Previous studies have indicated that myelin damage and oligodendrocyte loss in AD are a consequence of neuronal degeneration and contribute to the early phases of the disease, highlighting the significance of oligodendrocytes in its onset and progression102. We further compared the differences in intercellular communication between the AD and control groups and observed a significant decrease in intercellular communication in AD group. Previous findings have suggested that microglia might contribute to AD pathogenesis through ligand-receptor (LR) axis communication103,104.

  This study revealed that the reduction in LR axis communication oligodendrocytes, astrocytes, neurons, and OPC may also be involved in AD development.Previous studies have demonstrated that MLT can induce neural stem cells (NSCs) to differentiate into oligodendrocytes. Additionally, and MLT-pretreated NSCs can restore neuronal cells in PD and AD mouse models by increasing mitochondrial activity105. As a consequence, the modulation of oligodendrocyte function represents a promising area for therapeutic development in AD research. Furthermore, we identified three key MRGs that were significantly upregulated in oligodendrocytes. We hypothesized that MLT may improve AD through its impact on oligodendrocytes by modulating these three MRGs. Further research is required to fully understand this issue.

  Immune infiltration analysis revealed significant disparities in the infiltration of 14 types of immune cells between patients with AD and controls, with 13 upregulated and one downregulated. Among the upregulated immune cells, Th17 cells have been implicated as crucial mediators of AD development106. Their derived proinflammatory cytokines are expressed and upregulated in AD and other neurological diseases107–109. The neutralization of Th17 cells drives IL-17, leading to a decrease in Aβ--induced neuroinflammation and AD-like behavior106 consistent with our findings. Furthermore, we observed higher infiltration of NK cells in the AD group than in the control group. This contradicts previous studies that reported reduced blood NK cells in AD patients compared to controls110. Other studies have revealed decreased cytotoxic function and lower functional potential of NK cells in patients with AD than in healthy controls111–113.

  Moreover, reduced NK cell levels improve neuroinflammation and cognitive decline in a mouse model of AD114. These inconsistencies are likely the result of using different materials in these studies. Although our study focused on the entorhinal cortex dataset, previous studies have obtained results from the peripheral blood of patients with AD. Further research and robust evidence are required to address these inconsistent conclusions Besides,, our study identified CD56dim NK cells as the only immune cell subset downregulated in patients with AD. These cytotoxic effector cells primarily produce IFN-γ upon stimulation110. Although the specific impact of CD56dim NK cells on AD pathogenesis remains uncertain, prior research has demonstrated decreased levels of specific markers, such as IFN-γ, in NK cells from patients with AD110, implying that the presence and activity of these immune cells might be diminished in patients with AD.

  TF prediction was performed on three MRGs to construct the integrated regulatory network, identifying of 24 related TFs in the TF regulatory network. Almost all of these TFs have been confirmed to be correlated with AD. For instance, in hippocampal progenitors, AP-2γ, encoded by Tfap2c, promotes neuronal proliferation and differentiation. Its ablation in the adult brain has been associated with reduced neurogenesis, disrupted neural coherence, and cognitive deficits115. Furthermore, AP-2γ may suppress NFKBIA expression116 and enhance Th17 and Th1 cell activation117, implying that it may influence the neuroinflammatory process of AD by affecting NFKBIA. Further research is required to confirm this hypothesis. GATA2 is essential for activating neuroglobin, a protein that protects neuronal cells in AD118. Neuroglobin levels increase in the early and moderate stages of AD but decrease in the advanced stages, potentially due to reduced GATA2 levels. Further research is required to confirm this relationship119,120. Nuclear respiratory factor 1 (NRF1), a TF that activates genes important for mitochondrial function and biogenesis, may contribute to neurodegenerative diseases such as AD by affecting both mitochondrial and nonmitochondrial functions121. This study, demonstrated that NRF1 regulates RASSF4 despite the absence of a distinct connection between them. A previous study revealed that RASSF4 overexpression could downregulate the mitochondrial membrane potential in colorectal cancer122, suggesting that NRF1 may affect mitochondrial function by regulating RASSF4, therebyinfluencing AD. Additional analysesare required to confirm this hypothesis. Upstream stimulatory factor-1 (USF1) controls the expression of genes associated with lipid metabolism, including APOE and Aβ precursor proteins. Women with the USF1 haplotype GCGCAC are at higher risk of AD-related neuropathological lesions, particularly neuritic and late-stage penile plaques. Younger carriers of the CCGCAC haplotype are more susceptible to nonneuritic and diffuse penile plaques123. Consequently, USF1 may be a potential target for the AD treatment. The expression of EGR1 and β-secretase 1 (BACE1) plays an important role in Aβdeposition inhibiting EGR1 binding to the BACE1 promoter suppresses BACE1 expression. This resulted in a reduction in Aβ deposition and enhancement of memory function in rats with AD. Bioinformatic analysis revealed that most of the remaining TFs were associated with AD122,124–127. Overall, these24 TFs are potential targets for AD therapy, however, further studies are required to confirm how these TFs regulate MRGs in AD.

  Finally, to broaden the treatment of AD, the main MRG targets were submitted to the DGIdb (https://dgidb.org/) to predict drugs for AD. Nine candidate drugs were identified in this study. Of these, dioscin, wedelolactone and gefitinib have been demonstrated to have therapeutic efficacy in AD128–130. Dioscin and wedelolactone are plant extracts known for their safety, low toxicity, and beneficial effects on various diseases, and they improve AD symptoms primarily by regulating oxidative stress, inflammation and neurotoxicity128,131,132. Gefitinib, an EGFR inhibitor, has also been confirmed to be effective in alleviating AD by attenuating Aβ pathology and improving cognitive function130,133. These findings indicated that these drugs are potential candidates for AD treatment. However, the efficacy of severalsmall molecule other medicines in treating AD remains unclear, necessitating further studies. We alsopredicted diseases associated with these biomarkers using the DisGeNet database (https://www.disgent.org/), including hyperactive behavior for GPRC5B and various diseases such as cerebrovascular diseases and neurodegenerative disorders for NFKBIA. GPRC5B and NFKBIA are potential therapeutic targets for these conditions131–133.

  Our study has certain limitations, First, our research used data from various web pages across the Internet, which may have introduced variability and potential bias. Second, although our findings tindicated that GPRC5B, NFKBIA, and RASSF4 are significant MRGs involved in AD development, there is a paucity of literature exploring the relationship between these MRGs and AD. Consequently, our results require further validation using both in vitro and in vivo experiments. Third, we observed a higher infiltration of NK cells in the AD group than in the controls, which contradicts previous studies that reported a decrease in blood NK cells in patients with AD. This discrepancy requires further investigation to reconcile these findings. These limitations underscore areas that require further investigation within our ongoing research and present opportunities for other researchers teams to contribute to this field.


## Conclusion
  For the first time, we identified three key factors significantly associated with AD using transcriptome data analysis in conjunction with MR analysis. Notably, GPRC5B and RASSF4 have rarely been reported in previous studies on AD. Based on these findings, we conducted a systematic analysis of the biological processes and important signaling pathways enriched by MRGs. Furthermore, we investigated changes in immune cell infiltration and metabolic pathways in patients with AD and control subjects, revealing associations between differential immune infiltrating cells and key MRGs. Moreover, we identified the crucial role of oligodendrocytes in AD pathogenesis using single-cell analysis and analyzed intercellular communication while visualizing key cellular functions. Finally, by constructing regulatory networks involving MRGs and their corresponding TFs, we predicted the potential drugs for AD and their related targets using network pharmacology. Our findings not only improve our understanding of the mechanisms of MLT therapy for AD but also provide valuable insights into key MRGs, cells involved in disease progression, potential therapeutic drugs, and related biological pathways that may open up new avenues for treating AD. However, it is important to note that our study was based on existing reports, therefore. Therefore, clinical trials and basic research are warranted to validate these results.
