BACKGROUND: Hotspot mutations occurring in the p110alpha domain of the PIK3CA gene, specifically p110alphaH1047R/L increase tumor metastasis and cell motility in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC). These mutations also affect the transcriptional regulation of DeltaNp63alpha, a significant isoform of the p53 protein involved in cancer progression. This study attempts to investigate the transcriptional impact of p110alphaH1047R/L mutations on the PIK3CA/DeltaNp63alpha complex in TNBC carcinogenesis. METHODS: We performed site-directed mutagenesis to introduce p110alphaH1047R/L mutations and evaluated their oncogenic effects on the growth, invasion, migration, and apoptosis of three different TNBC cell lines in vitro. We investigated the impact of these mutations on the p110alpha/DeltaNp63alpha complex and downstream transcriptional signaling pathways at the gene and protein levels. Additionally, we used bioinformatics techniques such as molecular dynamics simulations and protein-protein docking to gain insight into the stability and structural changes induced by the p110alphaH1047R/L mutations in the p110alpha/DeltaNp63alpha complex and downstream signaling pathway. RESULTS: The presence of PIK3CA oncogenic hotspot mutations in the p110alpha/DeltaNp63alpha complex led to increased scattering of TNBC cells during growth, migration, and invasion. Our in vitro mutagenesis assay showed that the p110alphaH1047R/L mutations activated the PI3K-Akt-mTOR and tyrosine kinase receptor pathways, resulting in increased cell proliferation, invasion, and apoptosis in TNBC cells. These mutations decreased the repressing effect of DeltaNp63alpha on the p110alpha kinase domain, leading to the enhancement of downstream signaling pathways of PI3K and tyrosine kinase receptors and oncogenic transformation in TNBC. Additionally, our findings suggest that the physical interaction between the DNA binding domain of DeltaNp63alpha and the kinase domain of p110alpha may be partially impaired, potentially leading to alterations in the conformation of the p110alpha/DeltaNp63alpha complex. CONCLUSION: Our findings suggest that targeting the p110alphaH1047R/L mutations in TNBC could be a promising strategy for developing transcriptional-based therapies. Restoring the interaction between DeltaNp63alpha and the p110alpha kinase domain, which is disrupted by these mutations, may provide a new approach to treating TNBC.
