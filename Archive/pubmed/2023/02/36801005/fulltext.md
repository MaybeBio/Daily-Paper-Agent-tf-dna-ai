# Prolonged generation of multi-lineage blood cells in wild-type animals from pluripotent stem cells


## Summary
  Regenerating prolonged multi-lineage hematopoiesis from pluripotent stem cells (PSCs), an unlimited cell source, is a crucial aim of regenerative hematology. In this study, we used a gene-edited PSC line and revealed that simultaneous expression of three transcription factors, Runx1, Hoxa9, and Hoxa10, drove the robust emergence of induced hematopoietic progenitor cells (iHPCs). The iHPCs engrafted successfully in wild-type animals and repopulated abundant and complete myeloid-, B-, and T-lineage mature cells. The generative multi-lineage hematopoiesis distributed normally in multiple organs, persisted over 6 months, and eventually declined over time with no leukemogenesis. Transcriptome characterization of generative myeloid, B, and T cells at the single-cell resolution further projected their identities to natural cell counterparts. Thus, we provide evidence that co-expression of exogenous Runx1, Hoxa9, and Hoxa10 simultaneously leads to long-term reconstitution of myeloid, B, and T lineages using PSC-derived iHPCs as the cell source.


## Introduction
  It is well acknowledged that hematopoietic-lineage cells arise from endothelial intermediates via endothelial-to-hematopoietic transition (EHT) (Bertrand et al., 2010; Boisset et al., 2010; Choi et al., 2012; Ivanovs et al., 2017; Kennedy et al., 2012; Lange et al., 2021; Sugimura et al., 2017). Combinational expression of Gata2, Gfi1b, cFos, and Etv6 by viral carriers converts fibroblasts into endothelial-like cells that subsequently generate hematopoietic stem/progenitor cell (HSPC)-like cells in vitro, but the proportion of CD45+ cells is extremely low (Pereira et al., 2013). Experimental evidence shows that human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs) or adult dermal microvascular endothelial cells (hDMECs) transduced with FOSB-, GFI1-, RUNX1-, and SPI1-expressing lentiviral carriers can convert to human multipotent progenitor cells, which give rise to long-term blood multi-lineages via a serum-free instructive vascular niche (Sandler et al., 2014). Combinational expression of ERG, HOXA5, HOXA9, HOXA10, LCOR, RUNX1, and SPI1 delivered by viral carriers can promote multi-lineage hematopoiesis from human pluripotent stem cells in NSG mice (Sugimura et al., 2017). Reproducible and efficient methods for engraftable hematopoietic generation with a set of defined factors from pre-edited pluripotent stem cells (PSCs) are still under development.

  Runx1 is a master regulator of the EHT process (Chen et al., 2009; North et al., 2002). Combinational expression of exogenous Runx1 and Hoxa9 in mouse embryonic stem cells (mESCs) leads to the generation of hematopoietic progenitor cells (HPCs) that preferentially reconstitute functional T lymphopoiesis in vivo, indicating the important role of Hoxa9 in T-lineage generation (Guo et al., 2020). HOXA9 plays key roles in the generation and amplification of hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs) (Ramos-Mejia et al., 2014). HOXA10, another HOX family member, also promotes HSPC expansion and engraftment (Dong et al., 2020; Lawrence et al., 1996).

  In this study, we unveil that the coordinated expression of exogenous Runx1, Hoxa9, and Hoxa10 in mESCs results in generation of induced HPCs (iHPCs), which are engraftable and reliably contribute to myeloid-, B-, and T-lineage cells over 6 months in wild-type animals. The cell hierarchy and tissue distribution patterns of the generative myeloid, B, and T cells resemble those of their natural cell counterparts. This study provides de novo evidence that generative HPCs guided by Runx1, Hoxa9, and Hoxa10 co-expression in gene-edited PSCs can achieve prolonged multi-lineage hematopoiesis in vivo, which suggests an alternative method for the urgent generation of hematopoiesis.


## Results

### Construction of an inducible Runx1-Hoxa9-Hoxa10 embryonic stem cell line
  We have previously established a two-step method for reconstituting T lymphopoiesis from PSCs guided by Runx1 and Hoxa9 co-expression (Guo et al., 2020) and for generating B lymphopoiesis from a PSC line co-expressing Runx1, Hoxa9, and Lhx2 (Zhang et al., 2022). We further put forth the hypothesis that a combination of Runx1, Hoxa9, and additional transcription factors that are preferentially expressed in myeloid-lineage fate commitment or maintenance might simultaneously reconstitute myeloid, B, and T lineages in vivo through the same two-step induction scheme. Forced expression of Hoxa10 in HSPCs promotes myelopoiesis (Bhatlekar et al., 2018) and engraftment (Magnusson et al., 2007). Hoxa10 expression is turned on at the early hemogenesis stage and is preferentially sustained in adult HSPCs but is absent in iHPCs (Guo et al., 2020). Therefore, we combined Hoxa10, Runx1, and Hoxa9 and established an inducible tandem reverse tetracycline transcriptional activator (rtTA)-Tet-responsive element (TRE)-Runx1-p2a-Hoxa9-p2a-Hoxa10-HygroR DNA cassette. Then, the rtTA-TRE-Runx1-p2a-Hoxa9-p2a-Hoxa10-HygroR cassette was introduced into the Rosa26 locus of an mESC line (C57BL/6 background, CD45.2 strain) by homologous recombination. The derived cell line was defined as the iRunx1-p2a-Hoxa9-p2a-Hoxa10-ESC line (iR1A9A10-ESC line). To report the lineage cells from the iR1A9A10-ESC line, we inserted a GFP-PuroR DNA cassette into the Hipp11 locus of the mESC line (C57BL/6 background, CD45.2 strain) (Figure S1A). The recombinant clones were screened in the presence of puromycin (1 μg/mL) and hygromycin B (150 μg/mL). The drug-screened positive clones were further validated by RT-PCR (Figure S1B) and genomic sequencing (Figure S1C).


### iR1A9A10-ESC produces hematopoietic progenitors capable of reconstituting multi-lineage hematopoiesis in vivo
  To functionally assess the hematopoietic multi-lineage reconstituting potential of the iR1A9A10-ESC line, we followed a two-step scheme for generation of hematopoiesis (Guo et al., 2020). Following the scheme (Figure 1A), the iR1A9A10-ESC differentiation started from embryoid body (EB) formation. At day 2.5, we harvested the EBs and replated them into gelatinized plates to initiate mesoderm differentiation. Hemogenic endothelial cells (HECs) express some common markers for hematopoietic and endothelial cells, such as CD31, CD41, and c-kit (Boisset et al., 2010; Jankowska-Steifer et al., 2018; Zovein et al., 2008). The induced CD41low cells could generate different types of colonies, indicating their hematopoietic potential (Figures S2A and S2B). Then, we sorted the induced HECs (iHECs; CD31+CD41lowCD45−c-kit+CD201+) at different time points and replated them on the feeder line OP9-Dl1 cells. The highest number of hematopoietic colonies was obtained from the iHECs sorted at day 11 after doxycycline was added at day 6 (Figures S2C–S2E, top). So we sorted the phenotypic iHECs at day 11 (Figure 1B) (Zhou et al., 2016) and co-cultured them with OP9-Dl1 cells to derive iHPCs (Figure 1C). We observed a higher proportion of iHPCs at day 10 of co-culture (Figures S2E, bottom, S2F, and S2G). These iHPCs were sorted for colony-forming unit (CFU) assay to verify their hematopoietic potential. The colonies derived from iHPCs were of different types, such as CFU-G, CFU-M, CFU-GM, CFU-E, and CFU-GEMM (Figures 1D–1F). The results of single-cell RNA sequencing revealed that the iHPC population was heterogeneous. Some iHPCs expressed myeloid-lineage-related genes, including Ctsg (CTSG), Mpl (MPL), Hbb-b1 (HBB-B1), and Lyz2 (LYZ2), and the others expressed lymphoid-lineage-related genes, including Il7r (IL7R), Tcf7 (TCF7), Lck (LCK), and Tox (TOX) (Figures S2H–S2J). To further assess the engraftment potential of iHPCs, we transplanted iR1A9A10-ESC-derived iHPCs (5 million per mouse) into irradiated (6.5 Gy) C57BL/6 mice (8–10 weeks of age, CD45.1 strain) with a continuous supply of doxycycline water (1 mg/mL). Six weeks after transplantation, the induced myeloid (iMye) cells, induced B (iB) cells, and induced T (iT) cells, derived from iHPCs, appeared in the peripheral blood (PB) of iHPC recipients (Figure 1G). ### Kinetics of generative myeloid, B, and T cells in recipient PB
  Sustained multi-lineage hematopoiesis is a crucial index of a successful engraftment. To investigate the duration of iMye-, iB-, and iT-lineage cells in iHPC recipients, we bled the mice regularly to monitor the kinetics of iHPC-derived (GFP+CD45.2+) cells at 6, 8, 12, and 16 weeks after transplantation. We found that the iMye, iB, and iT cells indeed persisted in the PB of recipients at 8, 12, and 16 weeks after transplantation (Figure 2A). Notably, the total GFP+CD45.2+ cells were up to ∼34% in the PB cell chimeras at 6 weeks after transplantation and gradually decreased over time. At 16 weeks after transplantation, the total GFP+CD45.2+ cells decreased to an average of 10% (Figure 2B). At 6 weeks after transplantation, we observed as high as ∼82.6% CD11b+ iMye cells in the GFP+CD45.2+cells in PB, which then gradually decreased to ∼65% at 16 weeks after transplantation. Meanwhile, the percentage of iB cells in the generative cell population started at around 7% at 6 weeks after transplantation and increased over time. The proportion of iB cells was ∼18.9% at 16 weeks after transplantation. Furthermore, the percentage of iT cells in the generative cell population started at about 10% at 6 weeks after transplantation and increased to ∼17% at 16 weeks after transplantation (Figure 2C). The induced myeloid-, B-, and T-lineage cells could still be detected in the PB at 24 weeks after transplantation (Figure S3A). ### Transcriptome characterization of generative myeloid, B, and T cells at single-cell resolution
  To characterize the transcriptome landscape of the mature generative blood cells (iMye cells, iB cells, and iT cells), we performed 10× single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) of the generative cells (Ter119−GFP+CD45.2+) sorted from the PB of recipients at 16 weeks after transplantation. To stringently exclude any contaminant cells, we filtered and kept only the cells expressing GFP mRNA from the 10× genomics. A total of 968 GFP+ single cells formed five clusters and were annotated as CD8+ iT cells (C1), CD4+ iT cells (C2), iB cells (C3), iNeutrophils (C4), and iMonocytes (C5) (Figure 3A). The iT cells were defined based on the expression of surface-marker-encoding genes, including Cd3e (CD3e), Tcf7/Tcf1 (TCF7), Il7r/Cd127 (CD127), Lef1 (LEF1), Bcl11b (BCL11B), Cd3g (CD3g), Thy1/Cd90 (THY1/CD90), Cd3d (CD3d), Cd5 (CD5), and Ccl5 (CCL5). And then the C1 cluster was identified as CD8+ iT cells based on the expression of the surface-marker-encoding genes Cd8a (CD8a), Cd8b1 (CD8b), and Nkg7 (NKG7). The C2 cluster was identified as CD4+ iT cells by the expression of Cd4 (CD4) (Figures 3B, S3B, and S3E). The C3 cluster was identified as iB cells by the surface-marker-encoding gene Cd19 (CD19), which was expressed at the point of B-lineage commitment (Scheuermann and Racila, 1995), as well as Cd79a (CD79a) and Cd79b (CD79b), which are part of the B cell antigen receptor complex, guiding B cell differentiation and responding to antigen stimulation (Chu and Arber, 2001). Meanwhile, the iB cells were characterized by the expression of the genes Ly6d (Ly6D), Ebf1(EBF1), Mzb1 (MZB1), Pax5 (PAX5), Cd22 (CD22), Ikzf3 (IKZF3), Bcl11a (BCL11a), H2-Aa (H2-Aa), and H2-Eb1 (H2-Eb1) (Figures 3C, S3C, and S3E). The iMye cells were defined by the expression of Itgam/Cd11b (CD11b), which expresses primarily in myeloid cells and is essential in myeloid differentiation (Ripperger et al., 2015; Tan, 2012). The iMye cells were divided into C4 cluster (iNeutrophils) and C5 cluster (iMonocytes). The iNeutrophils were characterized by the expression of genes Ly6g (Ly6g), Csf3r (CSF3R), Trem3 (TREM3), and Csf1 (CSF1). The Ly6g gene, which encodes Ly6G, expresses exclusively in neutrophils as a specific marker separating neutrophils from other leukocytes (Lee et al., 2013). The iMonocytes were identified by the expression of genes Fcgr3 (FCGR3/CD16), S100a4 (S100A4), Ccl9 (CCL9), Ms4a6c (MS4A6c), and Csf1r (CSF1R) (Figures 3D, S3D, and S3E). To confirm these five clusters, we used more surface-marker-encoding genes to identify the related cell types (Figures 3E and S3E). We also used a published 10× sequencing dataset of wild-type murine PB cells as reference (Xie et al., 2020). Our C1–C5 populations from the PB of iR1A9A10-iHPC recipients resembled their wild-type counterparts at the transcriptome level (Figures 3F and S3F–S3H). ### The tissue distributions and subsets of generative myeloid, B, and T lineages resemble their natural counterparts
  Granulocytes, monocytes, macrophages, and dendritic cells belong to mature subsets of myeloid cells, which collectively play key roles in hematopoiesis, homeostasis, and pathogen defenses (De Kleer et al., 2014). To assess whether the generative myeloid cells resembled their natural myeloid counterparts, we analyzed the tissue distributions and immunophenotypes of the iMye cells in recipients. Mature neutrophils (CD172a+CD11b+Ly6G+), eosinophils (CD172a+CD11b+Siglec-F+), basophils (FcεRIα+), and monocytes (CD172a+CD11b+Ly6G−Siglec-F−CD115+) were detected in the PB of recipients at 16 weeks after transplantation. Induced monocytes in PB could be separated into two subsets, non-classical monocytes (Ly6Clow) and classical monocytes (Ly6Chigh), according to the expression of the surface marker Ly6C (Figures 4A and 4E) (Friedman, 2007; Wolf et al., 2019). We detected generative granulocytes (Gr1+CD11b+), monocytes (CD11b+Gr1−F4/80−), and macrophages (CD11b+Gr1−F4/80+) (Austyn and Gordon, 1981) in the bone marrow and spleen of the recipients at 16 weeks after transplantation (Figures 4B, 4C, 4F, and 4G). As antigen-processing and -presenting cells, dendritic cells play critical roles in adaptive immune responses and immunological memory. We also observed the existence of induced dendritic cells (CD3−CD19−CD49b−Siglec-F−CD90−Ly6C−CD64−MHCII+CD11c+) in the spleen of recipients at 16 weeks after transplantation (Figures 4D and 4H) (Brown et al., 2019). To assess the physiological competence of lymphopoiesis, we further analyzed the subsets of iB cells and iT cells. Mature B cell subsets, including B-1a, B-1b, FO B, and MZ B, were detected in the spleen of recipients at 16 weeks after transplantation (Figures 4I and 4J). We then analyzed the activation status of iT cells in the spleen of recipients. Flow cytometry analysis indicated the presence of effector iT cells (CD11b−Gr1−Ter119−CD19−B220−NK1.1−CD4+CD44+CD62L− or CD11b−Gr1−Ter119−CD19−B220−NK1.1−CD8+CD44+CD62L−), memory iT cells (CD11b−Gr1−Ter119−CD19−B220−NK1.1−CD4+CD44+CD62L+ or CD11b−Gr1−Ter119−CD19−B220−NK1.1−CD8+CD44+CD62L+), and naive iT cells (CD11b−Gr1−Ter119−CD19−B220−NK1.1−CD4+CD44−CD62L+ or CD11b−Gr1−Ter119−CD19−B220−NK1.1−CD8+CD44−CD62L+) at 16 weeks after transplantation (Figures 4K and 4L). ### Secondary transplantation assay shows long-term reconstitution capacity of iR1A9A10-iHPC-derived progenitors
  We analyzed the generative progenitor cells in the bone marrow of recipients. We observed generative myeloid progenitors (MPs; Lin−CD127−c-kit+Sca1−), including common MPs (CMPs; Lin−CD127−c-kit+Sca1−CD34+CD16/32low); granulocyte-monocyte progenitors (GMPs; Lin−CD127−c-kit+Sca1−CD34+CD16/32high); and megakaryocyte-erythrocyte progenitors (MEPs; Lin−CD127−c-kit+Sca1−CD34−CD16/32low) (Akashi et al., 2000) in the bone marrow of recipients at 6 weeks after transplantation (Figure 6A). As lineage-restricted progenitors, common lymphoid progenitors (CLPs) derive from HSCs and have the potential to differentiate into all types of lymphoid cells, including T cells and B cells (Lai and Kondo, 2008). We indeed found that generative CLPs (Lin−Sca1lowc-kitlowCD127+CD135−) occurred in the bone marrow of recipients at 6 weeks after transplantation (Figure 6B). Phenotypic pro-B cells (Lin−IgM−IgD−CD43+B220+) were also observed in the bone marrow of recipients at 6 weeks after transplantation (Figure 6C). Further, to verify the hematopoietic differentiation ability of these generative progenitors, we sorted these progenitors in the bone marrow of recipients at 6 weeks after transplantation to do a CFU assay. We found that these progenitors could generate different types of hematopoietic colonies (Figures 6D and 6E). Then we performed a secondary transplantation assay using the bone marrow cells from primary recipients (week 6). CD11b+ iMye cells, CD19+ iB cells, and CD3/CD4/CD8+ iT cells were detected in the PB of secondary recipients at 12 and 21 weeks after transplantation (Figure 6F). At 12 weeks after transplantation, iMye cells, iB cells, and iT cells also appeared in the SP and LN of secondary recipients (Figure 6G). Further, we sacrificed the secondary recipients and sorted the lineage cells from different tissues for genomic sequencing. The data showed that these generative lineage cells in different tissues of secondary recipients were derived from the iR1A9A10-ESC line (Figure 6H). ## Discussion
  In this study, we showed evidence that combinational expression of exogenous Runx1, Hoxa9, and Hoxa10 could guide mESC differentiation toward hematopoietic progenitors, transplantation of which reliably reconstituted multiple blood cell types, including myeloid cells, B cells, and T cells, in vivo in wild-type animals with normal competitor cells. Notably, the iHPCs first gave rise to lineage progenitors including MPs, CLPs, and pro-B cells, which resembled the hierarchical behavior of natural HPC differentiation. Moreover, we also identified the complete subtypes of myeloid cells and B cells and the physiologically competent naive T cells.

  To confirm the most suitable dose of iHPCs for transplantation, we tested a range of cell numbers of iHPCs (half of 1 million, 1 million, 3 million, 5 million). We found that 5 million iHPCs as the input is more suitable for transplantation to generate iMye cells, iB cells, and iT cells in vivo (Figures S4A and S4B). We transplanted iHPCs into the recipients, which were treated with 1 mg/mL doxycycline in the drinking water for 16 weeks after transplantation. At 16 weeks after transplantation, GFP+ cells were detected in the PB of recipients. When iHPC recipients were maintained on doxycycline for 8 weeks after transplantation and then switched to water without doxycycline, GFP+ cells could not be detected in the PB at 16 weeks after transplantation (Figure S4C). These data demonstrated that continuous supply of doxycycline is necessary to maintain the iR1A9A10-iHPC-derived lineage cells.

  Overexpression of ERG, HOXA5, HOXA9, HOXA10, LCOR, RUNX1, and SPI1 using a lentivirus cocktail at the hemogenic endothelium stage leads to multi-lineage differentiation (Sugimura et al., 2017). The method for obtaining HPCs by expressing exogenous genes at the hemogenic endothelium stage of human ESC differentiation also applies to mESC differentiation. In our differentiation system, the expression of only three transcription factors, Runx1, Hoxa9, and Hoxa10, at the hemogenic endothelium stage is able to induce the generation of HPCs that can generate multi-lineages in recipients after transplantation. The random insertion of these genes (ERG, HOXA5, HOXA9, HOXA10, LCOR, RUNX1, and SPI1) using a lentivirus into individual cells makes it difficult to reproducibly confirm which factors play essential roles during the hemogenesis and hematopoiesis stages at the single-cell resolution (Sugimura et al., 2017). To avoid expression variations and random genomic insertions introduced by viral delivery, we adopted precise gene-editing and built the iR1A9A10-ESC line, which is a valuable material for investigating hematopoietic induction from PSCs at the single-cell resolution. Moreover, we transplanted iR1A9A10-iHPCs into congenic wild-type mice, which were robust enough to investigate T cell generation and maturation. However, the human induced HSPCs were transplanted into immune-deficient mice (Sugimura et al., 2017), the xenothymus of which compromises human T cell development.

  In this study, synergistic expression of exogenous Runx1, Hoxa9, and Hoxa10 in mESCs resulted in generation of engraftable myeloid-, B-, and T-lineage cells in vivo in wild-type animals with normal competitor cells, arguing that these three factors are compatible for guiding simultaneous Mye/B/T lineage fate commitments from PSCs. Hoxa10 alone confers long-term multi-lineage hematopoiesis on human multipotent progenitor cells (Dong et al., 2020). However, expression of Hoxa10 alone in the mESCs in our hematopoietic differentiation system failed to generate iHPC-derived GFP+CD45.2+ cells in wild-type recipients. The expression of exogenous Runx1 and Hoxa10 in the mESCs could generate myeloid cells and T cells, but no B cells, in the PB of recipients (Figure S5). In our previous research, ectopic expression of Runx1 and Hoxa9 in PSCs guided T lymphopoiesis in vivo, while Runx1 expression alone in PSCs resulted in HPCs that eventually failed to generate T cells in vivo (Guo et al., 2020). Thus, Hoxa9 and Hoxa10 are crucial in determining multi-lineage engraftment (Lawrence et al., 1997). Some combinations of transcription factors can generate multiple lineages, while others can only dominate a preferential lineage, indicating that there is no common theory in engraftable generation of differential blood cell lineages.

  Previous evidence showed that co-expression of Runx1, Hoxa9, Hlf, and Hoxa7 could generate the B lineage dominantly and low proportions of the myeloid and T lineages in immunodeficient mice. However, the Runx1-Hoxa9-Hlf-Hoxa7-ESC-derived iHPCs contributed to multi-lineage only transiently (Yu et al., 2022). In 2002, Kyba and collaborators found that Hoxb4 expressed in ESCs (129SvEv background) had shown the ability to engraft in 129SvEv mice and generate myeloid, B, and T lineages over 15 weeks after transplantation, indicating that Hoxb4 could induce mESCs to HSPCs to some extent (Kyba et al., 2002). Then we inserted the Hoxb4 gene into the Rosa26 locus of our mESC line and studied its hematopoietic potential in the induction system. We constructed an inducible mESC line in the current C57BL/6 strain expressing Hoxb4 (iHoxb4-ESC line), an inducible mESC line synergistically expressing Runx1 and Hoxb4 (iR1B4-ESC line), an inducible mESC line synergistically expressing Runx1, Hoxa9, and Hoxb4 (iR1A9B4-ESC line), and an inducible mESC line synergistically expressing Runx1, Hoxa9, Hoxa10, and Hoxb4 (iR1A9A10B4-ESC line). We followed the two-step scheme to produce iHPCs. We found that these mESCs expressing Hoxb4 in our hematopoietic induction system could generate iHECs, but lost the capacity to generate robust iHPCs (Figure S6). Although we found no evidence of the existence of generative HSCs in our study, the iHPCs derived from iR1A9A10-ESCs reconstituted myeloid, B, and T lineages in C57BL/6 mice over 6 months after transplantation (Figure S3A). We cannot rule out the possibility that the induction system in vitro lacks the conditions to maintain long-term HSCs. Compared with conventional cell cultures, 3D culture systems can mimic tissue environments and provide biocompatibility, bioactivity, and biomechanical stiffness, which may provide a more favorable environment for HSC expansion and maintenance in vitro (Bourgine et al., 2018; Bruschi et al., 2022; Huang et al., 2016; Tomimori et al., 2000). We may introduce the 3D culture systems into our hematopoietic differentiation system to obtain functional HSCs in the future.

  Point mutations, amplifications, and translocations of Runx1 are often associated with leukemia (De Braekeleer et al., 2009; Niini et al., 2000; Osato, 2004; Otalora-Otalora et al., 2019). Hoxa9 and Hoxa10 are highly expressed in acute myeloid leukemias (Collins and Hess, 2016; Lambert et al., 2019; Shah et al., 2016; Wang et al., 2020). Interestingly, the iR1A9A10-ESC-derived iHPC recipients showed no leukemogenesis and tumors over time. It seems that artificially manipulating these transcription factors starting from early hemogenesis produces iHPCs that produce tumor-free hematopoiesis after engraftment.

  In conclusion, this study establishes a novel approach for simultaneously reconstituting generative myeloid, B, and T lineages in vivo from PSCs guided by co-expression of Runx1, Hoxa9, and Hoxa10. Our study provides insights into reconstituting generative hematopoiesis using a PSC source.

### Hematopoietic differentiation
  This method follows an induction scheme developed previously (Guo et al., 2020). The ESCs were trypsinized by 0.05% trypsin-EDTA (Gibco) and resuspended in the basic differentiation medium (BDM) (IMDM, 15% fetal bovine serum [FBS] [Gibco], 200 μg/mL iron-saturated transferrin [Sigma], 0.45 mM monothiolglycerol [Sigma], 1% GlutaMAX [Gibco], and 50 μg/mL ascorbic acid [Sigma]). To remove the feeder layers, ESCs were plated into 0.1% gelatin-coated (Merck Millipore) wells, and the floating cells were collected after 30 min. For EB generation, the ESCs were resuspended at 100,000 cells/mL in the BDM supplemented with 5 ng/mL BMP4 (Peprotech) and plated at 20 μL/drop for inverted culture in 15 cm dishes. At day 2.5, EBs were replanted into gelatinized plates in BDM supplemented with 5 ng/mL BMP4 and 5 ng/mL VEGF (Novoprotein). At day 6, the medium was changed to BDM supplemented with 2% conditioned medium derived from the supernatants of AFT024-mIL3, AFT024-mIL6, AFT024-hFlt3L, and AFT024-mSCF cell culture. Doxycycline (1 μg/mL [Sigma]) was added at day 6. The medium was replaced every other day. The plates were seeded with OP9-Dl1 cells (20,000 cells/well, 12 well plate) 12 h prior to the hematopoietic maturation step in EM medium (α-MEM, 15% DFBS [Hyclone], 200 μg/mL iron-saturated transferrin, 0.45 mM monothiolglycerol, 1% GlutaMAX, 50 μg/mL ascorbic acid, 2% conditioned medium derived from supernatants of AFT024-mIL3, AFT024-hFlt3L, and AFT024-mSCF cell culture, and 1 μg/mL doxycycline). Then, 2,000–5,000 sorted iHECs were seeded into each well for hematopoietic maturation. The EM medium was half replaced every 2 days.


### Transplantation of iHPCs
  Eight- to ten-week-old C57BL/6 mice (CD45.1 strain) were sublethally irradiated (6.5 Gy) by an X-ray irradiator (RS2000 [Rad Source]), and 5 million iR1A9A10-ESC-derived iHPCs were injected into each irradiated C57BL/6 mouse via the retro-orbital vein. The mice were fed with water containing co-trimoxazole (Tianjin Lisheng Pharmaceutical) for 2 weeks. The recipients were fed with water containing doxycycline (1 mg/mL [Sigma]) after transplantation with iHPCs.


### CFU assay
  The sorted cell populations were suspended in IMDM with 2% FBS (Hyclone) and cultured in Methocult GF M3434 (STEMCELL Technologies) on 35 mm Petri dishes. Doxycycline (1 μg/mL) was added to the culture medium to induce the expression of Runx1, Hoxa9, and Hoxa10. Cultures were maintained in an incubator in a controlled atmosphere with a temperature of 37°C and 5% CO2 concentration. The colonies were identified using an inverted microscope. At day 10, different types of colonies growing in the culture were counted.


### Genomic sequencing
  One to one thousand sorted CD11b+ iMye cells, CD19+ iB cells, and CD3/CD4/CD8+ iT cells were used for genome extraction (Discover-sc Single Cell WGA Kit [Vazyme]). Genomic PCR was performed using primer pairs flanking the inserted Runx1-p2a-Hoxa9-p2a-Hoxa10 sequence element and a genome template (200 ng) of 1–1,000 sorted iMye cells, iB cells, and iT cells from the primary and secondary recipients. The sequencing results were visualized by Chromas software.


### RNA-seq and data analysis
  The PB cells (Ter119−GFP+CD45.2+) from recipients at 16 weeks after transplantation were used for scRNA-seq. Droplet-based scRNA-seq datasets were produced using a Chromium system (10× Genomics, PN120263) following the manufacturer’s instructions. Raw sequencing of droplet-based scRNA-seq datasets was processed and aligned to the Mus musculus genome assembly (mm10) using Cell Ranger software (v.4, 10× Genomics) and subjected to Seurat (v.3.2.3) to perform subsequent quality control (QC) and secondary analysis. The raw data were uploaded to the Gene Expression Omnibus public database (GSE189736). To pass QC, cells were required to have GFP expression and at least 1,000 detected and less than 10% of aligned reads mapping to mitochondrial genes. And genes were required to express in >10 cells. Nine hundred sixty-eight cells that passed QC were used for downstream analysis. Normalization of unique molecular identifiers (UMI) values was applied using the “LogNormalize” and “ScaleData” methods in Seurat. Then, dimension reduction by principal-component (PC) analysis using 2,000 highly variable genes (as defined by Seurat) and clustering was done on PC values of the most variable PCs. The most variable PCs were determined by the Jackstraw method. Then, the first 18 PCs were used for clustering and uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP) dimension reduction analysis with resolution of 0.2 and other default parameters. Further, to specify myeloid subpopulations, we divided the iMye population into C4 and C5 populations. The C4 and C5 populations were identified by implementing new clustering and dimension reduction analysis using the first 20 PCs and resolution of 0.2. We annotated subpopulations by important marker gene expression (neutrophils, Ly6g; monocytes, S100a4 and Ccl9). Violin and dot plots for gene expression were plotted using the VlnPlot function of Seurat and the ggplot2 package.


### Statistical analysis
  Data are presented as means ± SD calculated by GraphPad Prism. All data are expressed as the mean, and exact n numbers for each dataset are detailed in the figure legends.
