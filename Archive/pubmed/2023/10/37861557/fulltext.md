# Exploring the mechanism of action of Qian Lie Xing Fang during the treatment of benign prostatic hyperplasia via network pharmacology and molecular dynamics simulation analyses


## Abstract
  This study aimed to explore the historical research progress on benign prostatic hyperplasia from the perspective of traditional Chinese medicine theory and the treatment of benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) with Qian Lie Xing Fang (QLXF) via the warming and tonifying of kidney yang, promotion of blood circulation, and clearing of meridians. First, network pharmacology analysis was used to screen and identify possible pathways for BPH treatment with QLXF. Subsequently, molecular docking analysis helped explore the mechanism of action by which the components of QLXF affected androgen receptor (AR) and type 5 phosphodiesterase inhibitor (PDE-5) levels. Targets for treatment with QLXF were identified from the online Mendelian inheritance in man and DisGeNET databases. BPH-related genes were identified using GeneCards and online Mendelian inheritance in man databases, and their intersection was used to construct a protein–protein interaction network analysis graph. Subsequently, gene ontology and Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analyses were performed. The semiflexible docking of the ingredients of QLXF acting on the 2 targets was performed via molecular docking and molecular dynamics simulation, to elucidate the mechanism of action by which the active ingredients affect AR and PDE-5 levels further. This enabled us to explore the pattern of interactions between small active ingredient molecules, the target protein, and the stability after binding at the microscopic level. Gene ontology enrichment analysis showed that QLXF affected several processes, such as DNA transcription factor binding, kinase binding, protein homodimerization activity, protein structure domain-specific binding, and protein-coupled amine receptor activity in BPH patients. KEGG results showed that chemical carcinogenic reactive oxidative species and the JAK-STAT, Pl3k-Akt, FoxO, NF-κB, and other pathways were significantly enriched. Conducting molecular docking studies to investigate the interaction of active components from QLXF with AR and PDE-5, it was found that MOL002260 may possess the potential to inhibit PDE-5 activity, while MOL010578 may exhibit the capability to inhibit AR activity. QLXF is closely associated with various biological processes and KEGG signaling pathways related to BPH. The active ingredients of QLXF were investigated for their interactions with AR and PDE-5, with a primary focus on the small molecules MOL002260 and MOL010578.


## 1. Introduction
  Benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) is the most common prostate disease affecting older males.[1] Many males over 40 years old have some symptoms of prostate hyperplasia, such as difficulty urinating and incomplete urination. The incidence of BPH increases year by year with an increase in the age of males, and the symptoms worsen gradually, causing serious impediments in daily life.[2] There are numerous treatment modalities for BPH, including watchful waiting, behavioral therapy, Western medicine treatment, and surgical intervention. Currently, the medications used for treating BPH mainly include: (1) α-adrenergic receptor blockers, (2) 5α-reductase inhibitors, (3) M receptor antagonists, and (4) type 5 phosphodiesterase inhibitors (PDE5i).[3] The common surgical methods include transurethral resection of the prostate, transurethral vaporization of the prostate, and transurethral incision of the prostate.[4]

  Although the underlying mechanisms of BPH are not yet fully understood, various pathogenic processes have been proposed, including oxidative stress, inflammation, dysregulation of proliferation and apoptosis, as well as hormonal imbalances.[5] In fact, the androgen pathway is considered predominant. Dihydrotestosterone (DHT) is a potent prostatic androgen that can bind to androgen receptors (ARs), initiating gene transcription for differentiation and growth-promoting factors. Subsequently, this process stimulates prostate proliferation.

  Qian Lie Xing Fang (QLXF) is a Hubei traditional Chinese medicine formula developed by Professor Wang Chaoyang. It was specifically designed to treat clinical symptoms such as difficulty urinating and incomplete urination. It has notable therapeutic effects and good prospects in clinical practice. The prescription consists of Epimrdii Herba, Cuscutae Semen, Radix Bupleuri, Radix Salviae, Sparganii Rhizoma, Curcumae Rhizoma, Fritillariae Thunbrgii Bulbus, Thalluslaminariae, Sargassum, Hedysarum Multijugum Maxim, Plantaginis Semen, Vaccariae Semen, Radix Rhei Et Rhizome, Cyathulae Radix, and Eupolyphaga Steleophaga.

  The model for the targeted development of a single drug has been challenged because of the complexity of the pathogenesis of clinical diseases and has been associated with limitations such as drug resistance development and low efficacy.[6] Therefore, it might be more effective to select natural compounds that act on 2 or more targets.[7] Network pharmacology is a new drug exploration strategy based on herbal and compound therapeutic targets, which has the advantage of improving clinical efficacy while minimizing side effects and toxicity.[8] This method is considered to be an alternative and novel approach to drug development that elevates the existing research on “single drug targets” to that of “a drug with multiple targets involving multiple signaling pathways”[9,10] Network pharmacology has been an important aspect of research on the molecular basis of traditional Chinese medicine and its formulas in recent years. Holism is an important perspective provided by Chinese medicine and traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) for disease treatment. It is emphasized in the occurrence, development, and treatment of diseases. Traditional Chinese medicine formulas are also multi-component therapies and might provide a direction and reference for treating complex diseases. This emerging network science offers an opportunity to identify the active ingredients, possible targets, and possible molecular mechanisms of TCM multi-component therapy.[11] The organic combination of the network and TCM could facilitate the exploration of the scientific basis of TCM therapy. Many effective drugs do not act on a single target. Hence, the characteristic of network pharmacology analysis of traditional Chinese medicine is that it is different from the current drug research process focused on “one drug, one target, one disease,” and has transformed into a research method focused on “network targets and multiple components.” The origin of network pharmacology analysis of Chinese medicine can also be traced back to 2007 when Li Shao used bioinformatics methods in China to propose a framework for the generation of traditional Chinese medicine prescriptions based on a biological network, to explore the relationship between traditional Chinese medicine prescriptions, syndromes, and the synergistic effect of compound prescriptions, which resulted in a new method for the study of traditional Chinese medicine prescriptions. Li Shao et al subsequently researched cold and heat syndromes and cold and heat formulas using network pharmacology analysis, thereby proving the effectiveness of this method. Network pharmacology is used in traditional Chinese medicine to predict the material basis, potential targets, and efficacy of traditional Chinese medicine. Second, it can be used in combination with molecular biology experiments to elucidate the pharmacological mechanism of action. It can also facilitate the assessment of toxic and side effects of traditional Chinese medicine-related ingredients and help design new formulations.

  Owing to the extensive pharmacological effects of QLXF and the complex pathogenesis of BPH, network pharmacology analysis and experimental verification methods were used to provide an understanding of the mechanism by which QLXF can be used for treating patients with prostate hyperplasia thoroughly.


## 2. Experimental methods

### 2.1. Related databases and software
  The following traditional Chinese medicine and bioinformatics-related databases were used during the study: traditional Chinese medicine systems pharmacology database and analysis platform (TCMSP) (https://old.tcmsp-e.com/tcmsp.php), Swiss Target Prediction Tool (http://www.swisstargetprediction.ch/), Protein Interaction Database STRING (https://string-db.org), Protein database UniProt (https://www.uniprot.org/), online Mendelian inheritance in man (OMIM) database (https://omim.org/), Gene cards database (https:/www.genecards.org), feature enrichment analysis tool Metascape (http://metascape.org/), protein structure database RCSB PDB (http://www.rcsb.org/), and Swiss model, a protein modeling tool (https://www.swissmodel.expasy.org/). The related software used in the study were as follows: ChemWin 5.0 chemical structure formula drawing software, Open Babel 2.4.1 chemical data conversion tool, Pyrx 0.8 molecular docking software, VMD 1.9.3 visualization molecular dynamics software, NAMD 2.13b molecular dynamics simulation software, and Discovery Studio 2020 software for drug discovery and biomolecular calculation simulation.


### 2.2. Collection of drug structure-related information and prediction of targets for treatment with QLXF
  The OMIM and DisGeNET (https://www.disgenet.org/) databases were searched to identify targets for treatment with QLXF. The SMILES structural formula of the active ingredients of QLXF was obtained for target prediction using the [12]PubChem database (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov). Potential targets for the active ingredients of QLXF were acquired using [13]Swiss Target Prediction (http://swisstargetprediction.ch/), [14]ChEMBL (https://ebi.ac.uk/chembl/), [15]CTD (http://ctdbase.org/), and [16]PharMapper (http://www.lilab-ecust.cn/pharmmapper/). The Swiss Target Prediction database was launched in 2014 and is a network tool that is widely used for predicting small protein targets. The acquired SMILES structure was imported into the Swiss Target Prediction database, and the target was filtered using a probability value > 0 as the limiting condition. The ChEMBL, CTD, and PharMapper databases were used to identify corresponding targets. After reintegrating the targets collected from the 4 databases, they were calibrated as human source targets.


### 2.3. Analysis of BPH-related genes
  The [17]GeneCards and OMIM databases were used for the analysis of [18]BPH-related genes. The database is an online tool that is regularly updated and contains information about human genes and genetic diseases and can be used to predict human gene function. BPH-related genes were searched in the above databases using the keyword “Benign static hyperplasia” and limiting the species to “Homo sapiens.” The search results of the 2 databases were merged to acquire disease targets.


### 2.4. Construction of protein interaction networks and screening of central nodes
  QLXF treatment-related targets and BPH-related targets were intersected, and the results were visualized using EVenn (http://www.ehbio.com/test/venn/). Network topology analysis was conducted using an online platform for network analysis, that is, STRING (https://stringdb.org/). The relevant targets of BPH and QLXF were uploaded onto the STRING platform, the multiple proteins tool was selected, the species was limited to humans, and a protein target interaction network (PPI) with a confidence interval >0.7 was obtained.[19,20] Cytoscape is an application that helps visualize molecular connections and biological processes (BP). Potential active components and BPH targets were imported into Cytoscape 3.8.2 to construct a network of BPH components and targets. In a composite target network, each component or target is represented by a node, and connected lines represent the relationship between components and targets. The PPI network was imported into Cytoscape 3.7.1, and the CytoNCA plugin was used to analyze the degrees of freedom (degree), intermediate centrality (BC), and near centrality (cellular components [CC]). Targets with a degree > 2 times the median, for which both the BC and CC values exceeded the median were considered as core nodes for further research.


### 2.5. GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis
  Gene ontology (GO) is a continuously updated and internationally applicable gene functional classification system that can be used to analyze the functional enrichment of genes and proteins. Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis thoroughly explains different gene and protein functions by facilitating the identification of enriched biochemical pathways in the dataset. Metascape is an open online analysis platform designed to provide a one-stop solution for gene function annotation and analysis to life science researchers. This database integrates public databases, including GO, KEGG, Reactome, CORUM, etc, and utilizes advanced algorithms and analysis tools to provide efficient bioinformatics data mining and visualization analysis services. GO and KEGG enrichment analyses were used to assess common targets on the Metascape platform. The top 20 BP, CC, and molecular functions were selected during GO analysis with a P value < 0.05. KEGG analysis results were visualized using the R language.


### 2.6. Target protein treatment
  Structural information regarding proteins with the PDB IDs 3g1r and 1z95 were downloaded from the RCSB PDB database. Notably, 3g1r is a complex of the type 5 phosphodiesterase inhibitor (PDE-5) protein and small molecules such as finasteride, which is a therapeutic drug used for treating prostate hyperplasia, and has an inhibitory effect on PDE-5.[21] It can inhibit the conversion of PDE-5 into DHT through testosterone, thereby reducing the concentration of DHT in the serum and prostate. However, long-term medication consumption may lead to common adverse reactions, including sexual dysfunction-related problems, such as erectile dysfunction, abnormal ejaculation, and low libido. The crystal structure contains 2 identical PDE-5 protein peptide chains. The B chain and other small molecules in the structure were removed before molecular docking. The binding cavity of finasteride in the A-chain was used as the docking site, and this small molecule was considered a positive control. Notably, 1z95 is a complex of the AR protein and small molecules such as bicalutamide. Bicalutamide is an antiandrogenic drug used for the treatment of advanced prostate cancer. This drug is an AR inhibitor and can inhibit male hormones by binding to cytoplasmic male hormone receptors in target tissues. The crystal structure contains AR protein peptide chains and small bicalutamide molecules. AR was extracted and used as the receptor protein, the binding cavity of bicalutamide was used as the docking site, and bicalutamide was considered as the positive control. The missing residues in PDE-5 and AR were identified using SWISS-MODEL homologous modeling.


### 2.7. Molecular docking analysis
  Using Autodock software for docking, ligand library molecules and positive control molecules were hydrogenated and charged, point charges were calculated using Open Babel, and results were saved as a pdbqt file. The extracted original ligand, which was used as the docking center, helped us to determine that the box size was composed of 40 * 40 * 40 grid points, and the distance between each small grid point was 0.0375 nm. Default values were set for other parameters. While processing docking receptors, and performing hydrogen addition, charge addition, and point charge calculation, the results were stored as a pdbqt file. Custom scripts were used for batch docking, binding energy values were sorted from low to high after docking, small molecule binding conformations lower than the original ligand score were extracted, and the results were saved as PDB files. The acquired small molecule conformations were restored into the binding pocket, Discovery Studio was used to perform receptor–ligand interaction analysis and three-dimensional and two-dimensional interaction diagrams were generated to analyze the primary functional residues and forces.


### 2.8. Molecular dynamics simulation
  The stability of NAMD was determined by performing 30 ns molecular dynamics simulations with the docked composite conformation. Topology and parameter files were generated for different ligands using Gaussian and Paratool plugins in VMD. The Psfgen plugin was used to generate a protein-ligand complex topology file, and TIP3P water boxes were included around the complex. The minimum distance between the complex and the box boundary was set at 1 nm, Na+ and Cl− were included to set the NaCl concentration at 0.15 mol/L and make the overall charge neutral. After the model was constructed, 5000 steps of energy minimization were performed, followed by 100 ps of constrained dynamics simulation. Finally, a constant temperature and pressure (NPT) ensemble was used along with 30 ns of free motion simulation at a temperature of 310 K and pressure of 1 atm. The force field used in the simulation was CHARMM36, with a time step of 2 fs and periodic boundary conditions. Default values were set for other parameters.


## 3. Experimental results

### 3.1. Common target information between QLXF and BPH
  The names of each traditional Chinese medicine in the QLXF Formula were entered into the TCMSP database, and parameters such as oral bioavailability and drug-like activity were used for screening. The values of oral bioavailability and drug-like activity were set at ≥30% and ≥0.18, respectively, and target proteins corresponding to the active ingredients of each drug were screened and imported into the database. Using the TCMSP database and literature review, we identified 227 traditional Chinese medicine targets in QLXF. Finally, as shown in Figure 1, 177 non-repetitive potential targets were screened through the intersection of the QLXF and BPH disease targets.


### 3.2. PPI network construction and analysis
  Information was uploaded to the STRING database, and a PPI network consisting of 32 nodes and 94 edges was constructed. Common targets were visualized to create a PPI network. As shown in Figure 2, the larger the node in the graph, the higher the degree value, and the greater the likelihood of it being the core goal. We selected the top 10 targets with specific degree values for BC and CC.


### 3.3. GO analysis
  We conducted GO and pathway enrichment analyses of candidate targets to explore the molecular mechanism by which QLXF had suppressive effects on BPH. The enrichment analysis results for graphite oxide were explained from the perspective of BP, cell compositions, and molecular functions.

  As shown in Figure 3, the results of molecular functional analysis revealed information regarding the following: DNA binding, transcription factor binding, kinase binding, protein homodimerization activity, protein domain-specific binding, G protein-coupled amine receptor activity, kinase regulator activity, protein kinase activity, oxidoreductase activity, cytokine receptor binding, phosphatase binding, nuclear receptor binding, adrenergic receptor activity, protein heterodimerization activity, steroid binding, serine hydrolase activity, antioxidant activity, nitric-oxide synthase regulator activity, histone kinase activity, protease binding, and core promoter sequence-specific DNA binding.

  As shown in Figure 4, The analysis results of biological process analysis revealed information regarding the following: responses to hormones, cellular response to organic cyclic compounds, cellular responses to lipids, reaction to inorganic substances, responses to exogenous stimuli, responses to radiation, responses to ketone gland development, positive regulation of cell death, circulatory system process, inflammatory response, response to nutrient levels, inflammatory responses, responses to growth factors, responses to wounds, cellular responses to cytokine stimulus, and regulation of the apoptotic signaling pathway.

  As shown in Figure 5, The analysis results of functional cellular analysis revealed information regarding the following: membrane raft, transcription regulator complex, vesicle lumen, cyclin-dependent protein kinase holoenzyme complex, receptor complex, outer organelle membrane, extracellular matrix, perinuclear region of the cytoplasm, dendrite, GO:0035189:RB-E2F complex, endocytic vesicle, apical part of the cell, serine protease inhibitor complex, nuclear envelope, endoplasmic reticulum lumen, platelet alpha granule lumen, transcription repressor complex, side of the membrane, serine-type peptidase complex, and focal adhesion.


### 3.4. KEGG analysis
  As shown in Figure 6, KEGG enrichment analysis results revealed information about the following: pathways in cancer, lipids, and atherosclerosis, AGE-RAGE signaling pathways in diabetic complications, JAK-STAT signaling pathway, Pl3k-AKT signaling pathway, chemical carcinogenesis, and reactive oxygen species, Leishmaniasis, platinum drug resistance, FoxO signaling pathway, transcriptional misregulation in cancer, NF-kappa B signaling pathway, Shigellosis, insulin resistance, thyroid hormone signaling pathway, amebiasis, ErbB signaling pathway, malaria, prolactin signaling pathway, calcium signaling pathway, and serotonergic synapse.


### 3.5. Molecular docking
  Data for a total of 227 active ingredients of QLXF were collected, and molecular docking of the active ingredients and target proteins was performed using Autodock software. The structure formed after the docking of PDE-5 with MOL002260, a small molecule with the lowest binding energy, is shown in Figure 7. The binding energy was −12.7 kcal · mol−1. The small molecule was named [(2R, 3S)-2-(3,4-dihydroxyphenyl)-6-[(2R, 3R, 4S)-2-(3,4-dihydroxyphenyl)-3,5,7-trihydroxy-3,4-dihydro-2H-chromen-4-yl]-5,7-dihydroxy-3,4-dihydro-2H-chromen-3-yl] 3,4,5-trihydroxybenzoate. The results indicate that MOL002260 exhibits good activity after it was docked with PDE-5. The residues that form hydrogen bonds include ILE229, GLU310, LEU312, TRP230, NAP943, VAL309, and residues that form Pi–Pi interactions include TYR132, and LEU311, while residues that form Pi-sulfur interactions include MET313. Notably, these residues played an important role in the binding process.

  The structure obtained after the docking of AR and the small molecule MOL010578, which has the lowest binding energy, is shown in Figure 8. The binding energy was −11.9 kcal · mol−1, and the small molecule was aurantiamide, that is, N-[(2S)-1-[[(2S)-1-hydroxy-3-phenylpropan-2-yl] amino]-1-oxo-3-phenylpropan-2-yl] benzamide. The results indicate that MOL010578 exhibits good activity after it was docked with AR. The residues that form hydrogen bonds include LEU33, while residues that form Pi-alkyl interactions include ILE228, ILE227, MET224, LEU70, MET109, MET74, VAL75, and MET78, and residues that form Pi–sulfur interactions include MET71. Notably, these residues play an important role in the binding process.


### 3.6. Molecular dynamics simulation
  Figure 9 shows the complex of PDE-5 and MOL002260 in a simulated saline solvent environment. PDE-5 is a membrane protein. As shown in the figure, PDE-5 is thoroughly integrated with the lipid bilayer. Through molecular dynamics simulation, it was found that PDE-5 can stably bind to MOL002260. Figure 10 shows the complex of AR and MOL010578 in a saline solvent environment. PDE-5 is a membrane protein, and it can be seen from the figure that the complex is completely encapsulated by a cubic solvent environment.


## 4. Discussion
  BPH is a complex disease. Owing to the extensive and complex pharmacological effects of QLXF and the complex pathogenesis of BPH, network pharmacology methods were used to study the mechanism by which QLXF could be used for BPH treatment.

  We screened the targets for treatment with QLXF using the TCMSP website and collected the targets for BPH using GeneCards and OMIM databases. Finally, 177 targets were intersected, and the top 20 targets were selected as predicted targets using Cytoscape. Simultaneously, GO enrichment analysis results showed that QLXF could be used to perform an intervention in BPH patients as it modulated the activity of various pathways, such as those involving DNA binding, transcription factor binding, kinase binding, protein homodimerization, protein structure-specific binding, G protein-coupled amine receptor activity, response to hormones, cellular response to organic cyclic compounds, cellular response to lipids, reaction to inorganic substances, response to exogenous stimuli, membrane raft, transcription regulator complex, vesicle lumen cyclin-dependent protein kinase holoenzyme complex, and the receptor complex. KEGG analysis found that the JAK-STAT signaling pathway, Pl3k-Akt signaling pathway, chemical carcinogenic reactive oxygen species, Leishmania, platinum drug resistance, the FoxO signaling pathway, transcriptional misregulation in cancer, the NF kappa B signaling pathway, and other pathways were significantly enriched. Next, we further analyzed the JAK-STAT signaling pathway and found that AR, DHT, MDA, IL-6, and TNF-α, which occur as upstream targets of the pathway were affected, thereby weakening the expression of related downstream proteins. Subsequently, we validated the predicted results through animal experiments and conducted experimental studies.

  [22]The JAK-STAT signaling pathway plays an important role in the proliferation, growth, angiogenesis, cell survival, and metabolism of healthy cells. The components of this pathway interact with many other pathways, including the PI3K-Akt pathway.[23] The excessive activation of the JAK-STAT signaling pathway leads to a significant level of dysfunction in healthy cells, leading to competitive growth-related metabolic advantages and angiogenesis. An analysis of prostate tissue from BPH patients found that the overexpression of the JAK-STAT signaling pathway reduces cell apoptosis in BPH patients and increases prostate size.

  AR is a key factor driving prostate growth and differentiation, and is expressed in complex patterns in cells. In 1991, a study showed that a small number of AR-positive fibroblasts occurred in the ventral and dorsal lobes of the rat prostate, with the majority of cells being AR-negative. The expression of AR in different types of cells in the dorsal, ventral, and anterior lobes of the human prostate was discovered subsequently. At the cellular level, the epithelial cell compartment contains stem/progenitor cells and secretory lumen cells. In contrast, the stromal cell compartment is mainly composed of fibroblasts, myofibroblasts, smooth muscle cells, endothelial cells, and immune cells.[24] It was reported previously that specific proteins are expressed in these cells, and this renders a unique identity to these cells. Due to their paracrine effects, both cell compartments exhibit extensive cellular crosstalk in healthy and disease conditions. In the prostate, AR is abundant in the nuclei of luminal epithelial cells and stromal cells, while the expression of AR is absent or low in basal stem cells.[25] In addition, the stimulation of AR by DHT also increases the production of several growth factors and cytokines in stromal cells, which can promote cell proliferation in the human prostate. Therefore, the activation of AR with testosterone/DHT can support cell growth in all types of prostate cells.


## 5. Conclusion
  In summary, the efficacy of QLXF and the possible mechanisms through which it could be used for BPH treatment were explored using network pharmacology analysis. QLXF may exert its therapeutic effects through the JAK-STAT signaling pathway and key targets including AR, DHT, IL-6, and TNF-α. Key targets play a therapeutic role. The experimental findings show that QLXF can be used as a drug of choice for BPH treatment in clinical settings. However, further research is needed to validate other therapeutic mechanisms related to the treatment of BPH with QLXF.

  Therefore, in this study, we have focused on the JAK-STAT signaling pathway and shall subsequently investigate other signaling pathways, such as the PI3K-Akt, FoxO, and NF-κB signal pathways.
