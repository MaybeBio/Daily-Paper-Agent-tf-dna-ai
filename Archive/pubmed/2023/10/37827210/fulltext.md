Environmental occurrence and human exposure of emerging organophosphate esters (eOPEs) have increased significantly in recent years. Resorcinol bis(diphenyl) phosphate (RDP) is one of the major eOPEs detected in indoor dust, but the knowledge on its toxicities and health risks is rather limited. In this study, we investigated the in vitro estrogenic effects and underlying mechanism of RDP in comparison with a legacy OPE triphenyl phosphate (TPHP). Our results showed that RDP promoted MCF-7 cell proliferation with the lowest effect concentration of 2.5 muM, and the maximum enhancement of 1.6 folds is greater than that of TPHP (1.3 folds). The effect was inhibited completely by an estrogen receptor (ER) antagonist, suggesting that ER activation was responsible for the enhancement. In luciferase reporter gene assays both RDP and TPHP activated ER transcriptional activity at 2.5 muM, but RDP activity was higher than TPHP. Competitive fluorescence binding assays showed that RDP bound to ER with an IC(10) of 0.26 muM, which is 20 folds lower than TPHP (5.6 muM). Molecular docking simulation revealed that both RDP and TPHP interacted with ER at the binding pocket of estradiol, although the hydrogen bonds were different. Taken together, RDP exerted stronger estrogenic effects than TPHP through ER-mediated pathways and may pose more health risks.
