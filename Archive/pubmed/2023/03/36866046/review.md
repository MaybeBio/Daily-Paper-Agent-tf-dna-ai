## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (main text, methods, figure legends, and references as provided)
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific validity, technical soundness, interpretability claims, and significance of findings as presented in the provided manuscript
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors present DeepBend, a three-layer convolutional neural network with a multinomial first layer and a regularized final layer, designed to predict DNA bendability from sequence and to provide direct, model-based interpretation of sequence motifs and their spatial arrangements. The model is reported to match the predictive performance of state-of-the-art black-box models while offering interpretability. The authors report confirmation of known bendability motifs, discovery of novel motifs (notably GAAGAGC), and associations between predicted bendability, motif distributions, and chromatin features such as nucleosome positioning and TAD boundaries in yeast.
- **Visible evidence base** Main text figures (Figures 1–5), tables (Table 1), methods section, and references to supplementary materials (Figures S1–S33, Tables S1–S6, Notes S1–S3)
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Supplementary figures, tables, and notes referenced in the text were not provided. These include detailed model hyperparameters, full motif lists, global importance scores, Z-test results, and additional benchmarking data. Code availability is stated but was not reviewed.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript presents a well-motivated and thoughtfully designed interpretable deep learning model for DNA bendability prediction. The architectural choices are clearly justified and represent a meaningful contribution to the field of interpretable genomics. The predictive performance claims are supported by the data shown, and the biological findings, particularly the identification of GAAGAGC and the association between bendability and TAD boundaries, are potentially significant. However, the manuscript has several technical and presentational issues that need to be addressed. The statistical analysis of motif enrichment at boundaries is underpowered and conflates motif presence with motif influence. The claim that DeepBend provides "mechanistic" interpretation is overstated, as the model reveals correlations, not causal mechanisms. The comparison with DNACycP is incomplete, and the treatment of overlapping sequences in the tiled libraries, while acknowledged, requires more rigorous validation. The writing is generally clear but could be improved in places for nonspecialist readers.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in computational genomics, chromatin biology, and biophysics would be interested. The model offers a practical tool for predicting DNA bendability and extracting interpretable sequence features. The findings on TAD boundaries and nucleosome positioning connect sequence-level mechanical properties to higher-order chromatin organization, which is of broad interest. The methodological approach of visible neural networks is also relevant to the machine learning for genomics community.
- **Major Strengths** 
  1. The model architecture is genuinely interpretable by design, avoiding the pitfalls of post hoc analysis. The multinomial convolution layer and the variance regularization in the final layer are elegant and well-justified solutions.
  2. The benchmarking is thorough, comparing against multiple baseline models and carefully addressing the issue of sequence overlap in tiled libraries.
  3. The biological validation is strong, confirming known motifs and providing compelling evidence for the novel GAAGAGC motif.
  4. The connection between predicted bendability and chromatin features (nucleosome positioning, TAD boundaries) is a significant finding that extends the utility of the model beyond simple prediction.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **R1-M1** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes) The statistical analysis of motif enrichment at TAD boundaries is flawed. The authors use a two-sample Z-test comparing motif matching scores in boundary regions versus all other regions. However, this conflates motif presence with motif influence on bendability. A motif could be present at boundaries but not be the primary driver of the observed bendability pattern. The Z-test also assumes independence of observations, which is violated by the overlapping nature of the 50-bp windows used to generate the scores. The authors need to either use a more appropriate statistical test that accounts for spatial autocorrelation, or clearly state the limitations of this analysis. The claim that motifs "influence" bendability at boundaries is not supported by this analysis alone.
  - **R1-M2** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes) The comparison with DNACycP is incomplete and potentially misleading. The authors state that DeepBend and DNACycP show "nearly identical performance" (Table 1), but the table only shows Pearson's r values. The authors should report additional metrics such as mean squared error, and should perform a formal statistical comparison (e.g., a paired test across folds) to support the claim of equivalence. Furthermore, the authors do not discuss the interpretability of DNACycP in detail, which is a key selling point of DeepBend. A more detailed comparison of the interpretability features of both models would strengthen the paper.
  - **R1-M3** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No) The claim that DeepBend provides "mechanistic" interpretation is overstated. The model reveals sequence motifs and their spatial arrangements that correlate with bendability, but this does not establish a causal mechanism. The authors should temper their language throughout the manuscript, using terms like "associations" or "predictive features" instead of "mechanisms." This is particularly important in the Discussion section where the authors speculate on the role of GAAGAGC in transcription regulation.
  - **R1-M4** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No) The treatment of the tiled libraries (ChrV and Tiling) requires more rigorous validation. The authors state that they created specially designed datasets to avoid overlap between training and test sets, but the details of this procedure are not fully described in the main text. The authors should provide a more detailed description of how the non-overlapping train/test splits were created, and should also report the performance of the model when trained and tested on the same library with a standard random split (without overlap correction) to quantify the potential inflation.
- **Minor Comments**
  - **R1-m1** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Clarity) The description of the "position-wise variance regularization" in the final layer is somewhat unclear. The authors should provide a more intuitive explanation of why this regularization helps with interpretability, perhaps with a simple illustrative example.
  - **R1-m2** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Reproducibility) The authors state that hyperparameters were tuned but do not provide the full range of hyperparameters explored or the final values used. This information should be included in the supplementary materials for reproducibility.
  - **R1-m3** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Presentation) Figure 3D is difficult to interpret. The authors should consider presenting the data as a scatter plot with a clear legend, or as a boxplot comparing the two conditions directly.
  - **R1-m4** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Statistical rigor) The authors report a hypergeometric p-value for the enrichment of GAAGAGC in promoter regions. However, the choice of the background set (all promoter regions vs. all genomic regions) is not clearly justified. The authors should clarify this choice and discuss its potential impact on the result.
  - **R1-m5** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Literature context) The authors mention that "recent studies found several molecular covariates of TAD formation in yeast" but do not cite specific studies. This statement should be supported with appropriate references.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** The statistical analysis of motif enrichment at TAD boundaries (R1-M1) and the incomplete comparison with DNACyp (R1-M2) are the primary technical issues. The overstatement of mechanistic interpretation (R1-M3) also needs to be addressed.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**
  - **Originality** The model architecture is novel and represents a significant advance in interpretable deep learning for genomics. The application to DNA bendability is timely and relevant.
  - **Scientific importance** The findings have the potential to advance our understanding of how sequence encodes mechanical properties of DNA and how these properties influence chromatin organization. The identification of novel motifs and their association with TAD boundaries is of broad interest.
  - **Interdisciplinary readership** The work bridges machine learning, genomics, and biophysics. The writing is generally accessible, but some sections (e.g., the methods) may be challenging for nonspecialists.
  - **Technical soundness** The core modeling approach is sound, but the statistical analysis of the biological findings needs improvement (R1-M1). The benchmarking is thorough but could be more rigorous (R1-M2).
  - **Readability for nonspecialists** The main text is well-written and the figures are informative. However, the authors should consider adding a brief primer on convolutional neural networks for readers unfamiliar with deep learning.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The manuscript presents a valuable contribution with a novel and interpretable model. The biological findings are potentially significant. However, the statistical analysis of the TAD boundary enrichment needs to be revised, and the comparison with DNACyp needs to be more rigorous. The language regarding mechanistic interpretation should be tempered. Once these issues are addressed, the manuscript would be suitable for publication.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that DeepBend provides "mechanistic" interpretation of DNA bendability is not supported by the evidence presented. The model reveals correlations, not causal mechanisms.
- The claim that motifs "influence" bendability at TAD boundaries is not supported by the statistical analysis, which conflates motif presence with influence.
- The claim that DeepBend and DNACyp show "nearly identical performance" is not rigorously supported, as only Pearson's r is reported without a formal statistical comparison.
- The biological significance of the GAAGAGC motif, while intriguing, is based on correlative evidence and requires further experimental validation.
- The generalizability of DeepBend to other species or experimental conditions is not demonstrated.
