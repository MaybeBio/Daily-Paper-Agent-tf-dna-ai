OBJECTIVE: In this study, the role and potential mechanism of transformer 2beta (Tra2beta) in cervical cancer were explored. METHODS: The transcriptional data of Tra2beta in patients with cervical cancer from Gene Expression Profiling Interactive Analysis (GEPIA) and cBioPortal databases were investigated. The functions of Tra2beta were evaluated by using Western blot, MTT, colony formation, Transwell assays, and nude mouse tumor formation experiments. Target genes regulated by Tra2beta were studied by RNA-seq. Subsequently, representative genes were selected for RT-qPCR, confocal immunofluorescence, Western blot, and rescue experiments to verify their regulatory relationship. RESULTS: The dysregulation of Tra2beta in cervical cancer samples was observed. Tra2beta overexpression in Siha and Hela cells enhanced cell viability and proliferation, whereas Tra2beta knockdown showed the opposite effect. Alteration of Tra2beta expression did not affect cell migration and invasion. Furthermore, tumor xenograft models verified that Tra2beta promoted cervical cancer growth. Mechanically, Tra2beta positively regulated the mRNA and protein level of SP1, which was critical for the proliferative capability of Tra2beta. CONCLUSION: This study demonstrated the important role of the Tra2beta/SP1 axis in the progression of cervical cancer in vitro and in vivo, which provides a comprehensive understanding of the pathogenesis of cervical cancer.
