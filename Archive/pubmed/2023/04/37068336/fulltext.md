# Esculin alleviates LPS-induced acute lung injury via inhibiting neutrophil recruitment and migration


## Objectives
  Acute lung injury (ALI) poses a serious threat to human health globally, particularly with the Coronavirus 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic. Excessive recruitment and infiltration of neutrophils is the major etiopathogenesis of ALI. Esculin, also known as 6,7-dihydroxycoumarin, is a remarkable compound derived from traditional Chinese medicine Cortex fraxini. Accumulated evidence indicates that esculin has potent anti-inflammatory effects, but its pharmaceutical effect against ALI and potential mechanisms are still unclear.


## Methods
  This study evaluated the protective effect of esculin against ALI by histopathological observation and biochemical analysis of lung tissues and bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) in lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-challenged ALI mice in vivo. The effects of esculin on N-formyl-met-leu-phe (fMLP)-induced neutrophil migration and chemotaxis were quantitatively assessed using a Transwell assay and an automated cell imaging system equipped with a Zigmond chamber, respectively. The drug affinity responsive target stability (DARTS) assay, in vitro protein binding assay and molecular docking were performed to identify the potential therapeutic target of esculin and the potential binding sites and pattern.


## Results
  Esculin significantly attenuated LPS-induced lung pathological injury, reduced the levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines in both BALF and lung, and suppressed the activation of NF-κB signaling. Esculin also significantly reduced the number of total cells and neutrophils as well as myeloperoxidase (MPO) activity in the BALF. Esculin impaired neutrophil migration and chemotaxis as evidenced by the reduced migration distance and velocity. Furthermore, esculin remarkably inhibited Vav1 phosphorylation, suppressed Rac1 activation and the PAK1/LIMK1/cofilin signaling axis. Mechanistically, esculin could interact with β2 integrin and then diminish its ligand affinity with intercellular adhesion molecule-1 (ICAM-1).


## Conclusions
  Esculin inhibits β2 integrin-dependent neutrophil migration and chemotaxis, blocks the cytoskeletal remodeling process required for neutrophil recruitment, thereby contributing to its protective effect against ALI. This study demonstrates the new therapeutic potential of esculin as a novel lead compound.


## Introduction
  Since late 2019, a pandemic of coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) has seriously threatened human health and even life worldwide. Once infected with this novel β-coronavirus SARS-CoV-2, acute lung injury (ALI) is a critical cause leading to high mortality [1], [2]. The pathological phenotype of ALI include is characterized by non-cardiogenic pulmonary edema, inflammatory cell infiltration, capillary endothelial destruction and moderate to severe hypoxaemia, accompanied with the overproduction of proinflammatory cytokines such as tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), interleukin (IL)-6, and IL-1β, as well as monocyte chemoattractant protein (MCP)-1, which is a prototype of CC chemokine and a potent chemoattractant for monocytes and neutrophils [3]. Therefore, the development of an effective therapeutic strategy against ALI is essential to mitigate the serious threat to human health posed by COVID-19. Neutrophils are the primary mediators of innate immune system, but under the pathological conditions of COVID-19, the excessive infiltration and uncontrolled activation of neutrophils causes severe damage to lung tissue [4], [5], [6]. Therefore, reducing the excessive recruitment of neutrophils to the lung tissue has been considered as a potential treatment strategy for ALI.

  The recruitment and infiltration of neutrophils into inflammatory sites is strongly linked to migration and chemotaxis in response to inflammatory mediators such as bacterial metabolites, cytokines and chemoattractants [7]. In addition, highly dynamic cytoskeletal rearrangement is a critical feature of neutrophil migration and chemotaxis [8]. It is widely accepted that Rac1 is an important member of the Rho GTPase family that regulates cytoskeletal reorganization, while Vav1, which is abundantly expressed in neutrophils, acts as guanine nucleotide exchange factor (GEF) for Rac1 activation by converting Rac1 from the GDP form to the GTP form [9], [10]. Additionally, Rac1 activation enhances actin polymerization, promotes membrane ruffling and lamellipodia formation, and facilitates neutrophil migration and chemotaxis by activating LIM kinase 1 (LIMK1), whereas Rac1-induced LIMK1 activation depends on p21-activated kinase 1 (PAK1)-mediated LIMK1 phosphorylation at Thr508 [10]. Cofilin is an actin-binding protein and is phosphorylated by LIMK1 at Ser3 to regulate cytoskeletal remodeling [11]. Therefore, the Rac1/PAK1/LIMK1/cofilin signaling axis plays a critical role in regulating neutrophil migration, chemotaxis, recruitment and infiltration through cytoskeletal rearrangement. Accumulating evidence suggests that activation of the Rac1/PAK1/LIMK1/cofilin axis depends on the signal transduction mediated by the adhesion molecule receptors on the surface of cell membranes, such as integrin [12]. As a major adhesion molecule receptor of neutrophils, β2 integrin regulates the recruitment, infiltration and activation of neutrophils in inflammatory tissues by binding to endogenous ligands such as ICAM-1 and then transducing cytoskeletal rearrangement signals to the intracellular effector molecules [13], [14]. Therefore, β2 integrin has been considered as an attractive therapeutic target for inflammatory diseases by interfering with β2 integrin-mediated neutrophil migration.

  Accumulated studies have shown that compounds from medicinal herbs are potential candidates for anti-inflammatory and anti-ALI agents [15]. Esculin (also known as 6,7-dihydroxycoumarin) is a notable active compound derived from the traditional Chinese medicine Cortex fraxini, which has a long history of use in the treatment of gout, hyperuricemia, cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases, inflammation-related diseases, etc. [16]. Previous studies have reported that esculin has potent anti-inflammatory properties, e.g. esculin ameliorated tissue injury and paw swelling in adjuvant-induced arthritis rats by inhibiting the expression of pro-inflammatory factors [17]; esculin also ameliorated lung injury in hyperoxia-induced rats by inhibiting the production of NO and TNF-α [18]. In addition, esculin had protective effects on xylene-induced ear edema and carrageenan-induced pleurisy and paw edema, and also suppressed activation of the MAPK signaling pathway in lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-treated macrophages in vitro [19]. Accumulated evidence shows that esculin has potent anti-inflammatory effects, but the underlying mechanisms, in particular the directly affected signaling pathway and even the direct binding target of esculin, are still unclear. Therefore, from the perspective of neutrophil recruitment and migration, this study investigated the protective effect of esculin on LPS-challenged ALI mice in vivo and N-formyl-met-leu-phe (fMLP)-induced neutrophil migration and chemotaxis in vitro, and further identified the potential therapeutic target of esculin as well as the binding sites and pattern by drug affinity responsive target stability (DARTS) assay and molecular docking. The present findings will provide theoretical support for the development of esculin as a promising lead compound for the pharmacotherapy of ALI.


## Materials and Methods

### Materials and chemicals
  Esculin (≥98%, HPLC) was purchased from Aladdin Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Protein G-Sepharose™ 4B beads, LPS, fMLP and calcein-AM was obtained from Sigma-Aldrich (St. Louis, MO). Recombinant human intercellular adhesion molecules 1 (rhICAM-1) or human IgG were purchased from R&D Systems (Abingdon, VA). ELISA kits for TNF-α, IL-6 and IL-1β were obtained from Invitrogen (Vienna, Austria). ELISA kit for MCP-1 was purchased from Beyotime Biotechnology (Shanghai, China). Antibodies against IκBα (ab76429), p-IκBα (ab133462), Vav1 (ab97574) and phospho-Vav1 (ab76225) were acquired from Abcam (Cambridge, MA). Anti-PAK1 (#2602), anti-phospho-PAK1 (#2601), anti-LIMK1 (#3842), anti-phospho-LIMK1(#3841), anti-cofilin (#3318), anti-phospho-cofilin (#3311), anti-p65 (#8242), anti-p-p65 (#3033), anti-GST (#2624), anti-Rac1 (#4651), anti-ICAM-1 (#4915), anti-β2 integrin (#73663) and anti-β-actin (#4970) antibodies were purchased from Cell Signaling Technology (Berkeley, CA). Enhanced Supersignal West Pico Chemiluminescence (ECL) substrate kit was purchased from Thermo Scientific (Rockford, IL).


### LPS-induced acute lung injury of mice
  Male C57BL/6 mice (8 weeks old, SPF grade) were purchased from Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China), housed under standard SPF conditions with free access to water and food, room temperature of 22–26 °C, and relative humidity of 50%-60%. All animal experiments were conducted in accordance with the Guidelines for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals of Wenzhou University.

  All mice were randomly divided into four groups (n = 8 per group): normal control group (Ctrl), LPS-induced ALI model group (LPS), low-dose of esculin group (2 mg/kg, i.v.), and high-dose group of esculin (8 mg/kg, i.v.). To establish the ALI model, mice were anesthetized with pentobarbital sodium (40 mg/kg intraperitoneally) and intratracheally instilled with LPS (4 mg/kg). Subsequently, the ALI model mice were then treated with esculin (dissolved in saline) by intravenous injection into the tail vein immediately after LPS exposure. Twenty-four hours after the administration of esculin, mice were anesthetized and the bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) was collected by washing the lung three times with 0.5 mL of pre-cooled saline via the endotracheal cannula. The mice were then sacrificed by cervical dislocation and lung tissues were harvested for further analysis.


### Histopathological analysis of lungs
  Lung tissues were washed with pre-cooled PBS and fixed in 10% formaldehyde, dehydrated in ethanol, embedded in paraffin and sliced into 4 μm sections for hematoxylin-eosin (H&E) staining. Pathological damage scores were assessed as previously described [20].


### BALF analysis
  After centrifugation, the protein content in the cell-free supernatant of BALF was measured using a BCA protein assay kit (Pierce Biotechnology, Rockford, Illinois, USA). The BALF pellet was resuspended in PBS and stained with Wright-Giemsa stain to quantify the numbers of total cells and neutrophils.


### MPO activity and ELISA assay
  Lung tissues were homogenized with cold-PBS (1:9, w/v) on ice using a TissueLyser II tissue grinder (Qiagen, Venlo, Netherlands) and centrifuged at 3000 rpm at 4 °C. Myeloperoxidase (MPO) activity in the supernatant of the homogenate was measured using a commercial assay kit. Cytokine levels in the cell-free supernatant of BALF and the homogenate supernatant of lung tissues were determined using ELISA kits (Invitrogen, Vienna, Austria).


### Neutrophil isolation
  Neutrophils were freshly isolated from whole blood of healthy human volunteers by density gradient centrifugation as previously described [20]. Neutrophil percentage was greater than 95% assessed by Wright-Giemsa staining and viability was greater than 95% evaluated by trypan blue.


### Transwell assay
  Neutrophils were labeled with 2 μM calcein-AM for 30 min. After washing, the cells were treated with or without 5 μM esculin in PBS for another 30 min. The cells (5 × 105/well) were then seeded onto the upper chambers of Transwell. Serum-free IMDM (500 μL) containing fMLP (200 nM) as chemoattractant was added to the lower chambers of Transwell. After 2 h of incubation, the chambers were removed and the fluorescence intensity of the cells migrating through the chamber as well as the total cells was determined using a microplate reader (excitation wavelength 485 nm/emission wavelength 530 nm).


### Neutrophil chemotaxis in Zigmond chamber
  Neutrophils were seeded on ICAM-1-coated coverslips and then incubated with or without 5 μM esculin in PBS for 30 min. The coverslip was placed on Zigmond chamber (Neuroprobe Inc., Gaithersburg, MD). Hanks' buffer (containing 1% gelatin) was added to one groove of Zigmond chamber and the Hanks' buffer (containing 1% gelatin and 200 nM fMLP) was added to the other groove. The chamber was then placed in the JuLI™ Stage automated cell imaging system (NanoEntek Inc., Seoul, Korea), and time-lapse images were captured at 10-second intervals for 12 min and the quantitative evaluation, including migratory tracks, velocity and accumulated distance, was analyzed using NIH ImageJ software (Bethesda, MD).


### Rac1 activation assay
  Rac1 activation was evaluated by a GST pull-down assay [21]. Briefly, neutrophils were seeded on ICAM-1-precoated culture dishes and then treated with or without esculin 5 μM in PBS for 2 h. Subsequently, the cells were lysed on ice for 20 min and the lysates were centrifuged at 15,000 g for 15 min. The supernatant of the cell lysate was then incubated with GST-PAK1 p21-binding domain (GST-PAK1 PBD) beads. The protein levels of Rac1 bound to GST-PAK1 PBD beads were analyzed by Western blotting.


### Binding assay between β2 integrin and ICAM-1
  The binding assay was performed according to previous report [22]. Briefly, neutrophils were lysed with RIPA lysis buffer (containing 1 mM PMSF) on ice, the lysate supernatant was incubated with antibody against β2 integrin, and then Protein G-Sepharose™ 4B beads were added to the mixture. The immunoprecipitated beads bound with β2 integrin were incubated with or without esculin 5 μM in PBS. ICAM-1 was then incubated with these beads for another 1 h. The beads were washed with ice-cold lysis buffer, and after boiling in Laemmli loading buffer, the bound ICAM-1 was determined by Western blotting.


### Drug affinity responsive target stability (DARTS) assay
  Neutrophils (2 × 107) were incubated with or without 5 μM esculin in PBS for 1 h and lysed with 800 μL ice-cold M−PER mammalian protein extraction reagent (Cat#78503, Pierce, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Inc.) containing phosphatase inhibitor cocktail (Cat#P8340, Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA). After centrifugation at 15,000 g for 15 min at 4 °C, the protein content of the supernatant was determined using a BCA protein assay kit. The lysate supernatant was then divided equally into two portions, and then incubated with or without pronase for 30 min. The samples were boiled with Laemmli loading buffer, and analyzed by Western blotting.


### Western blotting
  Cells or lung tissues were homogenized with RIPA lysis buffer on ice for 20 min, then the lysates were centrifuged at 15, 000 × g. The protein concentration of the samples was determined using a BCA protein assay kit, then the proteins were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred to PVDF membranes. After four washes with PBST, the membranes were incubated with the indicated primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C, then incubated with secondary antibodies for another 1 h. Immunoblots were detected using the enhanced Supersignal West Pico Chemiluminescence (ECL) substrate kit (Thermo Scientific, Rockford, IL) and visualized on the Amersham Imager 600 system (GE Healthcare Life, Chicago, IL) and quantified using NIH ImageJ software (Bethesda, MD).


### Molecular docking
  To investigate the potential binding sites and pattern of β2 integrin to esculin as an antagonist, the molecular docking was performed using AutoDock vina 1.1.2 package. The 3D chemical structure of esculin (PubChem CID: 5281417) was obtained from PubChem database (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/). The X-ray crystallographic structure of αLβ2 integrin [also known as lymphocyte function-associated antigen-1 (LFA-1), CD11a/CD18] headpiece (PDB code: 5E6U) was obtained from RCSB Protein Data Bank (https://www.rcsb.org). The crystal structure of the αLβ2 integrin headpiece was prepared by removing water molecules and bound ligands using Autodock4.2 Protein Preparation Workflow. The binding sites of αLβ2 integrin were identified as centre_x: −2.427, centre_y: −68.925, and centre_z: 17.487 with dimensions size_x: 40, size_y: 40, and size_z: 40. The best docking models with the lowest binding energy were selected, then the analysis of the amino acid residues involved in ligand interactions, chemical bond type and strength of interactions was performed using PyMol 2.6.0 software.


### Statistical analysis
  Statistical analysis was conducted using the SPSS 20.0 software (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA), and one-way ANOVA (Tukey's test) and two-tailed Student's t-test were performed. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.


## Results

### Esculin alleviates pathological change of lung tissue and suppresses NF-κB signaling activation in LPS-induced ALI mice
  First, the protective effect of esculin on LPS-induced ALI was investigated in vivo. From the histomorphology, we found that the alveolar wall in the lung tissues of normal mice was intact, while LPS caused remarkable pathological changes, including alveolar collapse, edema, and leukocyte infiltration (Fig. 1 A). When the LPS-induced ALI model mice were administrated with esculin at 8 mg/kg, these pathological changes were significantly alleviated (Fig. 1 A) and the lung injury score was also significantly reduced (Fig. 1 B). We further evaluated the levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines in both the BALF and lung tissues. The levels of TNFα, IL-6, and IL-1β were significantly increased in both the BALF (Fig. 1 C) and lung tissue (Fig. 1 D) of LPS-challenged mice, whereas esculin significantly reduced the expression of these pro-inflammatory cytokines.Fig. 1Esculin alleviates pathological changes and suppresses activation of NF-κB signaling in LPS-induced ALI mice. The representative histopathological images (A) and pathological scores (B) of lung tissues are shown. The levels of inflammatory cytokines TNF-α, IL-6 and IL-1β in BALF (C) and lung tissues (D) were detected by ELISA. (E) The levels of p-IκBα, IκBα, p-p65, p65 and β-actin were determined by Western blotting. (F) Relative expression levels of p-IκBα/GAPDH, IκBα/GAPDH and p-p65/p65 were quantified using the ImageJ software. Scale bar = 100 μm. Data are expressed as mean ± SD. *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001.

  NF-κB signaling is essential for initiating and maintaining inflammation, and regulating the release of inflammatory cytokines [23]. Therefore, we further investigated whether esculin can suppress the activation of NF-κB signaling. As shown in Fig. 1 E and F, LPS increased the level of phosphorylated IκBα in the lung tissue of ALI mice, whereas esculin remarkably reduced IκBα phosphorylation. Phosphorylated IκBα can be further degraded, resulting in the release of NF-κB dimer. LPS significantly induced IκBα degradation in the lung tissue of ALI mice, whereas esculin significantly suppressed LPS-induced IκBα degradation (Fig. 1 E and F). Once IκBα is degraded, the p65 phosphorylation can be enhanced and then translocated from the cytoplasm to the nucleus, initiating the transcription of proinflammatory cytokines. Furthermore, esculin significantly decreased LPS-induced p65 phosphorylation (Fig. 1 E and F), suggesting that esculin inhibits the transcriptional activity of NF-κB. Taken together, these results demonstrate that esculin can protect lung tissue against LPS-induced acute pathological injury, suppress NF-κB activation and reduce the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines.


### Esculin inhibits neutrophil recruitment and activation in LPS-induced ALI mice
  Recruitment and activation of neutrophils in response to inflammatory mediators such as bacterial metabolites, cytokines and chemoattractants is considered to be one of the major defense mechanisms of innate immunity in an attempt to eliminate pathogens in affected tissues [7]. However, uncontrolled excessive recruitment and infiltration of neutrophils is the major etiopathogenesis of ALI. LPS significantly increased the total number of cells in BALF, whereas esculin significantly decreased the total number of cells (Fig. 2 A). Also, the number of neutrophils in BALF was significantly increased in LPS-induced ALI mice, whereas esculin significantly suppressed the excessive recruitment and infiltration of neutrophils in BALF (Fig. 2 B). In addition, LPS greatly increased the protein content of BALF, and this increase was significantly attenuated by esculin treatment (Fig. 2 C). MPO, one of the major enzymes released upon neutrophil activation, not only generates cytotoxic oxidants but also amplifies deleterious inflammatory signaling cascades. It is widely accepted that MPO serves as an index of neutrophil recruitment and activation. We found that the MPO activity (Fig. 2 D) and MCP-1 level (Fig. 2 E) were significantly increased in the lung tissues of LPS-induced ALI mice, whereas esculin administration significantly decreased the activity of MPO and MCP-1 level in the lung tissues of ALI mice.Fig. 2Esculin inhibits neutrophil recruitment and activation in LPS-induced ALI mice. The number of total cells (A) and neutrophils (B) were counted in BALF. Protein contents in BALF (C), MPO activity (D) and MCP-1 level (E) in the supernatant of tissue homogenate were measured using commercial assay kits. Data are expressed as mean ± SD. *p < 0.05, ***p < 0.001.


### Esculin impairs fMLP-induced neutrophil migration and chemotaxis in vitro
  The destructive potential of neutrophils leading to ALI requires limiting their recruitment and infiltration, a promising therapeutic strategy for ALI. First, we investigated whether esculin could inhibit neutrophil migration in vitro using Transwell assay. After calcein-AM labeling and treatment with esculin, neutrophils were incubated in Transwell chamber in response to fMLP, a potent chemoattractant to induce neutrophil migration and chemotaxis. As shown in Fig. 3 A, esculin treatment significantly inhibited the neutrophil migration in response to fMLP through Transwell chamber into the lower well of the cell culture plate. Furthermore, an automated cell imaging system equipped with a Zigmond chamber was used to evaluate the effects of esculin on neutrophil chemotaxis in response to fMLP. The migratory tracks of neutrophils were plotted using ImageJ software as well as the quantitative analysis of migration velocity and accumulated distance. As illustrated in Fig. 3 B, neutrophils in the control group exhibited strong migratory capacity in response to fMLP gradient, and they migrated further than the neutrophils in the esculin-treated group. The average migration velocity (Fig. 3 C) and accumulated distance (Fig. 3 D) of neutrophils in the control group were 13.8 μm/min and 164 μm, respectively, while treated with esculin, the average velocity and accumulated distance of esculin-treated neutrophils were greatly reduced compared with those of control neutrophils. Thus, these results indicated that esculin impaired fMLP-induced neutrophil migration and chemotaxis in vitro.Fig. 3Esculin impairs fMLP-induced neutrophil migration and chemotaxis in vitro. (B) Tracks of fMLP-stimulated chemotaxis of neutrophils treated with or without esculin (5 μM). Migratory velocity (C) and accumulated distance (D) were quantified using NIH ImageJ software. ### Esculin suppresses Rac1/PAK1/LIMK1/cofilin signaling axis involved in cytoskeletal remodeling
  Next, we evaluated the effect of esculin on Vav1 phosphorylation and Rac1-GTP level (activated form of Rac1), as well as the activated status of the PAK1/LIMK1/cofilin signaling axis. As shown in Fig. 4 A and B, the level of phosphorylated Vav1 in fMLP-stimulated neutrophils was significantly reduced by esculin. Consistent with the change in Vav1 phosphorylation, esculin also significantly reduced the level of the activated form of Rac1 (GTP-Rac1) in fMLP-treated neutrophils (Fig. 4 C and D). To understand whether esculin affects this signaling axis, we evaluated the phosphorylation levels of PAK1, LIMK1, and cofilin. As shown in Fig. 5 A and B, esculin significantly reduced the phosphorylated levels of PAK1, LIMK1 and cofilin in fMLP-challenged neutrophils. ### Esculin interacts with β2 integrin and antagonizes endogenous binding
  To evaluate whether esculin could block the binding between β2 integrin and its endogenous ligand ICAM-1, a protein binding assay was performed in vitro. As shown in Fig. 6 A and B, the affinity between β2 integrin and ICAM-1 was significantly decreased by the incubation of β2 integrin with esculin, and the blocking efficiency reached 72%, indicating a direct interaction of esculin with β2 integrin. The DARTS assay is an effective tool for identifying potential drug targets based on the principle that ligand binding increases the resistance of the target proteins to proteolysis. Furthermore, the DARTS assay indicated that esculin significantly decreased the protease sensitivity of β2 integrin and inhibited pronase-induced degradation of β2 integrin in neutrophil lysates (Fig. 6 C and D). Therefore, these results further support the direct interaction of esculin with β2 integrin.Fig. 6Esculin interacts with β2 integrin and antagonizes the binding between β2 integrin and ICAM-1. (B) Quantification of bound ICAM-1 was performed using ImageJ software. (C) The effect of esculin on the proteolytic susceptibility of β2 integrin was analyzed by DARTS assay. (D) Densitometry analysis of β2 integrin from DARTS assay was performed using ImageJ software. (E) Molecular docking of esculin (PubChem CID: 5281417) with human αLβ2 integrin (PDB code: 5E6U).   The theoretical binding mode was investigated by molecular docking analysis to explore the potential binding sites and pattern between esculin and the ligand binding pocket in the headpiece of αLβ2 integrin, a major type of β2 integrin in neutrophils. The theoretical three-dimensional binding mode of the complex with the lowest docking energy is illustrated in Fig. 6 E. Esculin interacts with ASN64, GLN117 and GLN360 in the headpiece of αLβ2 integrin via hydrogen bonding, and thus may directly compete and restrict the binding of the endogenous ligand ICAM-1. The carboxylate group in esculin forms a salt bridge interaction with AGR119 of αLβ2 integrin. All these interactions help to anchor esculin in the ligand binding pocket. In addition, the estimated the binding energy of αLβ2 integrin-esculin complex was found to be − 3.9 kcal/mol, suggesting that esculin binds tightly to αLβ2 integrin. The docking simulation provides further supportive evidence that esculin antagonizes the endogenous binding between β2 integrin and ICAM-1 and further inhibits neutrophil migration and chemotaxis by predicting the potential binding sites in the active site pocket of β2 integrin. These results demonstrated that esculin can block the engagement of β2 integrin with ICAM-1 and suppress the downstream signal transduction for regulating cytoskeletal rearrangement, ultimately causing the subsequent reduction of neutrophil recruitment and infiltration into the inflammatory sites.


## Discussion
  Acute lung injury (ALI), first described in 1967, is a clinical syndrome characterized by severe hypoxaemia and bilateral pulmonary infiltrates [3]. Multiple risk factors such as sepsis or traumatic injury can lead to the development of ALI, while the rapid and widespread emergence of COVID-19 has dramatically exacerbated this existing issue [2]. Currently, there are no approved therapeutic drugs for ALI, so new treatment modalities should be developed to further improve the clinical outcomes. Here, we found that esculin significantly attenuated LPS-induced pathological lung injury, reduced inflammatory cytokine levels and suppressed NF-κB signaling activation. Furthermore, esculin reduced neutrophil infiltration and MPO activity in BALF. In addition, esculin impaired the migratory capacity and chemotactic behavior of fMLP-induced neutrophils. Also, esculin potentially affected cytoskeletal rearrangement via the inhibition of Vav1 phosphorylation and Rac1/PAK1/LIMK1/cofilin signaling axis. Interestingly, esculin interacted with β2 integrin and diminished its ligand affinity with ICAM-1, thereby contributing to the inhibition of β2 integrin-mediated signal transduction of cytoskeletal rearrangement, suggesting a novel therapeutic potential of esculin as a lead compound against ALI.

  It has been suggested that the anti-inflammatory effect of esculin is strongly associated with the inactivation of the NF-κB signaling pathway [16]. As an inducible transcription factor, NF-κB regulates the expression of a large number of pro-inflammatory genes. For example, esculin inhibited the production of TNF-α and IL-1β in LPS/d-galactosamine-induced acute liver injury mice [24]; esculin ameliorated the histopathologic damage and reduced the production of inflammatory cytokines in ethanol-induced gastric injury mice [25]; esculin inhibited the expression of iNOS, IL-1β, and TNF-α in dextran sulfate sodium (DSS)-induced colitis mice [26]; all by suppressing the activation of NF-κB signaling. Although accumulated studies reported that esculin possesses remarkable anti-inflammatory potentials derived from suppressing the activation of NF-κB signaling pathway, the precise mechanism by which esculin blocks NF-κB signal transduction and reduces the release of pro-inflammatory factors, as well as which is the direct effector targets binding with esculin, remains unclear. Also, little is known about the effect of esculin on neutrophil migration and chemotaxis in the progression of acute inflammation. Neutrophils are the first leukocytes to arrive at the sites of infection or tissue injury [7], [8]. During the acute inflammatory response, neutrophils are rapidly recruited from the bloodstream to the inflamed tissues through a multistep recruitment cascade that is tightly controlled [8]. It is well-known that the deregulation of neutrophil recruitment, such as the excessive infiltration and uncontrolled activation, is strongly associated with the development of ALI [27]. Distinct adhesive interactions between endothelial cells, ECM and leukocytes, as well as the signaling mechanisms, contribute to the temporal and spatial fine-tuning of the leukocyte recruitment cascade [28], [29]. The steps of the neutrophil recruitment cascade include the initial neutrophil contact with the endothelial surface, then rolling, adhesion, spreading, intraluminal crawling and transmigration in response to chemokines or chemoattractants, and all steps highly depend on leukocyte adhesion molecules of the β2 integrin family (CD11/CD18) [29], [30]. The β2 integrin family consists of distinct α-subunits (CD11) and a conserved β-subunit (CD18) forming four heterodimers, of which lymphocyte function‐associated antigen-1 (LFA-1, αLβ2, CD11a/CD18) and macrophage antigen-1 (Mac-1, αMβ2, CD11b/CD18) are mainly expressed in neutrophils [31]. β2 integrin plays a critical role in neutrophil crawling on the endothelium, transendothelial migration, and migration in tissue through its interaction with endogenous ligands, such as ICAM-1, ICAM-2, platelet-endothelial cell adhesion molecule-1 (PECAM-1), junctional adhesion molecule (JAM) and ECM components [32], [33].

  Given the essential role of neutrophil recruitment in inflammation-related diseases and the critical role of β2 integrin on neutrophil migration, β2 integrin has been considered as therapeutic target for various inflammatory diseases, as evidenced by the preclinical and clinical therapeutic applications of antibodies targeting β2 integrin [29], [34]. For example, efalizumab (Raptiva®, Genentech Inc.) is a humanized antibody against α-subunit (CD11a) of β2 integrin LFA-1, blocking the binding between LFA-1 and ICAM-1 [35]. Clinical trials have shown that efalizumab administrated by subcutaneous injection can improve the dermatologic features of psoriasis by reducing leukocyte recruitment [36], [37]. Efalizumab was approved by the U.S. FDA in October 2003 for the treatment of chronic, moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis, however, it was withdrawn in 2009 due to the high risk of progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML) in patients receiving long-term efalizumab administration [38]. Therefore, more research is focused on screening novel β2 integrin antagonists from natural products with a low cost and high efficacy for the treatment of inflammation-related diseases while avoiding the defects of antibody drugs. The present study revealed that esculin impaired the migration behavior of neutrophils, mechanistically, it interacted with β2 integrin then diminished the ligand affinity of β2 integrin binding to ICAM-1, thereby contributing to the protective effects of esculin against ALI by inhibiting neutrophil recruitment. We then predicted the recognition and binding mode of esculin to β2 integrin, providing preliminary information for optimizing the structure of esculin and improving its binding specificity and affinity.

  Furthermore, outside-in signaling of β2 integrin modulates a variety of essential signaling events [39]. Integrin engagement with endogenous ligands can induce integrin clustering as signaling platform that recruits signaling molecules, such as cytoskeletal regulatory molecules, to form a signal transduction complex that regulates actin cytoskeleton assembly as well as morphological changes [39]. In addition, β2 integrin engagement can activate Rac1, an essential cytoskeletal regulatory molecule belonging to the Rho family of small GTPases, through guanine nucleotide exchange factors (GEFs) [40], [41]. Accumulating evidence indicates that Vav1 is one of three members of the Vav family (the others being Vav2 and Vav3), which is a well-studied family of GEFs for Rho GTPases [42]. Vav1 is highly expressed in neutrophils and acts as important signaling molecule that regulates neutrophil motility via cytoskeletal rearrangements, such as the formation of membrane ruffling and pseudopod [43]. Once activated by Vav1, Rac1 can first activate PAK1 and trigger the signaling cascade that the activated PAK1 is able to activate LIMK1 by phosphorylation, while the activated LIMK1 further exerts cytoskeletal remodeling by phosphorylating and regulating the activity of cofilin, eventually leading to the formation of actin filaments and pseudopod for migration [12], [43], [44]. Therefore, the signaling axis consisting of β2 integrin/Rac1/PAK1/LIMK1/cofilin has been suggested to be involved in the process of neutrophil recruitment and infiltration, which causes lung injury under pathological conditions. In this study, we found that esculin significantly reduced Vav1 phosphorylation and Rac1 activation, as well as the phosphorylation of PAK1, LIMK1 and cofilin in fMLP-induced neutrophils. Therefore, the suppression of this signaling axis by esculin may impair the highly dynamic cytoskeletal reorganization of neutrophils, thereby limiting their recruitment and infiltration in vivo.

  In conclusion, we found that esculin significantly ameliorated LPS-induced pathological lung injury, reduced the levels of inflammatory cytokines, and suppressed the activation of NF-κB signaling in the lungs of LPS-treated mice. In addition, esculin significantly decreased the numbers of total cells and neutrophils, protein content and MPO activity in BALF. Furthermore, the results from fMLP-induced neutrophils indicated that esculin could affect the migratory capacity and chemotactic behavior of neutrophils. Additionally, esculin inhibited Vav1 phosphorylation and suppressed the Rac1/PAK1/LIMK1/cofilin signaling axis, which may then affect cytoskeletal rearrangement. Mechanistically, esculin interacted with β2 integrin, then diminished its ligand affinity with ICAM-1, thereby contributing to the protective effects of esculin against ALI by inhibiting β2 integrin-dependent signal transduction of cytoskeletal rearrangement, and neutrophil migration, recruitment, and infiltration. Collectively, the present findings reveal a novel therapeutic potential of esculin as a lead compound against ALI.
