# Exploring the active ingredients and mechanism of Shenzhi Tongxin capsule against microvascular angina based on network pharmacology and molecular docking


## Methods:
  The main ingredients of the SZTX capsule, along with their targets proteins and potential disease targets associated with MVA, were extracted from public available databases. This study utilized the STRING database and Cytoscape 3.7.2 software to establish a protein–protein interaction network and determine key signaling pathway targets. Subsequently, the DAVID database was utilized to conduct Gene Ontology and the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes analyses on the intersection targets. To further investigate the molecular interactions, Autodock and PyMOL software were employed to perform molecular docking and visualize the resulting outcomes.


## Results:
  A total of 130 and 142 bioactive ingredients and intersection targets were identified respectively. Six core targets were obtained through protein–protein interaction network analysis. Gene Ontology enrichment analysis showed that 610 biological processes, 75 cellular components, and 92 molecular functions were involved. The results of Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes enrichment analyses indicated that SZTX capsule molecular mechanism in the treatment of MVA may be related to several pathways, including mitogen-activated protein kinases, PI3K-Akt, HIF-1, and others. The results of molecular docking showed that the 7 key active ingredients of SZTX capsule had good binding ability to 6 core proteins.


## Conclusion:
  SZTX capsule potentially exerts its effects by targeting multiple signaling pathways, including the mitogen-activated protein kinases signaling pathway, PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, and HIF-1 signaling pathway. This multi-target approach enables SZTX capsule to inhibit inflammation, alleviate oxidative stress, regulate angiogenesis, and enhance endothelial function.


## 1. Introduction
  Angina pectoris, a common symptom of myocardial ischemia, is the primary manifestation in approximately half of individuals with coronary artery disease (CAD). It is widely acknowledged that the underlying cause of angina pectoris is predominantly attributed to obstructive CAD.[1] However, a study[2] revealed that among the approximately 400,000 individuals in the United States who underwent coronary angiography due to suspected obstructive CAD, 59% exhibited either normal coronary arteries or no significant obstructive CAD (stenosis < 50%). Coronary microvascular dysfunction (CMD) could account for this phenomenon, as it affects the structure and/or function of the coronary microcirculation, resulting in a reduced coronary flow reserve (CFR).[3] CMD patients experience an alarming 4-fold increases in mortality rates and a 5-fold increase in major adverse cardiac events, significantly impacting their quality of life and prognosis.[4] CMD is the primary mechanism behind microvascular angina (MVA), as approximately two-thirds of patients without obstructive CAD exhibit CMD, resulting in recurrent angina episodes. This can explain myocardial ischemia under normal coronary angiography, and is an important healthcare problem.[3,5] MVA can be diagnosed if the following criteria are met[6]: symptoms of myocardial ischemia; absence of obstructive CAD (<50% coronary artery diameter reduction and/or fractional flow reserve > 0.80); objective evidence of myocardial ischemia; objective evidence of CMD. MVA usually manifests as oppressive chest discomfort or pain behind the sternum, occurring during exertion or at rest, along with symptoms of dyspnea, reduced CFR, or microvascular spasms.[7] A comprehensive international prospective cohort study conducted by the Coronary Vasomotor Disorders International Study Group[8] concluded that the diagnosis of MVA signifies a substantial risk of major adverse cardiac events irrespective of gender and race, particularly in individuals with a history of hypertension and CAD. While the prognosis remains comparable, women with MVA generally experience a lower quality of life than men. Currently, there is limited robust evidence supporting the effectiveness of antianginal medications in treating MVA. Caution should be exercised when prescribing β receptor blockers, especially those lacking vasodilatory properties, to patients with microvascular or epicardial spasm, as these drugs may potentially exacerbate coronary vasoconstriction by exposing α-Adrenaline receptors.[7] Furthermore, it is essential to note that sublingual nitroglycerin may not relieve MVA in many patients, as nitrates primarily target epicardial conduction vessels.[3] MVA is a prevalent condition, affecting many patients undergoing coronary angiography for angina pectoris, with estimates suggesting that up to 40% to 50% of them may have MVA. This high prevalence contributes to a worsened prognosis for cardiovascular disease and diminished quality of life, thereby increasing the burden on patients and healthcare systems.[9] However, the lack of safe and effective therapeutic drugs underscores the need to develop novel, safer, and more efficient medications to treat MVA.

  With a rich history spanning many centuries, Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) has been utilized as a complementary approach to treating CAD due to its proven safety and efficacy. MVA is classified under the TCM category of “chest obstruction and heartache,” with its pathogenesis often attributed to qi deficiency, poor circulation, and blood stasis, resulting in symptoms such as chest tightness and pain. As depicted in Table 1, Shenzhi Tongxin (SZTX) capsule comprises the following components: Ren Shen (RS), Shui Zhi (SZ), San Qi (SQ), Dan Shen (DS), Chuan Xiong (CX), Mai Dong (MD), and Bing Pian. RS, known for its qi-replenishing properties, has been found to contain ginsenoside as its main active ingredient. Research has demonstrated that ginsenoside exhibits various cardiovascular protective effects, including anti-atherosclerosis, myocardial protection, and anti-ischemic heart disease.[10] SZ potentiates blood circulation and resolves blood stasis. Recent pharmacological investigations have revealed that hirudin, one of its components, exerts cardiovascular protective effects, including the inhibition of platelet aggregation, anti-oxidative stress, anti-apoptosis, attenuation of myocardial hypertrophy, and enhancement of endothelial function.[11–13] DS, SQ, CX have the functions of activating blood circulation and removing blood stasis. The active ingredients found in DS and CX exhibit a range of beneficial effects, including antioxidation, anti-inflammatory properties, endothelial protection, myocardial protection, anticoagulation, and the ability to reduce the proliferation and migration of smooth muscle cells.[14,15] Panax Notoginseng saponins has cardioprotective benefit by inducing autophagy of myocardial cells, antiplatelet aggregation, enhancing endothelial cell migration, and angiogenesis.[16] Contemporary pharmacological research has revealed that BP possesses a range of functions, including anti-inflammatory, anti-oxidative, anti-apoptotic, and analgesic properties, and the ability to facilitate drug delivery to target organs or tissues.[17] Hence, SZTX capsule exhibits a notable cardioprotective effect and holds promise as an adjunctive therapy for managing MVA. Nonetheless, there is a need for more research reports on the specific efficacy of SZTX capsule in MVA treatment, and its potential active ingredients and therapeutic mechanisms still need to be discovered.

  TCM, mainly compound medicine, exhibits distinct characteristics such as multi-component, multi-target, and multi-pathway effects. However, the precise, practical components and mechanisms of action still need to be clarified, posing challenges for conventional verification. Consequently, innovative approaches are required in TCM research. In the information era, network pharmacology has gained prominence as a valuable tool for exploring the potential mechanisms of TCM in treating diverse ailments.[18] Network pharmacology was first proposed by Hopkins in 2007, integrating multiple disciplines, including systems biology, network biology, molecular pharmacology, omics, and computational biology.[19] Network pharmacology utilizes the “drug-target-disease” interaction network to investigate drugs’ active components and action targets, allowing for a comprehensive understanding of the potential mechanisms underlying drug treatments for various diseases. It offers a fresh perspective and a novel approach to exploring TCM and its compound formulations.[20] Molecular docking technology is based on the receptor structure and its interaction with drug components to design drugs. It studies the binding ability of the two abovementioned, screens potential active components, and initially reveals the drug basis of TCM.[21]

  In this study, network pharmacology was employed to predict the primary active components, core targets, and action pathways associated with the therapeutic effects of SZTX capsule in MVA treatment. The study aimed to establish the network relationship between the drug and the disease, while the binding capacity of the main active components and key targets was verified through molecular docking technology (Figure S1, Supplemental Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/MD/J216). As a result, the molecular mechanism underlying the efficacy of SZTX capsule in MVA treatment was initially elucidated, offering a theoretical foundation for further in vitro and in vivo research endeavors.


## 2. Materials and methods

### 2.1. Screening of active ingredients and corresponding targets
  Using the Traditional Chinese Medicine Systems Pharmacology Database and Analysis Platform (TCMSP, https://old.tcmsp-e.com/tcmsp.php), a retrieval was conducted to extract information on the 7 herbs present in SZTX capsule, followed by the screening of their respective active ingredients. The screening process involved selecting active ingredients that met the criteria of oral bioavailability ≥ 30% and drug-likeness ≥ 0.18. There was no information about SZ and MD in the TCMSP database, so we determined their bioactive ingredients through the BATMAN-TCM database (http://bionet.ncpsb.org/batman-tcm/). To predict the targets associated with the active ingredients, we utilized the Swiss Target Prediction (http://www.swisstargetprediction.ch/) and the Drugbank database (https://www.drugbank.ca/). Subsequently, the potential targets of the drug were cross-referenced with the Uniprot database (Universal, https://www.uniprot.org/), to convert protein names into corresponding gene symbols. Using Cytoscape 3.7.2, we constructed the component-target network of SZTX capsules, and critical active ingredients were identified based on their degree of centrality. It is important to note that this study is a compilation of data from previously published articles and does not delve into matters concerning patient ethics or related considerations. Hence, ethical committee approval is not required for this study.


### 2.2. Screening of MVA-related targets
  By using the keywords “microvascular angina” and “cardiac syndrome X,” we conducted comprehensive searches across multiple databases, including OMIM (https://omim.org/), GeneCards (https://www.genecards.org/), DisGeNET (https://www.disgenet.org/), and TTD (http://db.idrblab.net/ttd/). The targets obtained from each database were subsequently merged, and duplicate entries were eliminated to obtain a comprehensive set of targets related to MVA.


### 2.3. Constructing a “Drug-Component-Target-Disease” network
  The Venny 2.1.0 platform (https://bioinfogp.cnb.csic.es/tools/venny/index.html) was employed to identify the intersecting targets between the active ingredients and disease targets. These selected targets were assumed to be the relevant targets of SZTX capsule in treating MVA. Subsequently, the targets were imported into the Cytoscape 3.7.2 software to construct a comprehensive “Drug-Component-Target-Disease” Network. The network was further analyzed using the “Network Analyzer” function, where nodes represented drugs, active ingredients, diseases, and targets, while edges depicted the relationships between these nodes.


### 2.4. Construction and analysis of protein–protein interaction network
  The drug-disease intersection genes were inputted into the STRING database (https://cn.string-db.org/), with the species was restricted to “Homes sapiens.” A protein–protein interaction (PPI) network was constructed by setting a medium confidence score threshold of >0.4. The resulting PPI data was downloaded and stored in the TSV format. Subsequently, the obtained file was imported into the Cytoscape 3.7.2 software for analysis, enabling the identification of core targets within the network.


### 2.5. Gene Ontology and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes enrichment analysis
  All targets were imported into the DAVID database to conduct an enrichment analysis of Gene Ontology and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG). The data obtained was screened with a significance level of P < .05. The first 10 plots were then selected. We obtained the main biological processes (BP), molecular functions (MF), cellular components (CC), and signal pathways involved in the treatment of MVA with SZTX capsule. We also analyzed the core target and its pathway, selected the 10 pathways with the most obvious enrichment, and constructed a “ core compound-core target-pathway network” by using Cytoscape 3.7.2 software to visualize the results.


### 2.6. Molecule docking
  The core active ingredients were used as ligands, and the 3D structure of small molecules were obtained from the PubChem database (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/). In PDB database (http://rcsb.org/), the 3D structure of the core protein was selected under the conditions of low resolution and original ligand. We used PyMOL to remove water molecules and other processes on the protein, and imported it into AutoDockTools to add hydrogen atoms. Subsequently, the active ingredient ligands and protein receptors were transformed into files in pdbqt format. AutoDockTools was employed to perform docking simulations, and the result with the lowest binding energy was selected.


## 3. Results

### 3.1. Screening of active compounds and corresponding targets
  From the TCMSP database, a total of 22 active ingredients were identified for RS, 65 for DS, 8 for SQ, 7 for CX, and 3 for BP after rigorous screening in the SZTX capsule after screening. The active ingredients of SZ and MD were not found in the TCMSP database. We obtained 13 active ingredients of SZ and 18 of MD from the BATMAN-TCM database. A total of 130 active ingredients of SZTX capsule were obtained.

  After removing the repeated target genes, 630 targets of active ingredients were obtained. The component-target network (Fig. 1) was constructed by using Cytoscape 3.7.2 software. The network showed that SZTX capsule can achieve the therapeutic effect through multiple targets and components, reflecting the complexity of TCM in treating diseases. The “Network Analyzer” function was used to conduct topology analysis on the network, and the top 7 components of the degree were taken as the core components of SZTX capsule (Table 2).

  Despite D-Mannitol and Ursolic acid exhibiting low oral bioavailability and drug-likeness, studies have demonstrated their potential benefits. D-Mannitol has been shown to enhance lipid metabolism and increase energy expenditure. At the same time, Ursolic acid has been found to inhibit the NF-κB pathway and possess scavenging and anti-apoptotic properties against reactive oxygen species (ROS), thereby exerting a protective effect on the heart.[22,23] They do have a role, so they are included in the study. The structures of core ingredients are shown in Figure 2.


### 3.2. Screening for targets related to MVA
  By employing the keywords “microvascular angina” and “cardiac syndrome X,” this study amalgamated disease-associated targets from 4 databases, namely GeneCards, OMIM, DisGeNET, and TTD. Targets closely associated with MVA were selected, resulting in 1789 disease targets after merging and eliminating duplicated genes.


### 3.3. Targets of SZTX capsule in the treatment of MVA
  We took the intersection between targets of SZTX capsule and MVA through Venny 2.1.0 tool. As shown in Figure 3, 142 intersection targets were finally obtained, which may be the potential targets of SZTX capsule in treating MVA.


### 3.4. Construction of “Drug-Component-Target-Disease” Network
  Using Cytoscape 3.7.2 software, a “Drug-Component-Target-Disease” Network (Fig. 4) was constructed by importing the drugs, active ingredients, disease, and intersection targets. In the network, the “rectangle” represents “Shenzhi Tongxin Capsule,” the “triangle” represents drugs, the “hexagon” represents the active ingredients of drugs, the “V” represents MVA, and the “diamond” represents the intersection target. The Figure comprises 257 nodes, 992 edges, 106 active ingredients, and 142 potential targets.


### 3.5. Construction and analysis of PPI network
  The names of all target genes were uploaded to the STRING database, with “Homo sapiens” selected as the species. After removing free nodes, a PPI network (Fig. 5) was constructed. The network consists of 142 nodes and 1525 edges, with an average node degree of 21.5. In the network, nodes represent proteins, and edges represent the relationships between proteins.

  The protein interaction information was obtained from the STRING database and imported into Cytoscape 3.7.2 software. A PPI network was constructed through topological analysis. In the network, nodes with larger sizes and darker colors indicate higher-quality proteins, indicating their importance in the treatment. The core targets, which had the deepest color, were located in the center of the network (Fig. 6). These 6 genes were speculated to be the core targets of SZTX capsule in the treatment of MVA, and their basic information was provided in Table 3.


### 3.6. Gene Ontology enrichment analysis
  Using the DAVID database, we performed enrichment analysis and identified 777 significantly enriched items (P < .05). Among these, 610 items were enriched in BP, 75 items in CC, and 92 items in MF. We selected the top 10 enriched items from each category to visualize the results and generated a bubble chart, as shown in Figure 7. The enriched BP include positive gene expression regulation, negative gene expression regulation, and response to hypoxia. Regarding CC, the enriched terms encompass extracellular space, cell surface, receptor complex, plasma membrane, and extracellular region. Regarding MF, the enriched functions mainly revolve around RNA polymerase II transcription factor activity, protein binding, zinc ion binding, and identical protein binding.


### 3.7. KEGG enrichment analysis
  Using a significance threshold of P < .05, we conducted a KEGG enrichment analysis and identified 127 signaling pathways. To provide a concise overview, we selected the top 10 pathways for visualization (Fig. 8). Notably, these pathways include in cancer, Chemical carcinogenesis-receptor activation, Mitogen-activated protein kinases (MAPK) signaling pathway, Proteoglycans in cancer, lipid and atherosclerosis, among others, which are considered vital signaling pathways. are the main signaling pathways. Furthermore, based on this analysis and relevant literature sources,[24,25] we deduced that the MAPK signaling pathway significantly treats MVA with SZTX capsule. Hence, we specifically visualized the MAPK signaling pathway in Figure 9.

  The relationship between the core compounds, core targets, and pathways was visualized represented using Cytoscape 3.7.2. In the visualization (Fig. 10), the inner circle represents the core active components, the diamond in the middle circle represents the core targets, and the outer hexagon represents the signaling pathways. Notably, quercetin was found to modulate the MAPK signaling pathway, PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, and lipid and atherosclerosis by targeting TP53, TNF, and IL6. This observation suggests that SZTX capsule exerts its therapeutic effects on MVA through multiple targets and pathways.


### 3.8. Molecule docking analysis
  According to network pharmacological analysis, the core ingredients of SZTX capsule in treating MVA were identified as stigmasterol, crocetin, beta-sitosterol, D-mannitol, quercetin, ursolic acid, and kaempferol. PPI network analysis suggested that ALB, AKT1, INS, IL6, TNF, and TP53 might be the core targets of the capsule. Molecular docking was performed between the core ingredients and targets, and the results are presented in Table 4. The binding energies were <0 kcal/mol, indicating successful binding between the components and proteins. Lower binding energies signify more stable binding. Binding energies ≤ −5.0 kJ/mol indicate a solid binding capacity between the receptor and ligand.[26] Stigmasterol exhibited the lowest binding energy of −8.28 kcal/mol with TP53. Other compounds and targets also showed favorable binding energies, except for D-mannitol. The docking results with strong binding were further visualized using Pymol, as depicted in Figure 11.


## 4. Discussion
  MVA, commonly called “cardiac syndrome X,” is characterized by angina pectoris symptoms, despite having standard coronary angiography and the absence of coronary vasospasm.[27] The coronary microcirculation comprises pre-arterioles (diameter < 500 µm), arterioles (<200 µm), and capillaries (<10µm), which are not visible through angiography.[28] Microcirculation plays a significant role in the heart by contributing to most resistance. It facilitates blood distribution based on myocardial oxygen demand and regulates blood flow. CMD affects cardiac blood circulation, resulting in myocardial ischemia and presenting as angina pectoris. Endothelial dysfunction is considered to be the potential mechanism of CMD, where an imbalance of endothelium-derived relaxing factors such as NO and endothelium-derived constrictors such as endothelin leads to the enhancement of vasoconstriction and myocardial ischemia.[28,29] Inflammation-induced cytokines can damage the function of endothelial cells. Endothelial dysfunction is a necessary step in the progress of inflammation, studies show that low inflammation promotes vascular dysfunction in MVA patients.[30] The CFR in MVA patients with elevated CRP is significantly reduced, indicating the presence of CMD. This suggests that inflammation plays a role in modulating the coronary microvascular response in MVA patients.[31] Traditional anti-angina and risk reduction therapies for CMD lack evidence to support the treatment of CMD and may not be effective in alleviating MVA.[32] MVA is classified as “chest arthralgia” in TCM. TCM has gained widespread acceptance in treating CAD due to its safety and efficacy. Research has demonstrated that TCM’s active ingredients, extracts, and compounds can improve endothelial function and reduce inflammation and oxidative stress.[33,34] SZTX capsule is known for its Qi-replenishing, Yin nourishing, and blood-stasis-removing properties. However, the specific active ingredients and underlying mechanisms of SZTX capsule in treating MVA remain unclear. Due to its complex composition and multi-target characteristics, this study employed network pharmacology to predict the material basis and potential mechanism, followed by molecular docking to validate the predicted results.

  A comprehensive screening from the TCMSP database yielded 130 active ingredients of SZTX capsule. Among them, the core active ingredients were stigmasterol, crocetin, beta-sitosterol, D-mannitol, quercetin, ursolic acid, and kaempferol. Notably, stigmasterol, a phytosterol compound, exhibit various biological activities, including anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and immune regulatory effects. It also holds potential therapeutic benefits in managing dyslipidemia and cardiovascular disease.[35] The anti-inflammatory properties of stigmasterol are attributed to its ability to stimulate the production of anti-inflammatory cytokines, suppress the release of inflammatory mediators, and inhibit the activity of inducible nitric oxide synthase and cyclooxygenase-2.[36] Furthermore, stigmasterol exerts regulatory effects on cholesterol metabolism and transport. It facilitates intestinal cholesterol secretion via a pathway independent of liver X receptor, thereby leading to the delay of progression of CAD.[37] Oxidative stress exacerbates endothelial dysfunction by promoting endothelial cell apoptosis and upregulating adhesion factors, thereby contributing to myocardial ischemia and atherosclerosis development. However, crocetin protects vascular endothelium and myocardial cells by mitigating oxidative stress and exerting cardiovascular benefits.[38] It has been proved that crocetin delays the progress of atherosclerosis by inhibiting the inflammatory reaction related to the p38 MAPK pathway and oxidative stress.[39] Beta-sitosterol is a common phytosterol, which has cholesterol-lowering, endothelial protection, anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and anti-atherosclerosis effects.[40,41] Recent research shows beta-sitosterol plays vital roles in transcriptional regulation in endothelial protective and antiatherogenic effects against ox-LDL.[40] Quercetin and kaempferol are flavonoids. The intake of flavonoids can improve endothelial function and reduce the risk of cardiovascular disease.[42] Quercetin confers notable cardiovascular benefits, including endothelium-independent vasodilatory effects, inflammatory marker attenuation, nitric oxide (NO) protection, and endothelial function during oxidative stress.[43] However, a double-blind, placebo-controlled, randomized clinical trial demonstrated that supplementation of quercetin (500 mg/day) in post-myocardial infarction patients for 8 weeks significantly enhanced total antioxidant capacity and improved the insecurity dimension of their quality of life. However, it did not yield significant effects on inflammatory factors.[44] A recent study showed that kaempferol alleviated ox-LDL-induced apoptosis in aortic endothelial cells by upregulating miR-26a-5p via inactivation of the TLR4/NF-κB signaling pathway.[45] Clinical trials investigating the cardioprotective effects of kaempferol are limited, and its potential impact remains uncertain. Further experiments are necessary to assess the potential benefits of kaempferol on the cardiovascular system.[46] Endothelial dysfunction, oxidative stress, and inflammation are closely related to the occurrence and development of cardiovascular diseases. SZTX capsule may play a role in cardiovascular protection by improving endothelial function, anti-inflammation, and anti-oxidative stress.

  The PPI network analysis speculates that ALB, AKT1, INS, IL6, TNF, and TP53 are the key targets of SZTX capsule in treating MVA. ALB, which is synthesized in the liver and is the most abundant protein in vertebrate plasma, may play a predictive role in stress cardiomyopathy.[47] Animal experiments have demonstrated that treatment with ALB exhibits a powerful protective effect against focal ischemia, while higher levels of ALB are associated with a reduced risk of adverse events.[48] ALB synthesis increases in inflammatory states and its therapeutic effects are mediated in part by regulating inflammation and oxidative stress.[49] As a significant hub in multiple signaling pathways, AKT plays a crucial role in phosphorylating numerous downstream targets involved in various cardiovascular processes, including cell growth and proliferation, angiogenesis, vasodilation, and vascular remodeling.[50] The vascular protective effect on endothelial cells primarily relies on endothelial nitric oxide synthase (eNOS), which generates NO, leading to vasodilation and alleviating myocardial ischemia.[51] Stimulators such as vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) can activate AKT in endothelial cells, resulting in AKT-dependent phosphorylation of eNOS and facilitating NO release.[52] It has been demonstrated that AKT1 is the primary isoform responsible for the phosphorylation of eNOS and the promotion of endothelial angiogenesis. Moreover, the signaling of AKT1 in endothelial cells is essential for the resolution of ischemia following injury.[53,54] Cardiac Syndrome X is thought to be an inflammation-mediated disease characterized by pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL6 and TNF. Inflammation plays a significant role in inducing endothelial dysfunction and reducing NO bioavailability.[55] The canakinumab Anti-Inflammatory Thrombosis Outcomes Study (CANTOS) demonstrated that targeted inhibition of the IL6 upstream signal IL1β and modulation of the IL6 signaling pathway could effectively reduce the occurrence of cardiovascular events.[56] A two-sample Mendelian randomization study noted that higher TNF levels were associated with a higher risk of cardiovascular disease.[57] TP53-induced glycolysis and apoptosis regulator (TIGAR) protects against oxidative stress. Research has demonstrated that TIGAR decreases the levels of ROS and activates the CYP27A1/LXRα pathway, upregulating ABCA1 and ABCG1 expression and promoting cholesterol efflux.[58] The occurrence of MVA may be attributed to the decreased release or bioavailability of NO. SZTX capsule may enhance the phosphorylation of eNOS, through AKT1, leading to increased NO release, vasodilation, and alleviation of myocardial ischemia. Additionally, SZTX capsule can regulate inflammatory reaction, improve endothelial function, and mitigate oxidative stress, thereby reducing cardiovascular adverse events.

  The treatment of MVA with SZTX capsule involves various biological processes, including positive regulation of gene expression, negative regulation of gene expression, and response to hypoxia. According to the KEGG enrichment analysis, the therapeutic effect of SZTX capsule mainly depends on the MAPK signaling pathway, PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, Hypoxia inducible factor-1 (HIF-1) signaling pathway, and lipid and atherosclerosis pathway. The MAPK pathway encompasses extracellular signal-regulated protein kinase, c-Jun NH2-terminal kinase, and p38 MAPK, which transmits extracellular signals to the nucleus to regulate cell growth, migration, differentiation, and apoptosis.[59] Extensive evidence supports the activation of MAPKs during myocardial ischemia and/or reperfusion, and their role in cellular injury is significant. However, the impact of p38 MAPK activation on myocardial ischemic injury remains controversial.[25] It has been demonstrated that downregulation of the Formyl peptide receptor 1 gene effectively attenuated inflammation, myocardial apoptosis, and ventricular remodeling in rats with ischemia/reperfusion injury by inhibiting the activation of the MAPK signaling pathway.[60] The PI3K-Akt signaling pathway is an intrinsic adverse feedback regulatory pathway restricting pro-inflammatory and apoptotic events triggered by noxious stimuli.[61] The protective effect of Jinhe Yangxin prescription is attributed to its modulation of this pathway, which effectively inhibits oxidative stress, suppresses inflammatory cytokines production, and attenuates apoptosis following myocardial ischemia.[61] NO plays a crucial role in maintaining the normal vascular function of both endothelial cells and smooth muscle cells. The phosphorylation of eNOS in the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway is responsible for regulating eNOS activity and subsequent NO production, which is vital in preventing and treating endothelial dysfunction.[62] HIF-1 is a critical regulator of the cellular response to hypoxia, leading to various effects such as endothelial dysfunction, cell proliferation, angiogenesis, and inflammatory response. Moreover, it accelerates atherosclerotic plaque growth and rupture by upregulating key factors such as VEGF, NO, ROS, and PDGF.[63] However, in the context of myocardial ischemia, HIF can elicit a cascade of beneficial reactions upon sensing hypoxia signals. These include promoting angiogenesis and facilitating metabolic adaptation to ischemic conditions, which can ultimately mitigate the long-term detrimental effects of ischemic injury and ischemia-reperfusion.[64] The activation of HIF-1 has been proven to have a cardioprotective effect in the case of myocardial ischemia.[64] KEGG enrichment analysis revealed that SZTX capsule could modulate multiple signaling pathways, leading to anti-inflammatory, apoptosis inhibiting, endothelial function-improving, and angiogenesis regulating effects. These effects collectively contribute to ameliorating symptoms and improving prognosis in MVA.

  This study performed molecular docking on 7 critical active ingredients and 6 key targets of SZTX capsule. The results revealed successful interactions between all active ingredients and their respective targets. Of the 42 receptor-ligand docking results, 31 exhibited binding energies ≤ −5 kcal/mol. Notably, stigmasterol, beta-sitosterol, and ursolic acid demonstrated binding energies < −5.0 kcal/mol when interacting with the core targets. This suggests their potential significance and stability in the mechanism of MVA treatment by SZTX capsule.

  Although this study conducted research and analysis on the practical components and mechanisms of action of SZTX capsule in the treatment of MVA and obtained certain conclusions, it should be noted that the target prediction of multi-component TCM based on network pharmacology has limited accuracy. This is because various factors, such as different processing methods, decoction techniques, and decoction duration, can influence the efficacy of TCM. Therefore, the composition of TCM formulations used in experiments or clinical settings is more complex than that of individual TCM components. Network pharmacology techniques provide qualitative predictions of drug components and targets, but the precise pharmacological effects need to be verified through animal experiments or clinical trials.


## 5. Conclusions
  In this study, we employed network pharmacology and molecular docking techniques to systematically analyzed the key targets and signaling pathways involved in treating MVA with SZTX capsule. Our findings revealed that the main active ingredients of the capsule include stigmasterol, crocetin, beta-sitosterol, D-mannitol, quercetin, ursolic acid, and kaempferol. These active ingredients exert their effects by targeting ALB, AKT1, INS, IL6, TNF, and TP53 and modulating signaling pathways such as MAPK, PI3K-Akt, and HIF-1. Through their anti-inflammatory, anti-oxidative stress, angiogenesis-regulating, and endothelial function-improving properties, these active ingredients contribute to regulating myocardial oxygen supply, alleviating angina symptoms, and overall cardioprotective effects. Our study highlights the multi-component, multi-target, multi-pathway, and multi-mechanism characteristics of SZTX capsule in treating MVA. These findings provide a solid theoretical foundation for future clinical research and drug development in this area.
