# Mechanism of Yiqi Huoxue Huatan recipe in the treatment of coronary atherosclerotic disease through network pharmacology and experiments


## Abstract
  In recent years, with population aging and economic development, morbidity and mortality of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease associated with atherosclerosis (AS) have gradually increased. In this study, a combination of network pharmacology and experimental verification was used to systematically explore the action mechanism of Yiqi Huoxue Huatan Recipe (YHHR) in the treatment of coronary atherosclerotic heart disease (CAD). We searched and screened the active ingredients of Coptis chinensis, Astragalus membranaceus, Salvia miltiorrhiza, and Hirudo. We also searched multiple databases for related target genes corresponding to the compounds and CAD. STRING was used to construct the protein-protein interaction (PPI) network of genes. Metascape was used to perform gene ontology (GO) enrichment analysis and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis for common targets to analyze the main pathways, and finally, the molecular docking and main possible pathways were verified by experimental studies. Firstly, a total of 1480 predicted target points were obtained through the Swiss Target Prediction database. After screening, merging, and deleting duplicate values, a total of 768 targets were obtained. Secondly, “Coronary atherosclerotic heart disease” was searched in databases such as the OMIM, GeneCards, and TTD. 1844 disease-related targets were obtained. Among PPI network diagram of YHHR-CAD, SRC had the highest degree value, followed by AKT1, TP53, hsp90aa1 and mapk3. The KEGG pathway bubble diagram was drawn using Chiplot, the Signal pathways such as NF kappa B signaling pathway, Lipid and AS, and Apelin signaling pathway are closely related to the occurrence of CAD. The PCR and Western blot methods were used to detect the expression of NF-κB p65. When compared with that in the model group, the expression of NF-κB p65mRNA decreased in the low-concentration YHHR group, with P < .05, while the expression of NF-κB p65mRNA decreased significantly in the high-concentration YHHR group, with P < .01. On the other hand, when compared with that in the model group, the expression of NF-κB p65 decreased in the low-concentration YHHR group, but was not statistically significant, while the expression of NF-κB p65 was significant in the high-concentration YHHR group, and has statistical significance with P < .05. YHHR has been shown to resist inflammation and AS through the SRC/NF-κB signaling pathway.


## 1. Introduction
  In recent years, with population aging and economic development, morbidity and mortality of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease associated with atherosclerosis (AS) have gradually increased.[1] According to the report of the World Health Organization, coronary atherosclerotic heart disease (CAD) is the leading cause of death in non-communicable diseases.[2] AS has a complex pathogenetic mechanism and is generally believed to be associated with lipid metabolism disorder, microcirculatory dysfunction, inflammatory response, and endothelial dysfunction.[3–5]

  Supported by evidence-based studies, statins can be effective in reducing the morbidity and mortality of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease by targeting low-density lipoprotein.[6] However, there are patients who are statin intolerant, poorly compliant, or resistant. In addition, side effects of statins such as liver function damage and myolysis can lead to poor prognosis.[6,7] Considering the few side effects, more and more natural Chinese herbal medicines have been applied in the treatment of cerebral ischemia-reperfusion injury, myocardial ischemia reperfusion injury, AS, thrombogenesis, and CADs.[8,9]

  Yiqi Huoxue Huatan Recipe (YHHR) was developed based on a large number of clinical practices combined with the theory of traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) literature, and contains main ingredients such as Astragalus membranaceus, Salvia miltiorrhiza, Hirudo, and Coptis chinensis. The active ingredients of Astragalus membranaceus, such as Astragalus polysaccharide and Astragaloside, have immunoregulatory, antioxidant, and anti-inflammatory effects that promote cardiac microvessels formation and protect the heart.[10] These astragalus extracts also help reduce the release of ROS, and increase the activation of nuclear factor (erythroid-derived 2)-like 2 (Nrf2) and expression of antioxidant cytoprotective factor.[11] Tanshinone IIA (TS IIA) is the main fat-soluble active ingredient of Salvia miltiorrhiza and is generally used for the prevention and treatment of cardiovascular diseases including AS. As studied, TS IIA is an effective free radical scavenger with antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects, and can inhibit the expression of chemokines as well as the progression of AS by inhibiting the proliferation and migration of ox-LDL-induced macrophages.[12–15] Hirudin, the main ingredient of Hirudo, and its analogue lepirudin can be used as anticoagulant medicines, which have been confirmed in the studies on heparin-induced thrombocytopenia, deep vein thrombosis, and acute coronary syndrome.[16,17] Some studies showed that Coptis chinensis can inhibit activation of pro-inflammatory factor signaling pathway, thereby ameliorating hepatic ischemia-reperfusion injury.[18,19]

  TCM has a complex composition that allows for its multiple targets and pleiotropic properties. Therefore, it is difficult to describe the exact mechanism of action. Network pharmacology is a newly developed interdisciplinary subject for drug systems research based on artificial intelligence and big data that is systematic, relevant, and predictable.[20] It can be used to build a multi-molecular, multi-target, and multi-link “drug-compound-target” network model to understand the mechanism of action of TCM.[21] In this study, network pharmacology was used to predict the molecular mechanism of YHHR in the treatment of CAD.


## 2. Materials and method

### 2.1. Research procedures
  The research was conducted in steps as follows: Identify the active ingredients of YHHR using the Traditional Chinese Medicine Systems Pharmacology Database and Analysis Platform (TCMSP, https://old.tcmsp-e.com/tcmsp.php), SwissADME (http://www.swissadme.ch/), literature search, and other methods. Then, predict the targets of YHHR active ingredients through TCMSP, Swiss Target Prediction database (STP, www.swiss target prediction.ch), and literature search. Screen for disease targets using GeneCards (https://www.genecards.org/), OMIM (https://www.omim.org/), and TTD database (https://ngdc.cncb.ac.cn/) systems. Find the common targets of YHHR active ingredients and CAD through VENNY 2.1 (https://bioinfogp.cnb.csic.es/tools/venny/), screen and construct the YHHR ingredient-CAD target-pathway ingredient-disease target network diagram through Cytoscape_v3.8.0, and obtain network topology parameters to determine the core targets and the main active ingredients associated with medicine efficacy. Input the common targets into the String website (https://cn.string-db.org/) for the protein-protein interaction (PPI) analysis, and select the core targets according to topological structure. Perform Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis for common targets through the Metascape database (https://metascape.org/) to obtain relevant biological information. Molecular docking and Verifying related signaling pathway proteins through in vitro cell experiments. The flow chart is shown in Figure 1.


### 2.2. Screening of active ingredients and targets of YHHR
  YHHR consists mainly of Astragalus membranaceus, Salvia miltiorrhiza, Hirudo, and Coptis chinensis, which were used as key words to search the ingredients in the TCMSP database. Then, compounds with an oral bioavailability ≥ 30% and a drug likeness ≥ 0.18 were selected for screening.[22] The active chemical ingredients of Hirudo were collected according to related literature in PubMed, and the active ingredients were screened using the SwissADME (http://www.swissadme.ch/) database with inclusion criteria as follows: 1. High GI absorption; 2. Fulfillment of 2 of out 5 DL-related rules (Lipinski, Ghose, Veber, Egan, and Muegge). Next, the targets of the active ingredients of YHHR were collected using TCMSP and Swiss Target Prediction databases, and the target protein was converted to the target gene using the UniProt database (https://www. uniprot.org/) with standardized gene names.


### 2.3. Determination of CAD-related targets
  Using “coronary atherosclerotic heart disease” as the keyword for disease, and “Homo sapiens” as the constraint, the potential targets in GeneCards, OMIM, and TTD databases were explored. In the GeneCards database, a higher Score value corresponded to a closer relationship between the target and the disease. As can be seen from experience, when there are too many targets, the target with a Score value greater than the median value is set as a potential target of CAD, and the target of the CAD would be obtained by deleting duplicate values after the merge of the targets from the 3 disease databases.


### 2.4. Construction of PPI network of YHHR ingredients-CAD disease targets
  The active ingredient targets of a compound and the disease targets were imported into the VENNY website (bioinfogp.cnb.csic.es) for identification and visualization, obtaining the common targets. Using Cytoscape_v3.8.0 and its built-in tools, the network of YHHR ingredients-CAD targets was constructed, and the network topological parameters of the active ingredients and targets, including degree, betweenness, and closeness, were analyzed. Furthermore, a PPI network model was built by submitting the common targets to the STRING11.0 database (https://string-db.org), and the PPI network was constructed by setting the biological species as “Homo sapiens” and the minimum interaction threshold as “highest confidence” (>0.9), while other settings were left as default. Then, the MCODE plug-in was used to further analyze the PPI network according to the Cytoscape_v3.8.0 visual analysis results, where the degree reflects the number of connections between the network nodes and other nodes and is an important parameter to determine whether a target protein is a key target.[23–25] In this study, a node with a degree value more than twice greater than the median value was considered an important node in the network, that is, the core target. Consequently, a potential protein function module was obtained, and its functions were described through analysis of the biological process in which it participated.


### 2.5. Biological function and pathway analysis
  The common targets were input into the Metascape platform, where P value < .05 and P value < .01 were used as thresholds of GO and KEGG pathways for GO analysis and KEGG pathway analysis, respectively, and the first several entries were selected for visualization. The GO enrichment analysis mainly considered the biological process, cell composition, and molecular function of the target gene, while the KEGG enrichment analysis showed the potential biological pathways and functions related to the target genes. Then, the Chiplot (www.chiplot.online) was used to draw the bubble diagram of the pathway.


### 2.6. Molecular docking
  Molecular docking of the main core components selected from YHHR with the main targets was carried out through protein data bank (PDB, https://www.rcsb.org/) Database to obtain the protein crystal structure pdb file. Through TCM system pharmacology database analysis platform (TCMSP, http://lsp.nwu.edu.cn/tcmsp.php )Obtain the small molecule compound mol2 file of YHHR. Import the protein crystal structure pdb file into PyMOL to remove irrelevant ligands and water molecules. At the same time, import the small molecule compounds into AutoDock for hydrotreating pretreatment. Set the protein GridBox docking box. Select Set Rigid for macrocrystal, save and run the dpf file for docking, and use PyMOL for visualization.


### 2.7. Pathway verification in in vitro experiments
  The study was conducted in accordance with the Basic & Clinical Pharmacology & Toxicology policy for experimental and clinical studies.[26]


### 2.8. Materials
  RAW264.7 cells (Jiangsu Aniphe); β-actin protein and NF-κB p65 protein antibody (proteintech); Ox-LDL (yiyun Biotechnologies); high-glucose medium for cells (Gibco), fetal calf serum (Shanghai XP Biomed Ltd.). Trypsin and cell lysate were purchased from Beijing Beyotime Company; Triozol reagent (Invitrogen), reverse transcription kit (TaKa Ra) and SYBR green PCR kit (Vazyme), primers GAPDH, NF-κB p65, SRC (Shanghai Sangon).


### 2.9. Cell culture
  Cell experiments were carried out in accordance with the guidelines and regulations of Shanghai University of Traditional Chinese Medicine. RAW264.7 cells were cultured in DMEM (high glucose medium + 10% fetal calf serum) in a 37°C thermostatic incubator with 5% CO2, and the culture solution was changed every 2 days. When cells grew and covered 70% to 80% of the medium surface, digestive passage was conducted, and then 5 to 7 generations of cells were taken for experiments and planted in a 6-well plate at a cell density of 1 × 105/mL. The 4 TCM were purchased from the pharmacy of Shuguang Hospital Affiliated to Shanghai University of Traditional Chinese Medicine. The prepared TCM compounds were decocted with deionized water (1:10, 1:15, w/v) for 30 minutes every time, and the filtered and concentrated solution was stored in a refrigerator at −20°C.


### 2.10. Experimental culture in groups
  The cells were divided into a no-treatment control group, a model group, a 5% YHHR-containing medicated serum group, and a 10% YHHR-containing medicated serum group. A monolayer culture was conducted overnight, and the primary culture medium was aspirated. Ox-LDL (80 μg/mL) was added to groups other than the control group to induce macrophage foaming for 24 hours, fresh complete culture medium was added to the model group, and medicated serum containing different concentrations of YHHR was added to the medically-intervened groups and cultured for 24 hour.


### 2.11. RNA extraction and real-time quantitative PCR detection
  The total cellular RNA of the collected cells was extracted with Trizol Reagen, and the cDNA was reverse-transcribed according to the reverse transcription steps of the kit. Then, cDNA was added as a template to the primers of NF-κB p65 and SRC, and the expression of NF-κB p65 mRNA was detected by the RT-qPCR method, with GAPDH as internal reference. The sequence of the primers is shown in Table 1.


### 2.12. Detection of western blot
  WB was used to analyze the expressions of NF-κB p65 protein and internal reference β-actin. The antibodies were subjected to NF-kB p65 (1:2000 dilution) and β-actin (1:5000 dilution). Cells were collected and added with 100 μL cell lysate for lysis, and centrifuged at 14,000 g for 10 minutes. Then, a 25 μL loading buffer was added to the supernatant (protein) obtained, and mixed evenly before placing it into a 100°C water bath for 5 minutes. Consequently, the prepared protein specimens were collected for electrophoresis, membrane transfer, and sealing, and applied to the primary antibody overnight. The next day, the membrane was rinsed before adding to the HRP-linked secondary antibody, which was incubated and added with exposure solution to expose PVDF membrane. Lastly, the bands were analyzed using the Image J software (Wayne Rasband, National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD).


### 2.13. Statistical analysis
  Statistical analysis was performed using the statistical software Graph Pad Prism 5.0. The data was expressed with x̄ ± s, and t-test was used for comparison between groups, which showed that the data were statistically significantly different, with P < .05.


## 3. Results

### 3.1. Information on active ingredients and targets of YHHR
  The TCM chemical ingredients such as Astragalus membranaceus, Salvia miltiorrhiza, and Coptis chinensis were searched in the TCMSP database, from which a total of 94 active ingredients were obtained, including 18 Astragalus membranaceus, 62 Salvia miltiorrhiza, and 14 Coptis chinensis. A total of 101 types of chemical ingredients of Hirudo were searched from the literature, and 34 active ingredients were selected from the SwissADME database. A total of 1480 predicted target points were obtained through the Swiss Target Prediction database (The active ingredients of Chinese herbal medicines searched from database are shown in Table 2). After screening, merging, and deleting duplicate values, a total of 768 targets were obtained.


### 3.2. Information on CAD-related targets
  “Coronary atherosclerotic heart disease” was searched in databases such as the OMIM, GeneCards, and TTD. In the GeneCards database, the target with a Score value greater than the median value was set as the potential target of coronary heart disease according to experiences. In other words, after screening by a relevance score > 5, a total of 1678 targets were obtained. Combined and merged with the results from the OMIM and TTD databases, 1844 disease-related targets were obtained after deletion of duplicate values.


### 3.3. Analysis of common targets related to YHHR and CAD
  In the VENNY website, 768 compound targets were intersected with 1844 CAD-related targets, obtaining 344 common targets, as shown in Figure 2.


### 3.4. Construction of PPI network of YHHR in the treatment of coronary heart disease
  Figure 3 illustrates a drug-active ingredients-disease-target network diagram constructed using the Cytoscape_v3.8.0 software. To explore the molecular mechanism of YHHR in the treatment of CAD, 344 common target genes were imported into the STRING database to construct the PPI network diagram, as shown in Figure 4.


### 3.5. Screening of core targets
  The drug-active ingredients-disease-target network diagram shown in Figure 3 was analyzed using the built-in tools of Cytoscape_v3.8.0 based on the construction of PPI network, by which a total of 304 nodes and 3770 edges were obtained. Analyzed using the built-in Network Analyzer of Cytoscape_v3.8.0, it can be seen that the leading active ingredients in YHHR are quercetin (degree: 100, betweenness centrality: 0.036178966, closeness centrality: 0.387795276), obakunone (degree: 39, betweenness centrality: 0.006394149, closeness centrality: 0.346221441), and moupinamide (degree: 27, betweenness centrality: 0.003363639, closeness centrality: 0.339070568). Quercetin was predicted to be the main ingredient of YHHR used to treat coronary heart diseases (see Table 3 for details). The mean degree value of topological parameters was 24.8, and the median degree value was 16. The node with a degree value more than twice the median value (degree ≥ 32) was used as the key target, and 81 core targets were screened out. Among these core targets, SRC had the highest degree value (140), followed by AKT1 (132), TP53 (126), hsp90aa1 (120), and mapk3 (118). Therefore, these 5 targets were believed to be the most significant core targets of YHHR in the treatment of CAD.


### 3.6. GO and KEGG pathway analyses of common targets
  The aforementioned 344 overlapping targets were imported into Metascape database for GO enrichment analysis (including GO Cellular Components, GO Biological Processes, and GO Molecular Functions) and KEGG pathway analysis, and the results are shown in Figures 5–7. The top 5 cellular components were membrane raft (GO: 0045121), receptor complex (GO: 0043235), side of membrane (GO: 0098552), transcription regulator complex (GO: 0005667), and focal adhesion (GO: 0005925). The top 5 terms in the biological process category were “response to hormone” (GO: 0009725), “positive regulation of cell migration” (GO: 0030335), “positive regulation of locomotion” (GO: 0040017), “positive regulation of cell motility” (GO: 2000147), and “positive regulation of cellular component movement” (GO: 0051272). These biological processes were closely related to molecular functions as follows: protein kinase activity (GO: 0004672), kinase binding (GO: 0019900), nuclear receptor activity (GO: 0004879), RNA polymerase II-specific DNA-binding transcription factor binding (GO: 0061629), and lipid binding (GO: 0008289).

  The KEGG pathway bubble diagram was drawn using Chiplot, in which the bubble colors from green to black represent the gradually increased Log10(P) values, the area of bubbles represents the number of genes in this pathway, and the horizontal axis represents the ratio of genes in this pathway to all genes. The top 20 results were selected to draw the bubble diagram, as shown in Figure 8. The larger the bubble, the more genes were enriched in this pathway. The redder the color, the smaller the P value, and the more significant the results.


### 3.7. Molecular docking results
  11 core components selected from YHHR were molecular docked with the target SRC, among which Obacunone and Moupinamide failed to dock (Table 4). Other docking results are shown in Figure 9 below.


### 3.8. Verification of main pathway results through in vitro cell experiments
  Based on the cell culture, the PCR and Western blot methods were used to detect the expression of NF-κB p65. As demonstrated in Figure 10, when compared with that in the model group, the expression of NF-κB p65mRNA decreased in the low-concentration YHHR group, with P < .05, while the expression of NF-κB p65mRNA decreased significantly in the high-concentration YHHR group, with P < .01. On the other hand, when compared with that in the model group, the expression of NF-κB p65 decreased in the low-concentration YHHR group, but was not statistically significant, while the expression of NF-κB p65 was significant in the high-concentration YHHR group, and has statistical significance with P < .05.

  As can be seen from Figure 11, when compared with that in the model group, the expression of SRC, which was detected by the PCR method, in the low-concentration YHHR group decreased, but was not significantly different, while that in the high-concentration YHHR group significantly decreased, with P < .01.


## 4. Discussion
  Previous studies have shown that YHHR can be used to stabilize atherosclerotic plaques, protect vascular endothelial function, relieve inflammatory response, and reduce the probability of coronary heart diseases. However, its specific mechanism is still yet to know.[27,28] Network pharmacology is expected to provide a certain theoretical basis for YHHR in the treatment of coronary heart diseases.

  In this study, the active ingredients of YHHR were first obtained through databases and literature mining. It was found that the main ingredients in YHHR used to treat coronary heart disease may be quercetin, obacunone, mounpinamide, Berberine, Amyrin, Crocetin, (3R) -3- (2-hydroxy-3,4-dimethoxyphenyl) chroman-7-ol, and other compounds. Through follow-up analysis, it has been confirmed that quercetin can reverse the decrease of oxLDL-induced AMPK activation and the increase of NADPH oxidase expression, thereby maintaining the function of akt/enos and inhibiting the NF-κB signal transduction to resist AS[28–31]; obacunone has several pharmacological effects, Including antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, anti-tumor, and hypoglycemic[32–34]; moupinamide can inhibit the AP-1 and JNK signaling pathways of macrophages by resisting oxidative damage induced by hydrogen peroxide, which can achieve antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects[35,36]; Berberine is widely distributed in various organs, and can mitigate the development of AS by regulating blood lipid, improving hypercholesterolemia, and inhibiting vascular endothelial inflammation[37,38]; Amyrin has been confirmed to have antiplatelet, anti-inflammatory, anti-ulcer, lipid-decreasing, and anti-tumor effects[39,40]; Corcetin can improve oxygen transport rate and diffusion rate, inhibit pro-inflammatory mediators, and protect cells from reactive oxygen species damage, thereby protecting heart and blood vessels.[41] On the other hand, although (3R) -3- (2-hydroxy-3,4-dimethoxyphenyl) chroman-7-ol is one of the main active ingredients of Astragalus membranaceus, its pharmacological action in the treatment of coronary heart disease needs to be further studied.

  Through construction of a drug-active ingredients-disease-target network diagram, 81 core targets of medicine active ingredients in the treatment of coronary heart disease were obtained, in which SRC, AKT1, TP53, hsp90aa1, and mapk3 were the most significant targets. SRC, one of the main proteins in inflammatory response, is previously proven to exhibit anti-tumor effect through covalent modification, isomer regulation, and other mechanism.[42] For AS, SRC can be bound to and activate p47phox and activate reduced nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (NOX), from which a large amount of O2 is produced, thereby promoting endothelial cells, smooth muscle cells, and macrophages to participate in oxidative stress.[43] SRC also participates in the activation of the mitogen activated protein kinase (MAPK) and extracellular signal-regulated kinase 1/2 (ERK1/2) signaling pathways, and promotes cell inflammatory response and proliferation & migration,[44,45] thereby regulating the entire endothelial-smooth muscle macrophage network and promoting the development of AS. AKT1 may inhibit activation of NLRP3 inflammatory corpuscles in myocardial cells and is an important target for intervention in inflammation activation and initiation.[46] TP53 may inhibit the transcription of vascular endothelial growth factor, and therefore, prevent AS.[47] Several studies show that heat shock protein 90 (HSP90) was the target of anti-AS and could resist inflammation,[48–51]oxidative stress, and smooth muscle proliferation, and regulate the endothelial function by inhibiting the expression of HSP90. MAPK is closely associated with the onset of ischemic stroke and is involved in the entire process of occurrence and development of AS.[52]

  Through GO and KEGG enrichment analysis, it was found that YHHR may treat CAD mainly through NF-κB signaling pathway, calcium signaling pathway, and Apelin signaling pathway. NF-κB regulates the genes involved in immunity, inflammation, and cell survival. Several typical pathways that may lead to the activation of NF-κB include tumor necrosis factorα(tnf-α), interleukin-1 (IL-1), or bacterial and viral infection, and the NF-κB-induced genes include almost all immune defenders, such as chemokines, cytokines, adhesion molecules, inflammatory mediators, and apoptosis inhibitors, suggesting that NF-κB can play a key role in the development of immunity, inflammation, and AS.[53–57] One study showed that calcium signaling may be crucial to regulate expression of aortic genes during AS development,[58] and may take effect through the mechanical responses to genetic, mechanical, and environmental damage. These damages can trigger the imbalance of intracellular calcium homeostasis, thereby causing disease development. PPARγ is an upstream gene of LXRα and a member of the nuclear receptor superfamily, and it can activate LXRα and act on the target gene ABC superfamily to mediate cholesterol efflux, thereby promoting the reverse transport of cholesterol,[59] as PPAR plays an important role of regulation in the cholesterol efflux mechanism of macrophages.[60] Apelin is an endogenous peptide that could bind to the apelin receptor (APJ), both of which can be widely expressed in different tissues and organ systems. Many studies have shown that the apelin/APJ system can resist inflammation, oxidative stress, and apoptosis, regulate autophagy, and block excitatory toxicity in addition to other neuroprotective properties, and is involved in several important physiological processes such as angiogenesis, cardiovascular function, cell proliferation, and energy metabolism regulation.[61] As described above, it has previously been confirmed that NF-κB plays the core role in inflammation, immunity, and AS.

  According to the above analysis results, the leading core target SRC and the key pathway NF-κB signaling pathway were used as the mechanism of YHHR in the treatment of CAD. Experiments confirmed that YHHR can regulate the expression of genes and proteins of NF-κB P65 signaling pathway, which is concentration-dependent, and also reduce the expression of SRC. YHHR has also been shown to resist inflammation and AS through the SRC/NF-κB signaling pathway.

  In conclusion, this study is a preliminary investigation of the potential mechanism of YHHR in the treatment of CAD through network pharmacology and in vitro experimental verification, providing a certain scientific basis for the treatment of coronary heart disease with YHHR. However, there are still certain limitations to this study. Firstly, there are limitations of the databases, that is, as the research data were constantly updated, different data of ingredients of medicines, genes, and proteins can be accessed online. Next is the complex mechanism of YHHR in the treatment of CAD; due to the multi-efficacy and multi-target properties of treatment using TCM, it is believed that it has a complex mechanism of action in the pathway. In this study, only very few parts have been verified, and further in-depth studies will be conducted in the future.
