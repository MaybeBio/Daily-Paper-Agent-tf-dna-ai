### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that ToLCPaB greatly reduced the accumulation of its helper virus DNA, and that betaC1 regulates helper virus accumulation by interfering with ATP hydrolysis activity of Rep.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract states that ToLCPaB reduced helper virus DNA accumulation, but no quantitative data (e.g., fold change, time course, replication assays) are provided. Similarly, the claim that betaC1 interferes with ATP hydrolysis activity is based on biochemical studies, but no enzymatic assay details, kinetic parameters, or statistical comparisons are given.  
- **Why it matters** Without quantitative evidence, it is impossible to assess the magnitude of the effect or whether the observed reduction is biologically significant. The central mechanistic claim hinges on demonstrating a clear and reproducible effect on both ATP hydrolysis and viral DNA accumulation.  
- **Resolution test** Provide quantitative data showing the extent of helper virus DNA reduction (e.g., Southern blot or qPCR with statistical analysis) and ATP hydrolysis assays with appropriate controls, replicates, and statistical significance.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Interaction specificity  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that betaC1 interacts with Rep, specifically the C-terminal region of betaC1 with the C-terminus of Rep (RepC), and that betaC1K49A still interacts with RepC.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
- **Concern** The interaction claims are based on unspecified methods (likely co-immunoprecipitation or yeast two-hybrid), but no data are shown. The statement that betaC1K49A did not affect interaction is made without experimental evidence. Additionally, the interaction with mutant RepC proteins (D227A, D289A vs. D262A, K272A, D286A) is described, but the functional significance of these mutations is not explained.  
- **Why it matters** The specificity of the interaction is critical to the proposed mechanism. If the interaction is not robustly demonstrated or if the mutant analysis is not properly controlled, the model could be based on artifacts.  
- **Resolution test** Show interaction data (e.g., co-IP, pull-down, or Y2H) with appropriate positive and negative controls, and demonstrate that the mutant RepC proteins are properly expressed and folded. Provide a rationale for the choice of mutations and their expected effects on ATP binding or hydrolysis.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Mechanistic link  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that betaC1 interferes with ATP hydrolysis activity of Rep, and that docking studies suggest interference with ATP binding.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract states that betaC1 interferes with ATP hydrolysis, but also that docking studies suggest interference with ATP binding. These are distinct mechanisms, and the abstract does not clarify whether the effect is on binding, hydrolysis, or both. The biochemical data and docking results are presented as complementary, but no direct evidence links the interaction to a specific step in the ATPase cycle.  
- **Why it matters** The proposed mechanism is that betaC1 regulates helper virus accumulation by interfering with ATP hydrolysis. If the actual effect is on ATP binding, the mechanistic model would need to be revised. The lack of clarity undermines the central claim.  
- **Resolution test** Provide experimental data distinguishing between effects on ATP binding (e.g., binding assays) and hydrolysis (e.g., ATPase assays with varying ATP concentrations). Clarify whether the docking predictions are validated by experimental results.

- **Concern ID** R1-M4  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Functional relevance  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that betaC1 regulates helper virus accumulation by interfering with ATP hydrolysis activity of Rep.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract does not provide evidence that the observed reduction in helper virus DNA accumulation is directly caused by the betaC1-Rep interaction and ATP hydrolysis interference. No experiments are described that test this causal link, such as using betaC1 mutants that cannot interact with Rep or that lack ATP hydrolysis activity in vivo.  
- **Why it matters** Without a direct test of causality, the correlation between interaction, ATP hydrolysis interference, and reduced viral accumulation could be coincidental. The proposed mechanism requires demonstration that disrupting the interaction or the ATPase interference abolishes the effect on viral accumulation.  
- **Resolution test** Perform in vivo experiments with betaC1 mutants (e.g., K49A) and Rep mutants that disrupt interaction, and measure helper virus DNA accumulation. Show that the effect on viral accumulation correlates with the ability to interact and interfere with ATP hydrolysis.
