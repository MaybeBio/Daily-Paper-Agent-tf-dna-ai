## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text including abstract, introduction, results, discussion, methods, and supplementary information descriptions
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific claims, methodological soundness, evidence quality, and alignment with stated conclusions; limited to content provided in the manuscript
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors present cryo-EM based models of TFIIH in three functional states (apo, transcription pre-initiation complex, and nucleotide excision repair complex), combined with microsecond-long molecular dynamics simulations and graph-theoretical network analysis, to propose that TFIIH undergoes conformational switching that makes XPB and XPD activities mutually exclusive between transcription and NER. They further map disease mutations onto dynamic community interfaces to propose mechanistic classes of mutation effects.
- **Visible evidence base** Three cryo-EM derived structural models, ~1 μs MD simulations per system, dCNA community analysis, PCA, path optimization (PNEB) calculations, Rosetta ddG stability predictions, and mapping of 34 disease mutations
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Supplementary Data 1-3, Supplementary Figures 1-5, Supplementary Movies 1-5, and ModelArchive accession details were referenced but not provided for assessment

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript addresses a significant and timely question regarding the conformational dynamics of TFIIH and how these dynamics relate to its dual functions in transcription and DNA repair. The combination of cryo-EM structures with extensive MD simulations and network analysis is methodologically ambitious and potentially valuable. However, the central claim of a "switch" that makes XPB and XPD activities mutually exclusive rests heavily on computational inference from a limited number of simulations, and the direct experimental validation of this proposed mechanism is absent. The disease mutation analysis is correlative and would benefit from clearer mechanistic validation. The manuscript is well-written and the figures are informative, but the gap between computational prediction and biological demonstration needs to be addressed.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in the fields of transcription regulation, DNA repair, structural biology, and computational biophysics would find this work of interest. The proposed conformational switching mechanism for TFIIH has implications for understanding how a single complex can serve distinct functions in different pathways. The methodological approach combining MD simulations with network analysis could also appeal to computational biologists studying large macromolecular assemblies. Clinically oriented researchers studying xeroderma pigmentosum, trichothiodystrophy, and Cockayne syndrome may find the mutation classification framework useful for interpreting genotype-phenotype relationships.
- **Major strengths** The manuscript presents a comprehensive computational analysis of TFIIH dynamics across three functional states, which is a substantial undertaking given the size and complexity of the complex. The use of multiple complementary computational methods (MD, dCNA, PCA, PNEB) provides a multi-faceted view of the system. The mapping of disease mutations onto dynamic community interfaces rather than static structures is a conceptually novel approach that could have broader applicability. The proposed sequential coordination model for XPB and XPD activities in NER is mechanistically appealing and provides testable predictions.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency for central claim
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims that "an internal regulatory mechanism switches XPB and XPD activities making them mutually exclusive between NER and transcription initiation" and that this switch "ensures precise DNA incision in NER."
  - **Evidence pointer** Figures 4-6, Results section "Network analysis uncovers a key regulatory switch controlling XPB/XPD activities in NER"
  - **Concern** The central claim of a regulatory switch that makes XPB and XPD activities mutually exclusive is derived entirely from computational analyses of three MD simulations. While the dCNA and PCA results are suggestive of different dynamic states, the leap from observed differences in contact probabilities and community structures to a functional "switch" mechanism is not directly supported by experimental evidence. The authors do not demonstrate that the proposed switch actually operates in cells or in vitro, nor do they show that disrupting the proposed switch abolishes the functional separation. The claim that the switch "ensures precise DNA incision" is particularly speculative, as no incision assays or functional experiments are presented.
  - **Why it matters** The mutual exclusivity and sequential coordination of XPB and XPD activities is the central mechanistic insight of the paper. If this claim is not adequately supported, the primary contribution of the work is diminished to a descriptive computational analysis of conformational differences, which is less impactful. The field requires functional validation to distinguish between a genuine regulatory mechanism and a computational inference that may not reflect biological reality.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should either (a) provide experimental evidence (e.g., crosslinking mass spectrometry, FRET-based assays, or biochemical reconstitution experiments) demonstrating that the proposed conformational switch operates and controls XPB/XPD activities, or (b) substantially temper the language of the claims to reflect that this is a computational prediction requiring experimental validation. At minimum, they should demonstrate that the proposed switch is robust across multiple independent simulations and show that the predicted inactive states actually lack enzymatic activity in vitro.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Validation of computational models
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims that the newly modeled regions of the NER-TFIIH complex, including the p62 subunit and XPA termini, were "built de novo" and that the resulting models are suitable for "extensive microsecond-long molecular dynamics simulations."
  - **Evidence pointer** Methods section "Model building", Results section "Reconstruction of a complete NER-TFIIH complex"
  - **Concern** The de novo building of the p62 subunit and XPA terminal regions into cryo-EM density is a challenging task, particularly for regions without known structural homologs. The manuscript does not provide sufficient validation of these models. No metrics are given for the quality of the fit to the EM density (e.g., cross-correlation scores, Q-scores, or local resolution estimates for the newly built regions). The stability of these regions during MD simulations is not discussed, and it is unclear whether the conclusions drawn about p62 dynamics are robust to the initial model-building choices. Without this validation, the reliability of the subsequent dynamic analyses involving these regions is uncertain.
  - **Why it matters** The conformational switching involving p62 repositioning is a key part of the proposed mechanism. If the p62 model is inaccurate or the de novo built regions are not well-supported by density, the conclusions about p62 dynamics and its role in regulating XPB/XPD activities could be artifacts of the model-building process rather than genuine features of the system.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should provide quantitative validation of the de novo built regions, including local resolution estimates, model-to-map fit metrics for the newly built segments, and evidence that these regions are stable in the simulations. They should also demonstrate that the key conclusions about p62 dynamics are insensitive to alternative model-building strategies or initial conformations.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Generalizability of simulation results
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims that "TFIIH dramatically alters its flexibility and dynamics depending on functional context" based on comparisons of three MD simulations, each of ~1 μs duration.
  - **Evidence pointer** Figure 3, Results section "TFIIH conformational dynamics is predicated on functional context"
  - **Concern** The conclusions about differential dynamics across functional states are based on single simulations of each state. While ~1 μs is substantial for a complex of this size, it may not be sufficient to sample the full conformational landscape, particularly for large-scale rearrangements. The manuscript does not report replicate simulations or convergence analyses. The observed differences in B-factors and community structures could reflect insufficient sampling rather than genuine functional differences. Additionally, the simulations start from cryo-EM structures that may already be biased toward particular conformations, and the equilibration protocol may not fully relax the systems.
  - **Why it matters** The claim that the three states have distinct dynamics is foundational to the proposed switch mechanism. If the observed differences are not robust across replicates or are artifacts of limited sampling, the entire interpretive framework is compromised. The field increasingly expects replicate simulations and convergence assessment for such strong dynamic claims.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should provide evidence of convergence (e.g., block averaging, replicate simulations, or examination of principal component distributions over time). They should also demonstrate that the key dynamic differences between states are reproducible across multiple independent simulations starting from different initial velocities or slightly perturbed starting structures.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M4
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Strength of disease mutation conclusions
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims that mapping disease mutations onto network models "reveals clustering into distinct mechanistic classes, affecting translocase functions, protein interactions and interface dynamics."
  - **Evidence pointer** Figure 7, Results section "Disease mutations disrupt key community interfaces impacting TFIIH structure and dynamics", Supplementary Data 1
  - **Concern** The analysis of 34 disease mutations is presented as revealing mechanistic classes, but the supporting evidence is largely descriptive. The manuscript does not provide a systematic statistical analysis demonstrating that the clustering of mutations by phenotype is significant relative to random expectation. The Rosetta ddG calculations provide predicted stability changes, but these are not validated experimentally. The claim that mutations cluster into "distinct mechanistic classes" appears to be based on visual inspection of the structural mapping rather than a formal classification analysis. Furthermore, the relationship between predicted stability changes and disease severity is not quantitatively assessed.
  - **Why it matters** The disease mutation analysis is presented as a significant outcome of the work, with potential clinical relevance. If the classification into mechanistic classes is not statistically robust or experimentally validated, the clinical implications are overstated. The field requires rigorous quantitative analysis to support genotype-phenotype correlations.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should provide a statistical framework for assessing whether the distribution of mutations across community interfaces is non-random, and whether the proposed mechanistic classes are statistically distinguishable. They should also validate at least a subset of the Rosetta predictions experimentally (e.g., using thermal stability assays or limited proteolysis).
- **Minor Comments**
  - **Concern ID** R1-m1
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Clarity of methods
  - **Affected element** PNEB path optimization description
  - **Evidence pointer** Methods section "Path optimization with the partial nudged elastic band method"
  - **Issue** The description of the PNEB calculations is brief and lacks important details. It is unclear how the initial and final states for each ATP hydrolysis cycle were defined, how the 70 replicas were initialized, and what collective variables or reaction coordinates were used to define the path. The statement that "the initial and end states of each cycle corresponded to the equilibrated apo state" is confusing, as it suggests the path starts and ends at the same state.
  - **Required correction** Provide a more detailed description of the PNEB setup, including the definition of the path endpoints, the collective variables used, the equilibration protocol for each replica, and the criteria used to assess convergence. Clarify the relationship between the three consecutive ATP hydrolysis cycles and the overall translocation mechanism.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m2
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Figure accessibility
  - **Affected element** Figures 1, 3, 5, 7
  - **Evidence pointer** Figure legends
  - **Issue** The figure legends are dense and some panels are not fully described. For example, in Figure 1, the color scheme for the TFIIH subunits is described in the legend but the specific colors for each subunit are not consistently labeled in the figure panels. In Figure 7, the mapping of mutations onto the structures is shown but the specific residues are not labeled, making it difficult to connect the figure to the text discussion.
  - **Required correction** Ensure that all panels are clearly labeled and that the color scheme is consistent and easily identifiable. Consider adding residue labels or a supplementary table listing the specific mutations shown in each panel.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m3
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Statistical reporting
  - **Affected element** Rosetta ddG analysis
  - **Evidence pointer** Methods section "Rosetta protein stability analysis", Supplementary Figure 5
  - **Issue** The Rosetta ddG results are presented without error estimates or measures of uncertainty. It is unclear whether the reported values are from single calculations or averages over multiple runs. The manuscript does not state how many independent Rosetta calculations were performed or how variability was assessed.
  - **Required correction** Report the number of independent Rosetta calculations, provide error estimates or standard deviations, and state the criteria used to classify mutations as stabilizing or destabilizing.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m4
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Discussion of limitations
  - **Affected element** Discussion section
  - **Evidence pointer** Discussion section, final paragraphs
  - **Issue** The Discussion does not adequately address the limitations of the computational approach. The authors do not discuss the potential for force field inaccuracies, the limitations of the cryo-EM starting structures, or the challenges of simulating such a large complex. A more balanced discussion acknowledging these limitations would strengthen the manuscript.
  - **Required correction** Add a paragraph in the Discussion acknowledging the limitations of the computational methods and the need for experimental validation of the proposed mechanisms.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 (lack of experimental validation for the central switch mechanism), R1-M2 (insufficient validation of de novo built models), R1-M3 (single simulations without convergence assessment)
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - Originality: The application of dCNA to compare TFIIH dynamics across functional states is novel, and the proposed switch mechanism is an original conceptual contribution. However, the originality is somewhat tempered by the lack of experimental validation.
  - Scientific importance: The question of how TFIIH achieves functional specificity across transcription and repair is important, and a validated mechanism would be of significant value. The current work provides a computational hypothesis that, if validated, would be important.
  - Interdisciplinary readership: The work bridges structural biology, computational biophysics, and DNA repair/transcription biology, and would appeal to readers across these fields. The clinical implications for understanding disease mutations add further breadth.
  - Technical soundness: The computational methods are appropriate and generally well-executed, but the lack of validation for de novo built regions, the absence of replicate simulations, and the limited convergence assessment are technical weaknesses that need to be addressed.
  - Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is generally well-written and the main concepts are accessible. However, some sections, particularly the network analysis description, may be challenging for readers without a computational background. The figures are helpful but could be improved with clearer labeling.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The manuscript presents a potentially valuable computational framework for understanding TFIIH dynamics and provides testable predictions. However, the central claim of a regulatory switch requires either experimental validation or substantially more robust computational evidence (replicate simulations, convergence analysis, and validation of the structural models). The disease mutation analysis would also benefit from statistical rigor and experimental validation of at least a subset of predictions. With these revisions, the work could make a meaningful contribution to the field.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that the proposed switch "ensures precise DNA incision in NER" is not supported by any functional data and should be either validated experimentally or substantially tempered.
- The classification of disease mutations into "distinct mechanistic classes" is not supported by formal statistical analysis and appears to be based on qualitative assessment.
- The claim that p62 "caps the channel through which ssDNA is threaded" in the NER complex is based on a single de novo built model without experimental validation of this specific interaction.
- The assertion that "p62 shifts away from XPD's DNA-binding cleft" during the transition from PIC to NER is inferred from comparisons of different cryo-EM structures and may not represent a dynamic transition accessible in solution.
- The generalizability of the proposed mechanism to TC-NER is discussed but not analyzed, and the manuscript does not provide evidence that the switch operates in the TC-NER context.
