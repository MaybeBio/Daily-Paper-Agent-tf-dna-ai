## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (preprint, bioRxiv 2023.05.11.540401)
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific claims, methodology, and evidence presented in the manuscript; no external validation performed
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors introduce Affinity Distillation (AD), a method to extract thermodynamic binding affinities (ΔΔG) from deep learning models trained on in vivo protein-DNA binding data (ChIP-seq, ChIP-nexus, PB-exo). AD uses in silico marginalization over randomized sequence contexts to isolate the energetic contribution of a query sequence. The authors report that AD outperforms motif-based methods (Weeder, STREME, MoDISco) in predicting in vitro-measured affinities across yeast (Pho4, Cbf1) and human (GR, MAX, Myc, GABPA, E2F1) TFs, and can capture non-additive (epistatic) effects.
- **Visible evidence base** Main text figures (Fig. 1A–I, 2A–I, 3A–M, 4A–F, 5A–O), Methods section, supplementary figure references (Fig. S1, S8, S9), supplementary sections 2, 3, 6, 7
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Supplementary figures and sections referenced but not provided in full; detailed hyperparameter settings; full cross-validation results; exact sequence of the GR ChIP consensus site; complete list of GR genomic loci tested; code repository not inspected

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a conceptually appealing and potentially impactful method for bridging deep learning predictions of TF binding with biophysical affinity measurements. The core idea of in silico marginalization to remove context effects is well-motivated and the results across multiple TFs and assay types are encouraging. However, several technical aspects require clarification and additional validation before the central claims can be fully accepted. The reliance on correlation coefficients and RMSE after calibration is appropriate, but the manuscript would benefit from more rigorous statistical comparisons, explicit handling of potential confounders (e.g., GC content, sequence length), and a clearer demonstration that the extracted affinities are truly thermodynamic rather than reflecting other sequence features learned by the model.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in regulatory genomics, computational biology, and biophysics. The method offers a way to interpret deep learning models of TF binding in physically meaningful terms, which is of broad interest. The demonstration of paralog-specific affinity prediction and non-additive effects is particularly relevant for understanding TF binding specificity and its role in gene regulation. The approach could also be useful for predicting the impact of non-coding variants on TF binding.
- **Major strengths** 1) The conceptual advance of extracting thermodynamic parameters from in vivo-trained deep learning models is novel and significant. 2) The method is validated across a diverse set of TFs (bHLH, ETS, E2F, nuclear receptor) and assay types (PB-exo, ChIP-nexus, ChIP-seq), suggesting generalizability. 3) The comparison against multiple motif-based methods is thorough and demonstrates clear advantages for AD. 4) The demonstration of non-additive effects and paralog-specific affinities highlights the unique capabilities of the approach. 5) The manuscript is generally well-written and the figures are informative.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **R1-M1** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes, Axis: Technical soundness) The manuscript does not adequately address the potential for the deep learning model to learn sequence features unrelated to thermodynamic affinity (e.g., chromatin accessibility, nucleosome positioning, co-factor binding) that correlate with the in vitro measurements. The claim that AD extracts "thermodynamic affinities" is strong and requires more direct evidence that the marginalization procedure isolates the intrinsic protein-DNA interaction energy. The authors should perform control experiments, such as applying AD to models trained on shuffled sequences or models with known affinity functions, to demonstrate that the extracted scores are specifically related to affinity.
  - **R1-M2** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes, Axis: Statistical rigor) The statistical comparison between AD and other methods is based on correlation coefficients and RMSE, but no confidence intervals or significance tests are provided. It is unclear if the observed improvements are statistically significant, especially for the smaller datasets (e.g., E2F1 with only 8 shared sequences). The authors should provide error bars or bootstrap confidence intervals for the reported metrics.
  - **R1-M3** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No, Axis: Reproducibility) The manuscript relies heavily on supplementary figures and sections that are not provided in the submitted text. Key details, such as the full cross-validation results, the exact composition of the GR ChIP consensus site, and the complete list of GR genomic loci, are missing. This limits the ability to fully assess the robustness and reproducibility of the findings.
  - **R1-M4** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No, Axis: Technical soundness) The calibration procedure to convert marginalization scores to ΔΔG values is described briefly. The manuscript should provide more details on the calibration model, the number of measurements used, and the sensitivity of the results to the calibration set size. It is also unclear if the calibration is performed separately for each TF and assay type, and if the linear relationship holds across the full range of affinities.
- **Minor Comments**
  - **R1-m1** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Clarity) The term "de novo distillation" in the title is somewhat ambiguous. Consider clarifying that the method extracts affinities without requiring prior knowledge of the motif or affinity model.
  - **R1-m2** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Readability) The description of the in silico marginalization procedure (step 2 in the Methods) could be clearer. A more detailed algorithmic description or pseudocode would be helpful.
  - **R1-m3** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Technical soundness) The choice of 100 random backgrounds for marginalization is not justified. The manuscript should discuss how this number was chosen and whether the results are sensitive to it.
  - **R1-m4** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Clarity) The comparison with MoDisco is described as using "counts contribution scores," but the exact implementation is not detailed. Clarify whether the standard MoDisco pipeline was used and how the motifs were scored.
  - **R1-m5** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Readability) The discussion of the GR ChIP-seq data processing (IDR framework) is brief. A brief description of the IDR threshold used would be helpful.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1, R1-M2, R1-M3, R1-M4
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** **Originality:** High. The concept of extracting thermodynamic affinities from in vivo-trained deep learning models is novel and addresses a key limitation of these models. **Scientific importance:** High. If validated, this method could significantly advance our understanding of TF binding specificity and enable high-throughput in silico biophysics. **Interdisciplinary readership:** Moderate to high. The work bridges machine learning, biophysics, and genomics, and should appeal to researchers in all three fields. **Technical soundness:** Currently not fully established. The core idea is sound, but the lack of control experiments and statistical rigor raises concerns. **Readability for nonspecialists:** Good. The manuscript is generally well-written and the figures are clear, though some of the technical details in the Methods may be challenging for a general audience.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The core idea is promising and the results are encouraging, but the manuscript requires additional validation and statistical rigor before the central claims can be fully accepted.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that AD extracts "thermodynamic affinities" is not fully supported by the evidence presented. The manuscript does not rule out the possibility that the model learns other sequence features that correlate with in vitro measurements.
- The claim that AD "outperforms" other methods is not supported by statistical tests.
- The claim that AD can "accurately discern affinities of TF paralogs" is based on a single example (Pho4 vs. Cbf1) and requires further validation.
- The generalizability of AD to other TFs and cell types is suggested but not fully demonstrated, particularly for TFs with more complex binding mechanisms.
