# Analysis of Zinc and Stromal Immunity in Disuse Osteoporosis: Mendelian Randomization and Transcriptomic Analysis


## Objective
  Disuse osteoporosis is known to be primarily caused by a lack of exercise. However, the causal relationships between zinc and immunity and disuse osteoporosis remain unknown. This study investigated these relationships and their potential mechanisms.


## Methods
  This study was an integrative study combining genome‐wide association studies and transcriptomics. Two‐sample Mendelian randomization analysis (MR) was used to analyze the causal relationships between exposures (zinc, immunity, physical activity) and the outcome (osteoporosis) with the aid of single‐nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) as instrumental variables (IVs). Four models, MR‐Egger, inverse variance weighted, weighted median and MR‐Pleiotrophy RESidual Sum and Outlier (MRPRESSO), were used to calculate odds ratio values. Sensitivity and heterogeneity analyses were also performed using MRPRESSO and MR‐Egger methods. The mRNA transcriptomic analysis was subsequently conducted. Zinc metabolism scores were acquired through single‐sample Gene Set Enrichment Analysis algorithms. Stromal scores were obtained using the R Package “estimate” algorithms. Important Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes and Gene Ontology pathways were also derived through gene set variation analysis. Cytoscape software helped construct the transcription factor (TF)–mRNA–microRNA (miRNA) network. Virtual screening and molecular docking were performed. Polymerase chain reaction validation was also carried out in vivo.


## Results
  Causal relationships were demonstrated between zinc and exercise (95% confidence interval [CI] = 1.30–2.95, p = 0.001), exercise and immunity (95% CI = 0.36–0.80, p = 0.002), exercise and osteoporosis (95% CI = 0.97–0.99, p = 0.0007), and immunity disorder and osteoporosis (95% CI = 1.30–2.03, p = 0.00002). One hundred and seventy‐nine mRNAs in important modules were screened. Combining the differential expressional genes (DEGs) and the Boruta selection, six DEGs were screened (AHNAK, CSF2, ADAMTS12, SRA1, RUNX2, and SLC39A14). TF HOXC10 and miRNA hsa‐miR‐204 were predicted. Then, the TF–mRNA–miRNA network was successfully constructed. RUNX2 and SLC39A14 were identified as hub mRNAs in the TF–mRNA–miRNA network. Eventually, the novel small drug C6O4NH5 was designed according to the pharmacophore structure of SLC39A14. The docking energy for the novel drug was −5.83 kcal/mol. SLC39A14 and RUNX2 were downregulated (of statistical significance p‐value < 0.05) in our animal experiment.


## Conclusion
  This study revealed that zinc had a protective causal relationship with disuse osteoporosis by promoting exercise and immunity. SLC39A14 and RUNX2 mRNA participated in this zinc‐related mechanism.


## Introduction
  Disuse osteoporosis is a common skeletal disease characterized by low bone density and decreased enlargement of bone space, producing porosity and fragility. 1

  Zinc is a nutritional trace element that is necessary for the growth of humans and animals. 2  It also functions as a co‐factor for many enzymes, including DNA and RNA polymerase. 3  Zinc is significant as a treatment modality for acute diarrhea in children 4  and the common cold. 5  Because zinc is an antioxidant and anti‐inflammatory agent, it effectively decreases the risk of infection in the elderly. There have been some reported associations between the growth retardation of bones and zinc deficiency. 6  Osteoporotic patients have been proven to have lower levels of skeletal zinc than healthy individuals. 7  An epidemiology study focusing on serum zinc and bone fractures demonstrated that zinc was positively correlated with increased bone mineral density and decreased risk of bone fracture. 8  Zinc can increase bone mass by stimulating osteoblastic bone formation 9  and suppressing osteoclastic bone resorption. The supplementation of zinc has been demonstrated not only to help prevent and reduce bone loss in osteoporosis but also to be beneficial to fracture healing. Cell death has a close relationship with metal ions, such as ferroptosis 10  and cuproptosis, and zinc has considerable potential in this field. Many studies have suggested that zinc‐related genes and protein changes could induce cell death and suppression of mitophagy in cardiomyocytes and cancer cells. 11 ,  12  Therefore, our study focuses on zinc in disuse osteoporosis through genome‐wide association studies (GWAS) and transcriptome analysis.

  Given osteoblasts’ bone‐remodeling function and osteoclasts’ bone‐absorption function, bones are involved in the continuous metabolism process. The osteoclast (OC) is a derivative of hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs) and some immune‐related cells, such as dendritic cells. B‐cells can differentiate OC under certain conditions. 13  Moreover, studies show that bone marrow nourishes and protects HSCs, the origin of all types of immune cells, revealing the close connection between OC and the immune system and laying a foundation for the osteoimmune system. 14  Since osteoimmunology plays an important role in various kinds of orthopaedic diseases, it is significant to discuss the possible therapeutic effects of metabolic bone diseases such as osteoporosis in this field.

  Mendelian randomization analysis (MR) is an epidemiology approach for digital analytics, utilizing genetic variants as exposure indicators to satisfy the instrumental variables (IVs) assumptions 15  and to judge whether a health‐concerned outcome is occasionally affected by a risk factor. 16  Nevertheless, Mendelian randomization analysis (MR) requires a large sample to ensure its validity. 17  Aggregated data in GWAS, concentrating on the association between exposure factors/diseases and genetic variation, is favorable to identifying the most promising therapeutic targets for disuse osteoporosis.

  This study has two main parts. First, causal relationships between zinc, immunity and disuse osteoporosis are explored using MR. Second, using multi‐algorithms, WGCAN and Boruta selection, mRNAs connecting zinc and immunity mechanisms are predicted and validated.


## Materials and Methods
  A detailed flow process diagram is shown in Figure 1.


### Data Downloading and Acquisition
  Datasets utilized in MR were all obtained from the IEU OpenGWAS database. 18  The exposure dataset associated with zinc, ukb‐b‐13,891, comprised 461,384 samples and 9,851,867 SNPs. The exposure dataset ebi‐a‐GCST006097 included 377,234 samples and was also regarded as an outcome dataset in MR analysis, evaluating the relationship between zinc and physical activity. The immune‐related dataset finn‐b‐D3_BLOOD was utilized as an outcome dataset to evaluate the relationship between physical activity and immune disorders. Osteoporosis‐related outcome datasets, ukb‐a‐87 and finn‐b‐M13_OSTEOPOROSIS, were also, respectively, downloaded.

  GSE100933 was downloaded from the GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE100933 accessed January 31, 2019), including 13 mRNA sequencing samples, seven microgravity samples and six normal gravity samples. 19  GSE74209, a microRNA dataset for osteoporosis, contains six osteoporotic samples and six healthy samples. 20  Zinc metabolism‐related gene sets were acquired from the Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) database (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/index.jsp), a database including pathway or mechanism information closely related to mRNAs.


### Mendelian Randomization Analysis for Zinc Metabolism
  There are no specific osteoporosis sets in the current databases. Therefore, we investigated the causative relationship between zinc and physical activity to indirectly confirm the link between zinc and osteoporosis due to inactivity. The GWAS analysis discussed above provided background information for the following transcriptome analysis. Lack of exercise is the primary cause of disuse osteoporosis; therefore, indirect validation is credible. 21  All gene variants strictly adhered to the following assumptions to confirm the reliability of analysis conclusions. First, only variants closely associated with exposure can be included; second, independent principles must be observed in exposure and outcome; third, the horizontal pleiotropy effect should be excluded. Single‐nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) satisfying the above assumptions can be regarded as IVs. 22  To confirm SNP independence, p < 5 × 10−8 or p < 1 × 10−5 is the threshold for genome‐wide significance. F‐value > 10 was another criterion for the first stage. Meanwhile, the coefficient r 2 was r 2 < 0.001, as the linkage disequilibrium correlation; further quality control existed based on minor allele frequency >1%.

  Four models, inverse variance weighted (IVW), MR‐Egger, weighted median and MR‐Pleiotrophy RESidual Sum and Outlier (MRPRESSO), were utilized together in MR analyses to evaluate the possible causal relationship between exposure and outcome with the R package TwoSampleMR. Before the MR evaluation, heterogeneity and pleiotropy tests were conducted using Cochran's Q test (IVW) 23  and the MR‐Egger intercept. Further sensitivity analyses were performed through the “MRPRESSO” package to supply a more stable inference basis for MR. 24  For all MR analyses, a p‐value < 0.05 was considered the threshold.


### Immune‐Related Stromal Score Calculation
  The “estimate” package was used to calculate the stromal scores of every sample. 25  All types of stromal cells can be differentiated from mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs). 26  They are also essential in immunity regulation and related responses. 27  In a normal medium or osteogenic medium, MSCs can differentiate into multiple kinds of stromal cells. 28  Therefore, the stromal score could help evaluate the state of the immunity‐related microenvironment in the cell culture.


### Zinc Metabolism Score Assessment
  Zinc metabolism scores were obtained by importing the mRNA expression matrix of GSE100933 into the single‐sample GSEA 29  algorithm using a gene set with two pathways (zinc ion transport and zinc ion homeostasis).


### Weighted Gene Co‐Expression Network Analysis
  Weighted gene co‐expression network analysis (WGCNA), a systematic biological approach to describe gene set association patterns among different samples, could identify highly covarying gene sets, candidate biomarker genes or therapeutic targets based on the interconnectedness of gene sets and the association between gene sets and phenotypes. 30  We used the “WGCNA” R package to analyze zinc‐ and immunity‐related mRNAs in the GSE100933 gene expression data. After clustering, we deleted abnormal values and set an appropriate soft threshold, β = 18 (Figure 4A). The final co‐expression module was obtained. Finally, gene significance (GS) and module membership (MM) are computed to screen genes in the most important module. The modules with the strongest correlation with zinc metabolism, microgravity and immunity are identified and focused on in the below procedure.


### Differential Expressional mRNA and Micro RNA Screening
  To normalize the GSE100933 and GSE74209 datasets, the R package limma was used to eliminate data offsets. 31  In GSE100933, according to different culture mediums (osteogenic and ordinary), microgravity groups and normal groups are compared, respectively. The criteria for differential expressional mRNAs and mRNAs are: logFC > 1 or logFC < 1, p‐value < 0.05. A Venn plot was drawn to exhibit DEGs. Then, common DEGs were screened from two groups of DEGs in different culture mediums. The below analysis is performed using these common DEGs.


### Gene Set Variation Analysis
  Gene set variation analysis, a non‐parametric unsupervised analysis method, was mainly used to evaluate whether different metabolic pathways were enriched in different samples by converting the expression matrix of genes between different samples into the expression matrix of gene sets between samples.


### Boruta Selection for Hub mRNAs
  For the next analysis, common mRNAs among DEGs and mRNAs in important modules were screened using Venn methods. The Boruta selection algorithm is the most reliable approach for hub mRNA screening. 32  In this algorithm, mRNAs labeled with “Confirmed” were screened. The R package Boruta was utilized.


### Correlation and Transcription Factor–mRNA–Micro RNA Network Construction
  The R package ggraph was used to show the correlation between mRNAs and immune cells. The microRNA (miRNA) prediction website Targetscan can predict mRNA‐related miRNA. 33  Conserved miRNAs and non‐conserved miRNAs were obtained by importing mRNAs into this server, and then the conserved miRNAs with the highest confidence were used. The differentially expressed miRNAs in GSE74209 were screened using the same procedure as above for DEG. Then, the common miRNAs between the above‐conserved miRNAs and differentially expressed miRNAs were identified. Based on the iRegulon plug‐in cytoscape software, the transcription factors (TFs) of the mRNAs could be predicted. Then, the TF–mRNA–miRNA network was constructed in Cytoscape.


### Virtual Screening and Molecular Docking
  Caver Web 1.0 is the latest tool for virtual screening. 34  Virtual screening was performed with reference to the structure and pharmacophore of the targeted protein. The generated small molecule drugs were then imported into Autodock software to perform molecular docking. Protein structures for targeting mRNAs were downloaded from the Uniport database, which includes almost all known mRNA‐related protein structures. 35  The online tool POCASA1.1 was utilized to compute possible docking locations in targeted proteins. 36  Based on the above reference, molecular docking was performed in Pymol and Autodock software.


### Animal Experiments
  We established a mouse model of disuse osteoporosis. 37  This study was approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Tianjin Hospital (Ethical Approval Number: 2023 Medical Ethics Review 097). A total dose of 2 U/100 g of botulinum toxin was injected into the quadriceps and gastrocnemius muscles of the right lower limb of C57BL/6 mice (11 weeks old). Control mice (n = 6) were injected with an equal volume of isotonic saline. Eighteen days after injection, mice were killed by cervical dislocation. The right femur, muscles, and soft tissues of the mice were removed. We observed the bone trabeculae of the mice with hematoxylin and eosin staining. Under the environment of liquid nitrogen, the extracted mouse femur was mashed. After that, TRIzol Regent was used to extract RNA from normal and osteoporosis groups. Then, the Prime Script RT Reagent Kit from Solarbio was used to reverse RNA into DNA. Rotating Petri dishes were used, and each group (disuse and normal) included six samples. Then, mRNA expression level was measured using a real‐time polymerase chain reaction (PCR) system. To normalize the cycle threshold (CT) of both mRNAs, the CT value of GAPDH was used as a reference value. Then, statistical analysis was performed by 2‐∆∆CT method. The primers of three mRNA sequences were as follows: Slc39a14 forward primer (5′‐GTGTCTCACTGATTAACCTGGC‐3′), Slc39a14 reverse primer (5′‐AGAGCAGCGTTCCAATGGAC‐3′), Runx2 forward primer (5′‐CCAACCGAGTCATTTAAGGCT‐3′), Runx2 reverse primer (5′‐GCTCACGTCGCTCATCTTG‐3′), Gapdh forward primer (5′‐AGGTCGGTGTGAACGGATTTG‐3′), and Gapdh reverse primer (5′‐TGTAGACCATGTAGTTGAGGTCA‐3′).


### Statistical Analysis
  For PCR data analysis, D'agostino–Pearson was first used to detect the normal distribution. Only data following the normal distribution could be used to perform parametric t‐tests in the GraphPad Prism software. All other statistical analyses were conducted in R studio software.


## Results

### Causal Relationship between Zinc and Osteoporosis
  A causal relationship diagram between zinc, physical activity and osteoporosis is shown in Figure 2A. In Figure 2B, the results for three IVW models are as follows: odds ratio (OR) = 1.96, 95% confidence interval (CI) = 1.32–2.90, p‐value = 0.0007 (IVW fixed effects); OR = 1.96, 95% CI = 1.30–2.95, p‐value = 0.001 (IVW); OR = 1.96, 95% CI = 1.30–2.95, p‐value = 0.001 (IVW multiplicative random effects). In the weighted median models, OR = 1.67, 95% CI = 0.95–2.95, p‐value = 0.075. In addition, OR = 1.96, 95% CI = 1.30–2.95, p‐value = 0.003 for the MRPRESSO model. In this MR analysis, zinc is revealed as a protective factor for exercise. For MR analyses of physical activity and immunity, causal relationships were revealed in the IVW and MRPRESSO, weighted median models (Figure 2C). For IVW, OR = 0.54, 95% CI = 0.36–0.80, p‐value = 0.002 (Bonferroni correction 0.004). For MRPRESSO, OR = 0.54, 95% CI = 0.42–0.68, p‐value = 0.0001 (Bonferroni correction 0.0002). For the weighted median, OR = 0.55, 95% CI = 0.32–0.92, p‐value = 0.02 (Bonferroni correction 0.04). A relationship between physical activity and osteoporosis was also demonstrated. In Figure 2D, in the MR‐Egger model, OR = 0.96, CI = 0.93–1.00, p‐value = 0.067. In the three IVW models, the results were: OR = 0.98, 95% CI = 0.97–0.99, p‐value = 0.0007 (Bonferroni correction 0.0014, IVW fixed effects); OR = 0.98, 95% CI = 0.97–0.99, p‐value = 0.0007 (Bonferroni correction 0.0014, IVW); and OR = 0.98, 95%CI = 0.97–0.99, p‐value = 0.0003 (Bonferroni correction 0.0006, IVW multiplicative random effects). In the MR‐PRESSO model, OR = 0.98, 95% CI = 0.97–0.99, p‐value = 0.004 (Bonferroni correction 0.008). Physical activity could prevent osteoporosis. For immunity and osteoporosis (Figure 2E), in the three IVW models, the results were: OR = 1.20, 95% CI = 0.78–1.84, p‐value = 0.00002 (Bonferroni correction 0.00004, IVW fixed effects); OR = 1.62, 95% CI = 1.30–2.03, p‐value = 0.00002 (Bonferroni correction 0.00004, IVW); and OR = 1.62, 95%CI = 1.33–1.98, p‐value = 0.000002 (Bonferroni correction 0.000004, IVW multiplicative random effects). In the MR‐PRESSO model, OR = 1.62, 95% CI = 1.33–1.98, p‐value = 0.0002 (Bonferroni correction 0.0004). In the weighted median model, the p‐value = 0.047 (Bonferroni correction 0.094), OR = 1.36, 95% CI = 1.00–1.85. The results for heterogeneity and sensitivity for the three MR analyses are shown in Table 1. For all MR analyses, the p‐values for Q‐test (IVW), the global test (MR‐PRESSO), and the MR‐Egger intercept were all larger than 0.05, suggesting no heterogeneity and horizontal pleiotropy. Through MR analyses, zinc was found to affect physical activity. A lack of activity was verified to induce osteoporosis. An immune disorder could increase the risk of osteoporosis and has a causal relationship with osteoporosis. These results indirectly indicated that a zinc disorder could induce disuse osteoporosis and immune disorder by affecting physical activity.


### Immune Infiltration and Zinc Metabolism Assessment
  According to the results of the “estimate” analysis (Figure 3A), the entire immune‐related stromal scores in microgravity groups were higher than in the normal groups. At the same time, as shown in Figure 3B, stromal scores of the microgravity group were both higher than normal in normal or osteogenic culture environments and of statistical significance (p‐value < 0.05). These results indicated that immune‐related factors might play certain roles in disuse osteoporosis. In Figure 3A, zinc metabolism scores for microgravity and normal groups in different culture environments are also exhibited. Along with the effects of microgravity interventions, zinc metabolism will change. For zinc metabolism, zinc metabolism scores of microgravity were higher than normal groups in osteogenic medium with statistical significance (p‐value < 0.05). The MR results suggested that changes in zinc metabolism and immunity could occur in disuse osteoporosis. Further, disorders of two mechanisms might induce disuse osteoporosis.


### Important Modules and mRNA in Weighted Gene Co‐Expression Network Analysis
  In the chosen threshold (Figure 4A), the clustering of samples is good (Figure 4B). After the merge of assemble modules, four modules (gray, blue, turquoise, and brown) were identified. In Figure 4C, four modules, not including gray, represent special correlations. Brown modules have a higher correlation with zinc metabolism, microgravity, and stromal scores and were identified from four modules. The p‐values of these phenotypes were all in accordance with the statistical criteria, p‐value < 0.05. Further, zinc metabolism and stromal scores were explored in this module. In Figure 4D, the coefficients representing the immune and zinc metabolism correlation are, respectively, −0.51 (p‐value = 6.6 × 10−12) and 0.68 (p‐value = 6.4 × 10−23). The above results suggest that the mRNAs in the brown modules were closely connected with the zinc metabolism and immune mechanism. A total of 159 mRNAs in brown modules were identified for the next analyses.


### Gene Set Variation Analysis Results
  In Figure 5A, the biological process pathway positively regulates the transcription regulatory region DNA binding; the molecular function pathway translates the initiation factor binding; the cell component pathway and the NADH dehydrogenase complex are closely related to the brown module's mRNAs. In Figure 5A, the complement and coagulation cascade pathway is demonstrated to be of the most important significance by comparing microgravity and normal samples. The complement and coagulation cascade pathway affected the immune response in multiple studies. 38


### Differential Expressional Gene Screen and Boruta Selection
  As shown in Figure 5B, 4230 DEGs and 2185 DEGs were, respectively, identified in the normal medium and osteogenic medium subgroups. Fifteen mRNAs were specially screened in the brown module. In the Boruta selection analysis, six mRNAs (CSF2, SLC39A14, AHNAK, ADAMTS12, SRA1, and RUNX2) were labeled “confirmed”. The other nine were labeled “tentative” and excluded (Figure 5C).


### Correlation and Transcription Factor–mRNA–miRNA Network Construction
  Zinc metabolism scores and stromal scores were found to have a close relationship with all six mRNAs, indicating that mRNAs also played important roles in immune and zinc metabolism (Figure 6A). In GSE74209, 139 differential expressional miRNAs were identified (Figure 6B). In Figure 6C, the TFs predicted in the Cytoscape software, HOXC10, helped construct the TF–mRNA network with five mRNAs. The common miRNA has‐mir‐204 was imported into the TF‐mRNA network to construct the TF–mRNA–miRNA network (see Figure 6C). In the above two networks, RUNX2 and SLC39A14 were found to interact with TF and miRNAs and are viewed as core mRNAs. RUNX2 has been studied previously as an osteogenic mRNA. 39  In particular, SLC39A14 was the focus for our next studies.


### Novel Small Molecular Drugs and Molecular Docking
  As the results of the caver website server suggest, the coordinates of the pharmacophore are x = 8.16, y = 12.94, z = −18.86 (Figure 6D). The surrounding residues were 138GLU, 139SER, and 141PRO. The potential drug C6O4NH5 was screened in the server. In the docking results (Figure 6E), 10 modes were constructed in the Autodock software. The binding energy of the model is −5.83 kcal/mol, lower than −5 kcal/mol, so there are possibilities of docking. 40  The average root mean square deviation in all modes was lower than 2A, indicating the reliability of the results. 41  The detailed docking situation was exhibited in Figure 6C, and three hydrogen bonds were formed in GLU204, PHE199, GLN‐365, and novel molecules. The bond length of all hydrogen bonds was lower than 3A, indicating a credible hydrogen bond formation. 42


### Animal Experiment Results and Analysis
  In Figure 7A, the number of trabeculae in the distal femur was significantly reduced in the disuse osteoporosis group compared to the control group. This indicated the success of the model building. Two core mRNAs were also verified. In Figure 7B, two mRNAs, RUNX2 and SLC39A14, were differentially expressed. The expression level of RUNX2 was decreased after the disuse process, from 1.31 to 0.18 (relative expression level, p‐value < 0.0001). At the same time, the expression level of SLC39A14 also decreased, from 0.48 to 0.07 (relative expression level, p‐value < 0.0001). The p‐values of all PCR data in the D'agostino–Pearson test were larger than 0.05, indicating a normal distribution).


## Discussion

### Research Advances in Disuse Osteoporosis
  Our study not only revealed a causal relationship between zinc and disuse osteoporosis and immunity and disuse osteoporosis but also SLC39A14 and RUNX2 mRNA were found to play key roles during the development of disuse osteoporosis in zinc‐related molecular mechanisms. Zinc has a protective causal relationship and immunity disorders play negative roles in disuse osteoporosis. Our integrative analysis connected GWAS and transcriptomics together.


### Causal Relationships in Zinc and Immunity Mechanisms with Disuse Osteoporosis
  In our study, a causal relationship between zinc and disuse osteoporosis was indirectly proved. With the increase in zinc, there was a 96% increase in physical activity (OR = 1.96). At the same time, physical activity decreased the likelihood of developing osteoporosis by 46% (OR = 0.54). The primary reason for disuse osteoporosis occurrence is the reduction of physical activity. 43  Therefore, zinc could have a protective causal relationship in disuse osteoporosis. In some previous clinical studies, higher zinc was found to be closely associated with higher bone mineral density. 8  In disuse osteoporosis, decreased physical activity was the most direct cause. Previous studies found that decreased zinc probably affected the skeletal muscle oxidative capacity, and then force production was inhibited. 44  This chain effect of zinc was consistent with our conclusion that zinc has protective effects on disuse osteoporosis by indirectly influencing physical activity.

  One previous MR study reported that many kinds of immune cells, such as monocytes, had negative or positive causal relationships with osteoporosis. 45  The MR analysis proved useful in studying immune mechanisms. In our study, physical activity was capable of decreasing 2% odds value (OR = 0.98) of immune disorder cases. As the increase of immune disorder cases, the odds value was 62% (OR = 1.62) on osteoporosis. Physical activity as a mediation, zinc was correlated with immune disorder. Other studies have reported that a zinc deficiency will cause the expression levels of interleukin (IL)‐1β and tumor necrosis factor (TNF)‐α to increase. 46  Considering immune cell aspects, Th1 T cells could be observed to reduce in number. 47  The role of zinc is closely related to the secretion of inflammatory factors and immune activation. Our transcriptomic analysis also corroborated the causal relationships in disuse osteoporosis. Immune disorders and zinc imbalance appeared in the process of microgravity, inducing disuse osteoporosis.


### Effects of Key Genes in Related Mechanisms
  AHNAK plays an important role in maintaining the integrity of the actin cytoskeleton and the regulation of calcium currents. 48  AHNAK regulates Ca2+ transport in osteoblasts undergoing dynamic movement. 49  Colony‐stimulating factors (CSFs) are a group of cytokines essential for the hematopoiesis of blood cells, regulating their function, and maintaining homeostasis and overall immunity. CSFs can enhance neutrophil‐mediated cartilage degeneration, leading to severe structural damage. 50 ,  51  The CSF2 signaling pathway plays an important role in tumor progression and various diseases through immune response. 52

  The ADAMTS family plays important roles in development, angiogenesis, and coagulation. Existing evidence indicates that ADAMTS12 is a key gene in the pathogenesis of arthritis and was significantly upregulated in the cartilage of osteoarthritis patients. 53 ,  54  ADAMTS12, a Tregs‐related immune factor, is strongly induced by TNF and IL‐1β. 55 ,  56 ,  57  One study investigated the relationship between fat and SRA1 expression and indicated that the expression of SRA1 in adipose was upregulated with obesity, which correlates with the regulation of Toll‐like receptors. 58  Therefore, SRA1 is considered a potential biomarker of immune‐metabolic disorders. 59

  RUNX2 is essential for osteoblast differentiation and chondrocyte maturation. However, the effect of RUNX2 on bones and joints could be both positive and negative. By regulating osteoblast differentiation, RUNX2 enhances bone formation while also contributing to osteoarthritis, which destroys articular cartilage. Therefore, it is of great significance to analyze RUNX2 for treating osteoarthritis and osteoporosis. 39  RUNX2 expression is also influenced by immune cells. Treg cells can inhibit the differentiation and function of osteoclasts and inhibit inflammation. Meanwhile, Runx2 plays an important regulatory role in the Treg cells related procedure. Th17/Treg cells can also induce inflammatory bone metabolic diseases. IL‐17F promotes osteogenesis, including proliferation, differentiation, and mineralization activities, by upregulating BMP‐2, Runx2, and Osterix expression and downregulating Noggin expression. 60 ,  61 ,  62

  The SLC39A family of metal transporters was identified through homologies with ZIP proteins. ZIP14 was the encoding protein of SLC39A14. Of all the ZIP transporters, ZIP14 is the most robustly characterized in terms of function at the integrative level. ZIP14 expression level is highly tissue‐specific, with the highest levels in the jejunum and liver. 63 ,  64  Secondary structure prediction of ZIP14 showed that it consisted of eight transmembrane domains, with a long extracellular N‐terminus and a short extracellular C‐terminus. Multiple alignment analyses also showed an important conserved motif (HEXPHE), which forms the pore region required for the zinc transport function. 64 ,  65  The zinc transport function of the ZIP14 mouse ortholog was validated by ZIP14‐transfected HEK293 human kidney cells, with multiple approaches to measure zinc uptake in the presence of 10 μM extracellular zinc. 66  More importantly, ZIP14 plays an essential role in bone development. Chondrocyte cells are necessary for bone elongation and growth hormone production, respectively. ZIP14 is reported to be highly expressed in the proliferative zone of the growth plate, specifically in primary pituitary cells and chondrocytes. 67 ,  68 ,  69  Another study pointed out that a unique feature of ZIP14 is that it is upregulated by pro‐inflammatory conditions, particularly the increase in interleukin 6 (IL‐6) and nitric oxide. This indicated that ZIP14 was closely related to immunity. 64  In terms of bone loss, SLC39A14 was seen as a protective factor. It was associated with three types of metal metabolism: zinc, iron and manganese. Along with the deficiency of SLC39A14, bone resorption of osteoclasts could be strengthened, and the regulatory ability to inhibit the onset of osteoporosis decreased. 70  These previous studies were consistent with our results that expression of SLC39A14 would be downregulated in the development of disuse osteoporosis.


### Strength and Limitations
  We combined GWAS (MR) and post‐GWAS (transcriptome). The conclusions obtained are of certain clinical significance. In addition, virtual screening and animal experiments provided more information for further study. Our study was limited by the content and workload of the study, and further work at the protein level is needed in the future. We did not ultimately validate the effects using in vivo studies of the potential drugs and genes for zinc and osteoporosis, which will be the focus of our next study. Further in‐depth research would be beneficial for clinical and practical applications.


### Conclusions
  We revealed that zinc has a protective causal relationship in disuse osteoporosis. Immune mechanisms are associated with this regulation. RUNX2 and SLC39A14 mRNA were found to play key roles in communicating the two mechanisms. Novel small molecular drug C6O4NH5 was designed specially to target SLC39A14.
