## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text, including abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, and conclusions. Supplementary figures and tables are referenced but not provided.
- **Assessment boundary** Evaluation is limited to the scientific content, methodology, and claims presented in the provided manuscript text. No assessment of the journal's editorial fit or publication history is made.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors use all-atom molecular dynamics simulations to propose that the glucocorticoid receptor (GR) binds DNA via sequence-specific contacts in the major groove, while binding RNA hairpins via shape recognition involving nonspecific electrostatic interactions, and discriminates against uniform dsRNA due to its rigid, narrow major groove.
- **Visible evidence base** Main text figures (Figs. 1–7), tables (Table 1), and referenced supplementary materials (Figures S1–S27, Tables S1–S4, Movies S1–S45). Supplementary content is not directly inspected.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Supplementary figures, tables, and movies are not provided. Simulation input files, force field parameter details, and convergence criteria are not fully described. Experimental validation data (e.g., mutagenesis or binding assays) are not included in this manuscript.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a computational investigation into the dual DNA/RNA recognition mechanisms of the glucocorticoid receptor. The study is well-motivated and addresses an important gap in understanding transcription factor-RNA interactions. The simulations are extensive and the analysis is thorough, with multiple complementary approaches (contacts, energies, entropies, PCA, groove geometry). However, the conclusions rest heavily on the quality of the initial docking models and the limited sampling of conformational states, particularly for flexible regions like helix 4. The claim of a "dual recognition mechanism" is plausible but requires stronger validation, especially regarding the functional relevance of the observed RNA binding modes. The manuscript is clearly written and will interest researchers in transcription regulation and nucleic acid recognition.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying transcription factor-nucleic acid interactions, particularly those focused on glucocorticoid receptor biology, zinc finger proteins, and the emerging role of RNA in transcriptional regulation. The study also appeals to computational biophysicists interested in protein-nucleic acid docking and MD simulation methodologies.
- **Major strengths** 1) Addresses a timely and understudied question regarding TF-RNA recognition. 2) Uses a multi-faceted computational approach to characterize binding. 3) Provides a plausible mechanistic hypothesis (shape vs. sequence recognition) that is experimentally testable. 4) The comparison across multiple RNA structures (hairpin, bulge, dsRNA) is well-designed.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that GR binds RNA hairpins with high affinity and discriminates against dsRNA, and that this is due to shape recognition.
  - **Evidence pointer** Methods: "To derive GR-RNA complexes, we perform the macromolecular docking in HDOCK"; Results: "The GR-RNA contact networks..."; Figure 3, Figure 5.
  - **Concern** The initial GR-RNA complex structures are generated by docking, not experimental structures. The authors do not validate these docked poses against any experimental data (e.g., NMR chemical shift perturbations from the cited Parsonnet et al. study). The conclusions about binding modes and interaction energies are therefore highly dependent on the accuracy of the initial docking. If the docked poses are incorrect, all downstream analyses (contacts, energies, PCA) are compromised. The authors should demonstrate that the docked complexes are stable and representative, perhaps by comparing to experimental data or performing additional sampling (e.g., enhanced sampling or multiple docking poses).
  - **Why it matters** The central mechanistic claims (sequence vs. shape recognition) are built on the assumption that the simulated GR-RNA complexes resemble the physiologically relevant binding modes. Without validation, the conclusions are speculative.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should provide evidence that the docked poses are reliable, such as: (1) comparison of predicted contacts with experimental NMR data; (2) convergence of the simulations from multiple starting poses; (3) or use of a more rigorous docking protocol with scoring function validation.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Claim pointer** The authors state that "the protein effectively moulds its binding site within DNA major groove" and that for RNA, "the GR binding has little effect on the grooves geometry."
  - **Evidence pointer** Results: "Nucleic acids helical and groove parameters"; Figure 6, Figure 7.
  - **Concern** The analysis of groove geometry is performed on the trajectory-averaged structures. However, the authors do not report the fluctuations or standard deviations of these parameters. Given that the RMSD analysis shows significant flexibility in some systems (e.g., dsRNA), the average groove widths may not be representative. The claim that GR "moulds" the DNA groove implies an induced-fit mechanism, but the authors do not compare the bound and unbound states in a time-resolved manner or assess the statistical significance of the differences.
  - **Why it matters** The shape recognition hypothesis relies on the idea that RNA structures are pre-organized to fit the GR binding site, while DNA is more adaptable. If the groove geometry differences are not statistically robust, this conclusion is weakened.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should report the distributions (e.g., mean ± standard deviation) of groove widths and helical parameters for both bound and unbound states, and perform statistical tests (e.g., t-tests or bootstrap) to assess significance.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Scientific importance
  - **Claim pointer** The authors conclude that "the combination of structural motifs defines the GR-RNA selective binding: a recognition mechanism, which may be shared by other zinc finger TFs."
  - **Evidence pointer** Conclusions section.
  - **Concern** The generalization to other zinc finger TFs is speculative and not supported by data presented in this manuscript. The authors do not perform any comparative analysis with other zinc finger proteins or discuss how their findings might extend beyond GR. While this is a reasonable hypothesis, it is presented as a conclusion rather than a suggestion for future work.
  - **Why it matters** Overstating the generality of the findings could mislead readers into assuming the mechanism is universal without evidence.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should either provide comparative data (e.g., simulations of other zinc finger TFs) or soften the language to clearly frame this as a hypothesis.
- **Minor Comments**
  - **Concern ID** R1-m1
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Readability for nonspecialists
  - **Affected element** Abstract
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract: "Using atomic molecular dynamics simulations, we demonstrate that the GR binding to nucleic acids requires a wide and shallow groove pocket."
  - **Issue** The term "atomic molecular dynamics simulations" is redundant; "molecular dynamics simulations" is standard. The abstract could be more accessible by briefly explaining what a "groove pocket" is.
  - **Required correction** Change to "molecular dynamics simulations" and consider adding a brief parenthetical explanation, e.g., "a wide and shallow groove pocket (a structural feature of the nucleic acid major groove)".
  - **Concern ID** R1-m2
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Affected element** Methods: "We employ the GROMACS 'covar' and 'anaeig' tools to calculate the configurational entropies"
  - **Evidence pointer** Methods section.
  - **Issue** The authors state they use GROMACS tools for configurational entropy, but the Schlitter formula is typically implemented in other tools (e.g., gmx covar with -entropy flag). The description is ambiguous.
  - **Required correction** Clarify the exact commands or tools used, and specify whether the entropy was calculated using the Schlitter approximation as implemented in GROMACS or via a custom script.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m3
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Affected element** Results: "The GR-dsRNA MD trajectory shows a gradual loss of protein-RNA contacts and the beginning of the protein dissociation."
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section, "Protein–nucleic acids contacts".
  - **Issue** The claim of "beginning of protein dissociation" is based on a 600 ns simulation. This is a short timescale for observing dissociation, and the authors do not provide quantitative evidence (e.g., time evolution of contacts or distance between protein and RNA) to support this statement.
  - **Required correction** Either provide quantitative data showing the time-dependent loss of contacts, or soften the language to "suggests the onset of dissociation."
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 and R1-M2 are blocking. The lack of validation of the docked poses and the insufficient statistical analysis of groove geometry are critical issues that must be resolved before the mechanistic claims can be considered robust.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - **Originality** The study addresses a novel question (dual DNA/RNA recognition by GR) and proposes a new mechanistic framework (sequence vs. shape). This is a creative and original contribution.
  - **Scientific importance** The findings have potential implications for understanding transcription factor function and RNA-mediated regulation. However, the importance is somewhat limited by the lack of experimental validation and the speculative generalization to other TFs.
  - **Interdisciplinary readership** The topic bridges structural biology, biophysics, and gene regulation, and should appeal to a broad audience. However, the heavy reliance on computational methods may limit accessibility for experimentalists.
  - **Technical soundness** The methodology is generally sound, but the concerns about docking validation and statistical rigor (R1-M1, R1-M2) undermine the technical robustness of the conclusions.
  - **Readability for nonspecialists** The manuscript is clearly written, but some technical terms (e.g., "groove pocket") could be better explained. The abstract is accessible, but the results section may be challenging for non-experts.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The manuscript presents a compelling hypothesis and a thorough computational analysis, but the blocking concerns regarding docking validation and statistical significance must be addressed before the claims can be fully supported.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that GR binds RNA hairpins with "similar affinities" to DNA is based on interaction energy calculations, which are not directly comparable to experimental binding affinities. This is an overinterpretation of the computational data.
- The statement that "the combination of structural motifs defines the GR-RNA selective binding" is presented as a definitive conclusion, but it is only supported by simulations of a limited set of RNA structures.
- The generalization to "other zinc finger TFs" is unsupported by any comparative data.
- The claim that helix 4 unfolding is a mechanism for RNA recognition is based on observations in a single trajectory and is not statistically validated.
