# Multi-target activity of copper complexes: Antibacterial, DNA binding, and molecular docking with SARS-CoV-2 receptor


## Abstract
  A series of pendant-armed mixed-ligand copper(II) complexes of the type [CuL1-3(diimine)] (1–6) have been synthesized by the reaction of pendant-armed ligands N,N-bis(2-(((E)-2-hydroxy-5-methylbenzylidene)amino)ethyl)benzamide (H2L1), N,N-bis(2-(((E)-2-hydroxy-5-methylbenzylidene)amino)ethyl)-4-nitrobenzamide (H2L2) and N,N-bis(2-(((E)-2-hydroxy-5-methylbenzylidene)amino)ethyl)-3,5-dinitrobenzamide (H2L3) with diimine = 2,2′-bipyridyl (bpy) or 1,10-phenanthroline (phen) in the presence of copper(II) chloride and analyzed using various spectroscopic methods. All the spectroscopic results support that the complexes adopt a pentagonal-bipyramidal shape around the copper ion. Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria were used to test all the complexes for antibacterial activity and all the complexes had greater potency against gram-negative pathogens. DNA-binding experiments of complexes with calf thymus DNA revealed a major-groove binding pattern, further supported by molecular docking studies. Complexes have significantly interacted with SARS-CoV-2 receptor via π-π, π-σ, π-alkyl, π-anion, π-cation, alkyl, hydrogen bond, van der Waals, and electrostatic interactions. The estimated binding energy and inhibition constant of these complexes are higher than standard drugs, chloroquine, and molnupiravir.


## Introduction
  Recent literature on mixed-ligand complexes shows a wide range of applications in industrial, environmental, and medicinal fields such as pharmacology, toxicology, and biochemistry [[1], [2], [3]]. Plenty of metal ions like copper plays a key role in the biological process in humans [[4], [5], [6], [7]]. These metals including their complexes have antimicrobial activities apart from being found at active sites or as structural components of many enzymes [8]. 2,2-Bipyridine and 1,10-phenanthroline are strong bidentate ligands that form stable chelates with many transition metals [9,10]. Mixed-ligand complexes with metal ions bound to two different and biochemically important ligands have developed interest as models for metalloenzymes. The physiologically interesting mixed ligand complexes of transition metals with amino acids play an important role in biological systems and have been a subject of great interest for researchers [11].

  Metal-based medications are an innovative group of anti-mycotic agents and based on their unique mode of action have the potential to be innovative pharmaceuticals [12]. It has been reported that some of the N–N donor-type ligand-based metal complexes act as effective in vitro inhibitors against candida Albicans [13,14].

  Several heteroleptic metal(II) complexes have strongly interacted with DNA [5], and there is still scope to design and study Schiff base with metal salt as new chemical nucleases. The diversified applications of heteroleptic complexes support the development of methods with increased selectivity and great importance in pharmacological and environmental chemistry [15,16]. We have recently reported similar type of pendant-armed heteroleptic copper(II) phenolate complexes and studied their biological and in silico studies [17].

  Herein, we have changed the ligand environment by adding the electron-donating methyl substituent, which would increase the biological activity. Bearing all these facts in our mind, we have focused on the synthesis of mixed-ligand copper(II) complexes of 5-methylsalicylaldehyde and 2,2′-bipyridyl/1,10-phenanthroline. The biological efficacy of the synthesized complexes was tested in terms of antibacterial and DNA binding studies. DFT calculations have been used to predict the optimized geometry of the complexes. Molecular docking studies were carried out against DNA, pathogenic organisms, and SARS-CoV-2 receptors [18].


## Experimental section

### Materials
  All chemicals were reagent grade and used without further purification. Solvents used for spectroscopic studies were HPLC grade. Calf-thymus (CT-DNA) was purchased from Bangalore Genei (India). 5 mM Tris(hydroxymethyl)aminomethane-hydrochloride (Tris-HCl)/50 mM NaCl buffer was prepared in deionized water in which the pH was adjusted (till pH 7.2) with HCl/NaOH solution. 5-Methylsalicylaldehyde was prepared according to the literature method [16].


### Physical methods
  Carlo Erba model 1106 elemental analyzer was used to perform a micro-analysis (% C, H, and N). FT-IR spectra were recorded using KBr pellets in the range 4000–400 cm−1 on a Bruker ALPHA FT-IR spectrophotometer. Electronic spectra in the range of 200–1100 nm were acquired using 1 cm quartz micro cuvettes on a Perkin Elmer Lambda–45 spectrophotometer. The mass spectra were recorded on Q-TOF 6000 ESI mass spectrometer. The concentration of complexes was taken at around 0.1 mM and the supporting electrolyte (TBAP) was at 0.1 M.


### Synthesis of ligands (H2L1–3)
  The ligands H2L1–3 were synthesized according to the procedure reported by our group [16,17,19].


### General procedure for synthesis of mixed-ligand copper(II) complexes
  A methanolic solution (10 mL) of Schiff base ligands (H2L1–3, 1 mmol) was added to a methanolic solution (10 mL) of CuCl2·2H2O (0.37 g, 1 mmol) with constant stirring followed by bpy/phen (1 mmol) in methanol (10 mL) at room temperature. Then, the reaction was continued for 2 h under reflux. After cooling the reaction mixture to room temperature, the resulting product was collected by evaporation of the solvent, washed with diethyl ether, and fully dried in vacuo. Efforts taken to get single crystals in different conditions and solvents went unsuccessful.

### Theoretical, biological, and molecular docking studies
  DFT calculation, in-vitro antibacterial screening, DNA binding, and molecular docking with DNA, pathogenic organisms, and SARS-CoV-2 receptors were carried out by following the procedure reported in our earlier publications [4,[6], [7], [8],20,21].


## Results and discussion

### Chemistry
  The reaction between equimolar amounts of ligands and diimines as co-ligand in the presence of CuCl2·2H2O yielded pendant-armed mixed-ligand copper(II) complexes (1–6) in good yield with characteristic color (Scheme 1 ). In Tris-HCl/NaCl buffer, CH3OH, CH3CN, DMF, and DMSO, all of the complexes are soluble. The formation of the ligands and complexes were authenticated by NMR, FT-IR, UV–Vis, ESI-MS, and elemental analysis methods. The data reveal that [Cu(L1-3)(diimine)] is the typical empirical formula for copper(II) complexes.Scheme 1Synthesis of pendant-armed mixed-ligand copper(II) complexes (1–6).Scheme 1


#### Characterization of ligands
  The 1H and 13C NMR spectra of ligands confirm the formation and stability of prototype ligands (Fig. 1 ). The integral intensities of each signal in the 1H NMR spectra were found to agree with the number and different types of protons present in the ligands. A singlet at 2.2–2.4 ppm is assigned to methyl of the pendant-armed ligands H2L1–3. Aromatic protons were observed at 6.8–7.8 ppm, the hydroxyl protons at 12.2–13.3 ppm, and two equivalent imine protons (–CHN) as a singlet at 8.2–8.8 ppm (Fig. 1A). The 13C NMR of ligands H2L1–3 shows the resonance at 20.3 ppm due to methyl carbon, and all the ligands show methylene carbon resonances in the region of 47–55.2 ppm. The oxygen (phenolic oxygen) coordinated aromatic carbon exhibits the signal at 158.8 ppm, and the remaining aromatic carbons show resonances in the region of 116.6–133.7 ppm. The signal in the region 163.2–168.6 ppm was observed due to an azomethine carbon (Fig. 1B).Fig. 1(A) 1H NMR and (B) 13C NMR spectra of the ligand H2L2.Fig. 1

  The ESI-MS spectra have been used to determine the molecular stoichiometry of the ligands and Fig. 2 depicts the mass spectrum of H2L1. The peak at 443.52 confirms the molecular weight of the ligand and the peak at 465.43 denotes the molecular weight of the ligand plus sodium and the peak at 886.45 is the dimer form of the ligand. Both NMR and ESI-MS spectral data along with FT-IR and elemental analysis have confirmed the formation of ligands and their purity [19].Fig. 2ESI-MS spectrum of the ligand H2L1.Fig. 2


#### Characterization of pendant-armed mixed-ligand copper(II) complexes
  The reaction between equimolar amounts of ligands (H2L1–3) and diimines (bpy/phen) as co-ligand in the presence of CuCl2·2H2O yielded pendant-armed mixed-ligand copper(II) complexes (1–6), which is confirmed by various spectro-analytical methods. The molecular weight of the complexes was authenticated by ESI-MS spectra, the peak at 661.24 confirms the molecular weight of the complex 1. The peak at 505.12 is equal to the pendant-armed ligand with copper Cu(L1), which is the first fragmentation of bpy from the complex 1 and the peak at 443.48 is equal to the ligand, which is the second fragmentation (Fig. 3 ). The mass data has strongly confirmed the proposed molecular formulae of the pendant-armed mixed-ligand Cu(II) complexes.Fig. 3ESI-MS spectrum of the complex 1.Fig. 3

  UV–Vis spectral data has been widely used for the determination of molecular geometry [6,17], therefore the spectra of newly synthesized pendant-armed mixed-ligand copper(II) complexes (1–6) were recorded using DMF (Fig. 4 ). All the complexes showed strong bands in the regions 216–276 nm, which belongs to the intra-ligand charge transfer transition (π–π*), and medium intense bands observed in the regions 346–395 nm denoted an MLCT transition. The complexes exhibit a well-resolved band at 724–786 nm, which can be assigned to the 2A1'→ 2E1″, transition in D5h symmetry. The above results, especially d–d transition support the pentagonal-bipyramidal environment around the copper(II) nuclei [17,22]. The magnetic moment (μeff) values (1.57–1.63 BM) of the copper(II) complexes slightly lower than the spin-only value (1.73 BM) of the high-spin Cu(II) center with S ½ indicating discrete mononuclear complexes [17].Fig. 4UV–Vis spectra of pendant-armed mixed-ligand copper(II) complexes (1–6); insert d–d band of the complexes.Fig. 4

  To further confirm the coordination of copper with ligand, the FT-IR spectra were recorded and noted in the following observations. First, the broad band at 3408–3389 cm−1, which belongs to ligands phenolic ν(O–H) stretching frequency has been demolished because the ν(O–H) proton is deprotonated and coordinated with copper [17]. Second, the sharp stretching frequency at 1542–1582 cm−1 corresponds to ligands diimines ν(CN) and it's shifted to a higher frequency (1624–1596 cm−1) due to the coordination of copper to diimine nitrogen's [17]. Third, the medium stretching frequency showed at 524–588 cm−1 and 442–516 cm−1, which indicates the (Cu–O) and (Cu–N), respectively (Fig. S1). The above observations are the strong witness the copper coordinates with phenolic –OH and diimine nitrogens [17].


#### Optimized molecular structure
  Further to support the formation of Cu(II) complexes and it's geometry, the density function theory (DFT) calculations have been used, which also gives the molecular structure description in the absence of crystal data. This method provides optimized structures along with bond lengths and angles [4,6,7,23]. The newly synthesized pendant-armed mixed-ligand copper(II) complexes (1–6) optimized by the B3LYP functional, the copper basis set is LANL2DZ, and the basis set of the remaining atom is 6–31G(d), and the best-optimized structure of the complexes are shown in Fig. 5 .Fig. 5Optimized molecular structures of pendant-armed copper(II) complexes (1–6) (A–F).Fig. 5

  The calculated bond lengths and bond angles values are compared with reported XRD data, and the calculated bond length and bond angles values are in good agreement with reported XRD data [[24], [25], [26], [27]]. Fig. 5 displayed the pentagonal bipyramidal geometry of the complexes at their minimum energy state, which is consistent with the reported X-ray data. These studies also supported the proposed molecular structure of the complexes [17].


### Biological studies

#### In vitro antibacterial activity
  Metal-based medicines are a new class of antimicrobial antibiotics that could be useful in the treatment of bacterial and fungal illnesses [28,29]. This motivates synthetic chemists to look for novel metal complexes for bioactive molecules, and copper has piqued their interest. Copper is probably the most researched cation in this regard, as a variety of low-molecular-weight copper complexes be effective in the treatment of tuberculosis, rheumatoid arthritis, stomach ulcers, and cancer [5,30].

  The copper(II) complexes were screened in vitro for antibacterial activity against certain pathogenic bacteria [[31], [32], [33]]. The complexes were found to exhibit considerable activity against Gram –ve and Gram + ve bacteria. The result of the antibacterial activities is shown in Fig. 6 & Fig. S2, and the screening data are summarized in Table 1 , Table S1. From the MIC values (Table S1), it is apparent that the complexes containing coordinated phen with extended aromatic rings show a maximum antibacterial effect than the coordinated bpy because their lipophilic and polar nature has increased the antibacterial activity.   It is well known that chelation is not the only criterion for antibacterial activity. Some important factors such as the nature of the metal ion, the nature of the ligand, the coordinating sites, the geometry of the complex, the concentration, hydrophilicity, lipophilicity, and the presence of co-ligands have a considerable influence on the antibacterial activity [[34], [35], [36]]. Complexes containing heterocyclic ligands have a more possibility of interacting with nucleosideases [37]. Compared to the standard drug ciprofloxacin, all complexes showed similar or slightly better antibacterial activity against all tested organisms. Complex 4 and 6 showed the highest antibacterial activity compared to other complexes because of phen ligand and electron-withdrawing substituent NO2. #### DNA binding experiments

##### Absorption spectral titration
  A first metal-based anticancer drug, cisplatin, mainly targets DNA [38,39]. Based on the success of cisplatin, much research has focused on metal complex DNA interactions [[5], [6], [7],23]. In this regard, the newly synthesized copper(II) complexes were tested with CT-DNA. In the absence and presence of increasing amounts of CT–DNA (0–100 M), the absorption spectra of complexes (1–6) at a constant concentration (10 M) are shown in Fig. 8 A and B. The absorption spectra reveal a rise in absorption intensities as the amount of CT–DNA added to complexes increases. Furthermore, after a complex–DNA interaction, the hyperchromic effect reflects the equivalent changes in the secondary structure of DNA in its conformation. There is no considerable shift in the absorption bands of the complexes in the presence of DNA, which strongly suggest the groove binding mode of the complexes to DNA. The intrinsic binding constant (K b) was determined and found to be in the range 3.83–5.09 × 104 M−1. The K b values are lower than those of the traditional intercalator EB-DNA (K b = 1.23 × 107 M−1) [40]. The relative propensity of complexes was: 6 > 4 > 2 > 5 > 3 > 1.Fig. 8Absorption spectra of the pendant-armed polyaza mixed-ligand mononuclear copper(II) complex 1 (A) and 2 (B) (10 μM) in Tris-HCl/NaCl (pH 7.2) buffer upon addition of CT–DNA (0–100 μM). The arrow shows the absorbance changes upon an increase in DNA concentration. Inset: Plot of [DNA]/(εa–εf) versus [DNA] for absorption titration of DNA with complexes. Emission spectra of the complexes 1 (C) and 2 (D), arrow shows the fluorescence changes upon increasing DNA concentration. Inset: Plots of emission intensity I0/I versus [DNA]/[Complex] for the titration of complexes.Fig. 8

  The observed hyperchromism and K b value for di –NO2 substituted complex 6, and complexes 4 and 2 in the case of mono-nitro substituted analogs, respectively, suggest its higher binding affinity to DNA than complexes containing other substituents. For these mixed ligand copper complexes, it can be said that the increased aromaticity and the number of NO2 groups enhance the potential groove mode of DNA binding. Mixed-ligand complexes, in general, have a wide range of geometry, size, hydrophobicity, electrical effects, and hydrogen-bonding ability, which are all important factors in determining DNA binding affinity [41]. Those with an extended aromatic ring (phen) (2, 4, 6) have a higher affinity for DNA binding than complexes with a coordinated bpy ring (1, 3, 5).


##### Fluorescence quenching studies
  Fluorescence quenching experiments can be employed to analyze the interaction mode of copper(II) complexes with CT–DNA and to monitor metal binding [42]. Because of its high intercalation between adjacent DNA base pairs, ethidium bromide produces bright fluorescent light in the presence of CT–DNA [43]. The increased fluorescence was previously reported to be suppressed by the addition of another molecule. Fig. 8C and D shows the emission spectra of EB bound to DNA in the absence and presence of complexes (1–6).

  Fig. 8C and D shows the addition of increasing amounts of EB bound to DNA emission spectra in the absence and presence of complexes (1–6). As the number of complexes increases, the intensity of the emission decreases, indicating that EB molecules are being displaced into the solution. The quenching graphs show that the Stern–Volmer equation suggests the fluorescence quenching of EB bound to DNA by complexes, confirming the interaction of the complexes with DNA [44]. Plotting I 0 /I versus [Complex] provided the value of KSV, which is the slope to intercept ratio. The KSV values for complexes were discovered to be between 2.67 and 3.25 × 103 M−1. The binding results indicate the ability of the complexes to display the DNA-bound EB, implying that the complexes effectively prevent EB emission enhancement, implying that it competes with EB. The conclusion of a –NO2 group and an extended aromatic moiety (phen) in complexes 6 and 4 may increase the complexes' ability to bind to DNA through groove mode.


### Molecular docking studies

#### Molecular docking with pathogens
  It is important to study these newly synthesized copper(II) complexes with pathogen receptors because in 2019 there have been an estimated 1.27 million deaths due to bacterial infections [45]. Therefore, safe and potent antibiotics are urgently required to cure these patients. From the experimental antibacterial studies, all the complexes showed remarkable antibacterial activity against tested Gram (+ve) and Gram (-ve) bacteria. Therefore, we are very much interested to study molecular docking studies against E. coli (PDB: 5E8Q), P. aeruginosa (PDB: 5EOE), and P. vulgaris (PDB: 5AVA), and S. aureus (PDB: 5ELZ) receptors.

  All the complexes strongly interacted with E. coli, P. aeruginosa, P. vulgaris, and S. aureus receptors via π-π, π-σ, π-alkyl, π-anion, π-cation, alkyl, hydrogen bond, van der Waals, and electrostatic interactions (Fig. 9 ). Based on the binding free energy values, complex 6 showed the highest binding energy and lowest inhibition constant. This observation is consistent with the experimental values. The treatment of SARS-CoV-2 is very limited and urgently require new drugs. Hence, many research groups focused on the synthesis of various chemical compounds and testing their activity against SARS-CoV-2 [[51], [52], [53]]. Recently, various metal complexes have been synthesized and studied molecular docking model against SARS-CoV-2 [18,[54], [55], [56]] and have shown promising results. ## Conclusions
  Newly synthesized pendant-armed mixed-ligand copper(II) complexes exhibit pentagonal-bipyramidal geometry around the metal ions, which are very potent against Gram-negative pathogens. This is also confirmed by molecular docking studies. All the complexes bind with DNA via groove mode, which is further supported by molecular docking studies. Complexes were significantly interacting with SARS-CoV-2 receptor via π-π, π-σ, π-alkyl, π-anion, π-cation, alkyl, hydrogen bond, van der Waals, and electrostatic interactions. Interestingly, complexes showed the highest binding energy and lowest inhibition constant (nM; nanomolar) compared to standard drugs chloroquine and molnupiravir (μM; micromolar). These new copper(II) complexes showed the highest binding energy and lowest inhibition constant against the tested pathogen, DNA, and SARS-CoV-2 receptors.
