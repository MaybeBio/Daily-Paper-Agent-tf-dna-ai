### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Technical soundness  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that "structure-based alignment and molecular dynamics simulations revealed an essential binding surface of gp16-CTD for prohead RNA."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided  
- **Concern** The identification of a binding surface is based solely on computational methods, with no experimental validation such as mutagenesis, crosslinking, or binding assays. The abstract does not specify the simulation timescales, force fields, or convergence criteria, which are critical for assessing the reliability of the predicted surface.  
- **Why it matters** A predicted binding surface without experimental support is a hypothesis, not a revealed feature. Overstating computational predictions as definitive findings could mislead readers and undermine the mechanistic model.  
- **Resolution test** Provide experimental evidence (e.g., site-directed mutagenesis, binding affinity measurements, or structural validation) that confirms the proposed RNA binding surface, and include detailed simulation methods and validation in the full manuscript.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Technical soundness  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that "simulations highlighted a dynamic interplay between the N-terminal domain and the CTD of gp16, which may play a role in driving movement of DNA into the procapsid."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided  
- **Concern** The dynamic interplay is inferred from simulations, but the abstract does not describe the simulation system, the conformational sampling approach, or how the proposed interplay is quantified. The functional relevance to DNA translocation is speculative, as no direct coupling between the observed dynamics and motor function is demonstrated.  
- **Why it matters** Without rigorous simulation details and functional correlation, the claim remains speculative. The proposed role in DNA movement is central to the mechanistic model, so unsupported dynamics weaken the overall conclusion.  
- **Resolution test** Provide simulation parameters, convergence analysis, and a clear demonstration that the observed interdomain dynamics correlate with functional states, ideally supported by experimental mutations that alter motor activity.
