- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no clinical data tables, sequence alignments, simulation methods, or structural figures provided

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a clinically and mechanistically interesting case series with a plausible genotype-phenotype correlation. The novelty of three unreported mutations and the use of molecular dynamics simulations to link structural effects to clinical severity are commendable. However, the abstract alone provides insufficient detail to evaluate the robustness of the clinical phenotyping, the validity of the simulation approach, or the strength of the proposed correlations. Several claims, particularly regarding the mechanistic distinction of V617I and the broader genotype-phenotype correlation, require additional evidence from the full manuscript.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Clinical geneticists and pediatric neurologists would find the expanded mutation spectrum and phenotypic variability useful for diagnostic and counseling purposes. Computational biologists studying transcription factor structure-function relationships and researchers investigating TCF4-related neurodevelopmental disorders would be interested in the proposed mechanistic insights from molecular dynamics simulations.
- **Major strengths** The report of four unrelated patients with de novo mutations, three of which are novel, adds valuable data to the Pitt-Hopkins syndrome literature. The inclusion of a patient with a milder phenotype associated with V617I is clinically notable. The use of molecular dynamics simulations to connect specific mutations to functional consequences represents a thoughtful approach to bridging genotype and phenotype.

The genotype-phenotype correlation is asserted without accounting for potential confounding factors such as age at assessment or variability in clinical evaluation methods.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that mutations T606A, K607E, and R578C affect DNA binding directly or indirectly, while V617I induces only subtle conformational changes.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract states the results of molecular dynamics simulations but provides no methodological details, such as simulation length, force field, starting structures, or replicate numbers. Without this information, it is impossible to assess whether the observed conformational changes are robust or merely artifacts of the simulation setup.  
- **Why it matters** The mechanistic distinction between the mutations is central to the paper's claim of a genotype-phenotype correlation. If the simulation results are not reproducible or statistically sound, the proposed link between structural effects and clinical severity is unsupported.  
- **Resolution test** Provide full simulation methods, including software, parameters, equilibration protocols, and at least three independent replicates. Show that the conformational differences between mutant and wild-type TCF4 are statistically significant, for example through root-mean-square deviation or fluctuation analyses with error bars.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Clinical evidence  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that the patient with V617I exhibits mild intellectual disability and more advanced motor and verbal skills, significantly different from other cases.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract does not specify how intellectual disability and motor or verbal skills were assessed, nor does it provide standardized scores or age-adjusted comparisons. The claim of "significantly different" is not supported by any statistical or normative data.  
- **Why it matters** The mild phenotype of the V617I patient is a key observation that underpins the genotype-phenotype correlation. Without validated clinical assessments, the claim of a distinct phenotype cannot be verified.  
- **Resolution test** Include standardized developmental or intelligence assessments (e.g., IQ scores, Vineland Adaptive Behavior Scales) with age at testing, and compare the V617I patient's scores to those of the other three patients and to published Pitt-Hopkins syndrome cohorts.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** No  
- **Axis** Generalizability  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that the study "reinforces the genotype-phenotype correlation" in Pitt-Hopkins syndrome.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
- **Concern** The study includes only four patients, and the correlation is based on a single patient with a mild phenotype. This is insufficient to establish or reinforce a generalizable genotype-phenotype correlation, especially given the known variability in Pitt-Hopkins syndrome even among patients with identical mutations.  
- **Why it matters** Overstating the strength of the correlation could mislead clinical counseling and future research priorities. The claim should be tempered to reflect the preliminary nature of the observation.  
- **Resolution test** Acknowledge the small sample size and frame the correlation as hypothesis-generating. If possible, include a review of previously reported cases with these or similar mutations to contextualize the findings.

- **Concern ID** R1-M4  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** No  
- **Axis** Novelty verification  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that three of the four missense mutations have not yet been reported.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract does not specify which three mutations are novel or provide a literature search strategy to support this claim. Without a clear statement of how novelty was determined, the claim is difficult to verify.  
- **Why it matters** Novelty is a key selling point of the paper. If any of the mutations have been reported elsewhere, the contribution is diminished.  
- **Resolution test** Clearly list which mutations are novel and describe the search criteria (e.g., databases searched, date of search, inclusion of gray literature). Provide references for any previously reported mutations.
