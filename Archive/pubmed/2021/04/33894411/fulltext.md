# Anesthetics isoflurane and sevoflurane attenuate flagellin-mediated inflammation in the lung


## Summary
  Isoflurane and sevoflurane are volatile anesthetics (VA) widely used in clinical practice to provide general anesthesia. We and others have previously shown that VAs have immunomodulatory effects and may have a significant impact on the progression of disease states. Flagellin is a component of Gram negative bacteria and plays a significant role in the pathophysiology of bacterial pneumonia through its binding to Toll-like Receptor 5 (TLR5). Our results showed that VAs, not an intravenous anesthetic, significantly attenuated the activation of TLR5 and the release of the neutrophil chemoattractant IL-8 from lung epithelial cells. Furthermore, flagellin-induced lung injury was significantly attenuated by VAs by inhibiting neutrophil migration to the bronchoalveolar space. The lungs of cystic fibrosis (CF) patients are highly colonized by Pseudomonas aeruginosa, which causes inflammation. The retrospective study of oxygenation in patients with CF who had received VA versus intravenous anesthesia suggested that VAs might have the protective effect for gas exchange. To understand the interaction between VAs and TLR5, a docking simulation was performed, which indicated that isoflurane and sevoflurane docked into the binding interphase between TLR5 and flagellin.


## Introduction
  Halogenated VAs, isoflurane and sevoflurane, have been shown to be able to modulate the immune responses [1–6]. Given the ubiquitous role of immunity in the progression of disease states, anesthetic drugs can be used as pharmacological tools for modifying the immune responses. Concurrent administration of anesthetic agents may affect patients’ outcome. They can also be used as prototypes to develop therapeutic candidates for immunotherapy. VAs have been suggested to protect patients from lung injury, though the mechanisms are not completely delineated yet [7].

  Lung consists of heterogeneous cells equipped with defense system against danger signals. TLRs are major components of the defense system. Individual TLRs recognize a distinct but limited repertoire of conserved microbe-specific molecular structures called pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs), contributing to the production of an array of cytokines. Targeting the signaling cascade responsible for overproduction of inflammatory cytokines is a particularly attractive treatment approach for reducing inflammation in lung injury [8].

  Lipopolysaccharide (LPS), a cell wall component of Gram negative bacteria, is a well-recognized PAMP for TLR4. Although LPS-induced lung injury models are major preclinical models to study lung injury so far, initial immunological responses via TLR4 can be minimal in airway cells due to a lack of the necessary co-receptors such as myeloid differentiation (MD)-2 and CD14 [9,10]. TLR5-mediated cytokine production and neutrophil recruitment is observed as an early response to lung infection.

  Flagella are whip-like structures that enable bacterial motility by propeller-like rotation, consisting of its major constituent flagellin and associated anchoring structures. Escherichia coli, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Salmonella typhi and Vibrio cholerae are among the bacteria with flagella [11]. Cocci such as Staphylococci and Streptococci do not have flagella and are non-motile. Flagellin is a ligand for TLR5 [12]. Inhibiting flagellin-mediated early inflammatory responses can be beneficial to attenuate tissue damage. TLR5 is expressed largely on neutrophils, monocytes, dendritic cells and epithelial cells [13,14]. TLR5 is preferentially expressed on the apical side of respiratory epithelia [9,10,15], and airway epithelial cells almost exclusively rely on TLR5 to sense bacteria with flagella [16–18]. Pseudomonas aeruginosa is one of the most common microbes responsible for nosocomial infections, particularly associated with ventilator-associated pneumonia with high morbidity and mortality [19]. Patients with CF are frequently infected with Pseudomonas aeruginosa. TLR5 signaling is considered as a target of intervention to attenuate CF lung disease [8]. Thus, identification of medications that inhibit flagellin-mediated inflammatory responses can be beneficial to attenuate lung damage. Here we examined the role of VAs on flagellin-mediated lung injury and their effect on TLR5 function.


## Materials and Methods

### Nuclear factor κ-light-chain-enhancer of activated B cells (NFκB) activation by flagellin in reporter cells
  THP-1 blue cells (InvivoGen; San Diego, CA) is a human monocytic leukemia cell line with an NFκB-inducible secreted embryonic alkaline phosphate (SEAP) reporter. The cells were cultured per the company’s protocol. They were first stimulated with flagellin at a range of concentrations (InvivoGen) (0–10 μg/mL) for 6 hours to determine the appropriate concentration. In the experiments where cells were exposed to VAs (0–2%), they were placed in an air-tight chamber and exposed to isoflurane or sevoflurane by a vaporizer using air as a carrier for 6 hours as we previously reported [20–22]. Intravenous anesthetic (propofol) (0–100 μΜ) was added to corresponding wells for 6 hours. NFκB activation was assessed by using the reaction buffer Quanti-Blue to quantitate SEAP in the medium per the company’s protocol. Samples were subjected to a spectrophotometer analysis at 655 nm. We also examined the effect of anesthetics using HEK-TLR5 NFκB reporter cells (InvivoGen) stimulated with flagellin (1 μg/mL) for 6 hours and HEK-TLR7 NFκB reporter cells (InvivoGen) stimulated with R837 (Imiquimod) (10 μg/mL) for 6 hours.


### IL-1β production upon flagellin stimulation
  In addition to TLR5, flagellin activates the cytosolic inflammasome, leading to IL-1β production. IL-1β level in the medium was measured using IL-1β ELISA kit per the company’s protocol (Peprotech; Canbury, NJ).


### IL-8 production from lung epithelial cells
  A549 cells (ATCC, Manassas, VA) were cultured per the company’s recommendation. Following overnight culture of Pseudomonas aeruginosa PAK strain WT and FliC deletion mutant, they were heat killed at 60°C for 30 min. FliC deletion mutant was a gift from Dr. Reuben Ramphal (University of Florida) [23]. A549 cells were co-incubated with 105 CFU bacteria for 6 hours with or without isoflurane, sevoflurane, or propofol. Following incubation, the supernatant was collected and subjected to ELISA for IL-8 measurement per the company’s protocol (R&D Systems; Minneapolis, MN).


### Flagellin intranasal instillation model
  All the experimental procedures complied with Animal Research Reporting of In Vivo Experiments (ARRIVE) guidelines regarding the use of animals in research [24] and the National Institute of Health guideline for the care and use of Laboratory animals, and were approved by Boston Children’s Hospital animal care and use committee. 1 μg of flagellin suspension was intranasally given to mice on the C57J/BL6 background (8–12 week, male mice) anesthetized by ketamine/xylazine [25]. Then, some of the mice were exposed to either 1% of sevoflurane or 1% of isoflurane. After 6 hours, mice were euthanized and subjected to bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL). BAL fluid was centrifuged and the supernatant was subjected to KC and total protein analysis. KC level was measured using ELISA kit per the company’s protocol (R&D systems). Pellets were subjected to flow cytometry analysis. Neutrophils were gated as CD11b+Ly6G+ population, using anti-mouse CD11b (M1/70)-PE and anti-mouse Ly6G (1A8)-APC antibodies (Biolegend; Dedham, MA). In a separate experiment, lung was fixed using 4% paraformaldehyde for histological analysis. Lung histology was subjected to Hematoxylin and Eosin (H&E) staining or immunohistochemistry (IHC) probed by biotin labeled anti-Ly6G antibody. Biotin labeled anti-Ly6G antibody was probed with peroxidase-conjugated streptavidin. Then, 3,3’-diaminobenzidine (DAB) staining was performed according to the company’s protocol (Millipore; Norcross, GA).


### Oxygenation of CF patients under VAs or intravenous anesthetics
  The protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) at Boston Children’s Hospital. The consent was waived by the IRB because the study was to retrospectively review chart without patient identifiers. We screened the electronic medical records of CF patients who underwent lung transplantation from 2010 to 2019 in our institution. These patients underwent pre-lung transplantation workup including arterial blood gas analysis in the cardiac catheterization laboratory under anesthesia care. Among them, we selected CF patients who received both VA based care and non-VA based care within two month periods. If patients did not have either one of anesthesia regimens, or received extracorporeal membrane oxygenation support for at least one of their procedures, we did not include them. We obtained age, weight, gender, inspired oxygen concentration (FiO2), and partial pressure of oxygen (PaO2). As a surrogate of oxygenation, P/F ratio (= PaO2/FiO2) was used.


### Flagellin to TLR5 binding assay
  The binding between flagellin and TLR5 was evaluated using flat bottom plates. 96 well culture treated flat plates were coated with flagellin (5 μg/mL) at 37°C for 2 hours. As a control, some wells were coated with 1% BSA. Wells were washed and blocked with 1% BSA for 2 hours at 37 °C. HEK-TLR5 cells were cultured per the company’s protocol. Then HEK-TLR5 cells were stained with 2’,7’-bis(2-carboxyethyl)-5(6)-carboxyfluorescein (BCECF-AM). 1 × 105 cells were given to each well with or without VA (isoflurane or sevoflurane) for incubation at 37°C for 1 hour. Wells were washed and remaining cells on the plate were detected using fluorescence plate reader. The binding % was calculated as [(Input cell fluorescence signal)− (fluorescence after wash signal)]/[(Input fluorescence signal)] × 100 (%).


### Docking analysis of VA isoflurane and sevoflurane on TLR5
  In silico prediction of potential isoflurane binding sites on TLR5 (PDB: 3V44) was performed using SwissDock, a web service to predict the molecular interactions that may occur between a target protein and a small molecule [26]. The protein structure was prepared using the built-in protein preparation wizard. Binding modes were scored using their FullFitness and clustered. Clusters were then ranked according to the average FullFitness of their elements. Estimated binding site with the highest score was then embedded into the grid for docking with isoflurane.


### Statistical analysis
  Statistical analysis was performed using PRISM5 software (GraphPad; La Jolla, CA, USA). Detailed statistical analysis was described in the corresponding figure legends. Statistical significance was considered when p< 0.05.


## Results

### Isoflurane and Sevoflurane attenuated flagellin-mediated TLR5 activation of NFκB
  To find the optimal concentration for flagellin to induce NFkB activation, first we determined the dose response to flagellin in THP-1 blue NFκB reporter cells. Based on our initial dose-response trial (Fig. 1a), we used 1 μg/mL of flagellin for the subsequent experiments. To test our hypothesis that VAs would attenuate TLR5, we examined flagellin-mediated NFκB activation in the presence of different anesthetics. We found that VAs isoflurane and sevoflurane attenuated flagellin-mediated NFκB activation, while intravenous anesthetic propofol did not have a significant effect (Fig. 1b–d). Flagellin activates TLR5 as well as cytosolic inflammasome. The latter leads to IL-1β production. Both isoflurane and sevoflurane did not attenuate IL-1β production (Fig. 1e). To test the hypothesis that VAs would attenuate TLR5-mediated activation, we stimulated HEK-TLR5 reporter cells with flagellin with or without isoflurane or sevoflurane. Both significantly attenuated TLR5-mediated activation significantly (Fig. 1f), but did not attenuate TLR7-mediated activation (Fig. 1g). Because both TLR5 and TLR7 use the same signaling pathway for NFκB activation, both VAs would likely interact with cell surface TLR5.


### Isoflurane and Sevoflurane attenuated the production of neutrophil chemoattractant IL-8 from lung epithelial cells via TLR5 signaling pathway.
  Gram negative flagellated bacteria invade the lungs and affect lung epithelial cells. Lung epithelial cells express a number of TLR receptors (TLR1, TLR2, TLR3, TLR4, TLR5 and TLR6) [27]. We examined the role of flagellin using Pseudomonas aeruginosa PAK wild-type (WT) and flagellin deletion mutant (FliC deletion mutant). Human lung epithelial cell line (A549) incubated with PAK WT induced IL-8 production, while incubation with PAK FliC deletion mutant did not. This finding demonstrated the critical role of flagellin in IL-8 production (Fig. 1h). Next we examined the effect of anesthetics on IL-8 production from A549 cells in the presence of PAK strain. As expected, IL-8 production was significantly attenuated by isoflurane and sevoflurane (Fig. 1i). Propofol (100 μM) did not affect IL-8 production. Of note, IL-1b was not detected in the media of A549 cells with PAK stimulation (data not shown). These results suggest that VAs isoflurane and sevoflurane potently attenuated the production of neutrophil chemoattractant IL-8 via TLR5 signaling pathway.


### Isoflurane and Sevoflurane attenuated flagellin-mediated neutrophil recruitment and inflammation
  Because the in vitro data demonstrated that VAs attenuated TLR5 function, we examined their effect in vivo. We tested the effect of VAs in flagellin-induced lung injury model. As expected, both isoflurane and sevoflurane exposure significantly decreased the number of neutrophils observed in the BAL fluid (Fig. 2a). The level of IL-8 homologue KC was lower in the VA arm (Fig. 2b). Total protein levels in BAL were also lower in VAs, indicating less lung injury (Fig. 2c). Histological analysis by immunohistochemistry and H&E staining also confirmed less neutrophil recruitment to the lung with both VAs (Fig. 2d–e). Flagellin-treated naïve lung showed hemorrhagic lung injury, which was significantly attenuated by VA treatment (Fig. 2e).


### Isoflurane and Sevoflurane improved the oxygenation levels of CF patients
  Because our data indicated the possible beneficial role of VAs in lung inflammatory diseases driven by flagellin, we sought to examine the clinical data relevant to our experimental platform. CF patients are well colonized with Gram negative strains including Pseudomonas aeruginosa. Because flagellin and IL-8 have been detected from the expectorations from CF patients [28], we extracted data of CF patients from the electrical medical record. We chose CF patients who underwent mechanical ventilation under both VA-based and non-VA-based care within one month apart with the availability of arterial blood gas. We found only 12 patients (8 males, 4 females) who met the criteria from 2010 to 2019. Average age was 12.1 +/− 4.92 (year), and average weight was 35.1 +/− 13.5 (Kg).

  We compared the degree of oxygenation using PaO2/FiO2 (P/F) ratio under both VA-based and non-VA-based care (Fig. 4a). P/F ratio was significantly higher under patients receiving VA-based care, which is in line with our observations in the preclinical model. Tidal volume and mean airway pressure were not significantly different between the two groups (Fig. 4b–c), further supporting the potential role of anesthetic choice on lung pathophysiology in these patients and the potential protective effect of VAs on lung injury.


### The docking simulation of Isoflurane and Sevoflurane onto TLR5
  We previously demonstrated that VAs bound to TLR2 and TLR4 [20,22]. Flagellin-TLR5 expressing HEK cell binding was significantly attenuated by both isoflurane and sevoflurane (Fig. 4a). No human or mouse TLR5 crystal structure is available yet. The structure of zebrafish TLR5 has been reported [29]. We performed structural analysis using SwissDock software predicted that both isoflurane and sevoflurane would bind to the zebrafish TLR5 (protein data bank PDB 3V44 [29]) (Fig. 4 b–c) and the binding pocket was located at the interface of TLR5-flagellin binding (Fig. 4d). Propofol did not dock to the pocket. The validation of this result needs to be done in human TLR5 in the future.


## Discussion
  We have shown that VAs isoflurane and sevoflurane exposure 1) attenuated TLR5 function in vitro, and 2) decreased the number of recruited neutrophils into the alveolar space in the flagellin-induced lung injury model in vivo. Cystic fibrosis patients, who are usually infected with Pseudomonas aeruginosa, demonstrated the higher P/F ratio under VA-based care, suggesting the promising role of VAs to attenuate lung inflammatory diseases driven by flagellin.

  In a prospective randomized study in patients with moderate to severe acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) receiving either midazolam or sevoflurane for 48 hours, patients in the sevoflurane arm had better oxygenation (higher P/F ratio) with a significant reduction in the lung injury marker, a receptor for advanced glycation end product (RAGE) [7]. Direct comparisons between VA- and propofol-based anesthetics during mechanical ventilation showed that the use of VAs was associated with less proinflammatory cytokine production [30]. Here we suggested that VA-based sedation/anesthesia could offer significant protection from lung injury derived from flagellin-mediated activation via TLR5 in the preclinical model, which is in line with the previous study suggesting that TLR5 pathway was considered to be a target for antiinflammatory therapy in CF patients [30]. Furthermore, our retrospective study also suggested a protective effect of VAs on the lung of CF patients. Future studies on patients’ BAL samples along with pulmonary mechanics and gas exchange under different anesthetics would provide an insight into the role of VAs. Because a number of CF patients undergo anesthetic care and sedation [31], the implication of these studies is significant.

  TLRs possess leucine-rich repeats (LRRs) and X-ray crystallographic structures for TLR members demonstrated significant similarity. Because we demonstrated that VAs directly interacted with TLR2 and TLR4, we speculated that TLR5 would also interact with VAs [20,22]. Docking simulation to zebrafish’s TLR5 suggested that both isoflurane and sevoflurane might directly bind to TLR5 in the interface between TLR5 and flagellin binding. Although reported TLR5’s structure is only for zebrafish at this point, it is highly possible that this is conserved in the human and mouse and both anesthetics directly interact with the corresponding species TLR5. Further investigations are needed to answer this question.

  In conclusion, we have demonstrated that VAs reduced TLR5 activation and attenuated flagellin-induced lung injury in both in vitro and in vivo studies. Our retrospective study of CF patients was in line with this finding, indicating that our laboratory findings could be of clinical relevance.
