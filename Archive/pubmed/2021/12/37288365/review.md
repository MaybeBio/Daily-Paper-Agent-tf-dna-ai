## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This is a well-structured and readable review that consolidates recent single-molecule studies of SpCas9 into a coherent mechanistic narrative. The authors successfully organize a complex literature into distinct stages of the Cas9 reaction pathway and highlight the unique contributions of each technique. The manuscript is suitable for a specialized audience but lacks critical depth in places, particularly in comparing conflicting results and discussing limitations of the reviewed studies. The absence of figures and Table 1 in the provided material prevents full evaluation of data presentation.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in CRISPR biology, single-molecule biophysics, and genome engineering will find this review useful. It provides a concise entry point for biophysicists entering the CRISPR field and offers mechanistic insights that could inform engineering of Cas9 variants with improved specificity. The review may also interest method developers seeking applications for single-molecule tools.
- **Major strengths** The manuscript provides a clear, stage-by-stage organization of the Cas9 mechanism, which aids readability. The authors effectively integrate findings from multiple single-molecule techniques, showing how complementary approaches converge on a unified model. The inclusion of recent work on engineered Cas9 variants and motor-protein-mediated dissociation adds contemporary relevance. The writing is generally accessible to nonspecialists with basic molecular biology knowledge.
- **Major Concerns** 
  - R1-M1: The review does not critically compare or reconcile conflicting findings across studies, particularly regarding the role of 1D diffusion in PAM search. The authors present the 3D collision model from Sternberg et al. and the 1D diffusion model from Globyte et al. as complementary, but do not discuss potential experimental differences that could explain the discrepancy. This weakens the scientific depth of the review.
  - R1-M2: The section on conformational rearrangements relies heavily on structural inferences and bulk FRET data, but the discussion of smFRET studies is superficial. The authors mention three groups identified an intermediate state, but do not describe the labeling strategies, timescales, or how these studies differ methodologically. This limits the reader's ability to assess the robustness of the conclusions.
  - R1-M3: The review claims that single-molecule studies provide a "framework for rational design" of Cas9 variants, but the link between specific mechanistic findings and engineering outcomes is not explicitly drawn. For example, the discussion of high-fidelity variants is brief and does not explain how single-molecule data informed their design. This overstates the translational impact without sufficient evidence.
- **Minor Comments** 
  - R1-m1: The abstract states the review "outlines" techniques and "discusses insights," which is accurate, but the phrase "comprehensive understanding" in the introduction could be tempered to avoid overstatement given the selective coverage.
  - R1-m2: The description of DNA curtains in the "Stable binding" section is redundant with the earlier technique description. Consider consolidating to avoid repetition.
  - R1-m3: The review mentions "engineered Cas9s" but does not provide a table or systematic comparison of their properties. A summary table of variants and their single-molecule characteristics would enhance utility.
  - R1-m4: The sentence "the HNH domain after cleavage was highly flexible" is vague. Specify the timescale and technique used to observe this flexibility.
  - R1-m5: The acknowledgements note that some work could not be included due to space limitations. This is acceptable, but the review would benefit from a brief statement in the introduction about selection criteria for included studies.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** None identified from the supplied material. The manuscript is a review, and the technical claims are attributed to cited primary studies. However, the lack of figures and Table 1 in the provided material prevents verification of data presentation accuracy.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - Originality: Moderate. The review synthesizes existing literature without presenting new data or a novel conceptual framework. Its value lies in organization rather than originality.
  - Scientific importance: Moderate to high. Cas9 is a widely used tool, and mechanistic understanding is relevant to many researchers. However, the review does not identify unresolved questions or propose new directions beyond general statements.
  - Interdisciplinary readership: Moderate. The content bridges biophysics and molecular biology, but the depth of technique descriptions may be too basic for specialists and too detailed for casual readers.
  - Technical soundness: Generally sound, but the lack of critical comparison of conflicting results and superficial treatment of some smFRET studies reduces rigor.
  - Readability for nonspecialists: Good. The writing is clear, and the stage-by-stage organization helps comprehension. However, some terms (e.g., "R-loop", "PAM-proximal") are used without definition, which may challenge readers outside the field.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The review is informative and well-written, but the identified concerns regarding critical comparison and depth of discussion should be addressed to strengthen its scientific contribution.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that single-molecule studies provide a "framework for rational design" of Cas9 variants is not explicitly supported by examples linking mechanistic findings to engineering outcomes.
- The statement that "single-molecule techniques will continue to contribute to the CRISPR field" is speculative and not substantiated with specific future directions.
- The assertion that "the first 8–10 bp crRNA–DNA matches are sufficient to support stable binding" is presented as a consensus but is based on a limited number of studies; conflicting data on seed region requirements are not discussed.
- The claim that "DNA-based motor proteins may promote final DNA dissociation" is presented as a general conclusion, but the evidence is limited to a few in vitro studies with specific motor proteins; generalizability is not established.
