## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, conclusion) as provided.
- **Assessment boundary** Evaluation is limited to the content supplied. Claims regarding performance, novelty, and utility are assessed against the evidence presented within this text. No external data, code, or supplementary materials were available for verification.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors introduce 155 biological language models (BLMs) for DNA, RNA, and protein sequence analysis, organized into four categories. These are integrated into a platform, BioSeq-BLM, that automatically constructs predictors, evaluates performance, and analyzes results. The authors claim that predictors generated by BioSeq-BLM achieve performance comparable to or better than existing state-of-the-art methods across several benchmark tasks.
- **Visible evidence base** The manuscript text includes descriptions of the BLM categories, the system architecture, and performance results for five example tasks (DNase I hypersensitive sites, miRNA precursors, DNA-binding proteins, RNA-binding proteins, intrinsically disordered regions, RNA secondary structure). Performance is reported via figures and supplementary tables referenced in the text.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** The following were not available for review: all figures (Figures 1–10), all supplementary tables (Tables S1–S9), the benchmark datasets, the command-line examples, the web server interface, the standalone package code, and the user manual. Without these, the quantitative claims and the usability of the platform cannot be independently verified.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript presents a potentially useful and broad platform for biological sequence analysis, built on a systematic categorization of language-model-based representations. The conceptual framing of biological sequences as a "book of life" with linguistic properties is engaging and provides a coherent motivation. However, the current text is largely descriptive, and the evidence for the central claims of performance superiority or parity is presented only through references to figures and supplementary tables that were not available for inspection. The novelty of the individual BLMs is unclear, as many appear to be existing feature extraction or deep learning methods reorganized under a new taxonomy. The manuscript would benefit from a clearer articulation of what is genuinely new, a more rigorous and transparent comparison with existing tools, and a demonstration of the platform's usability and scalability.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Computational biologists and bioinformaticians developing or applying machine learning methods for sequence-based prediction tasks would be the primary audience. Researchers interested in natural language processing applications in biology, and those seeking a unified platform to benchmark multiple sequence representations, may also find the resource valuable. The platform's promise of automated predictor construction could appeal to biologists with limited programming expertise.
- **Major strengths**
  - The manuscript addresses a relevant and growing area: the application of language models to biological sequences.
  - The organization of 155 BLMs into four categories provides a useful conceptual framework and a potentially valuable resource for the community.
  - The platform's design, with automated predictor construction and evaluation, addresses a practical need for accessible tools.
  - The inclusion of both web server and standalone package increases the potential user base.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Evidence for performance claims
  - **Claim pointer** The abstract and results sections claim that predictors generated by BioSeq-BLM achieve "comparable or even obviously better performance than the existing state-of-the-art predictors published in literatures."
  - **Evidence pointer** Figures 5–10 and Supplementary Tables S1–S8; location not provided in the text.
  - **Concern** The central claim of the manuscript is that BioSeq-BLM produces high-performing predictors. However, the actual performance data are not presented in the text. The results are only described qualitatively (e.g., "highly comparable," "outperforming," "obviously better results"). Without access to the figures and supplementary tables, it is impossible to assess the magnitude of the reported improvements, the statistical significance of the differences, or the fairness of the comparisons. The text also does not specify the evaluation metrics used for each task beyond a general list in the Methods section.
  - **Why it matters** The scientific value of the platform rests on the demonstrated quality of its predictions. Unverifiable performance claims undermine the manuscript's core contribution.
  - **Resolution test** Provide the performance data in the main text or ensure the supplementary tables are included in the review package. For each task, report the metrics for all compared methods, including confidence intervals or statistical tests where appropriate. Clarify the experimental setup, including train/test splits and hyperparameter tuning.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Novelty and conceptual contribution
  - **Claim pointer** The authors state that "BioSeq-BLM is the first study to define the BLMs and introduce 155 different BLMs for biological sequence analysis."
  - **Evidence pointer** Introduction and Table 1; location not provided.
  - **Concern** The manuscript does not clearly delineate which of the 155 BLMs are new contributions and which are existing methods (e.g., k-mer, word2vec, autoencoders) reorganized under the BLM umbrella. The term "biological language model" is used broadly, but the conceptual advance over existing feature engineering and deep learning approaches is not articulated. The comparison with BioSeq-Analysis2.0 in Table 1 is mentioned but not detailed in the text.
  - **Why it matters** For a methods paper, the novelty of the approach is a primary criterion. If the BLMs are largely re-implementations of known techniques, the contribution is primarily the platform itself, which needs to be positioned accordingly.
  - **Resolution test** Clearly state which BLMs are novel and which are existing methods. Provide a detailed comparison with BioSeq-Analysis2.0, highlighting specific new functionalities or representations. Discuss the conceptual distinction between a "language model" and a generic feature representation in this context.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Platform usability and validation
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims that BioSeq-BLM is "particularly useful for solving the problems of extracting the linguistic features and designing the techniques derived from natural language processing."
  - **Evidence pointer** Sections "BioSeq-BLM web server and stand-alone package" and "How to choose web server or stand-alone package"; location not provided.

No details are given on the implementation, computational requirements, scalability to large datasets, or the user experience. The claim of GPU acceleration and multithreading is mentioned but not benchmarked. The manual is referenced but not provided.
  - **Why it matters** A platform's utility depends on its practical usability. Without evidence of performance, scalability, and ease of use, the claim of being "particularly useful" is not substantiated.
  - **Resolution test** Provide benchmarks on runtime and memory usage for representative tasks. Describe the system architecture and dependencies. Include a clear tutorial or case study demonstrating the workflow from input to result.

The authors should be invited to resubmit a revised version that includes the missing data and addresses the concerns raised.
