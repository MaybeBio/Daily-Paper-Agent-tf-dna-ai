## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text, including main figures referenced in text, but no actual figure panels, supplementary figures, tables, or datasets were provided.
- **Assessment boundary** Assessment is limited to the claims, methods, and interpretations as described in the manuscript text. No raw data, experimental protocols, or code were available for independent verification.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report that the intrinsically disordered cytoplasmic tail of E-cadherin (E-cad) remains highly dynamic when bound to its folded ligand β-catenin (β-cat). Using single-molecule FRET, nanosecond fluorescence correlation spectroscopy, and coarse-grained simulations, they propose that E-cad diffuses across the entire β-cat surface on submillisecond-to-millisecond timescales, driven by a rugged energy landscape with many weak, transient contacts rather than a dominant free-energy barrier. They further suggest that this "Velcro-like" design reconciles specificity, high affinity, and flexibility, with potential functional implications for phosphorylation access and mechanical damping.
- **Visible evidence base** Text descriptions of FRET efficiency distributions, salt-dependent changes, affinity measurements, nsFCS correlation functions, RASP kinetics, temperature-dependent dynamics, and CG model predictions. References to Figures 1–6 and SI Appendix figures/tables, none of which were provided.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** All figure panels, SI Appendix content (including Table S1, Figs. S1–S6), Dataset S1, experimental methods, simulation details, and analysis code. Without these, quantitative claims cannot be independently assessed.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript addresses an important and timely question about the dynamics of intrinsically disordered proteins within fuzzy complexes. The combination of experimental and computational approaches is ambitious and potentially powerful. However, the provided text alone does not allow verification of the central claims. Key quantitative results, control experiments, and model validation are referenced but not shown. The interpretation hinges on several assumptions (e.g., the validity of the CG model, the interpretation of RASP kinetics) that require scrutiny of the missing materials. The scientific premise is strong and the conclusions are plausible, but the evidence base as presented is insufficient for a definitive assessment.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in the fields of intrinsically disordered proteins, protein-protein interactions, single-molecule biophysics, and cell adhesion signaling. The work provides a quantitative framework for understanding how disordered regions sample folded surfaces, which is relevant to drug discovery targeting such interfaces and to understanding allosteric regulation in dynamic complexes.
- **Major Strengths**  
  The study combines multiple orthogonal techniques (smFRET, nsFCS, RASP, CG simulations) to address dynamics across timescales. The use of a CG model that is refined against experimental FRET data is a thoughtful approach. The finding that the C-segment, despite being resolved in an X-ray structure, is dynamically exchanging on the β-cat surface is a notable and non-obvious result. The discussion of functional implications (kinase access, mechanical damping) is well-reasoned and adds biological relevance.
