The human estrogen receptor alpha (ERalpha) is an important regulator in breast cancer development and progression. The frequent ERalpha mutations in the ligand-binding domain (LBD) can increase the resistance of antiestrogen drugs, highlighting the need to develop new drugs to target ERalpha-positive breast cancer. In this study, we combined molecular docking, molecular dynamics simulations and binding free energy calculations to develop a structure-based virtual screening workflow to identify hit compounds capable of interfering with the recognition of ERalpha by the specific response element of DNA. A druggable pocket on the DNA binding domain (DBD) of ERalpha was identified as the potential binding site. The hits binding modes were further analyzed to reveal the structural characteristics of the DBD-inhibitor complexes. The core structure of the lead molecules was synthesized and was found to inhibit the E2-induced cell proliferation in MCF-7 cell lines. These  fi ndings provide an insight into the structural basis of ligand-ERalpha for alternate sites beyond the LBD-based pocket. The core structure proposed in this study could potentially be used as the lead molecule for further rational optimization of the antiestrogen drug structure with stronger binding of DBD and higher activity.
