## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a potentially interesting finding regarding expansion and functional diversification of WC-2 proteins in B. trispora. However, the evidence base is insufficient to evaluate the robustness of the claims. Key issues include lack of experimental detail for transcriptional regulation, absence of validation for protein-protein interactions, and reliance on unvalidated computational docking to support structural conclusions. The claim of a "unique situation not yet described in other fungi" requires comparative evidence that is not provided.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in fungal photobiology, blue-light signaling, and carotenoid regulation would find these results relevant. The potential diversification of WC complexes in Mucorales fungi could inform evolutionary studies of light sensing. Industrial microbiologists interested in carotenoid production in B. trispora may also follow this work, though the abstract does not directly address biotechnological applications.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses an under-explored area of WC protein diversity in a biotechnologically important fungus. The identification of four wc-2-like genes and the differential regulation under blue light suggest a more complex regulatory network than previously recognized. The combination of interaction assays and structural prediction is a reasonable exploratory approach.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
- **Claim pointer** "When the mycelia were exposed to blue light, their transcription levels are regulated differentially."
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only, location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract states that the four wc-2-like genes are differentially regulated under blue light, but no quantitative data, statistical analysis, time points, or comparison to dark controls are provided. It is unclear whether the observed changes are biologically significant or merely minor fluctuations.
- **Why it matters** Differential regulation is a central claim supporting the functional relevance of these genes in light signaling. Without robust expression data, the conclusion that these genes participate in light regulation is not substantiated.
- **Resolution test** Provide quantitative RT-PCR or RNA-seq data with appropriate replicates, statistical tests, and clear demonstration of blue-light-dependent changes relative to dark controls.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
- **Claim pointer** "BtWC-2a interacts with either BtWC-1a or BtWC-1c to form different photoreceptor complexes in yeast two-hybrid assays, which is the unique situation not yet described in other fungi."
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only, location not provided
- **Concern** The yeast two-hybrid interaction is reported without any details on controls, such as autoactivation tests, positive and negative controls, or confirmation by an orthogonal method such as co-immunoprecipitation. The claim of uniqueness requires a comparative survey of WC-1 and WC-2 interactions across fungi, which is not presented.
- **Why it matters** Protein-protein interaction data from yeast two-hybrid alone are prone to false positives. The uniqueness claim is a strong statement that demands comprehensive comparative evidence to be credible.
- **Resolution test** Provide full yeast two-hybrid data with proper controls, confirm interactions using an independent method, and include a literature-based or experimental comparison of WC-1 and WC-2 interaction patterns across relevant fungal species.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Technical validity
- **Claim pointer** "The protein-protein docking analysis by the predicted 3D structures showed that the two complexes are structurally different."
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only, location not provided
- **Concern** The structural conclusion is based on predicted 3D structures and docking analysis, but no details are given on the prediction methods, template selection, confidence scores, or docking algorithms. Predicted structures, especially without experimental validation, are insufficient to support claims of structural differences between complexes.
- **Why it matters** The structural difference is used to suggest functional divergence between the two complexes. If the predictions are unreliable, this conclusion is unsupported and could mislead subsequent interpretations.
- **Resolution test** Provide detailed methods for structure prediction and docking, including confidence metrics, and ideally validate key structural features experimentally or through comparison with experimentally determined homologs.

- **Concern ID** R1-M4
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Claim scope
- **Claim pointer** "These results suggested that WC proteins of B. trispora are still involved in light regulation by forming WCC and the regulation mechanism of the photobiology appears to be more complex."
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only, location not provided
- **Concern** The conclusion that WC proteins are involved in light regulation by forming WCC is inferred from interaction data and expression patterns, but no functional assays such as mutant phenotypes, complementation, or target gene activation are presented. The claim of increased complexity is speculative without direct functional evidence.
- **Why it matters** The central conclusion of the study rests on functional inference rather than direct demonstration. Without functional validation, the physiological relevance of the proposed complexes remains uncertain.
- **Resolution test** Perform functional studies such as gene knockout or silencing, assess phenotypic responses to blue light, and measure downstream target gene expression to confirm the role of the proposed complexes in light regulation.
