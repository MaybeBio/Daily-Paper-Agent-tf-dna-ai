## Complementary DNA-contact and domain-destabilizing mechanisms of PAX9 and MSX1 variants in non-syndromic tooth agenesis: an AlphaFold 3, ThermoMPNN, and AlphaGenome study


## Abstract

Missense variants in the DNA-binding domains of PAX9 and MSX1 cause non-syndromic tooth agenesis, yet most substitutions remain variants of uncertain significance. We investigated the structural mechanisms of 39 pathogenic missense variants in the PAX9 paired domain (n = 32) and MSX1 homeodomain (n = 7) alongside 77 non-coding/splice ClinVar variants. We modelled protein-DNA complexes with AlphaFold 3, estimated folding stability changes (ΔΔG) with ThermoMPNN, and compared pathogenic variants with gnomAD v4 domain variants. AlphaFold 3 confidence metrics and mutant interfaces were insensitive to point mutations. Instead, pathogenic PAX9 variants acted through two complementary mechanisms relative to recurrent population controls: loss of direct DNA contact (< 4 Å; 50% vs 17%; p = 0.0025) or domain destabilization (median ΔΔG 1.17 vs 0.34 kcal/mol; p = 4.6×10 -5 ). Leave-one-out cross-validation selected a stable rule (DNA contact or ΔΔG ≥ 1.5 kcal/mol; apparent sensitivity 84%, specificity 79%) concordant with published DNA-binding assays for five of eight PAX9 mutants; canonical AlphaMissense (> 0.564) showed 8%-21% specificity across both domains. Mechanism did not predict missing teeth, whereas missense variants were milder than null alleles (p = 0.001). AlphaGenome matched SpliceAI and jointly prioritized seven intronic variants.
