### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** The foldamers bind to Sac7d better than a DNA duplex of comparable length.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only, location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract states that the foldamers bind better than a DNA duplex, but no quantitative data (e.g., dissociation constants, kinetic parameters, or statistical comparison) are provided. It is unclear how the DNA duplex was chosen, whether it was of identical length or sequence, and whether the comparison was performed under matched conditions.  
- **Why it matters** This is a central claim that distinguishes the foldamers from natural DNA. Without quantitative binding data and a clearly defined comparator, the claim cannot be evaluated.  
- **Resolution test** Provide SPR-derived equilibrium dissociation constants (KD) for both the foldamer and the DNA duplex under identical buffer and temperature conditions, with error estimates and replicates. Specify the DNA sequence and length, and justify the choice of comparator.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** The interaction is diastereoselective and takes place at the DNA-binding site.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only, location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract claims diastereoselectivity and binding at the DNA-binding site, but no experimental evidence is described. It is unclear which diastereomers were compared, how selectivity was quantified, and which experiments (e.g., competition assays, mutagenesis, or NMR chemical shift perturbations) were used to localize the binding site.  
- **Why it matters** Diastereoselectivity implies a specific chiral recognition mechanism, and binding at the DNA-binding site is essential for the claim that these foldamers act as DNA mimics. Without supporting data, these claims are unsupported.  
- **Resolution test** Show binding data for at least two diastereomers (e.g., SPR or ITC) with clear differences in affinity. Provide evidence for binding site localization, such as competition with a known DNA ligand, NMR perturbation mapping, or mutagenesis of Sac7d residues.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3

- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** Crystallography revealed that the DNA-mimic foldamers have a binding mode of their own and can bind to Sac7d without being kinked.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only, location not provided  
- **Concern** The crystallographic claim is presented without any structural details. No resolution, refinement statistics, or description of the binding interface is given. The statement that the foldamers are "not kinked" is ambiguous, as it is unclear what conformational comparison is being made (e.g., free vs. bound foldamer, or foldamer vs. DNA in a Sac7d-DNA complex).

- **Resolution test** Provide crystallographic data (resolution, R/Rfree, PDB accession codes) and a structural comparison of the bound foldamer conformation to both the free foldamer and the known Sac7d-DNA complex. Define "kinked" operationally (e.g., bend angle calculations).
