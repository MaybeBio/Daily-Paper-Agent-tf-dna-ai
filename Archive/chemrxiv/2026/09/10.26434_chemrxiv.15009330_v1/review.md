## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence presented in the abstract; no methods, figures, tables, or supplementary materials were provided
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors propose a combined physics- and machine-learning-based method for generating optimal protein–ligand binding poses in relaxed states. They report that three existing AI-based tools (AlphaFold3, Boltz, RoseTTAFold All-Atom) show substantial shortcomings for this task. The method is applied to two targets (TNKS2 and 3CLPro) with datasets of 10,000 molecules each, and evaluated on an external validation set of approximately 3,000 molecules. The authors claim the method generalizes to unseen datasets and reduces the need for costly calculations by filtering out poses unlikely to yield favorable binding affinities upon relaxation.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no quantitative results, performance metrics, comparisons, or methodological details are provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, methods section, all figures and tables, dataset descriptions, validation protocols, baseline comparisons, and any statistical analyses

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a potentially valuable objective, namely the efficient generation of optimal binding poses to support downstream affinity prediction. However, the current submission, as represented by the abstract alone, does not provide sufficient evidence to evaluate the validity, novelty, or generalizability of the proposed method. Key quantitative outcomes, methodological specifics, and comparisons against existing tools are absent. The claims of substantial shortcomings in existing AI-based tools and the demonstrated generalization of the proposed method cannot be assessed without supporting data.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Computational chemists, structural biologists, and drug discovery researchers would be interested if the method is validated, because reliable and efficient binding pose generation is a bottleneck for physics-based affinity prediction. The potential to reduce computational cost in high-throughput screening pipelines would appeal to both academic and industrial groups. However, interest is conditional on the evidence supporting the claims.
- **Major strengths** The problem addressed is well recognized and practically important. The proposed combination of physics and machine learning is conceptually reasonable. The use of large datasets (10,000 molecules per target) and an external validation set suggests an intention to test generalization, which is appropriate for the stated application.
