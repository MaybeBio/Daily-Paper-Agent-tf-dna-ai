## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence as presented in the abstract; no figures, tables, methods, or supplementary materials were provided
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors introduce SO3LR-SF, a physics-based scoring function built on the pretrained SO3LR machine-learned force field (MLFF), which combines equivariant message-passing for semi-local interactions with physically motivated terms for short-range repulsion, electrostatics, and long-range dispersion. They report performance on three benchmarks: PLA15 (relative interaction error 5.8% against DLPNO-CCSD(T) references), FEP benchmark (average Spearman correlation 0.51), and Wang dataset (0.51). They report computational speedups of 80- to 300-fold over comparable MLFFs, an 8 Å trimming protocol, a restrained geometry optimization step, a multi-level explainability framework, and two pocket descriptors for applicability prediction.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no numerical tables, methodological details, statistical analyses, or visualizations were provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, all figures and tables, methods section, benchmark definitions, error bars or confidence intervals, baseline method details, and any code or data availability statements

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a potentially valuable contribution to structure-based drug design by leveraging a pretrained MLFF that was not trained on protein–ligand complexes, which addresses a real concern about transferability in this field. The reported performance metrics are competitive with established methods, and the computational speedups are substantial. However, the abstract alone does not provide sufficient methodological detail or statistical rigor to fully evaluate the claims. The lack of error bars, the absence of methodological descriptions for the explainability framework and pocket descriptors, and the unclear definition of "on par" in statistical comparisons are notable gaps. The core idea is promising and the results, if reproducible, would be of interest to the computational drug discovery community.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Computational chemists and biophysicists working on scoring functions and binding affinity prediction; medicinal chemists involved in structure-based drug design; researchers developing machine-learned force fields and seeking transferable models; method developers interested in explainable AI for molecular systems.
- **Major strengths** The use of a pretrained MLFF not trained on protein–ligand complexes provides a stringent transferability test, which is a conceptually strong design choice. The reported accuracy on the FEP benchmark and Wang dataset is competitive with or better than several established methods. The computational efficiency (1-10 seconds per complex) is a significant practical advantage. The inclusion of explainability tools and applicability descriptors addresses the "black box" criticism often leveled at ML-based scoring functions.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Statistical rigor
  - **Claim pointer** "SO3LR-SF achieves an average Spearman correlation of 0.51, on par with the best semi-empirical quantum-mechanical method tested (0.47, GFN-FF) and with MMGBSA (0.44), and surpassing Glide (0.27)"
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract, benchmark results section (location not provided)
  - **Concern** The abstract reports average Spearman correlations without any measure of variance, such as standard deviation, standard error, or confidence intervals. The claim that 0.51 is "on par" with 0.47 and 0.44, and "surpassing" 0.27, cannot be statistically evaluated without knowing the spread of correlations across the 8 targets in each benchmark. A difference of 0.04 between 0.51 and 0.47 may or may not be significant depending on the variance across targets.
  - **Why it matters** Without variance estimates, the reader cannot determine whether the reported differences are meaningful or within noise. This is critical for assessing whether SO3LR-SF genuinely outperforms or matches the comparators, which is a central claim of the abstract.
  - **Resolution test** Provide per-target Spearman correlations, along with mean and standard deviation or 95% confidence intervals, for all methods on both the FEP benchmark and the Wang dataset. Perform and report a statistical test (e.g., Wilcoxon signed-rank test) comparing SO3LR-SF to each baseline.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that SO3LR-SF is "on par" with GFN-FF and MMGBSA and "surpassing" Glide is unsupported without variance estimates or statistical tests.
- The claim that the explainability framework is "multi-level" is unsupported without methodological description.
- The claim that two pocket descriptors can "flag applicability before scoring" is unsupported without descriptor definitions, correlation details, and validation.
- The claim of an "80- to 300-fold speedup over comparable MLFFs" is not verifiable without specifying the comparators and hardware configurations.
- The claim that the 8 Å trimming protocol "reduces runtime threefold without loss of accuracy" is not verifiable without accuracy metrics before and after trimming.
- The claim that restrained geometry optimization "recovers ranking accuracy for targets with strained input structures" is not verifiable without defining "strained" and reporting before-and-after metrics.
- The overall claim that SO3LR-SF "requires no domain-specific training data" is supported by the design statement but cannot be fully verified without details on the pretraining data and any fine-tuning steps.
