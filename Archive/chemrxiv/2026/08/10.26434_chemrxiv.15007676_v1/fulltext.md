The study of ligand binding to G-quadruplex (G4) DNA under molecular crowding is essential for understanding its implications in cellular context; however, the underlying mechanism remains largely unresolved. Here we investigate the binding of TMPyP4 to parallel c-MYC G4 DNA under EG/PEG crowding using steady-state and fluorescence correlation spectroscopy (FCS). Ligand binding affinity (K) to G4 exhibits a pronounced non-monotonic, U-shaped dependence on crowder size – first decreasing due to viscosity-induced hindrance, then recovering for larger PEGs. Kinetic analysis of FCS data reveals that this arises from competing effects of crowder-induced viscosity and depletion interactions on ligand association (݇k on ) and dissociation (݇k off ) rates. A modified Collins-Kimball-Debye formalism, combined with hybrid MD simulations, shows that larger crowders create polymer-free depletion cavities that accelerate ligand/G4 association, counteracting viscous drag. These results provide a generalizable framework that connects polymer physics to nucleic acid-ligand recognition, useful for G4-targeted drug design in the crowded cellular milieu.
