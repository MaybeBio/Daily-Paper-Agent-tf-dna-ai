Protein adhesion to titanium dioxide surface underlies both biological implants and mussel based wet adhesives and is commonly attributed to the post translationally modified catechol bearing wild amino acid 2,3-dihydroxyphenylalanine. However, how the DOPA deficient protein dbfp7 adheres to the surface remains unclear. Using all atom molecular dynamics and umbrella sampling, we studied the mussel foot protein dbfp7, both short sequence-motif peptides and the intact N-terminal fragment across multiple conformers and orientations using rutile titanium oxide surface (110). Individual residues bind only weakly (at or below thermal energy) to the surface and binding did not strengthen with peptide length. Nonetheless, the intact protein fragment adheres irreversibly to the surface, engaging with multiple different interfaces rather than a single binding site. Aromatic residues were enriched at the interface when exposed to the surface but were not required, as an aromatic-poor face also binds equally well. These findings suggest that dbfp7 adheres to the surface through the collective action of many weak, non-specific contacts, providing a mechanism for robust adhesion in the absence of catechol chemistry.
