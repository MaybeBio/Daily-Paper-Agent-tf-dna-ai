While airborne coronavirus virions penetrate respiratory airways, lung surfactants (LS) adsorb on spike proteins and may hinder their binding to cell receptors. In this work, we utilize microsecond-long unbiased and adaptive sampling molecular dynamics simulations performed on the Anton 2 supercomputer to elucidate the molecular mechanisms and dynamic nature of adsorption of lung surfactant lipids and cholesterol to the receptor-binding domain (RBD) of the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein and its Delta and Omicron variants. From unbiased simulations of spontaneous multi-surfactant binding, we show that surfactants tend to destabilize the protein and induce enhanced structural fluctuations that can influence the receptor-binding interface. We identify four surfactant binding sites (SBS) that are conserved across the variants, one of which corresponds to the experimentally identified fatty acid binding site (FABS). We analyze the energetics of multi-surfactant binding and show that FABS is the strongest binding site on the RBD, consistent with our previous coarse-grained simulations. Using adaptive sampling with the tempered binding approach, we demonstrate unbinding and rebinding of surfactants from selected binding sites, elucidating the structural dynamics and the binding enthalpy profiles. Identification and quantitative characterization of the binding sites affinity and stability provide a better understanding of the specifics of the lung surfactant–spike protein interactions and may inform the design of surfactant therapies for prevention and mitigation of coronavirus infections.
