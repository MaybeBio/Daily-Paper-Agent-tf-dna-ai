G-quadruplex (G4) DNA structures in oncogenic promoter regions play crucial roles in gene regulation and represent promising targets for anticancer therapeutics. Polyaromatic small molecule ligands, including benzo[a]phenoxazin-based derivatives, selectively bind G4 structures through π-π stacking or groove/loop binding, thereby mitigating transcriptional activity. However, the kinetic and thermodynamic parameters governing ligand-G4 interactions, particularly the ligand’s residence time within the G4 complex, have not been sufficiently characterized, although the residence time has been found to be a key parameter of drug efficacy and resistance in proteins. Here, we combine fluorescence correlation spectroscopy (FCS) with atomistic molecular dynamics (MD) simulations, including well-tempered metadynamics (WT-MetaD), and infrequent metadynamics (iMetaD), to characterize the binding thermodynamics and kinetics of a substituted cationic benzo[a]phenoxazin ligand with parallel G4 DNA formed by a 22-mer c-MYC promoter sequence. Steady-state fluorescence titration shows high binding affinity of the ligand to the parallel c-MYC G4 ( K = (2.9 ∓ 0.3) x 10 7 M -1 ), primarily driven by π-π stacking of benzo[a]phenoxazin aromatic core on the solvent-exposed 5’-terminal G-tetrad, along with stabilizing hydrogen-bond and salt-bridge interactions. FCS measurements reveal rapid reaction-coupled diffusion kinetics, yielding a ligand association rate of ݇ k on = (2.4 ∓ 0.2) x 10 9 M -1 s -1 and a ligand residence time ( τ res ) of ~10 ms in the G4-bound state, showing that ligand dissociation is the rate-limiting step. WT-MetaD simulations, employing contact-pair-based and hydration collective variables, enable reconstruction of a converged free-energy surface that provides binding free energy consistent with the experimental value and reveals multiple π-stacked binding orientations of the ligand on the G-tetrad, separated by barriers of ~30–35 kJ mol -1 . Extensive iMetaD simulations capture highly directional ligand-dissociation pathways through the loop region formed by T7, G8 and G9 of G4-DNA and recover a ligand residence time of ~9 ms, which closely matches the FCS result and directly validate iMetaD-derived kinetics. These findings establish a quantitative benchmark between enhanced-sampling simulations and experimentally measured kinetic and thermodynamic parameters for the interaction of benzo[a]phenoxazin with c-MYC G4 DNA. This work provides a generalizable multiscale framework for elucidating deeper molecular insight into ligand-G4 recognition across energy and time scales, identifying ligand residence time as a critical parameter that may be optimized through rational design of next-generation benzo[a]phenoxazin-based G4-targeted compounds with better efficacy.
