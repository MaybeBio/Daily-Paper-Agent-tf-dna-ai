Accurate prediction of protein–ligand binding modes is crucial for structure-based drug discovery. However, current pose prediction methods are often integrated with binding affinity scoring functions without providing a reliable measure of the confidence or reliability of individual pose predictions. We present a hybrid docking and machine learning workflow for binding mode prediction and evaluate whether counterfactual perturbations of pocket-ligand interaction features can support uncertainty-aware pose prediction. Here, the candidate poses were generated with AutoDock Vina and ranked using a geometry-aware gradient-boosted tree model trained on PLINDER-derived protein-ligand complexes. To estimate uncertainty, we masked hydrogen-bond, hydrophobic, metal, polar, clash, and contact-shell feature groups and measured prediction sensitivity. The chemical counterfactual uncertainty detected the failed top-1 predictions with AUROC 0.895 and average precision 0.878. The selective prediction substantially improved reliability: retaining the lowest-uncertainty 50% of predictions increased top-1 success to 88.79%. These results suggest that uncertainty-aware pose prediction can provide actionable confidence estimates for docking-based binding mode prediction, and that counterfactual pocket perturbation offers a practical route toward interpretable failure detection.
