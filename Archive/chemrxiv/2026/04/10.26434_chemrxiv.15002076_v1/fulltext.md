Non-covalent interaction plays key role in molecular recognition in biomolecular complex and thereby to control biomolecular functions. In this work, molecular dynamics simulations were employed to investigate the conformational dynamics and stability of DNA in the Cren7–DNA complex and to understand how protein mutation can alter DNA stability through non-covalent interactions. Both the wild-type (WT) protein and four aromatic mutants (W26A, F41A, F50A, and Y58A) were examined to assess the effect of protein mutation on DNA structural stability. Cren7 binding induced bending of DNA in both WT and mutant complexes, although the extent of bending varied. The mutants exhibited enhanced bending compared to WT, with the following trend: F41A > Y58A > W26A > F50A > WT. The radius of gyration (Rg) of DNA shows higher fluctuations in Rg for mutants in compared to WT, reflecting reduced compactness and stability of the DNA duplex upon mutation. Principal component analysis (PCA) further highlighted differences in collective DNA motions between WT and mutants, with mutants sampling a broader conformational space, consistent with enhanced flexibility and instability. In parallel, hydrogen bonding between base pairs was perturbed during the simulations, consistent with conformational rearrangements of the DNA helix. Alteration in base stacking interactions further highlighted changes in DNA stability. Analysis of intra-base pair and base-pair step parameters revealed notable deviations in the mutants relative to WT. These changes were accompanied by modifications in backbone dihedral angles as well as in groove width and depth, reflecting global perturbations in DNA architecture. The results provide qualitative and quantitative insights into structural and conformational control of DNA via protein mutation, which is solely governed by non-covalent interactions. This study also provides a comprehensive protocol for investigating the stability and conformational dynamics of protein-DNA complexes at a microscopic level.
