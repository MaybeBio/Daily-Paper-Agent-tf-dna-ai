## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence as presented in the abstract; no access to full manuscript, figures, tables, or supplementary materials
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors propose a chemically transferable coarse-grained (CG) force field, PEBBLE, for interprotein interactions. Parameters are derived by matching two-body Lennard-Jones-Gaussian (LJG) potentials to potentials of mean force (PMFs) from all-atom molecular dynamics (AAMD) of GXG tripeptides, using relative entropy minimization (REM) for all 210 amino acid pairs. The model is claimed to reproduce AA PMFs and experimental binding free energies for three benchmarks (QVVAG dimerization motif, E3/K3 coiled-coil, barnase/barstar wild-type and mutants) and to reproduce assembly outcomes for tripeptides VFF (fibrillar) and ECG (non-assembling).
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no quantitative results, methodological details, or validation data are provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, all figures and tables, simulation protocols, parameter sets, error analyses, benchmark definitions, and comparison statistics

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a conceptually appealing and potentially impactful framework for transferable CG modeling of protein-protein interactions. The idea of learning interaction parameters from small peptide PMFs and generalizing to larger assemblies is well motivated and aligns with current needs in the field. However, the abstract alone provides insufficient quantitative evidence to assess the validity, robustness, or transferability of the claims. Key details on parameterization, validation metrics, and benchmark performance are absent, limiting the ability to evaluate technical soundness and scientific significance.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in computational biophysics, coarse-grained modeling, protein design, and biomolecular materials engineering would be interested. The promise of a chemically transferable CG force field that bridges molecular PMFs to higher-order assemblies could accelerate simulations of protein oligomerization and self-assembly, which are currently limited by AAMD costs.
- **Major strengths** The approach is novel in using tripeptide PMFs as building blocks for transferable interprotein interactions. The choice of LJG potential and REM parameterization is physically reasonable. The benchmark selection spans diverse protein systems, including a dimerization motif, a coiled-coil, and a globular complex, which strengthens the claim of generalizability. The inclusion of assembly outcome predictions for tripeptides adds a practical dimension.
- **Major Concerns** None identified from the supplied material, as the abstract provides no quantitative evidence to scrutinize. However, the absence of data itself is a major limitation for assessment.
- **Minor Comments** None identified from the supplied material.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** The abstract does not report any numerical results, such as PMF comparison errors, binding free energy deviations, or statistical uncertainties. Without these, the central claim of "good agreement" cannot be verified. The transferability claim rests on the assumption that tripeptide-derived parameters generalize, but no evidence is shown for sensitivity to training set composition or system size.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality is high, as the building-block approach to transferable CG interactions is not standard. Scientific importance is potentially high, given the need for efficient yet accurate models of protein interactions. Interdisciplinary readership is plausible, spanning chemistry, biology, and materials science. Technical soundness cannot be assessed from the abstract alone. Readability for nonspecialists is adequate, though the abstract assumes familiarity with PMF and CG concepts.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The abstract is promising, but the lack of quantitative results and methodological detail prevents a supportive recommendation. A full manuscript with rigorous validation would be required to assess the case.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim of "good agreement" with AA PMFs and experimental binding free energies is unsupported, as no numerical values or error metrics are provided.
- The claim of "reproduce assembly outcomes" for VFF and ECG is unverifiable without details on the simulation conditions and criteria for assembly.
- The generalizability from tripeptide PMFs to higher-order structures is asserted but not demonstrated with any comparative data.
- The statement that PEBBLE will "enable sampling" and "accelerate development" is speculative and not backed by performance benchmarks.
