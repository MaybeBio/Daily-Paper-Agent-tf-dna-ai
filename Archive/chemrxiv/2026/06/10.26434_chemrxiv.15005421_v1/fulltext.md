Rare-earth separation remains energy-intensive and waste-generating, motivating aqueous alternatives for selective lanthanide capture. We report a deterministic computational workflow that couples binding-site engineering of lanmodulin with multireference quantum characterization toward dysprosium prioritization. Four experimentally solved lanmodulin structures were harmonized, residue roles were assigned to preserve the first-shell coordination environment while allowing selected second-sphere and interface positions to mutate, and ProteinMPNN, LigandMPNN, Rosetta triage, and reduced OpenMM rescreening were used to rank redesigned pockets against dysprosium, neodymium, yttrium, aluminum, and iron. The workflow reduced 66 evaluated mask positions to 3 finalists while retaining rare-earth binding in all cases. The top-ranked Hans-derived pocket showed the strongest local confidence signal, the lowest pocket free-energy proxy, and stable rare-earth coordination, but aluminum and iron capture persisted across all three candidate pockets. The nominated pocket was then advanced to multiconfigurational treatment: compact Dy(III) and Nd(III) coordination clusters were extracted and characterized with CASSCF and CASPT2 using a 4f active space, confirming a physically valid 4f manifold for each metal and a well-converged multireference description. These results deliver a quantum-characterized dysprosium-prioritized lanmodulin pocket and matched Dy/Nd electronic-structure references, and show that competitor suppression remains the main design challenge.
