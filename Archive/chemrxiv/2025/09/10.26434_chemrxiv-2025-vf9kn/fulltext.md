BML-284 is a cell-permeable small molecule known to act as an agonist of the Wnt signaling pathway. We have previously reported photoswitchable azo derivatives of BML-284 capable of reversible trans–cis isomerization under visible light. Only the cis isomer of these derivatives activates Wnt signaling, offering potential for spatiotemporal modulation of Wnt signaling. However, the precise molecular target and binding mechanism of BML-284 and photoswitchable azo derivatives of BML-284 within the Wnt pathway remain uncharacterized. To address this, in this study, we perform molecular docking and molecular dynamic simulations that identify potential binding sites for both BML-284 and its photoswitchable analogs. In the absence of crystal structures for the Wnt receptor complex, we initially employed a model system using tubulin, which revealed distinct binding differences between the trans and cis isomers. Subsequently, docking studies with the Frizzled (FZD) receptor homologs, known Wnt pathway components, demonstrate that both BML-284 and the cis isomer of the photoswitchable agonists exhibit favorable binding interactions with the protein. These findings suggest that the FZD receptor is a likely molecular target for BML-284 and its photoswitchable analogs, supporting their role in Wnt pathway activation.
