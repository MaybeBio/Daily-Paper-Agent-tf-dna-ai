Fast, general, and interpretable quantum accuracy remains elusive for large, complex molecules. Here we introduce NN-xTB, a Hamiltonian-preserving framework that augments the explicit GFN2-xTB operator with small, bounded, environment-dependent shifts to a compact set of physically named parameters predicted by an E(3)-equivariant encoder. By confining learning to on-site/shell terms, hardness and its charge derivative, overlap/hopping scalings, anisotropic electrostatics/XC prefactors, short-range exchange, and selected global factors, NN-xTB restores local chemical sensitivity while retaining analytic long-range limits, native charge/spin treatment, and a self-consistent (SCF) solution---so all observables arise from the same adapted electronic state. Across complementary benchmarks, NN-xTB attains DFT-like accuracy at near–xTB cost: on GMTKN55 it reaches a WTMAD-2 of 5.58 kcal/mol (versus 25.0 kcal/mol for GFN2-xTB, and 9.3 kcal/mol for g-xTB), achieves the lowest force MAE on 8/10 rMD17 molecules and 10–40% RMSE reductions on large MACEOFF23/SPICE subsets against strong equivariant MLIPs, and cuts VQM24 frequency MAE from 200.6 to 12.7 cm^(-1) (>90\% reduction). Under temperature shift (3BPA), errors remain markedly lower up to 1200 K. The neural-network component in NN-xTB adds <20% wall-time overhead, preserving the throughput that motivates semi-empirical quantum methods. Overall, NN-xTB demonstrates that targeted, Hamiltonian-level adaptation can bridge much of the gap to DFT while keeping interpretability, asymptotics, and robustness, offering a practical, GPU-ready path to quantum-accurate molecular simulation and design at scale.
