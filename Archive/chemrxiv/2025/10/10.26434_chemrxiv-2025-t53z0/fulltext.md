Specific lipid binding is a likely mechanism by which phospholipids may modulate the conformational ensembles of membrane proteins. An absolute binding affinity is the natural quantitative descriptor for such interactions, but has not been directly accessible. Erwinia ligand-gated ion channel (ELIC) is a lipid-sensitive model for the pentameric ligand-gated ion channel protein family. ELIC is sensitive to palmitoyl-oleoyl phosphatidylglycerol (POPG) in model membranes, and densities from cryogenic electron microscopy (cryo-EM) resemble ordered phospholipids near the protein-membrane interface. Here, we present precise binding affinities for occupancy of this density by POPG, using an atomistic implementation of our Streamlined Alchemical Free Energy Perturbation (SAFEP) method. Achieving convergence required computationally inexpensive collective variables that were robust to the flexibility of phospholipids. POPG shows weak non-ideality in a binary POPC:POPG membrane, with excess chemical potentials that are slightly weaker for 10-30% POPG. Non-ideality in the bulk membrane negligibly affects predicted binding, due to the strong binding affinity (-9.7+/-0.8 kcal/mol). These results align with previous binding free energy estimates and show robust convergence across several metrics. They are also consistent with the strong non-bonded interactions between POPG and the protein, not observed with POPC. This affinity quantifies the equilibrium binding of the glycerol backbone, a necessary but insufficient condition for modulation. Unbiased simulations reveal that when the backbone is bound, the headgroup can adopt at least two distinct conformations, each interacting with protein residues with established functional implications.
