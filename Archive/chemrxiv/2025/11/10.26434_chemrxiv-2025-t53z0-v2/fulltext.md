The absolute binding affinity of a ligand determines site occupancy in functional assays and is a critical quantity for elucidating modulatory mechanisms. Established approaches for soluble ligands in enclosed cavities do not translate to lipophilic ligands on hydrophobic surfaces. These limitations impede efforts to resolve lipid-mediated mechanisms in transmembrane proteins. Here, we apply a formally exact computational method (Streamlined Alchemical Free Energy Perturbation, SAFEP) to calculate the absolute binding affinity of an anionic phospholipid for a potential modulatory site on a lipid-sensitive ion channel. Specifically, we calculate the affinity of palmitoyl-oleoyl phosphatidylglycerol (POPG) at a cryo-EM–suggested site on the Erwinia ligand-gated ion channel (ELIC). Within a double-decoupling scheme, we calculate the POPG bulk chemical potential in palmitoyl-oleoyl phosphatidylcholine (POPC)/POPG mixtures and the decoupling free energy from the cryo-EM–suggested site. We extend the restraint scheme of SAFEP to flexible chains and calculate an affinity of -9.7 +/- 0.8 kcal/mol for binding modes in which the glycerol backbone of POPG aligns with the cryo-EM lipid density. This value is consistent with our previous relative binding affinity calculations, while reducing the statistical error by a factor of three and relaxing assumptions required for that calculation. Unbiased simulations reveal that when the backbone is bound, the headgroup can adopt at least two distinct conformations, each interacting with protein residues with established functional implications. The bulk chemical potentials indicate weak non-ideality, showing modest reductions in excess chemical potential at 10–30 mol% POPG; these values can be paired with single decoupling of POPG from any site to avoid recalculating membrane non-ideality. Overall, our results confirm high POPG affinity for this site and demonstrate SAFEP as a tractable route for quantifying absolute lipid–protein affinities for membrane proteins in mixed lipid bilayers.
